# Preliminary review: HB 2322
_AI-assisted preliminary draft for DBM reviewer validation. Not an official position._

## 1. Summary of the bill
HB 2322 (20th Congress) creates the Congressional Commission on Agriculture and Fisheries (AGRICOM) (s1). Section 4 names the body differently, as a 'Congressional Agriculture and Fisheries Commission'. Mandate (s4): a comprehensive national assessment and evaluation of the performance of the agriculture and fisheries sector, to recommend transformative, concrete and targeted reforms 'with the end of ensuring food security in the Philippines'. Objectives (s3, text truncated) include re-examining public sector goals, modifying policies and public expenditures, re-prioritizing support programs, and restructuring and streamlining agriculture and fisheries-related bureaucracies. Structure: twelve members, six from the Senate and six from the House, one from each chamber representing the Minority (s5). Seven committee chairpersons are ex officio members (three Senate, four House), and the Senate President and the Speaker designate the rest (s5). Members receive no compensation, but travelling, communications and other necessary expenses are allowed (s5). The visible text names no chairperson of the Commission. Head: an Executive Director appointed by the Commission, working full-time with the rank of Executive Director IV (Salary Grade 29) (s6). The Executive Director heads a Technical Secretariat and may hire temporary, contractual or consultancy staff and set their compensation subject to civil service rules (s7). The Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) is the research arm (s8). An Agriculture and Fisheries Legislation and Policy Advisory Council of sectoral experts is created (s9). Powers (s11, text truncated) include policy and program recommendations, approval of the workplan and budget, hearings, subpoena powers and requests for information from government offices. The Commission must accomplish its mandate within three years from its organization (s10). Funding (s13): the 'amount necessary' for initial implementation is charged against available appropriations of the Senate and the House; afterwards, continuing costs, 'including any deficiency in the current year', go in the annual General Appropriations Act. The bill states no peso amount. A general repealing clause is included (s15). The visible text does not abolish or transfer any existing office.

## 2. Legislative history and related measures
Taken only from the research agent output. Same-Congress related measures: SB-20-1199, the Senate counterpart, matches the target almost word for word (12 members, same committee chairs, Executive Director at SG 29, same powers and appropriations sections). HB-20-5296 and HB-20-5129 are near-copies refiled in the House; both add 'Food Security' to the name. HB-20-11060 is a competing House design that keeps 12 members but adds co-chairpersons, an expanded mandate and a Technical Secretariat, and calls the body independent and temporary. SB-20-1345 is a competing Senate design: agriculture only, 10 members, co-chairpersons, and a fixed appropriation. A Senate committee report (CR-20-AGRICOM) recommends SB-20-1624 in substitution of SB 1199 and SB 1345. SB-20-1624 is now the Senate vehicle. It shrinks the body to 10 members with co-chairpersons and sets a fixed initial appropriation. The committee report front matter shown does not mention HB 2322. Possible precedents, matched on title only (texts not reviewed): RA-11899 (Congressional Commission on Education; the target's title follows its title almost exactly) and SB-18-2485 (18th Congress, status 'Lapsed Into Law'). EO-127-1999 was judged unrelated. Status: the pack does not give authors, filing dates, committee referrals or the current status of HB 2322 or of the other 20th Congress bills. It contains no House committee report or substitute bill, so whether HB 2322 has been consolidated cannot be established, and no earlier-Congress AGRICOM bill was found.

## 3. Substantial differences from related measures
Taken only from the diagnosis agent output, which compared the target with SB-20-1624 (the Senate substitute). Verdict: 'partly different'. Substantial differences: (1) Mandate goal: the target aims its reforms at ensuring food security (s4). The reference aims at making the Philippines globally competitive in agriculture and fisheries (SB-20-1624:s3). (2) Size: 12 members in the target (s5), 10 in the reference (SB-20-1624:s7). Members are unpaid in both, but expenses are allowed, so the larger body likely costs somewhat more. (3) Leadership: the target makes seven committee chairs ex officio members and names no chairperson. The reference makes the two agriculture committee chairs Co-Chairpersons. (4) Executive Director: SG 29 and full-time in both; the target adds qualifications (citizenship, probity, independence, relevant experience) (s6). (5) Research arm: only the target names PIDS (s8), and it does not say who pays for that work (fiscal direction unclear). (6) Assessment scope: only the reference lists what the national assessment must cover (agency compliance with mandates, hindering factors, best practices, M&E plan, LGU role). The target's scope is more general. (7) Operational tasks: the reference's objectives include developing and implementing farmer training programs and market support. The target stays focused on review and re-prioritization, so it likely costs less than the reference on this point. (8) Standing committees: in the target they may be chaired by members or by multisectoral experts (s12); in the reference each must be chaired by a member. (9) Initial funding: the reference fixes an initial amount charged to Senate and House appropriations. The target charges an unspecified 'amount necessary' to the same source (s13), so the two start-up costs cannot be compared. (10) Deficiency clause: only the target lets the GAA cover 'any deficiency in the current year' (s13), which the diagnosis agent rates as likely to raise cost. The diagnosis agent judged several sections that a baseline marked 'changed' to match in substance or differ only in wording: Technical Secretariat, Advisory Council, Duration (three years in both), Separability, Repealing and Effectivity.

## 4. Overlap with existing law and agencies
From the research agent and the bill's own references. (1) RA 8435 (Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization Act): s13 already requires a medium- and long-term Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization Plan, and s14 covers food security plans. AGRICOM's recommendations on sectoral plans and targets (HB-20-2322:s11) would overlap with this planning mandate, although AGRICOM's role is recommendatory. (2) RA 12022 (Anti-Agricultural Sabotage Act): s15 created a President-chaired Anti-Agricultural Economic Sabotage Council, which deals with smuggling, hoarding, profiteering and cartels. A sector-wide review commission may cover the same ground. The bill cites RA 8435 and RA 12022 once each, but neither citation appears in the visible section text (s3 and s11 are truncated). Nothing visible amends, repeals or abolishes either law or the Council; the only repeal provision is the general clause in s15. (3) According to the diagnosis agent, the bill's explanatory note says two congressional oversight committees on agriculture and fisheries policy and programs have already been created by law. (4) The bill names PIDS as research arm and lets it coordinate with existing advisory and consultative bodies under the Department of Agriculture and the Department of Science and Technology (s8). These are existing bodies whose work could overlap with AGRICOM's. (5) Note on inputs: the research agent reported that only some sections were in its pack. The target section text supplied here runs from s1 to s16, with s3 and s11 truncated.

## 6. Implementation risks
- Staffing and absorptive capacity: both comparators had unfilled authorized positions in 2025. NWRB had 124 authorized and 110 filled; the Climate Change Commission had 76 authorized and 62 filled (source: COA Annual Audit Reports 2025 Executive Summaries, comparator table; figures 'reviewer to confirm'). With only three years to accomplish its mandate (HB-20-2322:s10) and staff who may be temporary, contractual or on consultancy (s7), AGRICOM may face similar or greater difficulty filling posts quickly.
- Budget utilization: in 2025, obligations were below allotments at both comparators. NWRB had allotments of 310445046.28 and obligations of 270029391.08; CCC had allotments of 271494591 and obligations of 260006493.23 (source: COA Annual Audit Reports 2025 Executive Summaries, comparator table; reviewer to confirm). A newly organized temporary body may also under-spend in its first year.
- Open-ended funding: s13 sets no initial amount, and it allows the GAA to cover 'any deficiency in the current year'. The diagnosis agent notes this leaves the start-up cost unstated and harder to control (source: HB-20-2322:s13; Agent 2 design options and differences).
- Unfunded workload: s8 assigns PIDS to be the research arm with no stated funding arrangement, which shifts work to PIDS (source: HB-20-2322:s8; Agent 2 gaps and design options).
- Possible duplication with existing mandates: the RA 8435 planning mandate (RA-8435:s13), the Anti-Agricultural Economic Sabotage Council under RA 12022 (RA-12022:s15), the two existing congressional oversight committees on agriculture and fisheries mentioned in the explanatory note, and DA/DOST advisory bodies (source: Agent 1 existing-law overlaps; Agent 2 problem diagnosis; HB-20-2322:s8).
- Governance: the visible text names no chairperson of the Commission. Nine of its twelve seats go to ex officio committee chairs or designated members drawn from both chambers. The diagnosis agent flags the lack of a designated leader as a trade-off (source: HB-20-2322:s5; Agent 2 design options and gaps).
- Legal drafting inconsistency: the body's name differs between s1 ('Congressional Commission on Agriculture and Fisheries') and s4 ('Congressional Agriculture and Fisheries Commission'), which may create ambiguity in implementation (source: HB-20-2322:s1, s4; Agent 1 gaps).
- Cost estimate fit: the engine's comparator method relies on executive-branch agencies with permanent plantilla and may not represent a temporary congressional commission. Its range is wide, and the engine says it is not a budget-purpose costing (source: costing engine output caveats and assumptions).
