# Red-team findings on the preliminary review of HB 2322

Recommendation: **revise first**

| Severity | Type | Where | Problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| medium | factual error / missed overlap | history (HB-20-11060 description) | The draft says HB-20-11060 'adds co-chairpersons, an expanded mandate and a Technical Secretariat' compared with the target. The target already creates a Technical Secretariat headed by the Executive Director, so a Technical Secretariat is not a difference. The draft's own differences section also counts the Technical Secretariat as matching in substance. The co-chairpersons and expanded-mandate parts have no citation either. The only cited HB-20-11060 text is s4 ('independent, temporary...'). |
| medium | overstated certainty / status not in inputs | history ('SB-20-1624 is now the Senate vehicle') | The draft states as fact that SB-20-1624 is the current Senate vehicle. The only support is that a committee report recommends it in substitution. The draft itself says the pack gives no current status for 20th Congress bills, and the checklist asks the reviewer to 'Confirm the status of SB 1624 and the Senate committee report'. The sentence should say that the report recommends it, not that it is the vehicle. |
| medium | difference overstated / misread of target | differences item (1) Mandate goal | The draft contrasts the target (food security) with the reference (global competitiveness), which implies the target leaves out competitiveness. But the target's own objectives explicitly include raising global competitiveness. The difference is one of emphasis in s4, not a different goal, and the verdict 'partly different' relies partly on this item. |
| medium | uncited claims | differences items (3), (4), (6), (8), (9), (10) and the 'Duration (three years in both)' statement; history details for SB-20-1199, HB-20-5296/5129, SB-20-1345, SB-20-1624 | Most statements about the reference and related bills have no supporting citation in the claims list. These include: SB-20-1624 Co-Chairpersons, ED at SG 29 and full-time, assessment-scope list, member-chaired standing committees, fixed initial appropriation, absence of a deficiency clause, and three-year duration. They also include SB-20-1199 having the 'same committee chairs, Executive Director at SG 29, same powers and appropriations sections', HB-20-5296/5129 adding 'Food Security' to the name, SB-20-1345 being 'agriculture only' with co-chairpersons and a fixed appropriation, and SB-18-2485's 'Lapsed Into Law' status. The only cited reference/related sections are SB-20-1624:s3, s4, s7; SB-20-1199:s4; HB-20-5296:s5; HB-20-5129:s13; SB-20-1345:s5. The draft also never gives the reference's fixed peso amount, so the 'fixed initial amount' claims cannot be checked. |
| medium | hidden 'to verify' assumption | fiscal.assumptions (headcount and entity type) and fiscal.narrative caution (a) | The draft's assumptions table marks 'Headcount 30-130' and 'Entity type: commission' as to_verify: false, yet the bill sets no staffing pattern and the narrative itself questions whether the entity type fits. The narrative also says the 30-130 headcount 'is taken from the comparators'. That is only partly true: the comparators have 76 (CCC) and 124 (NWRB) authorized positions, so the low end of 30 has no stated source. The engine does not attach a to_verify flag to these items, so the draft's 'false' values are its own judgment, and they understate uncertainty in the inputs that drive the whole range. |
| medium | fiscal narrative omits key fit caution | fiscal.narrative, Method B description | The draft does not explain what Method B's per-staff cost contains. The figures in the draft's own risks section show that per-staff = total 2025 obligations / filled positions: NWRB 270,029,391.08/110 = 2,454,812.65 and CCC 260,006,493.23/62 = 4,193,653.12. Method B therefore loads all program and operating spending of two executive agencies, possibly including capital outlay, onto each AGRICOM staff member. Yet the engine says it excludes capital outlay. It also divides by filled positions but applies the result to a headcount based on authorized positions. Method B sets the top of the range (PHP 545,174,905), so the reviewer should be told that the upper bound is probably the least representative figure for a recommendatory congressional body. |
| low | figures not in red-team inputs | risks (staffing and budget utilization items) | The filled-position counts (110, 62) and allotment/obligation amounts (310,445,046.28; 270,029,391.08; 271,494,591; 260,006,493.23) do not appear in the costing engine output or the bill. The comparator table they are attributed to was not supplied for this check. They agree with the engine's per-staff figures (see above), and the draft marks them 'reviewer to confirm', but they could not be checked here against their source. |
| low | fiscal narrative omits engine caveat | fiscal.narrative | The narrative does not repeat the engine's caveat that the range excludes transfers of existing personnel and budgets from abolished offices. It also does not state the consequence for this bill: nothing is abolished or transferred, so no offset reduces the net new cost and the gross range is also the net new cost. The engine's exclusion of 'regional offices beyond the comparators' scale' is also left out. Quoting the range to the last peso (PHP 28879960 to PHP 545174905) implies more precision than an 'order-of-magnitude' estimate supports. |
| low | engine assumptions may not fit staff type | fiscal.narrative caution (a) | Caution (a) flags the comparator headcount, but not that Method A's benefit stack assumes regular plantilla employees paid on the salary schedule. The benefits are year-end and mid-year bonuses, GSIS 12%, PhilHealth, PERA, Pag-IBIG, ECC, clothing allowance, cash gift and PEI. Section 7 instead lets the ED hire temporary, contractual or consultancy personnel and 'determine their compensation', and s11(b) has the Commission approve 'compensation of all personnel'. For this body the cost structure could therefore differ from Method A's in either direction. |
| low | imprecise / unsupported count | risks (Governance item: 'Nine of its twelve seats go to ex officio committee chairs or designated members drawn from both chambers'); summary and claim ('the Senate President and the Speaker designate the rest') | The 'nine of twelve' count is not stated in the bill. It holds only if it means 7 committee chairs plus 2 Minority designees and assumes no chair is a Minority member. As written, all twelve seats are chairs or designated legislators, so the sentence is unclear. Also, not every non-ex officio member is designated by the Senate President or Speaker, because the Minority member from each chamber is designated by the Minority Leader. The draft misses that only about 2 Senate and 1 House seats are left to the presiding officers, and that the bill does not say how the count works if a committee chair belongs to the Minority. |
| low | missed risk | risks / fiscal.missing_data | The draft names only PIDS as carrying unfunded work. It misses that s11(e) lets the Commission require any government department, bureau or office to prepare reports and submit recommendations or plans. It also misses that s9 puts two officials from government agriculture and fisheries agencies and two LGU heads on an 11-member Advisory Council whose members 'shall always be present in all meetings'. Both shift workload and costs to executive agencies and LGUs, which the draft does not mention. |
| low | missed risk (budget control) | risks (Open-ended funding item) | The open-ended funding risk does not mention that the Commission approves its own budget and all disbursements, including compensation of all personnel (s11(b)). Together with the unstated initial amount and the deficiency clause in s13, this weakens external control over spending. Section 7 (the ED determines compensation) and s11(b) (the Commission approves compensation) also divide that authority without saying how they fit together. |
| low | incomplete note on truncation / possible missed provision | existing_law item (5) and fiscal.missing_data | The draft says only s3 and s11 are truncated. Section 9 also ends mid-provision with no closing punctuation ('...engaged in agriculture and fisheries'), so any text on Advisory Council compensation or expenses may be missing. The draft's statements that the bill cites RA 8435 and RA 12022 'once each' cannot be checked against the supplied sections. The same applies to the RA 8435 s14 food-security plans and the RA 12022 Council's smuggling, hoarding and cartel functions, which have no citation. |
| low | characterization not fully supported | fiscal.narrative caution (a) ('AGRICOM is a temporary congressional commission') and existing_law item (1) ('AGRICOM's role is recommendatory') | The bill never calls the body 'temporary' and has no explicit dissolution clause. Section 10 only requires the Commission to accomplish its mandate within three years; 'temporary' is HB-20-11060's wording. Its objectives are also worded as direct action, not recommendation: 'Modify agriculture and fisheries policies and public expenditures', 'Restructure and streamline ... bureaucracies'. The draft should note that the duration and recommendatory nature are inferred from the bill, not stated in it, and that extensions are therefore possible. |
