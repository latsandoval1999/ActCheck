                          AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE DEPARTMENT OF CULTURE, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Culture is a key driver of inclusive development, soft power, innovation, and
national identity. Deeply rooted in history and tradition, it shapes who we are as a
people and unites Filipinos across regions and generations. The 1987 Constitution
affirms this by mandating the State to prioritize arts and culture to foster patriotism
and nationalism, accelerate social progress, and promote total human liberation and
development.
       Culture empowers local and Indigenous communities, fosters participation and
solidarity, and contributes to human development, community cohesion, and socio­
economic transformation. It also offers sustainable pathways for development through
the conservation of heritage structures, the promotion of cultural tourism, and the
revitalization of local economies.
       Yet despite this vast potential, the Philippines has yet to establish a dedicated
Department of Culture, unlike many of its neighbors in Southeast and East Asia. This
institutional gap has led to fragmented policy direction, chronic underinvestment, and
missed opportunities to fully harness our rich cultural assets. While the National
Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA) serves as the primary coordinating and
policy-making body, it is not a line agency and lacks direct authority over many of the
cultural institutions responsible for implementation. As a result, the cultural sector
remains underprioritized in national development planning and falls short of its
potential as a strategic pillar of growth and national unity.
        According to UNESCO, cultural and creative industries generate nearly US$2.3
trillion in annual global revenues, equivalent to 3.1 percent of the world's gross
domestic product (GDP). Their contribution to national value added varies among G20
economies, ranging from 0.7 percent in Mexico to 3 percent in the United States.
These industries also provide employment for approximately 6.2 percent of the global
workforce.1
        In the Asia-Pacific region, countries like Japan and South Korea have
successfully institutionalized cultural governance, generating massive returns. In
2023, Japan's anime industry (including television and film productions, streaming
distribution, merchandising, music, events, and overseas licensing), supported by
Agency for Cultural Affairs (Bunkachd) under the Ministry of Education, Culture,
Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT), reached an all-time high of ¥3.3465 trillion
(approximately US$22.3 billion). Of the total revenue, ¥1.6243 trillion (around
US$10.8 billion) came from domestic sources, while ¥1.7222 trillion (about US$11.5
billion) was generated from international markets.2
        South Korea's cultural content industry (Korean Wave or Hallyu), supported by
its Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, has steadily grown into a high value-added
sector marked by robust domestic sales and expanding exports. South Korea's cultural
content industry grew steadily from 2021 to 2023, with total sales rising from W137.5
trillion (US$105.8 billion)3 to W154.2 trillion (US$110.5 billion),4 and exports increasing
from US$12.45 billion to US$13.3 billion during the same period.

        These data points underscore a vital truth: countries that invest in strong
cultural governance and infrastructure reap significant returns, not only in terms of
economic growth, employment, and exports, but also in global influence, innovation,
and national unity. In contrast, the Philippines continues to operate without a
dedicated Department of Culture, limiting its ability to fully harness the value of its
diverse cultural assets. With the Philippines'creative economy already contributing 7.3
percent to Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and growing, the absence of a dedicated
department risks leaving our rich cultural capital under-leveraged and fragmented.
        This bill seeks to address that institutional gap. By establishing the Department
of Culture, it provides the policy leadership, operational mandate, and organizational
structure necessary to protect, develop, and promote Philippine culture, both as a
heritage to be preserved and as a resource to be activated for inclusive growth. Among
its key features are the following:
    1. Centralized cultural governance through the integration of policy, regulatory,
        and programmatic functions currently dispersed across multiple agencies;
    2. Nationwide and international reach via regional offices and Sentro /?/za/centers
        to bring cultural development closer to communities and diaspora networks;
    3. Capacity-building and education through national institutes dedicated to living
        traditions, heritage conservation, and cultural management training;
    4. Support for artists and creatives through grant programs, awards, and the
        consolidation of the National Endowment Fund for Culture and Arts;
    5. Strategic alignment with Republic Act No. 11904 (Philippine Creative Industries
        Development Act), ensuring synergy between heritage preservation and the
        growth of cultural and creative industries;

 5           Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State:
 6             (a) To recognize that culture is a unifying, humanizing and modernizing
 7                  agent/instrument of any society, and the patrimony and right of every
 8                  citizen of the Philippines;
 9             (b) To recognize the vital role of culture in nation-building;
10             (c) To incorporate considerations of culture in the formulation of appropriate
11                  and significant political and economic policy that will better serve
12                  Filipinos and ensure that all Filipinos enjoy full cultural rights, take full
13                  advantage of a dynamic cultural experience, and be inspired, sustained
14                  by, draw from, contribute to and celebrate the country's culture;
 1           (d) To create a national body which will develop, manage and be responsible
 2                for the implementation of policy, legislation and strategic direction for
 3                the protection, regulation, preservation, development, management,
 4                dissemination and promotion of the country's culture, relative to the
 5                immense national resources, patrimony and wealth engendered and
 6                amassed through countless generations and centuries of distinctive
 7                Filipino ways of life and experiences in specific natural and social
 8                environments that this represents, and to facilitate the convergence of
 9                various agencies with cultural         mandates and      rationalize their
10                organizations and functions;
11           (e) To foster, strengthen, and accelerate the convergence of various cultural
12                offices and agencies, and rationalize their organizations and functions;
13           (f) To recognize that cultural education is the right of every citizen and
14                therefore ensure that cultural literacy is inculcated in the development
15                and education of all Filipinos;
16           (g) To promote the national language of the country while enhancing the
17                linguistic diversity of the Philippines; and
18           (h) To secure and improve the welfare of Filipino artists and cultural
19                workers.
20            (i) The Department shall continually review the state and needs of culture
21                and arts in the context of the country's developmental goals.
22            (j) To support and promote the development of the Philippine creative

28         Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
29   be defined as follows:
30           (a) "Art" shall refer to the expression or application of human creative skill
31                and imagination.
 1   (b) "Conservation" shall refer to all processes and measures of maintaining
 2         the cultural significance of a cultural property, including but not limited
 3         to, preservation, restoration, reconstruction, protection, adaptive re-use
 4         or any combination thereof.
 5   (c) "Cultural agencies" shall refer to any of the following national
 6         government agencies with their specific areas of responsibility: National
 7         Museum of the Philippines (cultural property), the National Library of the
 8         Philippines (books). National Historical Commission of the Philippines
 9         (Philippine   history),   the   National   Archives   of   the   Philippines
10         (documents), the Cultural Center of the Philippines (culture and arts)
11         and Komisyon sa Wikang Filipino (language).
12   (d) "Cultural Education" shall refer to the teaching and learning of cultural
13         concepts and processes.
14   (e) "Cultural Heritage" shall refer to the totality of cultural property
15         preserved and developed through time and passed on to posterity.
16   (f)   "Cultural Property" shall refer to all products of human creativity by
17         which people and nation reveal their identity, including architecture and
18         sites or human activity [churches, mosques and other places of religious
19         worship, schools] and natural history specimens and sites, whether
20         public or privately owned, movable or immovable, and tangible or
21         intangible.
22   (g) "Culture" shall refer to the broad spectrum of people's beliefs, practices,
23         customs and traditions, material culture, arts, and other forms of actual
24         and symbolic expressions and representations which are handed down
25         from one generation to the next in the process of interaction.
26   (h) "History" shall refer to a written record of past events relating to
27         Philippine history.
28   (i)   "Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples" shall refer to a
29         group of people or homogenous societies identified by self-ascription
30         and ascription by others, who have continuously lived as organized
31         community on communally bounded and defined territory, and who
32         have, under claims of ownership since time immemorial, occupied.
 1                 possessed and utilized such territories, sharing common bonds of
 2                 language, customs, traditions and other distinctive cultural traits, or who
 3                 have, through resistance to political, social and cultural inroads of
 4                 colonization, non-indigenous religions and cultures, became historically
 5                 differentiated from the majority of Filipinos. ICCs/IPs shall likewise
 6                 include peoples who are regarded as indigenous on account of their
 7                 descent from the populations which inhabited the country, at the time
 8                 of conquest or colonization, or at the time of inroads of non-indigenous
 9                 religions and cultures, or the establishment of present state boundaries,
10                 who retain some or all of their own social, economic, cultural and political
11                 institutions, but who may have been displaced from their traditional
12                 domains or who may have resettled outside their ancestral domains;
13           (j)   "Intangible Cultural Heritage" shall refer to practices, representations,
14                 expressions, knowledge, skills, as well as instruments, objects and
15                 artifacts associated therewith, that communities, groups and individuals
16                 recognize as part of their cultural heritage, such as:
17                           a. oral traditions, usages, customs, languages and other
18                               expressions;
19                           b. performing arts;
20                           c. social practices, religious rites, rituals, culinary traditions,
21                               and festive events;
22                           d. knowledge and practices concerning nature and the
23                               universe, worship; and
24                           e. traditional craftsmanship.

27         Sec. 4. Creation of the Department of Culture. - There is hereby created the
28   Department of Culture (Kagawaran ng Kultura), hereinafter referred to as the
29   Department, which shall be the primary policy-formulating, planning, coordinating,
30   implementing, and administrative entity of the executive branch of the National

 4   protection, preservation, regulation, development, management, dissemination and

 6   intangible, of the Philippines and the Filipino peopie, all of which, with their physical
 7   and social contexts and environments, are understood as together constituting and
 8   pertaining to Philippine culture for purposes of this Act.
 9         The Department shall continually review the state and needs of culture and arts
10   in the context of the country's developmental goals.

24         Sec. 24. Reorganization. - The laws and rules on government reorganization as
25   provided for in Republic Act No. 6656, otherwise known as the Reorganization Law,
26   shall govern the reorganization process of the Department and, where appropriate, its
27   attached agencies.
28         Sec. 25. Structure and Staffing Pattern. - Subject to the approval of the
29   Department of Budget and Management (DBM), the Department and, where
30   appropriate, its attached agencies, shall determine its organizational structure and


                                                17
 1   create new divisions or units as it may deem necessary, and shall appoint officers and
 2   employees of the Department and its attached agencies in accordance with the Civil
 3   Service Law, rules, and regulations.
 4         Sec. 26. Transition Period. - The transfer of functions, assets, funds, equipment,
 5   properties, transactions, and personnel of affected agencies, and the formulation of
 6   the internal organic structure, staffing pattern, operating system, and revised budget
 7   of the Department, shall be completed within six (6) months from the effectivity of
 8   this Act, during which time, the existing personnel shall continue to assume their posts
 9   in holdover capacities until new appointments are issued; Provided, that after the
10   abolition of the agency, as specified in Section 17(a) of this Act, the Department, in
11   coordination with the DBM, shall determine and create new positions, the funding
12   requirement of which shall not exceed the equivalent cost of positions abolished.
13         Sec. 27. Separation from Service. - Employees separated from the service as a
14   result of this reorganization shall, within ninety (90) days therefrom, receive the
15   separation and retirement benefits to which they may be entitled under Executive
16   Order No. 366. Provided that such separation pay and retirement benefits shall have
17   priority of payment out of the savings of the department or agency.
