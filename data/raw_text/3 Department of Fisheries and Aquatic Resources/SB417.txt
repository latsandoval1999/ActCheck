         PROVIDING FOR ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS,

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines, being an archipelago with very rich marine resources, can be a
marine powerhouse. Thus, it is logical to establish a Department that is focused
and accountable for the development, management, and conservation of our
country’s aquatic resources and fishing industries.

Fish and aquatic resources are some of the biggest blessings of God to the people.
Filipinos have always relied on fish as a basic food resource and commodity. Our
dependence on fish can be attributed to the archipelagic nature of the Philippines
with its 7,641 islands. Our national territory comprises all the islands and waters,
including its territorial sea, seabed, subsoil, the insular shelves, and other
submarine areas. The waters around and between the islands of the archipelago,
regardless of their breadth and dimensions, form part of the internal waters of the
Philippines. The Department of Agriculture’s Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic
Resources (DA-BFAR) notes that the Philippines’ coastline extends to 36,289
kilometers, while total territorial water area (including the exclusive economic zone)
is at 2,200,000 square kilometers, teeming with rich marine resources.1

As an archipelago, one of the major sources of livelihood in the country is fisheries.
Based on the latest DA-BFAR statistics, the number of registered fisherfolk
engaged in fishery activities totalled to 2,299,127 in 2023. The fishing industry
ranks as the 3rd highest contributor in the agriculture sector with 12.0% gross
value-added contribution in 2023. Fisheries production from the same year is at
4.26 million metric tons, which translates to a total contribution of Php 328.74 billion
in 2023.2

In the global context, the Philippines is considered to be one of the top fish
producing countries in the world, ranking 11th in 2022 with a total production of

Ironically, fisherfolks remain to have the highest poverty incidence among basic
sectors even if they contribute substantially to the country’s economy. While we
have very good people in the Department of Agriculture, the Department’s priority
is more on farming on land. It is time that the government provides for a focused,
single-minded, specialised department responsive to the needs and concerns of
the fisheries and aquatic resource industry.

Given the above tremendous potential of fisheries and aquatic resources and the
priority and resources made available to Departments, there is a need to create
the Department of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (DFAR) in order to strengthen
the institutional capacities of DFAR as the primary policy and regulatory body on
fisheries and aquatic resources. As with any administrative agency, DFAR will
serve public purpose, efficiency and the best interest of the industry. Hence, this
will be a significant step in order to better protect, utilize, maximize, and conserve
the nation’s marine resources as well as uphold the best interest of the fisherfolk.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate enactment of this measure is sought.
