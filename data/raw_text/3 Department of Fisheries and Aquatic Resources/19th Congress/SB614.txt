                                AN ACT
      CREATING A DEPARTMENT OF FISHERIES AND AQUATIC RESOURCES,
    PROVIDING FOR ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

 The Philippines, being an archipelago with very rich marine resources, can be a
 marine powerhouse. Thus, it is logical that a Department that is focused and
 accountable for the development, management, and conservation of our country's
 aquatic resources and fishing industries be established.

 Fish and aquatic resources are some of the biggest blessings of God to the people.
 Filipinos have always relied on fish as a basic food resource and commodity. Our
 dependence on fish can be attributed to the archipelagic nature of the Philippines
with its approximately 7,600 islands. Our national territory comprises all the islands
and waters, including its territorial sea, seabed, subsoil, the insular shelves, and other
submarine areas. The waters around and between the islands of the archipelago,
regardless of their breadth and dimensions, form part of the internal waters of the
Philippines. The Department of Agriculture's Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic
Resources (DA-BFAR) notes that the Philippines' coastline extends to 36,289
kilometers, while total territorial water area (including the exclusive economic zone)
is at 2,200,000 square kilometers, teeming with rich marine resources.1

As an archipelago, one of the major sources of livelihood in the country is fisheries.
Based on the latest DA-BFAR statistics2, the number of registered fisherfolk engaged
in fishery activities totaled to 2,078,913 in 2020. The fishing industry ranks as the 3>'d
highest contributor in the agriculture sector with 12.27% gross value added
contribution in 2020. Fisheries production from the same year is at 4.4 million metric
tons, which translates to a total contribution of Php 273.5 billion in 2020. At constant
prices, this equates to a 1.52% share to the country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP).

In the global context, the Philippines is considered to be one of the top fish producing
countries in the world, ranking 8th in 2018 with a total production of 4.35 million

 metric tons. Similarly, we are also the world’s 4* largest producer of aquatic plants
 ^including seaweeds} having produced a total of 1.48 million metric tonss. Overall,
 these figures reflect the importance of the industry in the country’s economy and its
 potential to grow.

 Moreover, by investing in methods to increase yields offish and aquatic resources, it
 niakes the produce more accessible to all. It inadvertently encourages healthy dietary
 choices, which in turn lessens the expenses on medical or health-related issues later
 on. While we have very good people in the Department of Agriculture, the
 Department's priority is more on agriculture. It is time that the government provides
 for a focused, single-minded, specialised department responsive to the needs and
 concerns of the fisheries and aquatic resource industry.

Given the above tremendous potential of fisheries and aquatic resources and the
priority and resources made available to Departments, there is a need to create the
Department of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (DFAR) in order to strengthen the
institutional capacities of DFAR as the primary policy and regulatory body on
fisheries and aquatic resources. As with any administrative agency, DFAR will serve
public purpose, efficiency and the best interest of the industry. Hence, this will be a
significant step in order to better protect, utilize, maximize, and conserve the nation's
marine resources as well as uphold the best interest of the fisher folk.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate enactment of this measure is sought.
