                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) is a coordinating
body under the Department of National Defense, which provides a comprehensive, all-hazard,
multi-sectoral, and community-based approach to disaster risk reduction and management.

However, considering the country’s location on the Pacific Ring of Fire and its recent ranking as
the world’s most disaster-prone country for the 16th consecutive year, with an expected average
of twenty-four (24) typhoons annually, the current structure of the NDRRMC has resulted in a
fragmented disaster response.

Hence, this bill seeks the immediate creation of the Department of Disaster Resilience (DDR) to
serve as the primary government agency in preparing for, responding to, and building the nation's
resilience against the constant threat of natural disasters and the effects of climate change. This
also ensures a focused, full-time effort to protect our people and our future.

The DRR will be empowered to:

    -   Lead the implementation of programs, projects and activities to reduce the
        country’s vulnerabilities to natural hazards and climate change;
    -   Oversee all aspects of the disaster cycle—from risk reduction, preparedness,
        and response to recovery and building communities;
    -   Administer, mobilize and monitor the utilization of disaster-resilience related
        funds, including but not limited to, the National Disaster Resilience Fund, and
        the Supplemental Disaster Resilience Support Fund;
    -   Inspect and examine the status of projects, programs, and activities
        undertaken by national government agencies and LGUs to support disaster
        resilience efforts;

4       Probabilistic Risk Assessment considers all possible scenarios, their likelihood, and
5       associated impacts. It is characterized by inherent uncertainties partly related to the natural
6       randomness of hazards, and partly because of our incomplete understanding and
7       measurement of the hazards, exposure, and vulnerability.
8    qqq.       Deterministic Risk Assessment - refers to an assessment that considers the possible
9       disaster impacts of a single scenario. This is in contrast to probabilistic risk assessment
10      which considers all possible scenarios, their likelihood, and associated impacts.
11   rrr. Risk Transfer - refers to the process of formally or informally shifting the financial
12      consequences of particular risks from one party to another whereby a household,
13      community, enterprise, or state authority will obtain resources from the other party after a
14      disaster occurs, in exchange for ongoing or compensatory social or financial benefits
15      provided to that other party.
16   sss. State of Calamity - a condition involving mass casualty and/or major damages to property,
17      disruption of means of livelihoods, roads and normal way of life of people in the affected
18      areas as a result of the occurrence of natural or human- induced hazard.
19   ttt. Sustainable Development - the development that meets the needs of the present without
20      compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
21   uuu.       Underlying Disaster Risk Drivers/ Underlying Risk Factors - Processes or conditions,
22      often development-related, that influence the level of disaster risk by increasing levels of
23      exposure and vulnerability or reducing capacity.
24   vvv.       Vital Facility Items - refers to government risk reduction and preparedness
25      equipment, accessories and other items such as radars, weather forecasting equipment,
26      flood monitoring instruments, seismographs, tsunami warning systems, and automated
27      weather systems.
28   www.       Vulnerability - refers to the characteristics and circumstances of a community,
29      system or asset that make it susceptible to the damaging effects of a hazard. Vulnerability
30      may arise from various physical, social, economic, and environmental factors.
31   xxx.       Vulnerable and Marginalized Groups - those who are at risk due to poverty and other
32      underlying risk factors including, but not limited to, women, children, elderly, differently-
33      abled people, and ethnic minorities.



                                                                                                    14
1       yyy. Whole-of-society Approach - the meaningful participation of and synergy of stakeholders that
2           represent different interests in all aspects of climate change adaptation, disaster risk and
3           vulnerability reduction and management across all levels. “Acknowledge the contribution
4           of an important role played by all relevant stakeholders, including individuals, families and
5           communities, intergovernmental organizations and religious institutions, civil society,
6           academia, the media, voluntary associations and, where and as appropriate, the private
7           sector and industry.”
8       zzz.        Whole of Government Approach - an approach that integrates the collaborative efforts
9           of the departments and agencies to achieve unity of effort toward a shared goal to broaden
10          the government’s options, increases      efficiency      to      maximize         all
11                  available       resources in a collaborative effort.”
12      aaaa.       Whole of Nation Approach - refers to an approach that seeks to bring about a
13          concerted effort towards national peace and security by creating consensus and
14          understanding of security that is shared not just among core security forces and oversight
15          government institutions, but also by civil society and all the nation’s communities.
16      bbbb.       Youth - refers to those persons aged fifteen (15) to thirty (30) years old, as provided
17          for under Republic Act No. 8044, otherwise known as “Youth in Nation-Building Act”.

18   SECTION 62. Standards for Recovery. – The Department shall observe internationally
19   accepted standards for recovery, planning, programming and implementation of the recovery
20   process shall:

24                 with the principle of “build forward better”;

28                 structures resulting from typhoons, flooding, landslides, and other hazards;

32                 capacities of communities in identifying programs and projects

34                 schools, sustainable livelihoods, and other public services in planning for
35                 settlement areas; and
36              8. Ensure restoration of peace and order and recovery of government functions.
