                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Given its location just above the equator, facing the open western Pacific
Ocean on the east, the Philippines is no stranger to natural disasters, with more
or less 20 typhoons entering the Philippine Area of Responsibility every year. The
country is also found along the Pacific Ring of Fire, an area in the basin of the
Pacific Ocean where many earthquakes and volcanic eruptions occur. As a result,
Filipinos have suffered numerous natural disasters over the years that have
resulted to loss of lives, injuries, and damaged or destroyed houses and
properties, with the cost of damage amounting to billions of dollars.


      The country has seen three of the top ten worst disasters in terms of
casualties in recent years, with Typhoon Sendong (Wash!) killing 1,439
individuals in December 2011, Typhoon Pablo (Bopha) killing 1,901 individuals in
December 2012, and Typhoon Yolanda (Haiyan) killing 6,300 individuals in
November 2013. Typhoon Yolanda, also referred to as Super Typhoon Yolanda,
is considered to be the world's strongest typhoon to hit at the time and is also
the deadliest typhoon, affecting mostly low-lying coastal islands where,
according to World Bank estimates, more than 60 percent of the population lives.


      Considering the vulnerability of the Philippines to various natural disasters
such as earthquakes, volcanic activities, tropical cyclones and other phenomena,
it is about time that we create a department that would function as the lead
agency in charge of, among others, disaster risk reduction, disaster preparedness
and response, rehabilitation and recovery. This bill seeks to create a Department
of Disaster Resilience, which shall take on the powers and functions of the Office
of Civil Defense, Climate Change Office of the Climate Change Commission, Geo-
Hazard Assessment and Engineering Geology Section of the Mines and
Geosciences Bureau, Health Emergency Management Bureau of the Department
of Health, Disaster Response Assistance and Management Bureau of the
Department of Social Welfare and Development, Bureau of Fire Protection, and
Program Management Office for Earthquake Resiliency of the Greater Metro
Manila Area. The Department shall oversee and coordinate the preparation,
implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of disaster and climate resilience
plans, programs, projects and activities to reduce the risk of natural hazards and
effects of climate change, and manage the impact of disasters with the ultimate
goal of saving lives and minimizing damage to property.


      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

35      their own needs.

 5         ddd) Vulnerability refers to the characteristics and circumstances of a

13            synergy of stakeholders that represent different interests in all aspects of

19            options, and increases efficiency to maximize all available resources in a
20            collaborative effort.

22            about a concerted effort towards national peace and security by creating
23            consensus and understanding of security that is shared not just among core
24            security forces and oversight government institutions, but also by civil society

16                                          RECOVERY
17
18          SEC. 62. Standards for Recovery. - The Department shall observe internationally
19   accepted standards for recovery, planning, programming and implementation of the
20   recovery process and shall:
21      (a) Improve the community's physical, social and economic resilience, consistent
22   with the principle of "build forward better";
23      (b) Use locally-driven and centrally-supported processes based on legal mandates
24   with supplementary capacity support when requested;
25      (c) Redirect development outside danger zones to minimize loss of lives and
26   structures resulting from typhoons, flooding, landslides, and other hazards;
27      (d) Employ outcome-driven planning and implementation;
28      (e) Maximize use of private-public sector partnership where possible;
29      (f) Consider local conditions such as culture, security situation, and existing
30   capacities of communities in identifying programs and projects;
31      (g) Ensure access to public transport, physical and mental health services, markets,
32   schools, sustainable livelihoods, and other public services in planning for settlement
33   areas; and
34      (h) Ensure restoration of peace and order and recovery of government functions.
