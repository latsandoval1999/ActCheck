                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE


        In 2023, according to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA)1 208 natural
extreme events and disasters occurred affecting 3 million families (13 million
individuals) with 157 lives lost. This would amount to P24.49 billion in total cost of
damages. This reality is further highlighted by being the world's most at-risk country
for 16 years straight according to the World Risk Index (WRI)2.


       When the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act was passed
in 2010, it transformed the National Disaster Coordinating Council (NDCC) into the
National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) which
oversees the Philippine disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM) system.
The National Council is composed of several interacting agencies sharing the

       This measure seeks the creation of a department that will focus on the
integration of disaster risk reduction and management and climate change
adaptation. In addition, the department shall also consolidate and assume the
functions of the Office of Civil Defense and the National Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Office with the purpose of substantially reducing the country's
vulnerabilities to disasters and other emergencies.


      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 3        Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State:
 4           a) To uphold the sacredness and dignity of human life by efficiently and
 5               effectively addressing humanitarian emergencies, including calamities and
 6               disasters, through the establishment of a focused,               streamlined,
 7               independent, empowered, capacitated, full-time, and specialized agency
 8               on disaster risk reduction and management as well as emergency
 9               response, which is national in scope and civilian in character. The people
10               are the most important assets of the nation, and all disaster risk reduction
11              and management efforts shall be responsive to the humanitarian needs
12              of the people, the dignity and value of the human person, and respect for
13              property;
14            b) To     promote     a    simplified, strategic,   systematic,     continuous,
15              comprehensive, inclusive, and integrated approach to disaster risk
16              reduction and management, with the end in view of substantially reducing
17              vulnerabilities and the risk of disasters and other humanitarian
 1          emergencies, towards the preservation of life and property, thereby
 2          ultimately preventing or deterring the loss of lives and the social,
 3          economic, cultural, and environmental assets of the country;
 4      c) To establish a permanent, institutionalized, cohesive, and comprehensive
 5          framework for disaster preparedness, prevention and mitigation, and
 6          response, to be implemented by a full-time and focused specialized
 7          agency with its own mandate, powers, and funding, in coordination with
 8          other government agencies, foreign governments, financial institutions,
 9          international organizations, the private sector, and civil society. In disaster
10          risk reduction and management, disaster prevention and mitigation shall,
11          first of all, aim the outright avoidance of the adverse impacts of hazards
12          and related disasters. However, with the knowledge and capacities to
13          effectively anticipate, respond to, and recover from, the impacts of likely,
14          imminent, or current hazard events or conditions, preparedness action
15          shall be carried out in the most effective and efficient manner. In a
16          disaster scenario, the response shall ensure the provision of emergency
17          services and public assistance, before, during, or immediately after, a
18          disaster; and
19     d) To create an integrated, systematic, comprehensive, and cohesive plan to
20          simplify, streamline, integrate, and coordinate the various programs,
21          projects,    and    activities   of   the   Government    and     such   other
22          actors/stakeholders in disaster risk reduction and management to ensure
23          accountability, responsibility, and transparency, as well as to achieve
24         efficiency and effectiveness in the performance of their functions.
25   Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:
26     a) Contingency Planning - a management process that analyzes specific
27          potential events or emerging situations that might threaten society or the
28         environment and establishes arrangements in advance to enable timely,
29         effective, and appropriate responses to such events and situations;
30     b) Disaster - a serious disruption of the functioning of a community or a
31         society      involving   widespread      human,   material,      economic,   or
 1        environmental losses and impacts, which exceeds the ability of the
 2        affected community or society to cope using its own resources;
 3   c) Disaster and Emergency Management - the organization                     and
 4        management of resources and responsibilities for addressing all aspects
 5        of disasters and emergencies, in particular preparedness, response, and
 6        initial recovery steps;
 7   d) Emergency- any occasion or instance for which, in the determination of
 8        the President, national assistance is needed to supplement local
 9        government efforts and capabilities to save lives and to protect property
10        and public health and safety, or to lessen or avert the threat of a
11        catastrophe in any part of the country;
12   e) Evacuation Centers and Faciiities - permanent and typhoon-resilient
13        structures equipped with facilities constructed exclusively for evacuation
14        and humanitarian relief operations during disasters and emergencies;
15   f)   Geographic Information System - a database which contains, among
16        others, geohazard assessments, information on climate change, and
17        climate risk reduction and management;
18   g) Government - includes the National Government, and any of its
19        subdivisions, agencies, or instrumentalities, including government-owned
20        and controlled corporations and their subsidiaries, and local government
21        units;
22   h) Hazard - a dangerous phenomenon, substance, human activity, or
23        condition that may cause loss of life, injury, or other health impacts.
24        property damage, loss of livelihood and services, social and economic
25        disruption, or environmental damage;
26   i)   Humanitarian - one devoted to the promotion of human welfare an
27        advancement of social reform;
28   j)   Response - any and all projects, programs, plans, or activities aimed at
29        preventing, mitigating, and/or preparing for disasters and other
30        humanitarian emergencies. This includes, but is not limited to, the
31        provision of emergency services and public assistance during or
32        immediately after a disaster; and

 8   result of the reorganization shall, within six (6) months from their separation from
 9   service, receive the retirement benefits to which they may be entitled under existing
10   laws, rules, and regulations.
11         Sec. 25. Appropriations. - The amount needed for the initial implementation of
12   this Act shall be taken from the current year's appropriations of the National Disaster
13   Risk Reduction and Management Council, the Local Disaster Risk Reduction and
14   Management Councils, and the Office of Civil Defense. Thereafter, the amount needed
15   for the operation and maintenance of the Department and its Line Agencies shall be
16   included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

19         Sec. 26. Visitoriai Powers. - The Secretary of the Department and/or his or her
20   authorized representative shall have visitoriai and examining authority over
21   governmental and nongovernmental entities engaged in post-disaster relief in order
22   to ensure that all donations, financial or otherwise, coursed through them, are actually
23   utilized for disaster risk reduction and management efforts.
24         During such examination, the entity concerned shall produce all the reports,
25   records, books of accounts, and other papers that may be required.
26        The refusal by any such entity to allow an examination of its books of accounts
27   and pertinent records, or the concealment of any material information concerning its
28   financial status, shall constitute a legal ground for the cancellation of its registration,
29   and the revocation of all tax-exemption privileges and other benefits granted to it by
30   law, in the case of a nongovernmental entity, or the institution of any proper legal
31   action against the erring official concerned, in the case of a governmental entity.

22   necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act within
23   ninety (90) days after the approval of this Act. The Secretary, in consultation with
24   relevant government agencies and other key stakeholders, shall take the lead in the

26        Sec. 30. Annual Report. - The Department shall submit to the Office of the

 3   provisions of this Act. The Committee shall be composed of six (6) members from the
 4   Senate and six (6) members from the House of Representatives with the Chairpersons

12   or as the need arises, the Congressional Oversight Committee shall conduct a
13   systematic evaluation of the accomplishments and impact of this Act, as well as the
14   performance and organizational structure of the Department, for purposes of
15   determining remedial legislation.
16         Sec. 33. Repealing Clause. - All references to the NDRMMC in existing laws,
17   including, but not limited to, the Philippine National Disaster Risk Reduction and
18   Management Act of 2010, shall be understood to refer to the Department created
19   under this Act.
20         All other laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, and other executive
21   issuances which are inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are
22   hereby amended or repealed accordingly.
23         Sec. 34. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
24   unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in
25   full force and effect.
