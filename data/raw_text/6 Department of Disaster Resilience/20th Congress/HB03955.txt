                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The Philippines is among the most disaster-prone countries in the
world, located within the Pacific Ring of Fire and along the typhoon belt.
Every year, the country experiences a wide range of natural hazards—
including powerful typhoons, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, floods, and
landslides—which have collectively caused thousands of deaths, massive
displacement, and billions in economic losses. These disasters continue to
disrupt essential services, deepen existing social inequalities, and
undermine national progress toward sustainable development.

      While the enactment of Republic Act No. 10121 or the “Philippine
Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010” was a landmark
policy reform, experience over the past decade has revealed significant
gaps in the coordination, implementation, and sustainability of disaster-
related programs. The current structure, where disaster management
responsibilities are fragmented across various national and local
government agencies, has resulted in overlapping functions, delayed
response, and uneven institutional capacities, especially at the local level.

      In light of these enduring challenges and the increasing frequency and
intensity of disasters exacerbated by climate change, there is a compelling
need to institutionalize a more cohesive, empowered, and accountable
disaster governance framework. This bill seeks to create the Department of
Disaster Resilience (DDR)—a dedicated national agency solely focused on
leading and coordinating the government’s efforts in building disaster and
climate resilience.


                                                                                         Page 1
       The proposed DDR will centralize and harmonize policies and actions
related to disaster risk reduction and management, climate change
adaptation, environmental protection, and sustainable development. It will
also provide direct support to local governments and communities before,
during, and after disasters, with a strong emphasis on science-based
planning, risk-informed governance, and inclusive, people-centered
interventions.

      At its core, the creation of the DDR affirms the State’s duty under the
Constitution to protect the life, health, and property of every Filipino, and to
promote a balanced and healthful ecology. It also reinforces the Philippines’
commitments under international frameworks such as the Sendai
Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction, the Paris Agreement on Climate
Change, and the Sustainable Development Goals.

       This measure is not merely about institutional restructuring—it is
about ensuring that no community is left behind in the face of crisis. It is
about transforming the way we prepare, respond, and recover—by acting
early, coordinating effectively, and building back stronger and safer.

     In view of the growing risks and urgent realities we face, the swift
enactment of this measure is not only necessary, it is long overdue.

276   SEC. 3. Definitions of Terms. – As used in this Act:

277   (a)   Adaptation refers to the adjustment in natural or human system in
278         response to actual or expected climatic stimuli or their effects, which
279         moderates harm or exploits beneficial opportunities.

280   (b)   Assisting Actor refers to any Assisting International Actor and any
281         Assisting Domestic Actor responding to a disaster in the country.

282   (c)   Assisting Domestic Actor refers to any not-for-profit entity established
283         under domestic laws, which is responding to a disaster in the country.

284   (d)   Assisting International Actor refers to any foreign state, organization,
285         entity or individual responding to a disaster within transiting through
286         the country to respond to a disaster in another country.

287   (e)   Build Forward Better refers to an approach to building and/or
288         reconstructing an area or community, which entails a shift from simple
289         recovery and restoration to safer, more adaptive, resilient, and
290         inclusive communities.

291   (f)   Capacity refers to the combination of all strengths, attributes and
292         resources available within a community, society or organization that
293         can reduce the level of risk, or impacts of a disaster. Capacity may
294         include infrastructure and physical means, institutions, societal coping
295         abilities, as well as human knowledge, skills and collective attributes
296         such as social relationships, leadership and management. Capacity
297         may also be described as capability.

298   (g)   Civil Society Organizations (CSOs)refer non-state actors whose aims
299         are neither to generate profits nor to seek governing power, but to

                                                                            Page 13
300         unite people to advance shared goals and interests. They have
301         presence in public life, expressing the interests and values of their
302         member or others, based on ethical, cultural, scientific, religious or
303         philanthropic   considerations.    CSOs     include   nongovernment
304         organizations   (NGOs),    professional   associations,   foundations,
305         independent research institutes, community-based organizations
306         (CBOs), faith-based organizations, people’s organizations, social
307         movements, and labor unions.

308   (h)   Climate Change refers to a change in the state of the climate that can
309         be identified by changes in the mean and/or the variability of its
310         properties, and that persist in an extended period, typically decades
311         or longer, whether due to natural variability or as a result of human
312         activity as defined under Republic Act No. 9729, otherwise known as
313         the “Climate Change Act of 2009”. These include internal processes
314         or external forces such as modulation of the solar cycles, volcanic
315         eruptions and persistent anthropogenic changes in the composition of
316         the atmosphere or in land use.

317   (i)   Climate Change Adaption refers to the adjustment in natural or human
318         systems in response to actual or expected climatic stimuli or their
319         effects, which moderates harm or exploits beneficial opportunities.

320   (j)   Climate Finance refers to finance that aims at reducing emissions,
321         and enhancing sinks of greenhouse gases and aims at reducing
322         vulnerability of, and maintaining, and increasing the resilience of,
323         human and ecological systems to negative climate change impacts.

324   (k)   Community-Based Disaster Risk Reduction and Management
325         (CBDRRM) refers to a process of disaster risk reduction and
326         management in which at risk communities are actively engage in the
327         identification, analysis, treatment, monitoring and evaluation of
328         disaster risks in order to reduce their vulnerability and enhance their


                                                                           Page 14
329         capacities, and where the people are at the heart of decision-making
330         and implementation of disaster risk reduction and management
331         activities.

332   (l)   Complex Emergency refers to a form of human-induced emergency
333         in which the cause of the emergency as well as the assistance to the
334         afflicted is complicated by intense level of political considerations.

335   (m)   Contingency Planning refers to a management process that analyzes
336         specific potential events or emerging situations that might threaten
337         society or the environment and establishes arrangements in advance
338         to enable timely, effective and appropriate response to such events
339         and situations.

340   (n)   Dangerous Anthropogenic Interference refers to the effects in the
341         climate system as a result of the increase of greenhouse gas
342         concentrations in the atmosphere that leads to widespread climate
343         change impacts such as widespread demise of coral reefs.

344   (o)   Disability refers to an evolving concept that results from the interaction
345         between persons with impairments, societal barriers, as defined
346         under Republic Act No. 7277, attitudinal and environmental barriers
347         that hinder their full and effective participation in society on an equal
348         basis with others.

349   (p)   Disaster refers to a serious disruption of the functioning of a
350         community or a society involving widespread human, material,
351         economic or environmental losses and impacts, which exceeds the
352         ability of the affected community or society to cope using its own
353         resources.

354   (q)   Disaster Mitigation refers to the lessening or limitation of the adverse
355         impacts of hazards and related disasters. Mitigation measures
356         encompass         engineering   techniques      and     hazard-resistant


                                                                              Page 15
357         construction as well as improved environmental policies and public
358         awareness, land use planning, and climate change.

359   (r)   Disaster Preparedness refers to the knowledge and capacities
360         developed by governments, professional response and recovery
361         organizations, communities and individuals to effectively anticipate,
362         respond to, and recover from, the impacts of likely, imminent or
363         current hazard events or conditions. It includes preparedness for
364         response such as contingency planning, stockpiling of equipment and
365         supplies, the development of arrangements for coordination,
366         evacuation, and public information, and preparedness for recovery
367         such as procurement of land for resettlement sites.

368   (s)   Disaster Prevention refers to the outright avoidance of adverse
369         impacts of hazards. It expresses the concept and intention to
370         completely avoid potential adverse impacts through action taken in
371         advance such as construction of dams or embankments that eliminate
372         flood risks, land-use regulations that do not permit any settlement in
373         high-risk zones, and seismic engineering designs that ensure the
374         survival and function of a critical building in any likely earthquake.

375   (t)   Disaster Response refers to the provision of emergency services and
376         public assistance during or immediately after a disaster in order to
377         secure and save lives, reduce health impacts, ensure public safety
378         and meet the basic subsistence needs of the people affected.
379         Disaster response includes disaster relief focused on immediate and
380         short-term needs of the victims and the vulnerable.

381   (u)   Disaster Risk refers to the potential disaster losses in lives, health
382         status, livelihoods, assets and services that could occur to a particular
383         community or society in the future, and is determined by a
384         combination of the vulnerability, capacity, exposure of persons and
385         assets, hazard characteristics and the environment.


                                                                              Page 16
386   (v)   Disaster Risk Governance refers to the way in which public
387         authorities, civil servants, media, private sector, and civil society
388         coordinate at community, national and regional levels in order to
389         manage disaster and climate related risks. This means ensuring that
390         sufficient levels of capacity and resources are made available to
391         prevent, prepare for, manage, and recover from disasters. It also
392         entails mechanisms, institutions, and processes for citizens to
393         articulate their interests, exercise their legal rights and obligations,
394         and mediate their differences.

395   (w)   Disaster Risk Management refers to the systematic process of using
396         administrative directives, organizations, and operational skills and
397         capacities to implement strategies, policies and improved coping
398         capacities in order to lessen the adverse impacts of hazards and the
399         possibility of disaster.

400   (x)   Disaster Risk Reduction refers to the concept and practice of reducing
401         disaster risks through systematic efforts to analyze and manage the
402         casual factors of disasters, including through reduced exposure
403         hazards, lessened vulnerability of people and property, wise
404         management       of   land   and   the   environment,   and   improved
405         preparedness for adverse events.

406   (y)   Disaster Risk and Vulnerability Reduction (DRVR) refers to the
407         concept and practice of reducing potential loss of life, injury, or
408         destroyed or damage assets or disruption which could occur to a
409         system, society or community in a specific period of time, determined
410         probabilistically and conditions determined by physical, social,
411         economic and environmental factors or processes which increase the
412         susceptibility of an individual, a community, assets or system to the
413         impacts of hazards through systematic efforts to analyze and reduce
414         casual factors of disasters all which contribute to strengthening



                                                                            Page 17
415          resilience and therefore to the achievement of sustainable
416          development.

417   (z)    Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Information System refers
418          to a specialized database contains, among others, information on
419          disasters and their human, material, economic, and environmental
420          impact, risk assessment and mapping, and vulnerable and
421          marginalized groups.

422   (aa)   Early Warning System refers to the set of capacities needed to
423          generate and disseminate timely and meaningful warning information
424          to enable individuals, communities and organizations threatened by a
425          hazard to prepare and to act appropriately and in insufficient time to
426          reduce the possibility of harm or loss. A people-centered early
427          warning system necessarily comprises four (4) key elements:
428          knowledge of the risks; monitoring, analysis and forecasting of the
429          hazards; communication or dissemination of alerts and warnings; and
430          local capabilities to respond to the warnings received. The concept of
431          “end-to-end warning system” emphasizes that warning systems need
432          to span all steps from hazard detection to community response. Early
433          warning systems include systems developed at the level of
434          communities or based on indigenous knowledge.

435   (bb)   Ecosystem Management and Restoration refers to an integrated
436          process to conserve, improved and restore the health of the
437          ecosystem that sustains ecosystem services for human well-being.

438   (cc)   Eligible Assisting Actor refers to any assisting actor that has been
439          determined to be eligible to receive legal facilities as provided in this
440          Act and its implementing rules and regulations.

441   (dd)   Emergency refers to a unforeseen or sudden occurrence, especially
442          danger, demanding immediate action.



                                                                              Page 18
443   (ee)   Emergency Management refers to the organization and management
444          of resources and responsibilities for addressing all aspects of
445          emergencies, in particular preparedness, response and, initial
446          recovery steps.

447   (ff)   Exposure refers to the degree to which the elements at risk are likely
448          to experience hazard events of different magnitudes.

449   (gg)   Gender-Based Violence refers to violence that is directed against a
450          woman because of her gender, or that affects the women
451          disproportionately. It includes acts that inflict physical, mental or
452          sexual harm or suffering, threats of such acts, coercion and other
453          deprivations oh liberty.

454   (hh)   Geographic Information System (GIS) refers a system used to
455          capture, store, manipulate, manage and display all types of spatial or
456          geographical data.

457   (ii)   Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Area (GIDA) refers to
458          areas with a marginalized population which is physically and
459          socioeconomically separated from the mainstream society and
460          characterized by physical factors, and isolated due to distance,
461          weather conditions and transportation difficulties in island, upland,
462          lowland, landlocked, hard to reach and underserved communities,
463          and/or socioeconomic factors such as high poverty incidence,

647               DEPARTMENT OF DISASTER RESILIENCE

648   SEC. 4. Creation of the Department of Disaster Resilience. – There is hereby
649   created a Department of Disaster Resilience, hereinafter referred to as the
650   Department.

651           The Department shall be the primary government agency responsible
652   for leading, organizing, and managing the national effort to reduce disaster
653   risk, prepare for and respond to disasters, recover and rehabilitate, and
654   “Build Forward Better” after the occurrence of disasters.

655           The Department shall oversee and coordinate the preparation,
656   implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of disaster and climate change
657   resilience plans, programs, projects, and activities, provide leadership in the
658   continuous development of strategic and systematic approaches to disaster
659   prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, recovery and rehabilitation,
660   to anticipatory adaptation strategies, measures, techniques and options.

661           The Department shall augment the capacity of LGUs in collaboration
662   with relevant national government agencies and other stakeholders to
663   implement disaster risk reduction and management and climate change
664   action plans, programs, projects, and activities.

831   require the assistance of law enforcement agencies and the uniformed
832   services, such as the PNP and the AFP, as well as emergency responders,
833   to assist in the implementation of emergency measures.

834   SEC. 8. Emergency Measures at the Local Level. –

835   (a)   LGUs shall enact an ordinance on and implement through the Local
836         Disaster Resilience Office (LDRO) the necessary and appropriate
837         emergency measures, including preemptive and forced evacuation, in
838         anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of emergencies, hazards
839         and disasters, and only when there is imminent danger of loss of lives
840         and/or damage to property.

841   (b)   Emergency measures, including preemptive and forced evacuation,
842         shall be carried out in a manner that is humane, respectful of the
843         dignity and culture of persons, without using disproportionate force,
844         without discrimination, and with conscious attention to the needs of
845         vulnerable and marginalized groups.

846   (c)   All measures shall be taken to inform the population, in a manner and
847         language that they can understand, of the need for emergency
848         measures and evacuation for their safety, as well as the location
849         where they will be taken and for how long they are going to stay there.
850         Adequate evacuation centers and transportation to and from the
851         affected area shall be provided by the local government.

852   SEC. 9. Hierarchy of Evacuation Measures. –




                                                                           Page 33
853   (a)   Preemptive evacuation shall be the preferred and primary mode of
854         evacuation of people that will be affected by emergencies, hazards
855         and disasters.

856   (b)   Forced evacuation shall be considered as an emergency measure of
857         last resort that may be undertaken in anticipation of, during or after an
858         emergency, hazard or disaster and be carried out by the affected local
859         government in accordance with a duly issued order by the local chief
860         executive: Provided, That in case of the failure of the local government
861         to appropriate issue such order, the forced evacuation shall be
862         implemented by the Department, which may direct and compel the
863         assistance of law enforcement and other relevant national agencies
864         to implement said emergency measure.

865                       During such forced evacuation, government officials
866         may direct and compel the population to be affected or is currently
867         affected by an emergency, hazard or disaster to relocate to safer
868         locations for their own safety.

870   (a)   Any person who willfully and deliberately disregards or opposes the
871         order of forced evacuation issued by the local chief executive or the
872         Department, releases the local government or the Department from
873         any liability for injury, death, damage to or loss of property attribute to
874         said disobedience.

875   (b)   During evacuation, priority assistance shall be given to the vulnerable
876         groups and persons who willfully comply with the order of forced
877         evacuation.
