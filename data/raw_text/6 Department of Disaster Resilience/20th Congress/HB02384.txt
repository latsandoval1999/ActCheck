                           EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The Philippines lies in a region where disasters happen, naturally. It is in
the Pacific Ring of Fire where eighty percent (80%) of the volcanic eruptions and
earthquakes occur. According to the Philippine Institute of Volcanology and
Seismology (PHILVOLCS), there are twenty-four (24) active volcanoes in the
country, today. The country is also located along the “Typhoon Belt”, an area in
the western Pacific Ocean where one-third of the world’s tropical cyclones
originate. An average of twenty (20) typhoons visits the country every year, seven
(7) of which taking landfall endangering lives and creating heavy damage to
properties and infrastructures. These geographical location and environmental set
up expose the country to climate conditions that increase in frequency and
intensity, every year.

      According to the Global Risk Index 2021 published by Germanwatch,
based on an annual average, from 2000 to 2019, the Philippines ranked fourth
among countries most-affected by extreme weather events. This risk index
measures and analyses impacts of extreme weather events in terms of fatalities
and economic losses. Over the past two decades, our country had the highest
extreme weather occurrence in the world experiencing 317 weather-related
events. According to the same study, the average fatality per year over the same
period was 859.36, ranking us seventh (7th) in the index. In terms of economic
                                                                                 1
losses, extreme weather events cost the Philippines an average of US$3.2 billion
per year which ranked us at eighth (8th) in the world. The Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA) in its 2020 published report put the damages at a total of Php463
billion from 2010 to 2019, with the agricultural sector taking most of the brunt at
sixty-seven (62.7%).

       Our vulnerability to the impacts of climate change and natural hazards is
exacerbated by the wanton destruction of our environment which causes floods,
landslide, forest fires, heat waves, and droughts. It is even worsened by the growth
of our population, increasing our consumption on energy which is dependent on
fossil fuel. Population increase also means greater consumption of agricultural
and marine resources, and an increasing pressure on the natural environment.

       The purpose of this measure is to create a department that will focus on our
country’s effective response to the risks brought about by natural and human-
induced calamities. It will be the mandate of this department to create policies to
reduce disaster-risks, establish an enabling environment for local and national
agencies to collaborate on climate change preparedness and create infrastructures
to mitigate the impact of natural hazards. It will also harmonize all local and
national plans and policies for short and long-term climate goals. Through the
creation of this department, we are putting the climate agenda at the forefront of
our policy decision-making.

       Our action on this matter, is extremely, urgent. The Climate Change
Knowledge Portal even pegged the estimations that sixty percent (60%) of our
land area and seventy-four (74%) percent of our population are exposed to natural
hazards. We must keep in mind that of the 74% exposed population, most of them
come from the marginalized and vulnerable sectors.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

15   ARTICLE II

16   THE DEPARTMENT OF DISASTER RESILIENCE

17   SECTION 4. Creation of the Department of Disaster Resilience. – There is

18   hereby created the Department of Disaster Resilience, herein after referred to

19   as the Department.

20   SECTION 5. Mandate. The Department shall be the principal government


                                                                                19
 1   institution responsible for ensuring safe, adaptive, and disaster-resilient

 2   communities. It shall lead, spearhead, initiate, and integrate the crafting and

 3   implementation of plans, programs, projects and activities to reduce the risk of

 4   natural hazards and effects of climate change, and manage the impact of

 5   disasters with the primordial goal of saving lives and minimizing damage to

 6   property, in close collaboration with all relevant stakeholders at the national

 7   and local levels.

 8         The mandate of the Department shall cover all natural hazards, including

 9   the effects of climate change, such as the following:

10    1. Geological Phenomena and Related Hazards

11         a. Earthquake – ground rupture, ground shaking, liquefaction, tsunami,

12   fire, and landslides;

13         b. Volcanic activity – ash fall, pyroclastic flow, lava flow, lahar,

14   fissuring, volcanic gas, and seiche (lake water oscillation);

15         c. Mass movement – landslides, debris flow, sinkholes;

16    2. Hydrological and Meteorological Phenomena and Related Hazards

17         a. Tropical cyclone – severe winds, storm surge, hail;

18         b. Flood;

19         c. Erosion;

20         3.   Climatological variability

                                                                                  20
 1           a. El Niño;

 2           b. Extreme temperatures;

 3           c. La Niña; and

 4           d. Solar radiation, among others.

 5     The     Department      shall   oversee   and   coordinate   the   preparation,

 6   implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of disaster and climate resilience

 7   plans, programs, projects and activities, provide leadership in the continuous

 8   development of strategic and systematic approaches to disaster prevention,

15   ARTICLE III

16   EMERGENCY MEASURES

17   SECTION 7. Emergency Measures. – (a) The Department may undertake and

18   implement such immediate measures as may be necessary to ensure the

19   protection and preservation of life or property in anticipation of, during, and in

20   the aftermath of emergencies, hazards and disasters, including preemptive and


                                                                                    32
 1   forced evacuations.

 2         Local government units shall enact an ordinance on and implement

 3   necessary and appropriate emergency measures, including preemptive and

 4   forced evacuations, to ensure the protection and preservation of life or property

 5   in anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of disasters.

 6         Emergency measures, including preemptive and forced evacuations,

 7   shall be carried out with the primordial goal of saving lives and minimizing loss

 8   of property and livelihood, while respecting the dignity and culture of persons,

 9   observing equality, and using proportionate force, and with conscious attention

10   to the needs of vulnerable and marginalized groups.

11         Appropriate steps shall be taken to inform the public of the need to

12   implement emergency measures, such as evacuations, for their safety.

13         Preemptive evacuation shall be the preferred and primary mode of

14   moving people that will be affected by disasters.

15         Forced evacuation shall be considered as an emergency measure of last

16   resort that may be undertaken in anticipation of, during, or after a disaster and

17   carried out by the affected local government unit, pursuant to a duly issued

18   order by the local chief executive: Provided, That in case of the failure of the

19   local chief executive to issue such an order, the forced evacuation shall be

20   implemented by the Department, which may direct and compel the assistance

21   of law enforcement and other relevant government agencies to implement such

                                                                                   33
 1   measure.

 2         Any person who willfully and deliberately disregards or disobeys a

 3   preemptive or forced evacuation implemented by the local government unit or

 4   the Department releases such local government unit or the Department from

 5   any liability for injury, death, damage to, or loss of property due to such

 6   disobedience.

 7         (b) Whenever it becomes necessary, the Department may recommend to

 8   the President the calling out of the Armed Forces of the Philippines to prevent

 9   or suppress lawless violence occurring before, during, and in the aftermath of

10   disasters.

11         (c) The Department shall ration the distribution of basic goods in

12   critical shortage, and/or restrict or ban the transfer of such goods outside the

13   area covered by the emergency measure.

14         (d) The Department may temporarily take over or direct the operation of

15   any private utility or business for urgent public purpose in anticipation of,

16   during, and in the aftermath of emergencies, hazards, and disasters and only

17   when there is imminent danger of loss of lives and/or damage to property,

18   subject to payment of just compensation.

19         (e) Local government units shall likewise enact the abovementioned

20   emergency measures including preemptive and forced evacuations, to ensure

21   the protection and preservation of life or property in anticipation of, during, and

                                                                                     34
 1   in the aftermath of disaster.

 2         (f) With the concurrence of the DOF, the Department shall recommend

 3   to the Monetary Board the deferment of payment for monetary debts of LGUs

 4   and/or private entities that have been affected by disasters.

 7   ARTICLE XIV

 8   RECOVERY

 9   SECTION 54. Standards for Recovery. – The Department shall observe

10   internationally accepted standards for recovery, planning, programming and

11   implementation of the recovery process. Towards this end, the Department

12   shall:

13            a) Improve the community’s physical, social and economic resilience,

14   consistent with the principle of “building forward better”;

15            b) Use locally-driven, centrally-supported processes based on legal

16   mandates with supplementary capacity support when requested;

17            c) Redirect development outside danger zones to minimize loss of lives

18   and structures resulting from typhoons, flooding, landslides, and other hazards

19            d) Employ outcome-driven planning and implementation;

20            e) Maximize use of Private-Public sector partnership where possible;

                                                                                 71
 1         f) Consider local conditions such as culture, security situation and

 2   existing capacities of communities in identifying programs and projects;

 3         g) Ensure access to public transport, physical and mental health

 4   services, markets, schools, sustainable livelihoods, and other public services in

 5   planning for settlement areas; and

 6         h) Ensure restoration of peace and order and recovery of government

 7   functions.

 9   ARTICLE XV

10   REMEDIAL MEASURES UNDER STATE OF CALAMITY

11   SECTION 55. Declaration of State of Calamity. – A state of calamity may

12   be declared when any of the following requisites are present:

13         a. A natural hazard, including climate change, poses imminent threat to

14   human life and/or danger to property; or

15         b. A significant impact is caused by a natural hazard or climate change,

16   which demands immediate action.

17         The following shall exercise this power, respectively:

18         a. The President, upon recommendation of the SDR, whether in whole

19   or part of an area;

20         b. The SDR, upon recommendation of the RDRO, for disasters
                                                                                   72
 1   covering two or more provinces of the same region; and

 2         c. The local sanggunian, upon recommendation of the concerned local

 3   disaster resilience office, within its jurisdiction.

 4   SECTION 56. Remedial Measures under State of Calamity. – Upon the

 5   declaration of a state of calamity, the following remedial measures shall be

 6   immediately undertaken by concerned government agencies at the national and

 7   local levels:

 8         a) Imposition of price ceiling on basic necessities and prime

 9   commodities by the Price Coordinating Council (PCC), as provided for under

10   Republic Act no. 7581, otherwise known as the “Price Act”, as amended by

11   Republic Act no. 10623;

12         b) Monitoring, prevention and control by the Local Price Coordination

13   Council of overpricing or profiteering and hoarding of prime commodities,

14   medicines and petroleum products;

15         c) Programming or reprogramming of funds for the repair of critical

16   facilities or public infrastructures that are vital for the quick delivery of

17   humanitarian assistance, to reduce further threat to lives and arrest further

18   deterioration of properties and loss of livelihoods in the affected communities,

19   following the “build forward better” principle;

20         d) Grant of no-interest loans by government financing or lending

21   institutions to the most vulnerable and marginalized groups or individuals;
                                                                                   73
 1         e) Local tax exemptions, incentives or reliefs under such terms and

 2   conditions as may be deemed necessary by the affected local government unit;

 3   and

 4         f) Use of alternative modes of procurement under Article XVI of this

 5   Act, by the Department, LGUs or the relevant government instrumentalities in

 6   relation to the urgent procurement of emergency works, goods or services to

 7   effectively respond to, quickly recover from disasters, and build forward better.

 5   SECTION 91. Cultural Heritage. – To protect, preserve, and promote the

 6   nation’s historical and cultural heritage, the Department shall assist the relevant

 7   cultural and heritage agencies and the appropriate LGUs to give priority

 8   protection and restoration to all national cultural treasures or national historical

 9   landmarks, sites, or monuments in post-disaster recovery or rehabilitation

10   measures.

11   SECTION 92. Indigenous People. – The Department, with the assistance of

12   the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) and the applicable

13   LGUs, shall devise and implement mechanisms that foster social protection for

14   indigenous communities that are vulnerable to the effects of natural disasters.

15         The Department shall ensure respect for, and protection of, the traditional

16   resource right of the Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples

17   (ICCs/IPs) to their ancestral domains, and recognize the customary laws and

18   traditional resource use and management, knowledge, and practices in ancestral

19   domains.

20         In ancestral domains that are disaster-prone, the Department, with the

21   assistance of the NCIP and applicable LGUs, shall create an Ancestral Domain

 5   SECTION 93. Incentives Program. – The Department shall establish an

 6   incentives program that recognizes the outstanding promotion and

 7   implementation by local disaster resilience offices, CSOs, academe, and other

 8   institutions of disaster resilience programs and/or projects, and the meritorious

 9   acts of individuals or entities during natural disasters.

11   Infrastructure. – The Department shall be assisted by the DPWH, DOTr,

16   structures to attain the disaster risk reduction goals of this Act.
