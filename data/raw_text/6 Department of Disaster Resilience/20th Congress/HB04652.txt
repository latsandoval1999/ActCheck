                           EXPLANATORYNOTE


       The Philippines lies in a region where disasters happen, naturally. It is in
the Pacific Ring of Fire where eighty percent (80%) of the volcanic eruptions
and earthquakes occur. According to the Philippine Institute of Volcanology and
Seismology (PHILVOLCS), there are twenty-four (24) active volcanoes in the
country, today. The country is also located along the “Typhoon Belt”, an area in
the western Pacific Ocean where one-third of the world’s tropical cyclones
originate. An average of twenty (20) typhoons visits the country every year,
seven (7) of which taking landfall endangering lives and creating heavy damage
to properties and infrastructures. These geographical location and environmental
set up expose the country to climate conditions that increase in frequency and
intensity, every year.

      According to the Global Risk Index 2021 published by Germanwatch,
based on an annual average, from 2000 to 2019, the Philippines ranked fourth
among countries most-affected by extreme weather events. This risk index
measures and analyses impact of extreme weather events in terms of fatalities
and economic losses. Over the past two decades, our country had the highest
extreme weather occurrence in the world experiencing 317 weather-related
events. According to the same study, the average fatality per year over the same
period was 859.36, ranking us seventh (7th) in the index. In terms of economic
                                                                                  1
losses, extreme weather events cost the Philippines an average of US$3.2 billion
per year which ranked us at eighth (8th) in the world. The Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA) in its 2020 published report put the damages at a total of
Php463 billion from 2010 to 2019, with the agricultural sector taking most of the
brunt at sixty-seven (62.7%).

       Our vulnerability to the impacts of climate change and natural hazards is
exacerbated by the wanton destruction of our environment which causes floods,
landslide, forest fires, heat waves, and droughts. It is even worsened by the
growth of our population, increasing our consumption on energy which is
dependent on fossil fuel. Population increase also means greater consumption of
agricultural and marine resources, and an increasing pressure on the natural
environment.

       The purpose of this measure is to create a department that will focus on
our country’s effective response to the risks brought about by natural and
human-induced calamities. It will be the mandate of this department to create
policies to reduce disaster-risks, establish an enabling environment for local and
national agencies to collaborate on climate change preparedness and create
infrastructures to mitigate the impact of natural hazards. It will also harmonize
all local and national plans and policies for short and long-term climate goals.
Through the creation of this department, we are putting the climate agenda at the
forefront of our policy decision-making.

      Our action on this matter is extremely, urgent. The Climate Change
Knowledge Portal even pegged the estimations that sixty percent (60%) of our
land area and seventy-four (74%) percent of our population are exposed to
natural hazards. We must keep in mind that of the 74% exposed population;
most of them come from the marginalized and vulnerable sectors.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

 1   SECTION 4. Creation of the Department of Disaster Resilience. – There

 2   is hereby created the Department of Disaster Resilience, herein after referred

 3   to as the Department.

 4   SECTION 5. Mandate. The Department shall be the principal government

 5   institution responsible for ensuring safe, adaptive, and disaster-resilient

 6   communities. It shall lead, spearhead, initiate, and integrate the crafting and

 7   implementation of plans, programs, projects and activities to reduce the risk

 8   of natural hazards and effects of climate change, and manage the impact of

 9   disasters with the primordial goal of saving lives and minimizing damage to

10   property, in close collaboration with all relevant stakeholders at the national

11   and local levels.

12         The mandate of the Department shall cover all natural hazards,

13   including the effects of climate change, such as the following:

14    1. Geological Phenomena and Related Hazards

15         a. Earthquake – ground rupture, ground shaking, liquefaction,

16   tsunami, fire, and landslides;

17         b. Volcanic activity – ash fall, pyroclastic flow, lava flow, lahar,

18   fissuring, volcanic gas, and seiche (lake water oscillation);

19         c. Mass movement – landslides, debris flow, sinkholes;

20    2. Hydrological and Meteorological Phenomena and Related Hazards


                                                                                 20
 1           a. Tropical cyclone – severe winds, storm surge, hail;

 2           b. Flood;

 3           c. Erosion;

 4           3.    Climatological variability

 5           a. El Niño;

 6           b. Extreme temperatures;

 7           c. La Niña; and

 8           d. Solar radiation, among others.

 9     The        Department   shall    oversee    and   coordinate    the   preparation,

 2   ARTICLE III

 3   EMERGENCY MEASURES

 4   SECTION 7. Emergency Measures. – (a) The Department may undertake

 5   and implement such immediate measures as may be necessary to ensure the

 6   protection and preservation of life or property in anticipation of, during, and

 7   in the aftermath of emergencies, hazards and disasters, including preemptive

 8   and forced evacuations.

 9         Local government units shall enact an ordinance on and implement

10   necessary and appropriate emergency measures, including preemptive and

11   forced evacuations, to ensure the protection and preservation of life or

12   property in anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of disasters.

13         Emergency measures, including preemptive and forced evacuations,

14   shall be carried out with the primordial goal of saving lives and minimizing

15   loss of property and livelihood, while respecting the dignity and culture of

16   persons, observing equality, and using proportionate force, and with

17   conscious attention to the needs of vulnerable and marginalized groups.

18         Appropriate steps shall be taken to inform the public of the need to

19   implement emergency measures, such as evacuations, for their safety.

20         Preemptive evacuation shall be the preferred and primary mode of


                                                                                 33
 1   moving people that will be affected by disasters.

 2         Forced evacuation shall be considered as an emergency measure of last

 3   resort that may be undertaken in anticipation of, during, or after a disaster and

 4   carried out by the affected local government unit, pursuant to a duly issued

 5   order by the local chief executive: Provided, That in case of the failure of the

 6   local chief executive to issue such an order, the forced evacuation shall be

 7   implemented by the Department, which may direct and compel the assistance

 8   of law enforcement and other relevant government agencies to implement

 9   such measure.

10         Any person who willfully and deliberately disregards or disobeys a

11   preemptive or forced evacuation implemented by the local government unit or

12   the Department releases such local government unit or the Department from

13   any liability for injury, death, damage to, or loss of property due to such

14   disobedience.

15         (b) Whenever it becomes necessary, the Department may recommend

16   to the President the calling out of the Armed Forces of the Philippines to

17   prevent or suppress lawless violence occurring before, during, and in the

18   aftermath of disasters.

19         (c) The Department shall ration the distribution of basic goods in

20   critical shortage, and/or restrict or ban the transfer of such goods outside the

21   area covered by the emergency measure.

                                                                                   34
 1         (d) The Department may temporarily take over or direct the operation

 2   of any private utility or business for urgent public purpose in anticipation of,

 3   during, and in the aftermath of emergencies, hazards, and disasters and only

 4   when there is imminent danger of loss of lives and/or damage to property,

 5   subject to payment of just compensation.

 6         (e) Local government units shall likewise enact the abovementioned

 7   emergency measures including preemptive and forced evacuations, to ensure

 8   the protection and preservation of life or property in anticipation of, during,

 9   and in the aftermath of disaster.

10         (f) With the concurrence of the DOF, the Department shall recommend

11   to the Monetary Board the deferment of payment for monetary debts of LGUs

12   and/or private entities that have been affected by disasters.

 3   ARTICLE XIV

 4   RECOVERY

 5   SECTION 54. Standards for Recovery. – The Department shall observe

 6   internationally accepted standards for recovery, planning, programming and

 7   implementation of the recovery process. Towards this end, the Department

 8   shall:

 9            a) Improve the community’s physical, social and economic resilience,

10   consistent with the principle of “building forward better”;

11            b) Use locally-driven, centrally-supported processes based on legal

12   mandates with supplementary capacity support when requested;

13            c) Redirect development outside danger zones to minimize loss of

14   lives and structures resulting from typhoons, flooding, landslides, and other

15   hazards

16            d) Employ outcome-driven planning and implementation;

17            e) Maximize use of Private-Public sector partnership where possible;

18            f) Consider local conditions such as culture, security situation and

19   existing capacities of communities in identifying programs and projects;

20            g) Ensure access to public transport, physical and mental health
                                                                                 72
 1   services, markets, schools, sustainable livelihoods, and other public services

 2   in planning for settlement areas; and

 3         h) Ensure restoration of peace and order and recovery of government

 4   functions.

 6   ARTICLE XV

 7   REMEDIAL MEASURES UNDER STATE OF CALAMITY

 8   SECTION 55. Declaration of State of Calamity. – A state of calamity may

 9   be declared when any of the following requisites are present:

10         a. A natural hazard, including climate change, poses imminent threat

11   to human life and/or danger to property; or

12         b. A significant impact is caused by a natural hazard or climate

13   change, which demands immediate action.

14         The following shall exercise this power, respectively:

15         a. The President, upon recommendation of the SDR, whether in whole

16   or part of an area;

17         b. The SDR, upon recommendation of the RDRO, for disasters

18   covering two or more provinces of the same region; and

19         c. The local sanggunian, upon recommendation of the concerned local

20   disaster resilience office, within its jurisdiction.
