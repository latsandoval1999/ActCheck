      CREATING THE DEPARTMENT OF DISASTER RESILIENCE, DEFINING ITS

                                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Every year, Filipino communities face the devastating impact of typhoons, floods,
earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and other natural hazards. These calamities not only threaten lives
but also deprive families of their homes, livelihoods, and sources of income. Our Filipino workers,
farmers, fisherfolks, and low-income communities are often the first and hardest hit whenever
disasters strike. In 2025, the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services
Administration (PAGASA) recorded a total of 23 tropical cyclones that affected more than 20
million Filipinos nationwide.1

        The challenges facing the country are expected to become even more complex in the years
ahead. In a report released by the PAGASA on 9 June 2026, the agency warned of the possible
persistence of El Niño conditions into early 2027, increasing the risk of drought, extreme heat, and
disruptions to agriculture and water supply. These emerging threats, together with the country’s
continuing exposure to other natural hazards, highlight the need for a more proactive and holistic
approach to disaster resilience.

        Modern disasters can no longer be viewed solely as emergencies requiring immediate
response. They require comprehensive year-round planning, risk reduction, rehabilitation, and
coordinated recovery efforts across all levels of government. This demands sustained coordination
among national agencies, local government units (LGUs), and affected communities to minimize
risks and strengthen preparedness.

        This measure seeks to establish a Department of Disaster Resilience as the primary
government agency in protecting communities before, during, and after disasters. It aims to
strengthen coordination among national and local governments, improve disaster preparedness and
emergency response, and ensure that efforts to save lives, protect livelihoods, and rebuild
communities are carried out in a more efficient and coordinated manner.

           In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

 4                Sec. 2. Declaration of Policies and Principles - The State recognizes that:
 5
 6        a. The Philippines is one of the most vulnerable countries to climate change and various kinds
 7           of natural hazards, including, among others, typhoons, earthquakes, floods, volcanic
 8           eruptions, landslides, storm surges, and drought;
 9        b. Tremendous physical and economic, losses are sustained by the country, and in certain
10           cases, damage to our national, cultural, and historical heritage, each time such natural
11           hazards wreak havoc on an unsuspecting and unprepared populace;
12        c. There is an urgent need to establish a focused, streamlined, independent, empowered,
13           capacitated, full-time, specialized agency on disaster risk reduction and management as
14           well as emergency response, which is national in scope and civilian in character; and
15        d. The people are the most important assets of the nation, and all disaster risk reduction and
16           management efforts shall be responsive to the humanitarian needs of the people, the dignity
17           and value of the human person, and respect for property.
18
19            Towards this end, the state shall endeavor to ensure:
20
21        1. That a strategic, systematic, continuous, comprehensive, inclusive, and integrated approach
22           to disaster risk reduction and management is established, with the end in view of
23           substantially reducing vulnerabilities and the risk of disasters and other humanitarian
24           emergencies, towards the preservation of life and property, thereby ultimately preventing
25           or deterring the loss of lives and the social, economic, cultural, and environmental assets
26           of the country;
27        2. That a permanent, institutionalized, cohesive and comprehensive framework for disaster
28           preparedness, prevention and mitigation, and response, is adopted, to be implemented by a
29           highly specialized and focused agency bearing its own mandate, powers, and funding, in
30           coordination and with the participation of the Philippine government, other foreign
31           governments and financial institutions, international organizations, the private sector, and
32           civil society;
33        3. That the differing concerns and needs of various sectors or those with higher vulnerabilities
34           such as women, children, elderly, persons with disabilities, and indigenous peoples, are
35           specifically served, with respect to disaster resilience and disaster management;
36        4. The implementation of the principles, concepts and action plans of climate change and
37           disaster risk reduction contained in international and national frameworks and
38           commitments in various phases of policy formulation, development and investment plans,
39           poverty reduction strategies and other development tools, and techniques by all national
40           and local agencies, institutions and instrumentalities of the government to enhance the
41           country's capacity for climate change adaptation and mitigation; and
42        5. To inculcate a culture of resilience and preparedness for natural disasters at the national,
43           regional and local levels, and adopt a whole-of-society approach in disaster resilience to

 4              Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, these terms are defined as

16         provide assistance in another affected State;
17      e. Biological Hazards shall refer to hazards that are of organic origin or conveyed by
18         biological vectors, including pathogenic microorganisms, toxins and bioactive substances.
19         Examples are bacteria, viruses or parasites, as well as venomous wildlife and insects,
20         poisonous plants and mosquitoes carrying disease-causing agents;
21      f. Build Forward Better shall refer to an approach to building and/or reconstructing an area
22         or community which entails a shift from simple recovery and restoration to safer, more
23         adaptive, resilient, and inclusive communities;
24      g. Business Continuity shall refer to the capacity of a business entity to continue the delivery
25         of its products and/or services at acceptable pre-defined levels following a disruptive
26         incident;
27      h. Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) shall refer to non-state actors whose aims are neither

32         associations, foundations, independent research institutes, community-based organizations
33         (CBOs), faith-based organizations, people's organization, social movements, and labor
34         unions;

36         changes in the mean and/or the variability of its properties, and that persist in an extended

38         human activity as defined under Republic Act No. 9729, otherwise known as the "Climate
39         Change Act of 2009". These include internal processes or external forces such as
40         modulation of the solar cycles, volcanic eruptions and persistent anthropogenic changes in
41         the composition of the atmosphere or in land use;
42      j. Climate Mitigation shall refer to efforts to reduce or limit greenhouse gas emissions or
43         enhance greenhouse gas sequestration;
44      k. Cultural Heritage shall refer to the totality of cultural property preserved and developed

52         to support the economic, social, and environmental well-being of areas and/or people
53         affected by a natural disaster;
54      o. Disability shall refer to an evolving concept that results from the interaction between
55         persons with impairments, societal barriers, as defined under Republic Act No. 7277, and
56         attitudinal and environmental barriers that hinder their full and effective participation in

58      p. Disaster shall refer to a serious disruption of the functioning of a community or a society
59         involving widespread human, material, economic or environmental losses and impacts,
60         which exceeds the ability of the affected community or society to cope using its own
61         resources. Unless otherwise used specifically in this Act, the term "disaster" shall refer to
62         "natural disaster";

 1                         THE DEPARTMENT OF DISASTER RESILIENCE:
 2                            MANDATE, POWERS, AND FUNCTIONS
 3
 4              Sec. 4. Creation of the Department of Disaster Resilience. - There is hereby created
 5   the Department of Disaster Resilience, hereinafter referred to as the Department.
 6
 7               The Department shall be the principal government institution responsible for ensuring
 8   safe, adaptive, and disaster-resilient communities. It shall lead, spearhead, initiate, and integrate
 9   the crafting and implementation of comprehensive, continuous, and strategic plans, programs,
10   projects and activities to reduce the risk of all types of natural hazards and effects of climate
11   change, and manage the impact of disasters with the primordial goal of saving lives and minimizing
12   damage to property, in close collaboration with all relevant stakeholders at the national and local
13   levels.
14
15                The Department shall oversee and coordinate the preparation, implementation,
16   monitoring, and evaluation of disaster and climate resilience plans, programs, projects and
17   activities, provide leadership in the continuous development of strategic and systematic
18   approaches to disaster prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, recovery and rehabilitation
19   and anticipatory adaptation strategies, measures, techniques, and options. It shall also manage and
20   direct the implementation of national, local, and community-based disaster resilience programs in
21   collaboration with relevant national government agencies, LGUs, CSOs, and other stakeholders,
22   as applicable.
23
24              Sec. 5. Powers and Functions of the Department. - The Department shall exercise
25   and perform the following powers and functions: Overall functions:
26
27      1. Craft the policies and lead the implementation of the programs, projects and activities
28          necessary to reduce the country's vulnerabilities to natural hazards and climate change,
29          using the National Disaster Resilience Framework (NDRF) and NDRPIP as anchors;
30      2. Review and build upon, to the extent necessary, the existing disaster resilience framework,
31          plans and strategies in the crafting of an NDRF and the corresponding NDRPIP;
32      3. Oversee, review, and approve the translation, integration, and implementation of the
33          NDRPIP into LDRPs;
34      4. Communicate and disseminate critical information to help the public prepare for, respond
35          to, and recover from a disaster;
36      5. Receive, manage, and administer the Disaster Resilience Fund, the Disaster Contingency
37          Fund, donations and other funds, assets and/or properties received by the Department to
38          accomplish the purposes of this Act;
39      6. Recommend to the President of the Philippines the declaration of a state of calamity due to
40          a natural disaster, and the lifting thereof when conditions stabilize;
41      7. Coordinate with, request the assistance of, and recommend appropriate actions to national
42          government agencies, government-owned or controlled corporations, local government
43          units, and other government instrumentalities to ensure the effective implementation of this
44          Act and the attainment of its goals and objectives;

31               Sec. 6. Emergency Measures. - The Department shall undertake and implement such
32   immediate measures as may be necessary subject to existing laws, due process requirements, and
33   payment of just compensation to ensure the protection and preservation of life or property in
34   anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of emergencies, hazards and disasters, including
35   preemptive and forced evacuations.
36
37          a. Local government units shall enact an ordinance on and implement necessary and
38             appropriate emergency measures, including preemptive and forced evacuations, to
39             ensure the protection and preservation of life or property in anticipation of, during, and
40             in the aftermath of disasters. It shall be mandatory for Local Government Units to
41             provide evacuation centers, with due consideration to the capacity of local facilities,
42             when requested by the Department, in anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of
43             emergencies, hazards and disasters. In providing such evacuation centers, due regard
44             shall be given to the most vulnerable and marginalized groups, respecting the dignity
45             and culture of persons, observing equality, and using proportionate force in
46             implementing such emergency measures. Preemptive evacuation shall be the primary
47             and preferred mode of moving people who may be affected by disasters.
48          b. Forced evacuation shall be considered as an emergency measure of last resort, and shall
49             be carried out by the affected local government unit, pursuant to a duly issued order by
50             the local chief executive: Provided, That in case of the failure of the local chief
51             executive to issue such an order, the forced evacuation shall be implemented by the
52             Department, which may direct and compel the assistance of law enforcement and other
53             relevant government agencies to implement such measure.
54          c. Any person who willfully and deliberately disregards or disobeys a preemptive or
55             forced evacuation implemented by the local government unit or the Department, as the
56             case may be, releases such local government unit or the Department from liability
57             arising solely from the person's refusal or failure to comply with such evacuation order.
58          d. Whenever necessary, the Department may recommend to the President the calling out
59             of the Armed Forces of the Philippines to prevent or suppress lawless violence
60             occurring before, during, and in the aftermath of disasters.

 5             or business for urgent public purpose in anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of

 4               Sec. 66. Prohibited Acts. - Any public official, private person, group or corporation
 5   who commits any of the following prohibited acts shall be held liable and be subjected to the
 6   criminal and administrative penalties as provided for in Section 71 of this Act, without prejudice
 7   to the imposition of other criminal, civil and administrative liabilities under existing laws.
 8
 9      1. Prohibited Acts of Public Officials. - The following acts, if committed by public officials
10         without justifiable cause, shall be considered as gross neglect of duty:
11                  i. Dereliction of duties that leads to destruction, loss of lives, critical damage of
12                     facilities and misuse of funds;
13                 ii. Failure to enforce laws, standards, or regulations such as the National Building
14                     Code, Solid Waste Management Act, Water Code, and other relevant laws,
15                     leading to destruction, loss of lives, and/or critical damage of facilities;
16               iii. Failure to create a functional LDR Office within six (6) months from the
17                     approval of this Act;
18                iv. Failure to appoint a permanent local disaster resilience officer within six (6)
19                     months from approval of this Act;
20                 v. Failure to prepare and implement a Contingency and Adaptation Plan for
21                     hazards frequently occurring within their jurisdictions;
22                vi. Failure to formulate and implement the LDRP with the corresponding budget
23                     allocation; and
24               vii. Sexual exploitation and abuse and other forms of sexual misconduct on the
25                     affected population or at-risk communities.
26      2. Prohibited Acts of Public Officials and Private Persons or Institutions. –
27                  i. Delay, without justifiable cause in the delivery, proper handling or storage of
28                     aid commodities, resulting to damage or spoilage;
29                 ii. Withholding the distribution of relief goods due to (i) political or partisan
30                     considerations; (ii) discrimination based on race, ethnicity, religion, or gender;
31                     and (iii) other similar circumstances;
32               iii. Preventing the entry and distribution of relief goods in disaster-stricken areas.
33                     including appropriate technology, tools, equipment, accessories, disaster teams
34                     or experts;
35                iv. Buying, for consumption or resale, from disaster relief agencies any relief
36                     goods, equipment or other and commodities, which are intended for distribution
37                     to disaster-affected communities;
38                 v. Buying, for consumption or resale, from the recipient disaster affected persons
39                     any relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities received by them;
40                vi. Selling of relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities, which are intended
41                     for distribution to disaster victims;
42               vii. Forcibly seizing relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities intended for
43                     or consigned to a specific group of victims or relief agency;
44              viii. Diverting or misdelivery of relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities
45                     to persons other than the rightful recipient or consignee;
46                ix. Accepting, possessing, using or disposing relief goods, equipment or other aid
47                     commodities not intended for nor consigned to him or her;
48                 x. Misrepresenting the source of relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities
49                     by:
50                              1. Either covering, replacing or defacing the labels of the containers to
51                                  make it appear that the goods, equipment or other aid commodities
52                                  came from another agency or persons;
53                              2. Repacking the goods, equipment or other aid commodities into
54                                  containers with different markings to make it appear that the goods
55                                  came from another agency or persons or was released upon the
56                                  instance of a particular agency. person, or persons; and
57                              3. Making false verbal claim that the goods, equipment or other and
58                                  commodity in its untampered original containers actually came from
59                                  another agency or persons or was released upon the instance of a
60                                  particular agency, person, or persons;
61                xi. Substituting or replacing relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities with
62                     the same items or inferior/cheaper quality;

                                                                                                      24
 1                 xii. Illegal solicitations by persons or organizations representing others as defined
 2                      in the standards and guidelines set by the Department;
 3                xiii. Deliberate use of false at inflated data in support of the request for funding,
 4                      relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities for emergency assistance or
 5                      livelihood projects;
 6                 xiv. Stealing, taking, or processing of any of the equipment, accessories and other
 7                      vital facility or items or any part thereof;
 8                  xv. Selling or buying stolen equipment, accessories, and other vital facility items
 9                      or any part thereof;

15                      said prohibited acts.

17                Sec. 67. Penalties. - Any individual, corporation, partnership, association or other
18   juridical entity that commits any of the prohibited acts in the preceding section shall be made liable
19   for the following:
20
21      a. The penalty of imprisonment of not less than twelve (12) years but not more than fifteen
22         (15) years or a fine of not less than One Million Pesos (Php 1,000,000.00) but not more
23         than Three Million Pesos (Php3,000,000.00), or both imprisonment and fine at the
24         discretion of the court shall be imposed upon any person found guilty of violating
25         subsections (a)(1) to (b)(16) of the preceding section, as well as the confiscation or
26         forfeiture in favor of the government of the objects and the instrumentalities used in the
27         commission of the prohibited acts;
28      b. The penalty of imprisonment of not less than six (6) years but not more than eight (8) years
29         or a fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 500,000.00) but not more
30         than One Million Pesos (Php 1,000,000.00), or both imprisonment and fine at the discretion
31         of the court, shall be imposed upon any person who attempts to commit any of the
32         prohibited acts in the preceding Section, in violation of Subsection (b)(17) thereof;
33      c. The penalty of imprisonment of not less than two (2) years but not more than six (6) years
34         or a fine of not less than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 200,000.00) but not more
35         than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 500,000.00), or both imprisonment and fine at
36         the discretion of the court, shall be imposed upon any person found guilty of unlawfully
37         benefiting from the proceeds or fruits of any of the prohibited in the preceding section, in
38         violation of subsection (b)(18) thereof;
39      d. Aside from the penalty of fine or imprisonment or both, perpetual disqualification from
40         public office shall be imposed if the offender is a public officer, along with the confiscation
41         or forfeiture in favor of the government of the objects and the instrumentalities used in the
42         commission of any of the prohibited acts in the preceding section;
43      e. If the offender is a corporation, partnership or association, or other juridical entity, the
44         penalty shall be imposed upon the officer or officers of the corporation. partnership,
45         association or entity responsible for the violation without prejudice to the cancellation or
46         revocation of these entities' registration, license or accreditation issued to them by any
47         licensing or accredited body of the government. If such offender is an alien, he or she shall,
48         in addition to the penalties prescribed in this act, be deported without further proceedings
49         after service of sentence;
50      f. Payment of the full cost of repair or replacement of the government risk reduction and
51         preparedness equipment, accessories and other vital facility Items, or any part thereof;
52         which is the object of the crime shall likewise be imposed upon any person who shall
53         commit any of the prohibited acts in the preceding Section; and
54      g. The prosecution for offenses set forth under the preceding section shall be without
55         prejudice to any criminal liability for violation of Republic Act No. 3815, as amended,
56         otherwise known as the "Revised Penal Code", and other existing laws, as well as the
57         imposition of applicable administrative or civil liabilities.

14
15               Sec. 70. Appropriations and Management of Disaster Resilience Fund. - The budget
16   of the Department shall be composed of the following, which shall be collectively called the
17   Disaster Resilience Fund:
18
19      a. Annual General Appropriations - shall provide the funding requirements for, among others,
20         research, operations, maintenance, plans, and programs of the Department of Disaster
21         Resilience, including, but not limited to, establishment of evacuation centers, retrofitting
22         of structures, establishment of emergency operating centers, and implementation of other
23         disaster risk reduction projects.
24      b. Disaster Contingency Fund - shall be in a lump-sum form to provide funds in case of
25         imminent hazards or actual disasters for, among others, immediate response, relief and
26         quick recovery measures for disaster affected areas.
27      c. Rehabilitation and Recovery Fund - shall provide the funding requirements for
28         rehabilitation and/or reconstruction projects of disaster affected areas such as public
29         classrooms, government buildings, public hospitals, permanent shelters and livelihood
30         programs.
31
32               The Disaster Resilience Fund shall be managed by the Department to finance its
33   operations, and disaster risk reduction, disaster preparedness, response, recovery, rehabilitation
34   and building forward better programs, projects and activities formulated at the national level.
35
36               Sec. 71. Continuing Appropriations. - The unutilized balances from the Disaster
37   Contingency Fund, and Rehabilitation and Recovery Fund shall be treated as Continuing
38   Appropriations, which may be used to finance the cost of projects, programs, and/or activities even
39   when such costs are incurred beyond the fiscal year of the GAA from which such funds were
40   appropriated.
41
42               Sec. 72. Fund Regulations. - The DBM and COA shall establish rules and regulations
43   that apply specifically to the Disaster Resilience Fund to ensure that funds required for disaster
44   response, recovery, and rehabilitation are available and/or released efficiently and expeditiously
45   through innovative budgeting and auditing mechanisms, which may include, among others,
46   providing for longer validity periods for funds and/or exemption from cash-based budgeting rules;
47   imposing auditing or reporting requirements, which apply specifically to the utilization of the
48   Disaster Resilience Fund.
49
50                Sec. 73. Local Disaster Resilience Fund and Local Disaster Contingency Fund. -
51   LGUs shall annually set aside not less than five percent (5%) of their local budget, to constitute a
52   Local Disaster Resilience Fund (LDRF). The LGU shall use its LDRF to maintain and operate its
53   local disaster resilience office to maintain its officers, employees and staff, and to implement plans,
54   programs and activities under its LDRP. Thirty percent (30%) of the LDRF shall be set aside as
55   Local Disaster Contingency Fund (LDCF), which shall be used for disaster response and quick
56   recovery measures. Nothing contained in this Act shall prevent LGUs from providing additional
57   funding or allocating additional resources for disaster resilience activities, plans and programs
58   within their jurisdiction.
59
60              Sec. 74. Supplemental LDRF. - The Department may allocate and disburse funds to
61   supplement an LGU's LDRF based on the parameters and requirements indicated in the IRR. In
62   determining such parameters and requirements, the IRR shall aim to provide supplemental funds

                                                                                                         26
 1   to LGUs with low income and/or high exposure to natural hazards. Supplemental LDRFs shall, as
 2   much as possible, fill gaps or shortages in LGU resources to ensure that LGUs can implement
 3   disaster resilience measures such as, among others, implementing their LDRPs and Pre-Disaster
 4   Recovery Plan (PDRP)s, establishing their local disaster resilience offices, obtaining and/or
 5   maintaining the necessary equipment and staff in their local disaster resilience offices, and
 6   implementing the NDRF and at the local levels.
 7
 8              Sec. 75. People's Survival Fund. - The Department shall manage and administer the
 9   People's Survival Fund, created under Republic Act no. 10174, and facilitate its utilization by
10   LGUs, through RDROs, to implement climate change adaptation and mitigation projects of LGUs
11   and enhance the climate resilience of vulnerable communities.
12
13                Sec. 76. Multi-Donor Trust Fund. - The Department shall create and manage,
14   together with the Bureau of Treasury, a Multi-Donor Trust Fund for the processing, releasing and
15   accounting of money and other similar resources intended for disaster assistance. The Department
16   shall establish a system to ensure transparency in the management and use of the Multi-Donor
17   Trust Fund.
18
19               Sec. 77. Financial Administration. - The Department shall manage all funds
20   appropriated to it by Congress and received from other sources. The Department shall also manage
21   all donations received by It, subject to the auditing powers of the COA. To fund its operations, the
22   Department shall have the power to collect fees derived from the DRRTI and other related
23   activities.

10                Sec. 97. Interpretation. - Any doubt in the interpretation of any provision of this Act
11   shall be resolved in favor of a liberal interpretation that will fulfill the objectives of this Act,
12   especially in relation to the provision of effective, efficient and timely disaster response,
13   rehabilitation and recovery.
14
15                Sec. 98. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
16   or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected shall continue to be in full force and effect.
17
18               Sec. 99. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations
19   inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
20
21                Sec. 100. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
22   in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
