                                  AN ACT
         CREATING THE DEPARTMENT OF DISASTER RESILIENCE, DEFINING ITS
          POWERS AND FUNCTIONS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

 1               Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as "Department of Disaster Resilience
 2    Act of 2025."
 3
 4                Sec. 2. Declaration of Policies and Principles - The State recognizes that:
 5
 6        a. The Philippines is one of the most vulnerable countries to climate change and various kinds
 7            natural hazards, including, among others, typhoons, earthquakes, floods, volcanic
 8            eruptions, landslides, storm surges, and drought;
 9        b. Tremendous physical and economic, losses are sustained by the country, and in certain
10            cases, damage to our national, cultural, and historical heritage, each time such natural
11            hazards wreak havoc on an unsuspecting and unprepared populace;
12        c. There is an urgent need to establish a focused, streamlined, independent, empowered,
13            capacitated, full-time, specialized agency on disaster risk reduction and management as
14            well as emergency response, which is national in scope and civilian in character;
15        d. The people are the most important assets of the nation, and all disaster risk reduction and
16            management efforts shall be responsive to the humanitarian needs of the people, the dignity
17            and value of the human person, and respect for property.
18
19            Towards this end, the state shall endeavor to ensure:
20
21        1. That a strategic, systematic, continuous, comprehensive, inclusive, and integrated approach
22            to disaster risk reduction and management is established, with the end in view of
23            substantially reducing vulnerabilities and the risk of disasters and other humanitarian
24            emergencies, towards the preservation of life and property, thereby ultimately preventing
25            or deterring the loss of lives and the social, economic, cultural, and environmental assets
26            of the country;
27        2. That a permanent, institutionalized, cohesive and comprehensive framework for disaster
28            preparedness, prevention and mitigation, and response, is adopted, to be implemented by a
29            highly specialized and focused agency bearing its own mandate, powers, and funding, in
30            coordination and with the participation of the Philippine government, other foreign
31            governments and financial institutions, international organizations, the private sector, and
32            civil society;
33        3. That the differing concerns and needs of various sectors or those with higher vulnerabilities
34            such as women, children, elderly, persons with disabilities, and indigenous peoples, are
35            specifically served, with respect to disaster resilience and disaster management;
36        4. The implementation of the principles, concepts and action plans of climate change and
37            disaster risk reduction contained in international and national frameworks and
38            commitments in various phases of policy formulation, development and investment plans,
 1          poverty reduction strategies and other development tools, and techniques by all national
 2          and local agencies, institutions and instrumentalities of the government to enhance the
 3          country's capacity for climate change adaptation and mitigation;
 4      5. To inculcate a culture of resilience and preparedness for natural disasters at the national,
 5          regional and local levels, and adopt a whole-of-society approach in disaster resilience to
 6          enhance collaboration, planning, and dialogue among all sectors of society, and in
 7          improving their strategies and/or action plans for disaster risk reduction.

 9              Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, these terms are defined as
10   follows:
11
12      a. Adaptation shall refer to the adjustment in natural or human systems in response to actual
13          or expected climactic stimuli or their effects, which moderates harm or exploits beneficial
14          opportunities.
15      b. Assisting Actor shall refer to any Assisting International Actor and any Assisting Domestic
16          Actor responding to a disaster in the country.
17      c. Assisting Domestic Actor shall refer to any not-for-profit entity established under domestic
18          laws, which is responding to a disaster in the country.
19      d. Assisting International Actor shall refer to any foreign state, organization, entity or
20          individual responding to a disaster within or transiting through the country to respond to a
21          disaster in another country
22      e. Biological Hazards shall refer to hazards that are of organic origin or conveyed by biological
23          vectors, including pathogenic microorganisms, toxins and bioactive substances. Examples
24          are bacteria, viruses or parasites, as well as venomous wildlife and insects, poisonous plants
25          and mosquitoes carrying disease-causing agents;
26      f. Build Forward Better shall refer to an approach to building and/or reconstructing an area or
27          community which entails a shift from simple recovery and restoration to safer, more
28          adaptive, resilient, and inclusive communities.
29      g. Business Continuity shall refer to the capacity of a business entity to continue the delivery
30          of its products and/or services at acceptable pre-defined levels following a disruptive
31          incident;
32      h. Capacity shall refer to the combination of all strengths, attributes and resources available
33          within a community, society or organization that can reduce the level of risk, or impacts of
34          a disaster. Capacity may include infrastructure and physical means, institutions, societal
35          coping abilities, as well as human knowledge, skills and collective attributes such as social
36          relationships, leadership and management. Capacity may also be described as capability.
37      i. Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) shall refer to non-state actors whose aims are neither to
38          generate profits nor to seek governing power, but to unite people to advance shared goals
39          and interests. They have a presence in public life, expressing the interests and values of
40          their members or other, based on ethical, cultural, scientific, religious or philanthropic
41          considerations. CGOs include nongovernment organizations (NGOs), professional
42          associations, foundations, independent research institutes, community-based organizations
43          (CBOs), faith-based organizations, people's organization, social movements, and labor
44          unions.
45      j. Climate Change shall refer to a change in the state of the climate that can be identified by
46          changes in the mean and/or the variability of its properties, and that persist in an extended
47          period, typically decades or longer, whether due to natural variability or as a rest of human
48          activity as defined under Republic Act No. 9729, otherwise known as the "Climate Change
49          Act of 2009". These include internal processes or external forces such as modulation of the
50          solar cycles, volcanic eruptions and persistent anthropogenic changes in the composition
51          of the atmosphere or in land use.
52      k. Climate Mitigation shall refer to efforts to reduce or limit greenhouse gas emissions or
53          enhance greenhouse gas sequestration.
54      l. Cultural Heritage shall refer to the totality of cultural property preserved and developed
55          through time and passed on to posterity;.
56      m. Contingency Planning shall refer to a management process that analyzes specific potential
57          events or emerging situations that might threaten society or the environment and

 3   n. Department shall refer to the Department of Disaster Resilience or the DDR;
 4   o. Development Assistance shall refer to any financial, material or other forms of assistance to
 5       support the economic, social, and environmental well-being of areas and/or people affected
 6       by a natural disaster;
 7   p. Disability shall refer to an evolving concept that results from the interaction between
 8       persons with impairments, societal barriers, as defined under Republic Act No. 7277, and
 9       attitudinal and environmental barriers that hinder their full and effective participation in
10       society on an equal basis with others.
11   q. Disaster shall refer to a serious disruption of the functioning of a community or a society
12       involving widespread human, material, economic or environmental losses and impacts,
13       which exceeds the ability of the affected community or society to cope using its own
14       resources. Unless otherwise used specifically in this Act, the term "disaster shall refer to
15       "natural disaster";
16   r. Disaster Contingency Fund shall refer to a fund, in lump-sum form, managed by the
17       Department to expediently provide funds and resources for disaster relief or response in
18       affected areas;
19   s. Disaster Mitigation or Mitigation shall refer to the lessening or limitation of the adverse
20       impacts of hazards and related disasters. Mitigation measures include hazard-resistant
21       structures as well as improved environmental, land use planning, climate change and other
22       sectoral policies and public awareness;
23   t. Disaster Preparedness shall refer to the knowledge and capacities developed by
24       governments, professional, professional response and recovery organizations, communities
25       and individuals to effectively anticipate, respond to, and recover from, the impacts of
26       likely, imminent or current hazard events or conditions. It includes preparedness for
27       response such as contingency planning, stockpiling of equipment and supplies, the
28       development of arrangements for coordination, evacuation, and public information, and
29       preparedness for recovery such as procurement of land for resettlement sites.
30   u. Disaster Prevention shall refer to the intention to avoid, or the outright avoidance, of
31       potential adverse impacts of disasters and related hazards through action(s) taken in
32       advance. It expresses the concept and intention to completely avoid potential adverse
33       impacts through action taken in advance such as construction of dams or embankments that
34       eliminate flood risks, land-use regulations that do not permit any settlement in high-risk
35       zones, and seismic engineering designs that ensure the survival and function of a critical
36       building in any likely earthquake;
37   v. Disaster Resilience shall refer to the ability of a system, community, or society exposed to
38       hazards to resist, absorb, accommodate, adapt to, transform, and recover from the effects
39       of a hazard and/or the long-term impact of climate change in a timely and efficient manner,
40       including through the preservation and restoration of its essential basic structures and
41       functions through risk management;
42   w. Disaster Response shall refer to the provision of emergency services and public assistance
43       during or immediately after a disaster in order to secure and save lives, reduce health
44       impacts, ensure public safety and meet the basic subsistence needs of the people affected.
45       Disaster response includes disaster relief focused on immediate and short-term needs of
46       the victims and the vulnerable.
47   x. Disaster Resilience Fund shall refer to the budget managed by the Department, which
48       includes funds appropriated to it by Congress pursuant to an Appropriations Act, the
49       Disaster Contingency Fund, and the Rehabilitation and Recovery Fund;
50   y. Disaster Risk shall refer to the potential disaster losses in lives, health status, livelihoods,
51       assets and services that could occur to a particular community or society in the future, and
52       is determined by a combination of the vulnerability, capacity, exposure of persons and
53       assets, hazard characteristics and the environment.
54   z. Disaster Risk Governance shall refer to the way in which public authorities, civil servants,
55       media, private sector, and civil society coordinate at community, national and regional
56       levels in order to manage disaster and climate related risks. This means ensuring that
57       sufficient levels of capacity and resources are made available to prevent, prepare for,

27        be termed drivers of hazard and risk rather than hazards themselves, such as soil
28        degradation, deforestation, loss of biodiversity, salinization, and sea-level rise;
29   hh. Exposure shall refer to the degree to which the elements at risk are likely to experience
30        hazard events of different magnitudes;
31   ii. Geographic Information System (GIS) shall refer to a system used to capture, store,
32        manipulate, manage and display all types of spatial or geographical data;
33   jj. Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Area (GIDA) shall refer to areas with a
34        marginalized population, which is physically and socio-economically separated from the

36        conditions and transportation difficulties (island, upland lowland, landlocked, hard to reach

38        presence of vulnerable sector, communities in or recovering from situation of crisis or

40   kk. Geological or geophysical hazards shall refer to the hazards that originate from internal
41        earth processes, such as earthquakes, volcanic activities and emissions, and related
42        geophysical processes, which include mass movements, landslides, rockslides, surface

44        to some of these processes. While tsunamis are triggered by undersea earthquakes and

47   ll. Hazard shall refer to a phenomenon, substance, human activity or condition that may cause

37              Sec. 4. Creation of the Department of Disaster Resilience. - There is hereby created
38   the Department of Disaster Resilience, hereinafter referred to as the Department.
39
40                The Department shall be the principal government institution responsible for ensuring
41   safe, adaptive, and disaster-resilient communities. It shall lead, spearhead, initiate, and integrate
42   the crafting and implementation of comprehensive, continuous, and strategic plans, programs,
43   projects and activities to reduce the risk of all types of natural hazards and effects of climate
44   change, and manage the impact of disasters with the primordial goal of saving lives and minimizing
45   damage to property, in close collaboration with all relevant stakeholders at the national and local
46   levels.
47                The Department shall oversee and coordinate the preparation. implementation,
48   monitoring, and evaluation of disaster and climate resilience pians, programs, projects and
49   activities, provide Leadership in the continuous development of strategic and systematic
50   approaches to disaster prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, recovery and rehabilitation
51   and anticipatory adaptation strategies, measures, techniques, and options. It shall also manage and
52   direct the implementation of national, local, and community-based disaster resilience programs in
53   collaboration with relevant national government agencies, LOUs, CSOs, and other stakeholders,
54   as applicable.
55
56              Sec. 5. Powers and Functions of the Department. - The Department shall exercise
57   and perform the following powers and functions: Overall functions:

28              Sec. 6. Emergency Measures. - The Department shall undertake and implement such
29   immediate measures as may be necessary to ensure the protection and preservation of life or
30   property in anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of emergencies, hazards and disasters,
31   including preemptive and forced evacuations.
32
33          a. Local government units shall enact an ordinance on and implement necessary and
34             appropriate emergency measures, including preemptive and forced evacuations, to
35             ensure the protection and preservation of life or property in anticipation of, during, and
36             in the aftermath of disasters. It shall be mandatory for Local Government Units to
37             provide evacuation centers, with due consideration to the capacity of local facilities,
38             when requested by the Department, in anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of
39             emergencies, hazards and disasters. In providing such evacuation centers, due regard
40             shall be given to the most vulnerable and marginalized groups, respecting the dignity
41             and culture of persons, observing equality, and using proportionate force in
42             implementing such emergency measures. Preemptive evacuation shall be the preferred
43             as the primary mode of moving people that will be affected by disasters.
44          b. Forced evacuation shall be considered as an emergency measure of last resort, and shall
45             be carried out by the affected local government unit, pursuant to a duly issued order by
46             the local chief executive: Provided, That in case of the failure of the local chief
47             executive to issue such an order, the forced evacuation shall be implemented by the
48             Department, which may direct and compel the assistance of law enforcement and other
49             relevant government agencies to implement such measure.
50          c. Any person who willfully and deliberately disregards or disobeys a preemptive or
51             forced evacuation implemented by the local government unit or the Department, as the
52             case may be, releases such local government unit or the Department from any liability
53             for injury, death, damage to, or loss of property due to such disobedience.
54          d. Whenever necessary, the Department may recommend to the President the calling out
55             of the Armed Forces of the Philippines to prevent or suppress lawless violence
56             occurring before, during, and in the aftermath of disasters.


                                                                                                      10
 1          e. The Department may ration the distribution of basic goods in critical shortage, and/or
 2             restrict or ban the transfer of such goods outside the area covered by the emergency
 3             measure.
 4          f. The Department may temporarily take over or direct the operation of any private utility
 5             or business for urgent public purpose in anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of
 6             emergencies, hazards, and disasters and only when there is imminent danger of loss of
 7             lives and/or damage to property, subject to the payment of just compensation.
 8          g. Local government units shall likewise enact the abovementioned emergency measures
 9             including preemptive and forced evacuations, to ensure the protection and preservation
10             of life or property in anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of disaster.

11                Sec. 60. Pre-Arranged Contracts or Agreements with Private Sector Entities. -

14   contracts or agreements with private sector entities for the purpose of, among others, establishing,
15   a logistics system for the efficient and prompt distribution of goods, equipment or other materials
16   required for disaster response and management; securing food, medicines, fuel, or other supplies
17   from groceries, pharmacies, gas stations or other sources in the event of a disaster in a specific
18   area; ensuring an alternative source of power or water from private utilities in the event of a disaster
19   in a specific area; or other arranging the immediate supply of other goods, services or equipment
20   necessary for disaster response and management. The conditions, limitations and parameters of
21   contracts or arrangements authorized under this Section shall be provided in this Act's IRR.

23               Sec. 67. Prohibited Acts. - Any public official, private person, group or corporation
24   who commits any of the following prohibited acts shall be held liable and be subjected to the
25   criminal and administrative penalties as provided for in Section 71 of this Act, without prejudice
26   to the imposition of other criminal, civil and administrative liabilities under existing laws.
27
28      1. Prohibited Acts of Public Officials. - The following acts, if committed by public officials
29         without justifiable cause, shall be considered as gross neglect of duty:
30                      i. Dereliction of duties that leads to destruction, loss of lives, critical damage
31                         of facilities and misuse of funds;
32                     ii. Failure to enforce laws, standards, or regulations such as the National
33                         Building Code, Solid Waste Management Act, Water Code, and other
34                         relevant laws, leading to destruction, loss of lives, and/or critical damage of
35                         facilities;
36                   iii. Failure to create a functional LDR Office within six (6) months from the
37                         approval of this Act;
38                    iv. Failure to appoint a permanent local disaster resilience officer within six (6)
39                         months from approval of this Act;
40                     v. Failure to prepare and implement a Contingency and Adaptation Plan for
41                         hazards frequently occurring within their jurisdictions;
42                    vi. Failure to formulate and implement the LDRP with the corresponding
43                         budget allocation; and
44                   vii. Sexual exploitation and abuse and other forms of sexual misconduct on the
45                         affected population or at-risk communities.
46      2. Prohibited Acts of Public Officials and Private Persons or Institutions. –
47                      i. Delay, without justifiable cause in the delivery, proper handling or storage
48                         of aid commodities, resulting to damage or spoilage;
49                     ii. Withholding the distribution of relief goods due to (i) political or partisan
50                         considerations; (il) discrimination based on race, ethnicity, religion, or
51                         gender; and (i) other similar circumstances;
52                   iii. Preventing the entry and distribution of relief goods in disaster-stricken
53                         areas. including appropriate technology, tools, equipment, accessories,
54                         disaster teams or experts;
55                    iv. Buying, for consumption or resale, from disaster relief agencies any relief
56                         goods, equipment or other and commodities, which are intended for
57                         distribution to disaster-affected communities;

 5                          intended for distribution to disaster victims;

 7                          for or consigned to a specific group of victims or relief agency;
 8                    viii. Diverting or misdelivery of relief goods, equipment or other aid

43                Sec. 68. Penalties. - Any individual, corporation, partnership, association or other
44   juridical entity that commits any of the prohibited acts in the preceding section shall be made liable
45   for the following:
46
47      a. The penalty of imprisonment of not less than twelve (12) years but not more than fifteen
48          (15) years or a fine of not less than One Million Pesos (Php 1,000,000.00) but not more
49          than Three Million Pesos (Php3,000,000.00), or both imprisonment and fine at the
50          discretion of the court shall be imposed upon any person found guilty of violating
51          subsections (a)(1) to (b)(16) of the preceding section, as well as the confiscation or
52          forfeiture in favor of the government of the objects and the instrumentalities used in the
53          commission of the prohibited acts;
54      b. The penalty of imprisonment of not less than six (6) years but not more than eight (8) years
55          or a fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 500,000.00) but not more
56          than One Million Pesos (Php 1,000,000.00), or both imprisonment and fine at the discretion


                                                                                                        27
 1           of the court, shall be imposed upon any person who attempts to commit any of the
 2           prohibited acts in the preceding Section, in violation of Subsection (b)(17) thereof;
 3      c. The penalty of imprisonment of not less than two (2) years but not more than six (6) years
 4           or a fine of not less than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 200,000.00) but not more
 5           than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 500,000.00), or both imprisonment and fine at
 6           the discretion of the court, shall be imposed upon any person found guilty of unlawfully
 7           benefiting from the proceeds or fruits of any of the prohibited in the preceding section, in
 8           violation of subsection (b)(18) thereof;
 9      d. Aside from the penalty of fine or imprisonment or both, perpetual disqualification from
10           public office shall be imposed if the offender is a public officer, along with the confiscation
11           or forfeiture in favor of the government of the objects and the instrumentalities used in the

45
46               Sec. 71. Appropriations and Management of Disaster Resilience Fund. – The budget
47   of the Department shall be composed of the following, which shall be collectively called the
48   Disaster Resilience Fund:
49
50      a. Annual General Appropriations - shall provide the funding requirements for, among others,
51         research, operations, maintenance, plans, and programs of the Department of Disaster
52         Resilience, including, but not limited to, establishment of evacuation centers, retrofitting
53         of structures, establishment of emergency operating centers, and implementation of other
54         disaster risk reduction projects.
55      b. Disaster Contingency Fund - shall be in a lump-sum form to provide funds in case of
56         imminent hazards or actual disasters for, among others, immediate response, relief and
57         quick recovery measures for disaster affected areas.

                                                                                                         28
 1      c. Rehabilitation and Recovery Fund - shall provide the funding requirements for
 2         rehabilitation and/or reconstruction projects of disaster affected areas such as public
 3         classrooms, government buildings, public hospitals, permanent shelters and livelihood
 4         programs.
 5
 6               The Disaster Resilience Fund shall be managed by the Department to finance its
 7   operations, and disaster risk reduction, disaster preparedness, response, recovery, rehabilitation
 8   and building forward better programs, projects and activities formulated at the national level.
 9
10               Sec. 72. Continuing Appropriations. - The unutilized balances from the Disaster
11   Contingency Fund, and Rehabilitation and Recovery Fund shall be treated as Continuing
12   Appropriations, which may be used to finance the cost of projects, programs, and/or activities even
13   when such costs are incurred beyond the fiscal year of the GAA from which such funds were
14   appropriated.
15
16               Sec. 73. Fund Regulations. - The DBM and COA shall establish rules and regulations
17   that apply specifically to the Disaster Resilience Fund to ensure that funds required for disaster
18   response, recovery, and rehabilitation are available and/or released efficiently and expeditiously
19   through innovative budgeting and auditing mechanisms, which may include, among others,
20   providing for longer validity periods for funds and/or exemption from cash-based budgeting rules;
21   imposing auditing or reporting requirements, which apply specifically to the utilization of the
22   Disaster Resilience Fund.
23
24                Sec. 74. Local Disaster Resilience Fund and Local Disaster Contingency Fund. -
25   LGUs shall annually set aside not less than ten percent (10%) of their local budget, to constitute a
26   Local Disaster Resilience Fund (LDRF). The LCD shall use its LDRF to maintain and operate its
27   local disaster resilience office to maintain its officers, employees and staff, and to implement plans,
28   programs and activities under its LDRP. Thirty percent (30%) of the LDRF shall be set aside as
29   Local Disaster Contingency Fund (LDCF), which shall be used for disaster response and quick
30   recovery measures. Nothing contained in this Act shall prevent LGUs from providing additional
31   funding or allocating additional resources for disaster resilience activities, plans and programs
32   within their jurisdiction.
33
34                Sec. 75. Supplemental LDRF. —The Department may allocate and disburse funds to
35   supplement an LGU's LDRF based on the parameters and requirements indicated in the IRR. In
36   determining such parameters and requirements, the IRR shall aim to provide supplemental funds
37   to LGUs with low income and/or high exposure to natural hazards. Supplemental LDRFs shall, as
38   much as possible, fill gaps or shortages in LGU resources to ensure that LGUs can implement
39   disaster resilience measures such as, among others, implementing their LDRP/Provincial LDRP,
40   establishing their local disaster resilience offices, obtaining and/or maintaining the necessary
41   equipment and staff in their local disaster resilience offices, and implementing the NDRF and at
42   the local levels.
43
44              Sec. 76. People's Survival Fund. - The Department shall manage and administer the
45   People's Survival Fund, created under Republic Act no. 10174, and facilitate its utilization by
46   LGUs, through RDROs, to implement climate change adaptation and mitigation projects of LGUs
47   and enhance the climate resilience of vulnerable communities.
48
49                Sec. 77. Multi-Donor Trust Fund. - The Department shall create and manage,
50   together with the Bureau of Treasury, a Multi-Donor Trust Fund for the processing, releasing and
51   accounting of money and other similar resources intended for disaster assistance. The Department
52   shall establish a system to ensure transparency in the management and use of the Multi-Donor
53   Trust Fund.
54
55               Sec. 78. Financial Administration. - The Department shall manage all finds
56   appropriated to it by Congress and received from other sources. The Department shall also manage
57   all donations received by It, subject to the auditing powers of the COA. To fund its operations, the
