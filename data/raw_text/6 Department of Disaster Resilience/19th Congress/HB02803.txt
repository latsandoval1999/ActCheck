                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The lives of our ancestors and the passage of time taught us how to strive.
They recognized that disasters are a fact of life, and that it is upon them to adapt
if they want to survive. They prevented, prepared for and avoided disasters,
despite the absence of today's technology. Like them, we established the National
Disaster Resiliency and Reduction Management Council. Yet, in the years of
implementing the law disasters still ravaged our countrymen, lives and
opportunities are forever lost. Our disaster interventions have been under
scrutiny for its inefficiency. Hence, let us be swift to review and learn from our
shortcomings. Today, let’s expediently craft and pass a new policy that will save
and secure lives.

        The bill aims to establish a Department, that will mobilize and intervene in
times of disaster. A Department that will use technology to foresee and prepare
the country for different kinds of disaster. Further, this bill aims to provide a
holistic and comprehensive framework for disaster management for all the
stakeholders. A framework, anchored on time efficient and holistic intervention,
from the National Government, Local Government Units, the private sector and
the local communities.

In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is urgently and earnestly sought.

2    SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

 3   a. Adaptation – refers to the adjustment in natural or human systems in
 4      response to actual or expected climactic stimuli or their effects, which
 5      moderates harm or exploits beneficial opportunities.
 6   b. Assisting Actor – refers to any Assisting International Actor and any
 7      Assisting Domestic Actor responding to a disaster in the country.
 8   c. Assisting Domestic Actor – refers to any not – for – profit entity
 9      established under domestic laws, which is responding to a disaster in
10      the country.
11   d. Assisting International Actor – refers to any foreign state, organization,
12      entity or individual responding to a disaster within or transiting
13      through the country to respond to a disaster in another country.
14   e. Build Forward Better – refers to an approach to building and/or
15      reconstructing an area or community, which entails a shift from simple
16      recovery and restoration to a safer, more adaptive, resilient, and
17      inclusive communities.
18   f. Capacity – refers to the combination of all strengths, attributes and
19      resources available within a community, society or organization that
20      can reduce the level of risk, or impacts of a disaster. Capacity may
21      include infrastructure and physical means, institutions, societal coping
22      abilities, as well as human knowledge, skills and collective attribute
23      such as social relationships, leadership and management. Capacity
24      may also be described as capability.
25   g. Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) - refer to non – state actors whose
26      aims are neither to generate profits nor seek governing power, but to
27      unite people to advance shared goals and interests. They have a
28      presence in public life, expressing the interests and values of their
29      members or others, based on ethical, cultural, scientific, religious or
30      philanthropic considerations. CSOs include non-government
31      organizations (NGOs), professional associations, foundations,
32      independent research institutes, community based organizations
33      (CBOs), faith – based organizations, people’s organizations, social
34      movements, and labor unions.

36      identified by changes in the mean and/or the variability of its
37      properties, and that persists in an extended period, typically decades
38      or longer, whether due to natural variability or as a result of human
39      activity as defined under RA 9729, otherwise known as the “Climate
40      Change Act of 2009”. These include internal processes or external
41      forces such modulation of the solar cycles, volcanic eruptions and

 3   r. Disaster Preparedness – refers to knowledge an capacities developed by
 4      governments, professional response and recovery organizations,
 5      communities and individuals to effectively anticipate, respond to,
 6      recover from, the impacts of likely, imminent or current hazard events

 8      contingency planning, stockpiling of equipment and supplies, the

29      assets, hazard characteristics and the environment.

31      authorities, civil servants, media, private sector, and civil society

36      mechanisms, institutions, and processes for citizens to articulate their

 7        immediately after a disaster to meet the life preservation and basic
 8        subsistence needs of affected people and in the restoration of essential
 9        public activities and facilities.
10   qqq. Risk Assessment - a methodology to determine the nature and extent
11        of risk by analyzing potential hazards and evaluating existing
12        conditions of vulnerability that together could potentially harm
13        exposed people, property, services, livelihood and the environment on
14        which they depend.
15   rrr. Probabilistic Risk Assessment - simulates those future disasters which,
16        based on scientific evidence, are likely to occur (UNISDR, 2015). As a
17        result, these risk assessments resolve the problem posed by the limits
18        of historical data (UNISDR 2015). Probabilistic models "complete"
19        historical records by reproducing the physics of the phenomena and
20        recreating the intensity of a large number of synthetic events (UNISDR,
21        2015). Probabilistic Risk Assessment considers all possible scenarios,
22        their likelihood, and associated impacts. It is characterized by inherent
23        uncertainties partly related to the natural randomness of hazards, and
24        partly because of our incomplete understanding and measurement of
25        the hazards, exposure, and vulnerability.
26   sss. Deterministic Risk Assessment - refers to an assessment that considers
27        the possible disaster impacts of a single scenario. This is in contrast to
28        probabilistic risk assessment which considers all possible scenarios,
29        their likelihood, and associated impacts.
30   ttt. Risk Transfer - refers to the process of formally or informally shifting
31        the financial consequences of particular risks from one party to another
32        whereby a household, community, enterprise, or state authority will
33        obtain resources from the other party after a disaster occurs, in
34        exchange for ongoing or compensatory social or financial benefits
35        provided to that other party.
36   uuu. State of Calamity - a condition involving mass casualty and/or major
37        damages to property, disruption of means of livelihoods, roads and
38        normal way of life of people in the affected areas as a result of the
39        occurrence of natural or human- induced hazard.
40   vvv. Sustainable Development - the development that meets the needs of
41        the present without compromising the ability of future generations to
42        meet their own needs.

 1   www. Underlying Disaster Risk Drivers/ Underlying Risk Factors - Processes
 2      or conditions, often development-related, that influence the level of
 3      disaster risk by increasing levels of exposure and vulnerability or
 4      reducing capacity.
 5   xxx. Vital Facility Items - refers to government risk reduction and
 6      preparedness equipment, accessories and other items such as radars,
 7      weather forecasting equipment, flood monitoring instruments,
 8      seismographs, tsunami warning systems, and automated weather
 9      systems.
10   yyy. Vulnerability - refers to the characteristics and circumstances of a
11      community, system or asset that make it susceptible to the damaging
12      effects of a hazard. Vulnerability may arise from various physical,
13      social, economic, and environmental factors.
14   zzz. Vulnerable and Marginalized Groups - those who are at risk due to
15      poverty and other underlying risk factors including, but not limited to,
16      women, children, elderly, differently-abled people, and ethnic
17      minorities.
18   aaaa. Whole-of-society Approach - the meaningful participation of and
19      synergy of stakeholders that represent different interests in all aspects
20      of climate change adaptation, disaster risk and vulnerability reduction
21      and management across all levels. “Acknowledge the contribution of
22      and important role played by all relevant stakeholders, including
23      individuals,     families    and      communities,    intergovernmental
24      organizations and religious institutions, civil society, academia, the
25      media, voluntary associations and, where and as appropriate, the
26      private sector and industry.”
27   bbbb. Whole of Government Approach - an approach that integrates the
28      collaborative efforts of the departments and agencies to achieve unity
29      of effort toward a shared goal to broadens the government’s options,
30      increases efficiency to maximize all available resources in a
31      collaborative effort.”
32   cccc. Whole of Nation Approach - refers to an approach that seeks to bring
33      about a concerted effort towards national peace and security by
34      creating consensus and understanding of security that is shared not
35      just among core security forces and oversight government institutions,
36      but also by civil society and all the nation’s communities.
37   dddd. Youth - refers to those persons aged fifteen (15) to thirty (30) years
38      old, as provided for under Republic Act No. 8044, otherwise known as
39      “Youth in Nation-Building Act”.

11   and/or damage to property.

12         SECTION 7. Emergency Measures at the National Level. —The
13   Department may require the assistance of law enforcement agencies and the
14   uniformed services, such as the PNP and the AFP, as well as emergency
15   responders, to assist in the implementation of emergency measures.

16         SECTION 8. Emergency Measures at the Local Level.

17      1. Local government units shall enact an ordinance on and implement
18         through the LDRO the necessary and appropriate emergency measures,
19         including preemptive and forced evacuation, in anticipation of, during,
20         and in the aftermath of emergencies, hazards and disasters, and only when
21         there is imminent danger of loss of lives and/or damage to property.
22      2. Emergency measures, including preemptive and forced evacuation, shall
23         be carried out in a manner that is humane, respectful of the dignity and
24         culture of persons, without using disproportionate force, without
25         discrimination, and with conscious attention to the needs of vulnerable
26         and marginalized groups.
27      3. All measures shall be taken to inform the population, in a manner and
28         language that they can understand, of the need for emergency measures
29         and evacuation for their safety, as well as the location where they will be
30         taken and for how long they are going to stay there. Adequate evacuation
31         centers and transportation to and from the affected area shall be provided
32         by the local government.

33         SECTION 9. Hierarchy of Evacuation Measures.

34      a. Preemptive evacuation shall be the preferred and primary mode of
35         evacuation of people that will be affected by emergencies, hazards and
36         disasters.

                                                                                      21
 1      b. Forced evacuation shall be considered as an emergency measure of last
 2         resort that may be undertaken in anticipation of, during or after an
 3         emergency, hazard or disaster and be carried out by the affected local
 4         government in accordance with a duly issued order by the local chief
 5         executive: Provided, That in case of the failure of the local government to
 6         appropriately issue such order, the forced evacuation shall be
 7         implemented by the Department, which may direct and compel the
 8         assistance of law enforcement and other relevant national agencies to
 9         implement said emergency measure.

26         levels; and

28         risk and vulnerability reduction and management before, during, and
29         after a disaster. The Climate and disaster resilience plan shall be in

32      through various platforms such as websites, mobile apps, and social
33      media to empower local communities and individuals.

13                                    RECOVERY
14

15          SECTION 62. Standards for Recovery. – The Department shall observe
16   internationally accepted standards for recovery, planning, programming and
17   implementation of the recovery process shall:

18      1. Improve the community’s physical, social and economic resilience,
19         consistent with the principle of “build forward better”;
20      2. Use locally-driven and centrally-supported processes based on legal
21         mandates with supplementary capacity support when requested;
22      3. Redirect development outside danger zones to minimize loss of lives and
23         structures resulting from typhoons, flooding, landslides, and other
24         hazards
25      4. Employ outcome-driven planning and implementation;
26      5. Maximize use of private-public sector partnership where possible;
27      6. Consider local conditions such as culture, security situation, and existing
28         capacities of communities in identifying programs and projects;
29      7. Ensure access to public transport, physical and mental health services,
30         markets, schools, sustainable livelihoods, and other public services in
31         planning for settlement areas; and
32      8. Ensure restoration of peace and order and recovery of government
33         functions.

16   a Congressional Oversight Committee on Climate Change and Disaster
17   Resilience to monitor and oversee the implementation of the provisions of this
18   Act. The Committee shall be composed of six (6) members from the Senate and
19   six (6) members from the House of Representatives with the Chairpersons of the
20   Committees on National Defense and Security of both the Senate and the House
21   of Representatives as joint Chairpersons of this Committee. The five (5) other
22   members from each Chamber are to be designated by the Senate President and
23   the Speaker of the House of Representatives, respectively. The minority shall be
24   entitled to pro rata representation but shall have at least two (2) representatives
25   from each Chamber.

26         SECTION 74. Review. Five (5) years after the effectivity of this Act, or as
27   the need arises, the Congressional Oversight Committee shall conduct a
28   systematic evaluation by the Congressional Oversight Committee of the
29   accomplishments and impact of this Act, as well as the performance and
30   organizational structure of its implementing agencies, for purposes of
31   determining remedial legislation.
