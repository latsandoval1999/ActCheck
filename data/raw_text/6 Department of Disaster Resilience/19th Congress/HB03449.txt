                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines is regularly hit by typhoons all year round. All throughout these
years, we are witnesses to the extent of devastation these typhoons and other natural
calamities can cause to our nation. Just recently, typhoon Yolanda left hundreds of our
fellow Filipinos homeless and thousands of them dead. It took a considerable number of
months before the whole affected community regained an ounce of strength to start
recovering. During a state of calamity, every Filipino family becomes vulnerable to
countless problems entailed from a strong typhoon and/or other natural disaster. Each
person needs to start from scratch to get back a little of what they used to have.

       In a state of calamity, it is therefore understandable for those affected people to
prioritize obligations and responsibilities one by one. The need to eat and find shelter
after a typhoon or an earthquake wiped out everything a person or a family has, is of
course always the main concern. Then the other responsibilities follow after that.

       During these moments, it is hard enough for the head of the family, or a person
living alone, to recover from their loss, hence, it is the obligation of the state to help each
other and every one affected by such calamities and to lessen their burden as much as
possible.

      One of the ways this can be done is to allow the affected people and/or families a
grace period on the payment of utility bills until such time that the situation has stabilized
in areas hit by calamities.

       Through this measure, the victims of typhoons and other fortuitous events will be
able to focus on securing their basic and primary needs first before anything else. They
will not have to worry about the due bills which they have to pay and so they can make
use of their money, or what’s remaining thereof – wisely.
