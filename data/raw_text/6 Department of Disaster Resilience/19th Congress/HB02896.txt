                                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

The distinct geography of the Philippines exposes it to natural hazards. Located along
the so-called "Pacific Ring of Fire,” the Philippines is regularly impacted by floods,
typhoons, landslides, earthquakes, volcano eruptions, and droughts. According to the
status report of the Asian Disaster Preparedness Center and UN Office for Disaster
Risk Reduction, at least 60% of the country's total land area is vulnerable to natural
hazards.1 An average of 20 major storms hit the Philippines a year. Additionally, it is
home to 25 active volcanoes.

In 2020, Super Typhoon Goni which is considered the strongest landfalling tropical
cyclone on record slammed into the Philippines affecting approximately 2,030,130
people and damaging infrastructure worth Php 12,867,014,693.78 and agriculture
worth Php 5,008,430,180.00.2 Goni's peak winds were as high as 195 mph,
comparable to Super Typhoon Haiyan in 2013. Meanwhile, the Taal volcano, one of
the Philippines' most active volcanoes erupted in July 2021 displacing almost 20,000
persons from 190 Barangays in CALABARZON.3

These, among many other circumstances, exposed the weakness in the structural and
institutional set-up of Republic Act No. 10121 or the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction
Act and Management of 2010.

To further strengthen our country’s capacity to address impacts brought by climate
change, this bill proposes to establish the Department of Disaster Resilience and
Emergency Management which would oversee the implementation of disaster risk
reduction and management policies and programs nationwide. Under the bill, the DDR
shall be the primary agency responsible for leading, organizing, and managing the
national effort to reduce disaster risk, prepare for and respond to disasters, recover
and rehabilitate, and build forward better after the occurrence of disasters. It shall
oversee and coordinate the preparation, implementation, and monitoring and
evaluation of disaster and climate resilience plans, programs, projects and activities,
provide leadership in the continuous development of strategic and systematic
approaches to disaster prevention, preparedness, response, recovery and
rehabilitation, to anticipatory adaptation strategies, measures, techniques, and
options.

It shall integrate the Climate Change Commission (CCC), National Disaster Risk
Reduction, People’s Survival Fund (PSF), Yolanda Recovery and Rehabilitation
Efforts, and Office of the Presidential Assistant for Rehabilitation and Recovery
(OPARR). This shall streamline the planning and execution of policies and projects on
climate change and unify the country’s approach towards resiliency. It shall also do
away with redundant bureaucratic processes that may result in delays, confusion and
diluted accountability.

The DRR shall coordinate with other pertinent agencies, local government units
(LGUs), and even private entities and establish a Multi-stakeholder Convergence Unit
which shall align disaster resilience efforts of the private sector, civil society
organizations, academe and other stakeholders by assisting, coordinating or providing
services that strengthen public-private cooperate and coordination in disaster
resilience.

True economic progress is unattainable when we continuously fail to deal with reality
of climate change and hence, suffer from its effects. With the creation of the FRR, the
Philippine will be able to quickly respond, prepare, implement, monitor, and evaluate
disaster and climate resilience plans, programs, and projects for the welfare of the
people, particularly the vulnerable and the poor.

The approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                       ARTICLE I
                               GENERAL PROVISIONS
SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “The Philippine Disaster
Risk Reduction and Management System Act”.


Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the country's severe
vulnerability to natural and man-made disasters and their catastrophic impact on the
people's constitutional rights to life, liberty and property, particularly of the poor and
the marginalized. The State further recognizes the need to institutionalize the
principles of climate change adaptation and disaster risk reduction and management,
and the imperative to embody these principles in responsive and capable institutions.
As such, it is hereby declared a policy of the State to:
a)   Address the root causes of vulnerabilities to disasters by strengthening the
     country's institutional capacity for disaster risk reduction and management and



                                            3
     building the resilience of local communities to disasters including climate change
     impacts;
b)   Adopt a disaster risk reduction and management approach that is holistic,
     comprehensive, integrated, and proactive in lessening the socioeconomic and
     environmental impacts of disasters including climate change, and promote the
     involvement and participation of all sectors and all stakeholders concerned, at all
     levels, especially the local community;
c)   Promote a whole-of-government and whole-of society approach to the
     development of disaster risk reduction and management, to ensure the buy-in of
     all concerned stakeholders, whether from the government sector, private sector,
     or non-government sector;
d)   Adhere to and adopt the universal norms, principles and standards of
     humanitarian assistance and the global effort on risk reduction as concrete
     expression of the country's commitment to overcome human sufferings due to
     recurring disasters;
e)   Develop, promote, and implement a comprehensive National Disaster Risk
     Reduction and Management Plan (NDRRMP) that aims to strengthen the
     capacity of the national government and the local government units (LGUs),
     together with partner stakeholders to build the disaster resilience of communities,
     and to institutionalize arrangements and measures for reducing disaster risks,
     including projected climate risks, and enhancing disaster preparedness and
     response capabilities at all levels;
f)   Incorporate internationally accepted principles of disaster risk management in
     the creation and implementation of national, regional and local sustainable
     development and poverty reduction strategies, policies, plans and budgets;
g)   Adopt and implement a coherent, comprehensive, integrated, efficient and
     responsive disaster risk reduction program incorporated in the development plan
     at various levels of government adhering to the principles of good governance
     such as transparency and accountability within the context of poverty alleviation
     and environmental protection;
h)   Mainstream disaster risk reduction and climate change in development
     processes such as policy formulation, socioeconomic development planning,
     budgeting, and governance, particularly in the areas of environment, agriculture,



                                            4
     water, energy, health, education, poverty reduction, land-use and urban
     planning, and public infrastructure and housing, among others;
i)   Institutionalize the policies, structures, coordination mechanisms and programs
     with continuing budget appropriation on disaster risk reduction from national
     down to local levels towards building a disaster-resilient nation and communities;
j)   Streamline existing disaster risk reduction and management institutions,
     structures and mechanisms, in order to reduce redundancy, increase synergy
     and efficiency, and provide decisive and accountable leadership in times of crisis.
k)   Ensure that disaster risk reduction and climate change measures are gender
     responsive, sensitive to indigenous know ledge systems, and respectful of
     human rights;
l)   Develop and strengthen the capacities of vulnerable and marginalized groups to
     mitigate, prepare for, respond to, and recover from the effects of disasters;
m)   Incorporate community and people's participation on a greater level, to ensure
     that all national disaster risk reduction programs, activities and policies are
     aligned with and responsive to the needs of the people;
n)   Recognize the local risk patterns across the country and strengthen the capacity
     of LGUs for disaster risk reduction and management through decentralized
     powers, responsibilities, and resources at the regional and local levels;
o)   Recognize and strengthen the capacities of LGUs and communities in mitigating
     and preparing for, responding to, and recovering from the impact of disasters;
p)   Engage the participation of civil society organizations (CSOs), the private sector
     and volunteers in the government's disaster risk reduction programs towards
     complementation of resources and effective delivery of services to the Citizenry;
q)   Provide maximum care, assistance and services to individuals and families
     affected by disaster, implement emergency rehabilitation projects to lessen the
     impact of disaster, and facilitate resumption of normal social and economic
     activities; and
r)   Ensure that affected communities rise stronger than they were before incidents
     of disasters, towards reducing their vulnerability and preventing the repetition of
     the catastrophic loss of human life and livelihood and destruction of property.

this Act:

                                       ARTICLE II
         THE DEPARTMENT OF DISASTER RESILIENCE AND EMERGENCY
                         ASSISTANCE AND MANAGEMENT
Sec. 4. The Department of Disaster Resilience and Emergency Assistance and
Management. –To carry out the objectives of this Act and those of R.A. No. 10121,
the Department of Disaster Resilience and Emergency Assistance and Management
is hereby established. The Department shall be the primary policy-making, planning,


                                             6
coordinating, integrating, supervising, implementing, monitoring and evaluating
authority with regard to disaster risk reduction and management in the country.


Sec. 5. Organization of the Department. – The Department shall consist of two
separate and distinct entities: The National Disaster and Risk Reduction and
Management Council (NDRRMC) and the Department Proper. The NDRRMC shall be
attached for administrative and budgetary purposes to the Department.


Sec. 6. Organization of the Department Proper. – The Department proper shall be
comprised of the following:
a)    The Office of the Secretary, which shall encompass the Office of the Secretary
      and the Offices of the Undersecretaries and the Assistant Secretaries, and their
      immediate support staff;
b)    The Regional Field Offices;
c)    The Bureaus enumerated under Section 17 of this Act;
d)    The National Emergency Response Corps (NERC);
e)    The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Research and Training
      Center (NDRRMC-RTC)
f)    The attached agencies enumerated under Section 21 of this Act; and
g)    The Department Services enumerated under Section 18 of this Act;


Sec. 7. Selection of Personnel. – The selection of the personnel of the Department
shall be based strictly on merit and fitness in accordance with the Civil Service Code
and other applicable laws and rules and regulations. The formulation of the
organizational structure and staffing pattern shall be completed within one (1) year
after the effectivity of this Act.


                                     ARTICLE III
 THE NATIONAL DISASTER RISK REDUCTION AND MANAGEMENT COUNCIL
Sec. 8. The National Disaster Risk Reduction Council. – The NDRRMC shall be
the policy-formulating and coordinating body of the Department which shall ensure a
whole-of-government approach to formulating and implementing the National Disaster
Risk Reduction and Management Plan.



                                          7
Sec. 9. Organization. – The membership of the NDRRMC under R.A. No. 10121 shall
be retained: Provided, that the NDRRMC shall be chaired by the President: Provided
further, that the Secretary of the Department of Disaster Management shall be the
vice-chair of the NDRRMC, and shall preside as Chairman when the President is
unable to attend a meeting.


Sec. 10. Powers and Functions of the NDRRMC. – The NDRRMC shall:
a)   Formulate the NDRRMP which shall be a comprehensive, all-hazards, multi-
     sectoral, inter-agency and community-based approach to disaster risk reduction
     and management. The NDRRMP shall serve as the principal guide to disaster
     risk reduction and management efforts in the country and shall be reviewed on a
     ten (10)-year interval, or as may be deemed necessary, in order to ensure its
     relevance to the times;
b)   Develop vertical and horizontal coordination mechanisms for a more coherent
     implementation of disaster risk reduction and management policies and
     programs by sectoral agencies and LGUs;
c)   Constitute a technical management group composed of representatives of the
     concerned departments, offices, and organizations, that shall coordinate and
     meet as often as necessary to effectively manage and sustain national efforts on
     disaster risk reduction and management;
d)   Serve as a coordinating body for all inter-agency efforts during risk reduction and
     management operations;
e)   Advise the President on the status of disaster preparedness, prevention,
     mitigation, response and rehabilitation operations being undertaken by the
     government, CSOs, private sector, and volunteers, and submit proposals to
     restore normalcy in affected areas;
f)   Recommend to the President the declaration of a State of Calamity in areas
     extensively damaged and to submit proposals to restore normalcy in the affected
     areas;
g)   In coordination with the Climate Change Commission, formulate and implement
     a single harmonized framework and action plan for climate change adaptation
     and disaster mitigation;
h)   Receive, administer, mobilize and report the National Disaster Risk Reduction
     and Management Fund, and other recovery and rehabilitation funds;


                                           8
i)   Receive donations and grants from any person, government institution,
     corporation, international organization, and other similar entities, and the power
     to recommend the procurement of developmental assistance for the purpose of
     disaster risk reduction and management subject to Section 32 of this Act;
j)   Monitor and provide the necessary guidelines and procedures on the Local
     Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Fund (LDRRMF) releases as well as
     utilization, accounting and auditing thereof;
k)   Develop appropriate risk transfer mechanisms that shall guarantee social and
     economic protection and increase resiliency in the face of disaster; and
l)   Establish and monitor local and national capacity for disaster risk financing and
     insurance and other risk transfer strategies.


Sec. 11. Monitoring and Reporting. –
a)   On or before the last quarter of the preceding year, the member-agencies of the
     NDRRMC shall submit to the Secretary their respective disaster risk reduction
     and management annual work and financial management plans for the next year;
b)   The member-agencies of the NDRRMC shall also submit to the Secretary
     quarterly progress reports on the implementation of their respective DRRM
     annual work and financial management plans within thirty (30) days from the end
     of each quarter.


                                     ARTICLE IV
                           THE DEPARTMENT PROPER
Sec. 12. The Department Proper. – The Department proper shall be the Secretariat
and executive arm of the Department. It shall be in charge of implementing and
monitoring the implementation of disaster risk reduction and management policies,
activities and programs as embodied in the NDRRMP.


Sec. 13. The Secretary. – The Department shall be headed by a Department
Secretary who shall be a member of the Cabinet. He shall be appointed by the
President for a term of six (6) years and shall be a member of the Cabinet


Sec. 14. Powers and Functions of the Secretary. – The Secretary shall:



                                           9
a)   Monitor the integration of disaster risk reduction and management policies into
     local and national development plans;
b)   Oversee and direct the comprehensive implementation of disaster risk reduction
     and management policies, plans and programs as embodied in the NDRRMP;
c)   Review and evaluate Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plans to
     ensure their consistency with the NDRRMP through the regional offices;
d)   Call upon other instrumentalities or entities of the government and
     nongovernment and civic organizations for assistance in terms of the use of their
     facilities and resources for the protection and preservation of life and properties
     in the whole range of disaster risk reduction and management, which includes
     the power to call on the reserve force as defined in Republic Act No. 7077 to
     assist in relief and rescue during disasters or calamities;
e)   Exercise visitorial and examining powers over governmental and non-
     governmental entities engaged in DRRM operations;
f)   Commandeer any private property for urgent public purposes, subject to
     payment of just compensation in appropriate cases;
g)   Coordinate or oversee the implementation of the country's obligations with
     disaster risk reduction and management treaties to which it is a party and see to
     it that the country's disaster risk management treaty obligations be incorporated
     in its disaster risk reduction and management frameworks, policies, plans,
     programs, and projects; and
h)   Lead the mainstreaming of disaster risk reduction and management in all stages
     and levels of national and local planning and budgeting processes;
i)   Raise awareness among national agencies, local government units, and local
     communities on the importance of disaster risk reduction and management;
j)   In lieu of the President, preside and lead meetings of the NDRRMC;
k)   Coordinate with the members of the NDRRMC for the implementation,
     monitoring, review, and revision of the NDRRMP;
l)   Provide secretarial support to the NDRRMC;
m)   Advise the NDRRMC on matters relating to disaster risk reduction and
     management consistent with the policies and scope as defined in this Act;
n)   Assess the strengths and weaknesses of NDRRMC members on aspects of
     DRRM and provide the corresponding capacity building through the Department
     proper;


                                          10
o)   Direct the deployment of the National Emergency Response Corps (NERC)
     and/or Regional Emergency Response Corps (RERC) the to assist in relief and
     rescue during disasters or calamities;
p)   Create regional and field offices and such other service units and divisions as
     may be necessary;
q)   Exercise administrative supervision and control over the Regional Field Offices
     (RFOs);
r)   Exercise supervision over the Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management
     Offices (LDRRMOs) in all provinces, cities, municipalities, and barangays in the
     country;
s)   Exercise all powers and functions provided to department secretaries under
     Book IV, Chapter 2 of Executive Order No. 292, otherwise known as the
     "Administrative Code of 1987"; and
t)   Perform such other functions as may be necessary or proper to attain the
     objectives of this Act.


Sec. 15. The Undersecretaries and Assistant Secretaries. – The Secretary shall
be assisted by three (3) undersecretaries for disaster mitigation, disaster
preparedness and response, and disaster recovery and rehabilitation, respectively,
and four (4) career assistant secretaries who shall perform such functions as may be
assigned by the Secretary.


Sec. 16. Qualifications. – No person shall be appointed as Secretary, undersecretary
or assistant secretary unless he is a citizen and resident of the Philippines, is of good
moral character, and is of proven competence and expertise in disaster risk reduction
and management over the course of his career. He shall not hold any other position,
whether public or private, during his tenure.


Sec. 17. The Department Bureaus. – The Department shall have the following
bureaus:
a)   The Bureau of Disaster Resilience (BDR) – The BDR shall assist the Secretary
     in formulating, implementing, monitoring, and reviewing policies and programs
     with regard to disaster mitigation, prevention, and post disaster rehabilitation. To
     this end, the BDR shall:


                                           11
1.   Advise the Secretary on all matters regarding disaster and emergency
     mitigation and preparation;
2.   Advise the Secretary on the status of disaster mitigation and prevention and
     post-disaster   rehabilitation   operations   being    undertaken     by   the

3.   Cascade technical expertise on disaster mitigation and prevention and
     post-disaster rehabilitation to LGUs through the RFOs;
4.   Develop, update, and make available a Disaster Risk Reduction and
     Management Information System and Geographic Information System

     tools;
5.   Conduct and institutionalize pre-disaster risk assessment to identify
     vulnerable and at-risk communities and local government units, and provide

     units through the RFOs;
6.   Develop unified and harmonized tools, guidelines, and policies on the

Sec. 18. The Department Services. – The Department shall have the following
Services:

a)   Planning Service – The Planning Service shall provide the department with

b)   Financial and Management Service – The Financial and Management Services

Sec. 19. The National Emergency Response Corps. – There is hereby established
a National Emergency Response Corps (NERC). The NERC shall be a dedicated,
trained and reliable team who can be deployed on short notice in the event of a
disaster. It shall be composed of full-time and part-time disaster managers,
paramedics, debris clearing personnel, volunteers, and aid and relief managers. The
NERC shall be under the administrative control of the BDPR.


Sec. 20. The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Research and
Training Center. – Within one (1) year from the approval of this Act, the Authority
shall establish a National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Research and
Training center which shall serve as the Department's centralized repository and for
its technical materials and research and development on disaster risk reduction and
management.

Sec. 22. The Regional Field Offices. – The current Regional Disaster Risk Reduction
and Management Councils are hereby renamed as the "Regional Field Offices"
(RFOs) and are hereby transferred under the administrative supervision of the
Department. RFOs shall have the following mandates:
a)   To ensure that the principles of disaster risk reduction and management are
     institutionalized on the local level, particularly in their Comprehensive
     Development Plan (CDP) and Comprehensive Land-Use Plan (CLUP), and in
     pursuit of this mandate thereof, review the Local Disaster Risk Reduction and
     Management Plan (LDRRMP) of LGUs within their jurisdiction and ensure their
     alignment with the NDRRMP;
b)   To ensure the compliance of LGUs with their mandates under R.A. 10121;
c)   To pro-actively assess the weaknesses and vulnerabilities of LGUs in all phases
     of disaster risk reduction and management and remedy the same through
     technical assistance and capacity building activities;
d)   To consult LGUs and communities within their jurisdiction on their vulnerabilities
     in all phases of disaster risk reduction and management and forward their
     concerns to the central office to be included in the formulation and review of the
     NDRRMP; and
e)   To act as the operations center during disaster preparation and response
     operations.

Sec. 24. The Regional Directors. – The RFOs shall be headed by a Regional Director

Disaster Preparation and Response, and Knowledge Management. No person shall
be appointed as Regional Director unless he is a citizen and resident of the Philippines,
is of good moral character, and is of proven competence/expertise in emergency
management and disaster risk reduction and management over the course of his

                                      ARTICLE V
                    DISASTER MITIGATION AND PREVENTION
Sec. 25. Harmonization of CCA and DRRM Plans. – The NDRRMC shall ensure
that the Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plan is harmonized and consistent
with the National Climate Change Action Plan as defined under Republic Act No. 9279,
otherwise known as the "Climate Change Act of 2009" as amended. To this end, the
NRRMC shall coordinate with the Climate Change Commission: Provided, that this
provision shall not be construed as a limitation on the mandate, powers, and functions
of the Climate Change Condition as set forth in R.A. No. 9279.


Sec. 26. Pre-Disaster Risk Assessment. – The Department proper shall formulate a
national policy on risk assessment for environmental risk, national disaster risk, natural
disaster risk and climate change risk, and shall take into consideration the
interdependence of disaster risk reduction, environmental management, climate
change adaptation and sustainable development. The Department proper shall
develop tools and standards for Pre-disaster risk assessment and ensure that it is
cascaded to the LDRRMOs through the RFOs.


Sec. 27. National DRRM Information Management System. – The Department
proper shall establish a National Information Management System for DRRM. It shall
be the centralized repository of all disaster risk-reduction and climate change data,
which shall include a geographic information system component, down to the
barangay level. The database shall be available at all times to all levels of the DRRM
system, to members of academe and experts in the field of DRRM, and to the general
public.


Sec. 28. Risk Assessment on the Local Level. – The LDRRMOs and BDRRMOs
shall conduct and risk assessments in coordination with the appropriate RFO and
other stakeholders, which shall include vulnerable and marginalized groups, and
submit the same to the RFOs. The LDRRMOs, with assistance provided by the RFOs,
shall also develop Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Information System and


                                           19
Geographic Information System-based national and local risk maps as policy,
planning, and decision-making tools.


                                       ARTICLE VI
                    DISASTER PREPARATION AND RESPONSE
Sec. 29. Standing Orders for Disaster Preparation and Response. – Within one
(1) year from the effectivity of this Act, the NDRRMC shall formulate and publish
standing orders for all its member-agencies and their respective field office which shall
delineate their respective roles and functions and the chain of command during
disaster preparation and response: Provided, that the standing orders shall take into
Section 15 of R.A. No 10121.


Sec. 30. Regional Field Offices as Operations Center. – The Regional Field Offices
shall serve as the Operations Center for all disaster preparation and response
operations within their jurisdiction. For the purpose of effective coordination, there shall
be formed a Disaster Response Committee to be composed, as a minimum, of the
concerned local chief executives and their LDRRMO officers, the Regional Director of
the RFOs, and such officers as may be assigned by the Secretary.


Sec. 31. Evacuation Centers. – LGUs shall identify and build permanent evacuation
centers in accordance with standards to be issued by the Department proper to
minimize the use of schools: Provided, that schools which are used as evacuation
centers shall be provided with just compensation by the concerned LGU.


Sec. 32. Donations. – The Secretary shall have the power have the power to receive
donations and grants from any person, government institution, corporation,
international organization, and other similar entities, and the power to recommend the
procurement of developmental assistance for the purpose of disaster risk reduction
and management: Provided, that the Department shall certify the receipt of all DRRM
donated funds and ensure their judicious management including their proper and
accurate audit reporting to constituents.


Sec. 33. Procurement. – The declaration of a state of calamity, as defined in R.A. No.
10121, shall be a sufficient basis for an LGU to undergo negotiated procurement, as


                                            20
defined under R.A. No. 9184, otherwise known as the “Government Procurement
Reform Act”.


                                     ARTICLE VII
               POST-DISASTER REHABILITATION AND RECOVERY
Sec. 34. Rehabilitation Committee. – The members of the Disaster Response
Committee as defined under Sec. 30 of this Act may continue to serve as the
coordinating committee for disaster rehabilitation and recovery operations.


Sec. 36. Standards for Rehabilitation and Recovery. – The Secretary shall
promulgate rules and regulations which will set standards for disaster rehabilitation
operations. Such rules and regulations shall, at the very least:
a)   Improve the physical, social and economic resilience of the community, in
     accordance with the principles of "Build Back Better";
b)   Redirect development outside of danger zones; and
c)   Maximize the use of Public-Private Partnerships.


                                    ARTICLE VIII
                           COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION
Sec. 37. Community Input in Pre-Disaster Risk Assessment. – In conducting pre-
disaster risk assessments, the LDRRMO and BDRRMO shall include interviews and
consultations with local communities in order to identify their vulnerabilities, Such
consultations shall complement data gathered from data-oriented pre-disaster risk
assessments and shall be incorporated in the formulation of all disaster risk reduction
and management plans on the local and national levels.


Sec. 38. Volunteers. – The Department proper and the RFOs shall provide training in
first aid, disaster management and leadership in times of disasters. Individuals who
complete such training shall be deputized by the Department and considered as
partners in disaster management who shall have the following responsibilities:
a)   Directly receive early warning messages from the Department and disseminate
     the same to their communities;
b)   Mobilize their communities away from vulnerable areas;



                                          21
c)   Disseminate the provisions of this Act as well as its corresponding rules and
     regulations to local communities;
d)   Conduct interviews and consultations with local communities on their satisfaction
     with the DRRM programs, activities and policies of the government;
e)   Provide first aid when necessary; and
f)   Aid the government in distributing goods and maintaining order during disaster
     response and rehabilitation operations.


                                     ARTICLE IX
     RESOURCES FOR DISASTER RISK REDUCTION AND MANAGEMENT
Sec. 39. Human Resources for Disaster Risk Reduction and Management. – The
department proper shall provide grants and other forms of support for individuals who
study and complete courses in disaster risk reduction and management: Provided, that
individuals shall render disaster risk reduction and management service for at least
two (2) years in a community determined by the Department proper.


Sec. 40. The NDRRM Fund. – At least three per cent (3%) of all estimated regular
government revenues shall be set aside for the NDRRM Fund to be used for the
purposes identified under Section 22 of R.A. No. 10121. The NDRRM fund shall not
be reduced, transferred, realigned, or used for purposes other than those identified
under R.A. No. 10121.

                   MISCELLANEOUS AND FINAL PROVISIONS
Sec. 41. Transition Period. – The transfer of functions, assets, funds, equipment,
properties, transactions, assets, funds, properties transactions and personnel of the
affected agencies, and the formulation of the internal organic structure, staffing
pattern, operating and revised budget of the Department, shall be completed within six
(6) months from the effectivity of this Act, during which time the existing personnel
shall continue to assume their posts in holdover capacities until new appointments are
issued.


Sec. 42. Separation from Service. – Employees separated from the service as a
result of this reorganization shall, within six (6) months from their separation from the


                                           22
service, receive the retirement benefits to which they may be entitled under existing
laws, rules and regulations.


Sec. 43. Prohibited Acts. – The following Acts shall be prohibited:
a)   Dereliction of duties which leads to destruction, loss of lives, critical damage of
     facilities and misuse of funds;
b)   Preventing the entry and distribution of relief goods in disaster-stricken areas,
     including appropriate technology, tools, equipment, accessories, disaster
     teams/experts;
c)   Buying, for consumption or resale, from disaster relief agencies any relief goods,
     equipment or other and commodities which are intended for distribution to
     disaster affected communities;
d)   Buying, for consumption or resale, from the recipient disaster affected persons
     any relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities received by them;
e)   Selling of relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities which are intended
     for distribution to disaster victims;
f)   Forcibly seizing relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities intended for or
     consigned to a specific group of victims or relief agency;
g)   Diverting or mis delivery of relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities to
     persons other than the rightful recipient or consignee;
h)   Accepting, possessing, using or disposing relief goods, equipment or other aid
     commodities not intended for nor consigned to him/her;
i)   Misrepresenting the source of relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities
     by:
     1)    Either covering, replacing or defacing the labels of the containers to make
           it appear that the goods, equipment or other aid commodities came from
           another agency or persons;
     2)    Repacking the goods, equipment or other aid commodities into containers
           with different markings to make it appear that the goods came from another
           agency or persons or was released upon the instance of a particular agency
           or persons; or
     3)    Making false verbal claim that the goods, equipment or other and
           commodity m its untampered original containers actually came from



                                             23
           another agency or persons or was released upon the instance of a
           particular agency or persons;
j)   Substituting or replacing relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities with
     the same items or inferior/cheaper quality;
k)   Illegal solicitations by persons or organizations representing others as defined in
     the standards and guidelines set by the Authority
l)   Deliberate use of false at inflated data in support of the request for funding, relief
     goods, equipment or other aid commodities for emergency assistance or
     livelihood projects; and
m)   Tampering with or stealing hazard monitoring and disaster preparedness
     equipment and paraphernalia.


Sec. 44. Penal Clause. – Any individual, corporation, partnership, association, or
other juridical entity that commits any of the prohibited acts provided for in the
preceding section shall be prosecuted and upon conviction shall suffer a fine of not
less than one hundred thousand pesos (Php 100,000.00) or any amount not to exceed
seven hundred fifty thousand pesos (Php 750,000.00) or imprisonment of not less than
six (6) years and one (1) day or more than twelve (12) years, or both, at the discretion
of the court, including perpetual disqualification from public office if the offender is a
public officer, and confiscation or forfeiture in favor of the government of the objects
and the instrumentalities used in committing any of herein prohibited acts.


Sec. 45. Appropriations. – The amount needed for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be taken from the current fiscal year's appropriation of the NDRRMC, OCD,
and all agencies herein transferred to the Department as well. Thereafter, the amount
needed for the operation and maintenance of the Authority shall be included in the
General Appropriations Act.
Sec. 46. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The members of the NDRRMC
shall within sixty (60) days form the effectivity of this Act the implementing rules and
regulations necessary for its effective implementation.
