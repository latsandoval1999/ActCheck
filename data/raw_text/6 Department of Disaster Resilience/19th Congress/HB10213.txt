                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

           HavingbeengeographicallysituatedalongthePacificTyphoonBeltandinthe
    Pacific Ring of Fire, the Philippines is prone to natural disasters and calamities.
    Majority of the country's total land area and nearly three fourths of the Filipino
    population are vulnerable to multiple hazards, such as typhoons, earthquakes, floods,
    storm surges, tsunamis, volcanic eruptions, and landslides.

           In the past three decades, natural disasters have negatively impacted 120
    million people and resulted in 33,000 deaths. According to World Risk Index 2020
    which serves to assess risk and vulnerability on a global scale, the Philippines ranks
    ninth in terms of disaster risk and second highest among Asian countries.1

         The financial aftermath of disasters is a massive burden on peoples' security
    and well-being, and on national, local, and household budgets. Local governments
    and communities bear the heaviest brunt, repeatedly hitting hardest the poor and
    most vulnerable.2

    innovative approaches, and invest in robust disaster risk governance systems to
    bolster the Philippines' resilience against future calamities. There is also a need for a
    comprehensiveandcoordinatedapproachtodisasterriskgovemance,withafocuson
