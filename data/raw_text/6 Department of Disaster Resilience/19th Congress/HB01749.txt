                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

Due to its geographical location, the Philippines is exposed to high incidents of
environmental hazards such as typhoons, floods, storm surges, tsunamis,
earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, landslides, and droughts. This leaves various
communities throughout the country in highly vulnerable situations. Typhoon Haiyan
(Yolanda), one of the most powerful tropical cyclones ever recorded, was an example
of how a natural disaster can devastate lives, properties, and entire communities. It
left the country with more than 7,000 deaths (with thousands still unaccounted for)
and 16 million affected people. The overall financial and economic impact of Typhoon
Haiyan is estimated at $14 Billion.

The Philippines ranked 17th in the world as the most affected country from extreme
weather events in the Global Climate Risk Index (CRI) 2021 with a score of 26.67
using 2019 data.1 In 2018, we ranked third in the World Risk Index of most disaster-
prone countries in the world published by the United Nations University Institute of
Environment and Human Security (UNU-HES), with a risk index of 27.69%. The
Philippines also ranks third on the WRI’s list of countries with the greatest exposure
to natural disasters, with a score of 52.46%, just ahead of Japan (45.91%).

Given our exposure to threats of natural disasters, Republic Act No. 10121, otherwise
known as the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010”
(PDDRM), was enacted on 27 May 2010. The Act shifted the policy environment and
the way the country deals with disasters from mere response to preparedness. RA
10121 provides a comprehensive, all-hazard, multi-sectoral, inter-agency, and
community-based approach to disaster risk management through the formulation of
the National Disaster Risk Management Framework.2

Notwithstanding the efforts and resources to reduce the risks and vulnerabilities to
natural disasters, challenges are abounding so much so that our government’s efforts

    2. Lack of capacity of line agencies and LGUs to perform DRRM functions;
    3. Less priority given to DRRM activities;
    4. Weak implementation of laws and policies;

    9. Difficulty in tracking foreign disaster assistance. 3

The need for greater coordination and synchronization of disaster risk management
at all levels should be a top priority. Thus, this measure will create a single agency
that has a distinct and separate mandate for disaster management. The Department
of Disaster Management shall be the focal government agency for policy-making,
coordination, integration, supervision, monitoring and evaluation for all disaster and
rehabilitation management policies and programs in the country. 4

With strong institutions, we can reverse the current trends of rising disaster impact.
Everyone of us must be empowered to make decisions which reduce risk – from
government to citizens.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate enactment and approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

 4   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State:
 5
 6      a. To uphold the sacredness and dignity of human life by efficiently and effectively
 7         addressing the vulnerabilities to disasters, through the establishment of a
 8         focused, streamlined, independent, empowered, capacitated, full-time,
 9         specialized agency on disaster risk reduction and management;
10
11      b. To establish a permanent, institutionalized, cohesive and comprehensive
12         framework for disaster preparedness, prevention and mitigation, and response,
13         to be implemented by a full-time and focused specialized agency with its own
14         mandate, powers, and funding, in coordination and with the participation of the
15         Philippine government, other foreign governments and financial institutions,
16         international organizations, the private sector, and civil society.
17
18      c. Inculcate a culture of awareness, resilience and preparedness, and
19         rehabilitation for natural and man-made disasters;
20
21      d. To create an integrated, systematic, comprehensive, and cohesive plan to
22         simplify, streamline, integrate, and coordinate the various programs, projects,
23         and activities of the Government and such other actors/stakeholders in disaster
24         risk reduction and management to ensure accountability, responsibility, and
25         transparency, as well as to achieve efficiency and effectiveness of in the
26         performance of their functions.

23   SEC. 26. Emergency and Contingency Powers. - Whenever it becomes necessary,
24   the Department Secretary, subject to the chain of command and with the consent of
25   the President, may call out the Armed Forces of the Philippines and/or the Philippine
26   National Police to prevent or suppress lawless violence, and to, ensure the protection
27   and preservation of life and/ or properties, in the whole range of disaster risk reduction
28   and management.
29
30          In times of disasters, and whenever warranted by the circumstances, the
31   Department Secretary may call upon other instrumentalities or entities of the
32   government and nongovernment and civic organizations for assistance in terms of the
33   use of their facilities and resources for the protection and preservation of life and
34   properties in the whole range of disaster risk reduction and management. This
35   authority includes the power to call on the reserve force as defined in Republic Act No.
36   7077 to assist in relief and rescue during disasters or calamities.

38   SEC. 27. Prohibited Acts. - In addition to the prohibited acts penalized under Section
39   20, in relation to Section 19, of the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and
40   Management Act of 2010:
41
42          a. Any person, group or corporation who directly or indirectly requests or
43             receives any part of the funds devoted to disaster risk reduction and
44             management, as well as monetary contributions and donations in kind,
45             where such person, group or corporation has no right to receive such
46             proceeds from the fund, in whole or in part, monetary contribution or
47             donation, shall be guilty of the crime of corruption of calamity funds or
48             donations, and shall be prosecuted and upon conviction shall suffer a fine
49             of not less than Two Hundred Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php 250,000.00) or
50             any amount not to exceed One Million Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php
51             1,500,000.00) or Imprisonment of not less than twelve (12) years and one
52             (1) day, nor more than twenty (20) years, or both, at the discretion of the
                                                 10
 1             court, including perpetual disqualification from public office if the offender is
 2             a public officer, and confiscation or forfeiture in favor of the government of
 3             the objects and the instrumentalities used in committing the herein
 4             prohibited act;
 5
 6          b. Any person who, with intent to gain, but without violence against or
 7             intimidation of persons nor force upon things, shall take personal property
 8             devoted to any disaster risk reduction and management activity, without the
 9             consent of the Secretary, shall be guilty of the crime of qualified theft, and
10             shall be prosecuted and upon conviction shall be punished by the penalties
11             next higher by three degrees than those respectively specified in Article 309
12             of the Revised Penal Code;
13
14          c. Any public officer who, by reason of the duties of his office, is accountable
15             for calamity funds or property devoted to disaster risk reduction and
16             management efforts, shall appropriate the same or shall take or
17             misappropriate or shall consent, through abandonment or negligence, shall
18             permit any other person to take such public funds, or property, wholly or
19             partially, or shall otherwise be guilty of the misappropriation of malversation
20             of such funds or property, shall be punished by the penalties next higher by
21             three degrees than those respectively specified in Article 217 of the Revised
22             Penal Code;
23

24                 In all the above, if the offender is a corporation, partnership or
25             association, or other juridical entity, the penalty shall be imposed upon the
26             officer or officers of the corporation, partnership, association of entity
27             responsible for the violation, without prejudice to the cancellation or
28             revocation of these entitles license or accreditation issued to them by any
29             licensing of accredited body of the government. If such offender is an alien,
30             he or she shall, in addition to the penalties prescribed in this Act, be

33                 The prosecution for the offenses shall be without prejudice to any liability

 7   SEC. 31. Congressional Oversight Committee. - There is hereby created a
 8   Congressional Oversight Committee to monitor and oversee the implementation of the
 9   provisions of this Act. The Committee shall be composed of six (6) members from the
10   Senate and six (6) members from the House of Representatives with the Chairpersons
11   of the Committees on National Defense and Security of both the Senate and the House
12   of Representatives as joint Chairpersons of this Committee.

14   The five (5) other members from each Chamber are to be designated by the Senate

22   under this Act. All other laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, and other
