                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill seeks to establish a Department of Disaster Resilience to improve the
institutional capacity of the government in disaster response, mitigate the effects of disasters
and natural calamities, provide a collective disaster risk reduction and management plan, and
promote capacity-building in local communities to effectively respond to both natural disasters
and climate change.

       The Philippines as an archipelago in the Pacific region, is prone to tropical cyclones or
typhoons, flooding, drought, rain-induced landslides, volcanic eruptions, storm surges and
earthquakes which usually result to human casualties and damages to properties and crops in
different parts of the country.

       Just in 2021 alone, the country was hit by more than five (5) typhoons such as Fabian,
Jolina, and Odette1. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority, damages incurred due to
natural extreme events and disasters amounted to Php463 billion from 2010 to 2019.2 Only
recently, in July 2022, the Philippines experienced a 7.0 magnitude earthquake affecting at least
121 cities and municipalities and 615 injured persons with 11 human casualties.3

       Despite the collaborative efforts, plans and preparation for calamaties and natural
disasters by the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council, the local
government units and other government offices, thousands of people and properties nationwide
were still severely affected by these extreme natural events heavily impacting the vulnerable
sectors such as agriculture and small business enterprises.

       The establishment of the Department of Disaster Resilience will guarantee closer
coordination between the national government and local government units and a stronger
disaster resilience and risk management system.

 3         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It shall be the policy of the State to:

11             most important assets of the nation, and all disaster risk and management efforts
12             shall be responsive to the humanitarian needs of the people, the dignity and value of
13             the human person, and respect for property;
14         (c) Promote a simple, strategic, systematic, continuous, comprehensive, inclusive, and
15             integrated approach to disaster risk reduction and management aimed at
16             substantially reducing vulnerabilities and the risk of disasters and other

392            SEC. 4. Creation and Mandate of the Department of Disaster Resilience. - There is
393   hereby created a Department of Disaster Resilience, herein after referred to as the Department.
394            The Department shall be the primary government agency responsible for leading,
395   organizing, and managing the national effort to reduce disaster risk, prepare for and respond to
396   disasters, recover and rehabilitate, and build forward better after the occurrence of disasters.
397            The Department shall oversee and coordinate the preparation, implementation,
398   monitoring, and evaluation of disaster and climate resilience plans, programs, projects and
399   activities, provide leadership in the continuous development of strategic and systematic
400   approaches to disaster prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, recovery and
401   rehabilitation, to anticipatory adaptation strategies, measures, techniques, and options.
402            The Department shall augment the capacity of local governments units in
403   collaboration with relevant national government agencies and other stakeholders to implement
404   disaster risk reduction and management and climate change action plans, programs, projects,
405   and activities.
406            The Department shall be the principal government institution responsible for ensuring
407   safe, adaptive, and disaster resilient communities. It is mandated to provide a clear and
408   comprehensive direction in the implementation of plans, programs, and projects to reduce the
409   risk of natural hazards and the effects of climate change and manage the impact of disasters.
410            The mandate of the Department covers all natural hazards, which include, among
411   others, the following:
412       (a) Geological Phenomena and Related Hazards
413           1)   Earthquake - ground rupture, ground shaking, liquefaction, tsunami, tire,
414                landslides, and seiche or lake water oscillation
415           2)   Volcanic activity - ash fall, pyroclastic flow, lava flow, lahar, Fissuring, and
416                volcanic gas
417           3)   Mass movement - landslides, debris flow, sinkholes
418       (b) Hydrological, Oceanographic and Meteorological Phenomena and Related Hazards
419           1)   Tropical cyclone - severe winds, storm surge, rouge waves, severe rainfall
420                including hail
421           2)   Flood, rain-triggered landslides and storm surges

                                                                                                     15
422          3)   Erosion
423          4)   Marine heatwaves, extreme sea events, and sea level rise
424      (c) Climate Variability/Change with Related Hazards of Compound Events and Cascading
425          Impacts
426          1)   El Niño or La Niña (ENSO) with associated rainfall and temperature (in particular,
427               projected extreme ENSOs)
428          2)   Extreme temperature (heat wave or cold wave), extreme weather conditions
429               (drought or excessive rainfall)
430          3)   Wildfire (forest or land fires)
431      (d) Biological and Related Hazards
432          1)   Epidemic/Pandemic - viral, bacterial, parasitic, fungal, prior infectious diseases
433          2)   Insect Infestation. - grasshoppers/locusts
434      (e) Extra-Terrestrial - Meteorite/Asteroid impact
435      The Department shall manage and direct the implementation of national, local, and
436   community-based disaster resilience and disaster management programs, projects and
437   activities, including disaster response, recovery and rehabilitation, when applicable, in
438   collaboration with relevant national government agencies, LGUs, CSOs, academic institutions
439   and other stakeholders: Provided, That for human-induced disasters, the Department of the
440   Interior and Local Government (DILG), Department of National Defense (DND), Department
441   of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), and other relevant government instrumentalities,
442   including the Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP), Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP),
443   Philippine National Police (PNP), Philippine Coast Guard (PCG), and Office of Civil Defense
444   (OCD), shall continue to perform their functions: Provided, further, That the Department shall
445   remain as the lead agency in recovery and rehabilitation efforts in collaboration with all
446   stakeholders when applicable.
447          Notwithstanding, the mandates and powers stated herein shall be without prejudice to
448   the President's directive to address natural, human-induced, or other disasters such as an
449   epidemic.

675      SEC. 6. Emergency Measures. –
676      (a) To protect and preserve life and property and ensure and promote public safety and
677   welfare, the Department may undertake and implement the following emergency measures
678   in anticipation of, during, and in the aftermath of disasters:
679          1) Carrying out of preemptive evacuation,
680          2) Imposition of curfew;
681          3) Rationing of the distribution of basic goods in critical shortage, and when
682              necessary, preventing or restricting the transfer of such goods outside of the area
683              affected by the disaster, including access to rice inventory of the National Food
684              Authority (NRA) office or storage located in the affected area;
685          4) When there is imminent danger of loss of lives or damage to property, temporarily
686              take over or direct the operation of any private utility or business, subject to
687              payment of just compensation, and
688          5) With the concurrence of the DOF, recommend to the Monetary Board the
689              deferment of the payment of monetary obligations of local government units and
690              private entities that have been severely affected by disaster.



                                                                                                 23
691      (b) Local government units shall enact ordinance on and implement necessary and
692          appropriate emergency measures to ensure the protection and preservation of life and
693          proper Ty and the promotion of public safety and welfare in anticipation of, during, and
694          in the aftermath of disasters.
695      (c) Emergency measures shall be carried our in a manner that is humane, respectful of the
696   and culture of discrimination and disproportionate force, and with conscious attention to the
697   needs of vulnerable and marginalized groups.
698      (d) Appropriate steps shall be taken to inform the public of the need to implement
699   emergency measures for their safety.
700
701      SEC. 7. Preemptive and Force Evacuations. -            Preemptive evacuation shall be the
702   preferred and primary mode of moving and relocating people that will be affected by
703   impending disasters.
704      A forced evacuation shall be considered as an emergency measure of last resort that may be
705   undertaken in anticipation of or during a disaster and carried out by the concerned local
706   government unit: Provided, That in case of the failure or inability of the local government units
707   to implement the same, the forced evacuation shall be implemented by the Department, which
708   may direct and compel the assistance of law enforcement and other government agencies to
709   implement such measure.
710      Any person who willfully disregards or disobeys a preemptive or forced evacuation carried
711   out by the local government unit or the Department releases such local government unit or the
712   Department, as the case may be, from any liability for injury, death, damage to, or loss of
713   property due to such disobedience.

1321          No. 9184, otherwise known as the "Government Procurement Reform Act", its
1322          implementing rules and regulations (IRR) and other issuances of the Government

1329                                           RECOVERY

1331      SEC. 54. Standards for Recovery.         -    The Department shall observe internationally
1332   accepted standards for recovery, planning, programming and implementation of the recovery
1333   process. Towards this end, the Department shall:
1334      (a) Improve the community's physical, social and economic resilience, consistent with the
1335          principle of "building forward better";
1336      (b) Use locally driven centrally supported processes based on legal mandates with
1337          supplementary capacity support when requested;
1338      (c) Redirect development outside danger zones to minimize loss of lives and structures
1339          resulting from typhoons, flooding, landslides, and other hazards;
1340      (d) Employ outcome-driven planning and implementation;
1341      (e) Maximize use of private-public sector partnership where possible;
1342      (f) Consider local conditions such as culture, security situation and existing capacities of
1343          communities in identifying programs and projects;
1344      (g) Ensure to public transport, physical and mental health services, markets, schools,
1345          sustainable livelihoods, and other public services in planning for settlement areas, and
1346      (h) Ensure restoration of peace and order and recovery of government functions.

1561      SEC. 77. Prohibited Acts. - Any public official, private person, group or corporation who
1562   commits any of the following prohibited acts shall be held liable and be subjected to the
1563   criminal and administrative penalties as provided for in Section 78 of this Act, without
1564   prejudice to the imposition of other criminal, civil and administrative liabilities under existing
1565   laws.
1566      (a) Prohibited Acts of Public Officials. The following acts, if committed by public officials
1567           without justifiable cause, shall be considered as gross neglect of duty:
1568           1) Dereliction of duty which leads to destruction, loss of lives, critical damage of
1569                facilities and misuse of funds;
1570           2) Failure to enforce laws, standards, or regulations such as the National Building
1571                Code, Solid Waste Management Act, Water Code, and other relevant laws, thereby
1572                increasing the risk of disasters or adversely affecting of disaster resilience;
1573           3) Failure to expeditiously act on reported violations;
1574           4) Failure to create LDRO(s) or PDRO(s) and appoint a permanent Local or Provincial
1575                Disaster Resilience Officer within six (6) months from the approval of this Act and
1576           5) Failure to prepare and implement an LDRP or PDRP, as applicable or a contingency
1577                plan for hazards frequently occurring within their jurisdiction.
1578      (b) Prohibited Acts of Public Officials and Private Persons or Institutions.
1579           1)   Delaying without justifiable cause the delivery of aid commodities, their improper
1580                handling or storage, resulting to damage or spoilage;
1581           2) Withholding the distribution of relief goods due to (a) political or partisan
1582                considerations, (b) discrimination based on race, ethnicity, religion, or gender; and
1583                (c) other similar circumstances;
1584           3) Preventing the entry and distribution of relief goods in disaster-stricken areas,
1585                including appropriate technology, tools, equipment, accessories, disaster teams or
1586                experts;
1587           4) Buying, for consumption or resale, from disaster relief agencies any relief goods,
1588                equipment or other and commodities which are intended for distribution to disaster
1589                affected communities;



                                                                                                      51
1590         5) Buying, for consumption or resale, from the disaster affected persons any relief
1591              goods, equipment or other aid commodities received by them;

1594         7) Forcibly seizing relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities intended for or
1595              consigned to a specific group of victims or relief agency;
1596         8) Diverting or misdelivering relief goods, equipment or other aid commodities to
1597              persons other than the rightful recipient or consignee;
