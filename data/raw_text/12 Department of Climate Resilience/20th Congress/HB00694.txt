                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines, situated within the Pacific Ring of Fire and the typhoon belt, faces one of the
most complex and overlapping hazard profiles in the world. On average, the country experiences
more than twenty typhoons annually, five of which are destructive, in addition to frequent
earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, flooding, droughts, and slow-onset climate change effects such
as sea level rise and biodiversity loss. According to the World Risk Index 2023, the Philippines
ranks as the most at-risk country globally due to the convergence of exposure, vulnerability, and
insufficient adaptive capacities1.

Despite the enactment of Republic Act No. 10121 in 2010, which created the National Disaster
Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) and advanced the disaster risk reduction
paradigm, major institutional gaps persist. Fragmented mandates, weak vertical coordination,
limited scientific integration, and persistent underperformance by many local government units
(LGUs) continue to undermine national resilience efforts 2.

This bill seeks to create the Department of Climate Resilience (DCR) as the lead national agency
responsible for policy, planning, coordination, innovation, capacity building, and command in
disaster risk reduction, emergency response, climate adaptation, and recovery. The proposed
Department shall institutionalize the National Climate Resilience Framework (NCRF), implement

Department shall further mainstream resilience procurement, establish Green Resilience

This bill is undergirded by studies demonstrating the economic return of proactive investments in
resilience. The World Bank and Global Facility for Disaster Reduction and Recovery (GFDRR)
estimate that every dollar spent on disaster risk reduction generates four dollars in future benefits 12.
Similarly, empirical data show that climate risk insurance, risk-sensitive land use planning, and
early warning systems have prevented billions in losses and saved countless lives in comparable
hazard contexts 13.

Towards this end, the State shall institutionalize strong coordination mechanisms, enforceable
compliance standards, DCRM workforce professionalization, and sustainable funding streams to
ensure that disaster risk governance is robust and adaptive to future challenges.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                       ARTICLE I
                                   GENERAL PROVISIONS



       SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Department of Climate
Resilience Act of 2025.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It shall be the policy of the State to:

           (a) Uphold the right of all persons to life, safety, and human dignity by ensuring the
               protection of lives, livelihoods, infrastructure, cultural heritage, and ecosystems
               before, during, and after disasters;
           (b) Recognize climate resilience as an integral component of national development,
               environmental sustainability, and human security, and mainstream it into all levels
               of governance and sectors of society;
           (c) Promote a proactive, anticipatory, and risk-informed approach to disaster risk
               reduction and management (DRRM), grounded in scientific evidence, multi-hazard
               assessments, and inclusive stakeholder engagement;
           (d) Ensure the establishment of a strong institutional and legal foundation through the
               creation of a permanent national agency with the requisite authority, capacity, and
               mandate to oversee, coordinate, and implement climate resilience programs and
               policies;
           (e) Guarantee the professionalization, capacity development, and equitable
               deployment of the DRRM workforce through education, training, and accreditation
               mechanisms that reflect technical and ethical standards;
           (f) Institutionalize integrated national and local systems for early warning, emergency
               operations, recovery planning, data management, and public communication,
               including the use of digital governance, predictive analytics, and innovative
               technologies;
           (g) Strengthen the accountability, performance, and capacity of Local Government
               Units (LGUs) as frontliners of climate resilience, by providing technical, financial,
               and institutional support, subject to enforceable standards and results-based
               performance metrics;
           (h) Ensure the fair and equitable treatment of all persons, especially vulnerable and
               marginalized groups, through the integration of principles of disaster justice, gender
               equality, accessibility, and inclusive risk governance;
           (i) Facilitate sustained financing and investment in resilience-building measures,
               including but not limited to public budgeting, private sector participation, risk
               financing instruments, and climate-related investment mechanisms;
           (j) Encourage innovation, localization, and community empowerment through
               research and development, resilience education, participatory governance, and
               indigenous and local knowledge systems;
           (k) Promote inter-agency, inter-LGU, inter-sectoral, and international cooperation to
               foster a whole-of-government, whole-of-society, and whole-of-system approach to
               climate resilience;
           (l) Monitor and evaluate disaster risk governance outcomes through evidence-based
               performance metrics, transparency mechanisms, and a robust system of policy
               feedback, public accountability, and continuous improvement.

        SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are defined
as follows:

           (a) Critical Infrastructure refers to physical and virtual assets, systems, and networks
               whose incapacitation or destruction would have a debilitating impact on national
               security, economic continuity, public health, or safety.
           (b) Climate Justice refers to the fair treatment, meaningful participation, and equitable
               protection of vulnerable and marginalized populations in all phases of disaster risk
               governance, ensuring that no sector is left behind before, during, or after disasters.
           (c) Climate Resilience refers to the capacity of local communities, institutions, and
               systems to anticipate, absorb, adapt to, and rapidly recover from the impacts of
               hazards in a manner that preserves and enhances quality of life and development
               sustainability.
           (d) Digital Twin Technology refers to a digital replication of physical environments
               and infrastructure systems to simulate hazard impacts, optimize response, and plan
               recovery.
           (e) Green Resilience Corridors refers to networks of natural and engineered ecological
               buffers and spaces designed to mitigate climate and disaster risks while providing
               environmental, social, and economic co-benefits.
           (f) Incident Command System (ICS) refers to a standardized, modular, scalable
               structure for on-scene management and interagency coordination during
               emergencies and disasters.
            (g) Localized Resilience Hubs refers to decentralized, multi-stakeholder centers
                embedded at the provincial or city level that coordinate community-based risk
                reduction, local early warning, emergency response, and post-disaster recovery
                initiatives.
            (h) National Climate Resilience Scorecard refers to a comprehensive national
                benchmarking system to assess LGU and agency performance in disaster
                preparedness, risk reduction, and resilience outcomes.
            (i) National Risk Register refers to a comprehensive, regularly updated database that
                identifies, maps, and assesses systemic, emerging, and compound risks facing the
                country across sectors, geographic areas, and time scales.
            (j) Pre-Disaster Recovery Planning (PDRP) refers to a proactive planning process
                conducted before disasters occur to identify recovery strategies, timelines, and
                responsibilities.
            (k) Resilience Bonds refers to innovative financial instruments that link infrastructure
                investment with disaster risk reduction incentives through insurance mechanisms.
            (l) Resilience Education refers to formal, non-formal, and community-based education
                strategies that integrate disaster risk reduction and climate adaptation into school
                curricula, public information campaigns, and lifelong learning systems.
            (m) Resilience Procurement refers to government acquisition strategies that prioritize
                climate- and disaster-resilient goods, services, technologies, and infrastructure to
                ensure sustainability and continuity of operations.
            (n) Risk-Informed Decision-Making refers to the use of multi-hazard, vulnerability, and
                exposure data, modeling, and forecasts in planning and policy processes to mitigate
                risk.



                                    ARTICLE II
                         DEPARTMENT OF CLIMATE RESILIENCE:
                          MANDATE, POWERS, AND FUNCTIONS


       SEC. 4. Creation and mandate of the Department of Climate Resilience. – A Department
of Climate Resilience (DCR) is hereby created, hereinafter referred to as the “Department”.

        The Department shall be the principal entity tasked with overseeing the formulation,
coordination, and implementation of comprehensive, multi-level, and multi-sectoral programs,
plans, and actions relating to disaster risk reduction and management, climate and hazard
resilience, disaster response, recovery, and rehabilitation. It shall integrate national, regional, local,
and community-based initiatives to strengthen anticipatory, absorptive, adaptive, and
transformative capacities across all levels of governance.

        In pursuit of its mandate, the Department shall lead the development and execution of
policy, planning, and operational mechanisms for risk-informed governance and resilience
building to ensure alignment with national development goals and climate change adaptation
strategies.
       The Department shall exercise overall management and coordination of national, regional,
and local efforts relating to all forms of hazards, including but not limited to the following:

          (a) Geological or Geophysical Phenomena and Related Hazards:
                 (1) Earthquakes – including ground shaking, surface rupture, liquefaction,
                     induced landslides, fires, and tsunamis;
                 (2) Volcanic Activity – including ash fall, pyroclastic flows, lava flows, lahars,
                     volcanic fissures, and gas emissions;
                 (3) Mass Movements – including landslides, debris flows, subsidence, and
                     sinkholes.
          (b) Hydrological, Oceanographic, and Meteorological Phenomena and Related
              Hazards:
                 (1) Tropical Cyclones – including destructive winds, torrential rains, flooding,
                     storm surges, and rogue waves;
                 (2) Flooding – including flash floods, riverine floods, urban floods, and dam-
                     related floods;
                 (3) Erosion – including coastal and riverbank erosion;
                 (4) Sea-Level Rise – including coastal inundation and salinization;
                 (5) Marine Heatwaves and Oceanic Anomalies – including coral bleaching and
                     marine ecosystem disruption.
          (c) Climate Variability and Climate Change-Related Hazards, Compound Events, and
              Cascading Impacts:
                 (1) El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) Events – including El Niño and La
                     Niña and associated extreme weather phenomena such as droughts and
                     prolonged rainfall;
                 (2) Extreme Temperature Events – such as heatwaves and cold spells;
                 (3) Droughts – both meteorological and agricultural;
                 (4) Wildfires – including forest fires and grassland fires;
                 (5) Compound and Cascading Events – such as flooding following typhoons
                     compounded by storm surges and landslides.
          (d) Biological Hazards and Related Threats:
                 (1) Epidemics and Pandemics – including viral, bacterial, parasitic, fungal, and
                     prion-based infectious diseases;
                 (2) Zoonotic Outbreaks – originating from animal-to-human transmission;
                 (3) Insect and Pest Infestations – such as locust swarms, agricultural pests, and
                     vector-borne disease carriers;
                 (4) Antimicrobial Resistance – as an emerging threat to public health systems.
          (e) Technological and Industrial Hazards:
                 (1) Hazardous Material Incidents – including chemical spills, gas leaks, and
                     radiological exposure;
                 (2) Structural Failures – such as building collapses, dam breaks, and industrial
                     explosions;
                 (3) Transportation Accidents – including maritime, aviation, and rail disasters;
                 (4) Energy System Disruptions – such as grid failures, fuel shortages, and
                     nuclear incidents;
                 (5) Cyber Risks – especially those that impact critical infrastructure during
                     emergencies.
           (f) Human-Induced and Socio-Political Hazards:
                  (1) Armed Conflicts and Insurgencies – including forced displacement and
                      complex humanitarian emergencies;
                  (2) Terrorism and Sabotage – including bombings and attacks on infrastructure;
                  (3) Civil Unrest – including mass protests and riots affecting emergency
                      operations;
                  (4) Misinformation and Disinformation – which compromise emergency
                      communication and public safety.
           (g) Extra-Terrestrial Hazards:
                  (1) Asteroid and Meteorite Impacts;
                  (2) Solar Storms and Space Weather – including geomagnetic storms affecting
                      satellite and communication systems.

        The Department shall lead in formulating and executing an integrated, proactive, science-
based, and risk-informed climate resilience policy and shall institutionalize whole-of-government,
whole-of-society, and whole-of-nation approaches toward building disaster- and climate-resilient
communities. It shall serve as the central authority for multi-stakeholder coordination and shall
uphold principles of equity, inclusiveness, transparency, and anticipatory governance.

        Provided, That in cases of human-induced disasters, the Department of the Interior and
Local Government (DILG), Department of National Defense (DND), Department of Social
Welfare and Development (DSWD), and other relevant agencies, including the Armed Forces of
the Philippines (AFP), Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP), Philippine National Police (PNP),
Philippine Coast Guard (PCG), and the Office of Civil Defense (OCD), shall continue to perform
their respective mandates:

        Provided, further, That the Department shall remain the lead agency in coordinating all
recovery and rehabilitation efforts, including those arising from complex emergencies and hybrid
disasters.

        Notwithstanding the foregoing, the powers and functions granted under this Act shall be
without prejudice to the authority of the President to directly intervene in responding to disasters,
emergencies, or other crises of national concern, including those arising from pandemics, hybrid
threats, or other systemic risks.

       SEC. 5. Powers and Functions. – To accomplish its mandate, the Department shall
exercise the following powers and perform the following functions:

           (a) Formulate, implement, and periodically update the National Climate Resilience
               Framework (NCRF), which shall guide national and local policy direction, planning
               priorities, and institutional mechanisms for disaster risk reduction, climate
               adaptation, and recovery. The NCRF shall be harmonized with existing
               development, climate, and public health frameworks.
           (b) Create, operationalize, and regularly update the National Risk Register and
               National Risk Atlas, which shall consolidate hazard, exposure, and vulnerability
               data, including climate and emerging risks, and provide spatial and temporal
               projections to inform planning, investment, and public awareness.
(c) Establish and operate the National Disaster Response Coordination Center
    (NDRCC), which shall serve as the central incident management and emergency
    operations hub of the government, operating under a standardized Incident
    Command System (ICS) structure during disasters and other complex emergencies.
(d) Operationalize predictive, risk-informing and anticipatory systems, including but
    not limited to Digital Twin Technology, interoperable early warning systems, real-
    time hazard monitoring, and dynamic risk analytics, to enhance foresight, simulate
    hazard impacts, support operational decisions, and improve readiness at all levels;
(e) Standardize, professionalize, and certify the national disaster risk management
    workforce, in collaboration with the Civil Service Commission (CSC),
    Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and Technical Education and Skills
    Development Authority (TESDA), including the creation of a national
    qualifications framework, training programs, and resilience career pathways;
(f) Monitor and enforce compliance by Local Government Units (LGUs) with national
    resilience benchmarks through the National Climate Resilience Scorecard, and
    assess institutional capacities and outcomes using a Local Resilience Performance
    Index, with performance directly linked to budgetary support and incentive
    mechanisms;
(g) Establish and supervise Local Climate Resilience Offices (LCROs) in all provinces,
    cities, and municipalities, ensuring full-time, trained, and accredited personnel with
    permanent plantilla positions dedicated to disaster risk governance, and oversee the
    nationwide accreditation and continuing development of such offices;
(h) Design, administer, and regulate disaster risk financing instruments, including the
    National Resilience Fund, the issuance of Resilience Bonds, participation in risk
    pooling mechanisms, and other innovative financial tools, in close coordination
    with the Department of Finance (DOF), National Economic and Development
    Authority (NEDA), and other concerned agencies;
(i) Operate and manage the Resilience Innovation Challenge Fund (RICF) to support
    research, design, pilot-testing, and scaling of innovative, localized, and science-
    based resilience solutions and indigenous risk reduction practices, including the
    conduct of an annual national competition for community-driven innovations;
(j) Institutionalize Public–Private–People Partnerships (4Ps) in disaster governance,
    including:
      (i)    The mandatory development and registration of Business Continuity Plans
             from private sector entities operating critical infrastructure;
     (ii)    Integration of civil society organizations, academia, faith-based groups, and
             community-based institutions in preparedness, response, recovery, and
             resilience planning;
    (iii)    Risk transparency and disclosure requirements for private sector actors
             based on the National Risk Register and relevant hazard data.
(k) Coordinate and lead the conduct of national multi-agency simulation exercises,
    resilience audits, and full-scale drills simulating compound and cascading hazards,
    in coordination with the Department of National Defense (DND), Department of
    Health (DOH), Department of Transportation (DOTr), Armed Forces of the
    Philippines (AFP), Philippine Coast Guard (PCG), Bureau of Fire Protection
    (BFP), and Philippine National Police (PNP), among others;
           (l) Uphold the principles of disaster justice, equity, and inclusion in all disaster risk
               governance programs, policies, and investments, and ensure that targeted assistance
               and protective mechanisms are in place for the most vulnerable sectors, including
               persons with disabilities, the elderly, children, women, indigenous peoples, and
               impoverished communities;
           (m) Coordinate the mainstreaming of climate resilience education in formal and non-
               formal education systems, including the integration of disaster risk reduction and
               climate adaptation content into school curricula, professional education, technical-
               vocational training, and community-based learning in partnership with the
               Department of Education (DepEd), CHED, and the media sector;
           (n) Perform such other acts and assume such responsibilities as may be necessary to
               implement this Act, or as may be assigned by the President, particularly in cases of
               national emergencies, complex disasters, systemic risks, or emerging threats that
               require centralized action and unified command.

       SEC. 6. National Climate Resilience Framework (NCRF). – The Department shall
formulate, implement, monitor, and periodically update a National Climate Resilience Framework
(NCRF), which shall serve as the comprehensive, science- and data-informed strategy to guide all
public and private stakeholders in strengthening national and local resilience to all forms of
hazards. It shall serve as the central instrument of policy and action for the prevention, mitigation,
preparedness, response, recovery, and adaptation efforts of the State.

        The NCRF shall be integrated and aligned with the Philippine Development Plan, the
National Climate Change Action Plan, the National Security Strategy, the National Adaptation
Plan, the Sustainable Development Goals, the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction, and
other applicable international instruments to which the Philippines is a signatory.

       The Department shall exercise the following powers and functions in relation to the
National Climate Resilience Framework:

           (a) Establish and maintain a National Risk Register and Risk Atlas containing updated,
               multi-hazard risk assessments based on exposure, vulnerability, adaptive capacity,
               and scenario modeling, in coordination with relevant national agencies, local
               government units, scientific institutions, and other stakeholders;
           (b) Define national resilience goals and targets for all levels of government, sectors,
               and institutions, and prescribe standardized performance benchmarks and
               indicators through a Local Resilience Performance Index (LRPI), which shall be
               linked to budget allocations, resource access, and program support;
           (c) Institutionalize risk-informed decision-making across all national and local
               planning systems, including land use, infrastructure development, procurement,
               budgeting, and investment approvals, through the mandatory integration of disaster
               and climate risk assessments in all plans, programs, and policies;
           (d) Operationalize a digital, interoperable, and real-time decision-support platform
               leveraging digital twin technology, geospatial data, and dynamic forecasting
               models to enhance situational awareness, early warning, and anticipatory
               governance;
           (e) Prescribe the standards, protocols, and compliance mechanisms for the
               establishment and operation of Local Climate Resilience Offices (LCROs),
               including minimum technical staffing, mandatory training and certification,
               institutional planning systems, and interagency coordination functions;
           (f) Institutionalize the National Climate Resilience Scorecard to assess and report the
               performance of local government units, national agencies, public institutions, and
               private sector entities in risk reduction, preparedness, early warning, response,
               recovery, and adaptation, with results to be disclosed annually and used for
               benchmarking, compliance, and resource prioritization;
           (g) Promote convergence among national agencies, LGUs, academic and research
               institutions, civil society organizations, and the private sector in implementing
               resilience strategies and shared responsibilities, through formal coordination
               protocols and joint resilience programs;
           (h) Manage and oversee the implementation of financing instruments in support of the
               NCRF, including the National Resilience Fund, Resilience Bonds, pooled risk
               financing, climate risk insurance schemes, and performance-based resilience
               budgeting mechanisms;
           (i) Mandate and monitor the integration of resilience education, public awareness
               campaigns, and community preparedness training in schools, workplaces, and
               localities to strengthen the overall resilience of the population; and
           (j) Establish mechanisms for the annual review, multi-sectoral evaluation, and
               adaptive learning of the NCRF to ensure its responsiveness to emerging hazards,
               scientific advancements, institutional innovations, and local context-specific needs.

        The Department shall issue the necessary rules, guidelines, and directives to operationalize
the National Climate Resilience Framework across all branches of the executive, local government
units, government-owned and controlled corporations, and other public and private institutions.

        SEC. 7. Integrated Resilience Planning and Governance Instruments. – The Department
shall develop and operationalize an integrated suite of resilience instruments, which shall serve as
core tools in the implementation of the National Climate Resilience Framework (NCRF). These
instruments shall include:

           (a) National Risk Register and Risk Atlas – The Department shall maintain and
               regularly update a comprehensive National Risk Register and Risk Atlas that shall:
                  (1) Identify, map, and classify all known and emerging hazards, vulnerabilities,
                       and exposure levels nationwide;
                  (2) Include data from:
                         (i)  Geological, hydrological, meteorological, biological, and
                              technological hazards;
                        (ii)  Sectoral vulnerability data (e.g., housing, critical infrastructure,
                              health, education, and economy);
                       (iii)  Population demographics including age, gender, disability, income,
                              and other social indicators;
                       (iv)   Localized risk assessments conducted by LGUs and academic
                              institutions;
            (v)     Climate models, sea-level rise projections, and extreme weather
                    forecasts;
        (3) Serve as the primary planning tool for national and local disaster and
            climate risk assessments, resource allocation, and investment programming;
        (4) Be updated at least every three (3) years, or as necessary based on new
            scientific evidence, and shall be accessible to LGUs, national agencies,
            and the public, subject to security protocols.
(b) Digital Twin Technology and Predictive Risk Analytics – The Department shall
    develop and apply Digital Twin Technology to create dynamic, digital simulations
    of key physical environments, infrastructure systems, and urban regions to:
        (1) Model hazard scenarios including earthquake impacts, flooding, volcanic
            activity, pandemics, and compound climate risks;
        (2) Support scenario-based planning for preparedness, evacuation,
            infrastructure resilience, and resource prepositioning;
        (3) Integrate real-time data from satellites, ground sensors, mobile networks,
            and local monitoring systems
        (4) Facilitate collaborative simulations among government agencies, utilities,
            LGUs, and private operators for coordinated response and continuity
            planning.
(c) Pre-Disaster Recovery Planning (PDRP) – The Department shall institutionalize
    Pre-Disaster Recovery Planning at the national and local levels. PDRP shall:
        (1) Identify post-disaster recovery priorities, timelines, and agency
            responsibilities before disaster events occur;
        (2) Include baseline data on infrastructure, economic activities, critical
            services, land use, housing stock, and social vulnerability;
        (3) Establish financial planning strategies and cost estimates for rapid recovery
            phases;
        (4) Require the inclusion of recovery scenarios in LGU DRRM plans and
            mainstreaming in development planning processes;
        (5) Be aligned with sectoral resilience targets and regional development
            strategies.
(d) Resilience Bonds and Risk Financing Mechanisms – To promote risk-sensitive
    investments and sustainable disaster financing, the Department shall, in
    coordination with the Department of Finance (DOF), the National Economic and
    Development Authority (NEDA), and other relevant agencies:
        (1) Develop and manage Resilience Bonds to incentivize infrastructure and
            service providers to reduce disaster risk;
        (2) Expand participation in regional risk pools and insurance mechanisms;
        (3) Support the creation of catastrophe bonds and climate risk insurance for
            LGUs and vulnerable sectors;
        (4) Use risk models and cost-benefit data from the National Risk Register to
            determine coverage thresholds and policy triggers;
        (5) Report on the performance and utilization of resilience financing tools
            annually to Congress.
(e) The National Climate Resilience Scorecard – To monitor and evaluate the
    performance of LGUs and agencies in climate resilience, the Department shall:
        (1) Develop a tiered scoring system based on LGU compliance with resilience
            indicators including planning, budgeting, capacity-building, risk
            communication, infrastructure resilience, and public participation;
        (2) Link scorecard ratings to performance-based financing, grants, and access
            to national assistance;
        (3) Require LGUs to submit annual resilience reports subject to validation by
            third-party technical panels;
        (4) Publish aggregated results to promote transparency and inter-LGU
            benchmarking.
(f) Resilience Innovation Challenge Fund – The Department shall establish an annual
    Resilience Innovation Challenge Fund to support novel solutions and local
    innovations in DRRM. The Fund shall:
        (1) Be awarded through a competitive process to LGUs, civil society
            organizations, academic institutions, and the private sector;
        (2) Prioritize projects addressing data gaps, early warning innovations, nature-
            based solutions, community resilience, and inclusive DRRM approaches;
        (3) Include a special track for youth- and women-led innovations and
            technologies for underserved communities.
(g) Resilience Procurement Framework – The Department shall prescribe and enforce
    a national Resilience Procurement Framework to ensure that all government
    procurement processes prioritize disaster- and climate-resilient goods, services, and
    infrastructure. This framework shall include design standards, lifecycle risk
    evaluations, supplier criteria, and adaptive performance benchmarks, in
    coordination with the Government Procurement Policy Board (GPPB) and relevant
    agencies.
(h) Localized Resilience Hubs – The Department shall establish Localized Resilience
    Hubs at the provincial and highly urbanized city levels to serve as decentralized
    centers for coordinating multi-stakeholder DRRM and climate adaptation efforts.
    Each hub shall:
        (1) Operate as a platform for data-sharing and local early warning systems;
        (2) Provide technical assistance and training to LGUs and communities;
        (3) Facilitate integrated emergency response and recovery planning;
        (4) Serve as innovation nodes for testing and scaling community-driven
            resilience solutions.
(i) Green Resilience Corridors – In coordination with the Department of Environment
    and Natural Resources (DENR), Department of Public Works and Highways
    (DPWH), and relevant agencies, the Department shall plan and implement Green
    Resilience Corridors that integrate natural buffers, ecological infrastructure, and
    engineered protection systems to mitigate flood, heat, and landslide risks while
    enhancing biodiversity, carbon sequestration, and local livelihoods. These corridors
    shall be incorporated into national and local land use plans and supported through
    ecological restoration programs and climate finance mechanisms.
(j) Disaster Justice and Inclusion Mechanisms – The Department shall institutionalize
    disaster justice and inclusion protocols to ensure that all policies, programs, and
    decision-making processes equitably benefit and protect vulnerable and
    marginalized groups, including but not limited to women, children, senior citizens,
               persons with disabilities, indigenous peoples, and the urban poor. These
               mechanisms shall include grievance redress systems, participatory decision-making
               structures, and mandatory equity audits of resilience investments.
           (k) Resilience Education and Capacity Development Framework – In partnership with
               the Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher Education (CHED),
               Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and relevant
               institutions, the Department shall implement a national Resilience Education and
               Capacity Development Framework. This shall mainstream disaster risk reduction,
               climate adaptation, and systems thinking into all levels of formal and non-formal
               education, vocational training, and continuing professional development.

       The Department shall promulgate the necessary implementing rules, technical standards,
and performance indicators for the effective operationalization of these instruments, and shall
review and update them at least once every three (3) years in light of emerging risks and
innovations.

       SEC. 8. Organizational Structure. – The Department shall be composed of a Central

(LCROs), with such Bureaus, Divisions, Services, and Units as may be necessary for the efficient
and effective discharge of its powers and functions.

subject to confirmation by the Commission on Appointments. The Secretary shall have authority
and supervision over the entire Department and shall be responsible for the execution of national
policies on climate resilience.

         SEC. 9. Local Implementation and Support Mechanisms. – The Department shall

               liaison with the Department.

               Local Climate Resilience Plans (LCRPs) integrating multi-hazard risk assessments,
               climate projections, and socio-economic vulnerability indicators. The LCRPs shall

               guidance by the Department.
           (c) Localized Resilience Hubs – Provincial and highly urbanized city governments

               academic institutions, civil society, and the private sector. These hubs shall serve

                   (1) Community-based training and simulation;

                   (4) Coordination of post-disaster recovery activities and public-private

               performance of LGUs and LDROs through the Local Resilience Performance Index
               (LRPI), and shall establish a system of incentives, compliance enforcement, and
               capacity interventions based on annual performance ratings. Results shall be
               published and used to inform national resource allocation.

        SEC. 11. Emergency and Post-Disaster Management.. – The Department shall exercise
leadership, supervision, and coordination over all phases of emergency operations and post-
disaster management in accordance with the National ClimateResilience Framework (NCRF). It
shall ensure the timely, efficient, and accountable delivery of services, deployment of resources,
and execution of recovery plans, in coordination with relevant national agencies, local government
units, and partner sectors.

         In the event of a disaster or imminent threat thereof, the Department shall activate the
National Emergency Operations Center (NEOC), which shall serve as the unified command center
for strategic coordination, operational decision-making, situational analysis, resource tracking, and
interagency tasking. The NEOC shall operate in accordance with the Incident Command System
(ICS) and national response protocols.

        To ensure anticipatory and adaptive emergency governance, the Department shall establish
the following:

           (a) National Emergency Operations Center (NEOC). – The Department shall operate
               and maintain a 24/7 National Emergency Operations Center (NEOC), which shall
               serve as the central command and control facility for situational monitoring,
               interagency coordination, decision support, and deployment management during
               emergencies. The NEOC shall be supported by Regional Emergency Operations
               Centers (REOCs) and linked to Local Emergency Operations Centers (LEOCs)
               established by LGUs.
           (b) Incident Command System (ICS) – This shall be the standardized, scalable, and
               modular structure for tactical and operational response at the incident level. The
               Department shall ensure that all national agencies and local government units are
               trained, certified, and equipped to deploy and operate under ICS protocols. It shall
               issue harmonized guidelines on chain of command, resource coordination,
               emergency communications, and field operations integration.
           (c) Multi-Hazard Contingency and Recovery Planning – The Department shall lead the
               development, updating, and testing of Multi-Hazard Contingency Plans (MHCPs),
               Pre-Disaster Recovery Plans (PDRPs), and Emergency Support Functions (ESFs),
               which shall be aligned with the NCRF and updated based on risk forecasts and
               seasonal threats.
           (d) Resource Mobilization and Logistics – This protocol developed by the Department
               shall include pre-positioning of critical supplies, coordination with humanitarian
               actors, digital inventory systems, and real-time tracking of deployment. Strategic
               partnerships with the private sector, military, and international partners shall be
               pursued to enhance surge capacity and ensure access to remote and underserved
               areas.
           (e) Emergency Response Cluster Activation – In coordination with other government
               agencies, the Department shall activate sector-specific response clusters (e.g.,
               health, shelter, food security, water and sanitation, education, protection, transport,
               energy, and communications) based on the nature and scope of the emergency.
               Cluster leads shall be designated in advance and response plans shall be aligned
               with the National Climate Resilience Framework.
           (f) Emergency Communications and Information Management – The Department shall
               establish a unified emergency communications protocol, in collaboration with the
               Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), to ensure
               real-time data sharing, secure information flows, public alerting, and media
               engagement. This shall include provisions for accessible early warnings,
               community-level advisories, and counter-disinformation mechanisms.
           (g) Technical Assistance to Local Government Units – During disaster situations, the
               Department shall provide direct technical assistance, surge support, and rapid
               response teams to affected local governments. It shall coordinate and, when
               necessary, assume command over local operations in cases of widespread
               disruption, incapacitation, or declared states of calamity, in accordance with
               constitutional and statutory limits.
           (h) Recovery and Resilient Rehabilitation – The Department shall lead the conduct of
               post-disaster needs assessments, the formulation of recovery plans, and the
               implementation of resilient rehabilitation programs. These shall be aligned with
               pre-disaster recovery plans, integrate climate and disaster risk reduction measures,
               and observe the principles of building back better, environmental sustainability,
               social inclusion, and institutional strengthening. The Department shall monitor the
               progress of recovery programs, including infrastructure restoration, housing
               reconstruction, livelihood recovery, psychosocial support, and local systems
               rehabilitation.

       All government agencies, instrumentalities, and local government units shall comply with
the emergency protocols, recovery plans, and coordination directives issued by the Department in
accordance with this Act.

       SEC. 12. Recovery and Resilient Rehabilitation. – The Department shall lead and
coordinate national efforts in recovery and resilient rehabilitation following disasters,
emergencies, or complex crisis events, ensuring that such efforts are inclusive, risk-informed, and
aligned with long-term development and resilience goals.

           (a) Pre-Disaster Recovery Planning (PDRP) – The Department shall institutionalize
               PDRP as a mandatory process at the national and local levels. PDRPs shall define
               sectoral recovery strategies, roles and responsibilities, funding arrangements, and
               implementation timelines prior to the occurrence of disasters. These plans shall be
               integrated into Local Climate Resilience Plans and the National Climate Resilience
               Framework.
           (b) Post-Disaster Needs Assessment (PDNA) – The Department shall develop and
               maintain standardized methodologies and technical protocols for conducting a
               comprehensive PDNA, in coordination with concerned national agencies, LGUs,
               and development partners. PDNAs shall assess physical damages, economic losses,
               social impacts, and recovery needs, serving as the basis for resilient rehabilitation
               programming and budget allocations.
           (c) Resilient Rehabilitation Planning – Rehabilitation plans shall prioritize building
               back better principles, integrating climate and disaster risk reduction measures,
               nature-based solutions, resilient infrastructure design, and inclusive reconstruction
               programs. The Department shall ensure that recovery strategies reduce future
               vulnerabilities and contribute to systemic resilience.
           (d) Recovery Financing and Risk Transfer – The Department shall manage and
               coordinate access to domestic and international recovery financing mechanisms,
               including the National Resilience Fund, resilience bonds, catastrophe risk
               insurance, and public-private recovery partnerships. Recovery budgets shall be
               informed by PDNA findings, subject to public disclosure and oversight.
           (e) Community and Stakeholder Participation – Recovery and rehabilitation efforts
               shall be designed and implemented with the meaningful participation of affected
               communities, civil society organizations, vulnerable groups, the private sector, and
               local governments. Mechanisms for inclusive consultation, grievance redress, and
               participatory monitoring shall be established.
           (f) Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning – The Department shall establish a system
               for monitoring, evaluating, and documenting all recovery and rehabilitation
               programs to ensure transparency, accountability, and adaptive learning. Evaluation
               findings shall inform future policy reforms and updates to the National Climate
               Resilience Framework.
           (g) Inter-Agency Coordination – The Department shall ensure the effective
               coordination of line agencies involved in recovery, including but not limited to
               infrastructure, housing, health, agriculture, social welfare, education, and
               environment sectors. Sectoral recovery efforts shall be harmonized through joint
               planning mechanisms and integrated implementation protocols.

        SEC 13. Resilience Financing and Resource Mobilization. – The Department shall
establish and oversee a comprehensive, transparent, and accountable resilience financing system
that mobilizes, allocates, and sustains resources to support national and local disaster risk
reduction, preparedness, response, recovery, and long-term resilience-building initiatives. It shall
implement the following capacity development programs and resilience education initiatives that
are inclusive, context-sensitive, gender-responsive, and rights-based, with specific provisions for
meaningful participation of vulnerable and marginalized groups:

           (a) National Resilience Fund – There is hereby created a National Resilience Fund, to
               be administered by the Department, and funded through annual General
               Appropriations, official development assistance, international grants, private sector
               contributions, and other lawful sources. The Fund shall finance programs and
               projects aligned with the National Climate Resilience Framework, including
              capacity development, early warning systems, resilient infrastructure, innovation
              initiatives, and post-disaster recovery.
          (b) Resilience Bonds and Risk Transfer Instruments – The Department shall design,
              promote, and operationalize resilience bonds, catastrophe-linked instruments, and
              other risk transfer mechanisms to incentivize pre-disaster risk reduction
              investments and ensure rapid access to recovery financing. These instruments shall
              be developed in collaboration with the Department of Finance, the Insurance
              Commission, and the private financial sector, and may be made available to LGUs
              and public infrastructure agencies subject to performance criteria.
          (c) Performance-Based Budgeting – The Department shall develop and implement a
              performance-based resilience budgeting system, wherein allocations to national
              agencies and LGUs shall be guided by their compliance with risk-informed
              planning standards, Local Resilience Performance Index scores, and
              implementation track records. The Department shall issue guidelines and monitor
              adherence in coordination with the Department of Budget and Management
              (DBM).
          (d) Resilience Investment Certification – No major public infrastructure or
              development project shall be approved unless it is certified as climate- and disaster-
              resilient by the Department, based on multi-hazard risk assessments, sustainability
              appraisals, and resilience audit protocols. This provision shall apply to projects
              funded by national government, official development assistance, and public-private
              partnerships.
          (e) Public-Private and Multisectoral Financing Partnerships – The Department shall
              facilitate financing partnerships with private sector institutions, development
              banks, and philanthropic organizations to co-finance resilience-building efforts,
              including innovation challenges, digital infrastructure, localized hubs, and green
              resilience corridors. Incentives such as tax reliefs, co-branding, and streamlined
              procurement may be authorized through implementing rules.
          (f) Resource Pooling and Inter-LGU Mechanisms – Subject to appropriate safeguards,
              the Department shall support the creation of regional or inter-LGU resilience
              financing mechanisms, such as pooled insurance schemes, joint investment
              platforms, and mutual aid systems, to address common vulnerabilities and enhance
              shared capacities.
          (g) Financial Transparency and Oversight – All resilience funds and financing
              instruments shall be subject to rigorous transparency and accountability
              mechanisms, including digital disclosure systems, third-party audits, and citizen
              monitoring platforms. The Department shall submit an annual Resilience Financing
              Report to Congress, detailing allocations, disbursements, outcomes, and lessons
              learned.

                                         ARTICLE III
                                      FINAL PROVISIONS


        SEC. 15. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Accountability for Resilience. – The Department
shall establish and institutionalize a national system of monitoring, evaluation, and accountability
for climate resilience, anchored on the National Climate Resilience Framework (NCRF) and
guided by principles of transparency, performance-based governance, and continuous
improvement, such as the following:

           (a) National Resilience Monitoring and Evaluation System – The Department shall
               develop and institutionalize a National Resilience Monitoring and Evaluation
               System (NRMES), which shall measure, track, and report on progress in risk
               reduction, preparedness, early action, response effectiveness, recovery quality, and
               long-term resilience outcomes. The NRMES shall be data-driven, results-oriented,
               interoperable with other government information systems, and capable of
               disaggregating data by location, sector, gender, age, disability, and socioeconomic
               status.
           (b) Local Resilience Performance Index (LRPI) – In coordination with the Department
               of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the Department shall implement the
               Local Resilience Performance Index (LRPI) as a standardized evaluation tool for
               all provinces, cities, and municipalities. The LRPI shall assess LGU performance
               in planning, financing, implementing, and institutionalizing DRRM and climate
               adaptation programs, with results to be published annually. Performance shall be
               linked to eligibility for access to national resilience funds, technical assistance, and
               program incentives.
           (c) National Climate Resilience Scorecard – The Department shall maintain and
               publicly disclose an annual National Climate Resilience Scorecard, which shall
               synthesize the country’s overall resilience status based on indicators aligned with
               the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction, the Sustainable Development
               Goals (SDGs), and other international commitments. The Scorecard shall include
               sectoral performance (e.g., health, infrastructure, education), institutional
               effectiveness, community resilience metrics, and critical infrastructure resilience.
           (d) Compliance, Audit, and Enforcement Mechanisms – The Department shall establish
               and operationalize compliance protocols and audit systems to ensure adherence to
               resilience standards, procurement guidelines, risk-informed planning mandates,
               and emergency response protocols by national agencies, LGUs, and private sector
               entities involved in resilience programs. It shall conduct regular field audits,
               independent evaluations, and third-party verifications, and may recommend the
               withholding of funds or referral to appropriate authorities for violations, gross
               negligence, or willful non-compliance.
           (e) Citizen Feedback, Grievance Redress, and Participatory Monitoring – A national
               grievance redress and citizen feedback mechanism shall be instituted to allow
               individuals, communities, and civil society organizations to report deficiencies,
               suggest improvements, or file complaints concerning resilience programs, projects,
               and disaster response actions. The Department shall ensure that mechanisms are
               accessible, inclusive, and responsive, and that they inform adaptive changes in
               policies and programs.
           (f) Transparency, Open Data, and Public Accountability – All performance data, audit
               results, resilience scores, risk maps, and investment tracking reports generated by
               the Department shall be made publicly accessible through a digital transparency
               portal. The portal shall include open data dashboards, geospatial risk overlays, real-
               time disaster response tracking, and summary reports to ensure public
               accountability and informed decision-making by all stakeholders.
           (g) Periodic Independent Review – An independent multi-sectoral commission
               composed of representatives from the academe, civil society, the private sector, and
               international partners shall be convened at least once every three (3) years to
               conduct an external review of the Department’s effectiveness, institutional
               integrity, and contribution to national resilience. Recommendations of the review
               commission shall be submitted to Congress, the Office of the President, and made
               available to the public.

       The Department shall issue detailed guidelines, indicators, reporting protocols, and
accountability standards to implement this Section, in coordination with oversight bodies including
the Commission on Audit (COA), National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA), and
the Civil Service Commission (CSC).

        SEC. 16. Resilience Research, Foresight, and Knowledge Management. – To advance
science-based, anticipatory, and adaptive resilience governance, the Department shall establish
and institutionalize a national system for resilience-oriented research, foresight, and knowledge
integration, establishing the following strategies:

           (a) The Department shall establish a dedicated Center for Climate Resilience Research
               and Innovation, which shall serve as the lead implementing and coordinating body
               for resilience-related research, pilot testing of technologies, local innovation
               programs, and knowledge dissemination. The Center shall facilitate collaborative
               projects with universities, local government units, scientific institutions, civil
               society organizations, and private sector partners, and shall support the translation
               of research into actionable policies and local solutions.
           (b) The Department, through the Center for Climate Resilience Research and
               Innovation, shall develop and implement a rolling five-year National Resilience
               Research and Innovation Agenda in coordination with the Department of Science
               and Technology (DOST), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), academic
               institutions, and international knowledge networks. The Agenda shall prioritize the
               following areas:
                  (1) Hazard mapping, scenario modeling, and impact forecasting using
                       emerging technologies such as Artificial Intelligence (AI), remote sensing,
                       and high-performance computing;
                  (2) Locally adapted climate and disaster risk reduction technologies and
                       indigenous knowledge systems;
                  (3) Nature-based solutions and green infrastructure for disaster mitigation and
                       adaptation;
                  (4) Resilient infrastructure design, retrofitting methods, and materials science;
                  (5) Community-based risk governance, social protection models, and equity-
                       centered approaches;
                  (6) Behavioral and systems science for early warning uptake, preparedness, and
                       recovery;
                  (7) Innovation in financial instruments and insurance models for risk transfer.
          (c) The Department shall develop and maintain a centralized and interoperable
              Resilience Knowledge Platform to serve as a public repository of resilience data,
              research findings, learning modules, analytics tools, and best practices. The
              Platform shall promote open access, enable evidence-based policymaking, and
              support local governments, civil society, and the private sector in enhancing risk
              governance capacities.
          (d) The Department shall establish and administer a Resilience Innovation Challenge
              Fund, which shall provide grants, technical support, and incubation assistance to
              research institutions, startups, social enterprises, and grassroots innovators
              pursuing solution-driven initiatives aligned with the National Climate Resilience
              Framework. The Fund shall prioritize high-impact innovations capable of local
              implementation and scalable adaptation.
          (e) A distinct technical unit within the Department shall be designated or created as the
              Center for Resilience Futures and Strategic Foresight. It shall conduct scenario
              planning, long-term systems modeling, risk horizon scanning, and simulation-
              based policy testing to inform anticipatory governance. The Center shall work in
              collaboration with international foresight institutions and local research groups.
          (f) The Department shall ensure that resilience-related research outputs, innovations,
              and knowledge products are converted into practical guidance materials, toolkits,
              planning aids, and operational templates for use by LGUs, local communities, and
              institutions. Emphasis shall be placed on usability, gender-sensitivity, cultural
              appropriateness, and support for marginalized sectors.

        SEC. 17. Resilience Data Governance and Digital Infrastructure. – The Department
shall develop and implement a comprehensive Resilience Data Governance Framework, and invest
in modern digital infrastructure in coordination with the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), National Mapping
and Resource Information Authority (NAMRIA), and relevant government agencies. It shall lead
the following initiatives:

   (a) National Resilience Data Ecosystem – The Department shall lead the establishment of an
       integrated, interoperable, and secure National Resilience Data Ecosystem that shall
       consolidate multi-hazard risk assessments, vulnerability data, exposure mapping, early
       warning indicators, infrastructure audits, and real-time disaster analytics. The Ecosystem
       shall be standardized, geospatially enabled, and capable of disaggregation by gender, age,
       disability, income, and location to support inclusive, evidence-based decision-making.
   (b) Resilience Digital Twin Platform – The Department shall develop and operationalize a
       Resilience Digital Twin Platform—an advanced simulation environment replicating
       physical, environmental, and socioeconomic systems of the country. The Platform shall
       support predictive modeling, disaster scenario testing, recovery planning, infrastructure
       stress simulations, and optimization of preparedness and mitigation investments at both
       national and local levels.
   (c) Data Standards, Sharing, and Privacy – The Department shall prescribe national data
       standards for resilience and risk governance, in consultation with relevant agencies and
       stakeholders. It shall issue protocols for secure data sharing, protection of personal and
       sensitive information, and interagency interoperability. All data collected and generated
       through Department programs shall comply with the provisions of the Data Privacy Act of
       2012 and other applicable laws.
   (d) Local Government Digital Enablement – The Department shall support LGUs in adopting
       and upgrading their own local risk databases, Geographic Information Systems (GIS),
       emergency operations digital tools, and cloud-based contingency and recovery planning
       systems. It shall provide technical assistance and capacity building to ensure that digital
       governance tools are usable, maintained, and integrated into local development planning
       processes.
   (e) Open Data and Public Access – The Department shall maintain a publicly accessible
       Resilience Open Data Portal which shall house non-sensitive data on hazards, risks,
       disaster impacts, resilience programs, infrastructure vulnerabilities, financing allocations,
       and real-time updates. This shall promote transparency, academic research, community
       engagement, and private sector participation in resilience-building efforts.

       SEC. 18. Congressional Oversight and Public Transparency. – To ensure institutional
accountability, performance integrity, and continued alignment with the national interest, the
Department shall be subject to robust congressional oversight and public transparency
mechanisms, such as:

   (a) Congressional Oversight Committee – A Joint Congressional Oversight Committee on
       Climate Resilience shall be constituted, composed of five (5) Members each from the
       Senate and the House of Representatives, with representation from the majority and
       minority. The committee shall monitor the implementation of this Act, assess Department
       performance, review the NCRF and major policies, and recommend legislative or
       budgetary amendments as needed.
   (b) Annual State of Climate Resilience Report – The Secretary of the Department shall submit
       to the President and Congress an Annual State of Climate Resilience Report, which shall
       include an integrated assessment of national and local resilience conditions,
       implementation progress, risk trends, major programs and expenditures, research
       outcomes, and the National Climate Resilience Scorecard. The report shall be published
       and made accessible to the public through the Department’s website and the Resilience
       Knowledge Platform.
   (c) Public Access and Transparency Measures – The Department shall ensure full disclosure
       of resilience-related procurement, project implementation status, risk financing utilization,
       audit reports, and resilience performance data. A Transparency and Accountability
       Dashboard shall be maintained on its website, linked to the Open Data Portal and integrated
       with relevant platforms such as the Government Procurement Policy Board (GPPB) and
       the Commission on Audit (COA).
   (d) Civil Society and Citizen Participation – The Department shall establish participatory
       monitoring mechanisms involving civil society organizations, academic institutions, local
       resilience councils, and grassroots representatives in the assessment of resilience program
       effectiveness. Regular citizen forums, resilience scorecard consultations, and grievance
       redress system reviews shall be institutionalized.

        SEC. 19. Penal Provisions. – Any person, whether natural or juridical, including public
officials, private contractors, or implementing partners, who commits any of the following acts in
violation of this Act shall be held administratively, civilly, and criminally liable, without prejudice
to other applicable laws:

           (a) Gross Negligence or Willful Non-Compliance – Any officer or employee of a
               government agency, local government unit, or private entity involved in the
               implementation of this Act who knowingly, deliberately, or with gross negligence
               fails to observe mandatory risk reduction, preparedness, response, or recovery
               standards; refuses to comply with lawful directives of the Department; or obstructs
               the implementation of approved resilience programs shall be liable for
               administrative sanctions including suspension, dismissal from service, forfeiture of
               benefits, and disqualification from public office, as appropriate under the Civil
               Service Law and other applicable rules.
           (b) Misuse of Resilience Funds – Any person who misappropriates, misuses, or diverts
               funds allocated for disaster risk reduction, emergency response, resilience building,
               or post-disaster recovery under this Act, whether from public or authorized private
               sources, shall be punished with imprisonment of not less than six (6) years but not
               more than twelve (12) years, and a fine equivalent to double the amount misused,
               without prejudice to the imposition of perpetual disqualification from holding
               public office or from participation in any government procurement program.
           (c) Falsification, Obstruction, or Manipulation of Data – Any person who falsifies,
               conceals, or deliberately manipulates risk data, resilience scorecards, monitoring
               reports, infrastructure vulnerability assessments, or audit findings, or obstructs
               authorized data collection and transparency mechanisms, shall be penalized with
               imprisonment of not less than four (4) years but not more than ten (10) years, and
               a fine not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000), in addition to
               administrative liability, if applicable.
           (d) Failure to Implement Mandatory Evacuation or Early Warning Protocols – Any
               responsible official who, without justifiable cause, fails to issue or enforce
               mandatory evacuation orders, early warnings, or other urgent protective measures,
               resulting in loss of life or serious injury, shall be criminally and administratively
               liable under relevant laws and the provisions of this Act.
           (e) Violation of Risk-Informed Procurement and Infrastructure Standards – Any
               individual or entity that knowingly violates procurement standards, building codes,
               or critical infrastructure resilience requirements prescribed by the Department shall
               be subject to administrative and civil liability, and may be barred from participating
               in future resilience or recovery-related projects for a period not less than five (5)
               years.
           (f) Obstruction of Emergency Operations – Any person who impedes or obstructs duly
               authorized emergency response operations, including the mobilization of supplies,
               personnel, equipment, or access to disaster sites, shall be liable for penalties under
               this Act and other applicable laws, including but not limited to those governing
               obstruction of justice, sabotage, and interference with lawful authority.

        The imposition of the above penalties shall be without prejudice to prosecution under the
Revised Penal Code, the Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act, the Government Procurement
Reform Act, the Anti-Red Tape Act, and other special penal laws, when such acts constitute
distinct offenses.

       SEC. 20. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current year’s available appropriations of agencies whose
functions are herein absorbed, transferred, or realigned to the Department of Climate Resilience.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        To ensure sufficient and sustained financial capacity, the Department shall be prioritized
in the allocation of national government resources for resilience-building, emergency
preparedness, response capability enhancement, and post-disaster recovery. The Department is
hereby authorized to receive, manage, and utilize grants, donations, loans, and other official
development assistance, domestic or foreign, subject to existing budgetary, accounting, and
auditing rules and regulations.

       The appropriations shall cover funding for:

           (a) The establishment and operation of the central, regional, and local offices of the
               Department;
           (b) The development and implementation of the National Climate Resilience
               Framework (NCRF), Integrated Planning and Governance Instruments, and
               resilience innovation programs;
           (c) The maintenance of the National Emergency Operations Center and affiliated
               Regional and Local Emergency Operations Centers;
           (d) The capitalization of the Resilience Innovation Challenge Fund, Resilience Bonds,
               and other resilience financing mechanisms;
           (e) Human resources, systems development, digital infrastructure, and capacity-
               building initiatives;
           (f) Pre-positioned logistical hubs, early warning technologies, and rapid response
               assets; and
           (g) All other activities necessary to fulfill the Department’s mandate under this Act.

        SEC. 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Climate Resilience, in coordination with the Department
of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the National Economic and Development Authority
(NEDA), the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and other relevant agencies, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

         The Implementing Rules and Regulations shall be developed through a consultative
process involving local government units, civil society organizations, the private sector, academic
institutions, and other stakeholders.

        SEC. 22. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected shall continue to be in full force and effect.

        SEC. 23. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

       SEC. 24. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.



Approved.
