AN ACT

STRENGTHENING THE INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK FOR ANIMAL
PROTECTION, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8485,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS “THE ANIMAL WELFARE ACT OF 1998,” AS AMENDED
BY REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10631, CREATING THE ANIMAL WELFARE BUREAU,
MANDATING LOCALIZED ENFORCEMENT DESKS, RESTRUCTURING
PENALTIES FOR REPEAT OFFENDERS, DEFINING AND CRIMINALIZING
AGGRAVATED ANIMAL CRUELTY, BARRING THE AVAILMENT OF PROBATION
FOR HEINOUS ACTS OF ABUSE, ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL ABUSER
REGISTRY, AND INSTITUTING MANDATES FOR DISASTER MANAGEMENT.

EXPLANATORY NOTE

The persistent prevalence of egregious animal abuse highlights the critical
limitations of our current animal welfare penal framework. Under the current regime
of Republic Act No. 8485, as amended by Republic Act No. 10631, the relatively light
penalties allow even the most sadistic abusers to escape incarceration by availi ng
themselves of probation under Presidential Decree No. 968. This loophole effectively
undermines the law’s deterrent value and leaves the public crying out for real judicial
accountability.

This measure directly addresses this gap by establishing a distinct statutory
classification for Aggravated Animal Cruelty. By enumerating specific, severe
malicious acts, including extreme physical torture, mass poisoning, and exploitation
for profit, and elevating their penalties to prisién mayor (six years and one day to
eight years), this Bill pushes these offenses beyond the six-year statutory threshold
for probation.

Consequently, this mandatory, non-probationary sentencing structure
ensures that both repeat offenders and first-time perpetrators of heinous animal

abuse face certain, actual confinement in a national penitentiary.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

AN ACT

STRENGTHENING THE INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK FOR ANIMAL

OTHERWISE KNOWN AS “THE ANIMAL WELFARE ACT OF 1998,” AS AMENDED

MANDATING LOCALIZED ENFORCEMENT DESKS, RESTRUCTURING

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Comprehensive Animal
Welfare Reform and Enforcement Act”.

SEC. 2. Transition of the Animal Welfare Division to the Animal Welfare
Bureau (AWB). — The Animal Welfare Division currently existing under the Bureau
of Animal Industry (BAI) is hereby dissolved. In its stead, there is hereby created an
Animal Welfare Bureau (AWB) as an attached agency under the Department of
Agriculture (DA).

The AWB shall be the primary regulatory, enforcement, and policy-making
body of the National Government concerning animal welfare. It shall be headed by a
Director and an Assistant Director, both of whom must be licensed veterinarians
with at least five (5) years of active experience in animal welfare advocacy or practice.

The AWB shall have its own distinct line-item budget in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA) to ensure regular funding for its enforcement. regulatory,
and public education mandates.

SEC. 3. Localization and Enforcement Desks. — To ensure the swift enforcement
of this Act at the grassroots level, the following localized enforcement mechanisms
are hereby mandated:

a) Philippine National Police (PNP) Animal Welfare Desks. — There shall be
established a dedicated Animal Welfare Desk in every city and municipal
police station across the country. The PNP, in coordination with the AWB,
===== [page 3] =====
b)

shall train specialized police officers to handle, investigate, and prosecute
violations of animal welfare laws.

Barangay Animal Welfare Units (BAWU). — Every Barangay, through its
Sangguniang Barangay, shall establish a Barangay Animal Welfare Unit
(BAWU) under the supervision of the Barangay Chairperson. The BAWU shall
be responsible for monitoring local pet ownership, managing localized,
humane stray populations through Trap-Neuter-Return (TNR) programs, and
serving as the first responder to local reports of animal abuse, neglect, or
abandonment.

Allocation of Funds. — Local Government Units (LGUs) are hereby
authorized and mandated to allocate a portion of their National Tax Allotment
(NTA) and local development funds for the operations, training, and
maintenance of their respective BAWUs and municipal shelters.

SEC. 4. Clear Institutional Mandates for Disasters. — Animal protection and
rescue protocols shall be systematically integrated into national and local disaster
risk management frameworks:

a)

Mandatory Evacuation Integration. — The National Disaster Risk Reduction
and Management Council (NDRRMC) and all Local DRRM Offices (LDRRMOs)
shall include companion animals, livestock, and working animals in all
mandatory evacuation protocols, disaster response mapping, and temporary
shelter planning.

Statutory Presumption of Criminal Neglect. — It shall be a prima facie case
of criminal neglect and endangerment under this Act for any owner,
custodian, or guardian to leave an animal chained, tethered, caged, or
otherwise restrained without an immediate and unassisted means of escape
during a declared State of Calamity or a mandatory evacuation order.

Penalty in Disasters. — If such restraint or abandonment during a disaster
results in the severe injury or agonizing death of the animal, the owner or
custodian shall be prosecuted under Section 6 of this Act, and the defense of
force majeure or act of God shall be legally inadmissible.

SEC. 5. Amendment of Section 9 of Republic Act No. 8485. Section 9 of Republic
Act No. 8485, as amended by Republic Act No. 10631, is hereby further amended to
read as follows:

“Section 9. Penalties for Standard Violations and Repeat Offenders.
— Any person who subjects any animal to cruelty, maltreatment, or
neglect not falling under the definition of Aggravated Animal Cruelty
under Section 9-A of this Act shall, upon conviction, suffer the
graduated penalties provided under Republic Act No. 10631.

Provided, however, that Repeat Offenders—defined as any person who
commits a violation of this Act after having been previously convicted
under a final judgment for any violation of RA 8485, RA 10631, or this
Act—shall be punished with the penalty of Imprisonment of Six (6)
Years and One (1} Day to Eight (8) Years (prisién mayor and a fine of
not less than Three Hundred Thousand Pesos (P300,000.00) but not
exceeding Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00).
===== [page 4] =====
In view of the minimum imposable penalty exceeding the six-year
statutory threshold, any person convicted as a repeat offender under
this Section shall be strictly and absolutely disqualified from applying
for or availing of the benefits of probation under Presidential Decree No.
968, otherwise known as The Probation Law of 1976’, as amended;
consequently, the offender shall serve the full term of imprisonment
continuously and mandatorily within a national penitentiary.”

SEC. 6. Insertion of a New Section 9-A to Republic Act No. 8485. — A new
section, designated as Section 9-A, is hereby inserted into Republic Act No. 8485, as
amended, to read as follows:

“Section 9-A. Aggravated Animal Cruelty. — The crime of animal
cruelty or maltreatment shall be classified as Aggravated, and shall
carry the non-probationary penalty of Imprisonment of Six (6) Years
and One (1) Day to Eight (8) Years (prisién mayor and a fine of not less
than Three Hundred Thousand Pesos (P300,000.00) but not exceeding
Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00), when committed under
any of the following specific instances, regardless of whether the
offender is a first-time or a repeat offender:

a) Extreme Physical Torture and Mutilation. — Where the animal
is subjected to prolonged, intentional physical pain, skinning,
burning, burning alive, chemical dousing, dismemberment. or the
severing of limbs without professional veterinary medical
justification;

b) Cruelty for Commercial Gain or Digital Distribution. — Where
the act of cruelty, torture, or killing is recorded, filmed, or
streamed for the purpose of commercial sale, online broadcasting,
or the creation and distribution of ‘crush’ videos and digital
content;

c) Sordid motive or extreme malice. — Where the crime is
committed with evident premeditation, or out of pure vengeance
against a pet owner, or as an act of domestic intimidation and
psychological abuse against a family member;

d) Cruelty Resulting in Agonizing Death. — Where the
maltreatment, severe starvation, or intentional withholding of

medical care directly results in a slow, agonizing, and protracted
death of the animal;

e) Vehicular Hit-and-Run and Abandonment. — Where the
offender, while operating a motor vehicle, strikes an animal and
intentionally flees the scene without rendering immediate aid,
transporting the animal to a veterinary facility, or reporting the
incident to the nearest Barangay Animal Welfare Unit (BAWU) or
police station, resulting in the animal’s severe injury, prolonged
suffering, or death;

f) Mass Cruelty and Poisoning. — Where the offender intentionally
poisons, kills, or severely harms multiple animals simultaneously
===== [page 5] =====
in a single course of conduct, such as the deliberate poisoning of
neighborhood strays or community pets;

g) Cruelty Committed by a Syndicate or Organized Group. —
Where the offense is planned, coordinated, or executed by an
organized group of three (3) or more individuals, including
organized dogfighting or horse fighting rings.

Any individual convicted of Aggravated Animal Cruelty under this
Section shail be strictly barred from the availment of probation under
Presidential Decree No. 968, otherwise known as The Probation Law of
1976’, as amended. The court shall have no statutory discretion to grant
probation, and the convict shall serve actual, mandatory time in a state
penitentiary, notwithstanding their status as a first-time offender.”

SEC. 7. Mandatory Blacklisting and the National Animal Abuser Registry
(NAAR). — Any person convicted by final judgment of animal cruelty, maltreatment,
severe neglect, abandonment, or unlawful killing under this Act shall suffer the
accessory penalty of a Mandatory Lifetime Ban on Animal Ownership. Such person
shall be legally barred from owning, harboring, breeding, fostering, or possessing
custody over any type of animal. For this purpose, the AWB shall establish, maintain,
and update a centralized, public, and searchable electronic database to be known as
the National Animal Abuser Registry (NAAR).

The NAAR shall contain the names and identifying details of all individuals convicted
of animal welfare violations. All pet shops, commercial breeders, agricultural sellers,
and animal shelters shall be legally mandated to cross-reference the NAAR prior to
finalizing any sale, adoption, or transfer of animal ownership. Any establishment that
transfers an animal to a blacklisted individual shall have its business license revoked
and face an administrative fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00).

SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Agriculture, in coordination with the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the Department of Justice
(DOJ), the PNP, and accredited animal welfare Non-Government Organizations
(NGOs), shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
implementation.

SEC. 9. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. — Ali laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, including
relevant sections of Republic Act No. 8485 and Republic Act No. 10631, are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) davs after its

publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
