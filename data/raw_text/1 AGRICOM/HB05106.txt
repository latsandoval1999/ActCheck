                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The agriculture and fisheries sector remain a vital yet persistently underperforming pillar of
the Philippine economy. Despite our natural resource base and a large rural labor force, the sector’s
contribution to national growth continues to lag due to structural weaknesses in governance, policy
fragmentation, and underinvestment. In 2024, agriculture contracted by 2.2%, pulling down the
country’s overall economic growth to just 5.2%, well below target.

      In early 2025, the government declared a national “food security emergency” in response to
soaring rice prices—an alarming development in a country historically dependent on rice for daily
sustenance. While importation and tariff cuts were deployed as immediate measures, they
underscore deeper issues: over-reliance on imports, a weakened regulatory framework, and a farm
system that continues to underperform despite budgetary support.

       This situation is not new. Reputable economists and agricultural experts have long pointed
to institutional dysfunction at the root of the sector’s woes. The late Dr. Eliseo R. Ponce, a former
president of Visayas State University and a former Executive Director of the Department of
Agriculture’s Bureau of Agricultural Research, conducted a comprehensive review of the
agriculture bureaucracy. He identified critical issues such as over-centralization, fragmented
mandates, politicized and commodity-skewed budgeting, and the lack of accountability and
capacity support for local government units.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Congressional Commission on
Agriculture (AgriCom) Act.”

      Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote rural
development and food security through a modern, resilient, inclusive, and sustainable agriculture
and fisheries sector. Recognizing that institutional weaknesses and governance inefficiencies have
long hindered agricultural growth, the State shall pursue a whole-of-government and whole-of-
society approach to reforming the agriculture bureaucracy.

     Section 3. Creation of the Commission. – There is hereby created a Congressional Commission
on Agriculture, hereinafter referred to as the "Commission," to undertake a national review and
recommend reforms to strengthen Philippine agriculture and fisheries.

  Section 4. Objectives. – AgriCom shall be created to:

        (a) Conduct a comprehensive assessment of the current state of Philippine
            agriculture and fisheries;

        (b) Identify institutional, regulatory, structural, and policy barriers to productivity
            and inclusivity;
       (c) Recommend a blueprint of targeted, measurable, and time-bound reforms,
           including reorganization of the Department of Agriculture (DA) and its
           agencies;

       (d) Promote effective devolution of agricultural functions and build capacity
           among LGUs;

       (e) Advance digital transformation, innovation, and climate resilience in agriculture;

       (f) Ensure meaningful stakeholder participation from public and private sectors,
           civil society, academe, and farming and fishing communities.

     Section 5. Composition. – The Commission shall be composed of:

       (a) Five Members from the House of Representatives, including the Chairs of the
           Committees on Agriculture and Food, and on Rural Development;

       (b) Five Members from the Senate, including the Chair of the Committee on
           Agriculture, Food and Agrarian Reform.

      The Commission shall be co-chaired by one Member from each chamber, designated by the
Speaker of the House and the Senate President. The remaining Members shall reflect proportional
representation of the Majority and Minority.

      The Secretaries of the Department of Agriculture (DA), the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), and the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall
serve as Ex-Officio, non-voting members of the Commission to provide technical inputs and
ensure close coordination with the Executive Branch.

      Section 6. Duration. – The Commission shall exist for a period of three years from its
organization, unless extended by joint resolution of Congress.

     Section 7. Powers and Functions. – The Commission shall:

       (a) Review the mandates, functions, and performance of the DA and its attached
           agencies;

       (b) Examine the roles and capabilities of LGUs in agricultural development and the
           implementation of devolved services;

       (c) Evaluate the effectiveness of national programs in addressing productivity,
           competitiveness, food security, and farmers’ incomes;

       (d) Recommend reforms in governance, regulation, planning, budgeting, research
           and development, extension, and value chain integration;

       (e) Hold hearings, consultations, field visits, and expert forums, and receive
           testimonies and data from agencies and stakeholders;
       (f) Summon and require by subpoena any official or document necessary to its
           work;

       (g) Engage with national and local agencies, the private sector, civil society,
           academic institutions, and international partners.

       (h) Establish and adopt, within the first ninety (90) days of its organization and in
           consultation with the Philippine Statistics Authority, a Performance Metrics
           Framework which shall be the basis for the quantitative and qualitative
           assessment of all programs and agencies under review.

      Section 8. Scope of National Assessment. – The Commission’s assessment shall include, but not
be limited to:

       (a) Review of relevant laws and executive issuances affecting agriculture and
           fisheries, including the Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization Act, Rice
           Tariffication Law, and devolved service delivery;

       (b) Performance of DA and its attached agencies against key outcome indicators;

       (c) Alignment of DA budgeting with policy priorities and sectoral needs;

       (d) Role of LGUs in delivering agricultural support services and identifying
           coordination gaps;

       (e) Comparative assessment of Philippine agriculture with regional and global
           benchmarks;

       (f) Identification of best practices from both domestic and international settings
           that may be scaled or adopted.

       (g) A systematic review of the implementation of Republic Act No. 8435 or the
           Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization Act, to identify key accomplishments,
           implementation gaps, and structural barriers that hindered the fulfillment of its
           objectives.

      Section 9. Standing Committees. – The Commission may organize standing committees from
among its members, to study specific priority areas. Each standing committee shall be co-chaired
by a member of the Commission and may be vice-chaired by a relevant expert from the advisory
council, as determined by the Commission co-chairs.

      Section 10. Technical Secretariat and Advisory Council –

       (a) A Technical Secretariat shall be constituted to provide research, coordination,
           and administrative support. It shall be headed by an Executive Director with
           proven expertise in agriculture, governance, and development management.

       (b) An Advisory Council shall be created to support the Commission with technical
           and sectoral advice. It shall be composed of experts from the academe, business,
            local government, national agencies, and civil society. Members shall be selected
            by the Commission co-chairs in consultation with stakeholders.

       Section 11. Research Arm. – The Commission may designate one or more institutions to serve
as its research arm for data collection, policy analysis, and technical studies. These may include,
but are not limited to, the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS), Southeast Asian
Regional Center for Graduate Study and Research in Agriculture (SEARCA), Philippine Council
for Agriculture and Fisheries (PCAF), and relevant state universities and colleges.

      Section 12. Reports and Outputs. –

       (a) The Commission shall submit periodic reports to both Houses of Congress
           summarizing its findings and interim recommendations.

       (b) Within its term, the Commission shall submit a final report containing its
           complete assessment, proposed policy and institutional reforms, recommended
           legislation, and a reform roadmap with clear timelines and accountability
           mechanisms.

       (c) All published Committee and final reports of the commission shall include a
           dedicated section summarizing the inputs, proposals, objections received from
           farming and fishing communities and civil society organizations during public
           consultations, and shall provide a response as to how these were considered in
           the final recommendations.

      Section 13. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial operation of the
Commission shall be specifically appropriated in the next General Appropriation Act following
the effectivity of this Act. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

     Section 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, other provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

      Section 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, other
issuances, rules, and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      Section 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days from its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
