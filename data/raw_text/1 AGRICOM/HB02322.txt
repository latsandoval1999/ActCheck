  AN ACT CREATING A CONGRESSIONAL COMMISSION ON AGRICULTURE
   AND FISHERIES TO REVIEW, ASSESS, AND EVALUATE THE STATE OF
     PHILIPPINE AGRICULTURE AND RECOMMEND INNOVATIVE AND
     TARGETED POLICY REFORMS IN AGRICULTURE AND FISHERIES,
     APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The following measure seeks to establish a Congressional Commission on Agriculture
and Fisheries, or AGRICOM, to perform a comprehensive review, assessment and evaluation
of all existing programs, projects, activities and initiatives currently implemented for the
development of the Philippine agriculture and fisheries sector across all national agencies and
instrumentalities, in pursuit of a more targeted, efficient, efficacious and integrated approach
to agricultural development in the Philippines.

         In line with our country’s perennial national vision and aspirations to elevate agriculture
as the primary driver of long-term growth and development in all communities and localities
in the country, our government has enacted innumerable laudable policies and legislation in
pursuit of this formidable goal. Pieces of legislation adopted into law have, among many things,
created a long list of exhaustive and thoughtfully crafted policy frameworks targeting all
subsectors of agriculture and fisheries, and have established, in good faith, a running list of
advisory and consultative bodies aiming to give a multitude of recommendations from all sides
of the issues and challenges faced by our local agriculture and fisheries subsectors. On the part
of Congress, two congressional oversight committees on national agriculture and fisheries
policy and programs have been created by law: the Congressional Oversight Committee on
Agricultural and Fisheries Modernization (COCAFM) established by Republic Act No. 8435
(or the Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization Act of 1997), and more recently in 2024, the
Congressional Oversight Committee on Anti-Agricultural Economic Sabotage established by
Republic Act No. 12022 (or the Anti-Agricultural Sabotage Act).

        However, it is not the absence of legislation, nor the absence of policies, programs and
projects, nor the absence of advisory and consultative bodies for policies and programs relating

to agriculture and fisheries that is the source or cause of the problems facing the sector: it is the
fact that the frameworks, policies, programs, projects and bodies created by legislation and
other national policies are currently being implemented and operationalized in silos. Advisory
and consultative bodies currently convened fall under the jurisdictions and report solely to their
mother departments, with documents and other pertinent information undisclosed and
unavailable to the public even upon request. Some councils do not have regular meetings
because of the unavailability of their members, most of whom serve as ex officio members,
due to prior commitments or issues with tenure security. Most government instrumentalities
convened and mandated by law to perform functions relating to policy implementation,
research and development, price stability, capacity building of local players and participants,
are paralyzed by conflicting mandates enforced by different agencies and government bodies,
conditions which arise from the lack of coordination among the agencies and government
bodies ordering the directives. Policies implemented by one agency for an agricultural
subsector run contrary to the policies implemented by another for the same subsector.
Redundancies in programs and policies due to the disjointed nature of program and policy
implementation for the agriculture and fisheries sector continually lead to the double allocation
of funds for the same type of program but under different agencies, while other programs and
policies of significance suffer low budget allocations or even lack of funding. The list of issues
and challenges continues.

        This country takes its pursuit of developing our agriculture and fisheries sector into its
primary pillar of human capital development, sustainable and dignified structural
transformation, and economic security on both the national and local levels. The challenges
and issues that result from the current lack of an integrated approach to agriculture and fisheries
development speak volumes: while a quarter of our nation’s workers work in agriculture,
unskilled workers.1 in agriculture and fisheries experience, on average, at least five percent
lower monthly wage rates than their counterparts in manufacturing and services,2 labor
productivity in agriculture and fisheries continues to decline,3 all the while the sector only
produces about nine percent of our country’s gross domestic product and exports from the
sector lag behind our neighbors in the ASEAN Region,4 despite the latter (i.e. the countries of
Indonesia, Thailand and Vietnam) having larger agricultural bases compared to the Philippines.

        The following measure seeks to address the issues and challenges arising from the
current state of the national framework for agriculture and fisheries development by
establishing the Congressional Commission on Agriculture and Fisheries, or AGRICOM. Upon
establishment and upon its convening, the AGRICOM shall assess and re-examine existing
policies, programs and projects in agriculture and fisheries development in order to refocus and
reprioritize support programs for increasing productivity, global competitiveness, capacity
building for the sector and its subsectors, while streamlining and restructuring current policies,
programs, and programs to remove redundancies, to ameliorate issues in lack of funding or
underfunding of programs of utmost significance (ex. research and development projects) and
to avoid conflicts in policies implemented by agencies and government instrumentalities
involved in agriculture and fisheries development.

        The AGRICOM, supported by an independent technical secretariat, shall formulate
short-term and long-term policy and program recommendations relating to agriculture and
fisheries, consisting of specific, targeted, measurable and time-bound solutions to the issues
and challenges facing the sector. It shall, upon the recommendation of sectors and stakeholders
concerned and impacted by agriculture and fisheries development policies, programs, and
projects, report its accomplishments, findings and recommendations to the Filipino people, for
swift, prompt and proper action on the part of enforcement and implementing agencies.
Importantly, the AGRICOM aims to create a cooperative and productive relationship with
departments and other government instrumentalities involved with enforcement and
implementation by assisting the latter in assessing the challenges and streamlining the policies
and programs to be implemented by all institutions of government, thereby assisting these
bodies in balancing pressing mandates relating to agriculture and fisheries development with
their own respective multiple departmental mandates.

       To ensure the dignity of every Filipino farmer and fisherfolk, the foundation of the
Philippine economy, the country must begin by confronting the structural problems existing in
both our agriculture and fisheries sectors. This Commission will serve as the cornerstone of
that effort—bridging past lessons with future aspirations.

      Given the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT CREATING A CONGRESSIONAL COMMISSION ON AGRICULTURE
   AND FISHERIES TO REVIEW, ASSESS, AND EVALUATE THE STATE OF
     PHILIPPINE AGRICULTURE AND RECOMMEND INNOVATIVE AND
     TARGETED POLICY REFORMS IN AGRICULTURE AND FISHERIES,
     APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Congressional Commission
on Agriculture and Fisheries (AGRICOM) Act"

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall reaffirm the promotion of a just
and dynamic social order that will ensure the prosperity and independence of the nation that
frees the people from poverty through policies that provide adequate social services, promote
full employment, a rising standard of living, and an improved quality of life for all. The State
shall affirm labor as a primary social economic force, shall develop a self-reliant and
independent national economy effectively controlled by Filipinos, and shall promote
comprehensive rural development and agrarian reform. To such ends, the State shall take the
appropriate steps to ensure that the national agriculture and fisheries sector shall be a robust,
dynamic and sustainable means to economic security on participants on the production side of
the sector, and shall be a driver for human capital development, human liberation and
development, and overall well-being and welfare on the part of Filipino consumers.

SECTION 3. Objectives of AGRICOM. - The Commission shall, among other things,
critically review and assess agriculture and fisheries policies, strategies and programs to attain
the following objectives:

      a) Re-examine public sector goals and objectives with respect to agriculture and
         fisheries;

      b) Modify agriculture and fisheries policies and public expenditures to integrate modern
         technologies and climate change adaption with the ultimate goal of raising the


                                                                                                4
         productivity and the global competitiveness of our local agriculture and fisheries
         subsectors, and to employ long-term policy instruments for raising productivity,
         protecting the environment and improving market efficiency and ensure equity and
         social justice, particularly for the benefit of small farmers and their families;

     c) Refocus and re-prioritize support programs for agriculture and fisheries to ensure
        food security in basic staples for all local Filipino communities;

     d) Restructure and streamline agriculture and fisheries-related bureaucracies to address
        their institutional weaknesses and shortcomings, including the relationship between
        and among national, provincial and local stakeholders, in order to effectively
        implement agriculture and fisheries policies and programs, including the transfer of
        information and technology, particularly at the grassroots level; and

     e) Encourage the active involvement of producers, cooperatives, media, banks, financial
        institutions and other members of the private sector in the development of agri-
        business.


SECTION 4. Creation of AGRICOM. - There is hereby created a Congressional Agriculture
and Fisheries Commission, hereinafter referred to as the Commission, to undertake a
comprehensive national assessment and evaluation of the performance of the Philippine
agriculture and fisheries sector for the purpose of recommending transformative, concrete and
targeted reforms in the sector with the end of ensuring food security in the Philippines.

SECTION 5. Composition of AGRICOM. - The Commission shall be composed of twelve
(12) members, with six (6) members from the Senate and six (6) members from the House of
Representatives: provided, that one of the six (6) members coming from each House shall
represent the Minority as designated by the Minority Leader of each House.

The Chairpersons of the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Food and Agrarian Reform, the
Senate Committee on Economic Affairs, and the Senate Committee on Environment, Natural
Resources and Climate Change shall be members of the Commission on the part of the Senate.

The Chairpersons of the House Committee on Agriculture and Food, the House Committee on
Aquaculture and Fisheries Resources, the House Committee on Agrarian Reform, and the
House Committee on Climate Change shall be members of the Commission on the part of the
House of Representatives.

The other members from the Senate and the House of Representatives shall be designated by
the Senate President and the Speaker of the House of Representatives, respectively.

The members of the Commission shall receive no compensation, but travelling,
communications, and other necessary expenses shall be allowed, subject to existing laws, rules
and regulations.

SECTION 6. Executive Director. - The Executive Director (ED) to be appointed by the
Commission must be a recognized expert in the fields of agriculture and fisheries.


                                                                                            5
The ED shall be a citizen of the Philippines with good moral standing and reputation, of
recognized probity and independence, and have substantial experience and expertise in
policymaking related to, or the practice of, agriculture development, aquaculture and fisheries
development, economics, and sustainability.

The ED shall work full-time and shall have the rank, privileges, and emoluments of an
Executive Director IV (Salary Grade 29).

SECTION 7. Technical Secretariat. - A Technical Secretariat, to be headed by the Executive
Director, shall be organized to provide technical and administrative support to the Commission
in the exercise of its powers and performance of its functions under Section 11 of this Act.

The Executive Director shall organize its staff and technical panel, and hire and appoint such
employees and personnel, whether temporary, contractual, or on consultancy, and determine
their compensation subject to applicable civil service laws, rules and regulations, with a view
to ensuring a competent and efficient secretariat.

The Technical Secretariat shall co-exist with the Commission.

SECTION 8. Philippine Institute for Development Studies as Research Arm. - The PIDS
shall serve as the research arm of the Commission. During the course of the Commission's
existence, it shall produce data-based research and provide analysis to contribute to the
formulation of agriculture and fisheries policy recommendations. It may coordinate with
existing advisory and consultative bodies on agriculture and fisheries policies under the
Department of Agriculture and the Department of Science and Technology.

SECTION 9. Agriculture and Fisheries Legislation and Policy Advisory Council. - An
Agriculture and Fisheries Legislation and Policy Advisory Council is hereby created to provide
the Commission with expert assistance and advice. The members of the Advisory Council shall
always be present in all meetings of the Commission. The Senate President and the Speaker of
the House of Representatives, upon the recommendation of the members of the Commission,
shall choose recognized experts from the following sectors to form part of the Advisory
Council: three (3) members from state university academia with equal representation from
Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao, two (2) members from the business sector, two (2) members
from government agriculture and fisheries agencies, two (2) members who are heads of LGUs,
and two (2) members from civil society organizations and development partners engaged in
agriculture and fisheries

SECTION 10. Duration of AGRICOM. - The Commission shall accomplish its mandate
within three (3) years from its organization. It shall organize itself and commence its mandate
upon the effectivity of this Act.

SECTION 11. Powers and Functions of AGRICOM. - The Commission shall have the
following powers and functions:

   a) Formulate short-term and long-term policy and program recommendations relating to
      agriculture and fisheries consisting specific, targeted, measurable and time-bound
      solutions to include the following:



                                                                                             6
             i) Sectoral plans and targets;
            ii) Physical and digital infrastructure;
           iii) Trainings and programs;
           iv) Financing; and
            v) Coordination among departments and sectors concerned.

  b) Approve the workplan, guidelines and overall framework for the conduct of the national
     review, assessment and harmonization tasks and the budget for the programs of the
     Commission and all disbursements therefrom, including compensation of all personnel;

  c) Hold hearings and receive testimonies, reports and expert advice on the status of the
     Philippine agriculture and fisheries sector and its subsectors, exhaust available options to
     identify problems, and consolidate coherent courses of action as remedies;

  d) Summon by subpoena any public official or private person to testify before it, or require
     by subpoena duces tecum the production of necessary documents, as may be deemed
     necessary by the Commission;

  e) Secure from any department, bureau, office or instrumentality of the government
     technical information, preparation and production of reports, and the submission of
     recommendations or plans, as may be required;

  f) Conduct multi-stakeholder consultations, using proper mechanisms and guidance;

  g) Collaborate with emerging and innovative industries and services for the sharing of
     important information relative to their practice in the development of the agriculture and
     fisheries sectors;

  h) Review and act upon the recommendations of the Technical Secretariat or Standing
     Committees, or both, as it may deem appropriate;

  i) Report to Congress biannually its accomplishments, findings and recommendations on
     actions to be taken by Congress, the departments, and other government agencies
     concerned with the implementation of policies and programs related to the agriculture
     and fisheries sector;

  j) Submit a final report of its findings and recommendations with a program of
     implementation to the President and Congress at the end of its term;

  k) Recommend the adoption of legislation and other executive measures pursuant to its
     findings;

  l) Review and prioritize pending legislation before both Houses pursuant to the findings
     contained in the report of the Commission; and

  m) Perform such other duties and functions as may be necessary to attain its objectives.

SECTION 12. Standing Committees. - The members of the Commission shall establish from
among its members standing committees, to be chaired by such members or experts from


                                                                                               7
multisectoral groups, and engage resource persons from the public and private sectors as may
be needed.

SECTION 13. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against available appropriations of the Senate and the House of
Representatives. Thereafter, the amount necessary for its continuous implementation, including
any deficiency in the current year, shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act
subject to existing budgeting, accounting and auditing rules and regulations.

SECTION 14. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining parts not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
administrative orders, rules and regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with or
contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

SECTION 16. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
