PHILIPPINE AGRICULTURE AND AGRI-FOOD SYSTEMS, AND TO

APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Agriculture and fisheries remain the foundation of national development,
ensuring food security, sustaining rural livelihoods, and contributing significantly
to inclusive economic growth. These sectors not only provide the country’s staple
food and raw materials but also support millions of Filipino farmers, fisherfolk,
agri-workers, and rural households who form the backbone of the national
economy. Agriculture continues to shape the country’s cultural identity, regional
economies, and local food systems, playing a vital role in maintaining social
stability, territorial integrity, and community resilience.
Yet despite their strategic importance, the sector continues to suffer from long-
standing and deeply rooted challenges—stagnant productivity, outdated
technologies, inadequate extension systems, weak market linkages, fragmented
governance, chronic underinvestment, and the worsening impacts of climate
change. These structural issues have resulted in inefficient value chains, high
production losses, low rural incomes, and increased dependence on food imports.
Global disruptions—from supply chain volatility to geopolitical tensions—
further expose the fragility of the Philippines’ agri-food systems. Collectively,
these constraints hinder the nation’s capacity to achieve food sovereignty, rural
prosperity, and long-term resilience.

These persistent challenges have made it clear that agricultural reform cannot be
addressed by short-term or piecemeal interventions. There is an urgent need for a
unified, science-based, and forward-looking institutional mechanism that can
harmonize policies, guide long-term planning, coordinate national and local
initiatives, and provide Congress with the analytical rigor needed to legislate
transformative reforms.

The proposed “Congressional Commission on Agriculture (AGRICOM) Act”
responds to this need by establishing an independent, multisectoral, and evidence-
informed Congressional Commission mandated to spearhead a comprehensive
national review of the agriculture and fisheries sector. The AGRICOM will
function as the primary legislative policy advisory body responsible for
developing coordinated, inclusive, and future-oriented reforms aimed at making
Philippine agriculture more productive, competitive, sustainable, and resilient to
climate change.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SEC. 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Congressional
Commission on Agriculture (AGRICOM) Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy — The State recognizes the strategic importance
of agriculture and fisheries in national development. It shall uphold the right of
every Filipino to food security, sustainable livelihoods, and resilient rural
communities. The State affirms its obligation to ensure the modernization,
inclusiveness, and climate resilience of Philippine agriculture, and to address the
persistent issues of low productivity, rural poverty, underinvestment, weak
market linkages, and fragmented policies.

It is hereby declared the policy of the State to create an independent,
multisectoral, and evidence-informed Congressional Commission that will serve
as a platform for thorough analysis, inclusive consultation, and bold
recommendations aimed at transforming Philippine agriculture into a productive,
competitive, and sustainable sector that contributes significantly to national
growth and social justice.

To this end, the State commits to strengthening agricultural governance,
enhancing institutional coherence, and ensuring that farmers, fisherfolk, agri-
workers, and rural communities are at the center of decision-making. Agricultural
development shall be informed by scientific knowledge, technological
innovations, socioeconomic realities, and the long-term impacts of climate
change.

The State shall also pursue diversified food systems, sustainable land and water
management, and improved access to markets, infrastructure, and financial
services. Agricultural modernization shall be complemented by investments in
human capital, research and development, digital agriculture, and the integration
of value-adding enterprises that generate rural employment and competitiveness.

The State further recognizes that contemporary challenges, including climate
variability, global price shocks, extreme weather events, transboundary pests and
diseases, geopolitical tensions, and disruptions in global supply chains, require a
strong and adaptive institutional mechanism capable of generating evidence-
based policy proposals and comprehensive reform strategies. The AGRICOM
shall therefore be the principal legislative instrument for articulating long-term
national agricultural reforms.

SEC. 3. Objectives of AGRICOM. — The AGRICOM shall serve as a legislative
and policy think tank of Congress to spearhead a systematic and forward-looking
national review of the agriculture and fisheries sector. Its objectives shall include:

   a. Conducting a comprehensive assessment of agricultural production
      systems, agri-food value chains, support institutions, and regulatory
      frameworks;
   b. Identifying major gaps, inefficiencies, and constraints in the current policy
      and program architecture;
   c. Proposing transformative policy, structural, and legislative reforms that are
      inclusive, science-based, climate-adaptive, and aligned with the goals of
      food sovereignty, rural prosperity, and sustainable development;
   d. Institutionalizing participatory reform processes that engage farmers,
      fisherfolk, agri-workers, youth, women, cooperatives, agribusinesses,
      indigenous peoples, and other grassroots stakeholders;
   e. Formulating a strategic roadmap for agricultural transformation over a 10-
      year period, including investment strategies, institutional realignment,
      workforce development, and innovation ecosystems; and
   f. Benchmarking progress through a robust monitoring and evaluation
      framework, grounded in quantifiable key performance indicators that are
      updated annually.

To attain these objectives, the AGRICOM shall also undertake a complete
mapping of the Philippine agri-food system, including the movement of goods
from production to consumption, integration with regional and international
markets, and the underlying economic, technological, and environmental drivers
influencing agricultural performance. It shall review the alignment of national
and local plans, evaluate the efficiency of public expenditures in agriculture,
assess the adequacy of extension and research systems, and study the implications
of land tenure structures for productivity and equity. The AGRICOM shall
                                                                                    3
likewise analyze the resilience of rural communities, the state of agricultural
labor, the quality of rural infrastructure, and opportunities for value chain
upgrading and agro-industry development.

Each Priority Thematic Area shall be supported by nationwide consultations,
regional case studies, field validation activities, comparative analyses of global
best practices, and detailed sectoral diagnostics. The AGRICOM shall ensure that
recommendations are actionable, evidence-based, costed, and supported by
implementation pathways suited to national and local capacities.

SEC. 4. Creation of the AGRICOM — There is hereby established a
Congressional Commission on Agriculture (AGRICOM), a joint body of
Congress composed of Members of the Senate and House of Representatives,
mandated to lead the national agricultural reform review. It shall operate
independently of any national agency and submit recommendations directly to
Congress.

The AGRICOM shall have full autonomy to determine its research agenda,
analytical methods, consultation processes, and institutional arrangements
necessary to accomplish its mandate. It may establish partnerships with
universities, think tanks, government agencies, research institutions, private
sector entities, and internationalorganizations. It shall maintain transparent and
participatory processes by releasing periodic updates, reports, and relevant
documentation.

SEC. 5. Composition of the AGRICOM — The Commission shall be composed
of fourteen (14) Members of Congress:

   a. Co-Chairpersons:
      1. Chairperson, Senate Committee on Agriculture, Food, and Agrarian
         Reform

      2. Chairperson, Senate Committee on Aquaculture and Fisheries
      3. Chairperson, House Committee on Agriculture and Food
      4. Chairperson, House Committee on Aquaculture and Fisheries
   b. Members:
      1. Five (5) Senators designated by the Senate President
      2. Five (5) Representatives designated by the Speaker of the House

The Co-Chairpersons shall alternate in presiding over meetings. A quorum shall
consist of eight (8) members. Decisions shall require a majority vote of those
present.

The Commission may constitute Subcommittees and Technical Panels and may
designate sectoral experts, former public officials, economists, scientists, and
practitioners as resource advisers. The AGRICOM may convene regular and
special meetings, conduct field missions, and organize thematic summits and
regional consultations.

SEC. 6. Duration. — The AGRICOM shall exist for three (3) years from the date
of its organizational meeting. Congress may, through joint resolution, extend its
term for up to two (2) more years if deemed necessary.

Any extension shall be based on clear justifications, including the status of
deliverables, emerging policy needs, or evolving sectoral challenges requiring
further study or consultation.

SEC. 7. Powers and Functions — The AGRICOM shall have the following
powers and functions:

   a. Undertake a comprehensive review of the agricultural sector, assessing and
      evaluating its systems, performance, and outcomes, and developing both
      short- term and long-term policy and program recommendations. These

   recommendations shall provide specific, targeted, measurable, and time-
   bound solutions;
b. Improve the harmonization of policies and programs across the
   Department of Agriculture and all key agricultural agencies, ensuring
   alignment, coherence, and coordinated implementation;
c. Approve the AGRICOM’s work plan and budget, covering the national
   review, assessment, and program harmonization processes;
d. Hire and appoint personnel and staff necessary for the effective
   performance of its mandate;
e. Conduct hearings and receive testimonies, reports, studies, and expert
   advice relevant to its work;
f. Issue subpoenas to require the attendance of any public official or private
   individual needed for inquiries;
g. Request assistance from government offices and agencies to support the
   fulfillment of its objectives;
h. Hold multi-stakeholder consultations to gather insights from farmers,
   cooperatives, industry partners, local governments, academic institutions,
   and civil society groups;
i. Collaborate with industries, institutions, and organizations to develop and
   implement training programs, capacity-building initiatives, and technical

j. Review and act on the recommendations of the Technical Secretariat,

k. Submit regular reports to Congress detailing its accomplishments, findings

l. Recommend additional legislation necessary to strengthen, modernize, or
   reform the agricultural sector;
m. Formulate rules, guidelines, and implementing procedures to carry out the
   intent of this Act; and

   n. Exercise all other powers necessary and incidental to effectively fulfill its
      mandate and achieve the purposes of this Act.

In addition, the AGRICOM shall undertake systems analysis, market diagnostics,
competitiveness assessments, water and climate modeling reviews, social and
economic profiling of rural households, and evaluation of price stabilization
instruments. It may conduct foresight studies, scenario planning, and policy
simulations to ensure that recommendations remain relevant to future conditions.
It shall review the structure and effectiveness of agricultural subsidies, insurance
schemes, and financing programs, propose approaches to strengthen farmer and
fisherfolk organizations, and assess the institutional capacities of agencies and
local government units.

SEC. 8. Technical Secretariat. — The AGRICOM Technical Secretariat shall be
headed by an Executive Director appointed jointly by the Co-Chairpersons. It
shall be staffed by full-time personnel, including legal researchers, policy
analysts, agricultural economists, communications officers, and administrative
staff. It shall serve as the operational backbone of the AGRICOM.

The Secretariat shall establish divisions dedicated to policy research, data
management, legal analysis, stakeholder engagement, communications, and
monitoring. It may coordinate with regional networks of research institutions and
extension providers. It shall maintain the AGRICOM Knowledge Hub, which
shall host publications, data sets, dashboards, and policy briefs.

SEC. 9. Advisory Council. — The Advisory Council shall include up to fifteen
(15) members representing key stakeholder groups. It shall convene biannually
to review the AGRICOM progress and provide collective advice. It may propose
additional studies, highlight emerging sectoral issues, and raise concerns relevant
to rural communities.

                                                                                  7
SEC. 10. Reporting and Deliverables. — The AGRICOM shall produce an
Inception Report, Quarterly Technical Updates, Annual Progress Reports, Green
and White Papers, and a Final Report and Reform Roadmap.

The Final Report shall contain a national reform blueprint with policy
recommendations, investment strategies, institutional reforms, timelines, and
impact projections. All reports shall be accessible through an AGRICOM portal.

SEC. 11. Monitoring and Evaluation and Key Performance Indicators. — The
AGRICOM shall establish and monitor key performance indicators, including
crop yield, income, credit access, loss reduction, mechanization, MSME growth,
infrastructure access, extension reach, climate resilience, and gender and youth
participation.

Additional indicators may include competitiveness benchmarks, water efficiency,
logistics performance, digital adoption, governance scores, poverty reduction
impacts, and supply chain resilience.

SEC. 12. Appropriations. — Initial funding shall be charged to available
appropriations of the Senate and House. Thereafter, funding shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act. Congress may authorize supplemental
appropriations to support specialized studies, field missions, and digital
infrastructure.

SEC. 13. Transitory Provisions. — Within thirty (30) days from effectivity, the
Senate President and the Speaker shall jointly issue rules for the appointment and
organization of the AGRICOM. The Commission shall convene within sixty (60)
days and commence its work plan within ninety (90) days.

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and
effect.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. — All inconsistent laws, executive orders, and
issuances are repealed or amended accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
