EDUCATION ACT"

EXPLANATORY NOTE

Access to quality basic education remains uneven. Public schools continue to
face overcrowding and resource constraints that impair learning outcomes, while
many private basic education institutions, particularly small and community based
schools, play a critical role in expanding access but are increasingly strained by
declining enrollment and financial pressures.

To address these challenges, the State has long pursued a policy of shared
responsibility between the public and private sectors. Republic Act No. 6728, as
amended by Republic Act No. 8545 or the Expanded Government Assistance to
Students and Teachers in Private Education Act, established government support
mechanisms for private education through financial assistance to learners and
teachers.

However, the existing framework has become fragmented and limited in scope.
It does not adequately address public school congestion, ensure effective targeting of
beneficiaries, or provide a coherent institutional structure for sustained engagement
with private basic education.

This measure seeks to modernize government support for private basic
education by institutionalizing a Private Basic Education Voucher Program. It
consolidates existing assistance mechanisms into a unified voucher based framework
that expands coverage from kindergarten to senior high school, prioritizes learners

from low income households, and supports the decongestion of overcrowded public
schools.

The bill further recognizes the complementary role of private schools by
providing institutional support, including teacher development and targeted assistance
to participating institutions. To ensure effective administration and quality assurance,
it establishes a Bureau of Private Education under the Department of Education to
oversee program implementation and regulatory compliance.

By strengthening public private partnership in basic education, this bill
promotes equitable access, more efficient use of public resources, and improved
education outcomes.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Private Education
Voucher Expansion (PEVE) Act".

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and to
take appropriate measures to ensure that basic education is accessible to all.

Consistent with this mandate, the State shall establish and maintain a system
of scholarship grants, subsidies, and other forms of financial assistance for deserving
learners, in both public and private basic education institutions, with particular
emphasis on learners in need of financial assistance.

The State further recognizes the complementary roles of public and private
basic education institutions in the educational system and the contribution of private
schools in expanding access to quality education. Accordingly, the State shall adopt
mechanisms that enable learners and families to access quality basic education
through qualified private institutions, subject to standards and safeguards. Toward
this end, the State shall provide appropriate mechanisms to augment resources and
enhance the quality of private basic education, in support of its primary responsibility
to ensure the provision of basic elementary and secondary education to all Filipino
learners.

SEC. 3. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all private basic education institutions
that receive government assistance in the form of vouchers for qualified students,

SEC. 4. Criteria for Voucher Assistance - The programs for voucher assistance
Shall be based on a set of criteria which include among others, the following:

(a) Criteria for Students. — The voucher assistance shall be provided to students
from middle income families, as determined by the Philippine Statistics Authority
(PSA), who attended public schools identified by the Department of Education as
congested, or those living in areas with congested public schools or without a nearby
public school, and who wish to enroll in private basic education schools.

Students who are most vulnerable and underprivileged shall receive a higher
voucher amount, as determined by the Basic Education Assistance Council (BEAC).

(b) Criteria for Schools. - The participating private basic education schools shall
receive assistance based on Quality Assurance under Section 6 of this Act, and the
geographic spread and size of student enrollment, as likewise determined by the
BEAC, except schools with gaps in resources and facilities which are located in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged and conflict affected areas, or GIDCA
schools. Private schools shall undergo the process of recognition and periodic
assessment to be able to participate in the programs for voucher assistance.

SEC. 5. Empowering Student Choice. - The Department of Education (DepEd)
shall create a system that allows students and parents to select their preferred schools
from a registry of participating recognized schools, subject to availability of slots.

The registry of participating recognized private basic educational institutions
shall include their school's information, such as location, number of available slots,
bummer of 6 students per classroom and level, extra-curricular programs, current
tuition fees, and top-up required in cases where the voucher amount is less than the
tuition fee among others.

SEC. 6. Quality Assurance. - The DepEd shall establish, operate and maintain a
system and mechanism for monitoring program outcome to ensure accountability of
participating recognized private basic educational institutions. This covers the
following areas:

(a) Improved Student Performance - Appropriate performance indicators to
determine the extent and outcome of the government assistance and subsidies (GAS)
programs including youth literacy rates, national and international assessment scores.
For this purpose, the National Achievement Tests and all national assessment
examinations generally give by the DepEd shall be administered to all eligible students
in all participating private basic education institutions;

(b) Improved Student Outcomes — Tracer system to collect and evaluate the
career and employment path of its graduates;

(c) School Performance - School performance date indicating the mean,
median and mode of the scores of the student in the national examinations, and such
other measures of quality education delivery; and

(d) Priority Subsidy for Underprivileged Student - Distribution of the GAS
programs while ensuring that funding is equitably allocated following the order of
priority groups.

SEC. 8. Basic Education Voucher Program. - Under the basic education voucher
program, kindergarten, elementary, and secondary students enrolled in recognized
private basic education schools charging school fees shall be provided by the
government with vouchers in such amount as may be determined by the DepEd:
Provided, that the amount shall disburse payment of the vouchers to the schools
concerned within sixty (60) days from the submission of complete documents to the
DepEd.

The budget for the basic education voucher program shall be equitably
allocated and distributed among all regions including the Bangsamoro Autonomous
Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) based on official date sources including, but not
limited to, congestion data and open seats, giving priority to underprivileged students.

Pursuant to Section 2(V), Article V of the Bangsamoro Organic Law, the
Bangsamoro Government shall exercise its authority over education for the areas
under the BARMM and will directly implement the same program to be charged against
their block grant. The DepEd shall provide technical assistance in the implementation
of the GAS programs, when necessary.

Schools participating in the basic education voucher program may increase
tuition on the condition that seventy percent (70%) of the amount of the tuition
increases shall go to the payment of salaries, wages, allowances, and other statutory
benefits of teaching and non-teaching personnel, except administrators who are
principal stockholders of the school, and may be used to cover salary increase as
provided for in the collective bargaining agreements existing or in force at the time
when this Act is approved and made effective. At least twenty percent (20%) of the
tuition increases shall go to the improvement or modernization of buildings,
equipment, libraries, laboratories, gymnasia and similar facilities and to the payment
of other costs of operation. For this purpose, schools shall maintain a separate record
of accounts for all assistance received from the government, any tuition increase, and
the detailed disposition and use thereof, which record shall be made available for
periodic inspection as may be determined by the DepEd.

SEC. 9. In - Service Training Fund or Inset Fund. - For the purpose of improving
the quality of teaching in private basic education, there is hereby established the
DepEd an Inset Fund to provide for training programs for the upgrading of knowledge
and teaching competencies in critical subject areas and for the modernization of
teaching techniques and strategies, including training on the use of computers and of
other multi-media educational technologies to assist instruction: Provided, that the
amount of Inset Fund available to private basic education shall not exceed that
available to public basic education as per capita basis: Provided, further, that the
programs shall be approved by the DepEd.

SEC. 10. Teachers’ Salary Subsidy Fund. — A Teachers’ Salary Subsidy Fund is
hereby established in the DepEd to grant government subsidy to teachers in
recognized private basic education schools participating in the programs of assistance
under this Act: Provided, That the government shall provide the teachers with salary
subsidy, to be determined by the DepEd: Provided, further, That the government shall
disburse payment within sixty (60) days after the close of the school year upon
submission of complete documents, including but not limited to qualification and
actual teaching service, to the DepEd: Provided, furthermore, That the present
allocation out of increases from tuition and other income shall be maintained:
Provided, finally, That the teacher’s salary subsidy received by the teacher shall form
part of the exclusions from taxable gross income in accordance with Section B(7)(e)
of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.

SEC. 11. Creation of the Bureau of Private Education. — The Bureau of Private
Education (BPE) under the DepEd shall be created to serve as the focal office for the
administration, supervision, and regulation of all matters relating to private basic
education institutions, including government assistance and subsidies. It shall be
headed by a Bureau Director. It shall have the following powers and functions:

(a) Monitor and assess the implementation of the private basic education
institutions of the policies, plans, and programs established by the DepEd
as recommended by BPE;

(b)Set and enforce minimum standards for programs and institutions of
learning, subject to public hearing;

(c) Ensure that the activities, efforts, and performance of private basic
education institutions shall meet the high standards of excellence and
accountability;

(d) Administer and implement the policies and programs related to the
assistance and subsidies given by the government to students, teachers,
and schools in private education;

(e) Establish and support partnerships with individual private education
institutions to respond effectively to the pressing needs of students and
teachers, and to provide learning solutions to underserved communities;

(f) Monitor and ensure compliance of private basic education institutions to the
promulgated rules and regulations of the DepEd in all matters concerning
private schools, including tuition fee increases; and

(g) Such other powers and functions as may be necessary for the effective and
efficient implementation of this Act.

responsible for policy guidance and direction, monitoring and evaluation of new and
existing programs, while the Government Assistance and Subsidies Service (GASS)

Sec. 13. Appropriations.- The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation of the DepEd.
Thereafter, such amount shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

Sec. 14. Penalties. - In case of violation of this Act by a private school, the
DepEd may bar, after due process, such school from participating in or benefiting from
the programs of this Act, and from other programs of the DepEd, without prejudice to
administrative and criminal charges that may be filed against the school or its
responsible officers under existing laws.

Sec. 15. Transitory Provision. - Subject to the availability of funds, the
implementation of the provisions stipulated in this Act for the kindergarten and
elementary levels shall gradually commence in school year 2026-2027, as may be
determined by the DepEd.

Sec. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from
the approval of this Act, the DepEd shall, in consultation with the DepDev, the DBM,
and other concerned stakeholders from the national associations of private basic
education institutions, promulgate the rules and regulations implementing the
provisions of this Act.

Sec. 17. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in
full force and effect.

Sec. 18. Repealing Clause. - Sections 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, and 15
of Republic Act No. 8545, or the “Expanded Government Assistance to Students and
Teachers in Private Education Act”, which amended Republic Act No. 6728, or the
“Government Assistance to Students and Teachers in Private Education Act”, are
hereby repealed. All other laws, decrees, orders, and rules and regulations contrary
to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended
accordingly.

Sec. 19. Effectivity. - Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the implementing
rules and regulations, this Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
