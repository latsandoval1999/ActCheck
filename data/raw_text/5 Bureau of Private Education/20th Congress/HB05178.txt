AN ACT

ESTABLISHING THE PRIVATE BASIC EDUCATION VOUCHER PROGRAM,

AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, REPEALING FOR THE PURPOSE
SECTIONS 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7,12, 14, AND 15 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8545,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “EXPANDED GOVERNMENT ASSISTANCE TO
STUDENTS AND TEACHERS IN PRIVATE EDUCATION ACT”, WHICH AMENDED
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 6728, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “GOVERNMENT
ASSISTANCE TO STUDENTS AND TEACHERS IN PRIVATE EDUCATION ACT”

EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Federation of Associations of Private Schools and Administrators
(FAPSA) has proposed the creation of a body that would oversee and address the
problem of all private learning institutions in the country.

Eleazardo Kasilag, FAPSA President, explained that the Department of
Education is focused heavily on the public schools, hence, giving the private
schools only limited time to attend to their immediate concerns.

Presently, the DepEd only assigns one supervisor to oversee all the private
schools in a division. Only one supervisor representing private schools is tasked to
attend to other assignments like scouting, nutrition, and one academic subject
among others.

In the National Capital Region alone, there are 16 Schools Divisions with
only 16 supervisors to oversee 516 public elementary and 456 public junior and
senior high schools. In addition to that, there are also 1,982 private pre-schools,
2,122 private elementary schools, 965 private junior high schools and 721 private
senior high schools serving more than 2.8 million children in the region which are
also under their supervision.

This bill seeks to establish a Bureau of Private Schools under the DepEd in
full recognition of the vital role that the private education sector plays in the

===== [page 2] =====
Philippine education system and to oversee and address the problems and
concerns of private schools throughout the country.

It is hoped that through this initiative, the private education in the country
will be strengthened to become partners of the government in nation building
through the promotion of accessible, affordable, and quality education for all the
Filipino youth.

In view thereof, early approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT

ESTABLISHING THE PRIVATE BASIC EDUCATION’ VOUCHER PROGRAM,
PROVIDING FOR THE CREATION OF THE BUREAU OF PRIVATE EDUCATION,
AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, REPEALING FOR THE PURPOSE
SECTIONS 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7,12, 14, AND 15 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8545,

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Private Basic
Education Vouchers Assistance Act”.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - \t is the declared policy of the State to
protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and
shall take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all. As part of its
mandate, it is the State’s policy to establish and maintain a system of scholarship
grants, subsidies, and other incentives which shall be available to deserving
students, as determined by the guidelines, in both public and private schools,
especially to the underprivileged. Likewise, the State recognizes the
complementary roles of public and private educational institutions in the
educational system and the invaluable contribution that the private schools have
made and will make to education.

The State shall provide the mechanisms to augment the resources and
enhance the quality of private basic education, specifically promoting the
complementary roles of public and private schools and thus recognize the
government's priority of providing basic elementary and secondary education to
all Filipino students.
===== [page 4] =====
SEC. 3. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all private basic education
institutions that receive government assistance in the form of vouchers for
qualified students, teachers and schools. This covers all the years of basic
education.

The programs of assistance for students under this Act shall be extended
only to citizens of the Philippines.

SEC. 4. Criteria for Voucher Assistance. - The programs for voucher
assistance shall be based on a set of criteria which include among others, the
following:

(a) Criteria for Students. - The voucher assistance will be provided to
students from middle-income families, as determined by the Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA), who attended public schools identified by the Department of
Education as congested or those living in areas with congested public schools or
without a nearby public school who wish to enroll in private basic education
schools.

Students who are most vulnerable and underprivileged shall receive a
higher voucher amount as determined by the Basic Education Assistance Council
(BEAC).

(b) Criteria for Schools. - The participating private basic education schools
shall receive assistance based on Quality Assurance under Section 6 of this Act, and
the geographic spread and size of student enrollment, as likewise determined by
the BEAC, except schools with gaps in resources and facilities which are located in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged and conflict-affected areas, or GIDCA
schools. Private schools shall undergo the process of recognition and periodic
assessment to be able to participate in the programs for voucher assistance.

SEC. 5. Empowering Student Choice. - The Department of Education
(DepEd) shall create a system that allows students and parents to select their
preferred schools from a registry of participating recognized schools, subject to
availability of slots.

The registry of participating recognized private basic educational
institutions shall include their schools’ information, such as location, number of
available slots, number of students per classroom and level, extra-curricular
programs, current tuition fees, and top-up required in cases where the voucher
amount is less than the tuition fee, among others.

SEC. 6. Quality Assurance. - The DepEd shall establish, operate and
maintain a system and mechanism for monitoring program outcomes to ensure
===== [page 5] =====
accountability of participating recognized private basic educational institutions.
This covers the following areas:

(a) Improved Student Performance - Appropriate performance indicators
to determine the extent and outcome of the government assistance and subsidies
(GAS) programs including youth literacy rates, national and international
assessment scores, among others. For this purpose, the National Achievement
Tests and all national assessment examinations generally given by the DepEd shall
be administered to all eligible students in all participating private basic education
institutions;

(b) Improved Student Outcomes - Tracer system to collect and evaluate the
career and employment path of its graduates;

(c) School Performance - School performance data indicating the mean,
median and mode of the scores of the students in the national examinations, and
such other measures of quality education delivery; and

(d) Priority Subsidy for Underprivileged Students - Distribution of the GAS
programs while ensuring that funding is equitably allocated following the order of

priority groups.

SEC. 7. Other Forms of Assistance. - The following shall consist of
assistance to private basic education institutions and teachers participating in the
voucher program under this Act:

The forms of assistance granted to teachers in the participating recognized
private basic education schools include:

(a) In-service training fund for teachers in private basic education schools;
(b) Scholarships for qualified private basic education teachers enrolled in
quality graduate programs; and

(c) Participation in government-funded in-service training for basic
education teachers.

The DepEd may establish a program of assistance for the development of
qualified recognized private basic education schools to cover the following
areas of school operation:

(a) Institutional Development;

(b) Organizational Development;

(c) Human Resource Development;

(d) Curriculum and Instructional Development;
(e) Research and Planning; and

(f) Facilities and School Improvement.
===== [page 6] =====
Consistent with Section 10 of Republic Act No. 10533, or the “Enhanced
Basic Education Act of 2013”, the DepEd, in consultation with the Department of
Budget and Management (DBM), may develop other forms of financial
arrangements and engage the services of private basic education schools based on
the complementary roles of public and private institutions in the educational
system.

SEC. 8. Basic Education Voucher Program. - Under the basic education
voucher program, kindergarten, elementary, and secondary students enrolled in
recognized private basic education schools charging school fees shall be provided
by the government with vouchers in such amount as may be determined by the
DepEd: Provided, That the amount shail not be less than the amount currently
provided by the DepEd: Provided, That the government shall disburse payment of
the vouchers to the schools concerned within sixty (60) days from the submission
of complete documents to the DepEd.

The budget for the basic education voucher program shall be equitably
allocated and distributed among all regions including the Bangsamoro
Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) based on official data sources
including, but not limited to, congestion data and open seats, giving priority to
underprivileged students.

Pursuant to Section 2(V), Article V of the Bangsamoro Organic Law, the
Bangsamoro Government shall exercise its authority over education for the areas
under the BARMM and will directly implement the same program to be charged
against their block grant. The DepEd shall provide technical assistance in the
implementation of the GAS programs, when necessary.

Schools participating in the basic education voucher program may increase
tuition on the condition that seventy percent (70%) of the amount of the tuition
increases shall go to the payment of salaries, wages, allowances, and other
statutory benefits of teaching and non-teaching personnel, except administrators
who are principal stockholders of the school, and may be used to cover salary
increase as provided for in the collective bargaining agreements existing or in
force at the time when this Act is approved and made effective. At least twenty
percent (20%) of the tuition increases shall go to the improvement or
modernization of buildings, equipment, libraries, laboratories, gymnasia and
similar facilities and to the payment of other costs of operation. For this purpose,
schools shall maintain a separate record of accounts for all assistance received
from the government, any tuition increase, and the detailed disposition and use
thereof, which record shall be made available for periodic inspection as may be
determined by the DepEd.

SEC. 9. In-Service Training Fund or Inset Fund. - For the purpose of
improving the quality of teaching in private basic education, there is hereby
===== [page 7] =====
established in the DepEd an Inset Fund to provide for training programs for the
upgrading of knowledge and teaching competencies in critical subject areas and
for the modernization of teaching techniques and strategies, including training on
the use of computers and of other multi-media educational technologies to assist
instruction: Provided, That the amount of Inset Fund available to private basic
education shall not exceed that available to public basic education as per capita
basis: Provided, further, That the training programs shall be approved by the
DepEd.

SEC. 10. Teachers ’ Salary Subsidy Fund. - A Teachers’ Salary Subsidy Fund
is hereby established in the DepEd to grant government subsidy to teachers in
recognized private basic education schools participating in the programs of
assistance under in this Act: Provided, That the government shall provide the
teachers with salary subsidy, to be determined by the DepEd: Provided, further,
That the government shall disburse payment within sixty (60) days after the close
of the school year upon submission of complete documents, including but not
limited to qualification and actual teaching service, to the DepEd: Provided,
furthermore, That the present allocation out of increases from tuition and other
income shall be maintained: Provided, finally, That the teacher’s salary subsidy
received by the teacher shali form part of the exclusions from taxable gross income
in accordance with Section 32(B)(7)(e) of the National Internal Revenue Code of
1997, as amended.

SEC. 11. Creation of the Bureau of Private Education. - The Bureau of
Private Education (BPE) under the DepEd shall be created to serve as the focal
office for the administration, supervision, and regulation of all matters relating to
private basic education institutions, including government assistance and
subsidies. It shall be headed by a Bureau Director.

It shall have the following powers and functions:

(a) Monitor and assess the implementation of the private basic education
institutions of the policies, plans, and programs established by the DepEd as
recommended by BPE;

(b) Setand enforce minimum standards for programs and institutions of
learning, subject to public hearing;

(c) Ensure that the activities, efforts, and performance of private basic
education institutions shall meet the high standards of excellence and
accountability;

(d) Administer and implement the policies and programs related to the
assistance and subsidies given by the government to students, teachers, and
schools in private education;

(e) | Establish and support partnerships with individual private education
institutions to respond effectively to the pressing needs of students and
teachers, and to provide learning solutions to underserved communities;
===== [page 8] =====
(f) Monitor and ensure compliance of private basic education
institutions to the promulgated rules and regulations of the DepEd in all
matters concerning private schools, including tuition fee increases; and

(g) Such other powers and functions as may be necessary for the
effective and efficient implementation of this Act.

SEC. 12. Program Administration and Implementation. - The BEAC shall
be responsible for policy guidance and direction, monitoring and evaluation of
new and existing programs while the Government Assistance and Subsidies
Service (GASS) under the BPE shall be responsible for the day-to-day
administration and implementation on the programs of assistance to private basic
education.

The BEAC shail be composed of the Secretary of the DepEd, as Chairperson,
and the Secretary of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA)
and the Secretary of the DBM, as members.

The alternate representative of the Chairperson shall possess the rank of an
Undersecretary while the alternate representatives of the members shall possess
the rank of not less than an Assistant Secretary in order to secure a quorum.

The BEAC shall meet, from time to time, as the need arises, to assess the
effectivity of the programs under this Act and to propose to Congress other forms
of assistance to participating students and schools.

The GASS shall serve as the permanent secretariat for the implementation
of the GAS programs under this Act.

The DepEd may engage the services of a third party co-implementor of the
GAS programs under this Act.

SEC. 13. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the _ initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s
appropriation of the DepEd. Thereafter, such amount shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 14. Penalties. - {n case of violation of this Act by a private school, the
DepEd may bar, after due process, such school from participating in or benefiting
from the programs of this Act, and from other programs of the DepEd, without
prejudice to administrative and criminal charges that may be filed against the
school or its responsible officers under existing laws.

SEC. 15. Transitory Provision. - Subject to the availability of funds, the
implementation of the provisions stipulated in this Act for the kindergarten and
===== [page 9] =====
elementary levels shall gradually commence in school year 2026-2027, as may be
determined by the DepEd.

SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the approval of this Act, the DepEd shall, in consultation with the NEDA, the
DBM, and other concerned stakeholders from the national associations of private
basic education institutions, promulgate the rules and regulations implementing
the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 17. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not so declared shall remain in full force and
effect.

SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. - Sections 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7,12, 14, and 15
of Republic Act No. 8545, or the “Expanded Government Assistance to Students
and Teachers in Private Education Act”, which amended Republic Act No. 6728, or
the “Government Assistance to Students and Teachers in Private Education Act”,
are hereby repealed. All other laws, decrees, orders and rules and regulations
contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
amended accordingly.

SEC. 19. Effectivity. - Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the

implementing rules and regulations, this Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
