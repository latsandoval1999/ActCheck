                                      AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE PRIVATE BASIC EDUCATION VOUCHER PROGRAM,
PROVIDING FOR THE CREATION OF THE BUREAU OF PRIVATE EDUCATION,
AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, REPEALING FOR THE PURPOSE
SECTIONS 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, AND 15 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8545,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "EXPANDED GOVERNMENT ASSISTANCE TO
STUDENTS AND TEACHERS IN PRIVATE EDUCATION ACT”, WHICH AMENDED
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 6728, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "GOVERNMENT
ASSISTANCE TO STUDENTS AND TEACHERS IN PRIVATE EDUCATION ACT"

                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Access to quality basic education remains a pressing issue in the Philippines. Many
public schools continue to struggle with overcrowded classrooms, inadequate facilities, and
limited resources—all of which negatively impact teaching quality and student learning. Data
from the Department of Education show that nearly one in three public schools operate beyond
their ideal capacity, making it difficult to deliver effective instruction.

         Meanwhile, numerous private schools—particularly small and community-based
institutions—are facing declining enrollment and financial difficulties. These schools play an
important role in serving areas that the public sector cannot fully reach. However, without
adequate support, many of them cannot sustain operations or invest in improving education
quality.

        Republic Act No. 6728, later amended by Republic Act No. 8545 or the Expanded
Government Assistance to Students and Teachers in Private Education (E-GASTPE) Act, laid
the groundwork for government support to private schools. Yet, its implementation has been
limited due to narrow coverage, outdated systems, and insufficient institutional support for
private schools and their teachers.

        To address these gaps, the proposed Private Education Voucher Expansion (PEVE) Act
aims to modernize and strengthen government assistance for private basic education by repealing
outdated provisions of RA 8545 and establishing a more inclusive, responsive, and accountable
framework.

       The bill introduces the following reforms:

           ● Expanded Coverage: The voucher program will be extended to learners from
             kindergarten to senior high school, ensuring access to private education across
             all levels of basic education.

           ● Empowered Parental Choice: Eligible students and their families can select
             from a registry of DepEd-recognized private schools, depending on available
             slots. This learner-centered approach empowers parents to choose schools that
             best suit their children’s needs.

           ● Institutional Support for Private Schools: The measure provides additional
             government assistance, including in-service funds and a Teachers’ Salary
             Subsidy Fund, to improve teaching quality and attract competent educators.

           ● Creation of the Bureau of Private Education (BPE): A new bureau under
             DepEd will manage the voucher program, regulate private schools, enforce
             standards, and extend technical and developmental support to participating
             institutions.

        Through these reforms, the bill strengthens the government’s commitment to building a
balanced and inclusive education system. It not only upholds the original intent of GASTPE but
also responds to present-day challenges such as unequal access, the need for flexibility, and the
demand for innovation.

       By reinforcing the complementary roles of public and private schools, the measure
ensures that both sectors contribute effectively to national education goals. Ultimately, it seeks
to expand opportunities for learners, reduce overcrowding in public schools, and support the
sustainability and continuous improvement of private basic education.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Private Education Voucher
Expansion (PEVE) Act".

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the declared policy of the State to protect and promote the
right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and shall take appropriate steps to make
such education accessible to all. As part of its mandate, it is the State's policy to establish and
maintain a system of scholarship grants, subsidies, and other incentives which shall be available
to deserving students, as determined by the guidelines, in both public and private schools,
especially to the underprivileged. Likewise, the State recognizes the complementary roles of
public and private educational institutions in the educational system and the invaluable
contribution that the private schools have made and will make to education.

The State shall provide the mechanisms to augment the resources and enhance the quality of
private basic education, specifically promoting the complementary roles of public and private
schools and thus recognize the government's priority of providing basic elementary and
secondary education to all Filipino students.
SEC. 3. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all private basic education institutions that receive
government assistance in the form of vouchers for qualified students, teachers and schools. This
covers all the years of basic education. The programs of assistance for students under this Act
shall be extended only to citizens of the Philippines.

SEC. 4. Criteria for Voucher Assistance. - The programs for voucher assistance shall be based
on a set of criteria which include among others, the following:

       (a) Criteria for Students. - The voucher assistance will be provided to students from
       middle-income families, as determined by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), who
       attended public schools identified by the Department of Education as congested or those
       living in areas with congested public schools or without a nearby public school who wish
       to enroll in private basic education schools. Students who are most vulnerable and
       underprivileged shall receive a higher voucher amount as determined by the Basic
       Education Assistance Council (BEAC).

       (b) Criteria for Schools. - The participating private basic education schools shall receive
       assistance based on Quality Assurance under Section 6 of this Act, and the geographic
       spread and size of student enrollment, as likewise determined by the BEAC, except
       schools with gaps in resources and facilities which are located in geographically isolated
       and disadvantaged and conflict-affected areas, or GIDCA schools. Private schools shall
       undergo the process of recognition and periodic assessment to be able to participate in
       the programs for voucher assistance.

SEC. 5. Empowering Student Choice. - The Department of Education (DepEd) shall create a
system that allows students and parents to select their preferred schools from a registry of
participating recognized schools, subject to availability of slots.

The registry of participating recognized private basic educational institutions shall include their
schools' information, such as location, number of available slots, number of students per
classroom and level, extra-curricular programs, current tuition fees, and top-up required in cases
where the voucher amount is less than the tuition fee, 8 among others.

SEC. 6. Quality Assurance. - The DepEd shall establish, operate and maintain a system and
mechanism for monitoring program outcomes to ensure accountability of participating
recognized private basic educational institutions. This covers the following areas:

       (a) Improved Student Performance - Appropriate performance indicators to determine
       the extent and outcome of the government assistance and subsidies (GAS) programs
       including youth literacy rates, national and international assessment scores, among
       others. For this purpose, the National Achievement Tests and all national assessment
       examinations generally given by the DepEd shall be administered to all eligible students
       in all participating private basic education institutions;

       (b) Improved Student Outcomes - Tracer system to collect and evaluate the career and
       employment path of its graduates;
       (c) School Performance - School performance data indicating the mean, median and
       mode of the scores of the students in the national examinations, and such other measures
       of quality education delivery; and

       (d) Priority Subsidy for Underprivileged Students - Distribution of the GAS programs
       while ensuring that funding is equitably allocated following the order of priority groups.

SEC. 7. Other Forms of Assistance. - The following shall consist of assistance to private basic
education institutions and teachers participating in the voucher program under this Act:

The forms of assistance granted to teachers in the participating recognized private basic
education schools include:

       (a) In-service training fund for teachers in private basic education schools;
       (b) Scholarships for qualified private basic education teachers enrolled in quality
       graduate                                 programs;                                   and
       (c) Participation in government-funded in-service training for basic education teachers.

The DepEd may establish a program of assistance for the development of qualified recognized
private basic education schools to cover the following areas of school operation:

       (a) Institutional Development;

       (b) Organizational Development;

       (c) Human Resource Development;

       (d) Curriculum and Instructional Development;

       (e) Research and Planning; and

       (f) Facilities and School Improvement.

Consistent with Section 10 of Republic Act No. 10533, or the "Enhanced Basic Education Act
of 2013", the DepEd, in consultation with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM),
may develop other forms of financial arrangements and engage the services of private basic
education schools based on the complementary roles of public and private institutions in the
educational system.

SEC. 8. Basic Education Voucher Program. - Under the basic education voucher program,
kindergarten, elementary, and SECondary students enrolled in recognized private basic
education schools charging school fees shall be provided by the government with vouchers in
such amount as may be determined by the DepEd: Provided, That the amount shall not be less
than the amount currently provided by the DepEd: Provided further, that the government shall
disburse payment of the vouchers to the schools concerned within sixty (60) days from the
submission of complete documents to the DepEd.

The budget for the basic education voucher program shall be equitably allocated and distributed
among all regions including the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao
(BARMM) based on official data sources including, but not limited to, congestion data and open
seats, giving priority to underprivileged students.

Pursuant to Section 2(V), Article V of the Bangsamoro Organic Law, the Bangsamoro
Government shall exercise its authority over education for the areas under the BARMM and will
directly implement the same program to be charged against their block grant. The DepEd shall
provide technical assistance in the implementation of the GAS programs, when necessary.

Schools participating in the basic education voucher program may increase tuition on the
condition that seventy percent (70%) of the amount of the tuition increases shall go to the
payment of salaries, wages, allowances, and other statutory benefits of teaching and non-teaching
personnel, except administrators who are principal stockholders of the school, and may be used
to cover salary increase as provided for in the collective bargaining agreements existing or in
force at the time when this Act is approved and made effective. At least twenty percent (20%)
of the tuition increases shall go to the improvement or modernization of buildings, equipment,
libraries, laboratories, gymnasia and similar facilities and to the payment of other costs of
operation. For this purpose, schools shall maintain a separate record of accounts for all assistance
received from the government, any tuition increase, and the detailed disposition and use thereof,
which record shall be made available for periodic inspection as may be determined by the DepEd.

SEC. 9. In-Service Training Fund or Inset Fund. - For the purpose of improving the quality of
teaching in private basic education, there is hereby established in the DepEd an Inset Fund to
provide for training programs for the upgrading of knowledge and teaching competencies in
critical subject areas and for the modernization of teaching techniques and strategies, including
training on the use of computers and of other multi-media educational technologies to assist
instruction: Provided, That the amount of Inset Fund available to private basic education shall
not exceed that available to public basic education as per capita basis: Provided, further, That
the training programs shall be approved by the DepEd.

SEC. 10. Teachers' Salary Subsidy Fund. - A Teachers' Salary Subsidy Fund is hereby
established in the DepEd to grant government subsidy to teachers in recognized private basic
education schools participating in the programs of assistance under in this Act: Provided, That
the government shall provide the teachers with salary subsidy, to be determined by the DepEd:
Provided, further, That the government shall disburse payment within sixty (60) days after the
close of the school year upon submission of complete documents, including but not limited to
qualification and actual teaching service, to the DepEd: Provided, furthermore, That the present
allocation out of increases from tuition and other income shall be maintained: Provided, finally,
That the teacher's salary subsidy received by the teacher shall form part of the exclusions from
taxable gross income in accordance with Section 32(B)(7)(e) of the National Internal Revenue
Code of 1997, as amended.

SEC. 11. Creation of the Bureau of Private Education. - The Bureau of Private Education
(BPE) under the DepEd shall be created to serve as the focal office for the administration,
supervision, and regulation of all matters relating to private basic education institutions,
including government assistance and subsidies. It shall be headed by a Bureau Director. It shall
have the following powers and functions:
       (a) Monitor and assess the implementation of the private basic education institutions of
       the policies, plans, and programs established by the DepEd as recommended by BPE;

       (b) Set and enforce minimum standards for programs and institutions of learning, subject
       to public hearing;

       (c) Ensure that the activities, efforts, and performance of private basic education
       institutions shall meet the high standards of excellence and accountability;

       (d) Administer and implement the policies and programs related to the assistance and
       subsidies given by the government to students, teachers, and schools in private education;

       (e) Establish and support partnerships with individual private education institutions to
       respond effectively to the pressing needs of students and teachers, and to provide learning
       solutions to underserved communities;

       (f) Monitor and ensure compliance of private basic education institutions to the
       promulgated rules and regulations of the DepEd in all matters concerning private schools,
       including tuition fee increases; and

       (g) Such other powers and functions as may be necessary for the effective and efficient
       implementation of this Act.

SEC. 12. Program Administration and Implementation. - The BEAC shall be responsible for
policy guidance and direction, monitoring and evaluation of new and existing programs while
the Government Assistance and Subsidies Service (GASS) under the BPE shall be responsible
for the day-to-day administration and implementation of the programs of assistance to private
basic education.

The BEAC shall be composed of the Secretary of the DepEd, as Chairperson, and the Secretary
of the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) and the Secretary of the
DBM, as members. The alternate representative of the Chairperson shall possess the rank of an
UnderSECretary while the alternate representatives of the members shall possess the rank of not
less than an Assistant Secretary in order to secure a quorum.

The BEAC shall meet, from time to time, as the need arises, to assess the effectivity of the
programs under this Act and to propose to Congress other forms of assistance to participating
students and schools.

The GASS shall serve as the permanent Secretariat for the implementation of the GAS programs
under this Act.

The DepEd may engage the services of a third party co-implementor of the GAS programs under
this Act.

SEC. 13. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall
be charged against the current year's appropriation of the DepEd. Thereafter, such amount shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
SEC. 14. Penalties. - In case of violation of this Act by a private school, the DepEd may bar,
after due process, such school from participating in or benefiting from the programs of this Act,
and from other programs of the DepEd, without prejudice to administrative and criminal charges
that may be filed against the school or its responsible officers under existing laws.

SEC. 15. Transitory Provision. - Subject to the availability of funds, the Implementation of the
provisions stipulated in this Act for the kindergarten and elementary levels shall gradually
commence in school year 2026-2027, as may be determined by the DepEd.

SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the approval of
this Act, the DepEd shall, in consultation with the DEPDev, the DBM, and other concerned
stakeholders from the national associations of private basic education institutions, promulgate
the rules and regulations implementing the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 17. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is held invalid or unconstitutional,
the other provisions not so declared shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. - Sections 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, and 15 of Republic Act
No. 8545, or the "Expanded Government Assistance to Students and Teachers in Private
Education Act", which amended Republic Act No. 6728, or the "Government Assistance to
Students and Teachers in Private Education Act”, are hereby repealed. All other laws, decrees,
orders and rules and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

SEC. 19. Effectivity. - Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the implementing rules and
regulations, this Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette
or in a newspaper of general circulation.
