                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The 1987 Constitution states in Article XIV, Section 1 that "[t]he State shall
protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and shall
take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all." For this reason, it is
the responsibility of the state to create, support, and maintain a comprehensive,
appropriate, and integrated system of education that is In line with the needs of the
populace and society”.

        Anchored on the state’s mandate that no Filipino child should be deprived of
quality basic education due to economic limitations or geographical challenges, this
proposed measure seeks to institutionalize a Private Basic Education Voucher Program
and establish the Bureau of Private Education, thereby strengthening the
government’s commitment to accessible, equitable, and quality education for all
Filipino learners.

        Through this education voucher program, we strategically address the chronic
problem of overcrowding in public schools through a system of decongestion and
redistribution of our learners and maximizing private schools which are ready to
accommodate them with the support of the Government. This proposed policy is also
consistent with the state’s mandate under Section 2(1) of Republic Act No. 9155,
otherwise known as the Governance of Basic Education Act of 2001, which recognizes

                                            1
the complementary roles of public and private educational institutions in the national
educational system.

        Pursuant to the same legislative intent, the Department of Education (DepEd)
significantly focused its efforts and resources in addressing the pressing concerns of
the public school system. While the challenges confronting education delivery in public
schools are substantial and warrants the attention of DepEd, it is equally important
that the broader educational landscape be considered and taken into account.
Education delivery in private schools, which serves as a vital complement in our system
of education, faces its own set of issues and developmental needs that must be
acknowledged and addressed. There have been reports that the current organizational
setup assigns only one supervisor per division to oversee numerous private schools.
In Metro Manila alone, there are nearly 2,000 private schools distributed across 16
divisions—yet only 16 supervisors are designated to monitor and support them. These
same supervisors are also burdened with other tasks unrelated to private education,
such as nutrition programs, monitoring activities, among others. This situation has led
to a disproportionate allocation of time and resources for the private education sector,
despite its significant contribution to the country’s overall educational landscape.

       It is therefore imperative to create a Bureau of Private Education that will
exclusively focus on the development, regulation, and management of private
elementary and secondary schools. The proposed Bureau shall serve as a centralized
and dedicated body to address all concerns, initiatives, and programs relating to
private basic education, thereby ensuring timely and responsive governance.

        Ultimately, the enactment of this measure shall institutionalize a learner-
centered, inclusive, and future-ready education system that recognizes the integral
role of private education in national development. It affirms the State’s duty to provide
adequate support and regulation for all educational stakeholders and ensures that
both public and private institutions are empowered to nurture the next generation of
Filipinos.

       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1         SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Private Basic
 2   Education Vouchers Assistance Act”.

 4           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the declared policy of the State to protect
 5   and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and shall take
 6   appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all. As part of its mandate, it
 7   is the State’s policy to establish and maintain a system of scholarship grants, subsidies,
 8   and other incentives which shall be available to deserving students, as determined by
 9   the guidelines, in both public and private schools, especially to the underprivileged.
10   Likewise, the State recognizes the complementary roles of public and private
11   educational institutions in the educational system and the invaluable contribution that
12   the private schools have made and will make to education.
13
14   The State shall provide the mechanisms to augment the resources and enhance the
15   quality of private basic education, specifically promoting the complementary roles of
16   public and private schools and thus recognize the government’s priority of providing
17   basic elementary and secondary education to all Filipino students.

 1          SEC. 3. Coverage. This Act shall cover all private basic education institutions
 2   that receive government assistance in the form of vouchers for qualified students,
 3   teachers and schools. This covers all the years of basic education. The programs of
 4   assistance for students under this Act shall be extended only to citizens of the
 5   Philippines.
 6
 7          SEC. 4. Criteria for Voucher Assistance. The programs for voucher
 8   assistance shall be based on a set of criteria which include among others, the
 9   following:
10
11                (a) Criteria for Students. – The voucher assistance will be provided to
12         students from middle-income families, as determined by the Philippine Statistics
13         Authority (PSA), who attended public schools identified by the Department of
14         Education as congested or those living in areas with congested public schools
15         or without a nearby public school who wish to enroll in private basic education
16         schools.
17
18         Students who are most vulnerable and underprivileged shall receive a higher
19         voucher amount as determined by the Basic Education Assistance Council
20         (BEAC).
21
22                (b) Criteria for Schools. – The participating private basic education
23         schools shall receive assistance based on Quality Assurance under Section 6 of
24         this Act, and the geographic spread and size of student enrollment, as likewise
25         determined by the BEAC, except schools with gaps in resources and facilities
26         which are located in geographically isolated and disadvantaged and conflict-
27         affected areas, or GIDCA schools. Private schools shall undergo the process of
28         recognition and periodic assessment to be able to participate in the programs
29         for voucher assistance.
30
31          SEC. 5. Empowering Student Choice. The Department of Education
32   (DepEd) shall create a system that allows students and parents to select their
33   preferred schools from a registry of participating recognized schools, subject to
34   availability of slots.
35
36   The registry of participating recognized private basic educational institutions shall
37   include their schools’ information, such as location, number of available slots, number
38   of students per classroom and level, extra-curricular programs, current tuition fees,
39   and top-up required in cases where the voucher amount is less than the tuition fee,
40   among others.
41
42          SEC. 6. Quality Assurance. The DepEd shall establish, operate and maintain
43   a system and mechanism for monitoring program outcomes to ensure accountability
44   of participating recognized private basic educational institutions. This covers the
45   following areas:
46
47                (a) Improved Student Performance – Appropriate performance
48         indicators to determine the extent and outcome of the government assistance

 1         and subsidies (GAS) programs including youth literacy rates, national and
 2         international assessment scores, among others. For this purpose, the National
 3         Achievement Tests and all national assessment examinations generally given
 4         by the DepEd shall be administered to all eligible students in all participating
 5         private basic education institutions;
 6
 7               (b) Improved Student Outcomes – Tracer system to collect and
 8         evaluate the career and employment path of its graduates;
 9
10               (c) School Performance – School performance data indicating the

13
14                (d) Priority Subsidy for Underprivileged Students – Distribution of the
15         GAS programs while ensuring that funding is equitably allocated following the
16         order of priority groups.

 5          SEC. 8. Basic Education Voucher Program. Under the basic education
 6   voucher program, kindergarten, elementary, and secondary students enrolled in
 7   recognized private basic education schools charging school fees shall be provided by
 8   the government with vouchers in such amount as may be determined by the DepEd:
 9   Provided, That the amount shall not be less than the amount currently provided by
10   the DepEd: Provided, That the government shall disburse payment of the vouchers to
11   the schools concerned within sixty (60) days from the submission of complete
12   documents to the DepEd.
13
14          The budget for the basic education voucher program shall be equitably
15   allocated and distributed among all regions including the Bangsamoro Autonomous
16   Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) based on official data sources including, but not
17   limited to, congestion data and open seats, giving priority to underprivileged students.
18
19           Pursuant to Section 2(V), Article V of the Bangsamoro Organic Law, the
20   Bangsamoro Government shall exercise its authority over education for the areas
21   under the BARMM and will directly implement the same program to be charged against
22   their block grant. The DepEd shall provide technical assistance in the implementation
23   of the GAS programs, when necessary.
24
25          Schools participating in the basic education voucher program may increase
26   tuition on the condition that seventy percent (70%) of the amount of the tuition
27   increases shall go to the payment of salaries, wages, allowances, and other statutory
28   benefits of teaching and non-teaching personnel, except administrators who are
29   principal stockholders of the school, and may be used to cover salary increase as
30   provided for in the collective bargaining agreements existing or in force at the time
31   when this Act is approved and made effective. At least twenty percent (20%) of the
32   tuition increases shall go to the improvement or modernization of buildings,
33   equipment, libraries, laboratories, gymnasia and similar facilities and to the payment
34   of other costs of operation. For this purpose, schools shall maintain a separate record
35   of accounts for all assistance received from the government, any tuition increase, and
36   the detailed disposition and use thereof, which record shall be made available for
37   periodic inspection as may be determined by the DepEd.
38
39          SEC. 9. In-Service Training Fund or Inset Fund. For the purpose of
40   improving the quality of teaching in private basic education, there is hereby
41   established in the DepEd an Inset Fund to provide for training programs for the
42   upgrading of knowledge and teaching competencies in critical subject areas and for
43   the modernization of teaching techniques and strategies, including training on the use
44   of computers and of other multi-media educational technologies to assist instruction:
45
46          Provided, That the amount of Inset Fund available to private basic education
47   shall not exceed that available to public basic education as per capita basis, Provided
48   further, That the training programs shall be approved by the DepEd.

 2           SEC. 10. Teachers’ Salary Subsidy Fund. A Teachers’ Salary Subsidy Fund
 3   is hereby established in the DepEd to grant government subsidy to teachers in
 4   recognized private basic education schools participating in the programs of assistance
 5   under in this Act: Provided, That the government shall provide the teachers with salary
 6   subsidy, to be determined by the DepEd: Provided, further, That the government shall
 7   disburse payment within sixty (60) days after the close of the school year upon
 8   submission of complete documents, including but not limited to qualification and
 9   actual teaching service, to the DepEd: Provided, furthermore, That the present
10   allocation out of increases from tuition and other income shall be maintained:
11   Provided, finally, That the teacher’s salary subsidy received by the teacher shall form
12   part of the exclusions from taxable gross income in accordance with Section
13   32(B)(7)(e) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.

15          SEC. 11. Creation of the Bureau of Private Education. The Bureau of
16   Private Education (BPE) under the DepEd shall be created to serve as the focal office
17   for the administration, supervision, and regulation of all matters relating to private
18   basic education institutions, including government assistance and subsidies. It shall
19   be headed by a Bureau Director.
20
21         It shall have the following powers and functions:
22
23         (a) Monitor and assess the implementation of the private basic education
24         institutions of the policies, plans, and programs established by the DepEd as
25         recommended by BPE;
26
27         (b) Set and enforce minimum standards for programs and institutions of
28         learning, subject to public hearing;
29
30         (c) Ensure that the activities, efforts, and performance of private basic
31         education institutions shall meet the high standards of excellence and
32         accountability;
33
34         (d) Administer and implement the policies and programs related to the
35         assistance and subsidies given by the government to students, teachers, and
36         schools in private education;
37
38         (e) Establish and support partnerships with individual private education
39         institutions to respond effectively to the pressing needs of students and
40         teachers, and to provide learning solutions to underserved communities;
41
42         (f) Monitor and ensure compliance of private basic education institutions to the
43         promulgated rules and regulations of the DepEd in all matters concerning
44         private schools, including tuition fee increases; and
45
46         (g) Such other powers and functions as may be necessary for the effective and
47         efficient implementation of this Act.

 1         SEC. 12. Program Administration and Implementation. The BEAC shall
 2   be responsible for policy guidance and direction, monitoring and evaluation of new
 3   and existing programs while the Government Assistance and Subsidies Service (GASS)
 4   under the BPE shall be responsible for the day-to-day administration and
 5   implementation on the programs of assistance to private basic education.
 6
 7         The BEAC shall be composed of the Secretary of the DepEd, as Chairperson,
 8   and the Secretary of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) and
 9   the Secretary of the DBM, as members.
10
11          The alternate representative of the Chairperson shall possess the rank of an
12   Undersecretary while the alternate representatives of the members shall possess the
13   rank of not less than an Assistant Secretary in order to secure a quorum.
14
15           The BEAC shall meet, from time to time, as the need arises, to assess the
16   effectivity of the programs under this Act and to propose to Congress other forms of
17   assistance to participating students and schools.
18
19         The GASS shall serve as the permanent secretariat for the implementation of
20   the GAS programs under this Act.
21
22         The DepEd may engage the services of a third party co-implementor of the
23   GAS programs under this Act.
24
25         SEC. 13. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial
26   implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation
27   of the DepEd. Thereafter, such amount shall be included in the annual General
28   Appropriations Act.
29
30           SEC. 14. Penalties. In case of violation of this Act by a private school, the
31   DepEd may bar, after due process, such school from participating in or benefiting
32   from the programs of this Act, and from other programs of the DepEd, without
33   prejudice to administrative and criminal charges that may be filed against the school
34   or its responsible officers under existing laws.
35
36         SEC. 15. Transitory Provision. Subject to the availability of funds, the
37   implementation of the provisions stipulated in this Act for the kindergarten and
38   elementary levels shall gradually commence in school year 2026-2027, as may be
39   determined by the DepEd.
40
41          SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days
42   from the approval of this Act, the DepEd shall, in consultation with the NEDA, the
43   DBM, and other concerned stakeholders from the national associations of private basic
44   education institutions, promulgate the rules and regulations implementing the
45   provisions of this Act.
