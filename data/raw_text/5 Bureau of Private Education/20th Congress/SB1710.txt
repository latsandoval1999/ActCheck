EXPLANATORY NOTE

Education is the foundation of national progress. It empowers our youth, uplifts
families, and builds a nation capable of competing in a globalized world. Under the
1987 Constitution, the State is mandated to protect and promote the right of all
citizens to quality education at all levels and ensure that such education is accessible
to all.! It likewise recognizes the vital partnership between public and private
institutions in fulfilling this constitutional promise.?

In pursuit of this mandate, the government established the Government
Assistance to Students and Teachers in Private Education (GASTPE) program. Piloted
in the 1980s and later institutionalized under Republic Act (RA) No. 6728, as amended
by Republic Act No. 8545 or the “Expanded Government Assistance to Students and
Teachers in Private Education Act (E-GASTPE)’, the program aims to improve access
and quality of education through shared responsibility between the public and private

sectors.

Under E-GASTPE, the government provides financial assistance to students and
teachers in private schools through key programs such as: (a) Educational Service
Contracting (ESC) or financial assistance ranging from P8,500 to P13,000 to qualified
elementary school graduates who wish to pursue Junior High School (JHS) in private
schools; (b) Teacher’s Salary Subsidy of P1,500 per month for licensed teachers in
ESC-participating JHS; and (c) a Voucher Program for Senior High School (SHS) in the
form of around P14,000 to P22,500 in tuition subsidies to qualified Grade 10
completers to assist them in enrolling in SHS in private non-Department of Education
public schools.

While these programs have expanded opportunities for thousands of learners,
challenges remain. Public schools continue to face overcrowding, and the selection
and allocation process under the E-GASTPE does not guarantee that assistance
reaches the most disadvantaged students.

To address this pressing concern, this bill removes outdated provisions and
integrates more dynamic, flexible approaches to funding and regulation of private
education through vouchers; expands the government assistance to private basic
education to include learners from Kindergarten to Grade 6, decongests overcrowded
classrooms in public basic education schools through prioritization of learners
belonging to low income households through targeted-beneficiary system, and
supports teachers by providing in-service training funds, scholarships for advanced
studies, and financial support for institutional enhancements in accredited private
basic education schools.

In view hereof, the urgent passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Private Education Voucher
Expansion (PEVE) Act.”

Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the declared policy of the State to protect and
promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and shall take appropriate
steps to make such education accessible to all. As part of its mandate, it is the State’s
policy to establish and maintain a system of scholarship grants, subsidies, and other
incentives which shall be available to deserving students, as determined by the guidelines,
in both public and private schools, especially to the underprivileged. Likewise, the State
recognizes the complementary roles of public and private education; institutions in the
educational system and the invaluable contribution that the private schools have made

and will make to education.

The State shall provide the mechanism to augment the resources and enhance the

quality of private basic education, specifically promoting the complementary roles of

public and private basic schools and thus recognize the government's priority of providing

basic elementary and secondary education to all Filipino Students.

Sec. 3. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all private basic education institutions that

receive government assistance in the form of vouchers for qualified students, teachers

and schools. This covers all the years of basic education. The programs of assistance for

students under this Act shall be extended only to citizens of the Philippines.

Sec. 4. Criteria for Voucher Assistance - The programs for voucher assistance shall

be based on a set of criteria which include among others, the following:

b)

Criteria for Students. The voucher assistance will be provided to students from
middle-income families, as determined by the Philippines Statistics Authority
(PSA), who attended public schools identified by the Department of Education
as congested or those living in areas congested public schools or without a

nearby public school who wish to enroll on private basic education schools.

Students who are most vulnerable and underprivileged shall receive a higher

voucher amount as determined by the Basic Education Council (BEAC).

Criteria for Schools. - The participating private basic education schools shall
receive assistance based on Quality Assurance under Section 6 of this Act, and
the geographic spread and size of student enrollment, as likewise determined
by the BEAC, except schools with gaps in resources and facilities which are
located in geographically isolated and disadvantaged and conflict-afected
areas, or GIDCA schools. Private schools shall undergo the process of
recognition and periodic assessment to be able to participate in the programs

for voucher assistance.

Sec. 11. Creation of the Bureau of Private Education. - The Bureau of Private
Education (BPE) under the DepEd shall be created to serve as the focal office for the
administration, supervision, and regulation of all matters relating to private basic
education institutions, including government assistance and subsidies. It shall be headed
by a Bureau Director. It shall have the following powers and functions:

(a) Monitor and assess the implementation of the private basic education institutions
of the policies, plans, and programs established by the DepEd as
recommended by BPE;

(b) Set and enforce minimum standards for programs and institutions of learning,
subject to public hearing;

(c) Ensure that the activities, efforts, and performance of private basic education
institutions shall meet the high standards of excellence and accountability;

(d) Administer and implement the policies and programs related to the assistance
and subsidies given by the government to students, teachers, and schools in
private education;

(e) Establish and support partnerships with individual private education institutions
to respond effectively to the pressing needs of students and teachers, and to
provide learning solutions to underserved communities;

(f) Monitor and ensure compliance of private basic education institutions to the
promulgated rules and regulations of the DepEd in all matters concerning
private schools, including tuition fee increases; and

(g) Such other powers and functions as may be necessary for the effective and

efficient implementation of this Act.

Sec. 12. Program Administration and Implementation. - The BEAC shall be
responsible for policy guidance and direction, monitoring and evaluation of new and
existing programs while the Government Assistance and Subsidies Service (GASS) under
the BPE shall be responsible for the day-to-day administration and implementation of the
programs of assistance to private basic education.

The BEAC shall be composed of the Secretary of the DepEd, as Chairperson, and
the Secretary of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) and the
Secretary of the DBM, as members.

The alternate representative of the Chairperson shall possess the rank of an
Undersecretary while the alternate representatives of the members shall possess the rank
of not less than an Assistant Secretary in order to secure a quorum. The BEAC shall meet,
from time to time, as the need arises, to assess the effectivity of the programs under this
Act and to propose to Congress other forms of assistance
to participating students and schools.

The GASS shall serve as the permanent secretariat for the implementation of the
GAS programs under this Act.

The DepEd may engage the services of a third party co-implementor of the GAS
programs under this Act.

Sec. 13. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current year's appropriation of the DepEd.
Thereafter, such amount shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

Sec. 14. Penalties. - In case of violation of this Act by a private school, the DepEd
may bar, after due process, such school from participating in or benefiting from the
programs of this Act, and from other programs of the DepEd, without prejudice to
administrative and criminal charges that may be filed against the school or its responsible
officers under existing laws.

Sec. 15. Transitory Provision. - Subject to the availability of funds, the
implementation of the provisions stipulated in this Act for the kindergarten and
elementary levels shall gradually commence in school year 2026-2027, as may be
determined by the DepEd.

Sec. 16. /mplementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the
approval of this Act, the DepEd shall, in consultation with the NEDA, the DBM, and other
concerned stakeholders from the national associations of private basic education
institutions, promulgate the rules and regulations implementing the provisions of this

Sec. 17. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not so declared shall remain in full force and

Sec. 18. Repealing Clause. - Sections 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, and 15 of
Republic Act No. 8545, or the "Expanded Government Assistance to Students and
Teachers in Private Education Act", which amended Republic Act No. 6728, or the
"Government Assistance to Students and Teachers in Private Education Act", are hereby
repealed. All other laws, decrees, orders and rules and regulations contrary to or
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

Sec. 19. Effectivity. - Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the implementing rules
and regulations, this Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the

Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
