                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The 1987 Philippine Constitution is very explicit in promoting the right of all
Filipinos to quality education, to wit: "The State shall protect and promote the right of
all citizens to quality education at all levels, and shall take appropriate steps to make
such accessible to all" (Article XIV, Section 1).


       Article XIV, Section 1 provides: "The State shall protect and promote the right
of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and shall take appropriate actions to
make such education accessible to all."


       Section 2 likewise provides that The State shall:
       1. Establish, maintain, and support a complete, adequate, and integrated
          system of education relevant to the needs of the people and society;
       2. Establish and maintain, a system of free public education in the
          elementary and high school levels. Without limiting the natural rights of
           parents to rear their children, elementary education is compulsory for all
          children of school age;
       3. Establish and maintain a system of scholarship grants, student loan
          programs, subsidies, and other incentives which shall be available to
          deserving students in both public and private schools, especially to the
          under-privileged;
       4. Encourage non-formal, informal, and indigenous learning systems, as well
          as self-learning, independent, and out-of-school study programs
          particularly those that respond to community needs; and
       5. Provide adult citizens, the disabled, and out-of-school youth with training
          in civics, vocational efficiency, and other skills.


       Thus, it is the right of all students to keep their education, no matter what their
social and economic status is.


       Republic Act No. 6728, as amended by RA 8545, otherwise known as the
Government Assistance to Students and Teachers in Private Education Act, was
enacted to provide a more cost-effective alternative to building more classrooms and
hiring more teachers—which may take a while to complete compared with transferring
public school students to private schools through the voucher program to immediately
address school congestion.


       Through this bill, access to quality education especially to underprivileged
students is within reach as a comprehensive voucher system aimed at helping poor
students enroll in private basic educational institutions and alleviate congestion in
public schools will be established.


      This measure also removes outdated provisions and integrates more flexible
approaches to funding and regulation of private education through the voucher
system. Further, a central government institution tasked with carrying out the
objectives of this measure will be established.


       By redistributing the growing student population in public schools, this
legislation not only enhances the student's access to quality education but also
addresses the lingering issue of congestion in public schools nationwide.


       For these reasons, urgent passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

10          Sec. 4. Criteria for Voucher Assistance. - The programs for voucher assistance
11   shall be based on a set of criteria which include among others, the following:
12          a) Criteria for Students. - The voucher assistance will be provided to students
13              from middle-income families, as determined by the Philippine        Statistics
14              Authority (PSA), who attended public schools identified by the Department
15              of Education as congested or those living in areas with congested public
16              schools or without a nearby public school who wish to enroll in private basic
17              education schools.
18              Students who are most vulnerable and underprivileged shall receive a higher
19              voucher amount as determined by the Basic Education Assistance Council
20              (BEAC).
21          b) Criteria forSchoois. - The participating private basic education schools shall
22              receive assistance based on Quality Assurance under Section 6 of this Act,
23              and the geographic spread and size of student enrollment, as likewise
24              determined by the BEAC, except schools with gaps in resources and facilities
25              which are located in geographically isolated and disadvantaged and conflict-
26              affected areas, or GIDCA schools. Private schools shall undergo the process
27              of recognition and periodic assessment to be able to participate in the
28              programs for voucher assistance.
29          Sec. 5. Empowering Student Choice. - The Department of Education (DepEd)
30   shall create a system that allows students and parents to select their preferred schools
31   from a registry of participating recognized schools, subject to availability of slots.
 1         The registry of participating recognized private basic educational institutions
 2   shall include their schools' information, such as location, number of available slots,
 3   number of students per classroom and level, extra-curricular programs, current tuition
 4   fees, and top-up required in cases where the voucher amount is less than the tuition
 5   fee, among others.
 6         Sec. 6. Quality Assurance. - The. DepEd shall establish, operate and maintain a
 7   system and mechanism for monitoring program outcomes to ensure accountability of
 8   participating recognized private basic educational institutions. This covers the
 9   following areas:
10         a) Improved Student Performance - Appropriate performance indicators to
11             determine the extent and outcome of the government assistance and
12             subsidies (GAS) programs including youth literacy rates, national and
13             international assessment scores, among others. For this purpose, the
14             National Achievement Tests and all national assessment examinations
15             generally given by the DepEd shall be administered to all eligible students
16             in all participating private basic education institutions;
17          b) Improved Student Outcomes - Tracer system to collect and evaluate the
18             career and employment path of its graduates;
19         c) School Performance - School performance data indicating the mean,
20             median and mode of the scores of the students in the national examinations,
21             and such other measures of quality education delivery; and
22         d) Priority Subsidy for Underprivileged Students - Distribution of the GAS
23             programs while ensuring that funding is equitably allocated following the
24             order of priority groups.

23   vouchers to the schools concerned within sixty (60) days from the submission of
24   complete documents to the DepEd.
25         The budget for the basic education voucher program shall be equitably
26   allocated and distributed among all regions including the Bangsamoro Autonomous

16          Sec. 13. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation

24          Sec. 15. Transitory Provision. - Subject to the availability of funds, the
