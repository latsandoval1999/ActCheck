                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
        The right to quality education is a cornerstone of national development and a
fundamental guarantee under the 1987 Philippine Constitution. As provided in Article XIV,
Section 1, the State is mandated to protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality
education at all levels and to take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all.
While the government continues to strengthen the public education system, it also recognizes
the indispensable role of private educational institutions in realizing this constitutional
mandate. To this end, the State must adopt a more inclusive and equitable approach that
empowers Filipino families with greater access to quality basic education, regardless of
whether it is offered by public or private schools. Over the decades, this vision has been
pursued through a dual system that recognizes the vital, complementary roles of both public
and private educational institutions in delivering basic education.

        In many communities across the country, private schools have long filled the gaps left
by limited public school capacity, especially in underserved or densely populated areas.
Despite this, government support for private education remains constrained, fragmented, and
in need of systemic reform. The current programs, while beneficial, are no longer fully aligned
with the evolving needs of Filipino learners and the changing landscape of basic education.

        This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize a more effective, inclusive, and
accountable mechanism for supporting private basic education in the Philippines. It aims to
empower students and their families, particularly those in congested urban areas, low-income
communities, and geographically disadvantaged regions—with real educational choices
through a well-targeted voucher system. By providing financial assistance directly to learners,
the government enables them to access private schools that meet minimum quality standards,
helping to reduce class congestion in public schools while promoting equitable access to
learning opportunities.

         Crucially, this bill affirms the principle of student-centered funding. Instead of relying
solely on the expansion of infrastructure, the measure invests in learners themselves and
strengthens the capacity of private institutions that commit to quality, transparency, and service
to the Filipino public. It acknowledges the socioeconomic realities faced by many Filipino
families and provides differentiated levels of support based on income brackets and school
access. In doing so, the measure ensures that children from disadvantaged backgrounds are
prioritized and empowered with genuine school choice.
          The measure also introduces governance reforms, including the creation of a
dedicated bureau within the Department of Education to oversee private education policy,
ensure quality assurance, and enforce standards. By streamlining administration and building
institutional accountability, the State reinforces its responsibility to ensure that public funds are
used effectively and that students’ rights to safe, inclusive, and high-quality education are
upheld.

         In addition to student subsidies, the bill institutionalizes support for private school
teachers through the creation of a Teachers' Salary Subsidy Fund and an In-Service Training
(Inset) Fund. These funds are aimed at narrowing the resource and skills gap between public
and private educators, especially those in schools located in remote or disadvantaged
communities. Through targeted subsidies and professional development support, the bill
seeks to uplift the teaching profession within the private education sector.

         Finally, this legislative proposal represents a long-overdue recalibration of the
government’s education policy—one that is equitable, forward-looking, and rooted in the
principles of inclusive development. By providing targeted financial assistance to deserving
students and support to private educational institutions, this measure upholds the
constitutional right to quality education and ensures that no Filipino child is denied the
opportunity to learn simply because of where they live or how much their family earns.

        In view of the urgent need to strengthen the private education sector as a reliable
partner in nation-building and to provide Filipino learners with greater access to educational
choices, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                HOUSE BILL NO. 3161

           Introduced by Representative MARLYN L. PRIMICIAS-AGABAS

           AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE PRIVATE BASIC EDUCATION VOUCHER
    PROGRAM, PROVIDING FOR THE CREATION OF THE BUREAU OF PRIVATE
   EDUCATION, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, REPEALING FOR THE
  PURPOSE SECTIONS 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, AND 15 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO.
  8545, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “EXPANDED GOVERNMENT ASSISTANCE TO
   STUDENTS AND TEACHERS IN PRIVATE EDUCATION ACT", WHICH AMENDED
      REPUBLIC ACT NO. 6728, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “GOVERNMENT
    ASSISTANCE TO STUDENTS AND TEACHERS IN PRIVATE EDUCATION ACT"

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Private Basic Education Vouchers
Assistance Act”.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the declared policy of the State to protect and promote
the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and shall take appropriate steps to make
such education accessible to all. As part of its mandate, it is the State’s policy to establish and
maintain a system of scholarship grants, subsidies, and other incentives which shall be
available to deserving students, as determined by the guidelines, in both public and private
schools, especially to the underprivileged. Likewise, the State recognizes the complementary
roles of public and private educational institutions in the educational system and the invaluable
contribution that the private schools have made and will make to education.

         The State shall provide the mechanisms to augment the resources and enhance the
quality of private basic education, specifically promoting the complementary roles of public and
private schools and thus recognize the government’s priority of providing basic elementary
and secondary education to all Filipino students.

SEC. 3. Coverage. - This Act covers all private basic education institutions that receive
government assistance in the form of vouchers for qualified students, teachers and schools.
This covers all the years of basic education.

        The programs of assistance for students under this Act shall be extended only to
citizens of the Philippines.

SEC. 4. Criteria for Voucher Assistance. - The programs for voucher assistance shall be
based on a set of criteria which include among others, the following:
a. Criteria for Students. - The voucher assistance will be provided to students from middle-
   income families, as determined by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), who attended
   public schools identified by the Department of Education as congested or those living in
   areas with congested public schools or without a nearby public school who wish to enroll
   in private basic education schools.

b. Students who are most vulnerable and underprivileged shall receive a higher voucher
   amount as determined by the Basic Education Assistance Council (BEAC).

c. Criteria for Schools. - The participating private basic education schools shall receive
   assistance based on Quality Assurance under Section 6 of this Act, and the geographic
   spread and size of student enrollment, as likewise determined by the BEAC, except
   schools with gaps in resources and facilities which are in geographically isolated and
   disadvantaged and conflict-affected areas, or GIDCA schools. Private schools shall
   undergo the process of recognition and periodic assessment to be able to participate in
   the programs for voucher assistance.

SEC. 5. Empowering Student Choice. - The Department of Education (DepEd) shall create
a system that allows students and parents to select their preferred schools from a registry of
participating recognized schools, subject to availability of slots.

        The registry of participating recognized private basic educational institutions shall
include their schools’ information, such as location, number of available slots, number of
students per classroom and level, extra-curricular programs, current tuition fees, and top-up
required in cases where the voucher amount is less than the tuition fee, among others.

SEC. 6. Quality Assurance. - The DepEd shall establish, operate and maintain a system and
mechanism for monitoring program outcomes to ensure accountability of participating
recognized private basic educational institutions. This covers the following areas:

a. Improved Student Performance - Appropriate performance indicators to determine the
   extent and outcome of the government assistance and subsidies (GAS) programs
   including youth literacy rates, national and international assessment scores, among
   others. For this purpose, the National Achievement Tests and all national assessment
   examinations generally given by the DepEd shall be administered to all eligible students
   in all participating private basic education institutions.

b. Improved Student Outcomes - Tracer system to collect and evaluate the career and
   employment path of its graduates.

c. School Performance - School performance data indicating the mean, median and mode of
   the scores of the students in the national examinations, and such other measures of quality
   education delivery; and

d. Priority Subsidy for Underprivileged Students - Distribution of the GAS programs while
   ensuring that funding is equitably allocated following the order of priority groups.

SEC. 7. Other Forms of Assistance. - The following shall consist of assistance to private
basic education institutions and teachers participating in the voucher program under this Act:
The forms of assistance granted to teachers in the participating recognized private basic
education schools include:

a. In-service training fund for teachers in private basic education schools.

b. Scholarships for qualified private basic education teachers enrolled in quality graduate
   programs; and

c. Participation in government-funded in-service training for basic education teachers.

d. The DepEd may establish a program of assistance for the development of qualified
   recognized private basic education schools to cover the following areas of school
   operation:

e. Institutional Development.

f.   Organizational Development.

g. Human Resource Development.

h. Curriculum and Instructional Development.

i.   Research and Planning; and

j.   Facilities and School Improvement.

         Consistent with Section 10 of Republic Act No. 10533, or the “Enhanced Basic
Education Act of 2013”, the DepEd, in consultation with the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), may develop other forms of financial arrangements and engage the
services of private basic education schools based on the complementary roles of public and
private institutions in the educational system.

SEC. 8. Basic Education Voucher Program. - Under the basic education voucher program,
kindergarten, elementary, and secondary students enrolled in recognized private basic
education schools charging school fees shall be provided by the government with vouchers in
such amount as may be determined by the DepEd: Provided, That the amount shall not be
less than the amount currently provided by the DepEd: Provided, That the government
shall disburse payment of the vouchers to the schools concerned within sixty (60) days from
the submission of complete documents to the DepEd.

         The budget for the basic education voucher program shall be equitably allocated and
distributed among all regions including the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim
Mindanao (BARMM) based on official data sources including, but not limited to, congestion
data and open seats, giving priority to underprivileged students.

         Pursuant to Section 2(V), Article V of the Bangsamoro Organic Law, the Bangsamoro
Government shall exercise its authority over education for the areas under the BARMM and
will directly implement the same program to be charged against their block grant. The DepEd
shall provide technical assistance in the implementation of the GAS programs, when
necessary.
         Schools participating in the basic education voucher program may increase tuition on
the condition that seventy percent (70%) of the amount of the tuition increases shall go to the
payment of salaries, wages, allowances, and other statutory benefits of teaching and non-
teaching personnel, except administrators who are principal stockholders of the school, and
may be used to cover salary increase as provided for in the collective bargaining agreements
existing or in force at the time when this Act is approved and made effective. At least twenty
percent (20%) of the tuition increases go to the improvement or modernization of buildings,
equipment, libraries, laboratories, gymnasium and similar facilities and to the payment of other
costs of operation. For this purpose, schools shall maintain a separate record of accounts for
all assistance received from the government, any tuition increase, and the detailed disposition
and use thereof, which record shall be made available for periodic inspection as may be
determined by the DepEd.

SEC. 9. In-Service Training Fund or Inset Fund. - For the purpose of improving the quality
of teaching in private basic education, there is hereby established in the DepEd an Inset Fund
to provide for training programs for the upgrading of knowledge and teaching competencies in
critical subject areas and for the modernization of teaching techniques and strategies,
including training on the use of computers and of other multi-media educational technologies
to assist instruction: Provided, That the amount of Inset Fund available to private basic
education shall not exceed that available to public basic education as per capita basis:
Provided, further, That the training programs shall be approved by the DepEd.

SEC. 10. Teachers’ Salary Subsidy Fund. - A Teachers’ Salary Subsidy Fund is hereby
established in the DepEd to grant government subsidy to teachers in recognized private basic
education schools participating in the programs of assistance under in this Act: Provided, That
the government shall provide the teachers with salary subsidy, to be determined by the DepEd:
Provided, further, That the government shall disburse payment within sixty (60) days after
the close of the school year upon submission of complete documents, including but not limited
to qualification and actual teaching service, to the DepEd: Provided, furthermore, That the
present allocation out of increases from tuition and other income shall be maintained:
Provided, finally, That the teacher’s salary subsidy received by the teacher shall form part of
the exclusions from taxable gross income in accordance with Section 32(B)(7)(e) of the
National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.

SEC. 11. Creation of the Bureau of Private Education. - The Bureau of Private Education
(BPE) under the DepEd shall be created to serve as the focal office for the administration,
supervision, and regulation of all matters relating to private basic education institutions,
including government assistance and subsidies. It shall be headed by a Bureau Director.

It shall have the following powers and functions:

a. Monitor and assess the implementation of the private basic education institutions of the
   policies, plans, and programs established by the DepEd as recommended by BPE.

b. Set and enforce minimum standards for programs and institutions of learning, subject to
   public hearing.

c. Ensure that the activities, efforts, and performance of private basic education institutions
   shall meet high standards of excellence and accountability.
d. Administer and implement the policies and programs related to the assistance and
   subsidies given by the government to students, teachers, and schools in private education.

e. Establish and support partnerships with individual private education institutions to respond
   effectively to the pressing needs of students and teachers, and to provide learning
   solutions to underserved communities.

f.   Monitor and ensure compliance of private basic education institutions to the promulgated
     rules and regulations of the DepEd in all matters concerning private schools, including
     tuition fee increases; and

g. Such other powers and functions may be necessary for the effective and efficient
   implementation of this Act.

SEC. 12. Program Administration and Implementation. - The BEAC shall be responsible
for policy guidance and direction, monitoring and evaluation of new and existing programs
while the Government Assistance and Subsidies Service (GASS) under the BPE shall be
responsible for the day-to-day administration and implementation of the programs of
assistance to private basic education.

       The BEAC shall be composed of the Secretary of the DepEd, as Chairperson, and the
Secretary of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) and the Secretary of
the DBM, as members.

        The alternate representative of the Chairperson shall possess the rank of an
Undersecretary while the alternate representatives of the members shall possess the rank of
not less than an Assistant Secretary to secure a quorum.

        The BEAC shall meet, from time to time, as the need arises, to assess the effectivity
of the programs under this Act and to propose to Congress other forms of assistance to
participating students and schools.

       The GASS shall serve as the permanent secretariat for the implementation of the GAS
programs under this Act.

       The DepEd may engage the services of a third party co-implementor of the GAS
programs under this Act.

SEC. 13. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation of the DepEd. Thereafter, such
amount shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 14. Penalties. - In case of violation of this Act by a private school, the DepEd may bar,
after due process, such school from participating in or benefiting from the programs of this Act,
and from other programs of the DepEd, without prejudice to administrative and criminal
charges that may be filed against the school or its responsible officers under existing laws.

SEC. 15. Transitory Provision. - Subject to the availability of funds, the implementation of
the provisions stipulated in this Act for the kindergarten and elementary levels shall gradually
commence in school year 2026-2027, as may be determined by the DepEd.
SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the approval
of this Act, the DepEd shall, in consultation with the NEDA, the DBM, and other concerned
stakeholders from the national associations of private basic education institutions, promulgate
the rules and regulations implementing the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 17. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is held invalid or unconstitutional,
the other provisions not so declared shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. - Sections 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, and 15 of Republic Act
No. 8545, or the “Expanded Government Assistance to Students and Teachers in Private
Education Act”, which amended Republic Act No. 6728, or the “Government Assistance to
Students and Teachers in Private Education Act”, are hereby repealed. All other laws, decrees,
orders and rules and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

SEC 19 Effectivity. - Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the implementation of rules and
regulations, this Act shall take fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in
a newspaper of general circulation.
