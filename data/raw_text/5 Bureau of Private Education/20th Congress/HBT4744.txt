AN ACT

ESTABLISHING THE PRIVATE BASIC EDUCATION VOUCHER PROGRAM,
PROVIDING FOR THE CREATION OF THE BUREAU OF PRIVATE EDUCATION,
AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, REPEALING FOR THE PURPOSE
SECTIONS 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, AND 15 OF REPUBLIC ACT
NO. 8545, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “EXPANDED GOVERNMENT
ASSISTANCE TO STUDENTS AND TEACHERS IN PRIVATE EDUCATION ACT”,
WHICH AMENDED REPUBLIC ACT NO. 6728, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
“GOVERNMENT ASSISTANCE TO STUDENTS AND TEACHERS IN PRIVATE
EDUCATION ACT”

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philjppines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Private Basic
Education Vouchers Assistance Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the declared policy of the State to
protect and promote the right of ali citizens to quality education at all levels, and take
appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all. As part of its mandate, it
is the State's policy to establish and maintain a system of scholarship grants, subsidies,
and other incentives which shall be available to deserving students, as determined by

Likewise, the State recognizes the complementary roles of public and private

The State shall provide the mechanisms to augment the resources and enhance

basic elementary and secondary education to all Filipino students.

SEC. 3. Coverage. — This Act shall cover ail private basic education institutions
that receive government assistance in the form of vouchers for qualified students,
teachers and schools. This covers all the years of basic education.

The programs of assistance for students under this Act shall be extended only
to citizens of the Philippines.

SEC. 4. Criteria for Voucher Assistance. — The programs for voucher
assistance shall be based on a set of criteria which include, among others, the
following:

(a) Criteria for Students. — The voucher assistance may be provided to students
belonging to middle-income families and below, as determined by the Philippine
Statistics Authority (PSA), who attended public schools identified by the Department
of Education (DepEd) as congested or those living in areas with congested public
schools or without a nearby public school who wish to enroll in private basic
education schools.

Families with students who are recognized as most vulnerable and
underprivileged shall receive a higher voucher amount as determined by the Basic
Education Assistance Council (BEAC), in accordance with Section 6(d) of this Act.

For purposes of this Act, the DepEd shall include the definition and classification
of “middle-income” and “vulnerable and underprivileged” in the implementing rules
and regulations, under Section 17 of this Act: Provided, That the DepEd shall regularly
review and update the definition and classification accordingly, in coordination with
PSA, Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), and other key
government agencies.”

(b) Criteria for Schools. — The participating private basic education schools shall
receive assistance based on Quality Assurance under Section 6 of this Act, and with
due regard to their geographic distribution and student enrollment, as determined by
the BEAC: Provided, That schools with gaps in resources and facilities which are
located in geographically isolated, disadvantaged, and conflict-affected areas (GIDCA),
schools with the highest incidences of classroom congestion or multiple-shift
operations, and schools situated in high-risk or disaster-prone areas, shall be
exempted from the qualification criteria of geographic distribution and student
enrollment.

SEC. 5. Empowering Student Choice. — The DepEd shall create a system
that allows students and parents to select their preferred schools from a registry of
participating recognized schools, subject to availability of slots.

The registry of participating recognized private basic educational institutions
shall include their schools’ information, such as location, number of available slots,
number of students per classroom and level, extra-curricular programs, current tuition
fees, and top-up required in cases where the voucher amount is less than the tuition
fee, among others.

SEC. 6. Quality Assurance. — The DepEd shall establish, operate and
maintain a system and mechanism for monitoring program outcomes to ensure
accountability of participating recognized private basic educational institutions. This
covers the following areas:

(a) Improved Student Performance — Appropriate performance indicators to
determine the extent and outcome of the government assistance and subsidies (GAS)
programs including youth literacy rates, national and international assessment scores,
among others. For this purpose, the National Achievement Tests or any national
assessment examinations generally given by the DepEd shall be administered to all
eligible students in all participating private basic education institutions;

(b) Improved Student Outcomes — Tracer system to collect and evaluate the
career and employment path of its graduates, including learner performance in the
Comprehensive Rapid Literacy Assessment (CRLA), Rapid Math Assessment (RMA),
Philippine Informal Reading Inventory (PHIL-IRI), and other similar assessments
conducted by the DepEd;

(c) School Performance — School performance data indicating the mean,
median and mode of the scores of the students in the national examinations, and such
other measures of quality education delivery, which shall be published by the DepEd
and the participating schools regularly; and

(d) Priority Subsidy for Underprivileged Students — Distribution of the GAS
programs while ensuring that funding is equitably allocated following the order of
priority groups.

assistance to private basic education institutions and teachers participating in the

SEC. 8. Basic Education Voucher Program. — Under the basic education
voucher program, kindergarten, elementary, and secondary students enrolled in
recognized private basic education schools charging school fees shall be provided by
the government with vouchers in such amount as may be determined by the DepEd:
Provided, That the amount shail not be less than the amount currently provided by
the DepEd: Provided, further, That the government shali reimburse the vouchers
from the schools concerned within sixty (60) days from the submission of complete
documents, as required by the BEAC, to the DepEd.

The budget for the basic education voucher program shall be equitably
allocated and distributed among all regions including the Bangsamoro Autonomous
Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) based on official data sources including, but not
limited to, congestion data and open seats, giving priority to underprivileged students:
Provided, That pursuant to Section 2(V), Article V of the Bangsamoro Organic Law,

Schools participating in the basic education voucher program may increase

SEC. 9. In-Service Training or Inset. — For the purpose of improving the
quality of teaching in private basic education, the DepEd may propose allocation, in
its annual budget, for Inset programs for the upgrading of knowledge and teaching
competencies in critical subject areas and for the modernization of teaching
techniques and strategies, including training on the use of computers and of other
multi-media educational technologies to assist instruction: Provided, That the
amount available to private basic education shall not exceed the amount available to
public basic education as per capita basis: Provided, further, That the training
programs shall be approved by the DepEd.

SEC. 10. Teachers’ Salary Subsidy. —- The DepEd may propose allocation,
in its annual budget, for government subsidy to teachers in recognized private basic
education schools participating in the programs of assistance under this Act: Provided,
That the government shall provide the teachers with salary subsidy, to be
determined, reviewed, and updated regularly by the DepEd, at least every three
(3) years: Provided, further, That the government shall disburse payment within
sixty (60) days after the close of the school year upon submission of proof of
complete documents, including but not limited to qualification and actual teaching
service, to the DepEd: Provided, furthermore, That the present allocation out of
increases from tuition and other income shall be maintained: Provided, finally,
That the salary subsidy received by the teacher shall form part of the exclusions
from gross income in accordance with Section 32(B)(7)(e) of the National Internal
Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.

SEC. 11. Institutionalization of Anti-Fraud Mechanisms in the
Expanded Government Assistance to Students and Teachers in Private
Education (E-GASTPE). — DepEd shall adopt and implement robust anti-fraud
safeguards, including but not limited to:

compliance;

(b) Enhanced audit and monitoring protocols, including periodic field validations

(c) Whistleblower protection mechanisms to encourage reporting of

SEC. 12. Creation of the Bureau of Private Education. — The Bureau of
Private Education (BPE) under the DepEd shail be created to serve as the focal office
for the administration, supervision, and regulation of all matters relating to private
basic education institutions, including government assistance and subsidies. It shall
be headed by a Bureau Director.

It shall have the following powers and functions:

(a) Monitor and assess the implementation of the private basic education
institutions of the policies, plans, and programs established by the DepEd as
recommended by BPE;

(b) Set and enforce minimum standards for programs and institutions of
learning, subject to public hearing;

(c) Ensure that the activities, efforts, and performance of private basic
education institutions shall meet the high standards of excellence and accountability;

(d) Administer and implement the policies and programs related to the
assistance and subsidies given by the government to students, teachers, and schools
in private education;

(e) Establish and support partnerships with individual private education
institutions to respond effectively to the pressing needs of students and teachers, and
to provide learning solutions to underserved communities;

SEC. 13. Program Administration and Implementation. — The BEAC shall
be responsible for policy guidance and direction, monitoring and evaluation of new
and existing programs while the Government Assistance and Subsidies Service (GASS)
under the BPE shall be responsible for the day-to-day administration and
implementation on the programs of assistance to private basic education.

The BEAC shall be composed of the Secretary of the DepEd, as Chairperson,
and the Secretary of the DEPDev and Secretary of the DBM as members.

The alternate representative of the Chairperson shall possess the rank of an
Undersecretary while the alternate representatives of the members shall possess the
rank of not less than an Assistant Secretary in order to secure a quorum.

The BEAC shall meet, from time to time, as the need arises, to assess the
effectivity of the programs under this Act and to propose to Congress other forms of
assistance to participating students and schools.

SEC. 14. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the _ initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation
of the DepEd. Thereafter, such amount shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SEC. 15. Penalties. — In case of violation of this Act by a private school, the
DepEd may bar, after due process, such school from participating in or benefiting from
the programs of this Act, and from other programs of the DepEd, without prejudice
to administrative and criminal charges that may be filed against the school or its
responsible officers under existing laws.

SEC. 16. Transitory Provision. — Subject to the availability of funds, the
implementation of the provisions stipulated in this Act for the kindergarten and
elementary levels shall gradually commence in school year 2026-2027, as may be
determined by the DepEd.

Until such time as the DepEd has issued the necessary rules and guidelines
for the proper implementation of this Act, but in no case later than five (5) years from
its effectivity, the existing educational service contracting (ESC) program for the junior
high school shall continue to be implemented.

SEC. 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the approval of this Act, the DepEd shall, in consultation with the DEPDev, the
DBM, and other concerned stakeholders from the national associations of private basic
education institutions, promulgate the rules and regulations implementing the
provisions of this Act.

SEC. 18. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not so declared shall remain in full force and
effect.

SEC. 19. Repealing Clause. — Sections 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, and
15 of Republic Act No. 8545, or the “Expanded Government Assistance to Students
and Teachers in Private Education Act, which amended Republic Act No. 6728, or the
“Government Assistance to Students and Teachers in Private Education Act,” are
hereby repealed. All other laws, decrees, orders and rules and regulations contrary
to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended
accordingly.
