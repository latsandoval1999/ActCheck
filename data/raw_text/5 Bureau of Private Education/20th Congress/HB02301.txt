                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Every Filipino child deserves a fair chance at quality education—regardless of
whether they are born into wealth or poverty, or whether they live in the heart of the city
or in a remote province. Education is not a privilege for the few; it is a constitutional right
enshrined in Article XIV, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution, which mandates the State to
“protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and take appropriate
steps to make such education accessible to all.”

       Yet, for many families in the country, access to quality basic education remains a
struggle. Public schools, especially in urban centers, continue to suffer from overcrowded
classrooms, limited teaching resources, and overstretched faculty. Meanwhile, private
schools—which often have better facilities and smaller class sizes—remain out of reach
for many middle- and low-income families due to high tuition fees.

       This bill aims to institutionalize a comprehensive voucher system that will allow
Filipino learners, particularly those from disadvantaged backgrounds and congested
communities, to enroll in recognized private basic education institutions with the help of
government subsidies. By doing so, we not only decongest our public schools but also
empower families with real choices in their children’s education.

       The Private Basic Education Vouchers Assistance Act is not just a reform; it is a
commitment to fairness, equity, and genuine partnership. It reflects our shared hope that
no Filipino child should ever be deprived of quality education simply because of where
they were born or how much their family earns.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

14         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Private Basic
15   Education Vouchers Assistance Act”.
16
17          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to protect and
18   promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and shall take
19   appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all. As part of its mandate, it is
20   the State’s policy to establish and maintain a system of scholarship grants, subsidies, and
21   other incentives which shall be available to deserving students, as determined by the
22   guidelines, in both public and private schools, especially to the underprivileged.
23   Likewise, the State recognizes the complementary roles of public and private educational
24   institutions in the educational system and the invaluable contribution that the private
25   schools have made and will make to education.
26
27          The State shall provide the mechanisms to augment the resources and enhance
28   the quality of private basic education, specifically promoting the complementary roles
29   of public and private schools and thus recognize the government’s priority of providing
30   basic elementary and secondary education to all Filipino students.
31
32          SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all private basic education institutions
33   that receive government assistance in the form of vouchers for qualified students,
34   teachers and schools. This covers all the years of basic education.
 1
 2          The programs of assistance for students under this Act shall be extended only to
 3   citizens of the Philippines.

 5          SEC. 4. Criteria for Voucher Assistance. – The programs for voucher assistance
 6   shall be based on a set of criteria which include among others, the following:
 7
 8          (a)    Criteria for Students. – The voucher assistance will be provided to students
 9   from middle-income families, as determined by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA),
10   who attended public schools identified by the Department of Education as congested or
11   those living in areas with congested public schools or without a nearby public school
12   who wish to enroll in private basic education schools.
13
14        Students who are most vulnerable and underprivileged shall receive a higher
15   voucher amount as determined by the Basic Education Assistance Council (BEAC).
16
17          (b)    Criteria for Schools. – The participating private basic education schools
18   shall receive assistance based on Quality Assurance under Section 6 of this Act, and the
19   geographic spread and size of student enrollment, as likewise determined by the BEAC,
20   except schools with gaps in resources and facilities which are located in geographically
21   isolated and disadvantaged and conflict-affected areas, or GIDCA schools. Private
22   schools shall undergo the process of recognition and periodic assessment to be able to
23   participate in the programs for voucher assistance.
24
25          SEC. 5. Empowering Student Choice. – The Department of Education (DepEd)
26   shall create a system that allows students and parents to select their preferred schools
27   from a registry of participating recognized schools, subject to availability of slots.
28          The registry of participating recognized private basic educational institutions
29   shall include their schools’ information, such as location, number of available slots,
30   number of students per classroom and level, extra-curricular programs, current tuition
31   fees, and top-up required in cases where the voucher amount is less than the tuition fee,
32   among others.
33
34           SEC. 6. Quality Assurance. – The DepEd shall establish, operate and maintain a
35   system and mechanism for monitoring program outcomes to ensure accountability of
36   participating recognized private basic educational institutions. This covers the following
37   areas:
38           (a)   Improved Student Performance – Appropriate performance indicators to
39   determine the extent and outcome of the government assistance and subsidies (GAS)
40   programs including youth literacy rates, national and international assessment scores,
41   among others. For this purpose, the National Achievement Tests and all national
42   assessment examinations generally given by the DepEd shall be administered to all
43   eligible students in all participating private basic education institutions;
44
45          (b)   Improved Student Outcomes – Tracer system to collect and evaluate the
46   career and employment path of its graduates;
47
48         (c)   School Performance – School performance data indicating the mean,
49   median and mode of the scores of the students in the national examinations, and such
50   other measures of quality education delivery; and

 4   priority groups.

36          SEC. 8. Basic Education Voucher Program. – Under the basic education voucher
37   program, kindergarten, elementary, and secondary students enrolled in recognized
38   private basic education schools charging school fees shall be provided by the
39   government with vouchers in such amount as may be determined by the DepEd:
40   Provided, That the amount shall not be less than the amount currently provided by the
41   DepEd: Provided, That the government shall disburse payment of the vouchers to the
42   schools concerned within sixty (60) days from the submission of complete documents to
43   the DepEd.
44
45          The budget for the basic education voucher program shall be equitably allocated
46   and distributed among all regions including the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in
47   Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) based on official data sources including, but not limited
48   to, congestion data and open seats, giving priority to underprivileged students.
49

 7          Schools participating in the basic education voucher program may increase
 8   tuition on the condition that seventy percent (70%) of the amount of the tuition increases
 9   shall go to the payment of salaries, wages, allowances, and other statutory benefits of
10   teaching and non-teaching personnel, except administrators who are principal
11   stockholders of the school, and may be used to cover salary increase as provided for in

13   approved and made effective. At least twenty percent (20%) of the tuition increases shall
14   go to the improvement or modernization of buildings, equipment, libraries, laboratories,

16   purpose, schools shall maintain a separate record of accounts for all assistance received

21          SEC. 9. In-Service Training Fund or Inset Fund. – For the purpose of
22   improving the quality of teaching in private basic education, there is hereby established
23   in the DepEd an Inset Fund to provide for training programs for the upgrading of
24   knowledge and teaching competencies in critical subject areas and for the modernization
25   of teaching techniques and strategies, including training on the use of computers and of
26   other multi-media educational technologies to assist instruction: Provided, That the
27   amount of Inset Fund available to private basic education shall not exceed that available
28   to public basic education as per capita basis: Provided,
29   further, That the training programs shall be approved by the DepEd.
30
31          SEC. 10. Teachers’ Salary Subsidy Fund. – A Teachers’ Salary Subsidy Fund is
32   hereby established in the DepEd to grant government subsidy to teachers in recognized
33   private basic education schools participating in the programs of assistance under in this
34   Act: Provided, That the government shall provide the teachers with salary subsidy, to
35   be determined by the DepEd: Provided, further, That the government shall disburse
36   payment within sixty (60) days after the close of the school year upon submission of
37   complete documents, including but not limited to qualification and actual teaching
38   service, to the DepEd: Provided, furthermore, That the present allocation out of
39   increases from tuition and other income shall be maintained: Provided, finally, That
40   the teacher’s salary subsidy received by the teacher shall form part of the exclusions from
41   taxable gross income in accordance with Section 32(B)(7)(e) of the National Internal
42   Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.

48   headed by a Bureau Director.

21          SEC. 12. Program Administration and Implementation. – The BEAC shall be
22   responsible for policy guidance and direction, monitoring and evaluation of new and
23   existing programs while the Government Assistance and Subsidies Service (GASS)

25   implementation on the programs of assistance to private basic education.

27          The BEAC shall be composed of the Secretary of the DepEd, as Chairperson, and
