                                       AN ACT
     ESTABLISHING THE PRIVATE BASIC EDUCATION VOUCHER PROGRAM,
     PROVIDING FOR THE CREATION OF THE BUREAU OF PRIVATE EDUCATION,
     AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, REPEALING FOR THE PURPOSE
     SECTIONS 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, AND 15 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO.
     8545, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “EXPANDED GOVERNMENT
     ASSISTANCE TO STUDENTS AND TEACHERS IN PRIVATE EDUCATION ACT”,
     WHICH AMENDED REPUBLIC ACT NO. 6728, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
     “GOVERNMENT ASSISTANCE TO STUDENTS AND TEACHERS IN PRIVATE
     EDUCATION ACT”

           Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Private Basic
 2   Education Vouchers Assistance Act”.
 3
 4           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to
 5   protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and shall
 6   take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all. As part of its mandate,
 7   it is the State's policy to establish and maintain a system of scholarship grants,
 8   subsidies, and other incentives which shall be available to deserving students, as
 9   determined by the guidelines, in both public and private schools, especially to the
10   underprivileged. Likewise, the State recognizes the complementary roles of public and
11   private educational institutions in the educational system and the invaluable
12   contribution that the private schools have made and will make to education.
 1
 2          The State shall provide the mechanisms to augment the resources and enhance
 3   the quality of private basic education, specifically promoting the complementary roles
 4   of public and private schools and thus recognize the government's priority of providing
 5   basic elementary and secondary education to all Filipino students.
 6
 7          SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all private basic education institutions
 8   that receive government assistance in the form of vouchers for qualified students,
 9   teachers and schools. This covers all the years of basic education.
10
11           The programs of assistance for students under this Act shall be extended only
12   to citizens of the Philippines.
13
14          SEC. 4. Criteria for Voucher Assistance. – The programs for voucher
15   assistance shall be based on a set of criteria which include among others, the following:
16
17          (a) Criteria for Students. – The voucher assistance shall be given to students
18   enrolled in the private basic education schools and prioritizes those who are most
19   vulnerable and underprivileged, or those belonging to the bottom first to fifth income
20   deciles, as determined by the Philippine Statistics Authority. The priority students
21   shall receive a higher voucher amount as determined by the Basic Education
22   Assistance Council (BEAC).
23
24          (b) Criteria for Schools. – The participating private basic education schools shall
25   receive assistance based on Quality Assurance under Section 6 of this Act, and the
26   geographic spread and size of student enrollment, as likewise determined by the BEAC,
27   except schools with gaps in resources and facilities which are located in geographically
28   isolated and disadvantaged and conflict-affected areas, or GIDCA schools. Private
29   schools shall undergo the process of recognition and periodic assessment to be able
30   to participate in the programs for voucher assistance.
31
32          SEC. 5. Empowering Student Choice. – The Department of Education
33   (DepEd) shall create a system that allows students and parents to select their
34   preferred schools from a registry of participating recognized schools, subject to
35   availability of slots.
36
37           The registry of participating recognized private basic educational institutions
38   shall include their schools’ information, such as location, number of available slots,
39   number of students per classroom and level, extra-curricular programs, current tuition
40   fees, and top-up required in cases where the voucher amount is less than the tuition
41   fee, among others.
42
43          SEC. 6. Quality Assurance. – The DepEd shall establish, operate and
44   maintain a system and mechanism for monitoring program outcomes to ensure
45   accountability of participating recognized private basic educational institutions. This
46   covers the following areas:
47



                                               -2-
 1           (a) Improved Student Performance – Appropriate performance indicators to
 2   determine the extent and outcome of the government assistance and subsidies (GAS)
 3   programs including youth literacy rates, national and international assessment scores,
 4   among others. For this purpose, the National Achievement Tests and all national
 5   assessment examinations generally given by the DepEd shall be administered to all
 6   eligible students in all participating private basic education institutions;
 7
 8          (b) Improved Student Outcomes – Tracer system to collect and evaluate the
 9   career and employment path of its graduates;
10
11         (c) School Performance – School performance data indicating the mean,
12   median and mode of the scores of the students in the national examinations, and such
13   other measures of quality education delivery; and
14
15           (d) Priority Subsidy for Underprivileged Students – Distribution of the GAS
16   programs while ensuring that funding is equitably allocated following the order of
17   priority groups.
18
19          SEC. 7. Other Forms of Assistance. – The following shall consist of
20   assistance to private basic education institutions and teachers participating in the
21   voucher program under this Act:
22
23          The forms of assistance granted to teachers in the participating recognized
24   private basic education schools include:
25
26         (a) In-service training fund for teachers in private basic education schools;
27         (b) Scholarships for qualified private basic education teachers enrolled in
28             quality graduate programs; and
29         (c) Participation in government-funded in-service training for basic education
30             teachers.
31
32           The DepEd may establish a program of assistance for the development of
33   qualified recognized private basic education schools to cover the following areas of
34   school operation:
35
36         (a) Institutional Development;
37         (b) Organizational Development;
38         (c) Human Resource Development;
39         (d) Curriculum and Instructional Development;
40         (e) Research and Planning; and
41         (f) Facilities and School Improvement.
42
43          Consistent with Sec. 10 of Republic Act No. 10533, or the “Enhanced Basic
44   Education Act of 2013”, the DepEd, in consultation with the Department of Budget
45   and Management (DBM), may develop other forms of financial arrangements and
46   engage the services of private basic education schools based on the complementary
47   roles of public and private institutions in the educational system.
48

 1          SEC. 8. Basic Education Voucher Program . – Under the basic education
 2   voucher program, kindergarten, elementary, and secondary students enrolled in
 3   recognized private basic education schools charging school fees shall be provided by
 4   the government with vouchers in such amount as may be determined by the DepEd:
 5   Provided, That the amount shall not be less than the amount currently provided by
 6   the DepEd: Provided, That the government shall reimburse the vouchers from the
 7   schools concerned within sixty (60) days from the submission of complete documents,
 8   as required by the BEAC, to the DepEd.
 9
10          The budget for the basic education voucher program shall be equitably
11   allocated and distributed among all regions including the Bangsamoro Autonomous
12   Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) based on official data sources including, but not
13   limited to, congestion data and open seats, giving priority to underprivileged students.
14
15           Pursuant to Section 2(V), Article V of the Bangsamoro Organic Law, the
16   Bangsamoro Government shall exercise its authority over education for the areas
17   under the BARMM and will directly implement the same program to be charged against
18   their block grant. The DepEd shall provide technical assistance in the implementation
19   of the GAS Program, when necessary.
20
21          Schools participating in the basic education voucher program may increase
22   tuition on the condition that seventy percent (70%) of the amount of the tuition
23   increases shall go to the payment of salaries, wages, allowances, and other statutory
24   benefits of teaching and non-teaching personnel, except administrators who are
25   principal stockholders of the school, and may be used to cover salary increase as
26   provided for in the collective bargaining agreements existing or in force at the time
27   when this Act is approved and made effective. At least twenty percent (20%) of the
28   tuition increases shall go to the improvement or modernization of buildings,
29   equipment, libraries, laboratories, gymnasia and similar facilities and to the payment
30   of other costs of operation. For this purpose, schools shall maintain a separate record
31   of accounts for all assistance received from the government, any tuition increase, and
32   the detailed disposition and use thereof, which record shall be made available for
33   periodic inspection as may be determined by the DepEd.
34
35          SEC. 9. In-Service Training Fund or Inset Fund. – For the purpose of
36   improving the quality of teaching in private basic education, there is hereby
37   established in the DepEd an Inset Fund to provide for training programs for the
38   upgrading of knowledge and teaching competencies in critical subject areas and for
39   the modernization of teaching techniques and strategies, including training on the use
40   of computers and of other multi-media educational technologies to assist instruction:
41   Provided, That the amount of Inset Fund available to private basic education shall
42   not exceed that available to public basic education as per capita basis: Provided,
43   further, That the training programs shall be approved by the DepEd.
44
45          SEC. 10. Teachers' Salary Subsidy Fund. – A Teachers' Salary Subsidy
46   Fund is hereby established in the DepEd to grant government subsidy to teachers in
47   recognized private basic education schools participating in the programs of assistance
48   under in this Act: Provided, That the government shall provide the teachers with


                                               -4-
 1   salary subsidy, to be determined by the DepEd, which shall be disbursed within
 2   sixty (60) days after the close of the school year upon submission of proof of
 3   qualification and actual teaching service directly to the concerned participating
 4   private basic education schools: Provided, further, That the present allocation out
 5   of increases from tuition and other income shall be maintained: Provided,
 6   finally, That the teacher’s salary subsidy received by the teacher shall form part
 7   of the exclusions from taxable gross income in accordance with Section 32(B)(7)(e)
 8   of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.
 9
10          SEC. 11. Creation of the Bureau of Private Education. – The Bureau of
11   Private Education (BPE) under the DepEd shall be created to serve as the focal office
12   for the administration, supervision, and regulation of all matters relating to private
13   basic education institutions, including government assistance and subsidies. It shall
14   be headed by a Bureau Director.
15
16         It shall have the following powers and functions:
17
18         (a)   Monitor and assess the implementation of the private basic education
19               institutions of the policies, plans, and programs established by the DepEd
20               as recommended by BPE;
21         (b)   Set and enforce minimum standards for programs and institutions of
22               learning, subject to public hearing;
23         (c)   Ensure that the activities, efforts, and performance of private basic
24               education institutions shall meet the high standards of excellence and
25               accountability;
26         (d)   Administer and implement the policies and programs related to the
27               assistance and subsidies given by the government to students, teachers,
28               and schools in private education;
29         (e)   Establish and support partnerships with individual private education
30               institutions to respond effectively to the pressing needs of students and
31               teachers, and to provide learning solutions to underserved communities;
32         (f)   Monitor and ensure compliance of private basic education institutions to
33               the promulgated rules and regulations of the DepEd in all matters
34               concerning private schools, including tuition fee increases; and
35         (g)   Such other powers and functions as may be necessary for the effective
36               and efficient implementation of this Act.
37
38         SEC. 12. Program Administration and Implementation. – The BEAC shall
39   be responsible for policy guidance and direction, monitoring and evaluation of new
40   and existing programs while the Government Assistance and Subsidies Service (GASS)
41   under the BPE shall be responsible for the day-to-day administration and
42   implementation on the programs of assistance to private basic education.
43
44         The BEAC shall be composed of the Secretary of the DepEd, as Chairperson,
45   and the Secretary of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) and
46   Secretary of the DBM as members.
47

 1          The alternate representative of the Chairperson shall possess the rank of an
 2   Undersecretary while the alternate representatives of the members shall possess the
 3   rank of not less than an Assistant Secretary in order to secure a quorum.
 4
 5           The BEAC shall meet, from time to time, as the need arises, to assess the
 6   effectivity of the programs under this Act and to propose to Congress other forms of
 7   assistance to participating students and schools.
 8
 9         The GASS shall serve as the permanent secretariat for the implementation of
10   the government assistance and subsidies programs under this Act.
11
12         The DepEd may engage the services of a third party co-implementor of the
13   government assistance and subsidies program under this Act.
14
15          SEC. 13. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
16   implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation
17   of the DepEd. Thereafter, such amount shall be included in the annual General
18   Appropriations Act.
19
20          SEC. 14. Penalties. – In case of violation of this Act by a private school, the
21   DepEd may bar, after due process, such school from participating in or benefiting from
22   the programs of this Act, and from other programs of the DepEd, without prejudice
23   to administrative and criminal charges that may be filed against the school or its
24   responsible officers under existing laws.
25
26         SEC. 15. Transitory Provision. – Subject to the availability of funds, the
27   implementation of the provisions stipulated in this Act for the kindergarten and
28   elementary levels shall gradually commence in school year 2024-2025, as may be
29   determined by the DepEd.
30
31          SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
32   from the approval of this Act, the DepEd shall, in consultation with the NEDA, the DBM,
33   and other concerned stakeholders from the national associations of private basic
34   education institutions, promulgate the rules and regulations implementing the
35   provisions of this Act.
36
37           SEC. 17. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
38   unconstitutional, the other provisions not so declared shall remain in full force and
39   effect.
40
41          SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. – Sections 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, and
42   15 of Republic Act No. 8545, or the “Expanded Government Assistance to Students
43   and Teachers in Private Education Act, which amended Republic Act No. 6728, or the
44   “Government Assistance To Students and Teachers In Private Education Act”, are
45   hereby repealed. All other laws, decrees, orders and rules and regulations contrary
46   to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended
47   accordingly.

OBJECTIVES:
 •    To make quality basic education accessible by providing financial assistance to
      learners in public basic education schools who wish to study in private schools
 •    To expand the government assistance and subsidies of the law to cover
      kindergarten, elementary, junior high school, and senior high school learners

KEY PROVISIONS:
 •    Expands the coverage of private basic education voucher beneficiaries to
      kindergarten, elementary, junior high school, and senior high school learners
 •    Sets specific criteria for voucher assistance programs given to students and
      participating private schools
 •    Empowers student choice through a system that allows students and parents to
      select their preferred schools from a registry of participating recognized schools,
      subject to availability of slots
 •    Grants other forms of specific assistance to private basic education institutions
      and teachers participating in the voucher program
•   Mandates the Department of Education (DepEd) to establish a program of
    assistance for the development of qualified recognized private basic education
    schools
•   Prescribes specific conditions in the implementation of the basic education
    voucher program, to wit:
    (a)   Equitable allocation of the voucher program among all regions, including
          the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM),
          based on official data sources including giving priority to underprivileged
          students;
    (b)   70% of the amount subsidized allotted for tuition or of the tuition increases
          shall go to payment of salaries, wages, allowances and other benefits of
          teaching and non-teaching personnel, except administrators who are
          principal stockholders of the school, and may be used to cover salary
          increase as provided for in the existing collective bargaining agreements;
    (c)   At least 20% of the voucher proceeds shall go to the improvement or
          modernization of buildings, equipment, libraries, laboratories, gymnasia
          and similar facilities and to the payment of other costs of operation
•   Establishes the following support mechanisms:
    (a)   In-service Training Fund or Inset Fund for the purpose of improving the
          quality of teaching in private basic education; and
    (b)   Teachers’ Salary Subsidy Fund to grant government subsidy to teachers in
          recognized private basic education schools participating in the voucher
          program
•   Creates the Bureau of Private Education (BPE) under the DepEd to serve as the
    focal office for the administration, supervision, and regulation of all matters
    relating to private basic education institutions, including government assistance
    and subsidies, and provides for its specific powers and functions
•   Mandates the Basic Education Assistance Council to be responsible for policy
    guidance and direction, monitoring and evaluation of new and existing programs
    of assistance, to be headed by the Secretary of Education as Chairperson, with
    representatives from National Economic and Development Authority and
    Department of Budget and Management, as members
•   Mandates the Government Assistance and Subsidies Service under the BPE to
    be responsible for the day-to-day administration and program implementation on
    the programs of assistance to private basic education
•   Allocates funds for the implementation of this Act to be included in the annual
    General Appropriations Act

•   Penalizes a private school, after due process, for violating this Act
•   Provides that the implementation of the provisions stipulated in this Act for the
    kindergarten and elementary levels shall gradually commence in school year
    2024-2025, as may be determined by the DepEd, subject to the availability of
    funds
•   Repeals Sections 2, 3, 4(2), 4(3), 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, and 15 of Republic Act No. 8545
    pertaining to basic education
