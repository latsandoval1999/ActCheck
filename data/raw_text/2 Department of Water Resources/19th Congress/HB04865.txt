                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

In 2015, the Philippines, along with all United Nations Member States, adopted the 2030 Agenda for
Sustainable Development. At its center are the 17 Sustainable Development Goals, a call for action
by all countries to end poverty and other deprivations while tackling climate change and preserving
the natural environment. Of the 17 goals, Sustainable Development Goal 6 aims to ensure the
availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all.

Based on the latest country snapshot, the Philippines needs to accelerate its efforts to achieve the
targets under Goal 6. After five years of implementation, progress has been slow with only 47% of
the population using a safely managed drinking water service, 61% of the population using a safely
managed sanitation service, and 82% of the population having a handwashing facility with soap and
water at home.

In terms of Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM), the Philippines reported moderate
gains in increasing its degree of implementation from 51% in 2017 to 560/o in 2020. However, the
Philippines will still need to fast-track refoms to come closer to the 100% target by 2030. In
particular, the Philippines will have to allocate more resources and improve coordination between
water-related initiatives to strengthen its implementation of IWRM.

This bill proposes to improve the implementation of IWRM in the country through the creation of a
Department of Water Resources that shall be the primary agency responsible for water policy in the
country. In addition, the bureaucracy will be streamlined to support the adoption of IWRM as the
strategic framework for national water management, policymaking, and planning. With the creation
of the Department of Water Resources, more attention will also be given to the funding requirements
of water-related initiatives in the country.
