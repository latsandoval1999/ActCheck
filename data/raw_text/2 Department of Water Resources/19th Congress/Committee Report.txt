 1                                        CHAPTER I
 2                                 GENERAL PROVISIONS
 3         Section 1. Short Title.- This Act shall be known as the "National Water
 4   Resources Management Act"
 5         Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy.-
 6     (a) The State recognizes that water is fundamental to individual life, health, and
 7         dignity, and that every person has a right to access safe and clean water for
 8         personal and domestic use;
 9     (b) The State affirms that the right of all Filipinos to safe and clean drinking water
10         and sanitation is essential to the full enjoyment of life and all human rights and
11         hence, the attainment of a nationwide water supply, sanitation, and sewerage
 1          services at reasonable rates must be pursued in an orderly, rational, efficient,
 2          and dynamic manner;
 3      (c) The State emphasizes that water is a public good subject to diverse use in
 4          economic, agricultural, and industrial activities, and is thus a scarce resource
 5          which must be carefully regulated to prevent resource depletion and to ensure
 6          its availability for future generations;
 7      (d) The State also maintains that water is indispensable to the existence and
 8          sustainability of natural ecosystems, and as such its use must neither endanger
 9          the balance and health of the environment nor compromise the rhythm and
10          harmony of nature;
11      (e) The State further maintains that water resources are interconnected with
12          climate, soil condition, land use and land cover, forest cover, and other natural
13          resources. As such, the management of water will require inter-sectoral and
14          inter-agency collaboration as well as harmonization of laws, policies,
15          institutions, and stakeholders; and
16     (f) Finally, while the State maintains that all water resources remain under its full
17          ownership, control, and supervision, it emphasizes that it may enter into
18          arrangements with the private sector, as well as with civil society and
19          community-based organizations, on the basis of beneficial use, to ensure the
20          efficient and affordable delivery of water supply, sanitation, and septage
21          services to end-users.
22          In accordance with these precepts, it is hereby declared the policy of the State
23   to provide for the rational, evidence-based, and integrated management of all water
24   resources in the country to ensure universal access to safe, adequate, and affordable
25   water supply, sanitation, and septage services, through a policy and regulatory regime
26   that encourages innovation and responsible private sector participation, while ensuring
27   ecological preservation and environmental sustainability.
28         Sec. 3. Definition of Terms.-Jhe. following terms are hereby defined as follows:
29     (a) Bulk water supplier refers to any entity, natural or juridical, whether public or
30          private, supplying or intending to supply large quantities of raw or treated water
31         to buyers who will use it for own consumption, such as industrial companies.
 1       or for retail distribution to consumers such as water service providers or real
 2       estate developers managing their own distribution network;
 3   (b) Environmental services refer to qualitative functions of natural non-produced
 4       assets of land, water, and air, including related ecosystems, and their biota,
 5       and which may be categorized into three basic types: (i) disposal services which
 6       reflect the functions of the natural environment as an absorptive sink for
 7       residuals; (ii) productive services which reflect the economic functions of
 8       providing natural resource inputs and space for production and consumption,
 9       and (iii) consumer or consumption services which provide for physiological as
10      well as recreational and related needs of human beings;
11   (c) Flood Control refers to methods, acts, and protocols to be observed in order to
12       prevent and reduce the detrimental and catastrophic effects of flood waters
13      which include sediment-laden or turbid flows, and hyper-concentrated flows or
14      debris flows;
15   (d) Flood Risk Management (FRM) refers to such acts of defining and determining
16      the appropriate methods, acts and protocols aimed at preventing and reducing
17      the risk of incurring loss of both life and property due to flood waters. Flood
18      risk management consists of a cycle of prevention, mitigation, adaptation,
19      preparedness, and early warning, and, response and recovery. The elements
20      of FRM include: integrating land-use planning and coastal zone management
21      into water management; adopting a holistic approach so that FRM is part of
22      wider risk or multi-hazard management of earthquakes, landslides, and storm
23      surges; managing risk and uncertainty as a whole so it is not only hydrological
24      uncertainties but also social, economic, and political uncertainties on account
25      of human behavior and cultural dimensions of FRM;
26   (e) Infrastructure and public works refer only to wholly or primarily water-related
27      projects and shall not incorporate projects that do not otherwise fall within the
28      purview of the objectives, powers, and duties of the Department of Water
29      Resources created under this Act;
30   (f) Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) refers to a systematic,
31      collaborative and multi-stakeholder process which promotes the coordinated
32      development and management of water, land, and related resources within
 1       geophysical boundaries in order to maximize the resultant economic and social
 2       welfare in an equitable manner and without compromising the sustainability of
 3       vital ecosystems;
 4    (g) License refers to the Water Supply or Sanitation Services Operating License
 5       that the Water Regulatory Commission is authorized to grant and issue to
 6       Service Providers;
 7    (h) Licensee refers to the Service Provider to whom a License is granted or issued
 8       by the Water Regulatory Commission;
 9    (i) Missionary Water refers to water supply or services to areas that have no
10       existing safe water supply and services due to geographic limitation or absence
11       of economic and market viability;
12    (j) National Water Resources Management Plan (NWRMP) refers to a policy
13       document that incorporates a framework to guide the development and
14       management of all water resources in the Philippines, the general strategies
15       and work to be pursued to ensure the sustainability of the resource and work
16       towards the water security; and provides top-level direction on the utilization
17       of existing water resources in line with national development plans, policies,
18       and programs in accordance with the IWRM framework. The NWRMP shall also
19       include a waterinfrastructure development program          for all waters of the
20       country as well as provide general guidance on how this shall be supported
21       within an integrated financing framework that can draw on public, private and
22       international development funding sources;
23   (k) Regulatory Units refer to the regulatory offices of the Water Regulatory
24       Commission established under this Act;
25   (I) River basin refers to an area of land drained by a stream or body of fixed water
26       and its tributaries which have a common outlet for surface run-off;
27   (m) River Basin Organization (RBO) refers to multi-stakeholder organizations which
28       plan, coordinate, and monitor activities within a river basin cluster;
29   (j\) Sanitation refers to the provision of facilities and services for the safe
30       management of septage and sewage, from the collection to containment, and
31       storage and treatment onsite or conveyance, treatment, and eventual safe end
 1         use or disposal, and includes the safe management of solid waste and animal
 2         waste;
 3    (o) Septage refers to the liquid and solid material pumped from a septic tank,
 4         cesspool, or other primary treatment source.
 5    (p) Septage Management refers to the provision of proper collection, treatment,
 6         and disposal of septage;
 7    (q) Service Area refers to the area covered by the water distribution system,
 8         sewerage, and septage managementservices;
 9    (r) Service Provider- refers to any entity, natural or juridical, whether private or
10         public, providing or intending to provide water supply, including bulk water
11         suppliers, sewerage, septage treatment, and disposal services for domestic,
12         residential, institutional, industrial, or commercial use;
13    (s) Sewage refers to water-borne human or animal wastes, excluding oil or oil
14         waste, removed from residences, buildings, institutions, and industrial and
15         commercial establishments together with such groundwater, surface water and
16         storm water as may be present including such waste from vessels, offshore
17         structures, other receptacles intended to receive or retain waste or other places
18         or the combination thereof;
19    (t) Sewerage refers to any system or network of sanitary pipelines, ditches,
20         channels or conduits including pumping stations, lift stations and force mains,
21         service connections including other infrastructure, devices or appliances
22         appurtenant thereto, which includes the collection, transport, pumping and
23         treatment of sewage to a point of disposal;
24   (u) Sewers refer to pipes or such other works or structures which are built and
25         constructed to carry, transport, and dispose sewage;
26   (v) Tariffs refer to such amounts which may be charged by licensees for their water
27         supply, sewerage, or sanitation services based on principles, standards, and
28         guidelines established by the Water Regulatory Commission;
29   (w)            Water Demand Management refers to any method whether technical,
30         economic, administrative, financial, or social that will accomplish one or more
31         of the following:
 1         (i)    Reduce the quantity or quality of water required to accomplish a specific
 2                task;
 3        (ii)    Adjust the nature of the task or the way it is undertaken so that it can
 4                be accomplished with less water or with lower quality water;
 5        (iii)   Reduce the loss in quantity or quality of water as it flows from source,
 6                through use, to disposal;
 7       (iv)     Shift the timing of use from peak to off-peak periods; and
 8        (v)     Increase the ability of the water system to continue to serve society
 9                during times when water is in short supply.
10   (x) Water District refers to government-owned and controlled corporations
11       organized under Presidential Decree No. 198, otherwise known as the
12       "Provincial Water Utilities Act of 1973", as amended, created primarily to
13       acquire, install, operate, maintain and improve water supply and distribution
14       systems for domestic, industrial, and municipal uses of residents and to provide,
15       maintain, and operate wastewater collection, treatment and disposal facilities
16       within the boundaries of the district;
17   (y) Water poiiution refers to any alteration of the physical, chemical, biological, or
18       radiological properties of a body of water resulting in the impairment of its
19       purity or quality;
20   (z) Water Resource refers to water under the ground or groundwater; water above
21       the ground such as surface water, run-offs, floods, stormwater and urban
22       drainage; treated wastewater, water in the atmosphere or rain water; and the
23       waters of the sea within the territorial jurisdiction of the Philippines;
24   (aa) Water resource aiiocation refers to the process of sharing the limited water
25       resources betweencompeting users.          This consists of determining     water
26       availability and determining how the water should be shared among competing
27       users. For the purpose of thisAct,resource allocation      shall also mean water
28       resource allocation;
29   (bb) Water Resources Regions refer to the contiguous clusters of river basins created
30       under this Act for administrative purposes under the Department of Water
31       Resources;
 1      (cc) Water Resources Regional Offices (WRROs) refer to the field offices of the
 2            Department of Water Resources, as created under this Act, which are to be
 3            established in accordance with the identified water resources regions in the
 4            country;
 5      (dd) Water service cooperative refer to cooperatives organized primarily to own,
 6            operate, and manage water systems for the provision and distribution of
 7            potable water for the members and their households
 8     (ee) Water Service Providers (WSPs) refer to the water districts, any Local
 9            Government Unit (LGU)-run water utility, Barangay Waterworks and Sanitation
10            Association, Rural Waterworks and Sanitation Association, water service
11            cooperatives, and private sector entities that provide water supply services to
12            any given area;
13     (ff)   Water supply service refers to any activity comprising developed water sources,
14            communal faucet system or stand posts, waterworks system or individual house
15            connections, which include bulk suppliers, suppliers to subdivisions, or other
16            simiiar water service providers.
17                                                   CHAPTER II
18                              DEPARTMENT OF WATER RESOURCES
19            Sec. 4. Establishment of the Department of Water Resources. - There is hereby
20   established the Department of Water Resources, hereafter referred to as the
21   Department.
22            Sec. 5. Mandate and Objectives. -The Department shall be the primary poiicy,
23   planning, coordinating, implementing, monitoring, and administrative entity of the
24   Executive Branch of the government responsible for the comprehensive, sustainable,
25   climate-resilient, and integrated development and management of the water resources
26   of the Philippines, and their optimal allocation and use for domestic and municipal
27   water supply,       sanitation,   irrigation,    hydropower,   industry,   navigation,   flood
28   management, and recreation, and water utilization aspects of fisheries or aquaculture.
29            The Department shall also be the primary agency responsible for the planning
30   and policy formulation towards the attainment of universal access to safe, adequate,
31   affordable, and sustainable water supply, and improved sewerage and sanitation
32   services for all Filipinos.

24         Sec. 40. Setting Tariffs, Rates and Other Charges. - The Commission shall
25   establish tariffs, rates and other charges which are fair and reasonable, and ensure
26   environmental sustainability, economic viability, and a fair return on investments.
27         Service providers may apply for the setting of tariffs, rates, and charges based
28   on and consistent with a scientific and evidence-based rate-setting methodology that
29   the Commission shall, after due consultation, define and publish, taking into account
30   the following, among others:
31         (a)    Reasonable and prudent capital and recurrent costs of providing the
32                service including a reasonable rate of return on capital;


                                               46
 1          (b)   Efficiency of the service;
 2          (c)   Incentives for enhancement of efficiency;
 3          (d)   Capacity to pay of consumers;
 4          (e)   Equity considerations;
 5          (f)   Environmental sustainability;
 6          (g)   Administrative simplicity;
 7          (h)   In cases involving valid and subsisting concession agreements prior to
 8                the enactment of this Act, the methodology provided under the
 9                concession agreement;
10          (i)   To the fullest extent practicable, the terms and conditions of the tariffs,
11                rates or other price-setting mechanisms set forth in any concession
12                agreement, joint venture agreement or other similar agreement for the
13                provision of water or sanitation services by an existing service provider.
14         Tariffs, rates, and charges set by the Regulatory Units shall be presumed valid
15   and reasonable unless invalidated by the Commission, after due notice and hearing,
16   acting on a protest or contest duly filed with the Commission to ensure continuity of
