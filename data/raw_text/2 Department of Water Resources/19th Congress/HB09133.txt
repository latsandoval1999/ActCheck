                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

Access to potable, sanitary and safe drinking water is the most basic requirement for
human health, and it is the government's responsibility to ensure that every citizen has
access to good health and ensure the well-being of our people.

On the contrary, the governance and maintenance of our water resources remains lacking
in systematic and collaborative efforts which contributed as the major problem of unsafe
and unsanitary water. Safe drinking and proper sanitation facilities should not be a
privilege of those who are in rich or live in urban centers. With the growing population of
our country and economic growth brought inadequate management of urban, rural,
industrial, and agricultural wastewater resulting in the drinking-water of millions of
Filipinos becoming dangerously contaminated or chemically polluted. The surface and
groundwater in some areas across the country remain contaminated because of open
defecation, improper management, and disposal of human excreta and blackwater that
exposed communities to waterborne diseases especially those coming from rural areas
where poverty is high. Such inequalities are aggravated and the lack of access to potable
water and sanitation leads to poor health and low productivity.

To monitor and properly implement a system that will guarantee safe potable water, there
should be a primary agency responsible for national policy-making, coordination, and
management of all the water resources in the Philippines. All government agencies related
to water shall be rationalized under this Department. The Integrated Water Regulatory
Commission shall enforce effective and adequate delivery of water and sanitation services
throughout the country under a coverage or framework of quality and performance
targets. It must respond to the current environmental challenges and mange water
resources through a concerted government effort engaging various sectors.

Thus, this bill seeks to create a Department of Water Resources and Water Regulatory
Commission and Integrated Water Regulatory Commission.

the Department of Water Resources, hereinafter referred to as the Department.

SEC. 5. Mandate of the Department. -The Department shall be the primary agency
responsible for the comprehensive and integrated identification and mapping of all water
resources,     planning,   policy,   formulation,    and      management   of   the   ownership,

formulation towards the attainment of universal access to safe, adequate, affordable, and

SEC. 6. Guiding Principles -In the pursuit of its mandate, tie Department shall be guided
by the following key principles:


       (a) The concept of good water governance of water security and sufficiency shall
           be adopted across all levels of governance by stakeholders such as the national
           government,    local    government,   non-government      organizations,   private
           organization, individuals, even global societal actors, and in various sectors
           such as water supply, irrigation, fisheries, hydropower, navigation, ecosystem
           services, flood management, climate change, and recreation. It shall entail
           publicly transparent, socially accountable, and multi-stakeholder participatory
           water policy planning, management, and decision-making processes.
       (b) Access to safe water supply and improved sanitation is a human right that
           needs to be fulfilled and protected.
       (c) Integrated Water Resource Management (lwRM) shall be adopted as the basic
           framework which is the coordinated development and management of water,
           land, and related resources within hydrological boundaries to optimize
           economic and social welfare without compromising the sustainability of vital
           ecosystems. The lwRM Framework shall include an enabling environment that
           utilizes proper policies, legal instruments, and institutional frameworks for
          effective implementation, participatory and collaborative water policy and
          management decisions, and management instruments for efficient use of
           updated and accurate data, science base decision support systems, proper
           information education communication (lEC) programs, assessment and
           allocation tools,   or regulatory functions.     It   shall   observe the     mutual
           accountability mechanism among stakeholders at the national, regional, and
           global levels reinforcing multi-stakeholder decision-making the following a
           framework with specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, and, timely actions.
       (d) Each river basin has its unique characteristics relating to the physical, socio
           cultural, and other aspects, and water management shall be performed at river-
           basin level, involving all the stakeholders at that particular river basin.
       (e) Integrated and accurate data collection and analysis and the use of scientific
           decision support systems shall be undertaken in water resources management
           including flood modelling and warning systems that are essential or an
          effective, efficient, and sustainable water resources management system.
       (fl The development of water resources shall include strategies for the mitigation
           of water-related hazards and climate change adaptation.
       (g) Water resources and services should be provided where they are intended and
           most needed so that water is fairly and sustainably managed.
       (h) The proper operation and maintenance of waterworks systems resulting in the
           uninterrupted and adequate supply and distribution of potable water for
          domestic and other purposes, and the proper operation and maintenance of
          sewage systems which are essential services to public health and safety, are to
          be ensured at all times. The State shall therefore supervise and control the
          establishment, operation, and maintenance of such systems.
