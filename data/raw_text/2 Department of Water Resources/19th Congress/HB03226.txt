                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       While our country has been blessed with ample water sources, and bountiful
rainwater during the wet months, these remain vulnerable to wastage and are not
properly stored to serve us during the dry months. Moreover, it appears that the
country continues to be confronted with multi-faceted issues like the reportedly
dismal performance of water regulatory agencies, poor or no access to clean drinking
water in many parts of our country, rising water rates— all of which are aggravated
by climate change. Hence, it has become imperative that our government be made
ready and able to carry out a timely and effective response at all times.

       Thus, this proposed measure, which is anchored and principally based on the
remarkable output of the Committees on Government Reorganization, Public Works
and Highways, Ways and Means, and Appropriations in the 18th Congress, seeks to
rationalize the country’s water, sewage, and sanitation resource management,
including water rights, through the creation of the Department of Water Resources
and the Water Regulatory Commission. Through the concerted efforts of these critical
agencies, it is envisioned that, among others, clearer and more organized policies for
water supply and sanitation are established and that the country’s water resources are
developed and managed to ensure its optimal use such as in irrigation, power
generation, industrial, agricultural and commercial use.

       At this critical juncture in our country’s history where our people are still wary
of the pandemic and are truly burdened with the global crisis on oil and fuel, we
cannot succumb to a water crisis as well.

       In view of the foregoing, approval of this measure is earnestly requested.

13          SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Water Act”.

15         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. –Access to water is a basic human right. All water
16   belongs to the State which shall govern its development and utilization for the people.
17
18         It is hereby declared the policy of the State to ensure the provision of safe,
19   adequate, affordable and sustainable water supply and improved sanitation services,
20   while maintaining the protection, preservation and revival of the quality of the
21   country’s water resources and ecological balance.
22
23          In pursuit of this policy, this Act shall have the principal objective of ensuring
24   and accelerating universal access to water supply and sanitation services, in a
25   regulatory regime that encourages responsible private sector participation, and shall
26   foster and prioritize the establishment of infrastructure and public works that adopt
27   innovative solutions and international best practices to address the challenges of
28   climate change. Further, all water and water treatment infrastructure projects are
29   hereby declared as projects imbued with national interest.

25          SEC. 5. Mandate of the Department. –The Department shall be the primary
26   agency responsible for the comprehensive and integrated identification and mapping
27   of all water resources, planning, policy formulation, and management of the
28   ownership, appropriation, utilization, exploitation, development, and protection of
29   water resources in the Philippines to ensure the optimal use thereof for domestic and
30   municipal water supply, sanitation, irrigation, hydropower, industry, navigation,
31   flood management, recreation except fisheries or aquaculture.
32

34   and policy formulation towards the attainment of universal access to safe, adequate,
35   affordable, and sustainable water supply, and improved sanitation services for all
36   Filipinos.

40   Republic Act No. 9275, otherwise known as the Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004.

20         SEC. 6. Guiding Principles. –In the pursuit of its mandate, the Department shall
21   be guided by the following key principles:
22
23      (a) The concept of good water governance for water security and sufficiency shall
24          be adopted across all levels of governance by stakeholders such as the national
25          government, local government, non-government organizations, private
26          organizations, individuals, even global societal actors, and in various sectors
27          such as water supply, irrigation, fisheries, hydropower, navigation, ecosystem
28          services, flood management, climate change, and recreation. It shall entail
29          publicly transparent, socially accountable and multi-stakeholder participatory
30          water policy, planning, management and decision-making processes.
31
32      (b) Access to safe water supply and improved sanitation is a human right that
33          needs to be fulfilled and protected.
34
35      (c) IWRM shall be adopted as the basic framework which is the coordinated
36          development and management of water, land and related resources within
37          hydrological boundaries, to optimize economic and social welfare, without
38          compromising the sustainability of vital ecosystems. The IWRM Framework
39          shall include an enabling environment that utilizes proper policies, legal
40          instruments, and institutional frameworks for effective implementation,
41          participatory and collaborative water policy and management decisions, and
42          management instruments for efficient use of updated and accurate data,
43          science-based decision support systems, proper information, education,
44          communication (IEC) programs, assessment and allocations tools, or
45          regulatory functions. It shall observe the mutual accountability mechanism
46          among stakeholder at the national, regional, and global levels reinforcing

 8         diligent, conscientious and workman like manner, in accordance with
 9         applicable laws, rules, and regulations issued by the Commission and the
10         generally accepted standards and practices in the water supply and
11         sanitation industry;
12    iii. Comply with drinking water quality requirements and standards that may
13         be established by the Commission; and
14
15   b) Any person granted a license under this Act shall, to the extent allowed by
16      law and specified in the license, has the right to acquire or lease land, lay or
17      repair water or sanitation main lines and other relevant facilities in public
18      ways to fulfill the terms and conditions under the license.
19
20   c) Subject to any condition or limitation laid down in the license, a licensee may
21      discontinue water supply and sanitation services to a customer if such
22      customer defaults in the payment of fees due to the licensee for the water
23      supplied or sanitation services provided, or for acts of pilferage pursuant to
24      Sections 8, 9, 10, and 11 of Republic Act No. 8041, otherwise known as the
25      Water Crisis Act of 1995.

27   d) Any license issued under this Act shall contain provisions designed to ensure
28      that licensees:
29       i. Publish the Tariff and other charges approved by and the terms and
30          conditions imposed by the Commission for the provision of water supply
31          and sanitation services;
32     ii. Prepare, within three (3) months from the issuance of a license, in
33          consultation with its customers, a Customer Service Code specifying the
34          manner and procedure for: (a) metering, billing, and collection of the
35          licensee’s approved tariff and other charges, (b) disconnection or
36          suspension of service in case of non-payment of tariffs and/or other
37          charges, or acts of pilferage, and (c) recommendation and recovery of
38          arrears in tariffs and other charges;
39    iii. Maintain financial accounts in accordance with the manner and procedure
40          specified in the License and as may be required by the Commission; and
41    iv. Maintain and upon request by anyone during regular office hours,
42          promptly make available for scrutiny and inspection such data, statistics
43          and other information, as may be required by the Commission.

 1          SEC. 30. Setting Tariffs, Rates and Other Charges. – Regulatory units shall
 2   establish tariffs, rates and other charges which are fair and reasonable, and ensure
 3   economic viability and a fair return on investments.
 4
 5          Tariffs, rates and charges shall be based on and consistent with a rate-setting
 6   methodology that the Commission shall, after due consultation, define and publish,
 7   taking into account the following, among others:
 8
 9       a) Reasonable and prudent capital and recurrent costs of providing the service
10          including a reasonable rate of return on capital;
11       b) Efficiency of the service;
12       c) Incentives for enhancement of efficiency;
13       d) Willingness to pay of the customers/consumers;
14       e) Equity considerations; and
15       f) Administrative simplicity.
16
17         Tariffs, rates and charges set by the regulatory units shall be presumed valid
18   and reasonable unless a protest or contest is filed with the Commission.

20           SEC. 31. Direct Access. - Fifty-one percent (51%) of the registered water
21   consumers in a municipality, city or province may petition for direct access to water
22   from any water service provider subject to concurrence by the service provider and
23   approval by the Commission. The Commission shall promulgate rules and procedures
24   for this mode of service.
25
26          SEC. 32. Innovative Schemes to Improve Efficiency and Management of Systems. -
27   The Commission shall promote innovative schemes such as the consolidation or
28   integration of water supply and sanitation services, or providers in the same service
29   area, where it shall result in improved efficiency, service expansion and lower costs.
30
31          To this end, the Commission shall establish and issue such rules and
32   guidelines as may be necessary to (a) create incentives to encourage efficiency and
33   service expansion; (b) establish the standards and targets that service providers are
34   required to meet; and (c) define the fines and penalties that shall be imposed for
35   failure to meet such standards and targets.
