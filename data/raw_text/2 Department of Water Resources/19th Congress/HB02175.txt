                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

    Water is a basic human need and access to safe water is a universal
human right as declared by the United Nations.

        With global warming, water security is a growing problem worldwide, and
it calls for long term planning and responses. In the case of the Philippines,
although an archipelagic country blessed with an abundant source of water,
severe water shortage is being experienced due to the impact of rapid
population growth, urbanization, pollution, excessive and inefficient use of
water, and climate change. This is further aggravated by the lack of a holistic
program for water resources management.

      One of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) adopted by all United
Nations Member States in 2015 is to ensure availability and sustainable
management of water and sanitation for all by 2030.1 Despite progress, billions
of people still lack safe water, sanitation and handwashing facilities. Data
suggests that achieving universal access to even basic sanitation service by
2030 would require doubling the current annual rate of progress.

       At present, management of water resources in the country is lodged with
different units or agencies of the government such as National Water Resources
Board (NWRB), Local Water Utilities Administration (LWUA), Metropolitan
Waterworks and Sewerage System (MWSS), and the National Irrigation
Administration (NIA) thereby resulting to duplication of functions, lack of
coordination or even conflicts in the implementation of the national policies and
plans on water, sewage, sanitation, and irrigation thereby resulting to backlog in
the provision of water supply and sanitation services to the public.

       This bill seeks to ensure and accelerate universal access to water supply
and sanitation services and to encourage responsible private sector
participation, fostering and prioritizing infrastructure and public works that adopt
innovative solutions and international best practices to address the challenges
of climate change.

      The key provisions include the following:

  ● Creates, establishes, and mandates the Department of Water Resources
     to be the primary agency responsible for the comprehensive and integrated
     identification and mapping of all water resources, planning, policy
     formulation, and management of the ownership, appropriation, utilization,
     exploitation, development, sustainability and protection of water resources
     in the Philippines except fisheries or aquaculture;

  ● Declares all water and water treatment infrastructure projects as projects
     imbued with national interest;

  ● Adopts the Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) as the
     strategic framework for national water management, policymaking and
     planning;

  ● Empowers the Department on policy making, planning, management and
     regulation of water resources; and to develop institutional arrangements
     with public water organizations and ensure coordination with all
     stakeholders for all water resource development, integration and
     management activities, including sanitation, flood control, flood risk
     management and drought risk management;

  ● Empowers the Department to create when necessary, water resource
     subsidiaries, instrumentalities and entities to engage in water transmission,
     water distribution, waste water treatment and management, and sanitation
     in accordance with existing relevant laws;

  ● Allows the Department to enter into contracts, joint venture agreements or
     understanding, public-private partnerships, and memorandum of
     agreements or understanding, either domestic or foreign relating to
     investment and financing water-related projects, under such terms and
     conditions as the Department may deem proper and reasonable subject to
     existing laws;

  ● Authorizes the Secretary of the Department to act as the Chairperson of
     the governing board of the agencies that are attached to the Department;

  ● Attaches agencies to the Department as follows: 1. The Local Water
     Utilities Administration (LWUA); 2. The Metropolitan Waterworks and
     Sewerage System (MWSS); 3. The National Irrigation Administration (NIA);
     4. The Laguna Lake Development Authority (LLDA), whose mandate,
     powers and functions are defined under Republic Act No. 4850;

  ● Subsumes the offices, functions and personnel of the following agencies to
     the Department, namely: 1. The National Water Resources Board
     (NWRB); 2. The River Basin Control Office (RBCO) of the DENR; 3. The

 5         SEC. 4. The Department of Water Resources – There is hereby created
 6   and established the Department of Water Resources, hereinafter referred to as
 7   the Department.

 9           SEC. 5. Mandate of the Department. –The Department shall be the
10   primary agency responsible for the comprehensive and integrated identification
11   and mapping of all water resources, planning, policy formulation, and
12   management of the ownership, appropriation, utilization, exploitation,
13   development, and protection of water resources in the Philippines to ensure the
14   optimal use thereof for domestic and municipal water supply, sanitation,
15   irrigation, hydropower, industry, navigation, flood management, and recreation
16   except fisheries or aquaculture.
17
18         The Department shall also be the primary agency responsible for the
19   planning and policy formulation towards the attainment of universal access to
20   safe, adequate, affordable, and sustainable water supply, and improved
21   sanitation services for all Filipinos.
22
23          The Department shall be the primary national agency to enforce
24   Presidential Decree No. 1067, otherwise known as The Water Code of the
25   Philippines and Republic Act No. 9275, otherwise known as the Philippine Clean
26   Water Act of 2004.
27
28          The Department shall exercise the powers and functions of the defunct
29   Pasig River Rehabilitation Commission created under Executive Order No. 54
30   Series of 1999 and abolished under Executive Order No. 93 Series of 2019,
31   specifically the following roles that were transferred to the Manila Bay Task
32   Force and DENR through the latter EO:
33
34          (a) Update and take the lead in the overall implementation of the Pasig
35              River Rehabilitation Master Plan, which shall harness the Pasig
36              River’s potential for transportation, recreation and tourism purposes
37              while undertaking the rehabilitation and restoration of its maritime life;
38              and
39          (b) Ensure that the easements provided for in the Civil Code and other
40              relevant laws are enforced, especially in all the esteros and
41              waterways that drain into the Pasig River as well as abate the
42              dumping of untreated industrial wastewater and sewerage into the
43              river, including all acts and omissions in violation of Presidential
44              Decree (PD) No. 984, s. 1976, as amended entitled, Providing For
45              The Revision Of Republic Act No. 3931, Commonly Known As The
46              Pollution Control Law, And For Other Purposes, PD No. 274, s. 1973
47              entitled, Pertaining To The Preservation, Beautification, Improvement
48              And Gainful Utilization Of The Pasig River, Providing For The
49              Regulation And Control Of Pollution Of The River And Its Banks In
50              Order To Enhance Its Development, Thereby Maximizing Its
51              Utilization For Socio-Economic Purposes, and other related laws.

 1     SEC. 6. Guiding Principles. –In the pursuit of its mandate, the
 2   Department shall be guided by the following key principles:
 3
 4     (a) The concept of good water governance for water security and
 5        sufficiency shall be adopted across all levels of governance by
 6        stakeholders such as the national government, local government, non-
 7        government organizations, private organizations, individuals, even
 8        global societal actors, and in various sectors such as water supply,
 9        irrigation, fisheries, hydropower, navigation, ecosystem services, flood
10        management, climate change, and recreation. It shall entail publicly
11        transparent, socially accountable, and multi-stakeholder participatory
12        water policy planning, management, and decision-making processes.
13
14     (b) Access to safe water supply and improved sanitation is a human right
15        that needs to be fulfilled and protected.
16
17     (c) Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) shall be adopted as
18        the basic framework which is the coordinated development and
19        management of water, land, and related resources within hydrological
20        boundaries to optimize economic and social welfare without
21        compromising the sustainability of vital ecosystems. The IWRM
22        Framework shall include an enabling environment that utilizes proper
23        policies, legal instruments, and institutional frameworks for effective
24        implementation, participatory and collaborative water policy and
25        management decisions, and management instruments for efficient use
26        of updated and accurate data, science-based decision support
27        systems, proper information, education, communication (IEC)
28        programs, assessment and allocation tools, or regulatory functions. It
29        shall observe the mutual accountability mechanism among
30        stakeholders at the national, regional, and global levels reinforcing
31        multi-stakeholder decision-making following a framework with specific,
32        measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely actions.
33
34     (d) Each river basin has its unique characteristics relating to the physical,
35        socio cultural, and other aspects, and water management shall be
36        performed at river-basin level, involving all the stakeholders at that
37        particular river basin.
38
39     (e) Integrated and accurate data collection and analysis and the use of
40        scientific decision support systems shall be undertaken in water
41        resources management including flood modeling and warning systems
42        that are essential for an effective, efficient, and sustainable water
43        resources management system.
44
45     (f) The development of water resources shall include strategies for the
46        mitigation of water-related hazards and climate change adaptation.
47
48     (g) Water resources and services should be provided where they are
49        intended and most needed so that water is fairly and sustainably
50        managed.
51
52     (h) The proper operation and maintenance of waterworks systems resulting
53        in the uninterrupted and adequate supply and distribution of potable
                                           11

 7   SEC 7. Objectives of the Department. – The Department shall:
 8
 9   (a) Promote and adopt water demand management as a national policy to
10      ensure that water is optimized, that water efficiency and conservation
11      become a way of life, that the recycling and reuse of water and treated
12      wastewater are widely practiced, that water is properly priced to
13      encourage efficient use and conservation and that rainwater, flood waters,
14      and run-offs are captured or harvested, stored and treated for future use;
15
16   (b) Prioritize and seek the immediate attainment of universal access to safe,
17      adequate, affordable and sustainable water supply and improved
18      sanitation services for all Filipinos in a manner consistent with the
19      protection, preservation, and revival of the quality of the country’s water
20      resources, and to adopt all needed measures to advance the right of the
21      people to a balanced and healthful ecology in accord with the rhythm and
22      harmony of nature.
23
24   (c) Strengthen and coordinate integrated water resources management
25      planning and policy making to ensure sustainable and fair allocation and
26      efficient use of water resources among competing uses and users;
27
28   (d) Ensure that integrated water resources management is strongly
29      coordinated with land use and coastal and multi-hazard planning and
30      management;
31
32   (e) Develop sustainably, and manage water sources and water resources
33      toward water security, taking into consideration water, land, and related
34      resources aimed at increasing water dependability for different uses,
35      optimizing economic benefits and social welfare without compromising
36      the sustainability of vital environmental systems;
37
38   (f) Strengthen and coordinate policy making and planning towards ensuring
39      the availability of water for food security and energy security, as well as
40      towards the protection of communities and environments against flood
41      and other water induced hazards including sediment hazards;
42
43   (g) Ensure that the strategy for water resources development and
44      conservation is participatory, and that planners, policymakers and users
45      including community members are involved at all levels;
46
47   (h) Develop a standardized pricing framework for water use that accounts for
48      environmental externalities; and
49
50   (i) Ensure that all sources of water, including surface water, groundwater,
51      rainwater and floodwater are identified, mapped, monitored, conserved
52      and managed for productive use and the resulting wastewater is managed

11          SEC. 27. Registration and Licensing of All Water Supply and
12   Sanitation Services. – All water supply and sanitation service providers,
13   including bulk water suppliers and those providing services to subdivisions and
14   other service providers, shall register with the Commission and, subject to such
15   rules, guidelines, procedures and other issuances of the Commission, obtain a
16   license to operate from the appropriate regulatory units.
17
18         The Commission shall ensure that the grant and revocation of licenses
19   are carried out fairly, transparently, and without discrimination. Issuances of the
20   Commission shall specify, among others, the qualifications, requirements, and
21   procedure for the grant and revocation of licenses including the standards and
22   performance targets that shall continuously be complied with to keep the
23   licenses valid.
24
25          Unless otherwise provided in this Act or by the Commission, no public
26   water supply and sanitation service provider shall commence or conduct the
27   business of providing water supply and sanitation services without first obtaining
28   a license.
29
30          Concession agreements and other contracts for water supply provision
31   and water sanitation and treatment services currently existing shall remain valid
32   and enforceable unless otherwise terminated, after due notice and hearing for
33   reasons provided in the existing terms and conditions under the concession or
34   service agreement or contract, or when invalidated by the Commission, after
35   due notice and hearing, where national security, national emergency or public
36   interest so dictates.

45         (c) Grounds for modification, suspension or cancellation of the License;
46         (d) Minimum technical performance and service level standards;
47         (e) Expansion targets and service level improvements over time;
48         (f) Restrictions or conditions for transferability of the business or
49             controlling interest in the business;
50         (g) Reportorial requirements and obligations of the grantee;
51          (h)Submission to annual performance audit by the Commission or its duly
52             authorized representative(s).

13         SEC. 29. Rights and Duties of Licensees. –
14
15      (a) Any person granted a license under this Act shall have the obligation to
16         ensure that licensed activities are conducted to further public interest and,
17         in particular:
18
19         (1) Foster the maintenance and development of efficient, coordinated, and
20             viable operation of their licensed activities;
21         (2) Ensure that their water supply and sanitation services are provided in
22             a diligent, conscientious and workman like manner, in accordance with
23             applicable laws rules and regulations issued by the Commission and
24             the generally accepted standards and practices of the water supply
25             and sanitation industry; and
26         (3) Comply with drinking water quality requirements and standards that
27             may be established by the Commission.
28
29      (b) Any person granted a license under this Act shall, to the extent allowed
30         by law and specified in the license, has the right to acquire or lease land,
31         lay or repair water or sanitation main lines and other relevant facilities in
32         public ways to fulfill the terms and conditions under the license.
33
34      (c) Subject to any condition or limitation laid down in the license, a licensee
35         may discontinue water supply and sanitation services to a customer if
36         such customer defaults in the payment of fees due to the licensee for the
37         water supplied or sanitation services provided, or for acts of pilferage
38         pursuant to Sections 8, 9, 10, and 11 of Republic Act No. 8041, otherwise
39         known as the Water Crisis Act of 1995.
40
41      (d) Any license issued under this Act shall contain provisions designed to
42         ensure that licensees:

44             conditions imposed by the Commission for the provision of water
45             supply and sanitation services;
46         (2) Prepare, within three (3) months from the issuance of a license, in
47             consultation with its customers, a Customer Service Code specifying
48             the manner and procedure for: (a) metering, billing, and collection of
49             the licensee’s approved tariff and other charges; (b) disconnection or
50             suspension of service in case of non-payment of tariffs and/or other
51             charges, or acts of pilferage; and (c) recommendation and recovery of
52             arrears in tariffs and other charges;

 2             procedure specified in the license and as may be required by the
 3             Commission; and

 6             statistics and other information, as may be required by the

 9          SEC. 30. Setting Tariffs, Rates, and Other Charges. – Regulatory units
10   shall establish tariffs, rates and other charges which are fair and reasonable,
11   and ensure economic viability and a fair return on investments.
12
13          Tariffs, rates and charges shall be based on and consistent with a rate-
14   setting methodology that the Commission shall, after due consultation, define
15   and publish, taking into account the following, among others:
16          (a) Reasonable and prudent capital and recurrent costs of providing the
17              service including a reasonable rate of return on capital;
18          (b) Efficiency of the service;
19          (c) Incentives for enhancement of efficiency;
20          (d) Willingness to pay of the customers/consumers;
21          (e) Equity considerations; and
22          (f) Administrative simplicity.
23
24          Tariffs, rates, and charges set by the regulatory units shall be presumed
25   valid and reasonable unless a protest or contest is filed with the Commission.
26
27          SEC.31. Direct Access. - Fifty-one (51) percent of the registered water
28   consumers in a municipality, city or province may petition for direct access to
29   water from any water service provider subject to concurrence by the service
30   provider and approval by the Commission. The Commission shall promulgate
31   rules and procedures for this mode of service.
32
33          SEC. 32. Innovative Schemes to Improve Efficiency and
34   Management of Systems. – The Commission shall promote innovative
35   schemes such as the consolidation or integration of water supply and sanitation
36   services, or providers in the same service area, where it shall result in improved
37   efficiency, service expansion and lower costs.
38
39         To this end, the Commission shall establish and issue such rules and
40   guidelines as may be necessary to (a) create incentives to encourage efficiency
41   and service expansion; (b) establish the standards and targets that service
42   providers are required to meet; and (c) define the fines and penalties that shall
43   be imposed for failure to meet such standards and targets.
