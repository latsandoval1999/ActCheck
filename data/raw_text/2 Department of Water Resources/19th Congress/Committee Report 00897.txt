                                     AN ACT
     ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL FRAMEWORK FOR WATER RESOURCE MANAGEMENT
     AND CREATING THE DEPARTMENT OF WATER RESOURCES AND THE WATER
     REGULATORY COMMISSION, DEFINING THEIR MANDATES, POWERS AND FUNCTIONS,
     AND APPROPRIATINGS FUNDS THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:
 1                                                  CHAPTER I
 2                                           GENERAL PROVISIONS
 3           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Water Resources Act”.
 4           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Access to water is a basic human right. All water belongs to
 5   the State which shall govern its development and utilization for the people.
 6           It is hereby declared the policy of the State to ensure the provision of safe, adequate, affordable,
 7   and sustainable water supply and improved sanitation services, while maintaining the protection,
 8   preservation, and revival of the quality of the country’s water resources and ecological balance.
 9           In pursuit of this policy, this Act shall have the principal objective of ensuring and accelerating
10   universal access to water supply and sanitation services, in a regulatory regime that encourages
11   responsible private sector participation, and shall foster and prioritize the establishment of
12   infrastructure and public works that adopt innovative solutions and international best practices to
13   address the challenges of climate change. Further, all water and water treatment infrastructure projects
14   are hereby declared as projects imbued with national interest.
15            SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
16       (a) Bulk water supplier refers to any entity, natural or juridical, whether public or private,
17           supplying or intending to supply large quantities of raw or treated water to buyers for their own
18           consumption, such as industrial companies, or for retail distribution to consumers such as water
19           service providers or real estate developers managing their own distribution network.
20       (b) Environmental services refer to qualitative functions of natural non-produced assets of land,
21           water, and air, including related ecosystems, and their biota, and which may be categorized
22           into three basic types: (a) disposal services which reflect the functions of the natural
23           environment as an absorptive sink for residuals; (b) productive services which reflect the
24           economic functions of providing natural resource inputs and space for production and
25           consumption, and (c) consumer or consumption services which provide for physiological as
26           well as recreational and related needs of human beings.
27       (c) Flood Control refers to methods, acts, and protocols to be observed in order to prevent and
28           reduce the detrimental and catastrophic effects of flood waters which include sediment-laden
29           or turbid flows, and hyper-concentrated flows or debris flows.
30       (d) Flood Risk Management (FRM) refers to acts of defining and determining the appropriate
31           methods, acts, and protocols aimed at preventing and reducing the risk of loss of both life and
32           property due to flood waters. Flood risk management consists of a cycle of prevention,
33           mitigation, adaptation, preparedness and early warning, and response and recovery. The
34           elements of FRM include: integrating land-use planning and coastal zone management into
35           water management; adopting a holistic approach so that FRM is part of wider risk or multi-
36           hazard management of earthquakes, landslides, droughts, and storm surges; managing risk and
37           uncertainty as a whole so it not only mitigates hydrological uncertainties but also social,
38           economic and political uncertainties on account of human behavior and the cultural dimensions
39           of FRM.

17            SEC. 4. The Department of Water Resources – There is hereby created and established the
18   Department of Water Resources, hereinafter referred to as the Department.
19            SEC. 5. Mandate of the Department. –The Department shall be the primary agency responsible
20   for the comprehensive and integrated identification and mapping of all water resources, planning,
21   policy formulation, and management of the ownership, appropriation, utilization, exploitation,
22   development, and protection of water resources in the Philippines to ensure the optimal use thereof for
23   domestic and municipal water supply, sanitation, irrigation, hydropower, industry, navigation, flood
24   management, and recreation, and water utilization aspects of fisheries or aquaculture.
25            The Department shall also be the primary agency responsible for the planning and policy
26   formulation towards the attainment of universal access to safe, adequate, affordable, and sustainable
27   water supply, and improved sanitation services for all Filipinos.
28            The Department shall be the primary national agency to enforce PD 1067, otherwise known as
29   The Water Code of the Philippines.
30            In the implementation of Republic Act (RA) No. 9275, otherwise known as the Philippine
31   Clean Water Act of 2004, the Department shall be responsible for preparing, formulating and
32   implementing a five-year plan of action for a national and a regional septage management plan, which
33   shall contain measures for the construction of wastewater treatment facilities and provision,
34   installation, operation and maintenance and, where practicable, the integration of sewerage, septage
35   management, storm water management and other sanitation facilities. Compliance with the mandate
36   to implement septage management services and systems by any WSP pursuant to the Philippine Clean
37   Water Act of 2004 shall be reckoned within five (5) years from the date of promulgation or publication
38   of the septage management plan formulated by the Department.
39             SEC. 6. Guiding Principles. –In the pursuit of its mandate, the Department shall be guided by
40   the following key principles:
41      (a) The concept of good water governance for water security and sufficiency shall be adopted
42            across all levels of governance by stakeholders such as the national government, local
43            government units (LGUs), non-government organizations, private organizations, individuals,
44            global societal actors, and in various sectors such as water supply, irrigation, fisheries,
45            hydropower, navigation, ecosystem services, climate change, and recreation. It shall entail



                                                                                                             5
 1       publicly transparent, socially accountable, and multi-stakeholder participatory water policy
 2       planning, management, and decision-making processes;
 3   (b) Access to safe water supply and improved sanitation is a human right that needs to be fulfilled
 4       and protected;
 5   (c) Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) shall be adopted as the basic framework
 6       which is the coordinated development and management of water, land, and related resources
 7       within hydrological boundaries to optimize economic and social welfare without
 8       compromising the sustainability of vital ecosystems. The IWRM Framework shall include an
 9       enabling environment that utilizes proper policies, legal instruments, and            institutional
10       frameworks for effective implementation, participatory and collaborative water policy and
11       management decisions, and management instruments for efficient use of updated and accurate
12       data, science-based decision support systems, proper information, education, communication
13       programs, assessment and allocation tools, or regulatory functions. It shall observe the mutual
14       accountability mechanism among stakeholders at the national, regional, and global levels
15       reinforcing multi-stakeholder decision-making following a framework with specific,
16       measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely actions;
17   (d) Each river basin has its unique characteristics relating to the physical, socio cultural, and other
18       aspects, and water management shall be performed at river-basin level, involving all the
19       stakeholders at that particular river basin.
20   (e) Integrated and accurate data collection and analysis and the use of scientific decision support
21       systems shall be undertaken in water resources management including flood modeling and
22       warning systems that are essential for an effective, efficient, and sustainable water resources

25       supply of water: Provided, That the development of water resources shall include strategies for
26       the mitigation of water-related hazards and climate change adaptation.
27   (g) Water resources shall be fairly and sustainably managed, and water services shall be provided
28       where they are intended and most needed.
29   (h) The proper operation and maintenance of waterworks systems resulting in the uninterrupted
30       and adequate supply and distribution of potable water for domestic and other purposes, and the

44   raw water was sourced: Provided further, That the Department shall prescribe standards for programs

OBJECTIVES:

      To ensure and accelerate universal access to water supply and sanitation
       services
      To encourage responsible private sector participation, fostering and prioritizing
       infrastructure and public works that adopt innovative solutions and international
       best practices to address the challenges of climate change
      To declare all water and water treatment infrastructure projects as projects
       imbued with national interest.

KEY PROVISIONS:

      Creates and establishes the Department of Water Resources;
      Mandates the Department to be the primary agency responsible for the
       comprehensive and integrated identification and mapping of all water
       resources, planning, policy formulation, and management of the ownership,
       appropriation, utilization, exploitation, development, sustainability and
       protection of water resources in the Philippines except fisheries or aquaculture;
      Adopts the Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) as the basic
       framework;
      Empowers the Department on policy making, planning, management and
       regulation of water resources; develop institutional arrangements with public
       water organizations and ensure coordination with all stakeholders for all water
       resource development, integration and management activities, including
       sanitation, flood risk management and drought risk management;
      Allows the Department to create when necessary, water resource subsidiaries,
       instrumentalities and entities to engage in water transmission, water
       distribution, waste water treatment and management, and sanitation in
       accordance with existing relevant laws.
      Allows the Department to enter into contracts, joint venture agreements or
       understanding, public-private partnerships, and memorandum of agreements
       or understanding, either domestic or foreign relating to investment and
       financing water-related projects, under such terms and conditions as the
       Department may deem proper and reasonable subject to existing laws.
      Authorizes the Secretary of the Department to act as the Co-Chairperson of the
       governing board of the agencies that are attached to the Department
