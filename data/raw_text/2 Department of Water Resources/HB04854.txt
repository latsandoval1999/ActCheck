                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Water is fundamental to public health, to any industry, and our ecosystems. The
Philippines, being an archipelagic nation, surrounded by water, yet faces persistent and systemic
problems in water supply, quality, resource management and climate resilience. These issues
undermine development, increase costs, and expose communities to health and economic risks.
The 2020 State of the Philippine Water Report by the Philippine Institute for Development
Studies (PIDS) highlights that 40% of water supply systems in the country are either
underdeveloped or inefficient. These inefficiencies lead to significant water losses, with some
estimates suggesting that as much as 30% of water supplied in major urban areas is lost due to
leaks, theft, or poor infrastructure. According to the 2024 Annual Poverty Indicator Survey
(APIS) preliminary results, more than half of families (53.1%) had access to a safely managed
drinking water service from an improved source. These data leave us of the fact that a significant
number of Filipino families are still lacking basic access to a proper water supply.

       With the establishment of the Department of Water Resources, it shall serve as the
primary agency responsible for the comprehensive identification, mapping, policy formulation,
and management of all water resources in the Philippines. It shall oversee the ownership,
appropriation, utilization, development, and protection of these resources to ensure their optimal
use, sanitation, and management in accordance to every applicable industry such as flood
mitigation, agriculture, irrigation, etc. Likewise, the Department shall lead in planning and policy-
making to achieve universal access to safe, adequate, affordable, and sustainable water supply and
improved sanitation services for all Filipinos.

       Last 19th Congress, the bill was approved on third and final reading at the House of
Representatives, accumulating one hundred twenty seven (127) authors.

       The challenges in our water sector are real — from polluted rivers and shrinking
watersheds to system losses and the growing impact of climate change. By creating a Department
of Water Resources alongside an independent Water Regulatory Commission, we can bring
together the focus, expertise, and strong regulation needed to secure clean and reliable water for
every Filipino, while ensuring fair use for agriculture, industry, and the environment. With these

reforms, we take an important step toward a safer, healthier, and more sustainable future that we
all deserve.

       Let us not lose the pure and life-giving quality that water brings to us all. Olaf, a famous
Disney character once said, “Water has memories.” And just like every memory, we shall keep our
waters like a part of our treasury.

       In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is highly sought.

 6          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Access to water is a basic human right. All water
 7   belongs to the State which shall govern its development and utilization for the people. It
 8   is hereby declared the policy of the State to ensure the provision of safe, adequate,
 9   affordable, and sustainable water supply and improved sanitation services, while
10   maintaining the protection, preservation, and revival of the quality of the country’s water
11   resources and ecological balance.
12          In pursuit of this policy, this Act shall have the principal objective of ensuring and
13   accelerating universal access to water supply and sanitation services, in a regulatory
14   regime that encourages responsible private sector participation, and shall foster and
15   prioritize the establishment of infrastructure and public works that adopt innovative
16   solutions and international best practices to address the challenges of climate change.
17   Further, all water and water treatment infrastructure projects are hereby declared as
18   projects imbued with national interest.

29           SEC. 5. Mandate of the Department. – The Department shall be the primary
30   agency responsible for the comprehensive and integrated identification and mapping of
31   all water resources, planning, policy formulation, and management of the ownership,
32   appropriation, utilization, exploitation, development, and protection of water resources
33   in the Philippines to ensure the optimal use thereof for domestic and municipal water
34   supply, sanitation, irrigation, hydropower, industry, navigation, flood management, and
35   recreation, and water utilization aspects of fisheries or aquaculture.
36
37           The Department shall also be the primary agency responsible for the planning and
38   policy formulation towards the attainment of universal access to safe, adequate,


                                                   7
 1   affordable, and sustainable water supply, and improved sanitation services for all
 2   Filipinos.
 3          The Department shall be the primary national agency to enforce PD 1067,
 4   otherwise known as The Water Code of the Philippines.
 5          In the implementation of Republic Act (RA) No. 9275, otherwise known as the
 6   Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004, the Department shall be responsible for preparing,
 7   formulating and implementing a five-year plan of action for a national and a regional
 8   septage management plan, which shall contain measures for the construction of
 9   wastewater treatment facilities and provision, installation, operation and maintenance
10   and, where practicable, the integration of sewerage, septage management, storm water
11   management and other sanitation facilities. Compliance with the mandate to implement
12   septage management services and systems by any WSP pursuant to the Philippine Clean
13   Water Act of 2004 shall be reckoned within five (5) years from the date of promulgation
14   or publication of the septage management plan formulated by the Department.

16          SEC. 6. Guiding Principles. – In the pursuit of its mandate, the Department shall
17   be guided by the following key principles:
18          (a) The concept of good water governance for water security and sufficiency shall
19   be adopted across all levels of governance by stakeholders such as the national
20   government, local government units (LGUs), non-government organizations, private
21   organizations, individuals, global societal actors, and in various sectors such as water
22   supply, irrigation, fisheries, hydropower, navigation, ecosystem services, climate change,
23   and recreation. It shall entail publicly transparent, socially accountable, and multi-
24   stakeholder participatory water policy planning, management, and decision-making
25   processes;
26          (b) Access to safe water supply and improved sanitation is a human right that
27   needs to be fulfilled and protected;
28          (c) Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) shall be adopted as the basic
29   framework which is the coordinated development and management of water, land, and
30   related resources within hydrological boundaries to optimize economic and social welfare
31   without compromising the sustainability of vital ecosystems. The IWRM Framework shall
32   include an enabling environment that utilizes proper policies, legal instruments, and
33   institutional frameworks for effective implementation, participatory and collaborative
34   water policy and management decisions, and management instruments for efficient use
35   of updated and accurate data, science-based decision support systems, proper
36   information, education, communication programs, assessment and allocation tools, or
37   regulatory functions. It shall observe the mutual accountability mechanism among
38   stakeholders at the national, regional, and global levels reinforcing multi-stakeholder


                                                  8
 1   decision-making following a framework with specific, measurable, attainable, relevant,
 2   and timely actions;
 3          (d) Each river basin has its unique characteristics relating to the physical,
 4   sociocultural, and other aspects, and water management shall be performed at river-basin
 5   level, involving all stakeholders at that particular river basin;
 6          (e) Integrated and accurate data collection and analysis and the use of scientific
 7   decision support systems shall be undertaken in water resources management including
 8   flood modeling and warning systems that are essential for an effective, efficient, and
 9   sustainable water resources management system;
10          (f) The State shall prioritize the development of surface water to ensure adequate
11   and reliable supply of water: Provided, That the development of water resources shall
12   include strategies for the mitigation of water-related hazards and climate change
13   adaptation;
14          (g) Water resources shall be fairly and sustainably managed, and water services
15   shall be provided where they are intended and most needed;
16          (h) The proper operation and maintenance of waterworks systems resulting in the

18   other purposes, and the proper operation and maintenance of sewerage systems which
19   are essential services to public health and safety, are to be ensured at all times; and
20          (i) The State recognizes the role of the private sector and mobilization of private
21   resources in the development of waterworks systems through concession agreements,
22   joint venture agreements, bulk water supply agreements, management and service
23   agreements and other contracts for water supply provision, water sanitation and
24   treatment services, and the development, operation and maintenance of water supply and
25   septage management systems. The State shall encourage the private sector to provide the
26   needed investments for programs and projects to ensure adequate water supply and
27   promote water security.

36   adequate, affordable and sustainable water supply and improved sanitation services for
37   all Filipinos, either through public programs and projects or public-private partnership
38   or other mechanisms, in a manner consistent with the protection, preservation, and
39   revival of the quality of the country’s water resources, and to adopt all needed measures

15          SEC. 18. Reconstitution of the National Water Resources Board (NWRB) as the

18   called the “Board”. The water resources allocation and regulation functions of the NWRB
19   are hereby transferred to the Board, which shall supervise the effective appropriation and
20   regulation of the water resources in the country in accordance with PD 1067.
21          The Board shall be composed of the Secretary of Water Resources as the Chair,
22   with the Secretary of Environment and Natural Resources as the Vice-Chairperson, and
23   the heads of agencies of the NEDA, Department of Justice, Department of Science and
24   Technology, and the University of the Philippines – National Hydraulic Research Center
25   as members.

28   functions:
29          (a) Promulgate, among others, rules and regulations for the exploitation and
30   optimum utilization of water resources, in accordance with PD 1067 and other existing
31   laws, including the imposition on water appropriators of such fees or charges by the
32   Department of Water Resources: Provided, That the imposition and collection of such
33   fees and charges shall be implemented by the Department of Water Resources;
34          (b) Approve the guidelines and processes pertaining to water regulation, as
35   recommended by the Secretariat;
36          (c) Review and decide on requests for permits to extract or utilize the country’s
37   water resources and grant the corresponding water rights, upon recommendation of the
38   Resource Allocation Office: Provided, That the water rights are exercised within five (5)
39   years of granting of such rights: Provided, further, That applicants for water permits shall
                                                 24
 1   submit proof of land ownership of, or right to use the property where the water source is
 2   located: Provided, finally, That, if the applicant is currently using the water source being
 3   applied for domestic or municipal purposes upon effectivity of this Act, such applicant
 4   may submit such proof of prior use of possession of said property in lieu of ownership or
 5   right to use;
 6          (d) Exercise appellate jurisdiction over decisions in cases involving the following:
 7                   (1) Violations      and     disputes    relating         to    the
 8   appropriation, utilization, exploitation,    development,     control,    conservation    and
 9   protection of waters;
10                   (2) Violations and disputes on raw water fees; and
11                   (3) Violations and disputes involving water permits, administrative
12   allocation of water resources and transfer of water rights;
13
14          (f) Issue subpoena duces tecum and subpoena ad testificandum;
15          (g) Cite any person or party in contempt for refusal to appear, testify or comply
16   with the lawful orders of the Board in relation to any matter subject to its investigation;
17          (h) Hear, receive evidence, and decide on cases falling within its jurisdiction;
18          (i) Impose the appropriate sanctions over violations committed by appropriators
19   in accordance with existing laws, rules and regulations as well as future rules and

27          (m) Exercise such other powers as may be necessary to carry out its duties and
28   responsibilities under this law.

30   Board except on the basis of question of law by the Supreme Court on certiorari. Every

32   Systems. – The Commission shall promote innovative schemes such as the consolidation
33   or integration of water supply and sanitation services, or providers in the same service
34   area, where it shall result in improved efficiency, service expansion and lower costs. To

13          SEC. 34. Orders and Decisions of the Commission. – Any order, resolution, or
14   decision of the Commission shall be promulgated promptly, expeditiously, reasonably,
15   and in writing, and shall state clearly and distinctly the facts and law on which it is based.
16   The Commission shall publish and make available for public inspection, all decisions and
17   final orders in the adjudication of contested cases or applications.

19          SEC. 35. Appeals Procedure and Prohibition Against Injunction. –
20          (a) The orders, rulings, and decisions of the Commission are final and executory
21   unless appealed to the Court of Appeals within fifteen (15) days from receipt of notice of
22   such order, ruling or decision: Provided, That orders, rulings, and decisions of the
23   Commission approving tariffs, shall be immediately executory and may be suspended
24   only upon appeal and filing of a bond, in an amount to be fixed by the Commission, to
25   answer for damages occasioned by the suspension or stay of execution of such orders,
26   rulings, and decisions.
27          (b) No injunction may be issued by any court or administrative agency to restrain
28   any proceeding before, or the implementation or execution of any order, ruling, or
29   decision of the Commission, except on the basis of a question of law brought before the
30   Supreme Court on certiorari.
31          (c) Any act or decision of the Commission shall not be invalidated merely because
32   of a defector irregularity in, or in connection with, the appointment or vacancy in the
33   Office of the Chairperson or any other member of the Commission.

39   supervision and regulation enforcement fees, filing fees, testing fees, payments for
                                                  40
 1   ecosystem services, and other service income from the use of water resources. The Trust
 2   Fund shall be administered by the Department. The annual levies, and reasonable fees
 3   and surcharges as may be necessary for achieving the purposes, powers and function of
 4   the Commission under Section 22(f) of this Act shall also be remitted to the Water Trust
 5   Fund. Proceeds from the trust fund shall be utilized for water development, water
 6   sanitation and waste water treatment and management, and water sustainability
 7   programs and projects authorized under this Act: Provided, That a maximum of ten
 8   percent (10%) of the total proceeds generated from raw water extraction and all payments
