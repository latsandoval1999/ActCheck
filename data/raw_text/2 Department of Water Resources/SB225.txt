                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

    The Philippines has abundant water resources, including 421 rivers, and 221
lakes. With an average rainfall of 2400 millimeters, the country receives 146 billion
cubic meters of freshwater each year. Despite this, frequent water shortages persist
due to rainfall variations, geographic disparities, increasing water demand, and
recurring El Nino patterns."1


        Experts predict severe water shortages by 2025 in the Philippines due to the
impact of population growth, urbanization, and industrialization on water quantity

          Agriculture in the Philippines consumes 80% of the total water demand,
primarily for irrigation, despite being prioritized lower than domestic use. The
projected rice demand of 16 million tons by 2025 (a 60% increase from current
supply) cannot be met with existing irrigation conditions, and efforts to expand
irrigated area are hindered by water scarcity.4


          This fundamental problem on water shortage stems from fragmented
governance, inefficient resource management, widespread pollution, the impacts of
climate change, and rapid urbanization. Numerous government agencies have
overlapping mandates over various aspects of water, leading to a lack of coherent
policy,     uncoordinated           efforts,      insufficient        accountability,         inefficiencies,        and
underinvestment in crucial infrastructure.


          Proactive measures and legislative action on the effective and efficient
management of water resources are urgently needed for a reliable and sustained
water supply. This bill proposes establishing a National Water Resource Management
framework and creating a Department of Water Resources (DWR) to consolidate
existing agency functions for a unified approach, and an independent Water
Regulatory Commission (Commission) to regulate water service providers, set tariffs,
and enforce compliance. By centralizing authority, this legislation aims to ensure
universal access to safe and affordable water, promote sustainable resource
utilization, mitigate water-related risks, and secure the nation's water for future
generations.

 5        Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy-
 6        (a) The State recognizes that water is fundamental to individual life, health,
 7            and dignity, and that every person has a right to access safe and clean
 8            water for personal and domestic use;
 9        (b) The State affirms that the right of all Filipinos to safe and clean drinking
10            water and sanitation is essential to the full enjoyment of life and all human
11            rights and hence, the attainment of a nationwide water supply, sanitation,
12            and sewerage services at reasonable rates must be pursued in an orderly,
13            rational, efficient, and dynamic manner;
14        (c) The State emphasizes that water is a public good subject to diverse use in
15            economic, agricultural, and industrial activities, and is thus a scarce
 1              resource which must be carefully regulated to prevent resource depletion
 2              and to ensure its availability for future generations;
 3          (d) The State also maintains that water is indispensable to the existence and
 4              sustainability of natural ecosystems, and as such its use must neither
 5              endanger the balance and health of the environment nor compromise the
 6              rhythm and harmony of nature;
 7         (e) The State further maintains that water resources are interconnected with
 8              climate, soil condition, land use and land cover, forest cover, and other
 9              natural resources. As such, the management of water will require inter-
10             sectoral and inter-agency collaboration as well as harmonization of laws,
11              policies, institutions, and stakeholders; and
12         (f) Finally, while the State maintains that all water resources remain under its
13            full ownership, control, and supervision, it emphasizes that it may enter into
14            arrangements with the private sector, as well as with civil society and
15            community-based organizations, on the basis of beneficial use, to ensure the
16            efficient and affordable delivery of water supply, sanitation, and septage
17            services to end-users.
18          In accordance with these precepts, it is hereby declared the policy of the State
19   to provide for the rational, evidence-based, and integrated management of all water
20   resources In the country to ensure universal access to safe, adequate, and affordable
21   water supply, sanitation, and septage services, through a policy and regulatory regime
22   that encourages innovation and responsible private sector participation, while
23   ensuring ecological preservation and environmental sustainability.
