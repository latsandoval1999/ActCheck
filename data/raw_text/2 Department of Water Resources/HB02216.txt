                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This legislative measure aims to establish a comprehensive national
framework for water resource management. The proposed bill shall reorganize the
sector to ensure that a more uniﬁed, integrated and science-based approach is
practiced in water governance. It will do so by creating two bodies: the Department of
Water Resources (DWR) and the Water Regulatory Commission (WRC).

        According to the World Bank Organization, water scarcity affects more than
40% of the global population and water-related disasters account for 70% of all deaths
related to natural disasters. Estimates also show that given today’s environmental
climate and practices, the world may face a 40% shortfall in forecast demand and
available water supply by 2030. With this context in mind, it becomes even more
imperative for nations across the globe to prepare proactively for these looming water
crises.

       The Philippines frequently grapples with extreme natural disasters, especially
during the typhoon season. It leads to excessive ﬂooding that displaces many
communities and damages infrastructure, which costs billions of pesos annually. This
severely hinders national development and pushes citizens further into poverty. An
uncoordinated system for water management exacerbates these vulnerabilities, thus
making it diﬃcult to implement effective and sustainable ﬂood control measures,
integrate stormwater management and protect communities from water-induced
hazards.

       On the other hand, ineﬃcient water management also severely hinders
agricultural productivity, which is a vital aspect of food security. This particular sector
relies on eﬃcient water allocation and management for irrigation. If there is a
presence of ineﬃcient water usage (outdated irrigation systems, inadequate
infrastructure, etc), it directly translates to wasted resources, reduced crop yields and
ﬁnancial losses for our farmers.
      If our country seeks to achieve universal access to safe and sustainable water
supply and improved sanitation service by 2030, there is an obvious need to create a
dedicated and centralized authority that can effectively manage this valuable
resource.

       Given this, it is therefore high time to create two bodies responsible in the
overall management of water resources in the country. Through the DWR and the WRC,
our country can ﬁnally possess a primary body responsible for ensuring the
sustainable management of water. Establishing the DWR and the WRC is a vital step
towards safeguarding the future of our nation’s precious resources and ensure water
security for all.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 4           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Access to water is a basic human right. All water
 5   belongs to the State, which shall govern its development and utilization for the people.
 6   It is hereby declared the policy of the State to ensure the provision of safe, adequate,
 7   affordable and sustainable water supply and improved sanitation services, while
 8   maintaining the protection, preservation and revival of the quality of the country’s
 9   water resources and ecological balance.

15   innovative solutions and international best practices to address the challenges of
16   climate change. Further, all water and water treatment infrastructure projects are
17   hereby declared as projects imbued with national interest.

27          SEC. 4. The Department of Water Resources. – There is hereby created and
28   established the Department of Water Resources, hereinafter referred to as the
29   Department.

31          SEC. 5. Mandate of the Department. – The Department shall be the primary
32   agency responsible for the comprehensive and integrated identification and mapping
33   of all water resources, planning, policy formulation, and management of the
34   ownership, appropriation, utilization, exploitation, development, and protection of
35   water resources in the Philippines to ensure the optimal use thereof for domestic and
36   municipal water supply, sanitation, irrigation, hydropower, industry, navigation, flood
37   management, and recreation, and water utilization aspects of fisheries or aquaculture.
38
39           The Department shall also be the primary agency responsible for the planning
40   and policy formulation towards the attainment of universal access to safe, adequate,
41   affordable, and sustainable water supply, and improved sanitation services for all
42   Filipinos.

20             Tariffs, rates and charges set shall be presumed valid and reasonable

31         SEC. 31. Direct Access. – Fifty-one percent (51%) of the registered water
32   consumers in a service area, municipality, city or province may petition for direct
33   access to water from any water service provider, subject to concurrence by and
34   reasonable compensation to the service provider and approval by the Commission. If
35   the petition for direct access from any water service provider is approved by the
36   Commission, the new water service provider shall reimburse the existing service
37   provider for, as applicable, either the book value or the market value as determined by
38   an independent appraiser accredited by the Commission, whichever is higher, of the
39   water source, water supply or water distribution or transmission facilities, pipelines
40   and other equipment which shall be transferred to or utilized by the new water service
41   provider. The Commission shall promulgate the implementing rules and procedures



                                                                                            43
 1   for this mode of service, which shall include, among others, financial and technical
 2   capability of the new service provider.

 4          SEC. 32. Innovative Schemes to Improve Efficiency and Management of Systems.
 5   – The Commission shall promote innovative schemes such as the consolidation or
 6   integration of water supply and sanitation services, or providers in the same service
 7   area, where it shall result in improved efficiency, service expansion and lower costs.
 8   To this end, the Commission shall establish and issue such rules and guidelines as
 9   may be necessary to (a) create incentives to encourage efficiency and service
10   expansion; (b) establish the standards and targets that service providers are required
11   to meet; and (c) define the fines and penalties that shall be imposed for failure to meet
12   such standards and targets.

24          SEC. 42. Water Trust Fund. – There is hereby created a Water Trust Fund in the

26   supervision and regulation enforcement fees, filing fees, testing fees, payments for
27   ecosystem services, and other service income from the use of water resources. The
28   Trust Fund shall be administered by the Department. The annual levies, and
29   reasonable fees and surcharges as may be necessary for achieving the purposes,
30   powers and functions of the Commission under Section 22(f) of this Act shall also be
31   remitted to the Water Trust Fund. Proceeds from the trust fund shall be utilized for
32   water development, water sanitation and waste water treatment and management,
33   and water sustainability programs and projects authorized under this Act: Provided,
34   That a maximum of ten percent (10%) of the total proceeds generated from raw water
35   extraction and all payments for ecosystem services shall be given as share of the
36   concerned local government unit or indigenous peoples (IP) community who own the
37   specific areas or land where the raw water was sourced: Provided, further, That the
38   Department shall prescribe standards for programs and projects where proceeds
39   from payments for ecosystem services shall be utilized.
