     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1                                     CHAPTER I
 2                              GENERAL PROVISIONS
 3         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "National
 4   Water Resources Management Act".
 5         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Access to water is a basic human right.
 6   All water belongs to the State which shall govern its development and utilization
 7   for the people.
 8         It is hereby declared the policy of the State to ensure the provision of safe,
 9   adequate, affordable, and sustainable water supply and improved sanitation
10   services, while maintaining the protection, preservation, and revival of the
11   quality of the country's water resources and ecological balance.
12         In pursuit of this policy, this Act shall have the principal objective of
13   ensuring and accelerating universal access to water supply and sanitation
14   services, in a regulatory regime that encourages responsible private sector
15   participation and shall foster and prioritize the establishment of infrastructure
16   and public works that adopt innovative solutions and international best practices
17   to address the challenges of climate change. Further, all water and water
18   treatment infrastructure projects are hereby declared as projects imbued with
19   national interest.
20         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:


                                                                         P a g e 2 | 57
 1         (a)   Bulk water supplier refers to any entity, natural or juridical,
 2   whether public or private, supplying or intending to supply large quantities of
 3   raw or treated water to buyers for their own consumption, such as industrial
 4   companies, or for retail distribution to consumers such as water service providers
 5   or real estate developers managing their own distribution network.
 6         (b)   Flood control refers to methods, acts, and protocols to be observed
 7   in order to prevent and reduce the detrimental and catastrophic effects of flood
 8   waters which include sediment-laden or turbid flows, and hyper-concentrated
 9   flows or debris flows.
10         (c)   Flood Risk Management (FRM) refers to acts of defining and
11   determining the appropriate methods, acts, and protocols aimed at preventing
12   and reducing the risk of loss of both life and property due to flood waters. Flood
13   risk management consists of a cycle of prevention, mitigation, adaptation,
14   preparedness, and early warning, and response and recovery. The elements of
15   FRM include: integrating land-use planning and coastal zone management into
16   water management; adopting a holistic approach so that FRM is part of wider
17   risk or multi-hazard management of earthquakes, landslides, droughts, and
18   storm surges; managing risk and uncertainty as a whole so it not only mitigates
19   hydrological uncertainties but also social, economic and political uncertainties on
20   account of human behavior and the cultural dimensions of FRM.
21         (d)   Gender equality refers to the principle asserting the equality of men
22   and women and their right to enjoy equal conditions realizing their full human-
23   potentials to contribute to and benefit from the results of development, and with
24   the State recognizing that all human beings are free and equal in dignity and
25   rights.
26         (e)   Infrastructure and public works refer only to wholly or primarily
27   water-related projects and does not include projects that do not fall within the
28   purview of the objectives, power, and duties of the Department of Water
29   Resources created under this Act.
30         (f)    Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) refers to a

11         SEC. 4. The Department of Water Resources. – There is hereby created
12   and established the Department of Water Resources, hereinafter referred to as
13   the Department.
14         SEC. 5. Mandate of the Department. – The Department shall be the
15   primary agency responsible for the comprehensive and integrated identification
16   and mapping of all water resources, planning, policy formulation, and
17   management      of   the   ownership,    appropriation,   utilization,     exploitation,
18   development, and protection of water resources in the Philippines to ensure the
19   optimal use thereof for domestic and municipal water supply, sanitation,
20   irrigation, hydropower, industry, navigation, flood management, and recreation,
21   and water utilization aspects of fisheries or aquaculture.
22         The Department shall also be the primary agency responsible for the
23   planning and policy formulation towards the attainment of universal access to
24   safe, adequate, affordable, and sustainable water supply, and improved
25   sanitation services for all Filipinos.
26         The Department shall be the primary national agency to enforce PD 1067,
27   series of 1976, instituting “The Water Code of the Philippines”.
28         In the implementation of Republic Act (RA) No. 9275, or the “Philippine
29   Clean Water Act of 2004”, the Department shall be responsible for preparing,
30   formulating and implementing a five-year plan of action for a national and a
31   regional septage management plan, which shall contain measures for the
32   construction of waste water treatment facilities and provision, installation,

                                                                              P a g e 7 | 57
 1   operation and maintenance and, where practicable, the integration of sewerage,
 2   septage management, storm water management and other sanitation facilities.
 3   Compliance with the mandate to implement septage management services arid
 4   systems by any WSP pursuant to RA 9275 shall be reckoned within five (5) years
 5   from the date of promulgation or publication of the septage management plan
 6   formulated by the Department.
 7         SEC. 6. Guiding Principles. – In the pursuit of its mandate, the
 8   Department shall be guided by the following key principles:
 9         (a)   The concept of good water governance for water security and
10               sufficiency shall be adopted across all levels of governance by
11               stakeholders such as the national government, LGUs, non-
12               government organizations, private organizations, individuals, global
13               societal actors, and in various sectors such as water supply,
14               irrigation, fisheries, hydropower, navigation, ecosystem services,
15               climate change, and recreation. It shall entail publicly transparent,
16               socially accountable, and multi-stakeholder participatory water
17               policy planning, management, and decision-making processes;
18         (b)   Access to safe water supply and improved sanitation is a human
19               right that needs to be fulfilled and protected;
20         (c)   IWRM shall be adopted as the basic framework which is the
21               coordinated development and management of water, land, and
22               related resources within hydrological boundaries to optimize
23               economic    and     social   welfare    without   compromising    the
24               sustainability of vital ecosystems. The IWRM Framework shall
25               include an enabling environment that utilizes proper policies, legal
26               instruments,      and   institutional    frameworks   for    effective
27               implementation, participatory and collaborative water policy and
28               management decisions, and management instruments for efficient
29               use of updated and accurate data, science-based decision support
30               systems, proper information, education, communication programs,
31               assessment and allocation tools, or regulatory functions. It shall
32               observe the mutual accountability mechanism among stakeholders

                                                                       P a g e 8 | 57
 1         at the national, regional, and global levels reinforcing multi-
 2         stakeholder decision-making following a framework with specific,
 3         measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely actions;
 4   (d)   Each river basin has its unique characteristics relating to the
 5         physical, sociocultural, and other aspects, and water management
 6         shall   be   performed     at    river-basin level,   involving   all   the
 7         stakeholders at that particular river basin;
 8   (e)   Integrated and accurate data collection and analysis and the use of
 9         scientific decision support systems shall be undertaken in water
10         resources management including flood modeling and warning
11         systems that are essential for an effective, efficient, and sustainable
12         water resources management system;
13   (f)   The State shall prioritize the development of surface water to
14         ensure adequate and reliable supply of water: Provided, That the
15         development of water resources shall include strategies for the
16         mitigation of water-related hazards and climate change adaptation;
17   (g)   Water resources and services should be provided where they are
18         intended and most needed so that water is fairly and sustainably
19         managed;
20   (h)   The proper operation and maintenance of waterworks systems
21         resulting    in   the    uninterrupted    and   adequate    supply      and

32         maintenance of water supply and septage management systems.

OBJECTIVES:

   •   To ensure and accelerate universal access to water supply and sanitation
       services.

   •   To encourage responsible private sector participation, fostering and
       prioritizing infrastructure and public works that adopt innovative solutions
       and best international practices to address the challenges of climate change.

   •   To declare all water and water treatment infrastructure projects as projects
       imbued with national interest.



KEY PROVISIONS:

   •   Creates, establishes, and mandates the Department of Water Resources
       (the Department) to be the primary agency responsible for the
       comprehensive and integrated identification and mapping of all water
       resources, planning, policy formulation, and management of the ownership,
       appropriation, utilization, exploitation, development, sustainability and
       protection of water resources in the Philippines except for fisheries or
       aquaculture.

   •   Declares all water and water treatment infrastructure projects as projects


                                                                          Page 2 of 6
    imbued with national interest.

•   Adopts the Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) as the
    strategic framework for national water management, policymaking and
    planning.

•   Empowers the Department with policymaking, planning, management and
    regulation of water resources and to develop institutional arrangements
    with public water organizations and ensure coordination with all
    stakeholders for all water resource development, integration and
    management activities, including sanitation, flood control, flood risk
    management and drought risk management.

•   Empowers the Department to create, when necessary, water resource
    subsidiaries, instrumentalities and entities to engage in water
    transmission, water distribution, wastewater treatment and management,
    and sanitation in accordance with existing relevant laws.

•   Allows the Department to enter contract, joint venture agreements or
    understanding, public-private partnerships, and memorandum of
    agreements or understanding, either domestic or foreign relating to
    investment and financing water-related projects, under such terms and
    conditions as the Department may deem proper and reasonable subject to
    existing laws.

•   Authorizes the Secretary of the Department to Act as the Chairperson of
    the governing board of the agencies that are attached to the Department.

•   Attaches the following agencies to the Department:
    1. Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System (MWSS);
    2. National Irrigation Administration (NIA); and
    3. Laguna Lake Development Authority (LLDA).

•   Subsumes the offices, functions and personnel of the following agencies to
    the Department, namely:
    1. National Water Resources Board (NWRB);
    2. Local Water Utilities Administration (LWUA);
    3. River Basin Control Office (RBCO) of the Department of Environment
       and Natural Resources (DENR);
    4. Manila Bay Coordinating Council (MBCO) of the DENR;
    5. Water Supply and Sanitation Unit of the Department of the Interior and
       Local Government (DILG); and
    6. Water Resources Management Division of the Bureau of Soils and Water
       Management Section of the Environment Management Bureau (EMB) of
       the DENR.

•   Transfers to the Department the functions of the following agencies,
    bureaus, and units:

                                                                     Page 3 of 6
    1. The planning, programming, administration, monitoring, and
       management of the National Sewerage and Septage Management
       Program (NSSMP) of the DPWH;
    2. The following functions of the dissolved Pasig River Rehabilitation
       Commission and transferred to the Manila Bay Task Force per
       Executive Order No. 93, series of 2019:
       a. Updating and leading in the overall implementation of the Pasig
          River Rehabilitation Master Plan; and
       b. Ensuring that the easements provided for in the Civil Code and other
          relevant laws are enforced, especially in all the esteros and
          waterways as well as abating the dumping of untreated wastewater
          and sewage into water systems, including all acts and omissions in
          violation of PD 984, as amended, and other related laws.

•   Creates the Water Pollution Adjudication Board to assume the powers and
    function of the National Pollution control Commission with respect to the
    adjudication of water pollution cases.

•   Establishes interface and institutional arrangements with other agencies
    such as the DENR, the Department of Health (DOH), the Department of
    Energy (DOE), and the National Power Commission (NPC). The
    Department shall coordinate with the DA, DPWH, the National Disaster
    Risk Reduction Management Council (NDRRMC), the Climate Change
    Commission (CCC), the Department of Tourism (DOT), Philippine
    Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration, and
    (PAGASA), National Mapping and Resource Information Authority
    (NAMRIA), the Philippine Reclamation Authority (PRA), economic zone
    authorities and other relevant government agencies including local
    government units (LGUs).

•   Creates the National Water Sector Policy Board to approve the National
    Water Resources Management Framework Plan, provide overall policy
    direction and recommend policies on water resources requiring Presidential
    decisions, advise the President on the status of water management and
    supply, recommend to the President the declaration of a state of calamity in
    areas affected by water supply, and submit proposals to result normalcy in
    the affected areas and ensure a multi-stakeholder participation in the
    development , updating, and sharing information system as policy, planning
    and decision-making tools consistent with the IWRM.

•   Subrogates all rights and assumes all obligations of the government
    agencies and units whose powers and functions have been subsumed,
    absorbed, transferred or attached to the Department.

•   Creates the Water Regulatory Commission (WRC) which shall assume the
    economic regulatory units and functions of the MWSS, NWRB, LWUA,
    Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority (SBMA), Philippine Economic Zone
    Authority (PEZA), and Tourism Infrastructure and Enterprise Zone

                                                                       Page 4 of 6
    Authority (TIEZA), specifically, empowering the Regulatory Units of the

    1. Issue licenses authorizing the operation of water supply and/or

    3. Review, determine, fix and approve proposed water and sewerage tariffs,
       rates and charges that licenses may impose their customers/consumers;
    4. Enforce technical, financial and other performance standards set by the

    5. Respond to consumer complaints and investigate motu proprio price

    9. Appoint an interim management committee to ensure continuity of
       service in case a licensee fails to meet the condition of the license.

•   Provides quasi-judicial functions to the WRC for the purpose of any
    investigation, inquiry or proceeding.

•   Requires the registration and licensing of all water supply and/or sanitation
    services

•   Create the Water Trust Fund in the Bureau of Treasury to be utilized for
    water development, water sanitation and wastewater treatment and
    management, and water sustainability programs and projects authorized
    under this Act
