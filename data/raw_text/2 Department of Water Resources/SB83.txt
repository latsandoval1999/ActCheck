                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE


       One of Sustainable Goals of the United Nations is to ensure universal access to
clean water and sanitation. Access to clean water and proper sanitation is the
cornerstone of national development, public health, and environmental sustainability.
According to the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA), 97.6% of
Filipino families use improved drinking water sources and 84% have access to basic
sanitation services. However, despite this progress, our country is still behind other
Southeast Asian countries in terms of access to basic sanitation services. According to
the 2020 Annual Poverty Indicators Survey (APIS), approximately 8.4% of Filipino
households still rely on potentially unsafe sources of water, particularly unprotected
wells, undeveloped springs, rivers, ponds, lakes, rainwater, or water purchased from
tanker trucks and water peddlers. In 2022, APIS also reported that around 16% of
households have limited or no access to sanitation facilities.
      The water sector in the Philippines is highly fragmented, with more than 30
government offices and instrumentalities involved in various aspects such as water
supply, sanitation, irrigation, flood control, and water quality. This has resulted in
overlapping functions, duplication of efforts, and unclear accountability. Furthermore,
many water service providers are unregulated, water losses remain high, and long­
term planning is ineffective due to these institutional and governance gaps.
                                           1
       Moreover, faced with limited funding, local water districts often resort to
partnerships with the private sector. However, it has been reported that some of these
Public-Private Partnerships face issues such as declining service satisfaction, revenue
losses, and concerns over long-term sustainability.
       In order to address the complex challenges faced by the water sector, this bill
proposes the creation of the Department of Water Resources Management, a
centralized agency responsible for the planning and policy formulation towards the
attainment of universal access to safe, adequate, affordable, and sustainable water
supply, and improved sewerage and sanitation services for all Filipinos.
       Alongside this, the bill also establishes the Water Regulatory Commission, an
independent regulatory body tasked with overseeing the performance, compliance,
and service delivery of all water service providers, whether public or private. The
Commission is mandated to ensure that water services are delivered efficiently, safely,
and at fair rates, while safeguarding consumer rights and promoting accountability.
       Towards this end, the purpose of this bill is to improve the quality of life of all
Filipinos. Access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene is the most basic human need,
essential to the health and well-being of every Filipino.
       It is for this purpose that the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

24              Sec. 40. Setting Tariffs, Rates and Other Charges. - The Commission shall
25       establish tariffs, rates and other charges which are fair and reasonable, and ensure
26       environmental sustainability, economic viability, and a fair return on investments.
27             Service providers may apply for the setting of tariffs, rates, and charges based
28       on and consistent with a scientific and evidence-based rate-setting methodology that
29       the Commission shall, after due consultation, define and publish, taking into account
30       the following, among others:
31             (a)     Reasonable and prudent capital and recurrent costs of providing the
32                     service including a reasonable rate of return on capital;


                                                    45
     1          (b)   Efficiency of the service;
     2          (c)   Incentives for enhancement of efficiency;
     3          (d)   Capacity to pay of consumers;
 4              (e)   Equity considerations;
     5          (f)   Environmental sustainability;
 6              (g)   Administrative simplicity;
 7              (h)   In cases involving valid and subsisting concession agreements prior to
 8                    the enactment of this Act, the methodology provided under the
 9                    concession agreement;
10              (i)   To the fullest extent practicable, the terms and conditions of the tariffs,
11                    rates or other price-setting mechanisms set forth in any concession
12                    agreement, joint venture agreement or other similar agreement for the
13                    provision of water or sanitation services by an existing service provider.
14              Tariffs, rates, and charges set by the Regulatory Units shall be presumed valid
15       and reasonable unless invalidated by the Commission, after due notice and hearing,
16       acting on a protest or contest duly filed with the Commission to ensure continuity of
17       service, application for setting of tariffs, rates and other charges shall be decided
18       within a non-extendible period of ninety (90) days from the date of filing thereof, and
19       the failure of the Commission to act on any such applications shall result in a
20       provisional approval: Provided, That in no case shall proceedings on any applications
21       for the setting of tariffs, rates and other charges exceed to a total period of one
22       hundred twenty (120) days reckoned from the date of filing of the application.
23             Sec. 41. Direct Access. - Fifty-one percent (51%) of the registered water
24       consumers in a service area, municipality, city or province may petition for direct
25       access to water from any water service provider subject to concurrence by and
26       reasonable compensation to the service provider and approval by the Commission. If
27       the petition for direct access from any water service provider is approved by the
28       Commission, the new water service provider shall reimburse the existing service
29       provider for, as applicable, either the book value or the market value as determined
30       by an independent appraiser accredited by the Commission, whichever is higher, of
31       the water source, water supply, or water distribution or transmission facilities,
32       pipelines and other equipment which shall be transferred to or utilized by the new


                                                   46
     1    water service provider. The Commission shall promulgate the implementing rules and
     2    procedures for this mode of service, which shall include, among others, financial and
     3    technical capability of the new service provider.
     4           Sec. 42. Innovative Schemes to Improve Efficiency and Management of
     5    Systems. - The Commission shall promote innovative schemes such as the
     6    consolidation or integration of water supply, sanitation, and sewerage services, or
     7    providers in the same service area, where it shall result in improved efficiency, service
 8       expansion and lower costs.
 9              To this end, the Commission shall establish and issue such rules and guidelines
10       as may be necessary to (a) create performance-based and time-bound incentives to
11       encourage efficiency and service expansion; (b) establish the standards and targets
12       that service providers are required to meet; and (c) define the fines and penalties that
13       shall be imposed for failure to meet such standards and targets.
