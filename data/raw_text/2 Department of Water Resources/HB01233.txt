EXPLANATORY NOTE

This legislation seeks the creation of the Department of Water
Resources as the primary government agency for the management of water
utilities in the country.

When President Ferdinand R. Marcos, Jr. addressed this Congress during
his first State of the Nation Address, he stressed on the importance of
coordination and cooperation between government agencies and private entities
and stakeholders. Further, he instructed that the organizational structure of the
government must be inquired at in order to guarantee its full efficiency.

Today, it appears however that several government agencies still perform
similar functions and responsibilities, resulting to the unnecessary overlapping of
jobs.

Looking into several agencies, units and entities that handle the
management of water utilities, the country has the Local Water Utilities
Administration, the National Irrigation Administration, the National Water
Resources Board, the Laguna Lake Development Authority, as well as the DENR,
the DPWH, the DOH, the DOST and the DOE to name a few.

The obvious overlapping and duplication of functions on the management of
water utilities create a cloud of confusion as to which department handles the
multitude of concerns thereto. In effect, the needed government action could not
be expedited since we have to point our finger first.

In 2019, representatives filed 35 separate bills that would create a
“Department of Water Resources” (DWR), resulting in a consolidated bill, the
House Bill (HB) No. 4944, authorized by Albay Rep. Jose Maria Clemente “Joey”
Salceda and was approved by the House of committees on government
reorganization and public works and highways in November 2019.
===== [page 2] =====
House Bill No. 4944 aspired to make the DWR as the primary
government agency handle management of water utilities. This representation
shares the same vision for a Department that will averse and streamline the
management of water resources and utilities in the country. In this proposed
legislation, the Department will serve as the unified agency that will absorb all the
function and duties related to the management of water resources which are
currently scattered from among several government agencies and entities.

With this bill, significant improvements could be expected in managing this
important water sector, such as the reduction of costs for both the government and
regulated entities, the avoidance of duplication of functions, and the reconciliation
of differences in agencies exercising related responsibilities. This Department aims
to create regulatory synergy rather than dysfunction - and this shall serve as one
of our responses to the call of the President for coordination and cooperation
between government agencies.

CHAPTER 1
GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Department of Water
Resources Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — it is hereby declared the policy of the State;

a) To ensure that our country’s water supply is safe, affordable,
sufficient, and sustainable;

b) To guarantee the protection and preservation of the country’s water
resources;

c) To unify the administration and management of water utilities in the
country;

d) To prevent the duplication of functions in the government through the
creation of a single agency;

e) To oversee the participation of responsible private sectors regarding
our water resources.

SECTION 3. Definition of terms. — As use in this Act, the following terms are
defined as follows:
===== [page 4] =====
a. Bulk water suppliers shall refer to any entity, natural or juridical,
whether public or private, supplying or intending to supply large
quantities of raw treated water to buyers who will use it for own
consumption.

b. Department refers to the Department of Water Resources.

c. Drought Risk is the likelihood of incurring damages and losses brought
about by the effects of drought.

d. Integrated Water resource Management (IWRM}, as the Global Water
Partnership defined, is the process of promoting the coordinated
development and management of water, land, and related resources, to
maximize the resultant economic and social welfare in an equitable
manner without compromising the sustainability of vital ecosystems.

e. Flood Control refers to methods which are used to prevent or minimize
the effect of flood water in certain area.

f. Flood Risk means the probability of the occurrence of flood due to the
combination of the source thereof.

g. Hydropower is the mechanism which uses running or falling water to
power machines or to produce electricity.

h. River Basin and watersheds refers to areas land which drain to a
particular body water. Watersheds are classified as small versions of
basins.

CHAPTER II

THE DEPARTMENT OF WATER RESOURCES

SECTION 4. Creation of the Department of Water Resources. —There is hereby
created and stablished the Department of Water Resources, herein after
referred to as the “Department”. The Department shall have an organizational
structure, power, functions, and duties mandated by the immediately
preceding sections.

SECTION 5. Mandate. — the department shall be the primary policy making,
planning, coordinating, implementing, and administrative agency of the
Executive Branch of the government that will oversee and streamline the
Management of water resources and utilities in the country. The Department
will serve as the unified agency and shall absorb all the functions and duties
related to the management of water resources presently distributed to several
government agencies and instrumentalities.

The Department shall adopt the Integrated Water Resource management
({WRM)}, which is a process promoting the coordinated development and
management of water, land and related resources, as the strategic framework
for national water management, policymaking and planning.
