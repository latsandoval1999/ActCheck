                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

     According to water.org, a global nonprofit organization working to bring water and
 sanitation to the world, out of 115 million Filipinos, 60 million lack access to safe water
 sources and 43 million lack access to a safe toilet. On top of that, our water sources
 are constantly on tremendous pressure due to the ever-increasing demand of our
 population and a burgeoning economy. This is more pronounced during the dry season
 when many of our dams and water sources reach critical levels.

    Aside from water sources problems, another issue that we face is the quality of
 water from our sources. The quality of water from many areas continue to deteriorate
 due to several factors, among them is pollution.

      These problems and issues are difficult to resolve when the government has more
  than 30 different agencies or units that have water-related functions or deal with water
  issues. To effectively implement the Philippines' roadmap to achieve universal water
  and sanitation services coverage by 2028 and achieve synergy and close coordination
  among agencies with water-related functions, it is imperative to have a department
  that will house all these agencies with water-related functions.

     This bill seeks to establish the Department of Water Resources. This Department
  once created, will be the lead agency to implement the country's policies and programs
  on water. It will have the whole-of-government approach in dealing with water issues.
  It will provide synergy, close coordination among government agencies and
  synchronize all the government's policies and programs on water.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is urgently sought.

 7   extendible period of ninety (90) days from the date of filing thereof, and the failure of
 8   the Commission to act on any such applications shall result in a provisional approval:
 9   Provided, That in no case shall proceedings on any applications for the setting of tariffs,
10   rates and other charges exceed to a total period of one hundred twenty (120) days
11   reckoned from the date of filing of the application.
12          Sec. 41. Direct Access. - Fifty-one percent (51%) of the registered water
13   consumers in a service area, municipality, city or province may petition for direct access
14   to water from any water service provider subject to concurrence by and reasonable
15   compensation to the service provider and approval by the Commission. If the petition
16   for direct access from any water service provider is approved by the Commission, the
17   new water service provider shall reimburse the existing service provider for, as
18   applicable, either the book value or the market value as determined by an independent
19   appraiser accredited by the Commission, whichever is higher, of the water source,
20   water supply, or water distribution or transmission facilities, pipelines and other
21   equipment which shall be transferred to or utilized by the new water service provider.
22   The Commission shall promulgate the implementing rules and procedures for this mode
23   of service, which shall include, among others, financial and technical capability of the
24   new service provider.
25          Sec. 42. Innovative Schemes to Improve Efficiency and Management of
26   Systems. - The Commission shall promote innovative schemes such as the
27   consolidation or integration of water supply, sanitation, and sewerage services, or
28   providers in the same service area, where it shall result in improved efficiency, service
29   expansion and lower costs.
30          To this end, the Commission shall establish and issue such rules and guidelines
31   as may be necessary to (a) create performance-based and time-bound incentives to
32   encourage efficiency and service expansion; (b) establish the standards and targets that
33   service providers are required to meet; and (c) define the fines and penalties that shall
34   be imposed for failure to meet such standards and targets.
