                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      We are surrounded by water. In fact, almost seventy percent (70%) of
the earth’s surface is covered with water. Two percent (2%) of it, is
freshwater. However, only one percent (1%) of our freshwater can be
accessed. The rest is trapped in snowfields and glaciers. This tiny fraction of
accessible freshwater caters to around 6.8 billion people.

       According to the World Health Organization (WHO) and the United
Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF), “2.2 billion people lack access to safely
managed drinking water services, almost 2 billion people depend on health
care facilities without basic water services, and 297,000 children under five
die from diarrheal disease, every year, due to poor sanitation, poor hygiene,
or unsafe drinking water.” In the 2021 Report of the United Nations – Water,
2.3 billion people live in countries with water stress level, of which 733
million are in high and critically water-stressed countries. Around seventy
percent (70%) of the world’s water withdrawal is supplied to agriculture.
However, based on the 2020 Report of the UN Food and Agriculture
Organization (FAO), 3.2 billion people from agricultural areas suffer from
water scarcity, 1.2 billion of whom are in severely water-stressed areas.
Water stress basically means that the demand for safe and usable water
exceeds the supply.
       The Philippines is in a near-crisis water situation. Several areas in the
country suffer from water shortage and intermittent water supply. Metro
Manila severely suffered a two-week water crisis in 2019.
According to Water.Org, 3 million Filipinos rely on unsafe water sources. One
out of ten Filipinos do not have access to safe water sources, based on a study
conducted of the WHO. The Philippine Journal of Science published a study
stating that three (3) years from now, seventeen (17) out of the twenty (20)
major river basins in the country will experience water shortage.
We heavily rely on our surface and ground water sources for household and
industrial use. The growing population, rapid urbanization and accelerated
economic development increase the demand for water supply. The
competing demands of agriculture and industries induce water stress and
deplete the aquifers. Additionally, pollution, watershed degradation,
indiscriminate land use plan and climate change affect our water quality and
quantity. Most of our urban rivers are now heavily polluted. In fact, fifty (50)
major rivers are now considered biologically dead.

      It is, therefore, imperative that we prepare for this impending crisis.
We need a sustainable and effective development and management plan for
our limited water resource. We need the necessary infrastructures to bring
our plans into fruition.

      In light of the preceding, this bill’s approval is fervently sought.

 4   Chapter II. ORGANIZATION
 5   SECTION 4. The Department of Water Resources. – There is hereby created
 6   and established the Department of Water Resources, hereinafter referred to
 7   as the “Department,” which shall be organized within one hundred eighty
 8   (180) days after the effectivity of this Act. The Department shall have the
 9   organizational structure, powers and functions needed to perform its
10   mandate as provided in this Chapter.
11   SECTION 5. Mandate of the Department. The Department shall be primary
12   agency responsible for the comprehensive and integrated water resources
13   development and management for the Philippines. It shall exercise such
14   powers and functions needed to review, revise, harmonize and integrate
15   policies and regulations for conservation of water resources and effective
16   management of water supply, sanitation, irrigation, hydropower, flood
17   control, stormwater/ urban drainage. The Department shall be guided by the
18   integrated water resources management (IWRM) framework, take climate
19   risks into account, and ensure sustainability of natural ecosystem functions
20   and services. In managing water resources, the Department shall aim to
21   improve water conservation and increase system efficiencies, and optimize
22   the use of all freshwater in the country including groundwater, surface water,
23   rainwater, run-offs, floods, stormwater and urban drainage, and treated
24   wastewater to meet various needs.
