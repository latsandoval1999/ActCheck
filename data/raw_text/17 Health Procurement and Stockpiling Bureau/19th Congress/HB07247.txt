                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

This proposed measure seeks to protect public health and safety by stockpiling
essential and critical drugs, medicines, vaccines, devices, and materials to
effectively and swiftly address public health emergencies.

This bill creates the Health Procurement and Stockpiling Bureau (HPSB) as a
body under the Department of Health (DOH) to provide a transparent, fair,
proactive, and innovative procurement service to stockpile, conserve, and
facilitate the release of life-saving pharmaceuticals, vaccines, devices, and
materials in times of public health emergencies.

Among other salient responsibilities, the HPSB is responsible for developing
plans, policies, and programs on procurement management, taking the
procurement process following the Government Procurement Reform Act,
advising the Secretary of Health on matters about the procurement of goods and
services, infrastructure and consultancy services, managing the Bureau's
response activities during a public health emergency, conducting procurement
monitoring visits, providing technical assistance to DOH field offices on
procurement matters, identifying and stockpiling strategic and critical drugs and
medicines, vaccines, devices, and materials needed for public health
emergencies, maintaining a buffer supply of these items to ensure the availability
of these items, facilitating the provision of potentially life-saving
pharmaceuticals, and acting as the supply-chain manager to ensure the rotation
and replenishment of stocks.

Hence, this representation seeks the passage of this measure.

                                AN ACT
     PROVIDING FOR THE STOCKPILING OF STRATEGIC AND CRITICAL DRUGS
     AND MEDICINES, VACCINES, DEVICES, AND MATERIALS FOR PUBLIC
     HEALTH EMERGENCIES, CREATING FOR THE PURPOSE THE HEALTH
     PROCUREMENT AND STOCKPILING BUREAU UNDER THE DEPARTMENT
     OF HEALTH, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR
     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Health
 2   Procurement and Stockpiling Act”.
 3

 4         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and
 5   promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among
 6   them. The State also mandates the adoption of an integrated and comprehensive
 7   approach to health development.
 8

 9         Towards this end, it shall protect public health and safety by preventing
10   and controlling the spread of diseases and other health hazards through the
11   stockpiling of essential and critical drugs and medicines, vaccines, devices, and
12   materials to effectively and swiftly address the devastating consequences of
13   public health emergencies.
14

 2          SEC. 4. Creation of a Health Procurement and Stockpiling Bureau. –
 3   There is hereby created a body under the Department of Health (DOH) to be
 4   known as the Health Procurement and Stockpiling Bureau, hereinafter referred
 5   to as the Bureau. It shall serve as the principal agency mandated to undertake
 6   a transparent, fair, proactive, and innovative procurement service for the DOH
 7   and to stockpile, conserve, and facilitate the release of adequate amounts of
 8   potentially    life-saving pharmaceuticals, vaccines, devices, and materials in
 9   times of public health emergencies.
10

11         SEC. 5. Organization. – The Bureau shall absorb the Procurement
12   Service and       Logistics Management Division of the DOH. The Secretary of
13   Health shall determine the organizational structure and staffing modifications of
14   the Bureau subject to the approval of           the Department of Budget and
15   Management (DBM) in accordance with the existing civil        service laws, rules
16   and regulations.
17

18         The DOH shall ensure an effective and efficient monitoring system for the
19   proper implementation of the procurement and stockpiling of drugs and
20   medicines, vaccines, devices, and materials.

 6        SEC. 7. Sources. – Consistent with the country’s obligations under
 7   international treaties and agreements, drugs and medicines, vaccines, devices,
 8   and materials may be obtained from domestic or foreign sources and the
 9   procurement thereof shall be open to all eligible suppliers, manufacturers and
10   distributors. However, in the interest of availability, efficiency, and timely
11   delivery of drugs and medicines, vaccines, devices, and materials, the Bureau
12   shall encourage the development of domestic sources to ensure steady, available
13   and adequate supply of such drugs and medicines, vaccines, devices, and
14   materials that are essential in responding to public health emergencies, and in
15   such manner as may be allowed by law, to include countertrade and industrial
16   cooperation to augment stockpiling and availability of critical materials by:
17

18         (a) Purchasing, or making a commitment to purchase, either directly or
19   through countertrade, strategic and critical drugs and medicines, vaccines,
20   devices, and materials of domestic origin when such are needed for the stockpile;
21

22          (b) Contracting with domestic facilities, or making a commitment to
23   contract with domestic facilities, for the processing or refining of strategic and
24   critical drugs and medicines, vaccines, devices, and materials in the stockpile
25   when processing or refining is necessary to convert such into a form more
26   suitable for storage and subsequent disposition;
27

28          (c) Identifying existing domestic facilities and domestically produced
29   strategic and critical drugs and medicines, vaccines, devices, and materials to
30   meet the requirements of public health and essential civilian industries in times
31   of public health emergency when existing domestic sources of supply are either
32   insufficient or vulnerable to single points of failure; and
33

34         (d) Contracting with domestic facilities to recycle strategic and critical
35   devices and materials, thereby increasing domestic supplies when such devices
36   and materials would otherwise be insufficient to meet public health needs.

 7         SEC. 9. Establishment of Medical Stockpiling Fund and Tax
 8   Exemptions. – All donations, contributions, grants, bequests, or gifts, in cash
 9   or in kind, received from various sources, shall be placed into a fund, to be
10   known as the Medical Stockpiling Fund. This Fund shall be expended to support
11   the National Drug and Device Security Program in accordance with existing
12   budgeting, accounting and auditing rules and regulations.
13

14          The DOH may solicit and receive grants, bequests, endowments, donations
15   and contributions which shall form part of the Fund. Said grants, bequests,
16   endowments, donations and contributions used actually, directly and exclusively
17   for the purpose of the Fund shall be exempt from donor’s tax and the same shall
18   be considered as allowable deduction from gross income for purposes of
19   computing the taxable income of the donor, in accordance with Section 34
20   (H)(2)(a) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended. Likewise,
21   fund raising activities may be conducted by the DOH and the proceeds of which
22   shall accrue to the Fund and shall be exempt from any and all taxes.
23

24         Receipts from donations, whether in cash or in kind, shall be accounted
25   for by the DOH in accordance with accounting and auditing rules and
26   regulations. The receipts from cash donations and proceeds from the sale of
27   donated commodities shall be deposited with the National Treasury and recorded
28   as a special account in the General Fund and shall be made available to the DOH
29   through a special budget pursuant to Section 35, Chapter 5, Book VI of Executive
30   Order No. 292. The cash value of the donations shall be deemed automatically
31   appropriated for the purpose specified by the donor. Donations with a term not
32   exceeding one (1) year shall be treated as trust receipts.
33

34          The DOH shall submit the quarterly reports of all donations received,
35   whether in cash or in kind, and expenditures or disbursements thereon with
36   electronic signature to the Secretary of the DBM, through the Unified Reporting
37   System, and to the Speaker of the House of the Representatives, the President of
38   the Senate of the Philippines, the Chairpersons of the House Committee on
39   Appropriations and the Senate Committee on Finance, and the Chairperson of
40   the Commission on Audit, by posting such reports on the DOH website for a
41   period of three (3) years. The Secretary of Health shall send written notice to the
42   said offices when reports have been posted on its website, which shall be
43   considered the date of submission.
