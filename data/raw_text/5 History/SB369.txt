                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines is naturally endowed with rich coastal resources. It has a coastline
of 36,289 kilometers. Its territorial waters including its Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)
total 2.2 million square kilometers, of which 266,000 are coastal while the rest are oceanic
(Department of Agriculture-Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources, Philippine
Fisheries Profile, 2011:15). The country's coral reef areas are amongst the most extensive
in the world, second only to its neighbor Indonesia. It has large tracts of mangrove forests
and sizable sea grass areas scattered around Its coasts. In fact, the country is considered
to be the apex of the Coral Triangle, which is recognized as the world's center of marine
biodiversity (National Plan of Action for the Coral Triangle Initiative on Coral Reefs,
Fisheries and Food Security, 2009:1).

       A separate Department must be created to focus on fisheries and aquatic
resources. Technically speaking, fisheries deal with the sustainable management of
fisheries and other aquatic resources and ensuring an ecosystem-based approach to
cover production, through capture and culture, processing and marketing of fish and
other living species from the aquatic environment and the livelihoods and settlement of
artisanal fisherfolk. On the other hand, agriculture refers to cultivation of land for crops
and rearing animals to provide food. A separate department will institutionalize a
workforce that is solely dedicated to the management of the country's fisheries and
aquatic resources and untappIng the potentials of the blue economy. According to
Philippine Agriculture 2020 of the National Academy of Science and Technology of the
Department of Science and Technology (DOST), "Fisheries is too big of a problem to be
handled by the DA which Is already beset with many problems and challenges like
declining productivity and competitiveness."
       For an agency exercising supervision over fisheries and aquatic resources to be
truly efficient, it must have necessary funding to undertake and implement
comprehensive programs and policies. At present, BFAR has limited funding because it
depends on the budgetary allocation of its mother agency, the DA. It covers the function
meant for a department but only gets a limited budget meant for a line bureau. BFAR
reported that It needs PHP 60 billion to properly implement fisheries law enforcement and
management. But for Fiscal Year 2019, out of the DA budget of PHP 47,293,174,000,
BFAR was only allocated PHP 5,758,003,000.10. Elevating the Bureau to a Department
will likewise increase its budgetary allocations. The fisheries sector has a significant
contribution to the country's economy hence it is only fitting that a government agency
exercising authority over it will be equipped with financial muscle to perform its mandate.
      A Department of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources can provide Institutional support
to maximize the potential of the Blue Economy and to Implement the Comprehensive
National Fisheries Industry Development Plan and the Comprehensive Post Harvest,
Marketing and Ancillary Industries Plan. The estimated total contribution of the maritime
sector to the Philippine economy, including the fishing industry, was valued at Php210.39
billion in 2019. This accounts for 2.6% of the country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP).
To reiterate the findings of the NAST study, potentially, the Net Annual Benefit from our
marine ecosystem under the Blue Economy is conservatively estimated at Php 317.33
Billion. The Blue Economy has likewise produced employment to 1.53 million persons in
2019 and directly employs around 1.9 million registered fisherfolk. It still has great
potential for growth if a strong institution will provide necessary staffing and funding.
There is also a need for strong institutional support to implement the CNFIDP (2016-
2020) and the CPHMAIP (2018-2022). Over a 5-year period, given the funding of Php30.6
billion and the staffing, the CNFIDP targets a 1% annual growth in municipal capture
fisheries, 5% annual growth in commercial capture fisheries and 10% reduction In post­
harvest losses. In aquaculture, the CNFIDP targets a 4% Increase in milkfish production,
25% increase in seaweeds production, 10% Increase in shellfish production, 5.4%
increase in mangrove crab production, 10% increase in shrimp production and 6%
increase in tilapia production. In terms of the CPHMAIP, total Investment requirement Is
estimated at Php29 billion with projected accrued benefit at Php74 billion over a 5-year
period. Moreover, under the developmental framework of the Blue Economy, DFAR will
now have the necessary resources to capitalize the huge potential of our marine
resources, thus restoring its condition, enhancing sustainability, and at the same time,
providing Increased revenues to our country's coffers.
