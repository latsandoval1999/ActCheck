                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The 1987 Philippine Constitution declares as a primary goal of the State the
promotion of full employment and equality of employment opportunities for all. 1 While
the country has enacted significant reforms to streamline government transactions
through the Republic Act (“R.A”) No. 11032 (2018), otherwise known as the Ease of Doing
Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018, regulatory
modernization can further be leveraged not only to reduce red tape but also to strengthen
employment facilitation systems at the local level.

       Local government units already process thousands of business permit renewals
annually. This routine interaction between enterprises and government presents a
strategic opportunity to gather real-time labor market information that can significantly
improve job matching. At present, many Public Employment Service Offices (PESOs),
established under R.A. 10691, or the Public Employment Service Office Act of 2015, rely
largely on voluntary reporting of vacancies. As a result, jobseekers often face fragmented
access to employment information, while businesses struggle to reach qualified local
applicants.

       This bill proposes a minimal, non-burdensome disclosure mechanism requiring
businesses renewing their permits to indicate whether they have current or projected job
openings. The requirement is purely informational and facilitative. It does not condition
permit approval on hiring outcomes, does not impose penalties for non-hiring, and does
not introduce additional fees. Instead, it integrates employment reporting into an already

existing administrative process, ensuring that regulatory efficiency and labor market
development operate in tandem.

        By electronically transmitting vacancy information to PESOs, the measure
strengthens local employment ecosystems, enhances workforce analytics, and supports
evidence-based planning. It enables faster job matching, reduces friction between
employers and jobseekers, and promotes inclusive economic growth, particularly for
first-time workers, displaced employees, and underemployed sectors.

        This proposal aligns with the State’s commitments to employment generation,
digital governance, and public service modernization. It transforms a routine compliance
procedure into a proactive employment linkage mechanism without undermining the
principles of ease of doing business.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

AN ACT REQUIRING BUSINESSES TO SUBMIT JOB VACANCY INFORMATION
    TO THE PUBLIC EMPLOYMENT SERVICE OFFICE (PESO) UPON RENEWAL
    OF BUSINESS PERMITS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT
    NO. 11032, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “EASE OF DOING BUSINESS
    AND EFFICIENT GOVERNMENT SERVICE DELIVERY ACT OF 2018,” AND
    FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Post and Match Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote full employment, strengthen local labor market matching mechanisms, and
enhance coordination between businesses and government employment services. The
State recognizes that streamlined regulatory processes should be leveraged to support
economic opportunity, workforce development, and efficient labor market information
systems without imposing undue burden on enterprises.

       SEC. 4. A new subsection is hereby inserted under Section 9 of Republic Act No.
11032, to read as follows:
           “SECTION 9-A. INTEGRATION OF LOCAL EMPLOYMENT
      REPORTING IN BUSINESS PERMIT RENEWAL. — ALL BUSINESS
      ENTITIES APPLYING FOR THE RENEWAL OF BUSINESS PERMITS
      BEFORE LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS SHALL, AS PART OF THE
      STANDARD RENEWAL FORM, INDICATE WHETHER THEY HAVE
      EXISTING OR PROJECTED JOB VACANCIES WITHIN THE NEXT
      TWELVE (12) MONTHS.

              THE DISCLOSURE SHALL BE LIMITED TO:

         1.   JOB TITLE OR OCCUPATIONAL CLASSIFICATION;
         2.   NUMBER OF VACANCIES;
         3.   BASIC QUALIFICATION REQUIREMENTS; AND
         4.   PREFERRED CONTACT CHANNEL FOR APPLICANTS.

           SUCH INFORMATION SHALL BE ELECTRONICALLY
      TRANSMITTED TO THE PUBLIC EMPLOYMENT SERVICE OFFICE
      (PESO) OF THE CONCERNED LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNIT FOR
      PURPOSES OF JOB MATCHING, LABOR MARKET INFORMATION,
      AND EMPLOYMENT FACILITATION.

           SUBMISSION OF JOB VACANCY INFORMATION SHALL NOT
      BE CONSTRUED AS A CONDITION FOR APPROVAL OF PERMIT
      RENEWAL, NOR SHALL FAILURE TO HIRE FROM PESO REFERRALS
      CONSTITUTE A REGULATORY VIOLATION. THE REQUIREMENT IS
      PURELY INFORMATIONAL AND FACILITATIVE IN CHARACTER.

           NOTHING IN THIS ACT SHALL BE CONSTRUED TO REQUIRE
      BUSINESSES TO CONDUCT RECRUITMENT EXCLUSIVELY
      THROUGH PUBLIC EMPLOYMENT SERVICE OFFICES (“PESOS”)
      NOR TO RESTRICT THEIR INDEPENDENT HIRING PROCESSES.

           NO ADDITIONAL FEES OR CHARGES SHALL BE IMPOSED ON
      BUSINESSES FOR COMPLIANCE WITH THIS SECTION.”

       SEC. 5. Coordination with PESOs. – Local government units shall ensure system
interoperability between Business Permit and Licensing Offices (BPLO) and their
respective PESOs pursuant to Republic Act No. 10691. The Department of Labor and
Employment (“DOLE”) shall issue guidelines for standardized reporting formats
consistent with national labor market information systems. The reporting system shall be
integrated, where feasible, with the national business portal established under Republic
Act No. 11032.

        SEC. 6. Data Privacy Safeguards. – All information collected under this Act shall
comply with the Data Privacy Act of 2012. Data shall be used exclusively for employment
facilitation, workforce analytics, and policy planning.

       SEC. 7. National Vacancy Database and Reporting. — The DOLE shall maintain
a centralized national vacancy database consolidated from PESO submissions under this
Act. DOLE shall establish performance indicators, including vacancy referral rates, local
hiring outcomes, and PESO utilization metrics, and shall submit an annual report to
Congress based on such indicators.

       SEC. 8. Incentives for Compliance. — Local government units may establish
recognition or incentive mechanisms for businesses that consistently submit vacancy
information and actively participate in PESO employment facilitation programs. Such
incentives may include public recognition, expedited processing lanes, or inclusion in
local employment partnership registries, subject to existing laws and regulations.

       SEC. 9. Non-Compliance with Reporting. – Failure to submit vacancy information
or to submit a declaration of no vacancy shall not be a ground for denial, suspension, or
delay of permit renewal; however, such non-submission shall be documented solely for
administrative monitoring and reporting purposes.

       SEC. 10. Transitory Provision. — Within twelve (12) months from the effectivity
of this Act, local government units shall progressively integrate the vacancy reporting
requirement into their business permit renewal systems. During the transition period,
submission of vacancy information or a declaration of no vacancy may be accepted
through manual or interim reporting mechanisms as may be prescribed in the
implementing rules and regulations. Full electronic integration shall be required upon
the completion of the transition period.

        SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, DOLE, in coordination with the Anti-Red Tape Authority (ARTA), the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and the Department of
Information and Communications Technology (“DICT”), shall promulgate the necessary
rules and regulations.
       DOLE and DICT shall provide technical assistance, digital templates, and system
integration support to local government units to ensure effective compliance with this
Act.

      SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
