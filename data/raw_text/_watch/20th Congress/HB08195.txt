                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE


      In modern Filipino families, the Filipina has evolved into the man’s partner and co-
      decision-maker, challenging the traditional patriarchal structures mainly due to
      economic reasons. It has become inevitable that both the father and mother of the
      family are employed or economically gainful, and thus major decisions are made by
      them alongside their husbands.
      Article II Sec. 14 of the 1987 Constitution acknowledges the importance of women in
      our society: “The State recognizes the role of women in nation-building, and shall
      ensure the fundamental equality before the law of women and men.”
      Women make up a significant portion of the country’s labor force. According to an
      online news article, around 21.9 million women, or 56.27% are participating in the
      labor force as of December 2023.1 Although less than that of men2, this number is big

enough to warrant the question of whether workplaces are adequately equipped to
address their unique health and wellness needs. Issues such as lack of lactation
facilities, insufficient menstrual hygiene support, limited access to reproductive health
services, and inadequate counseling programs continue to affect women’s
productivity, dignity, and overall well-being.
This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize employer responsibility in providing
facilities and programs that promote women’s health. By requiring lactation rooms,
wellness clinics or partnerships, hygiene facilities, counseling services, and health
education programs, this measure ensures that women are supported not only as
workers but also as individuals with distinct health needs.
This bill also provides incentives to employers who comply with and go beyond the
minimum requirements, such as tax deductions, government grants, and recognition
awards. These incentives encourage employers to view women’s health and
wellness not as a burden, but as an investment in productivity, inclusivity, and
corporate reputation.
Thus, while the tax deductions may be seen to reduce government resources, it
should be viewed as an alternative way of putting government resources to good and
efficient use, in that the female employees need not queue for State-provided
programs and benefits as these are already on-hand when she needs them.
The measure aligns with the State’s commitment to gender equality, decent work,
and sustainable development. It recognizes that a healthy workforce is a happy and
productive workforce, and that supporting women’s wellness is essential to national
progress.
In view of the above cited reasons, approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
       PROMOTING WOMEN’S HEALTH AND WELLNESS IN THE WORKPLACE,
                      AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
  in Congress assembled:

  Section 1. Short Title – This Act shall be known as the “Women’s Health and
  Wellness in the Workplace Act of 2026.”

  Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:
  1. Promote and protect the health, wellness, and dignity of women in the workplace.
  2. Ensure that employers provide facilities and programs that support women’s
     physical, mental, and reproductive health.
  3. Recognize that women’s health and wellness are essential to productivity,
     equality, and national development.

  Sec. 3. Definition of Terms – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
  mean:
  1. Women’s Health and Wellness Facilities – Physical spaces and services provided
     in the workplace to support women’s health, including but not limited to lactation
     rooms, wellness clinics, counseling areas, and hygiene facilities.
  2. Wellness Program – Structured initiatives by employers to promote women’s
     health, including education, counseling, and preventive care.

  Sec. 4. Mandatory Facilities and Programs – All employers with at least twenty
  (20) female employees shall provide:
   1. Lactation Rooms – Safe, private, and hygienic spaces for breastfeeding or milk
      expression.
                                                                                           4


2. Wellness Clinics or Partnerships – On-site clinics or partnerships with nearby
   health providers offering reproductive health services, prenatal/postnatal care,
   and general wellness check-ups.
3. Hygiene Facilities – Adequate restrooms with menstrual hygiene supplies and
   disposal systems.
4. Counseling and Mental Health Support – Access to counseling services for stress
   management, postpartum recovery, and workplace wellness.
5. Health Education Programs – Regular seminars or workshops on women’s
   health, nutrition, and preventive care.

Employers or partner establishments, as the case may be, shall ensure that the
female employees who avail and use the facilities are afforded the privacy they need.

Sec. 5. Employer Incentives – Employers who comply with and go beyond
minimum requirements shall be entitled to:
1. Tax Deductions – Expenses incurred in establishing and maintaining women’s
    health facilities shall be deductible from taxable income.
2. Government Grants – Eligible for DOLE or DOH grants to support wellness
    programs.
3. Recognition Awards – Annual “Healthy Workplace for Women” awards to honor
    exemplary employers.

Sec. 6. The following agencies of the government shall have the following duties and
responsibilities:
 1. The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall oversee implementation
    and compliance of employers to this Act;
 2. The Department of Health (DOH) shall provide technical assistance, training, and
    monitoring of workplace health facilities;
 3. The Commission on Women (PCW) shall ensure gender sensitivity in program
    design and implementation.

Sec. 7. Monitoring and Compliance
1. Employers must submit annual reports to DOLE detailing their women’s health
   and wellness programs.
2. Non-compliance shall result in penalties, including fines and suspension of
   government incentives.

Sec. 8. Appropriations – The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

Sec. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations – The Secretary of Labor and
Employment, in consultation with the Secretary of Health and of Finance, and the
Commissioner of PCW, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary to carry
out and implement the provisions of this Act within sixty days (60) from the effectivity
of this Act.

Sec. 10. Separability Clause - Should any part or provision of this Act be held
unconstitutional or invalid, all other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby
shall continue to be in full force and effect.

Sec. 11. Repealing Clause – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, letters
of instruction, administrative rules and regulations or parts thereof which are contrary
to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

Sec. 12. Effectivity – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation whichever is earlier.
