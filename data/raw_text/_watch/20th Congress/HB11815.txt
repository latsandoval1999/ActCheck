                             AN ACT
PROTECTING DIGITAL LIKENESS AND PERSONA RIGHTS BY CONFERRING
ECONOMIC RIGHTS OVER AN INDIVIDUAL'S IMAGE, VOICE, AND LIKENESS,
ESTABLISHING TECHNICAL FORENSIC AND REGULATORY CAPABILITIES,
PROVIDING SAFE HARBORS AND EXEMPTIONS, AND IMPOSING PENALTIES FOR
VIOLATIONS THEREOF

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

With the rapid emergence and proliferation of generative Artificial Intelligence (AI)
technologies, cloning vocal signatures, manipulating visual media, and synthesizing
realistic deepfakes have become increasingly accessible. This technological leap has
severely blurred the line between authentic human conduct and fabricated digital media,
leaving individuals vulnerable to unauthorized digital exploitation. Beyond personal
privacy, this capability poses acute threats to personal reputation, individual dignity,
financial security, and public trust. When deployed without consent, synthetic media
becomes a potent tool for online harassment, defamation, fraud, commercial exploitation,
and targeted misinformation.

Across the globe, governments are recognizing that legacy laws are no longer sufficient
to combat algorithmic impersonation, prompting a massive wave of international
legislative action. To cite examples, there is the European Union's Artificial Intelligence
Act enacted in 2024 which establishes strict transparency and disclosure requirements
for synthetic media, enforcing mandatory machine-readable watermarking for generative
AI outputs and clear, visible labeling for all deepfakes and AI-manipulated content,
specifically stated in Article 50 of the Act. In Denmark, there is a pending bill which
proposes a copyright law that would give citizens ownership of their face, voice, and body
to fight against AI deepfakes. In the United States, the State of Tennessee pioneered
direct identity safeguards through the Ensuring Likeness Voice and Image Security Act
(ELVIS Act) enacted in 2024, protecting vocal and visual likenesses from AI cloning, while
federal initiatives like the TAKE IT DOWN Act (An Act to Require Covered Platforms to
Remove Nonconsensual Intimate Visual Deceptions, and for Other Purposes) passed by
the U.S. Congress and signed into law in May 2025, it criminalizes the online publication
of non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII)—including AI-generated deepfake
pornography—and mandates that online platforms remove such content within 48 hours.
South Korea enacted its AI Basic Act and severe criminal amendments penalizing the
distribution and possession of sexually explicit deepfakes, while Singapore enforces strict
election and safety laws, such as the Elections Integrity of Online Advertising
(Amendment) Act or (ELIONA), which targets deceptive digital media during election
periods.

While several statutory frameworks in the Philippines touch upon aspects of personal
identity and digital misuse, none were structurally designed to address the unique
complexities presented by generative AI and synthetic identity theft:

   •   The Civil Code of the Philippines (Republic Act No. 386): Article 26 protects
       personal dignity and privacy, providing civil remedies against prying into private
       life and the unauthorized commercial exploitation of a person's name or portrait.
       However, its remedies are strictly retroactive and civil in nature, lacking rapid-
       response mechanics or preventive administrative recourse against real-time digital
       dissemination.
   •   The Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines (Republic Act No. 8293):
       Section 123(c) prevents the registration of trademarks containing the name,
       portrait, or signature of a living individual without written consent. Beyond
       trademark registration, however, traditional intellectual property law remains
       anchored to fixed original works, leaving a critical statutory gap regarding non-
       commercial deepfakes, synthetic voice replication, and personal digital persona
       rights.
   •   The Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10175): Section
       4(b)(3) penalizes computer-related identity theft involving the unauthorized
       acquisition or misuse of identifying data online. Yet, establishing computer-related
       identity theft requires proving traditional elements of fraud or economic deceit,
       which fails to capture non-fraudulent reputational damage, deepfake harassment,
       or non-consensual biometric simulation.
   •   The Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173): While protecting
       personal data processing and empowering the National Privacy Commission to
       penalize unauthorized profiling, it treats personal image and voice primarily as
       informational data points rather than conferring an affirmative, standalone property
       right over one's synthesized digital persona.
   •   The Safe Spaces Act (Republic Act No. 11313): Section 12 addresses gender-
       based online sexual harassment, penalizing non-consensual cyber-flashing and
       the unauthorized upload of intimate photos or videos. While vital, its scope is
       restricted to sexually explicit content and gender-based harassment, leaving non-
       intimate commercial deepfakes, unauthorized political impersonations, and
       general vocal/visual cloning unaddressed.

To bridge these statutory divides, a specialized, sui generis legislative framework is
necessary. Existing laws remain fragmented, reactive, and reliant on legal definitions
tailored to pre-AI paradigms. Current statutes fail to hold online hosting services and
social media platforms explicitly accountable under compulsory notice-and-takedown
mandates with strict statutory deadlines. Furthermore, existing laws do not grant
individuals an inherent, enforceable property right over their voice timbre, speech
patterns, facial features, and digital likeness independent of a pre-existing copyrighted
work.

This measure — the "Digital Likeness and Persona Rights Act" — establishes a
comprehensive, specialized legal regime tailored directly to the challenges of artificial
intelligence. The bill explicitly vests in every individual an exclusive, personal economic
right over the commercial exploitation and public dissemination of their own image, voice,
and likeness, including any realistic digital imitation or deepfake thereof. By classifying
this right as a sui generis intellectual property right that subsists independently of
traditional copyright fixation, this legislation creates a robust legal foundation to protect
personal identity, impose strict notice-and-takedown obligations on digital platforms, and
provide both civil and criminal redress for victims of unauthorized AI manipulation.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Digital Likeness and
Persona Rights Act".

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to uphold
the rights of its citizens in the protection of their dignity, privacy, and personal identity.
The State recognizes the capability of Artificial Intelligence (AI) to create realistic digital
imitations of an individual's image, voice, and likeness, that if misused, could easily be
utilized as a means to spread misinformation, defamation, fraud, identity theft, emotional
stress, and other forms of harm. This Act aims to balance the protection of individual
rights with the promotion of technological innovation and freedom of expression. Toward
this end, the State shall implement measures to achieve the following objectives:

       a) Enable individuals to demand the removal of AI-generated content using their
       likeness without consent;

       b) Provide a legal basis for individuals to seek just compensation if harmed by
       unauthorized AI-generated or manipulated content; and

       c) Establish clear responsibilities and accountability for online platforms hosting
       deepfakes and other AI-generated content that violate this Act.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act, the following terms shall be defined
as follows:

      a) ‘Artificial Intelligence or AI’ refers to computerized methods and tools, including
      but not limited to machine learning and natural language processing, that act in a
      way that resembles human cognitive abilities when solving problems or performing
      certain tasks;

      b) ‘Consent’ refers to affirmative, conscious, and voluntary authorization made by
      an individual free from force, fraud, duress, misrepresentation, or coercion;

      c) ‘Persona Rights’ refer to the exclusive economic and proprietary rights of
      identifiable individuals over their own image, voice, and likeness, irrespective of
      traditional copyright fixation;

      d) ‘Deepfake’ refers to any video recording, motion-picture film, sound recording,
      electronic image, or photograph, or any technological representation of speech or
      conduct substantially derivative thereof:

          1. which appears to authentically depict any speech or conduct of a person
             who did not in fact engage in such speech or conduct; and
          2. the production of which was substantially dependent upon technical means,
             rather than the ability of another person to physically or verbally
             impersonate such person;

      e) ‘Digital Forgery’ refers to any intimate visual depiction of an identifiable
      individual created through the use of software, machine learning, artificial
      intelligence, or any other computer-generated or technological means, including
      by adapting, modifying, manipulating, or altering an authentic visual depiction, that,
      when viewed as a whole by a reasonable person, is indistinguishable from an
      authentic visual depiction of the individual;

      f) ‘Generative AI System’ refers to any system, tool, or platform that uses artificial
      intelligence to generate or substantially modify video, audio, or print text content;

      g) ‘Identifiable Individual’ refers to a person who appears in whole or in part in an
      intimate visual depiction or synthetic media; and whose face, likeness, voice, or
      other distinguishing characteristic is displayed or reproduced in connection
      therewith;

      h) ‘Likeness’ refers to an individual's image, name, voice, or other distinctive
      features that are uniquely identified with the individual;

      i) ‘Online Platform’ refers to any digital service which allows its users to upload,
      share, or disseminate content to the public, including social media networks, video-
      sharing platforms, and content hosting services; and
       j) ‘Voice’ refers to the unique vocal characteristics, timbre, and speech patterns or
       mannerisms of an individual.

SEC. 4. Nature and Scope of Persona Rights Over Image, Likeness, and Voice. —
Every individual shall possess an exclusive and personal right to control the commercial
exploitation and public dissemination of their own image, voice, and likeness, including
any realistic digital imitation or deepfake thereof. Notwithstanding the provisions of the
Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines, as amended, this right shall be treated as
an intellectual property right and shall subsist irrespective of whether the individual's
image, voice, or likeness appears in a copyrighted work.

SEC. 5. Prohibited Acts. — The following acts shall be considered unlawful:

       a) Generating, creating, or disseminating a deepfake of an individual's image,
       voice, or likeness for commercial use, or through a manner that implies
       endorsement of a product or service, without the explicit and informed consent of
       the individual concerned;

       b) Generating, creating, or disseminating a deepfake of an individual's image,
       voice, or likeness regardless of commercial intent, if the material is intended to
       cause harm, including, but not limited to, psychological, financial, or reputational
       harm, or to spread misinformation or disinformation against the identifiable
       individual;

       c) Committing digital forgery: Provided, That the intimate visual depiction was
       obtained or created under circumstances in which the person knew or reasonably
       should have known that the identifiable individual had a reasonable expectation of
       privacy; and

       d) Failure on the part of an online platform to prevent or remove a deepfake of any
       identifiable individual within forty-eight (48) hours from receipt of notice.

SEC. 6. Statutory Exceptions and Permitted Uses. — To safeguard freedom of
expression, creativity, and the public interest, the prohibitions under Section 5 of this Act
shall not apply to the following, provided they do not constitute commercial endorsement
or malicious defamation:

       a) Parody, Satire, and Artistic Expression - Use of an individual's likeness in works
       created primarily for caricature, satire, parody, or artistic expression;

       b) News, Public Affairs, and Documentary - Use of likeness in bona fide news
       reporting, broadcasting, public archives, historical accounts, or documentaries
       addressing matters of legitimate public interest; and

       c) Public Discourse - The incidental, non-malicious depiction of public officials or
       public figures in the course of political critique or public discourse.
SEC. 7. Platform Compliance, Designated Agents, and Counter-Notices. — Online
platforms operating in the Philippines shall have the following duties and protection:

      a) Every online platform operating or serving users in the Philippines shall establish
      a transparent notice-and-takedown mechanism and designate a local legal
      compliance agent or electronic channel specifically for receiving deepfake removal
      notices.

      b) Platforms shall provide a good-faith counter-notice procedure allowing users
      whose content was removed to challenge takedowns that fall under the statutory
      exceptions in Section 6.

      c) Online platforms that promptly comply with valid takedown notices within the
      prescribed 48-hour period shall enjoy safe harbor protection from civil damages
      arising from the hosting of the disputed material.

SEC. 8. Evidentiary Presumptions and Mandatory Watermarking. —

      a) Generative AI developers and platforms operating in the Philippines shall embed
      verifiable digital watermarks or clear metadata labels identifying synthetic media
      as AI-generated.

      b) Once the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT)
      or law enforcement certifies that media exhibits technical indicators of
      unwatermarked or malicious AI manipulation, a rebuttable presumption shall arise
      in judicial proceedings that the content is unauthorized.

SEC. 9. Extraterritorial Application. — This Act shall apply to any online platform,
service provider, or individual that generates, hosts, or disseminates unauthorized
deepfakes targeting any citizen or resident of the Philippines, regardless of the physical
location of the platform's servers or corporate domicile. Non-compliant foreign platforms
may face administrative restrictions, throttling, or suspension of operations within
Philippine cyberspace through the DICT and the National Telecommunications
Commission (NTC).

SEC. 10. Penalties. — Any person, juridical or otherwise, found violating Section 5(a),
(b), and (c) of this Act shall be punished with imprisonment of prision mayor or a fine of
not less than Five hundred thousand pesos (P500,000.00) but not more than One million
pesos (P1,000,000.00), or both, at the discretion of the court. Any person found guilty of
the punishable act under Section 5(d) of this Act shall be punished with imprisonment of
prision correccional or a fine of not less than Two hundred thousand pesos (P200,000.00)
but not more than Five hundred thousand pesos (P500,000.00), or both.

Any identifiable individual who suffered injury by reason of or on the occasion of the
commission of the acts punishable under Section 5 of this Act shall be entitled to the
award of moral, exemplary, and actual damages under the Civil Code of the Philippines.
When any of the acts punishable under Section 5 of this Act is committed by a juridical
person, the principal penalty of imprisonment shall be imposed upon the president,
manager, director, officer, agent, or employee who directly violated or caused, directed,
cooperated, or participated in the commission of the violation.

SEC. 11. Enhancement of Forensic and Detection Capabilities. — The Department
of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), in coordination with the National
Privacy Commission (NPC) and the Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center
(CICC), shall establish and maintain advanced technical capabilities for the detection,
analysis, and forensic verification of deepfakes, synthetic media, and digital forgeries.
The DICT shall likewise provide technical assistance, forensic validation, and
authentication support to law enforcement agencies and aggrieved individuals in
evaluating contested digital content.

SEC. 12. Appropriations and Budgetary Allocation. — The initial funding necessary
for the procurement of advanced media authentication software, watermark-tracking
tools, and the establishment of technical forensic infrastructure under this Act shall be
charged against the current year's appropriations of the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT) and the National Privacy Commission (NPC).
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for continuous capacity building,
technological upgrading, and operational maintenance of implementing agencies shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Justice (DOJ), the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), and the National Privacy Commission (NPC) shall
jointly promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part hereof is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, acts, decrees, executive orders, issuances, and
rules and regulations or parts thereof which are contrary to or inconsistent with this Act
are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
