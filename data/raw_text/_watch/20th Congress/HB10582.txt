                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

Republic Act No. 9136, or the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) of
2001, is one of the most important structural reforms in the Philippine electric
power industry, with the declared policy of promoting free and fair
competition, ensuring transparent and reasonable electricity prices, and
protecting the public interest in the provision of power and related services.

More than two decades after its enactment, however, captive customers of
distribution utilities continue to face opaque and potentially excessive
generation charges, particularly under automatic pass‑through mechanisms
and complex power supply agreements whose cost structures are difficult for
consumers and policymakers to scrutinize.

Experience has likewise shown that the existing framework on
cross‑ownership and market power abuse does not sufficiently prevent large
distribution utilities and their affiliated generation companies from entering
into arrangements that allow undue control over supply, pricing, and market
behavior to the prejudice of consumers and other industry participants.

In Mindanao, the potential privatization of the Agus and Pulangui
Hydroelectric Power Complexes would expose strategic, predominantly
renewable assets to commercial pressures that may compromise long‑term
affordability, public control, and regional equity.
These gaps and risks constrain the full realization of EPIRA’s intent and
undermine the protection of captive market end‑users.

Thus, this bill proposes to amend Sections 43(f), 45, and 47(f) of EPIRA to
strengthen consumer protection, deter market power abuse, and preserve
strategic public assets, while remaining faithful to the law’s pro‑market and
pro‑consumer objectives.

First, the amendment to Section 43(f) expressly empowers the ERC, with
respect to the generation component recoverable from captive markets, to
review, regulate, benchmark, confirm, adjust, or disallow generation rates and
corresponding recoverable generation costs, including those reflected in
automatic adjustment mechanisms and other pass‑through charges,
ensuring that only prudent and reasonable costs are charged to end‑users.

Second, the amendment to Section 45 tightens safeguards against
cross‑ownership and market power abuse by lowering the allowable share of
installed generating capacity per grid and clarifying the limits on bilateral
supply that distribution utilities may source from affiliated generation firms,
calibrated by demand level.

Third, the amendment to Section 47(f) permanently excludes the Agus and
Pulangui Hydroelectric Power Complexes from privatization and any form of
transfer to private entities, vesting their ownership, operation, and control in
the National Power Corporation.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
