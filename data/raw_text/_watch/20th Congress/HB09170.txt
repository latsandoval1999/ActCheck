                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article XIII, Section 11, and Article XV, Section 4 of the 1987 Constitution
declare the State’s duty to care for its elderly citizens by providing for and prioritizing
their needs in health and social security programs.

        Republic Act No. 10868, otherwise known as the “Centenarians Act of 2016,”
was enacted on June 23, 2016, to provide all Filipinos who reach the age of 100 years
old a letter of felicitation from the President and a gift in the amount of P100,000.00 in
honor of the centenarian’s longevity.

        On February 26, 2024, the “Centenarians Act of 2016” was amended by
Republic Act No. 11982 to grant benefits, in the form of cash gifts of P10,000.00, to all
Filipinos upon reaching the ages of 80, 85, 90, and 95. The amendatory bill was
proposed and enacted in consideration of the reality that most Filipinos do not reach
the age of 100 years old, and that the number of Filipinos who reach the ages of 80,
85, 90, and 95 decreases as their ages progress.

       According to a report by the Philippine Institute of Development Studies (PIDS)
published in February 2021,1 Filipinos have a life expectancy of 71 years, while recent
World Bank data puts the average Filipino life expectancy at 70 years, as of 2024.2

However, according to the World Health Organization (WHO), the average Filipino life
expectancy as of 2021 is 66.4 years old in total, with 69.9 years old for females and
63.4 for males.3

       This means that while Republic Act Nos. 10868 and 11982 rightfully seek to
honor the Filipino elderly who reach the milestone ages of 80, 85, 90, 95, and 100 by
providing them with cash benefits, most Filipinos do not live long enough to receive
these benefits. Even when they do, they are often no longer able to enjoy the benefits
themselves.

       As such, this bill seeks to further expand the coverage of the aforementioned
laws honoring octogenarians, nonagenarians, and centenarians by including
septuagenarians, or Filipinos aged 70 and 75, as recipients of cash benefits. This bill
also increases the amount of benefits received by Filipinos upon reaching the ages of
80 and 90, creating a gradual progression in the amount starting from age 70 to age
100.

       By doing so, this bill seeks not only to honor the Filipino elderly by providing
cash incentives for reaching milestone years, but also to provide them with the
financial support they increasingly need as their capacity for work decreases and
health complications increase with age.

      In the 20th Congress, similar bills were filed by Representatives Loreto Amante
and JC Abalos. This bill proposes a unique set of benefits for Filipino Septuagenarians,
Octogenarians, and Nonagenarians.

       Early approval of this measure is earnestly sought.
