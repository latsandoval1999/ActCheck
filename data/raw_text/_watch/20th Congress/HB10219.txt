                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

     “He who has pity on the poor lends to the Lord, and He will pay back what
     he has given.” — Proverbs 19:17

      The Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) has become the country's
flagship social protection program, providing conditional cash assistance to millions of
poor Filipino households and contributing significantly to improvements in education,
health, and poverty alleviation. Through Republic Act No. 11310, the State
institutionalized 4Ps as a long-term commitment to break the intergenerational cycle
of poverty and promote human capital development among the nation's most
vulnerable families.

       Despite its successes, the Philippine social protection system remains highly
fragmented. Various forms of government assistance—including emergency aid,
livelihood support, food subsidies, and crisis interventions—continue to operate
through separate programs administered under differing guidelines and delivery
mechanisms. While these interventions address legitimate needs, the absence of an
integrated framework often results in duplication, administrative inefficiencies,
inconsistent beneficiary targeting, and delays in the delivery of assistance. Moreover,
economic shocks, inflation, public health emergencies, natural disasters, and other
crises have demonstrated that vulnerability extends beyond traditional poverty
classifications, affecting millions of Filipinos who require immediate and responsive
government support.

      This measure seeks to expand the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program by
strengthening its social protection mandate and establishing Pantawid Pag-asa as an
integrated and rationalized support program for individuals and families experiencing
economic distress and crisis situations. The bill consolidates existing social
amelioration programs of similar nature under a unified framework to promote more
efficient service delivery, improve beneficiary targeting, and ensure timely assistance
to those in need. It likewise institutionalizes a centralized social protection database,
streamlines documentary requirements through digitalization and integration with the


                                           1
Philippine Identification System, and         strengthens   transparency,    grievance
mechanisms, and monitoring systems.

      The proposed amendments further provide for the periodic adjustment of cash
grants based on inflation and poverty thresholds, prioritize food-poor households in
program implementation, and strengthen the institutional capacity of the Department
of Social Welfare and Development through dedicated crisis intervention offices
nationwide. The measure also seeks to depoliticize the delivery of government
assistance by expressly prohibiting political endorsement, manipulation, or
exploitation of social amelioration programs, thereby ensuring that public funds are
distributed solely on the basis of genuine need and not political patronage.

     By expanding the scope of the 4Ps and integrating social protection interventions
under a more coherent, transparent, and responsive framework, this bill aims to build
a more resilient and inclusive social protection system capable of addressing both
chronic poverty and emerging socioeconomic vulnerabilities. It affirms the State's
commitment to ensuring that no Filipino is left behind in times of hardship while
promoting long-term pathways toward economic security and self-sufficiency.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 7         SEC. 21. Appropriations. - The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of
 8   this Act shall be charged against those authorized in the current and subsequent
 9   General Appropriations Act.

11         SEC. 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The rules and regulations
12   necessary for the effective and efficient enforcement of the provisions of this Act shall
13   be formulated by the DSWD, in consultation with relevant government agencies, LUs,
14   the private sector, and other stakeholders, and shall be implemented within ninety (90)
15   days from the effectivity of this Act.
16         Unjustified or willful failure or refusal of the concerned government agencies to
17   promulgate the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) within the specified period
18   shall subject the heads of these government agencies to administrative penalties
19   under applicable civil service laws.
20         Should the IRR contain provisions that are contrary to this Act, the heads of the
21   government agencies responsible for such provision, when done in bad faith or with
22   gross negligence, shall be held administratively liable.
