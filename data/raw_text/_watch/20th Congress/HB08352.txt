                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Under the Stockholm Declaration of 1972, it was announced that “safeguarding
the homo sapiens” is the primary objective of environmental laws without much
“emphasis on the protection of other components of the environment.” Protecting
nature for the benefit of mankind (anthropocentric) was the original motivating force
behind environmental laws.1

        In recent times, there has been an emerging shift in paradigm where several
countries have now considered that the protection of nature is actually for the benefit
of nature itself (eco-centric). Ecuador recognizes the rights of nature in its constitution
(2008). New Zealand grants legal personhood to the Whanganui River and Te
Urewera forest. Bolivia’s Law of Mother Earth (2012) also grants legal rights. In these
jurisdictions, nature can be and has been granted legal personality, a concept where
ecosystems like rivers, forests, and mountains are recognized as legal subjects with
rights rather than treated as mere property.2

       These “rights of nature” typically include the right of a natural ecosystem, or a
part thereof, to exist, persist, maintain, and regenerate its vital cycles. Since
ecosystems cannot speak, legal frameworks appoint human guardians (often
indigenous groups or government agencies) to represent the entity's interests. This

shift in legal approach transforms environmental law from a human-centered mindset
to one that protects ecosystems intrinsically, allowing them to be represented in court
to stop further damage, pollution and destruction. This novel framework centered on
recognizing nature as a distinct entity with legal personality can enable proactive
protection against environmental degradation, while mitigating the effects of climate
change.3

       Article II, Section 16 of the 1987 Constitution provides that, “[t]he State shall
protect and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology in
accord with the rhythm and harmony of nature.” This Rights of Nature bill seeks to
address the shortcomings in the country’s current response to environmental
degradation and climate crisis by redefining the problem and reframing the response.

         The bill seeks to provide a legal personality to natural ecosystems and
processes, recognizing their inherent and inalienable rights to exist, flourish, and
regenerate. Through this bill, any person shall have the right to defend, protect, and
enforce the rights bestowed upon natural ecosystems and processes recognized by
this bill.

       This bill aims to reorient the framework within which human beings interact with
nature. Instead of the previously utilitarian perspective of seeing nature as property to
be used, exploited, and preserved for the benefit of humans, this bill seeks to introduce
a new worldview that recognizes the inherent rights of nature, thus affecting the
subsequent laws and interactions of humans with nature.

        In the 20th Congress, similar bills were filed by Senator Risa Hontiveros and
Representatives Arnan Panaligan, Ferdinand Martin Romualdez, Yedda Romualdez,
Andrew Romualdez, Jude Acidre, Percival Cendaña, Jose Manuel Diokno, Dadah
Ismula, and Kaka Bag-ao. This bill adopts several provisions from the aforementioned
bills. This version was developed in consultation with the Rights of Nature network in
the Philippines.

      For a genuinely eco-centric Philippines, early approval of this measure is
earnestly sought.

 1           SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Rights of Nature
 2   Act.”
 3
 4           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   recognize and protect natural ecosystems and processes, or distinct and identifiable
 6   portions thereof, as legal entities possessing certain fundamental and inalienable
 7   rights. The State shall provide simple, effective, and expeditious mechanisms for the
 8   protection and enforcement of these rights. Such rights shall be founded on the
 9   following:
10
11            a. Interconnectedness. All creations are bound by a common living source,
12               and all activities in the ecosystem—land, air, water, and sea live and
13               function according to their respective roles to create and sustain the web
14               life‐–-shall at all times be respected.
15            b. Harmony. All human activities, within the framework of plurality and
16               diversity, should achieve a dynamic balance with the cycles and processes
17               inherent in Nature.
18            c. Collective good. The interests of society shall be fulfilled consistent with
19               the rights of Nature and shall involve all human activities.
20            d. Guarantee of the regeneration of Nature. The State, at its various levels,
21               and society, in harmony with the common interest, must ensure the
22               necessary conditions in order that Nature may regenerate, recognizing that
23               ecosystems are limited in their ability to regenerate, and that humans are
24               limited in their ability to undo their actions.

                                                1
 1          e. Multiculturalism. The exercise of the rights of Nature requires the
 2             recognition, recovery, respect, protection, and dialogue of the diversity of
 3             feelings, values, knowledge, skills, practices, skills, transcendence,
 4             transformation, science, technology and standards, of all the cultures of
 5             the world who seek to live in harmony with nature.
 6          f. Co-existence of Human Rights and Rights of Nature. All rights guaranteed
 7             under the Constitution and international human rights treaties and laws,
 8             shall be exercised in harmony and consistent with the rights of Nature.
 9          g. Promotion of ecologically-sound innovation. The promotion of ecologically-
10             sound innovation shall be in line with the rights of Nature and shall be
11             actively pursued by the State.
12          h. Indispensable role of the IPs/ICCs. The State recognizes the indispensable
13             role of the indigenous peoples/indigenous cultural communities in the
14             protection and promotion of the rights of Nature.
15
16         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms are
17   defined as follows:
18
19            a. Nature refers to the physical world, which encompasses all the biotic and
20               abiotic components and their interactions. It also refers to the meanings
21               in which different groups attribute to the physical environment to make
22               sense of the world.
23
24            b. Natural Ecosystems refer to the complex and dynamic communities of
25               plants, animals, microorganisms, species, and other beings and their
26               environment, both living and non-living, where human communities and
27               the rest of nature interact as a functional unit under the influence of
28               climatic, physiographic, and geological factors, as well as production
29               practices, Filipino cultural diversity, and the worldviews of nations and
30               indigenous peoples/indigenous cultural communities.
31
32            c. Ecological renewal and restoration refer to the process that will enable
33               the recovery of an ecosystem previously damaged by natural or
34               anthropogenic causes.
35
36            d. Ecological debt refers to what a country incurs when its ecological
37               footprint exceeds its biocapacity. This means that the countries make
38               use of resources of other countries to fulfill their consumption needs.
39
40            e. Indigenous Peoples/Indigenous Cultural Communities refer to the
41               definition provided in Republic Act No. 8371, otherwise known as the
42               “The Indigenous Peoples Rights Act of 1997."

 1          SEC. 4. The Fundamental Rights of Nature. – Natural ecosystems shall have
 2   the right to exist; to the maintenance of the vital cycles, functions, and processes that
 3   ensure their continued sustainability and well-being; to the conditions necessary for
 4   their ecological renewal and restoration; and to adequate and effective representation
 5   vis-à-vis the protection and enforcement of these rights. These rights shall be in
 6   addition to, and shall not impair or limit, any other right or remedy available under
 7   existing laws, administrative regulations, or jurisprudence. A violation of any existing
 8   environmental law or regulation shall be deemed a prima facie violation of these rights.
 9
10          The State likewise recognizes that there are limits on the ability of natural
11   ecosystems and processes to regenerate as well as on the ability of humanity to undo
12   and reverse any damage it has caused to the environment. Thus, all development that
13   affects or modifies natural ecosystems and processes must guarantee and uphold the
14   rights secured in this Act, be sustainable, ensure the maintenance of critical
15   ecosystem services, and respect intra- and intergenerational equity.
16
17          SEC. 5. Legal Personality of Natural Ecosystems and Processes. – The legal
18   personality of natural ecosystems and processes, including all of their living and non-
19   living elements, as well as any distinct and identifiable portions, aggregations, or
20   components thereof, shall be recognized under the law. In any action for the protection
21   or enforcement of the rights recognized herein, the natural ecosystem or process
22   involved shall be considered the real party-in-interest. Only natural ecosystems as
23   defined in this Act, including those under Section 13, shall be deemed to have legal
24   personality and entitled to protection under this Act. Likewise, the rights of nature
25   secured in this Act shall not be interpreted to confer liabilities, duties, obligations, or
26   responsibilities on nature.
27
28          SEC. 6. Obligations of the Government. – The Government shall take all
29   necessary actions to fully protect and enforce the rights recognized by this Act,
30   including, but not limited to, the following:
31
32             a. Develop and implement policies and regulations to guarantee and
33                uphold the rights recognized in this Act, including, but not limited to,
34                decision-making criteria for all government actions and authorized
35                activities that will guarantee and uphold these rights, including
36                prevention of harm to natural ecosystems and processes before harm
37                happens, early warning systems to ensure any potential or ongoing harm
38                is stopped, robust enforcement of these rights in courts of appropriate
39                jurisdiction, protection and restoration of natural ecosystems and
40                processes, and the exercise of precautionary measures to prevent
41                human activity from causing the extinction of living populations, the
42                alteration of the cycles and processes that ensure life, and the
43                destruction of livelihoods and cultural systems that are intertwined with
44                natural ecosystems and processes;

                                                 3
 1             b. Promote balanced forms of production and patterns of consumption that,
 2                while satisfying the needs of the Filipino people, safeguard the
 3                regenerative capacity and integrity of natural ecosystems and
 4                processes;
 5             c. Protect natural ecosystems and processes from the exploitation of their
 6                components, the commodification of living systems or the processes that
 7                support them, and the structural causes and effects of global climate
 8                change;
 9             d. Ensure long-term energy sovereignty, increased efficiency, and the
10                gradual incorporation of clean and renewable alternative sources into
11                the energy matrix;
12             e. Demand international recognition of ecological debt through the
13                financing and transfer of clean technology compatible with the rights
14                recognized in this Act;
15             f. Promote peace and the elimination of all weapons of mass destruction;
16                and
17             g. Promote the recognition of the rights of natural ecosystems and
18                processes in multilateral, regional, and bilateral international relations.

20          SEC. 7. Obligations of Juridical Persons. – All juridical persons shall take all
21   necessary actions to protect and enforce the rights recognized by this law including,
22   but not limited to, the following:
23
24             a. Embed the Right of Nature policy in their by-laws and organizational
25                processes upon submission of their application for registration to the
26                Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC);
27             b. Undertake regular reporting to the Department of Environment and
28                Natural Resources (DENR) of their impacts on specific ecosystems
29                where they are conducting their business operations;
30             c. Conduct environmental and human rights assessment prior to
31                conducting their operations or whenever there is significant change in
32                their operations such as merger or closure of business, which
33                demonstrate that their activities are not violating the rights recognized in
34                this Act or harming nature: Provided, That such assessment –
35                demonstrating that the Rights of Nature are not being violated – shall be
36                a prerequisite for the issuance of the Environmental Compliance
37                Certificate (ECC) by the DENR.

39          SEC. 8. Representation and Standing. – Any Philippine resident may file an
40   action to enforce the rights or obligations recognized under this Act. Upon the filing of
41   any such action, the Court shall issue an order which shall contain a brief description
42   of the cause of action and the reliefs prayed for, requiring all interested parties to
43   manifest their interest to intervene in the case within thirty (30) days from notice
44   thereof.

 1           SEC. 9. Reliefs. – If warranted, the court shall issue a judgment in favor of the
 2   natural process or ecosystem on whose behalf an action for the protection and
 3   enforcement of the rights recognized herein was filed, which shall include measures
 4   for its protection, preservation, ecological renewal and restoration, as well as the
 5   payment of attorney’s fees, costs of suit, and other litigation expenses. Monetary
 6   damages to be awarded shall be the amount necessary to fully rectify or repair the
 7   damage done to natural ecosystems and processes by the defendant.
 8
 9          SEC. 10. Trust Fund. – Any monetary award that any court may award in
10   defense of the Rights of Nature shall not accrue to the benefit of the representative
11   but it will be deposited to a Trust Fund: Provided, however, That any expenses
12   incurred by the representative shall constitute a lien to the monetary award.
13
14          All monetary awards shall be deposited in the Trust Fund under the
15   administration of the Conservation Committee provided for in this Act, which shall be
16   used to implement the measures for protection, preservation, ecological restoration,
17   or renewal ordered by the court.
18
19          SEC. 11. Appointment of Conservation Committee. – To administer the Trust
20   Fund and implement the measures necessary for protection, preservation, or
21   ecological renewal and restoration of the natural ecosystem or process on whose
22   behalf an action pursuant to this Act was filed, the court shall appoint a conservation
23   committee from the stakeholders concerned. The court shall give preference to
24   indigenous cultural communities from the same area, people’s organization, non-
25   governmental organization, or any public interest group accredited by or registered
26   with any government agency. Under no circumstances shall a person or entity
27   responsible for a violation of the rights recognized herein be appointed to a
28   conservation committee.
29
30         SEC. 12. Designation of Courts and Promulgation of Procedural Rules. – The
31   Supreme Court shall designate the court or courts that will hear and resolve cases
32   brought under this Act and shall promulgate the rules of pleading, practice, and
33   procedure to govern the proceedings brought under this Act.
34
35           SEC. 13. Strategic Lawsuits against Public Participation. – A Strategic Lawsuit
36   against Public Participation (SLAPP) is any action filed to harass, vex, or exert undue
37   pressure against any person or legal entity that has taken legal action or made a public
38   communication of any kind in connection with the enforcement or protection of the
39   rights recognized herein or any other environmental law or regulation.
40
41          A person against whom a legal action is filed may assert that the action
42   constitutes SLAPP as an affirmative defense in the same case.

 1         Upon such assertion, the court shall conduct a special hearing on the SLAPP
 2   as an affirmative defense at the earliest possible stage of the proceedings. The
 3   hearing shall be summary in nature, and the court shall resolve the SLAPP affirmative
 4   defense within thirty (30) days from the conclusion of the summary hearing.
 5
 6         If the court upholds the SLAPP as an affirmative defense, the action shall be
 7   dismissed with prejudice. The court may likewise award attorney’s fees, costs of suit,
 8   and damages under a counterclaim, if one has been filed.
 9
10         If the court rejects the SLAPP as an affirmative defense, the evidence
11   presented during the summary hearing shall form part of the evidence on the merits,
12   and the case shall proceed in accordance with the Rules of Court.

14          SEC. 14. Territories of Life. – All ancestral domains of the IPPs/ICCs shall be
15   deemed as Territories of Life, as it is grounded on nature’s intrinsic value and their
16   customary belief in the sanctity of the land and oneness with nature. As such, all
17   ancestral domains shall be deemed to have rights as provided under Sections 4 and
18   5 hereof. Likewise, all protected areas declared under Republic Act No. 11038,
19   otherwise known as the “Expanded National Integrated Protected Areas System Act
20   of 2018" or “E-NIPAS,” including those declared by the local government units (LGUs)
21   as protected areas, are deemed Territories of Life subject to the protection under this
22   Act. National development planning under the National Economic and Development
23   Authority (NEDA) and local development planning of the LGUs shall integrate the
24   appropriate development framework consistent with the Rights of Nature. The
25   Protected Area Management Board shall ensure that the management plans in all
26   protected areas are aligned with the objectives of the Act.
27
28          SEC. 15. Role of the Indigenous Political Structure (IPS). – All IPS that are
29   present, as mandated by existing law, shall have the following duties and functions in
30   relation to the protection of the rights of nature and implementation of this Act:
31
32             a. Represent the interest of ecosystems and processes within the
33                respective jurisdiction of the LGUs where their ancestral domain is
34                located, including participation in the local development planning;
35             b. Ensure that IPs are consulted and give Free, Prior, and Informed
36                Consent (FPIC) for any development that may affect the ecosystem in
37                the Territories of Life;
38             c. Promote indigenous ecological values and practices by coordinating with
39                the Department of Education to incorporate them into the education
40                system.

 4           SEC. 17. Protection of Individuals and Communities that Depend on the
 5   Environment for their Livelihood. – Nothing in this Act shall be interpreted to diminish
 6   or limit the rights of those whose livelihoods are dependent on the benefits derived
 7   from the environment, such as IPs, fisherfolk, farmers, and the like, except to the
 8   extent that their acts violate the Rights of Nature provided in this Act. They shall
 9   likewise be deemed stewards of nature in areas where they habitually reside, taking
10   into account their local knowledge and practices in enhancing the health and
11   protection of nature. Any development projects, whether public or private, shall
12   undergo the consultation required under Sections 26 and 27 of Republic Act No. 7160,
13   otherwise known as the “Local Government Code of 1991.” Individuals and
14   communities referred to in this Section shall be indispensable parties in any
15   consultation or conduct of any study in relation to the proposed development project.
16
17           SEC. 18. Review of the Environmental Impact Assessment Study System
18   (EIASS). – The DENR, in consultation with the public and private sector, including the
19   Indigenous Peoples’ Mandatory Representatives (IPMRs) and local communities
20   referred under Section 19, shall conduct a review of the EIASS to align this System
21   with the rights under the provisions of this Act, to ensure that no public or private
22   projects or activities are approved or authorized which would violate the rights secured
23   in this Act.
24

 4           SEC. 22. Penalties. – Any person who violates any of the provisions of this Act
 5   or its Implementing Rules and Regulations shall, upon conviction by final judgment,
 6   be punished by imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but more than two (2)
 7   years or a fine of not less than Five Million pesos (PhP5,000,000) but more than Ten
 8   Million pesos (PhP10,000,000), or both, at the discretion of the court. If the violation is
 9   committed by a juridical person, the officer responsible therefor shall serve the
10   imprisonment when imposed. If violation is committed by an alien, he or she shall be
11   immediately deported after service of sentence, without need for further proceedings.
