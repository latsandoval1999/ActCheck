EXPLANATORY NOTE

Para sa maralitang pasyenteng nangangailangan ng regular na dialysis sa malayong
bayan, ang ugnayang ito ay hindi lamang paksa sa isang mesa ng talakayan. Ito ay naririnig
sa pag-andar ng sasakyan patungo sa pinakamalapit na renal facility, nararamdaman sa
pagkapagod ng katawan bago pa man magsimula ang paggamot, at nakikita sa kamay ng
kasama niyang nagbibilang kung may pamasahe pa para sa susunod na biyahe. Kapag ang
paggamot ay naroon ngunit ang pasyente ay walang ligtas, maaasahan, at kayang bayarang
paraan upang makarating dito, ang pagitan ng tahanan at klinika ay nagiging bahagi mismo

ng karamdaman.

The critical fact is that access to dialysis is not exhausted by the availability of a
dialysis session. Chronic kidney disease requires continuing care, and a patient who must
repeatedly travel long distances confronts a distinct access barrier before entering the
treatment room. Fatigue, mobility limitations, the need for assistance boarding and leaving a
vehicle, dependence on a family caregiver, and the physical strain that may follow treatment
can turn an apparently ordinary trip into a missed or delayed session. A transport response is
therefore not a peripheral welfare measure. It is an adherence measure, a patient-safety
measure, and an instrument for making the public investment in renal care reachable in the

places where patients actually live.

The scale and trajectory of kidney disease make that barrier impossible to dismiss.

facility and get home safely? This bill supplies that missing link without duplicating
PhilHealth's coverage of the treatment itself.

The objection that transport assistance alone cannot cure the wider financial and
clinical burdens of kidney disease is correct, and it should inform rather than defeat the
measure. According to the AJKD Blog of the American Journal of Kidney Diseases, support
for kidney replacement therapy in the Philippines has been described as partial and dependent
on funding availability, while medication expenditures are largely out-of-pocket. Transport
aid cannot by itself meet needs for medicines, laboratory examinations, vascular access,
nutrition, transplantation, or other forms of renal replacement therapy. Neither should
Congress assume that a different treatment modality will automatically remove access
barriers; the same blog reports that a peritoneal dialysis-first policy was implemented in the
Philippines but was not successful, with the factors not fully explored. This bill makes no
such overclaim. It addresses one defined, recurring, and preventable interruption in care: the
inability of indigent patients to reach scheduled dialysis because the journey itself is

unaffordable, inaccessible, or unsafe.

Time is the urgent element. Dialysis schedules recur week after week, and the cost of
inaction accumulates trip by trip. A missed journey can become a missed session; a delayed
session can deepen the burden on the patient, caregiver, facility, and public health system.
The program proposed by this measure therefore directs coordinated transport assistance
through vouchers, shared or contracted rides, local government participation, caregiver
accompaniment where needed, and mobility-responsive arrangements linked to renal
facilities. It also requires cost discipline. Data from a 2019 National Academies of Sciences,
Engineering and Medicine report highlighted by Salud America!, shows that the Phoenix
Dialysis Transportation Program offered voucher subsidies for 75% of the cost of a taxi trip

The governing principle of this bill is simple: no indigent Filipino should miss life-
sustaining dialysis because the road to the clinic costs more than the family can bear. Kapit-
Buhay establishes a focused complement to existing health financing, rather than a substitute
for it. By matching eligible patients with mobility-responsive and cost-efficient transport
support, coordinating with local governments and dialysis facilities, recognizing caregiver
needs, and protecting against duplication of benefits, it converts nominal treatment
availability into real treatment access. It respects the work already undertaken by PhilHealth,
the Department of Health, health professionals, local governments, and renal facilities, while
giving each of them a clearer means to address the journey that existing coverage does not

necessarily pay for.

Kapag may dialysis na naghihintay, may masasakyan ding makapagdadala sa
pasyente roon, dahil ang buhay ay hindi dapat huminto sa pagitan ng bahay at ospital.

In view of the foregoing, immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING A KIDNEY CARE TRANSPORT ASSISTANCE
PROGRAM FOR INDIGENT PATIENTS REQUIRING REGULAR DIALYSIS,
PROVIDING FOR MOBILITY-RESPONSIVE AND COST-EFFICIENT
TRANSPORT SERVICES, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress

assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. —- This Act shall be known as the "Kapit-Buhay Act".

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — Pursuant to Article II, Section 15 and Article
XIII, Section 11 of the Constitution, it is the policy of the State to help ensure that an indigent
patient who requires regular dialysis can reach a dialysis facility safely, reliably, and with
dignity. Access to a covered treatment session is incomplete when transport cost, distance,
fatigue, mobility limitations, or the need for a companion prevents the patient from receiving

that treatment.

The State shall establish a targeted, fiscally responsible transport-assistance program
that complements existing health-insurance benefits, public-health programs, local
assistance, and support provided by dialysis facilities. The Program shall not reduce, replace,

or condition any benefit otherwise available to a patient.

In allocating assistance, the State shall prioritize patients residing in geographically
isolated and disadvantaged areas, patients with demonstrated mobility or caregiver-
accompaniment needs, and patients whose household resources and travel distance create a
substantial risk of missed treatment. Such priority shall not be used to deny assistance to any

otherwise eligible patient.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

(a) "Assistance" refers to transport support authorized under this Act, including
transport vouchers, reimbursement, shared rides, contracted transport, fuel support

where appropriate, and mobility assistance;

(b) "Caregiver" refers to a family member, guardian, or other person identified by the
patient or responsible adult as necessary to accompany and assist the patient during

travel to or from dialysis;

(c) "Department" refers to the Department of Health;

(d) "Dialysis facility" refers to a health facility lawfully providing dialysis services

and able to verify a patient's scheduled or completed dialysis treatment;

(e) "Geographically isolated and disadvantaged area" refers to an area identified as
such by the Department or another competent government authority for health-service

planning;

(f) "Indigent patient" refers to a patient assessed as indigent under applicable

government social-welfare or health-service eligibility standards;

(g) "Mobility-responsive transport" refers to transport arrangements that take account
of a patient's observable need for assistance in entering, riding in, or leaving a vehicle,
including fatigue following dialysis, use of a mobility device, or need for caregiver

accompaniment;

SECTION 4. Establishment of the Kidney Care Transport Assistance Program. —-
The Department shall establish and administer the Kidney Care Transport Assistance
Program to provide assistance for the travel of eligible indigent patients to and from a dialysis

facility for regular dialysis.

The Program shall be implemented initially in geographically isolated and
disadvantaged areas and in localities where patients have substantial travel barriers to dialysis
facilities. Within each participating locality, priority shall be given to patients with the
greatest verified transport barrier, including distance, lack of safe available transport, mobility

limitation, need for a caregiver, and risk of missed treatment due to transport constraints.

No patient shall be denied assistance solely because the patient receives health-
insurance coverage for dialysis treatment. Assistance under this Act shall cover transport-
related needs only and shall not be charged against, substituted for, or treated as a reduction

of a dialysis-treatment benefit.

SECTION 5. Forms and Scope of Assistance. — Subject to the availability of funds

and the patient's assessed need, the Program may provide one or more of the following:

(a) Transport vouchers or direct payment to an accredited or contracted transport

provider;

(b) Shared or scheduled rides for patients traveling along compatible routes and

treatment schedules;

(c) Contracted transport services, including vehicles capable of reasonably

accommodating patients with mobility needs;

(d) Reimbursement of reasonable and documented transport expenses when a voucher

(e) Support for one caregiver's transport when the patient's condition or mobility

(f) Other cost-efficient transport arrangements determined by the Department to be

The Department shall give preference to arrangements that safely group compatible

SECTION 6. Eligibility, Enrollment, and Verification. — An indigent patient may

enroll in the Program upon submission of:

(a) Proof of identity or an alternative form of identification accepted by the

Department;

(b) Proof of indigency or a referral from a government social-welfare office,

government health facility, or other authorized public office;

(c) A certification or treatment schedule from a dialysis facility or attending health

professional showing the need for regular dialysis; and

(d) Information reasonably necessary to assess the patient's route, transport barrier,

mobility needs, and need for caregiver accompaniment.

The Department shall establish a simple, accessible, and non-discriminatory
enrollment process. A patient may be assisted by a caregiver, social worker, barangay health

worker, dialysis-facility personnel, or authorized representative in submitting an application.

A dialysis facility's certification shall be limited to treatment scheduling and clinically

SECTION 7. Coordination with Local Governments, Dialysis Facilities, and Existing
Programs. _ The Department shall coordinate the Program with participating local
government units, dialysis facilities, public transport providers, patient-support organizations,
and other relevant entities to identify patient routes, schedule assistance, and avoid
duplication of available support. Such support includes medical and financial assistance
available through Malasakit Centers under Republic Act No. 11463, otherwise known as the
“Malasakit Centers Act,” and transportation assistance under the Assistance to Individuals in
Crisis Situations program of the Department of Social Welfare and Development, or other

similar government programs.

Participation by a local government unit in transport operations, patient referral, or
counterpart support shall be voluntary and governed by a written agreement identifying the
respective functions, funding source, data-protection obligations, and reporting requirements.
No local government unit shall be required to provide transport, personnel, facilities, or
counterpart funds under this Act unless the necessary national funding or a voluntary written

agreement is in place.

The Department may recognize and continue compatible transport-assistance
practices already provided through lawful local or facility programs, provided that such
practices meet the minimum safety, non-discrimination, accountability, and patient-dignity

standards under this Act.

SECTION 8. Patient Safety, Dignity, and Accountability. — Transport providers
participating in the Program shall maintain safe vehicles, observe reasonable pickup and drop-

off procedures, protect patient information, and treat patients and caregivers with respect.

The Department shall establish a patient complaint and correction process that permits
reporting by the patient, caregiver, dialysis facility, or local government unit. A provider shall

not be penalized for a missed or delayed trip caused by circumstances beyond the provider's

another person, sudden patient medical instability, or a dialysis-facility schedule change

Program performance shall be assessed among comparable service areas and routes,

SECTION 9. Data Protection and Reporting. — The Department shall collect only
information reasonably necessary to administer the Program, verify eligibility, arrange
transport, prevent duplicate claims, evaluate performance, and conduct audit. Personally
identifiable health and travel information shall be protected in accordance with Republic Act
No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and other applicable laws
and shall not be disclosed except as authorized by law or necessary for the patient's care and

transport arrangement.

The Department shall submit an annual report to Congress on Program
implementation. The report shall include the number of patients assisted, the types of
assistance used, coverage in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, transport-
related service outcomes, expenditure, complaints and their resolution, and recommendations

for improvement. The report shall not identify individual patients.

SECTION 10. Relationship to Other Laws and Programs. — This Act completes
existing national health-insurance coverage, renal-health initiatives, lawful local assistance
programs, and Department issuances concerning kidney care by addressing the separate
barrier of travel to regular dialysis. Nothing in this Act shall diminish or impair any patient
entitlement, health-insurance benefit, public-health service, local assistance, or lawful

issuance otherwise available.

Existing Department issuances and operational standards concerning renal care shall

continue to have effect to the extent that they are consistent with this Act.

SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department, in consultation with relevant government agencies,
local government units, dialysis facilities, patient representatives, transport providers, and
organizations with expertise in renal care, shall promulgate the rules and regulations

necessary for its effective implementation.

The rules shall provide for patient assessment, enrollment, prioritization, provider
accreditation or contracting, rate-setting, shared-ride safeguards, caregiver accompaniment,
reimbursement, complaints, audit, data protection, and phased implementation according to

available appropriations.

The absence or delay of the implementing rules and regulations shall not suspend

rights and duties that are sufficiently defined by this Act.

SECTION 12. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
Department, subject to applicable budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules. Thereafter, such
sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in

the annual General Appropriations Act.

Program assistance shall be phased according to available appropriations, with
priority for geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas and patients with the greatest
verified transport barriers. No obligation to provide assistance beyond available

appropriations shall arise under this Act.

SECTION 13. Separability Clause. ~- If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force

and effect.

SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or

modified accordingly.
