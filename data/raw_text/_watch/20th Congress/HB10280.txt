                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

The lifeline rate subsidy under the Electric Power Industry Reform Act was
designed to protect low-usage, low-income households from the full cost of
electricity, but its consumption threshold has remained largely unchanged for
years despite rising household electricity needs and inflation.

This bill raises the lifeline consumption threshold and increases the subsidy
discount, ensuring that more low-income households, not just the poorest of the
poor, are shielded from unaffordable electricity bills, particularly during the hot
summer months when consumption spikes.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. This Act shall be known as the "Expanded Lifeline Electricity
Rate Act."
      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State shall ensure that electricity,
a basic necessity, remains affordable and accessible to marginalized and low-
income households.
      SECTION 3. Expansion of Lifeline Rate Threshold. — The monthly
consumption threshold for lifeline rate eligibility is hereby increased to one
hundred (100) kilowatt-hours, from the previous lower threshold, subject to
periodic review by the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) every three (3) years
to account for inflation and consumption trends.
      SECTION 4. Subsidy Discount. — Qualified lifeline consumers shall enjoy
a discount of no less than fifty percent (50%) on the generation and distribution
charges of their monthly electricity bill, cross-subsidized in accordance with the
mechanism already established under Republic Act No. 9136.
      SECTION 5. Automatic Enrollment. — Distribution utilities shall
automatically enroll qualified households based on consumption data, without
requiring a separate application, subject to verification.

      SECTION 6. Monitoring. — The Energy Regulatory Commission shall
submit an annual report to Congress on the number of lifeline beneficiaries, the
subsidy amounts disbursed, and the impact on household electricity costs.
      SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The Energy
Regulatory Commission shall issue implementing rules within sixty (60) days
from effectivity.
      SECTION 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in force.
      SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, and
issuances inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
      SECTION 10. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
