                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Republic Act No. 10912, otherwise known as the Continuing Professional Development
(CPD) Act of 2016, was enacted to institutionalize lifelong learning among Filipino professionals
and ensure that they remain competent, globally competitive, and responsive to the ever-evolving
demands of their respective professions. The law recognizes that continuing professional education
is essential to safeguarding public welfare and maintaining the integrity of regulated professions.

       Nearly a decade after its enactment, however, experience has demonstrated that the
mandatory application of the law does not sufficiently account for the unique circumstances of
Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs), particularly those who continuously practice their professions
exclusively outside the Philippines.

        The Philippines continues to be one of the world's leading sources of highly skilled
professionals. A substantial number of Filipino professionals continue to build long-term careers
overseas. These Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) include licensed nurses, engineers, architects,
physicians, accountants, teachers, physical therapists, and other professionals who are already
subject to stringent licensing, accreditation, continuing education, competency assessment, and
professional development requirements imposed by foreign governments, professional regulatory
authorities, employers, and internationally recognized institutions. In many jurisdictions,
compliance with these requirements is mandatory to retain the legal authority to practice.

        Despite continuously maintaining their professional competence abroad, these Filipino
professionals are likewise required under Republic Act No. 10912 to earn Philippine Continuing
Professional Development (CPD) credit units solely for the renewal of their Professional
Identification Cards (PICs). For many OFWs, compliance entails considerable financial cost,
logistical difficulty, and administrative inconvenience. Accredited CPD providers are often
inaccessible from their countries of employment, while attending seminars in the Philippines
frequently requires taking leave from work, incurring travel expenses, or disrupting existing
contractual obligations.

        Consequently, professionals who are already complying with rigorous foreign continuing
education systems are effectively required to satisfy duplicative regulatory requirements that do not
necessarily enhance their competence beyond what has already been demonstrated in their
respective jurisdictions.
            This measure then seeks to rationalize the implementation of Republic Act No. 10912 by
    exempting qualified Overseas Filipino Workers from the mandatory CPD requirement for the
    renewal of their PICs. This bill does not diminish the value of Continuing Professional
    Development. Rather, it recognizes that qualified Overseas Filipino Workers are already engaged in
    continuous professional learning under internationally recognized regulatory systems. By
    eliminating unnecessary duplication while preserving safeguards for professionals who practice in
    the Philippines, the measure promotes a more responsive, practical, and equitable implementation
    of the CPD Act.

            It likewise serves as a meaningful recognition of the invaluable contributions of Overseas
    Filipino Workers, whose professional expertise not only enhances the global reputation of the
    Filipino workforce but also sustains millions of Filipino families through remittances and
    significantly contributes to the country's economic growth.

           In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
