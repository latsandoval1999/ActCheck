                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

Under Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution, the State is obligated to safeguard
and advance public health while fostering health awareness among citizens. Following this
mandate, Republic Act No. 11036, or the Mental Health Act, was enacted in June 2018 to
establish a national mental health framework, which includes round-the-clock emergency
hotlines for individuals in distress and provisions for integrating mental health care into the
broader public health system.

Furthermore, Section 16 of the same law specifically provides for the establishment and funding
of community-based mental health care facilities across provinces, cities, and clusters of
municipalities to bring appropriate services closer to the people:

       "Section 16. Community-based Mental Health Care Facilities. - The national government
       through the DOH shall fund the establishment and assist in the operation of community-
       based mental health care facilities in the provinces, cities and cluster of municipalities in
       the entire country based on the needs of the population, to provide appropriate mental
       health care services, and enhance the rights-based approach to mental health care."

Supplementing this policy, Section 2 of Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the
Universal Health Care (UHC) Act, requires the State to adopt a whole-of-system, whole-of-
government, and whole-of-society approach in the development, implementation, monitoring,
and evaluation of health policies, programs, and plans.

Despite these landmark legislations, millions of Filipinos continue to lack accessible, continuous,
and long-term mental health services. While pioneering local government initiatives have
demonstrated the high demand for localized care, these services often operate on restricted or
intermittent schedules. Communities in rural and underserved areas remain particularly
vulnerable due to severe shortage of permanent facilities and resident professionals. Such
situations highlight a systemic gap: the absence of permanent, full-time local mental health
infrastructure.
The urgency of this crisis is underscored by alarming national data:

   •   12.5 million Filipinos (approximately 11.3 to 11.6 percent of the population) are
       estimated to live with a mental health condition (Global Burden of Disease, reported
       2023).
   •   3,379 Filipinos died by suicide in 2024, an increase from 3,133 in 2023 (Philippine
       Statistics Authority, 2025).
   •   Fewer than 2 mental health workers per 100,000—translating to roughly 1,821
       professionals serving over 110 million people (WHO Mental Health Atlas, 2020).
   •   68.9 billion pesos was lost in 2019 due to lost productivity and mental illness costs,
       accounting for about 0.4 percent of GDP (The Lancet Psychiatry, 2022).
   •   83,655 calls were received by the National Center for Mental Health (NCMH) crisis
       hotline (1553) as of August 2024, with roughly 30 percent being suicide-related (Philippine
       Information Agency, 2024).

To alleviate the country’s mental health situation and bridge the critical implementation gaps,
this bill institutionalizes the establishment of a fully operational, public mental health facility in
every city and municipality across the country, alongside specialized Mental Health and
Wellness Units in all municipal, city, provincial, and district hospitals. By integrating mental health
care into the primary healthcare system and ensuring the permanent staffing of licensed
psychiatrists, psychologists, social welfare officers, and allied health professionals at the local
level, this measure shifts the national approach from reactive intervention to proactive,
community-based care.

Through this measure, the State will strengthen prevention, early detection, emergency
response, and long-term rehabilitation, bringing essential mental health services directly to every
Filipino family.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is strongly sought.

                                 AN ACT
MANDATING THE ESTABLISHMENT OF MENTAL HEALTH CENTERS IN EVERY CITY AND
MUNICIPALITY AND INSTITUTIONALIZING SPECIALIZED MENTAL HEALTH AND
WELLNESS UNITS IN ALL MUNICIPAL, CITY, PROVINCIAL, AND DISTRICT HOSPITALS,
PROVIDING FOR PROFESSIONAL STAFFING, POWERS, AND FUNCTIONS IN
COMPLIANCE WITH REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11036, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Establishment of Mental Health
Facilities in LGUs and in Local Hospitals Act”.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health of the people as protected in Article II, Section 15 of the 1987
Constitution, and to affirm the basic right of all Filipinos to mental health as mandated under
Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the “Philippine Mental Health Act”. The State
recognizes that mental health is an essential foundation of individual and collective well-being.
Towards this end, the State shall ensure accessible, affordable, rights-based, and
comprehensive mental health care services for all Filipinos through the establishment of public
mental health facilities in every city and municipality, alongside specialized Mental Health and
Wellness Units across all municipal, city, provincial, and district hospitals, directly integrated into
the primary health care system.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

       a) Mental Health – As defined under Republic Act No. 11036, refers to a state of well-
       being in which the individual realizes one's own abilities and potentials, copes adequately
       with the normal stresses of life, displays resilience in the face of extreme life events, works
       productively and fruitfully, and is able to make a positive contribution to the community.
       b) Mental Health Service – Refers to psychosocial, psychiatric, or neurologic activities
       and programs along the whole range of mental health support services, including
       promotion, prevention, treatment, and aftercare, provided by mental health facilities and
       licensed mental health professionals.

       c) Mental Health Center – Refers to the standalone public facility established under this
       Act in every city and municipality to provide accessible, community-based mental health
       care.

       d) Mental Health and Wellness Unit– Refers to a specialized medical section or facility
       within a municipal, city, provincial, or district hospital dedicated to providing care,
       inpatient/outpatient treatment, clinical stabilization, and support for individuals with mental
       health conditions, operating in coordination with local government units.

       e) Primary Care – As defined under Republic Act No. 11223, or the “Universal Health
       Care Act”, refers to initial-contact, accessible, continuous, comprehensive, and
       coordinated care accessible at the time of need.

       f) Primary Care Provider – Refers to a health care worker with defined competencies who
       has received certification in primary care as determined by the Department of Health or
       any health institution licensed and certified by the DOH.

SEC. 4. Establishment of Mental Health Facilities and Hospital Wellness Units. – There
shall be established in every city and municipality a public mental health center or facility, which
shall operate under the administrative supervision of the local government unit (LGU), in close
coordination and technical consultation with the Department of Health (DOH). Each facility shall
be fully equipped to deliver preventive, promotive, curative, and rehabilitative mental health
services in strict compliance with the facility standards and regulatory requirements of RA 11036.

For hospital-based Mental Health and Wellness Unit (MHWU), there shall be established a
dedicated "Mental Health and Wellness Unit" in all municipal, city, provincial, or district hospitals.
Each unit shall be fully equipped to deliver clinical psychiatric, psychological, and emergency
mental health services, acting in direct coordination with the LGU having jurisdiction over the
hospital.

SEC. 5. Objectives. – The mental health centers and MHWUs established under this Act shall
provide free, confidential, and accessible services to all constituents, including the following:

       a)     Counseling and Psychotherapy – Provision of individual, group, and family
       counseling sessions to address emotional, behavioral, and psychological concerns in the
       least restrictive environment;

       b)     Psychiatric Treatment and Medication Management – Diagnosis, treatment, and
       continuing care of mental health conditions, including the administration and monitoring
       of prescribed medications strictly conditioned upon informed consent;
       c)    Community-Based Mental Health Programs – Implementation of outreach
       programs designed to promote mental well-being, conduct case findings, and provide
       support at the barangay and community levels;

       d)     Crisis Intervention and Hotline Services – Immediate and accessible support for
       individuals in psychiatric or neurologic emergencies, including 24/7 hotlines and
       emergency clinical interventions; and

       e)    Mental Health Awareness and Education Campaigns – Conduct of seminars,
       workshops, and information drives to eliminate social stigma, raise awareness, and
       promote mental health literacy across all local communities.

SEC. 6. Staffing and Employment of Mental Health Professionals. – Each mental health
center or facility established under this Act shall be staffed with licensed mental health
professionals, which shall include, but not be limited to, the following:

       a) At least one (1) Psychiatrist, with a salary grade equivalent to Medical Officer III (Salary
       Grade 21);

       b) At least two (2) Clinical Psychologists, with a salary grade not lower than Salary Grade
       16;

       c) At least two (2) Social Welfare Officers trained in mental health and psychosocial
       support, with a salary grade not lower than Salary Grade 11; and

       d) Such number of mental health nurses, guidance counselors, support staff, and
       administrative personnel as may be necessary to ensure efficient service delivery.

The salary grades herein provided shall be in accordance with the latest Salary Standardization
Law (SSL) and as may be determined by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM).
The DOH, in coordination with the Civil Service Commission (CSC), shall determine and
prescribe the plantilla positions, professional qualifications, and salary grades for the positions
provided under this Section.

SEC. 7. Composition and Staffing of Mental Health and Wellness Units. – The MHWUs in
municipal, city, provincial, or district hospitals shall be composed of, but not limited to, the
following personnel:

       a) Municipal, City, or Provincial Health Officer of the LGU or their designated medical
       representative;

       b) Municipal, City, or Provincial Social Welfare and Development Officer of the LGU;

       c) At least one (1) Medical Doctor with specialized training in mental health or psychiatry;
       d) Psychiatric Nurses: At least two (2) psychiatric nurses for Provincial Mental Health and
       Wellness Units (PMHWU) and at least one (1) psychiatric nurse for Municipal, City, and
       District Mental Health and Wellness Units (M/C/DMHWU); and

       e) At least one (1) Licensed Psychologist, Counselor, or Therapist.

SEC. 8. Powers and Functions of Mental Health Centers and Wellness Units. – Every
established Mental Health Center and Hospital MHWU shall exercise the following powers and
functions:

       a) Integrated Program Implementation: Formulate and implement comprehensive, rights-
       based mental health programs encompassing promotion, prevention, curative treatment,
       and rehabilitation;

       b) Clinical Services and Medication Management: Provide counseling, psychotherapy,
       psychiatric diagnosis, and medication management strictly conditioned upon informed
       consent pursuant to RA 11036;

       c) Public Awareness: Conduct mental health education campaigns, seminars, workshops,
       and suicide prevention drives across communities, schools, and workplaces;

       d) Referral and Escalation System: Establish clear, functioning referral pathways to
       ensure seamless access to higher levels of care (such as tertiary psychiatric facilities)
       during emergency or serious clinical cases; and

       e) Data Monitoring: Develop a secure system for collecting, monitoring, and analyzing
       mental health case data to inform localized policy and public health interventions.

SEC. 9. Rights of Service Users and Professional Standards. – All mental health facilities
established under this Act shall strictly uphold and protect the rights of service users and mental
health professionals pursuant to Chapter II and Chapter III of RA 11036. This includes:

       a) Informed Consent: Free and prior informed consent in writing shall be obtained prior to
       the implementation of any treatment plan, therapy, or intervention, except during
       recognized psychiatric or neurologic emergencies under the safeguards of law;

       b) Humane Treatment: Absolute freedom from solitary confinement, cruel, inhumane, or
       degrading treatment, and non-evidence-based procedures; and

       c) Professional Welfare: Ensuring a safe and supportive work environment, continuous
       professional development opportunities, and adequate legal protection for all employed
       psychiatrists, psychologists, and social workers.

SEC. 10. Inter-Agency Collaboration and Duties. – For the effective implementation of this
Act, the DOH shall coordinate closely with the:
       a) Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and local government units
       to ensure local budget prioritization and operational integration;

       b) Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) to streamline community-
       based social welfare and protection mechanisms for individuals with mental health
       conditions; and

       c) Commission on Human Rights (CHR) to facilitate regular monitoring, inspection, and
       human rights compliance audits of all local mental health facilities.

SEC. 11. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall
be charged against the current appropriations of the DOH. Thereafter, the funds required for the
continued operation and maintenance of mental health facilities established under this Act shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

In addition, local government units are mandated to allocate a portion of their annual national
tax allotment and local health budgets specifically to support the operation, capital outlay, and
sustainability of these facilities.

SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with the DILG, CSC, DSWD, CHR, and other concerned
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
implementation.

SEC. 13. Non-Impairment Clause. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed as to diminish,
impair, or repeal rights recognized, granted, or made available to disadvantaged, marginalized,
or specific sectors under existing laws.

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
