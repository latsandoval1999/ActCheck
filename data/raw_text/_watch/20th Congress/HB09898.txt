                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Health care services in the Philippines are indeed very expensive despite continuous efforts
of the government to augment the financial and medical needs of individuals needing such
assistance. Many Filipinos, especially those belonging to the indigent sector are burdened by the
huge amount of hospital bills and medical expenses that they could obviously not afford to settle
but have no other choice but to bear in the hope that they could extend the lives of their sick
relatives.

       In 2007, Republic Act No.9439 or “An Act Prohibiting the Detention of Patients in
Hospitals and Medical Clinics on Grounds of Nonpayment of Hospital Bills or Medical Expenses”
was enacted to prohibit the detention of patients (including those who may have died) for
nonpayment, and to allow financially incapable patients to leave upon the execution of a
promissory note secured by either a mortgage or by a guarantee of a co-maker.

         However, even with the existence of such law, many hospitals, medical clinics and similar
facilities still practice detention of patients and cadavers/bodies of deceased patients because of
unsettled payment of hospital bills and medical expenses. Also, while there is a penalty imposed
for violation of said law, such amount is rather insignificant especially for hospitals owned by
corporations.

       Hence, this proposed legislation seeks to impose higher penalties for violation of said law,
including imprisonment, if necessary, to finally curb this inhumane practice.

       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.
