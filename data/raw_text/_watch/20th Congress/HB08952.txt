                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to enhance the resiliency and adaptability of Filipino
farmers to adverse weather and climate events by institutionalizing the use of
crop climate calendars, strengthening climate-sensitive decision-making, and
ensuring access to localized weather and climate information. It aims to equip
farmers with timely, science-based tools and knowledge that will enable them
to anticipate risks, optimize planting and harvesting schedules, and minimize
losses caused by increasingly unpredictable climate conditions.

       The Philippine agricultural sector remains highly vulnerable to
typhoons, droughts, floods, and shifting weather patterns brought about by
climate change. These hazards continue to disrupt farming activities, reduce
yields, and threaten food security and rural livelihoods. Many farmers still
rely on traditional practices and generalized weather forecasts, which are
often insufficient to guide critical farm decisions at the local level.

      This measure directly addresses these challenges by mandating the
Department of Agriculture (DA) to develop and promote crop climate
calendars that are customized to specific localities. These calendars provide
farmers with clear, visual guidance on cropping cycles, weather patterns, and
climate-related risks, enabling them to plan more effectively and reduce
uncertainties in farm operations.

      The bill further strengthens farmers’ capacity by directing the
Agricultural Training Institute (ATI) to deliver continuous training on
climate-sensitive decision-making. Through simplified and accessible learning
materials, farmers will gain practical skills in interpreting climate data and
adjusting their strategies based on evolving weather conditions.
       In addition, the measure requires the Philippine Atmospheric,
Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA) to provide
regular, localized, and easily understandable weather and climate information
directly to farmers and local government units. Ensuring that information
reaches the grassroots level in appropriate languages and formats, the bill will
bridge the gap between scientific data and on-the-ground application.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE USE OF CROP CLIMATE CALENDARS AND
LOCALIZED    WEATHER    INFORMATION,    ENHANCING     FARMER
RESILIENCY AND CLIMATE-SENSITIVE DECISION-MAKING IN RESPONSE
TO ADVERSE WEATHER AND CLIMATE EVENTS

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Climate-Resilient
Agriculture Act."

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
ensure food security and promote sustainable agricultural development by
strengthening the capacity of the agriculture sector to anticipate and respond to the
adverse impacts of weather disturbances and climate change.

       Towards this end, the State shall institutionalize science-based, data-driven,
and location-specific tools and systems that enable farmers and agricultural
stakeholders to make informed and climate-sensitive decisions across all stages of
crop production. It shall promote the development and utilization of crop climate
calendars and the timely dissemination of localized weather and climate information
as essential mechanisms for risk reduction and enhanced farm-level resilience.

       The State further recognizes that comprehensible climate information and
capacity-building interventions are critical in ensuring the long-term viability of the
agriculture sector in the face of increasing climate uncertainty.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

           a. Crop climate calendar refers to a visual tool that presents the
       phenology and cultivation practices of a crop against the timeline of the
       cropping season, containing information on the weather and climate states
       that occur in the locality and the risks faced by the crop per growing stage in
       relation to these;

                                            1
          b.​ Climate-sensitive decision refers to decisions that hinge on weather
      and climate states, with final outcomes and even decision alternatives which
      are varying depending on the prevailing weather phenomena; and

        c.​ Localized weather and climate information refers to information on
      weather and climate phenomena that are particularized down to the city or
      municipal level.

       SEC. 4. Crop Climate Calendars. – The Department of Agriculture (DA) shall
establish and promote the use of crop climate calendars throughout the Philippines.
In coordination with municipal, city and provincial agriculturists, the DA shall
implement a continuing program for all farmers and farmers’ organizations to
formulate, use, and interpret crop climate calendars that are tailor-made for each
specific locality or community. These crop climate calendars shall be distributed free
of charge to all farmers and farmers’ organizations and be made available online.

       SEC. 5. Climate-Sensitive Decision-Making. – The DA, through the Agricultural
Training Institute, shall provide regular appropriate training and capacity-building
for farmers and farmer’s organizations in the practice of climate-sensitive
decision-making and the use of relevant tools and applications. The ATI shall
formulate and develop clear, concise, and easily understandable charts, tables,
diagrams, briefers, and similar documents on climate-sensitive decision-making for
distribution to farmers and farmers’ organizations.

       SEC. 6. Localized Weather and Climate Information. – The Philippine
Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA)
shall provide regular and up-to-date localized weather and climate information to
city, municipal and provincial agriculturist’s offices and directly to the farmers
themselves through all available means of communication and information
dissemination including internet, social media, television, and radio. This
information shall be written in simple English, Filipino, and other regional language
or dialect as necessary and must be presented in a clear, concise and easily
understandable form.

      SEC. 7. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations for the year following its
enactment into law and thereafter.

        SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DA and PAGASA, in coordination with other relevant
government agencies, farmers’ organizations and stakeholders, shall issue the
necessary rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

       SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations, or any part thereof, which are inconsistent
with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
