21                                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
22
23   During a disaster, every minute matters and every household should be seen, heard,
24   and accounted for. Yet recent weather-related tragedies continue to expose gaps in
25   communication between households and the government, particularly in knowing who
26   needs help, where they are, and whether assistance has reached them.
27
28   In times of disaster, a single phone call can mean the difference between life and death.
29   Behind every report of flooding, every evacuation, and every call for rescue is a family
30   waiting, hoping, and trusting that help will come very soon.
31
32   This proposed measure seeks to establish an integrated, household-level climate and
33   disaster resilience system that connects households, barangays, LDRRMOs, and
34   relevant government agencies through a unified digital platform. It will allow households
35   to receive not just localized warnings but also report emergencies, request assistance,
36   access verified emergency contacts, and share their location when help is needed, to
37   enable rescuers to track and assist as soon as possible.
38
39   No household should be left without a way to call for help, and no government responder
40   should be left without a way to find them.
41
42   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

21                                  AN ACT
22        ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL HOUSEHOLD CLIMATE AND DISASTER
23      RESILIENCE INFORMATION AND RESPONSE SYSTEM, PROVIDING FOR A
24      UNIFIED HOUSEHOLD-LEVEL CLIMATE RISK MAP, REAL-TIME TWO-WAY
25     EMERGENCY COMMUNICATION, AND INTEGRATED DISASTER RESPONSE,
26                        AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
27
28   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
29   Congress assembled:

30        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Household
31   Emergency Response Act”.
32
33           SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect the
34   lives, safety, and welfare of its people from the effects of climate change and climate-
35   related disasters. The State recognizes that timely information, effective
36   communication, and rapid response are essential to saving lives and protecting
37   communities during emergencies.
38
39          Towards this end, the State shall ensure that every household has access to
40   timely and localized climate and disaster information and a reliable means to
41   communicate with, request assistance from, and receive updates from the government.
42   The State shall strengthen coordination among households, barangays, local disaster
43   risk reduction and management offices, and national government agencies through an
44   integrated and accessible information and response system.

45         SECTION 3. National Household Climate and Disaster Resilience
46   Information and Response System. – There is hereby established a National
47   Household Climate and Disaster Resilience Information and Response System,
 1   hereinafter referred to as the “System”, which shall integrate relevant climate, weather,
 2   hazard, emergency, and household vulnerability information maintained by government
 3   agencies and local government units.

 4   The System shall consist of:
 5         (a) Household Climate and Disaster Risk Map and Risk Profile;
 6         (b) National Climate and Disaster Resilience Mobile Application and Website;
 7         (c) Two-Way Emergency Communication and Reporting System;
 8         (d) Centralized Emergency Contact Directory; and
 9         (e) Integrated Disaster Response and Incident Tracking System.
10
11          SECTION 4. Household Climate and Disaster Risk Map. – The System shall
12   establish a digital, georeferenced map of households and relevant community facilities,
13   integrated with available government hazard and climate-risk information.

14         The map shall, where available, include layers identifying areas vulnerable to:

15         (a) flooding and excessive rainfall;
16         (b) landslides and soil instability;
17         (c) storm surges and coastal hazards;
18         (d) extreme heat;
19         (e) drought;
20         (f) typhoons and other climate-related hazards; and
21         (g) other hazards.

22            The map shall likewise identify relevant facilities, including evacuation centers,
23   hospitals, health facilities, police and fire stations, major roads, bridges, and other
24   facilities for disaster response.

25         SECTION 5. Household Climate Risk Profile. – Each household shall be
26   assigned a Household Climate Risk Profile based on its geographic location, hazard
27   exposure, and other relevant vulnerability information.

28           The profile may include the presence of persons with disabilities, senior citizens,
29   children, persons with medical or mobility needs, and other persons who may require
30   priority assistance during disasters.

31          Household information shall be collected, processed, stored, and shared only for
32   legitimate disaster preparedness, early warning, emergency response, recovery, and
33   related public safety purposes and shall be subject to the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and
34   other applicable laws.
 1           SECTION 6. National Climate and Disaster Resilience Mobile Application
 2   and Web Portal. – The Department of Information and Communications Technology
 3   (DICT), in coordination with the Climate Change Commission (CCC), National Disaster
 4   Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC), Philippine Atmospheric,
 5   Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA), Department of the
 6   Interior and Local Government (DILG), local government units, and other concerned
 7   agencies, shall establish and maintain a National Climate and Disaster Resilience
 8   Mobile Application and Web Portal.

 9         The System shall, at a minimum, enable a household to:

10         (a) view the climate and disaster risks applicable to its location;
11         (b) receive localized weather, climate, and disaster warnings and advisories;
12         (c) view evacuation centers, hospitals, emergency facilities, and other relevant
13            locations;
14         (d) access emergency contact numbers of the appropriate barangay, LGU,
15            LDRRMO, police, fire, medical, rescue, and other emergency services;
16         (e) receive evacuation orders, routes, and other instructions issued by competent
17            authorities;
18         (f) report flooding, landslides, infrastructure damage, stranded or missing
19            persons, medical emergencies, and other disaster-related incidents;
20         (g) request emergency assistance;
21         (h) attach photographs, videos, and other relevant information to an incident
22            report;
23         (i) share the household's location with authorized emergency responders when
24            requesting assistance; and
25         (j) receive updates regarding the status and action taken on an emergency
26            report.

27         The System shall be designed to operate through SMS to ensure accessibility in
28   areas with limited internet connectivity.

29          SECTION 7. Two-Way Emergency Communication and Incident Tracking. –
30   The System shall provide a two-way communication mechanism between households
31   and the appropriate barangay, LDRRMO, and other authorized government responders.

32         Every emergency report or request for assistance shall, where practicable, be
33   assigned a unique reference number and recorded in the System.

34         The household shall be able to receive updates on the status of its report,
 1   including its receipt, assignment to a responding unit, action taken, and resolution.

 2          Reports shall be automatically routed to the appropriate barangay or local
 3   disaster risk reduction and management office based on the location of the reporting
 4   household.

 5          SECTION 8. Emergency Location and Distress Function. The System shall
 6   provide an emergency location-sharing and distress function through which a household
 7   requesting emergency assistance may voluntarily transmit its current location to
 8   authorized responders.

 9         The location information shall be used solely for emergency response and shall
10   be accessible only to authorized personnel.

11         SECTION 9. Centralized Emergency Contact Directory. The System shall
12   maintain a verified and regularly updated directory of emergency contact numbers
13   nationwide, including, where applicable:

14          (a) barangay officials and emergency responders;
15          (b) LDRRMO;
16          (c) police and fire stations;
17          (d) hospitals and emergency medical services;
18          (e) evacuation centers;
19          (f) local government hotlines; and
20          (g) other government and accredited emergency service providers.

21        The System shall automatically identify and display the appropriate local
22   emergency contacts based on the location of the household.

23         SECTION 10. Role of Barangays. - Every barangay shall serve as the first-level
24   monitoring and response unit under the System and shall:

25          (a) monitor and act upon reports and requests for assistance from households
26              within its jurisdiction;
27          (b) maintain updated information on households; and
28          (c) coordinate with the LDRRMO on incidents requiring additional assistance or
29              resources.

30          The DILG shall prescribe the guidelines and standards for the implementation of
31   this Section in coordination with the NDRRMC and other concerned agencies.

32         SECTION 11. Role of the LDRRMO. The LDRRMO established under Republic
33   Act No. 10121 shall serve as the local coordinating and response office for the System
 1   and shall:

 2          (a) oversee the operation of the System within its jurisdiction;
 3          (b) coordinate and respond to incidents escalated by barangays;
 4          (c) coordinate with PAGASA and other concerned agencies on climate and
 5                disaster information; and
 6          (d) ensure the timely dissemination of localized warnings and advisories and the
 7                appropriate response to household reports.

 8          The DILG, in coordination with the NDRRMC, shall prescribe the operational
 9   guidelines and escalation protocols for the implementation of this Section.

10         SECTION 12. Role of PAGASA. PAGASA shall provide the System with official
11   weather forecasts, climate projections, hazard-related information, and other relevant
12   data necessary for timely and accurate public warnings and local disaster
13   preparedness.

14          PAGASA shall establish the appropriate standards and protocols for the
15   integration and dissemination of such information through the System.

16           SECTION 13. Role of the DICT. The Department of Information and
17   Communications Technology (DICT) shall develop, establish, and maintain the digital
18   infrastructure of the System, including the mobile application and web portal, and ensure
19   its interoperability with relevant government information systems.

20          SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and
21   regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the
22   provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

23         SECTION 15. Separability Clause. – If any portion of this Act is declared
24   unconstitutional, any provisions not affected thereby shall remain in force and effect.

25          SECTION 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following
26   the completion of its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
27   circulation in the Philippines.
