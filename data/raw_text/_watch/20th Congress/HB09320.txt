                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to maintain tariff protection on selected agricultural products
and ensure their continued exclusion from tariff elimination commitments under the
Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), and other similar trade
agreements. It establishes safeguards to protect local vegetable farmers, particularly
those in agriculture-dependent provinces.

       Farmers in agricultural areas, including Benguet and other highland
provinces, continue to experience severe economic losses due to declining farmgate
prices, import competition, smuggling1, and rising production and transport costs2.
Reports have shown instances where locally produced vegetables were sold at
extremely low prices, discarded3, or left unharvested because market returns no
longer covered production expenses. The continued influx of imported and
smuggled4 agricultural products have further weakened demand in increasingly
vulnerable conditions.

       To address these, the proposed measure retains tariff protection for essential
agricultural commodities and institutionalizes mechanisms to prevent the premature
removal of existing tariff protections. It authorizes the government to invoke
available modification, renegotiation, and withdrawal measures to protect the

       The protection of the country’s agricultural production is essential to ensure
food security, sustain rural livelihoods, and preserve the Philippines’ long-term

                          AN ACT
MAINTAINING TARIFF PROTECTION ON SELECTED AGRICULTURAL
PRODUCTS, ENSURING THEIR EXCLUSION FROM TARIFF ELIMINATION
UNDER THE REGIONAL COMPREHENSIVE ECONOMIC PARTNERSHIP
AND SIMILAR AGREEMENTS, PROVIDING SAFEGUARDS AND SUPPORT
FOR LOCAL VEGETABLE FARMERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Agricultural Tariff
Protection and Vegetable Farmers’ Safeguard Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect Filipino farmers and agricultural enterprises from unfair foreign
competition; promote food security, sustainable agricultural development, and rural
livelihoods; ensure that trade liberalization policies remain consistent with national
interest and social justice; uphold the constitutional mandate to prioritize Filipino
labor and locally produced goods; strengthen agriculture-dependent provinces and
local economies; and utilize lawful tariff measures, safeguards, and trade remedies
to preserve sensitive agricultural sectors.

       SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to vegetables classified under Section
II, Chapter 7, entitled “Edible Vegetables and Certain Roots and Tubers” of the 2012
World Customs Organization’s Harmonized System, including but not limited to:

          a. Cabbage;

          b. Carrots;

          c. Cauliflower;

          d. Potatoes;

          e. Radish;


                                           1
          f.​ Broccoli; and

          g.​ Lettuce.

       The Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the Tariff
Commission, may include additional vegetables determined to be sensitive
agricultural commodities.

       SEC. 4. Maintenance and Retention of Tariff Protection. – The State shall maintain
existing tariff rates on the covered agricultural products at levels sufficient to protect
domestic producers.

       No tariff reduction shall be implemented unless authorized by Congress
upon a clear showing that such reduction will not prejudice domestic farmers, food
security, and agriculture-dependent provinces.

      SEC. 5. Exclusion from Tariff Elimination under Trade Agreements. – The National
Government, through the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), DA, Department
of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), Department of Finance, Bureau
of Customs (BOC), and Tariff Commission shall ensure the continued exclusion of
the covered products from tariff elimination commitments under the Regional
Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), and similar future trade agreements.

      SEC. 6. Authority to Modify Tariff Commitments. – Pursuant to Article 2.15 of the
RCEP, the President, upon recommendation of the concerned agencies, is authorized
to:

          a. Initiate negotiations for the modification or withdrawal of tariff
       concessions;

          b. Invoke review, safeguard, renegotiation, or withdrawal mechanisms;
       and

          c. Implement necessary adjustments to protect domestic agricultural
       sectors.

       SEC. 7. Safeguard Measures against Import Surges. – The government shall
establish automatic and responsive safeguard mechanisms, including:

          a. Temporary additional tariffs when import volumes exceed threshold
       levels;

          b. Quantitative restrictions when necessary to prevent market disruption;

          c. Monitoring of import prices to prevent dumping and predatory pricing;
       and




                                            2
          d. Immediate tariff review upon determination of injury or threat of injury
      to local farmers.

      SEC. 8. Agricultural Injury Review Mechanism. – The Tariff Commission, in
coordination with the DA, shall conduct annual reviews of:

          a. Import volumes;

          b. Domestic and farmgate prices;

          c. Production levels;

          d. Farmer incomes; and

          e. Evidence of injury or threat of injury.

      Upon determination of injury, the Commission shall immediately recommend
appropriate tariff adjustments, safeguards, quotas, or emergency measures.

      SEC. 9. Priority Protection for Agriculture-Dependent Provinces. –
Implementation of this Act shall prioritize provinces heavily dependent on
vegetable production, including Benguet and similarly situated areas, considering:

          a. Higher production costs due to terrain and climate;

          b. Strategic contribution to national food supply; and

          c. Increased vulnerability to import competition.

      SEC. 10. Support Programs for Farmers. – In addition to tariff protection, the
government shall implement comprehensive support programs, including:

          a. Farm-to-market logistics improvement;

          b. Post-harvest facilities and cold storage systems;

          c. Access to credit, financing, and crop insurance;

          d. Seed, fertilizer, and input support;

          e. Market linkage and price stabilization programs; and

          f. Infrastructure development in farming communities.

      SEC. 11. Inter-Agency Coordination and Monitoring. – The DA, DTI, DEPDev,
BOC, and Tariff Commission shall:

          a. Monitor importation trends and market conditions;



                                           3
         b. Maintain a centralized database on agricultural trade;

         c. Recommend policy adjustments; and

         d. Submit annual reports to Congress on the implementation of this Act.

       SEC. 12. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the DA, DTI, DEPDev, BOC, and Tariff Commission shall promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations.

      SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
publication in the Official Gazette, or in a newspaper of general circulation.
