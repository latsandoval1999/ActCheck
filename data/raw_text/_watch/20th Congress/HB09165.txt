                          AN ACT
 EXEMPTING THE SYSTEM LOSS CHARGE COMPONENT IN THE SALE
  OF ELECTRICITY BY DISTRIBUTION COMPANIES AND ELECTRIC
 COOPERATIVES FROM THE COVERAGE OF THE VALUE-ADDED TAX,
   AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTION 109 OF THE NATIONAL
        INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED


                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

     This Bill seeks to amend the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as
amended by Republic Act Nos. 9377 and 10378, by exempting the system loss charge
component in the electricity bills from the coverage of the value-added tax (VAT).

        VAT is a form of sales tax—a tax on consumption levied on the sale, barter,
exchange or lease of goods or properties and services in the Philippines, as well as on
the importation of goods into the country. As an indirect tax, it is shifted or passed on
to the buyer, transferee, or lessee of goods, properties, or services. Being a pass-on tax,
the burden of paying the VAT thus ultimately falls upon the final consumers—the
consuming public.

       The system loss charge, on the other hand, is the component of an electric bill
representing the cost of electricity lost during transmission, as well as losses due to
pilferage and technical and administrative inefficiencies. Notably, consumers are made
to shoulder the cost of even pilfered electricity and the power consumed by distribution
companies such as Meralco and by electric cooperatives. As a result, consumers pay for
more electricity than they actually use. Compounding this inequity, the system loss
charge is further subjected to VAT, which drives electricity bills even higher.

       The table below illustrates the impact of the system loss charge and the
corresponding VAT on non-lifeline residential customers of Meralco:

       According to Meralco, the system loss charge comprises approximately five
percent (5%) of the total electricity bill. As of April 2026, the system loss charge for
residential consumers stands at ₱0.7752 per kilowatt-hour, subject to 11.04% VAT. By
exempting the system loss charge from VAT, residential Meralco customers consuming
between 50 to 200 kWh can save between ₱4.28 to ₱17.12 per month.

      Being a tax on consumption, VAT should, by definition, be imposed only upon
goods and services that people actually purchase and consume. The imposition of VAT
on the system loss charge is therefore not only illogical but legally indefensible—
consumers are being taxed for electricity they never actually received or consumed.

        According to data from Global Petrol Prices,1 as of September 2025, the average
residential electricity price in the Philippines is ₱12.383 per kWh ($0.203 USD), while
the average rate for businesses stands at ₱9.618 per kWh ($0.157 USD). In terms of
residential costs, the Philippines ranks 48th out of 145 countries globally and holds the
fourth-highest rate in Asia, following Cyprus, Singapore and Japan. Notably, Philippine
electricity costs are double those of Thailand and Indonesia.

       In another study conducted by the International Energy Consultants (IEC), a
Perth-based consultancy firm commissioned by Meralco to conduct the study, the rates
in Luzon are placed at ninth highest electricity tariffs of the 44 countries surveyed.

       Per the IEC study, the primary reason for such higher power rates as compared
to other countries is the absence of government subsidies for electricity, unlike countries
like Indonesia, Thailand, and Malaysia, where electricity rates are subsidized by the
government. These subsidies comprise a large part of their public budget. In Indonesia,
for example, energy subsidies account for 24.0 percent of their 2013 public expenditure
plan.

       Against this backdrop—and amid the persistent rise in the prices of basic
commodities, fuel, education, and healthcare, worsening inflation, and the continuing
erosion of the purchasing power of the peso—it is imperative that Congress take
concrete and immediate steps to ease the financial burden on ordinary Filipinos,
particularly those from the lower-income brackets.

       This Bill was first filed as House Bill 5543 in the 16th Congress by Bayan Muna
Partylist Representatives Neri Colmenares and Carlos Isagani Zarate. It was refiled as
HB 1616 in the 17th Congress and was passed by the House of Representatives on third
reading. It was refiled again as HB 245 and HB 5994 during the 18 th and 19th
Congresses, respectively.

       Passage of this Bill is thus earnestly sought.
