                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Civil registry documents, particularly Certificates of Live Birth, are
fundamental because it serves as the primary legal proof of an individual’s name,
parentage, date of birth, and citizenship. However, the integrity of the Philippine civil
registry is currently threatened by foreign nationals exploiting the system through
the fraudulent registration of births, with an estimated 50,000 spurious records
currently suspected in the database of the Philippine Statistic Office (PSA) (Cruz,
2026). Because birth certificates are the foundational document establishing
citizenship, these fraudulent registrations grant illicit access to exclusive national
rights, privileges, and government services reserved to legitimate citizens. The core
of this systemic failure is the absence of an administrative remedy to address such
fraudulent entries resulting in protracted proceedings thereby the continued
existence of spurious records in official databases. With some government officials
allegedly involved in this scheme (Casilao, 2026), a comprehensive, immediate, and
effective intervention is imperative.

       The 1987 Constitution mandates the government to serve and protect the
people, promote general welfare, and maintain honesty and integrity in public
service. Likewise, the State remains the ultimate authority over matters of
citizenship. Inherent to these Constitutional principles is the duty of the State to
preserve the sanctity and reliability of its civil registry, particularly documents
establishing birth and citizenship.

      This measure seeks to directly address these civil registry gaps by establishing
an administrative mechanism for the cancellation of birth certificates fraudulently
obtained by foreign nationals. Central to this proposed measure is the creation of a
Special Committee on Cancellation of Birth Certificates, composed of representatives
from key agencies authorized to resolve such matters with finality. This introduces a
streamlined procedure that ensures due process while preventing undue delay.

       The proposed measure, which was previously introduced during the 19 th
Congress, strengthens accountability by authorizing the Special Committee to
recommend the institution of appropriate criminal, civil, administrative, and
deportation proceedings against foreign nationals and any individuals, whether
private or public officers, who participated in or facilitated the fraudulent
registration. Through this bill, erroneous records are corrected while deterring future
abuses of our civil registry system.

       Additionally, this version of the bill recognizes evidentiary realities by
providing that the absence of any primary and secondary civil registry records, duly
certified by local civil registrars after diligent and exhaustive search, shall constitute
prima facie evidence of fraudulent registration. In such cases, this bill allows for the
expeditious cancellation of the birth certificate by the Special Committee.

       Finally, the enactment of this measure affirms the State’s commitment to
uphold the integrity of its legal identity systems, protect the rights and privileges of
lawful citizens, and reinforce public trust and confidence in government records and
procedures.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                              AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE ADMINISTRATIVE CANCELLATION OF BIRTH
CERTIFICATES REGISTERED THROUGH FRAUDULENT MEANS BY FOREIGN
NATIONALS, CREATING A SPECIAL COMMITTEE ON CANCELLATION OF BIRTH
CERTIFICATES, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in the Congress assembled:

        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Fraudulent Birth
Certificate Cancellation Law.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to promote a just and dynamic social order, promote general welfare, and maintain
honesty and integrity in public service. Towards this end, the State shall enact
policies to safeguard and protect the integrity of the most basic information of its
people, especially their birth and nationality.

       SEC. 3. Scope. – This Act shall cover the cancellation of birth certificates
registered through fraudulent means by foreign nationals.

      SEC. 4. Special Committee on Cancellation of Birth Certificates. – A
Special Committee on Cancellation of Birth Certificates, herein referred to as the
“Committee,” is hereby created for cancellation of birth certificates registered through
fraudulent means by a foreign national. The Committee is composed of the following:

       (a) Civil Registrar General of the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) as
           Chairperson; and
       (b) Representatives of the Department of Foreign Affairs, Interior and Local
           Government, and Justice with a rank of Undersecretary, and the Office of
           the Solicitor General with a rank of Assistant Solicitor General, as
           members.

       SEC. 5. Committee Secretariat. – The PSA Board Secretariat shall act as the
secretariat of the Committee. The Committee Secretariat shall accept complaints
involving birth certificates registered through fraudulent means by foreign nationals,
assign docket numbers, schedule hearings, and issue notices thereof.
      SEC. 6. Powers and Functions. – The Committee shall have the following
powers and functions:
      (a) Formulate rules of procedure concerning the investigation, filing, and
          reception of complaints, conduct of hearings, and appeals involving
          cancellation of birth certificates registered through fraudulent means by
          any foreign national;
      (b) Conduct clarificatory hearings and investigations on birth certificates
          registered through fraudulent means by any foreign national;
      (c) Issue summon, subpoena, and subpoena duces tecum to secure the
          production of all necessary evidence concerning cases of birth certificates
          registered through fraudulent means by foreign nationals such as
          affidavits, records, testimonies, and other pertinent documents;
      (d) Promulgate decisions, after due notice and hearing, concerning complaints
          of cancellation of birth certificates registered through fraudulent means by
          any foreign national;
      (e) Recommend to the Department of Foreign Affairs, Justice, Interior and
          Local Government, the Ombudsman, and other appropriate agencies the
          filing of deportation, criminal, civil, and administrative cases under
          existing laws against foreign nationals, private individuals, and public
          officers and employees who have participated in the fraudulent registration
          of birth certificates of a foreign national; and
      (f) Perform such other powers and functions necessary to attain its objectives.

        SEC. 7. Complaint. – Any person of legal age, including duly authorized
representatives of law enforcement agencies, may file with the Committee Secretariat,
a verified complaint for the administrative cancellation of a birth certificate registered
through fraudulent means by any foreign national. The complaint shall set forth the
following:

       (a) Name of the foreign national, which shall be sufficient to the extent that
           such individual is known by such name;
       (b) Last known address of the foreign national;
       (c) Particulars of the fraudulent birth certificate;
       (d) Specific acts, events, and circumstances which establish the illegal
           acquisition of birth certificate by the foreign national; and
       (e) Such other proof necessary to establish the illegal acquisition of a birth
           certificate by the foreign national.

       SEC. 8. Answer. – Within ten (10) days from the filing of the complaint, the
Committee Secretariat shall require the foreign national to submit a verified answer
to the attached complaint within a non-extendible period of fifteen (15) days from
receipt thereof.

        SEC. 9. Case Report. – Within thirty (30) days from the submission of the
verified answer of the foreign national, the Committee Secretariat shall submit to the
Committee a report including the case records stating all the pieces of evidence
provided by the parties.

       SEC. 10. Clarificatory Hearing. – Upon receipt of the case report, the
Committee may conduct a clarificatory hearing and inquire on any matter to properly
decide the case.
      SEC. 11. Weight of Evidence. – The Committee shall decide on the complaint
based on substantial evidence or the amount of relevant evidence that a reasonable
person would consider adequate to support a conclusion.

        SEC. 12. Absence of Local Civil Registrar Record. – The absence of any
primary or secondary civil registry documentation of birth, including original entries,
late registration records, reconstructed records, and transmitted or archived copies,
as certified after diligent and exhaustive search by the Local Civil Registrar, shall
constitute prima facie evidence that the birth certificate was fraudulently registered.
If the respondent fails to satisfactorily overcome such presumption with competent
and credible evidence within the period prescribed under Section 8 of this Act, the
birth certificate shall be deemed cancelled, subject to the official confirmation of the
Committee.

       SEC. 13. Decision. – The Committee shall evaluate all evidence presented by
the parties, and with a vote of at least two (2) Committee members, shall issue a
decision to cancel the birth certificate of a foreign national which was registered by
a fraudulent means within thirty (30) days from receipt of the report from the
Committee Secretariat, except for cases falling under Section 12 of this Act.
        In cases falling under Section 12, the Committee shall, upon determination of
the existence of conditions therein, forthwith confirm the cancellation of the birth
certificate and shall issue the corresponding implementing resolution of such
cancellation in all civil registry records and databases.
       The decision of the Committee shall be immediately executory pending appeal
to the Office of the President.
       The decision shall likewise recommend whether criminal, civil,
administrative, or deportation proceedings, as the case may be, should be pursued
against the foreign national, any person, public officer, or employee who may have
participated in the illegal acquisition of birth certificate.


        SEC. 14. Appeal. – Any person adversely affected by a decision of the
Committee may file an appeal with the Office of the President within fifteen (15) days
from receipt thereof. The Office of the President shall decide on the appeal within
thirty (30) days from the filing of the appeal.

       SEC. 15. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the Philippines
Statistics Authority. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.

      SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the approval of this Act, the Committee shall promulgate the pertinent rules
and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

      SEC. 17. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts which are not affected shall
remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. – All laws, ordinances, orders, rules, regulations
and other issuances or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
      SEC. 19. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
