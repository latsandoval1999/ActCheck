                          AN ACT
PROMOTING THE PRODUCTIVE UTILIZATION OF IDLE LANDS
THROUGH TAX REFORMS, INCENTIVES FOR AGRICULTURAL USE,
AND PARTNERSHIPS WITH FARMERS AND COOPERATIVES,
AMENDING PERTINENT PROVISIONS OF THE LOCAL GOVERNMENT
CODE OF 1991, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Land is one of the most vital resources of the Philippines. Yet vast tracts of
land across the country remain idle, underutilized, or deliberately held for
speculative purposes. According to data from the Philippine Statistics Authority
(PSA), the total farm area in the country was estimated at around 7.27 million
hectares based on the 2012 Census of Agriculture and Fisheries, highlighting the
vast land resources available for agricultural production1. Despite the country’s vast
agricultural potential, a significant portion of cultivable land remains idle or
underutilized due to various constraints such as absentee landownership, financial
limitations, lack of labor, or insufficient technical support2. Idle lands worsen food
insecurity, deny local governments potential revenues, and contribute to inequitable
land ownership.

       The Constitution recognizes the duty of the State to promote the equitable
distribution and productive use of land to advance national development. While the
Republic Act No. 7160 or otherwise known as “Local Government Code of 1991”
already authorizes local governments to impose taxes on idle lands, the existing
framework has proven insufficient to deter speculative landholding or prolonged
neglect of cultivable land.

      At the same time, many landowners are unable to cultivate their lands due to
financial limitations, lack of labor, or absence from rural areas. However, these lands
could be productively utilized by tenant farmers, agrarian reform beneficiaries, and
agricultural cooperatives if appropriate institutional mechanisms and incentives
were established.

       This measure therefore adopts a balanced and integrated approach to idle land
reform. First, it strengthens idle land taxation to discourage land hoarding and
speculative holding. Second, it introduces tax incentives and relief measures for
landowners who voluntarily allow farmers, agrarian reform beneficiaries, or
agricultural cooperatives to cultivate idle lands through formal utilization
agreements. Third, it establishes a policy framework to facilitate land matching,
support agricultural production, and ensure that idle lands contribute to national food
security.

       The bill also provides for the allocation of revenues generated from idle land
taxation to support agricultural modernization programs of the Department of
Agriculture and land reform support services of the Department of Agrarian Reform.
These resources will help farmers gain access to credit, farm inputs, and technical
assistance necessary to ensure the productivity of newly utilized lands.

      By transforming idle lands into productive agricultural assets, the State can
increase domestic food production, expand opportunities for small farmers,
stimulate rural economies, and promote equitable and sustainable land use.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Idle Agricultural
Lands Utilization Act.”

      SEC 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to promote the productive utilization of all cultivable agricultural lands, strengthen
national food security and agricultural productivity, and expand opportunities for
farmers, tenants, and agricultural cooperatives to access land for cultivation. Toward
this end, the State shall encourage voluntary land-sharing and utilization


                                                                                    1
arrangements between landowners and agricultural producers, while discouraging
the prolonged neglect of agricultural lands that can otherwise contribute to increased
food production, rural employment, and sustainable agricultural development.

      SEC 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   a) Idle Agricultural Land refers to privately owned agricultural land suitable for
      cultivation that has remained uncultivated, unplanted, or unused for
      agricultural production for a continuous period of at least two (2) years, except
      for lands undergoing soil rehabilitation, fallowing, or affected by force
      majeure.

   b) Landowner refers to any individual, corporation, or juridical entity holding
      legal title or ownership of agricultural land.

   c) Utilization Agreement refers to a formal written agreement between a
      landowner and a tenant, farmers’ association, or cooperative allowing the
      cultivation or productive use of idle agricultural land.

     SEC 4. Strengthening the Idle Land Tax — Section 236 of Republic Act
No. 7160, otherwise known as the “Local Government Code of 1991”, is hereby
amended to read as follows:

      “[A] The province [or] city, or a municipality within the Metropolitan Manila
Area, may levy an annual tax on idle lands at a rate of not [exceeding] LESS THAN
five percent (5%) BUT NOT MORE THAN TEN PERCENT (10%) of the
assessed value of the property [which shall be], in addition to the basic real property
tax.

    IDLE RURAL AGRICULTURAL LANDS EXCEEDING FIFTY (50)
HECTARES HELD BY NATURAL OR JURIDICAL PERSONS FOR
SPECULATIVE PURPOSES SHALL BE SUBJECT TO AN ADDITIONAL
SURTAX OF TWO PERCENT (2%) OF THEIR ASSESSED VALUE”

       SEC 5. Tax Incentives for Landowners. — Landowners who convert idle
agricultural lands into productive use or allow their lands to be cultivated by tenant
farmers, agrarian reform beneficiaries, or agricultural cooperatives shall be entitled
to the following incentives:

         a) Tax amnesty on idle land surtaxes for a period of three (3) years;
         b) Tax credits equivalent to up to twenty percent (20%) of the assessed
            idle land tax value applicable to future real property tax liabilities;
         c) Priority access to agricultural support programs of the Department of
            Agriculture.

       SEC 6. Agricultural Land Utilization Agreements. — Landowners may
voluntarily enter into land utilization agreements with farmers’ organizations,
agrarian reform beneficiaries, or cooperatives registered with the Cooperative
Development Authority.

      These agreements shall:
        a) Allow the cultivation or productive use of idle lands;
        b) Specify duration, compensation arrangements, and production sharing;
        c) Be registered with the concerned local government unit.

        SEC 7. Role of Local Government Units (LGUs). — The local government
units shall assist in identifying idle agricultural lands within their jurisdiction,
facilitate land matching between landowners and farmers, monitor compliance with
utilization agreements, and provide necessary support services to farmers
participating in land utilization arrangements under this Act.

       SEC 8. Allocation of Idle Land Tax Revenues. — The collected idle land
taxation shall proceed and allocated as follows;

         a) Fifty percent (50%) to the local government unit where the land is
            located;
         b) Thirty percent (30%) to the Department of Agriculture for agricultural
            modernization programs; and

         c) Twenty percent (20%) to the Department of Agrarian Reform for land
            reform support services and rural development.

       SEC 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination
with the Department of Agrarian Reform (DAR), Department of Finance,
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and concerned agencies,
shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

       SEC 10. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to implement this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

      SEC 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SEC 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SEC 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
