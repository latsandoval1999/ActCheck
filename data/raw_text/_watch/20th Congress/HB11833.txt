EXPLANATORY NOTE

Household food insecurity remains a pressing and persistent challenge in
the Philippines. According to the 2023 DOST-FNRI National Nutrition Survey, an
alarming 31.4% of Filipino households were classified as moderately to severely
food insecure, with rural areas experiencing a higher incidence at 35.3%
compared to urban households at 27.8%. These figures underscore that food
vulnerability is deeply tied to constraints in household-level food access and
heavy exposure to sudden global or domestic price and supply shocks.

To address this structural vulnerability, the State is mandated by the
fundamental law to step forward. Section 15, Article II of the 1987 Philippine
Constitution explicitly declares that "The State shall protect and promote the
right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them."
Furthermore, Section 9 of the same Article mandates that the State shall
promote a just and dynamic social order that will guarantee the enjoyment of
health, economic security, and an improved quality of life for all. True human
development and national progress cannot be attained when a significant portion
of our population suffers from chronic hunger and nutritional deficiencies.

In response to this, grassroots initiatives have proven that local food
production serves as an immediate, practical, and scalable intervention. Existing
practices demonstrate massive public uptake: the Department of Education
(DepEd) shows that the Gulayan sa Paaralan Program has covered a significant
number of schools nationwide, while numerous households have engaged in
community fruit and vegetable planting through initiatives like the Department
of the Interior and Local Government's (DILG) Halinat Magtanim ng Prutas at
Gulay (HAPAG) sa Barangay program.

However, relying solely on administrative campaigns leaves these vital
nutrition efforts vulnerable to shifting political priorities and uneven local
implementation. A stable, statutory framework is urgently required to
institutionalize these practices permanently.

This bill seeks to institutionalize the "Gulayan sa Barangay at Paaralan
Program" as a permanent, regular program of the national government. By
mandating every barangay and encouraging public schools with available vacant
spaces to establish community farming areas and food gardens, this measure
transforms localized gardening into a robust national strategy for food security,
community nutrition, and self-reliance.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

House BILL No, 11833

Introduced by: Kababaihan Kabalikatan para sa Kapakanan at Kaunlaran ng Bayan (4K) Party-list
Representative Iris Marie D. Montes

AN ACT
PROMOTING FOOD SECURITY AND COMMUNITY NUTRITION THROUGH
THE INSTITUTIONALIZATION OF THE GULAYAN SA BARANGAY AT
PAARALAN PROGRAM

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION. 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Gulayan sa
Barangay at Paaralan Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
strengthen food security and nutrition through community- and school-based
food production. To this end, the State shall support and institutionalize food
gardens and community farming areas in barangays and public schools as local
mechanisms for improving access to nutritious food, reinforcing feeding and
nutrition education, and reducing househoid exposure to food price and supply
shocks.

SEC. 3. Institutionalization of the Gulayan sa Barangay and Paaralan. - The
Gulayan sa Barangay at Paaralan Program is hereby institutionalized as a regular
program of the national government to strengthen food security and nutrition
through community- and school-based food production, particularly in food-
insecure, underserved, and geographically isolated areas.

Every barangay shall designate an area for community farming.

Public schools shall also, whenever suitable vacant lots are available within
schoo! premises, establish and maintain school-based food gardens under the
Program.

SEC. 4. Role of the Department of the Interior and Local Government. - The
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall oversee the
implementation of the barangay component of the Program. It shall coordinate
with local government units and barangays in identifying suitable vacant lots for
community farming and in facilitating the implementation of the Program at the
barangay level.

The DILG shall also monitor compliance by barangays with the provisions of
this Act.

===== [page 4] =====
The barangay chairperson and members of the sangguniang barangay shall
encourage community participation and provide appropriate assistance in the
planting, cultivation, and harvesting of agricultural produce.

The Department of Education, in coordination with the Department of
Agriculture, the Department of the Interior and Local Government, and the
concerned local government units, shall support and oversee implementation of
the Program in public schools.

SEC. 5. Role of the Department of Agriculture. - The Department of
Agriculture (DA), through its regional offices and in coordination with the DILG,
the Department of Education, and local government units, shall provide technical
assistance and support for the implementation of the Program.

Such support shall include the provision of vegetable seedlings, other
nutritious crop seedlings, gardening tools, and other necessary inputs for
community farming areas and school-based food gardens established under this
Act, and shall prioritize linkages with local farmers and community-based
producers in the sourcing of inputs and planting materials for the Program.

SEC. 6. Role of the Department of Education. - The Department of Education
(DepEd), in coordination with the DA, the DILG, and the concerned local
government units, shall oversee the implementation of the school component of
the Program. For this purpose, it shall identify suitable vacant lots within
government-owned school premises for school-based food gardens and support
their establishment and maintenance consistent with the objectives of this Act.

SEC. 7. Incentive and Awards Mechanism. - To encourage active
participation, innovation, and sustainability, the DILG and the DA shall establish
a National Search and Awards Program for Outstanding Barangay and School
Food Gardens.

Annual grants, productivity bonuses, or infrastructure support funds shall be
awarded to top-performing barangays and public schools that demonstrate
exceptional yield, community involvement, and integration of the Program into
local nutrition initiatives.

SEC. 8. Integration with School-Based Feeding and Nutrition Programs. -
Harvested produce from school-based food gardens established under Section 3
of this Act shail be prioritized for use in the School-Based Feeding Program.

Excess produce from barangay community farming areas may likewise be
utilized to supply local government supplementary feeding programs for
underweight and stunted children, or local community pantries.

SEC. 9. Capacity Building, Training, and Extension Services. - The DA,
through its agricultural extension workers and in partnership with local
agricultural state universities and colleges (SUCs) and local government units
(LGUs), shall conduct regular training workshops on urban agriculture, organic
farming, composting, and seed saving for barangay leaders, teachers, parent-
teacher associations (PTAs), and youth volunteers.

===== [page 5] =====
SEC. 10. Monitoring, Reporting, and Digital Mapping. - The DILG, in
coordination with the DA and DepEd, shall develop a registry and mapping
system of all active barangay community farms and school-based food gardens.

Each implementing agency shall submit an annual progress report to
Congress detailing the number of operational gardens, volume of produce
harvested, estimated market value saved by households, and the number of
beneficiaries reached.

SEC. 11. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations
for the DA, DepEd and DILG. Thereafter, such amounts as necessary for its
continued implementation shall be included in the General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG), the Department of Agriculture (DA), and the Department of Education
(DepEd), in consultation with the appropriate leagues of local government units,
including the Liga ng mga Barangay, as well as representatives of public schools
and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations necessary to implement this Act.

SEC. 13. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provisions not affected thereby
shall remain in force and effect.

SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. - Any law, presidential decree or issuance,
executive order, letter of instruction, rule or regulation inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act is hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 15. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
