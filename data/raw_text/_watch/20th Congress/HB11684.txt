                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The internet is no longer a luxury; it is a necessity. It is how our children
attend class, how our farmers and small entrepreneurs sell their products, how
our overseas Filipino workers communicate with their families, and, in
emergencies, how our communities receive warnings before a natural calamity
arrives. Yet for millions of Filipinos, especially in the provinces, the internet they
pay for every month is slow, unreliable, and far from what was promised in the
advertisement.

       The country has made progress. Based on Ookla data for August 2025,
the Philippines ranked 45th out of 182 countries in fixed broadband speed and
82nd out of 140 countries in mobile speed.1 However, the fastest connections
are concentrated in Metro Manila and a few major cities, while subscribers in
far-flung municipalities often receive only a fraction of the speed they pay for,
with no clear standard to hold their provider accountable.

      Republic Act No. 12234 or the the Konektadong Pinoy Act, opened the data
transmission industry to more players and directed the National
Telecommunications Commission (NTC) to issue performance standards on
speed, latency, and other quality-of-service measures. This bill builds on that
law by setting in statute a minimum floor below which no paid internet service
may fall, so that the standards cannot be diluted, and every Filipino subscriber,
whether in the city or in the barangay, knows what he or she is entitled to.

      This bill sets minimum entry-level speeds for wired and wireless
broadband by area. It requires providers to deliver at least eighty percent of their
advertised speed at least eighty percent of the time, and prohibits them from
advertising speeds they cannot consistently deliver. It directs the NTC to

measure and publish the actual performance of every provider and to raise the
standards over time. It also guarantees the rights of subscribers to timely
installation, automatic rebates for outages, and termination without penalty
when their provider fails to deliver. To avoid discouraging investment where it is
needed most, providers that expand into previously unserved and underserved
areas are given three years before the minimum speeds apply to them.

       The bill also closes the loopholes that leave subscribers paying for speeds
they never receive. Every advertisement must state the guaranteed minimum
speed, not only the “up to” speed. No paid plan may be throttled below the
minimum under a “fair usage” policy. When a provider falls short of the speed it
promised, the subscriber’s bill is reduced in proportion. Penalties are aligned
with the Konektadong Pinoy Act, so that substandard service is fined for every
day it persists, and a provider found in violation twice within a year must stop
signing up new subscribers in the affected area until it fixes its service. This
proposed Bill makes clear that it adds to, and never subtracts from, the
protections already granted by the Konektadong Pinoy Act.

     Every peso a Filipino family spends on internet should buy the service that
was promised. This bill makes sure that it does.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
ESTABLISHING MINIMUM INTERNET CONNECTION SPEEDS AND QUALITY
  OF SERVICE STANDARDS, PROTECTING THE RIGHTS OF INTERNET
SUBSCRIBERS, SUPPLEMENTING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 12234, OTHERWISE
    KNOWN AS THE “KONEKTADONG PINOY ACT,” AND FOR OTHER
                         PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Minimum
Internet Speed Act of 2026.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – Pursuant to Article II, Section 24 of
the Constitution, the State recognizes the vital role of communication and
information in nation-building. The State shall ensure that all Filipinos, in urban
and rural areas alike, have access to reliable, quality, and affordable internet
service, and shall protect subscribers against substandard service and
misleading advertising of internet speeds.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Advertised speed refers to the download and upload speeds represented
          by a provider in any advertisement, plan description, or subscription
          contract;
      (b) Broadband refers to high-speed internet service that is always on,
          transmitted through wired or wireless technologies such as, but not
          limited to, fiber optic cable, digital subscriber line, cable modem, fixed
          wireless, cellular mobile, and satellite;
      (c) Provider refers to any data transmission industry participant, as
          defined under Republic Act No. 12234, otherwise known as the
          “Konektadong Pinoy Act,” and any public telecommunications entity,
          that offers internet access service to the public for a fee;
      (d) Service reliability refers to the percentage of time over a given period
          during which a provider delivers the required percentage of the
          advertised speed to a subscriber;
      (e) Threshold speed refers to the minimum download speed that a provider
          must offer and deliver as its entry-level paid broadband service under
          Section 4 of this Act; and
      (f) Unserved and underserved areas shall have the meanings given under
          Section 3(aa) and Section 3(y) of Republic Act No. 12234, respectively,
          as identified by the Department of Information and Communications
          Technology (DICT) under Section 6(h) thereof.

       SECTION 4. Minimum Threshold Speeds. – Within one (1) year from the
effectivity of this Act, no provider shall offer paid broadband service with an
entry-level download speed lower than the following:

      (a) Fifty (50) megabits per second (Mbps) for wired broadband and ten (10)
          Mbps for wireless broadband in metropolitan areas and highly
          urbanized cities;
      (b) Twenty-five (25) Mbps for wired broadband and five (5) Mbps for
          wireless broadband in all other cities; and
      (c) Ten (10) Mbps for wired broadband and three (3) Mbps for wireless
          broadband in all municipalities.

       No provider shall, by reason of a fair usage policy, data cap, or similar
limitation, reduce the speed of any paid broadband plan below the applicable
threshold speed during the billing period.

       Providers that expand coverage into unserved and underserved areas shall
not be subject to the threshold speeds in such areas for a period of three (3)
years from the start of their commercial operations therein: Provided, That such
providers shall remain subject to Section 5 of this Act and to the performance
standards issued under Section 15 of Republic Act No. 12234: Provided, further,
That this exemption shall not apply in any area where the provider already
offered broadband service at the effectivity of this Act.

       Nothing in this Section shall apply to free internet service, including the
free public internet access program under Republic Act No. 10929, otherwise
known as the “Free Internet Access in Public Places Act,” which shall continue
to be governed by its own standards.

       SECTION 5. Delivery of Advertised Speeds. – Providers shall advertise and
offer only such internet speeds as they can consistently deliver. Providers shall
deliver to their subscribers at least eighty percent (80%) of the advertised speed
at least eighty percent (80%) of the time, including during peak hours. Speed
and service reliability shall be measured in accordance with the methodology
prescribed by the National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) under
Section 6 of this Act. Every advertisement, plan description, and subscription
contract shall state, in clear and conspicuous terms, the minimum download
and upload speeds that the provider guarantees to deliver under this Section,
alongside any maximum or “up to” speed.

      SECTION 6. Measurement, Monitoring, and Review of Standards. – To
monitor and enforce compliance with this Act, the NTC, in coordination with the
DICT, shall:

      (a) Within six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act and after public
          consultation with consumer organizations, civil society organizations,
          and local government units, prescribe the criteria and methodology for
          measuring broadband quality of service, including download and
          upload speed, latency, packet loss, jitter, and service availability, with
          measurements taken during peak hours and in rural areas;
      (b) Provide the public with a free, secure, and easy-to-use tool to test their
          internet connection, the results of which may be used in support of
          complaints, and which, when conducted in accordance with the
          methodology prescribed under paragraph (a), shall constitute prima
          facie evidence of the speed delivered to the subscriber;
      (c) Conduct regular network audits and quality-of-service tests, and
          publish at least monthly on its website the results per provider and per
          region, in a format that can easily be analyzed by the public, together
          with the list of providers found to have violated this Act; and
      (d) Review the threshold speeds and other quality-of-service standards
          annually and upgrade them as necessary to bring them at par with
          regional and international benchmarks: Provided, That the NTC shall
          not lower the threshold speeds set in this Act: Provided, further, That
          new standards shall be published at least thirty (30) days before they
          take effect.

      The performance standards issued by the NTC under Republic Act No.
12234 shall not be lower than the minimum standards set in this Act. Where the
performance standards issued under Republic Act No. 12234 are higher than
those set in this Act, the higher standards shall prevail.

      SECTION 7. Rights of Subscribers. – In addition to the rights provided
under existing laws, including Section 25 of Republic Act No. 12234, every
internet subscriber shall have the right to:

      (a) Receive internet service that is reliable, non-discriminatory, and
          compliant with the standards set under this Act and by the NTC;
      (b) Have service installed within thirty (30) days from application, and
          receive a full refund of any installation fee and advance payment if the
          provider fails to do so;
      (c) Receive, without need of demand, the timely correction of billing errors
          and a pro-rated rebate for any service interruption lasting more than
          twenty-four (24) continuous hours or for an aggregate of seventy-two
          (72) hours within a billing period, which rebate shall be credited in the
          next billing statement;
      (d) Terminate the subscription without pre-termination fees or penalties,
          and with a refund of any advance payment for the unused period,
          when the provider repeatedly fails to comply with Sections 4 and 5 of
          this Act or with the performance standards issued under Republic Act
          No. 12234;
      (e) Have complaints on slow internet speed, service interruption, and false
          advertising of speeds acted upon through an expeditious complaint
          mechanism to be established by the NTC, under which providers shall
          act on a complaint within seven (7) days from receipt, and the NTC
          shall resolve any complaint not so acted upon within thirty (30) days
          from its filing; and
      (f) Receive a reduction of the monthly fee, in proportion to the shortfall,
          for any billing period during which the provider fails to comply with
          Section 5 of this Act, as established by the measurements of the NTC
          or by test results under Section 6(b) of this Act.

      SECTION 8. Penalties. – The NTC, upon complaint, and after due notice
and hearing, shall require any provider that violates Sections 4, 5, or 7 of this
Act to comply with the standards not met, and shall impose the following
penalties:

      (a) For failure to comply with Section 4 or 5 of this Act, which shall be
          deemed a failure to comply with the minimum performance standards,
          the penalties under Section 21(1) of Republic Act No. 12234, which
          shall be imposed for each day of default;
      (b) For advertising speeds in violation of Section 5 of this Act, which shall
          constitute false advertising under Section 20(c) of Republic Act No.
          12234, the penalties under Section 21(3) thereof, without prejudice to
          the penalties under Republic Act No. 7394, otherwise known as the
          “Consumer Act of the Philippines”;
      (c) For violation of Section 7 of this Act, the penalties under Section 21(5)
          of Republic Act No. 12234; and
      (d) For repeated violations, the penalties under Section 21(1)(b) and
          Section 21(4) of Republic Act No. 12234: Provided, That upon a second
          finding of violation of Section 4 or 5 of this Act within a period of twelve
          (12) months, the NTC shall order the provider to stop accepting new
         subscribers in the affected service area until it demonstrates
         compliance.

       The foregoing shall be without prejudice to other remedies available under
existing laws, including the right of subscribers to claim damages.

      SECTION 9. Relation to Republic Act No. 12234. – This Act shall be
construed as supplementing Republic Act No. 12234. Nothing in this Act shall
be construed to diminish the rights of users under Section 25 of the said Act, or
to delay, suspend, or lower the performance standards, penalties, and other
obligations of providers under the said Act. Any doubt in the interpretation of
this Act shall be resolved in favor of the subscriber.

       SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DICT and the NTC shall, after consultation
with consumer organizations and civil society organizations, jointly issue the
rules and regulations necessary for the implementation of this Act. The NTC shall
submit an annual report on the implementation of this Act, including the
compliance of each provider, the complaints received and resolved, and the
penalties imposed, to the appropriate committees of the Senate and the House
of Representatives.

       SECTION 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts not affected
shall remain in full force and effect.

       SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules, and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
