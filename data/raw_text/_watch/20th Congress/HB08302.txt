                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Constitution mandates the State to protect and promote the right
  to health and to prioritize the needs of the underprivileged sick. Despite
  existing government programs, many indigent Filipinos continue to rely
  on the voluntary services of physicians who render pro bono medical care,
  particularly in public hospitals and underserved communities.

      This bill seeks to encourage such voluntary medical services by
  granting physicians an additional allowable deduction from gross
  income, consistent with the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as
  amended. The measure adopts a deduction-based incentive—not a tax
  credit—to ensure fiscal prudence and alignment with existing tax
  principles.

       To safeguard government revenues and prevent abuse, the bill
  imposes a statutory ceiling on the allowable deduction, requires proper
  documentation and certification by government health facilities, limits
  eligibility to services personally rendered, and prohibits double benefits
  for services already reimbursed under any government program.

      By providing a calibrated and accountable incentive mechanism, this
  measure strengthens private sector participation in delivering free
  medical services to indigent patients while maintaining fiscal
  responsibility.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                “AN ACT
    GIVING TAX INCENTIVES TO PHYSICIANS RENDERING PRO
               BONO SERVICES TO INDIGENT PATIENTS”
        Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
 Philippines in Congress assembled:

 SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Physician Pro
 Bono Care Act.”

 SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect
 and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
 consciousness among them. The State shall adopt an integrated and
 comprehensive approach to health development and shall endeavor to
 make essential goods, health, and other social services available to all
 people at affordable cost. There shall be priority for the needs of the
 underprivileged sick, elderly, women, and children. Consistent with Article
 II, Section 15 and Article XIII, Section 11 of the Constitution, the State
 shall encourage private sector participation in the delivery of free medical
 services to indigent patients through fiscally responsible incentive
 mechanisms consistent with the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997,
 as amended.

 SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

    (a)   “Pro bono medical services” shall refer to professional medical
          services personally rendered by a physician without
          compensation or expectation of compensation;

    (b)   “Indigent patient” shall refer to a person certified as indigent by
          the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), the
          local social welfare and development office, or any other
          competent government authority pursuant to existing laws and
          regulations;
   (c)   “Reasonable value of services” shall refer to the customary
         professional fee for similar medical services in the locality, as
         determined under guidelines to be issued by the Bureau of
         Internal Revenue (BIR), upon recommendation of the
         Department of Health (DOH).

SECTION 4. Additional Allowable Deduction for Pro Bono Medical
Services. – Physicians duly licensed by the Professional Regulation
Commission (PRC) who voluntarily render pro bono medical services to
indigent patients shall be entitled to claim an additional allowable
deduction from gross income equivalent to a percentage of the reasonable
value of the services actually rendered during the taxable year, as may be
prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations: Provided, That such
deduction shall in no case exceed twenty percent (20%) of the physician’s
gross income derived from the practice of profession during the same
taxable year, in accordance with the National Internal Revenue Code of
1997, as amended.

     The deduction shall be allowed only upon submission of proper
documentation and certification issued by a government hospital, rural
health unit, or other DOH-accredited health facility where the service was
rendered.

     Only services personally rendered by the physician shall qualify.
Expenses for medicines, supplies, hospital facilities, equipment use, or
other incidental costs shall not be included in the computation of the
deductible amount.

     No double benefit shall be allowed. Services that are reimbursed,
compensated, or subsidized under any government program, including
PhilHealth or similar schemes, shall not qualify for the deduction under
this Act.

     The additional deduction provided herein shall be separate from and
in addition to ordinary and necessary business deductions allowed under
the National Internal Revenue Code, but shall not be treated as a tax
credit.

SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR),
in consultation with the Department of Finance (DOF), the Department of
Health (DOH), and the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), shall
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act, including valuation standards, documentation
requirements, compliance monitoring, and anti-abuse safeguards.
SECTION 6. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, presidential proclamations, rules and regulations or parts thereof
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 8. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
