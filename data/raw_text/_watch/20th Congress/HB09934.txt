                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

The state’s duty to protect its citizens is paramount, yet a deeply concerning trend
is challenging the peace of our local communities. Schools and public spaces—
traditionally reserved as safe havens for learning and youth development—have
increasingly become the stages for unexpected, lethal acts of violence carried out
by minors.

Most recently, on June 22, 2026, the entire nation was shocked by a horrifying
mass shooting at San Jose National High School in Tacloban City. In that
incident, two heavily armed teenagers—aged 14 and 15—opened fire inside active
classrooms with a Glock 9mm semi-automatic pistol and a .38 caliber revolver,
killing three innocent students and wounding several others. This tragic event
does not stand alone; it follows a string of violent student-on-student offenses
nationwide, including a severe multi-victim knife attack just a week prior in
Cavite.

While Republic Act No. 9344, otherwise known as the "Juvenile Justice and
Welfare Act of 2006," was originally enacted with the noble intent of protecting
the rights and rehabilitation of minors, the evolving landscape of juvenile
delinquency requires a firm reassessment. Present-day minors have
unprecedented access to sophisticated weapons, criminal networks, and modern
communication.

When a 14-year-old or 15-year-old demonstrates the tactical capacity to load,
reload, and deploy military-grade firearms inside a crowded classroom, treating
them with absolute exemption from criminal prosecution creates a dangerous
legal vacuum. It deprives victims' families of meaningful justice and fosters an
environment of impunity.

This Bill introduces two critical, targeted updates to the law:

   1. Lowering the Minimum Age of Criminal Responsibility (MACR): It lowers the
      baseline from 15 to 12 years of age, but ensures safeguards by mandating
      a strict legal determination of discernment (the capacity to distinguish right
      from wrong and understand its consequences).

   2. Absolute Exclusion for Heinous and Gun-Related Mass Violences: It
      declares that any minor, from age 12 up to below 18, who commits an act
      of mass murder, severe gun violence, or heinous offenses inside or near
      educational institutions, shall automatically be excluded from absolute
      exemption. Instead, they will be subjected to the standard, specialized
      prosecution under a modernized family court system, while maintaining
      high-security rehabilitation under specialized youth facilities (Bahay Pag-
      asa).

We must strike a vital balance: we must remain dedicated to reforming troubled
youth, but we can no longer allow the law to act as an unyielding shield for those
who commit horrific acts of violence against their peers.

In view of the urgent need to secure our schools and restore public safety, the
immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9344, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
  THE "JUVENILE JUSTICE AND WELFARE ACT OF 2006," AS AMENDED,
   LOWERING THE AGE OF CRIMINAL LIABILITY, REMOVING CRIMINAL
 EXEMPTIONS FOR HEINOUS CRIMES AND MASS VIOLENCE COMMITTED
       WITHIN ACADEMIC INSTITUTIONS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Safe Schools and
Juvenile Accountability Act of 2026."

     SEC. 2. Amendment. – Section 6 of Republic Act No. 9344, as amended, is
hereby further amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 6. Minimum Age of Responsibility. – A child BELOW TWELVE (12)
      years of age at the time of the commission of the offense shall be exempt
      from criminal liability. However, the child shall be subjected to an
      intervention program pursuant to Section 20 of this Act.
      "A child TWELVE (12) YEARS OF AGE AND ABOVE but below eighteen
      (18) years of age shall likewise be exempt from criminal liability and be
      subjected to an intervention program, UNLESS the child has acted with
      DISCERNMENT, in which case, such child shall be subjected to the
      appropriate proceedings in accordance with this Act and the Revised Penal
      Code.
      "PROVIDED, HOWEVER, THAT THE EXEMPTION FROM CRIMINAL
      LIABILITY ESTABLISHED IN THIS ACT SHALL NOT APPLY TO ANY
      CHILD TWELVE (12) TO SEVENTEEN (17) YEARS OF AGE WHO
      COMMITS MURDER, PARRICIDE, INFANTICIDE, RAPE, DESTRUCTIVE
      ARSON, ROBBERY WITH HOMICIDE, OR ANY ACT OF MASS VIOLENCE
      AND SHOOTING UTILIZING FIREARMS OR DEADLY WEAPONS
      COMMITTED WITHIN OR IN THE IMMEDIATE VICINITY OF SCHOOL
      PREMISES, EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS, AND PLAYGROUNDS. SUCH
      JUVENILE OFFENDERS SHALL AUTOMATICALLY BE PROSECUTED
      UNDER THE SPECIALIZED JUVENILE JUSTICE SYSTEM."

      SEC. 3. Amendment. – Section 20-A of Republic Act No. 9344, as amended,
is hereby further amended to read as follows:

       "SEC. 20-A. Serious and Mass Crimes Committed by Children. – A child who
is twelve (12) years of age up to below eighteen (18) years of age and who commits
heinous crimes as defined under existing laws, or who perpetrates acts of gun
violence resulting in multiple casualties inside learning spaces, shall be
immediately placed under involuntary commitment in the Intensive Juvenile
Intervention and Support Center (IJISC) within the nearest Bahay Pag-asa.

       "The transfer to a specialized high-security youth facility shall be swift,
taking place within twenty-four (24) hours from custody to guarantee public
safety and the emotional well-being of the surviving community, while awaiting
legal trials in proper Family Courts."

       Sec.4. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      Sec. 5. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, presidential
issuances, and other administrative rules and regulations or parts thereof which
are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

      Sec. 6. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
