                                AN ACT
   AMENDING SECTIONS 15, 19, 29, 30, AND 31 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 12001,
     OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “REAL PROPERTY VALUATION AND
                   ASSESSMENT REFORM ACT”



                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Real property taxation is a vital and stable source of local government revenue. To ensure
that property assessments remain fair, transparent, and reflective of prevailing market
conditions, Republic Act No. 12001, otherwise known as the “Real Property Valuation and
Assessment Reform Act” (RPVARA), established a systematic and evidence-based
framework for real property valuation.

This measure does not seek to repeal or weaken the RPVARA. Rather, it recognizes that
its successful implementation must consider prevailing economic conditions and the
readiness of implementing institutions. The continuing geopolitical tensions in the Middle
East have contributed to global market uncertainties and volatility in fuel and commodity
prices. At the same time, the Philippines has recently endured successive typhoons,
flooding, earthquakes, and other natural calamities that have disrupted livelihoods,
damaged property, and strained the financial capacity of many Filipino families and local
government units (LGUs).

Under these circumstances, a measured transition in the implementation of RPVARA is
both prudent and necessary. Beyond mitigating the immediate financial impact on
taxpayers, it will provide local government units (LGUs) sufficient time to complete the
technical, institutional, and administrative requirements necessary to implement the law
effectively and uniformly.

Preparing a Schedule of Market Values (SMV), which serves as the foundation of the
valuation system under RPVARA, is a highly technical undertaking requiring extensive
data gathering, validation of property transactions, market analysis, stakeholder
consultations, and compliance with national valuation standards. Although the RPVARA
has already taken effect, several implementing mechanisms were issued later than
anticipated, while consultations with LGUs reveal varying levels of preparedness in terms
of technical expertise, staffing, market data, digital infrastructure, and institutional
capacity. These circumstances warrant additional time to ensure the proper
implementation of the law.

The readiness of LGUs is particularly important because approved SMVs affect not only
real property taxation but also various taxes, fees, and charges under existing laws.
Premature implementation may result in inconsistent valuations, administrative disputes,
and unintended financial burdens on taxpayers, including senior citizens and other
property owners who rely on fixed or limited incomes.

Accordingly, this measure aims to provide a reasonable transition period by postponing
the tax-impacting implementation of approved SMVs, while allowing their preparation,
updating, review, validation, calibration, harmonization, approval, and publication to
continue in accordance with the law. This approach preserves the objectives of RPVARA
while ensuring that its implementation proceeds on a sound, uniform, and equitable basis.

By providing LGUs sufficient time to prepare and by mitigating the immediate impact of
updated property valuations on taxpayers, especially senior citizens, this measure seeks
to strengthen the implementation of RPVARA while promoting a transparent, credible,
equitable, and sustainable real property valuation system.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
