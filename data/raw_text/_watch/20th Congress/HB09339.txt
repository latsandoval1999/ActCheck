20                                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   Agricultural cooperatives play a vital role in strengthening food security, increasing
23   farmers’ incomes, and promoting inclusive rural development. Under Republic Act No.
24   9520, otherwise known as the “Philippine Cooperative Code of 2008,” cooperatives are
25   recognized as instruments for improving the quality of life of their members and
26   advancing economic and social development.
27
28   Despite their importance, many agricultural cooperatives continue to face challenges
29   such as limited access to financing, inadequate institutional support, low productivity,
30   and insufficient market integration. These difficulties hinder the growth and
31   competitiveness of the agricultural sector, particularly among small farmers and
32   marginalized rural communities.
33
34   This bill seeks to institutionalize stronger government support mechanisms for
35   agricultural cooperatives by promoting their development, modernization, and
36   protection. It aims to enhance their capacity through support structures, training,
37   inclusive growth programs, and improved access to opportunities that will empower
38   farmers, women, and youth participation in the agricultural economy.
39
40   Through this measure, the State recognizes agricultural cooperatives as essential
41   partners in achieving sustainable agricultural development, poverty reduction, and
42   national food security.
43
44   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

29                                                 CHAPTER I
30                                            DECLARATION OF POLICY
31
32   SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Agricultural Cooperatives
33   Act”.
34
35   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared to be the policy of the State, in
36   line with the Constitution, that expanding productivity is the key to raising the quality of
37   life for all, especially the underprivileged, together with a sustained increase in the
38   amount of goods and services produced by the nation for the benefit of the people.
39
40   In line with this, the State recognizes the need to hasten the growth, integration, and
41   modernization of the agricultural sector in order to meet the rising dilemma of food
42   insecurity, which disproportionately affects the marginalized sectors of society. The
43   State further recognizes the indispensable need to encourage the participation of
44   women and youth as a primary force in the government's drive to empower the
45   agricultural sector. Therefore, the State shall focus on establishing a structure and
46   network that institutionalizes support, capacity-building, and inclusive growth for
47   agricultural cooperatives.
 1   Similarly, it is the declared policy of the State to foster the creation and growth of
 2   cooperatives as a practical vehicle for promoting self-reliance and harnessing people
 3   power towards the attainment of economic development and social justice. Towards
 4   this end, the Government and all of its branches, subdivisions, instrumentalities, and
 5   agencies shall ensure the provision of technical guidance, financial assistance, and
 6   other relevant services to enable farmers, fisherfolk, and other agricultural workers to
 7   organize themselves into self-sustaining cooperatives that work together to strengthen
 8   the Philippine agricultural industry.

10                                     CHAPTER II
11                                 GENERAL PROVISIONS
12
13   SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall mean:
14
15         (a) Agribusiness - refers to a range of activities that occur in the agricultural
16             value chain utilized in modern food production, including but not limited to
17             farming, forestry, fishing operations, as well as seed supply, agrichemicals,
18             related machineries, processing, storage, marketing, distribution, and sales,
19             whether wholesale or retail;
20         (b) Agricultural Cooperative - refers to a type of cooperative comprising farmers,
21             fisherfolk, agrarian reform beneficiaries, dairy farmers, community forestry
22             farmers, aquaculture farmers, and other individuals engaged in agricultural
23             activities. These individuals voluntarily organized themselves into an
24             agricultural-based economic enterprise, including subsidiary agricultural
25             cooperatives formed by existing multi-purpose cooperatives and multi-
26             purpose cooperatives whose primary business activity is at least fifty percent
27             (50%) related to agricultural production, agro-industry, agri-business, or
28             services related to agricultural production. Agricultural activities
29             encompassed by the cooperative include the production, processing,
30             agrologistics, and distribution of agricultural, fishery, and forestry products.
31             The cooperative also provides a range of services aimed at improving the
32             livelihood, income, and overall welfare of its members. These services
33             include savings and credit, marketing, agricultural extension, farm
34             education, agri-tourism, digital agriculture, and other allied services related
35             to climate resiliency and safety nets;
36         (c) Asset Development - refers to the growth and improvement, according to
37             targeted goals and objectives, of key instruments and resources for
38             agricultural operations, such as, but not limited to, land, buildings, which may
39             include residences, warehouses, or barns, irrigation systems, fences, and
40             trees;
41         (d) Build Scale - refers to the growth or expansion of agribusiness in a
42             proportional and profitable manner;
43         (e) Cooperative - refers to an autonomous and duly registered association of
44             persons, with a common bond of interest, who have voluntarily joined
45             together to achieve their social, economic, and cultural needs and
 1              aspirations by making equitable contributions to the capital required,
 2              patronizing their products and services, and accepting a fair share of the
 3              risks and benefits of the undertaking in accordance with universally accepted
 4              cooperative principles;
 5          (f) Clustering - refers to the process by which multiple agribusinesses, each
 6              involved in different stages of the agricultural value chain, combine their
 7              operations into a single hub. This collaboration aims to enhance operational
 8              efficiency, streamline processes, and maximize profits through integrated
 9              efforts;
10          (g) Criteria for Assessment and Ratings - refers to a set of standards in
11              determining that the performance of an agricultural cooperative, which
12              includes, among others, enterprise management, adherence to cooperative
13              principles, and product or service quality;
14          (h) Family Farming - refers to an agribusiness operation that is managed and
15              operated by a family unit, related by consanguinity or affinity, whether
16              nuclear or extended, and which predominantly relies on the labor of the
17              members of such family for its operations. The family and the farm, therefore,
18              are inextricably linked, and co-evolve with each other;
19          (i) Local Development Hub (LDH) - refers to a farming zone or transport
20              infrastructure, which is generally far from formal markets. An LDH serves
21              around five hundred (500) to one thousand (1,000) farmers, and links them
22              to buyers, which may be, among others, traders, processors, and export
23              companies, among others.
24          (j) National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC) - refers to a
25              federation of agricultural cooperatives that are engaged in agribusiness and
26              financial business for the benefit of the members through its subsidiaries.
27          (k) Wholesale Market - refers to a market, owned by the government and
28              managed by the National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC)
29              through its subsidiary/ies, where a trader buys farm produce from the
30              farmers, through the agricultural cooperatives, in bulk and re-sells the goods
31              to business houses or retailers to further sell the goods to end consumers.
32          (l) Cooperative Climate Resilience Fund - refers to a grant-making mechanism
33              that aims to ensure that agricultural cooperatives and their farmer-members
34              are protected from and resilient to the impact of climate change.

35   SEC. 4. Registration and Supervision. - Agricultural Cooperatives shall be governed
36   by the general law on cooperatives, except as otherwise provided herein. In connection
37   with this, agricultural cooperatives must be registered with the Cooperative
38   Development Authority (CDA) in order to be entitled to the privileges and exemptions
39   extended to cooperatives under Republic Act (RA) No. 9520, otherwise known as the
40   Philippine Cooperative Code of 2008.
41
42   An agricultural cooperative shall file its application for registration with the CDA regional
43   office that has jurisdiction over its area of operation. No fees shall be assessed or
 1   charged to any agricultural cooperative by any government entity, national or local, at
 2   any stage of its organization and registration process.
 3
 4   This notwithstanding, as the agency with the mandate to promote agricultural
 5   development by providing policy framework, public investments, and support services,
 6   the Department of Agriculture (DA) shall be the regulatory agency exercising primary
 7   supervision over the National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC). The
 8   Federation shall exercise supervision and control over its member-primary agricultural
 9   сооperatives.
10
11   SEC. 5. Capital Requirements of Agricultural Cooperatives. - The authorized share
12   capital of an agricultural cooperative shall be stated in its Articles of Cooperation. Prior
13   to its registration, at least twenty-five percent (25%) of the authorized share capital shall
14   be subscribed to by the members, and at least twenty-five percent (25%) of the
15   subscribed share capital shall be paid-up: Provided, That the paid-up capitalization
16   requirement for primary cooperatives shall not be less than Fifteen Thousand Pesos
17   (P15,000.00).
18
19   SEC. 6. Cooperative Business Activities and Allied Services. - A duly registered
20   agricultural cooperative may engage in all or any of the following activities:
21          a. Raising and/or culture of plants, high-value crops, animals, fish, poultry,
22              livestock, other agri-products as source of food, feed, and fiber, and other
23              agri-fishery or non-food commodities;
24          b. Production of salt;
25          c. Community and social forestry;
26          d. Provision of savings and credit facilities for marginal farmers and fishermen;
27          e. Facilitating the procurement and distribution of farm inputs/implements for its
28              members;
29          f. Processing, marketing, and sale of members' agri-products or produce;
30          g. Maintenance and management of irrigation systems, water impounding, and
31              other agricultural facilities; and
32          h. Other allied services such as, but not limited to, warehousing, cold storage
33              and other post-harvest services, hauling, transport of members' products or
34              produce, and such other similar business services and activities that will
35              reduce cost or add value.

36   SEC. 7. Bond of Membership. - The bond of membership of agricultural cooperatives
37   shall be any of the following:
38          a. Residential - members are farmers, farm workers, or fisherfolk who are
39             actually and physically residing within the area of operation;
40          b. Occupational - members are those who are engaged in raising or culture of
41             plants or animals, or are part of the technical and scientific communities of
42             the agriculture sector;
 1          c. Associational - members are from registered associations, groups, clubs,
 2             cultural and other similar aggrupation of farmers, farm workers, and fisherfolk;
 3             and
 4          d. Institutional - members are employees, workers, or officers of a particular
 5             institution who are undertaking or will undertake agricultural projects.
 6             Provided, that at least fifty-one per centum (51%) of its members are duly
 7             registered with the Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA).

12   SEC. 8. Tax Treatment of Agricultural Cooperatives that Transact with
13   Nonmembers. - Notwithstanding any law or regulation to the contrary, duly registered
14   agricultural cooperatives that transact with both members and non-members, and with
15   reserve funds of One Hundred Million Pesos (P100,000,000.00) or below shall be
16   exempt from all national internal revenue taxes, fees, and charges of whatever name
17   and nature.
18
19   On the other hand, if such agricultural cooperatives have reserve funds of greater than
20   One Hundred Million Pesos (P100,000,000.00) shall pay the following taxes on their
21   transactions with non-members:
22         a. Income Tax on income derived from transactions with non-members:
23             Provided, That the same tax is not consequently imposed on interest
24             individually received by members. The tax base for all cooperatives liable to
25             pay income tax shall be the income directly related from the business
26             transactions with non-members after deducting the amounts for the statutory
27             funds;
28         b. Value-Added Tax (VAT) on transactions with non-members: Provided,
29             however, that duly registered agricultural cooperatives shall be exempt from
30             the imposition of VAT, on the following:
31                         i. Sales by agricultural cooperatives duly registered with the DA
32                            to their members as well as sale of their produce, whether in its
33                            original state or processed form, to non-members; their
34                            importation of direct farm inputs, machineries and equipment,
35                            including spare parts thereof, to be used directly and
36                            exclusively in the production and/or processing of their
37                            produce, and their resale to other agricultural cooperatives;
38                        ii. Gross receipts from lending activities towards its members or
39                            other agricultural cooperatives;
40                            Provided, That the exempt transactions in the foregoing shall
41                            include sales made by agricultural cooperatives duly registered
42                            with the DA organized and operated by their members to
43                            undertake the production and processing of raw materials or of
44                            goods produced by their members into finished or process
 1                            products for sale by the cooperatives to their members and non-
 2                            members: Provided, however, that any processed product or its
 3                            derivative arising from the raw materials produced by their
 4                            members, sold in the name and for the account of the
 5                            cooperative shall be deemed a product of the cooperative and
 6                            its members: Provided, further, That at least twenty five per
 7                            centum (25%) of the net income of the cooperatives is returned
 8                            to the members in the form of interest and/or patronage
 9                            refunds.
10          c. All sales of goods and/or services rendered for non-members shall be subject
11             to the applicable percentage taxes; and
12          d. All other taxes unless otherwise provided herein.

13   Donations to duly accredited charitable, research and educational institutions and
14   reinvestment to socioeconomic projects within the area of operation of the сooperative
15   may be tax deductible.
16
17   SEC. 9. Tax Treatment of Agricultural Cooperatives that Transact with Members
18   Only. - Duly registered agricultural cooperatives under this Act which do not transact
19   any business with non-members or the general public shall not be subject to any taxes,
20   fees, and charges imposed under the internal revenue laws and other tax laws.
21
22   SEC. 10. Other Benefits and Privileges of Agricultural Cooperatives. - An
23   agricultural cooperative shall enjoy the following benefits and privileges:
24          a. Taxes, Duties, and Liens:
25                     (1) All benefits, privileges, and tax treatments of cooperatives as
26                         defined and enumerated under RA 9520 and other relevant laws
27                         shall apply to agricultural cooperatives;
28                     (2) Agricultural cooperatives shall be exempt from customs duties,
29                         advance sales or compensating taxes on their importation and
30                         local purchase of machineries, equipment, accessories, batteries,
31                         and spare parts used by them: Provided, That all tax- and duty-
32                         free imports and local purchases shall not be sold nor the beneficial
33                         ownership thereof be transferred to any person until after five (5)
34                         years, otherwise, the cooperative and the transferee or assignee
35                         shall be solidarily liable to pay twice the amount of the imposed tax
36                         and/or duties; and
37                     (3) All real properties owned, and those directly used for the
38                         operations, management, and maintenance of agricultural
39                         cooperatives shall be exempt from the payment of real property
40                         taxes imposed under existing laws.
41          b. Trainings and Conferences - All trainings and conferences necessary for the
42              organization, registration, management and operations of an agricultural
43              cooperative, rendered by the appropriate government agency, shall be given
44              free of charge, including costs of venue, training materials, speakers and
 1            resource persons. The CDOs shall be primarily responsible for these
 2            trainings and conferences within their respective localities.
 3         c. Consolidation - Agricultural cooperatives may consolidate agricultural lands
 4            owned by its members within in a contiguous area for purposes of agricultural
 5            development.
 6         d. Direct Purchase by National and Local Government Agencies - To promote
 7            and support agricultural cooperatives, national and local government
 8            agencies shall directly purchase at least fifty percent (50%) of their targeted
 9            agricultural products from accredited agricultural cooperatives; Provided,
10            That said products are necessary in the performance of their respective
11            mandates.
12            Further, in areas where there are agricultural cooperatives, at least fifty per
13            centum (50%) of the supply to government institutions, agencies, and LGUs,
14            of rice, corn, grains, fish and other marine products, meat, eggs, milk,
15            vegetables, and other agricultural commodities such as fertilizers, seeds and
16            other agricultural inputs and implements, and related products, shall be
17            sourced from such cooperatives.
18            For the purposes of this Act, the procurement by national and local
19            government agencies of said agricultural products shall be exempt from the
20            application of the bidding process prescribed under relevant government
21            procurement laws; Provided, That said agencies shall undergo a negotiated
22            procurement under the applicable guidelines of the Government
23            Procurement Policy Board.
24         e. Loans and Grants:
25                    (1) All loans applied for by agricultural cooperatives in any
26                        government-owned or -controlled banks or government financial
27                        institutions shall enjoy preferential treatment as to the
28                        requirements, rates, securities, and collaterals; and
29                    (2) The DA shall coordinate with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP)
30                        to lower the requirements for all loans applied for and granted to
31                        agricultural cooperatives.
32         f. Strengthening Program for Agricultural Cooperatives - the government
33            through the DA and CDA shall introduce a program aimed at strengthening
34            agricultural cooperatives through mergers and consolidations. This initiative,
35            part of the government's broader effort to enhance the stability and resilience
36            of the agriculture sector, offers income support incentives to encourage
37            smaller agricultural cooperatives and farmers associations to consolidate that
38            can lead to improved operational efficiencies, reducing costs and improving
39            service delivery for agricultural cooperatives. These may include grants or
40            subsidies that help build business scale and improve capital base,
41            operational synergies, broader service offerings, and risk mitigation.

42
43   SEC. 11. Organization as a Multi-Purpose Cooperative. - Notwithstanding any law
44   to the contrary, an agricultural cooperative may be organized as a multi-purpose
 1   cooperative from its registration with the CDA: Provided, that it has complied with the
 2   minimum requirements for an agricultural cooperative as prescribed by the CDA Rules
 3   and Regulations.
 4
 5   SEC. 12. Pre-Cooperative Agricultural Organization. - Prior to registration with the
 6   CDA, a group of natural persons intending to form an agriculture cooperative may
 7   voluntarily form a Pre-Cooperative Agricultural Organization (PCAO) that shall have its
 8   own juridical personality to undertake activities that shall determine the viability of the
 9   creation of an agricultural cooperative. The PCAO must be registered as such with the
10   Cooperative Development Offices (CDOs) of the appropriate LGU where the PCAO
11   intends to operate. The Bureau may assist and endorse PCAOs to CDA for registration.
12
13   The DA and CDA, in coordination with the CDOs, shall assist PCAOs so that they can
14   formally organize as agricultural cooperatives; Provided, that should the PCAO decide
15   to formally organize into a cooperative, it must comply with the requirements under the
16   law. Any contract executed by the PCAO prior to its registration as an agricultural
17   cooperative shall remain valid and binding between the parties insofar as it redounds
18   to the benefit of the cooperative.
19
20   The DA and CDA shall jointly issue guidelines on the mechanisms for PCAOs.

22                               CHAPTER IV
23            NATIONAL FEDERATION OF AGRICULTURAL COOPERATIVES
24
25   SEC. 13. Organization of the National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives
26   (NFAC). - There is hereby organized the National Federation of Agricultural
27   Cooperatives (NFAC), (hereinafter, the "Federation"), which shall be the single
28   federation of agricultural cooperatives.
29
30   SEC. 14. Consolidation/Merger of Existing Duly-Registered Agricultural
31   Cooperatives Federation. - Agricultural Cooperative Federations duly-registered with
32   the CDA prior to the effectivity of this Act shall merge or consolidate in accordance with
33   existing applicable laws, rules and regulations. The merged or consolidated federation
34   shall serve as the single National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC).
35
36   The DA and CDA shall issue the guidelines on the merger and/or consolidation of
37   existing federations of agricultural cooperatives.
38
39   SEC. 15. Mandatory Membership of Primary Agricultural Cooperatives to the
40   Federation. - It is compulsory for Primary Agricultural Cooperatives to become
41   members of the National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC).
42
43   SEC. 16. Governance of the Federation. - The Chairpersons of the member primary
44   agricultural cooperatives shall comprise the General Assembly of the Federation who
 1   shall elect and appoint the Officers and Management Staff in accordance with its duly-
 2   approved Articles of Cooperation and By-Laws.
 3
 4   Sec. 17. Primary Business Operations Through Subsidiary/ies. - The Federation
 5   shall be allowed to organize subsidiary cooperatives and/or corporations that will
 6   engage in Agribusiness and Financial Business the surplus and/or income of which
 7   shall be utilized to fund, wholly or in part, the needed services of the member-primary
 8   agricultural cooperatives. Such subsidiary/ies shall be registered with the necessary
 9   regulatory authorities including, but not limiting to, the DA, CDA, SEC and Board of
10   Investments and shall enjoy the relevant exemptions and privileges under this Act, the
11   Cooperative Code, and other laws, rules and regulations.
12
13   Sec. 18. Channel And/Or Partner of Farmer-Related Government and Non-
14   Government Programs, Projects and Activities. - The Federation shall serve as
15   channel and/or partner in the implementation of various Programs, Projects and
16   Activities of National Government Agencies, Local Government Units and Non-
17   Government Organizations for the benefit of the farmers and their respective families.
18
19   Sec. 19. Inter-Cooperative Arrangements. - The Federation shall be allowed to enter
20   into inter-cooperative arrangements with and among other non-agriculture cooperative
21   federations and other entities to pursue value and supply chain solutions and other
22   agribusiness development programs, subject to the guidelines to be jointly promulgated
23   by the DA and CDA.
24
25   Sec. 20. Partnership and Joint Venture with Private Entities. - Duly registered
26   private entities may enter into a partnership or joint venture with the Federation in order
27   to provide the latter with investments and technical support, such as, but not limited to
28   technological, logistics, and facilities support, among others. Such partnership or joint
29   venture shall be registered with the necessary regulatory authorities, including the DA
30   and CDA, and shall enjoy the relevant exemptions and privileges under this Act, the
31   Cooperative Code, and other laws, rules and regulations. This notwithstanding, the
32   equity or contribution of such private entity shall not exceed forty percent (40%) of the
33   partnership or joint venture.
34
35   Sec. 21. Supervision and Control. - The DA shall exercise supervision and control
36   over the Federation. In like manner, the Federation shall exercise supervision and
37   control over its member-primary agricultural cooperatives through the conduct of regular
38   audit/s in accordance with the standards approved by the DA and the CDA. Sanctions
39   imposed on non-compliant member-primary cooperatives shall be approved by the DA
40   and CDA.
41
42   The DA and CDA shall, in consultation with the Federation, issue guidelines on the
43   supervision and control of agricultural cooperatives.

23                                 CHAPTER VII
24                     BUREAU OF AGRICULTURAL COOPERATIVES
25
26   Sec. 24. Creation of the Bureau of Agricultural Cooperatives. - The DA shall
27   establish a Bureau of Agricultural Cooperatives (hereafter, the "Bureau"), which shall
28   be the primary coordinating, implementing, and administrative body responsible for
29   matters relating to agricultural cooperatives, including, but not limited to, monitoring
30   their performance and implementing plans and programs for their growth and
31   development. The Bureau shall be headed by a qualified Director, and its organizational
32   and staffing pattern shall be determined by the DA, in accordance with the rules set
33   forth by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM).
34
35   Sec. 25. Powers and Functions of the Bureau of Agricultural Cooperatives. - The
36   Bureau of Agricultural Cooperatives shall exercise the following powers and functions:
37         a. Collaborate and coordinate with various government agencies, institutions,
38             and units for the development and implementation of projects and plans for
39             agricultural cooperatives;
40         b. Promote the use of digital platforms and innovative technologies for the
41             growth and development of agricultural cooperatives, in coordination with the
42             relevant agencies;
43         c. Consolidate and study all relevant information regarding agricultural
44             cooperatives that shall be used in the formulation of policies and programs
45             for the sector;
 1         d. Develop advocacy programs for capacity-building and further education of
 2            members of agricultural cooperatives, with a particular focus on women and
 3            youth;
 4         e. Provide technical assistance to agricultural cooperatives;
 5         f. In partnership with the NFAC, establish Local Development Hubs (LDHs) in
 6            order to foster cooperation, collaboration, and mutual support among
 7            agricultural cooperatives, in coordination with the CDA and LGUs;
 8         g. Promote agricultural cooperative clusters and assist in the creation thereof,
 9            as a means to forge strategic partnerships among cooperatives; and
10         h. Monitor and evaluate the performance of agricultural cooperatives according
11            to an established assessment and ratings system.

12   Sec. 26. Sustainability Mapping. - Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act,
13   the Bureau shall undertake a comprehensive nationwide survey in order to assess the
14   agricultural sustainability status of each region and LGU. The findings of such
15   nationwide survey shall serve as the foundation for the formulation of a ten (10)-year
16   Food Security Master Plan. The Master Plan shall identify priority sectors and projects
17   aimed at promoting agricultural development and sustainability throughout the country.
18   The plan shall be designed to enhance food security, improve agricultural productivity,
19   and ensure long-term sustainability of the agricultural sector.
20
21   Sec. 27. Clustering and Consolidation. - In order to pool their resources for increased
22   viability and market competitiveness, the DA, Department of Agrarian Reform (DAR),
23   Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), CDA and NFAC shall jointly establish a
24   program that will promote, encourage agricultural cooperatives and facilitate their
25   cooperation into clusters that will encompass the whole agricultural value chain,
26   including but not limited to land consolidation, postharvest processing, distribution,
27   logistics, marketing, and promotions.
28
29   Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DA, DAR, DTI and CDA, in
30   consultation with the NFAC, shall jointly issue the guidelines for the implementation of
31   the foregoing program, and shall provide the necessary assistance at every step of the
32   clustering and consolidation process, including the formulation of cluster development
33   plans.
34
35   Notwithstanding any law to the contrary, the clustering or merger and consolidation of
36   agricultural cooperatives shall be in accordance with the Implementing Rules and
37   Regulations of this Act.
38
39   Sec. 28. Local Development Hubs. - The DA and CDA shall jointly establish Local
40   Development Hubs (LDHs) in order to strengthen the market linkages between farmers
41   and buyers, fostering agricultural development and economic growth at the local level.
42   Further, they shall jointly issue the guidelines for the operations and maintenance
43   thereof.
 1   Sec. 29. Collaboration with the NFAC, Cooperative Development Councils and
 2   National Government Agencies. - The Bureau shall, in collaboration with the National
 3   Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC), LGUs through the Regional,
 4   Provincial, City, and Municipal Cooperative Development Councils and the CDOs,
 5   facilitate and promote programs and policies that enables the growth and development
 6   of agricultural cooperatives. The DA, CDA, and the relevant LGU or agency in
 7   consultation with NFAC shall issue a Joint Memorandum Circular for the implementation
 8   of this provision. Agricultural cooperatives shall be entitled to financing support from the
 9   relevant government agencies that shall allocate funds for the establishment of projects
10   and programs for the development and promotion of agricultural cooperatives.

12   Sec. 30. Sustainability and Innovation. - The Bureau shall allocate funding and
13   support for the development and implementation of innovative and sustainable
14   agricultural practices.
15
16   Sec. 31. Mentorship and Training. - The Bureau shall implement programs that will
17   focus on the capacity- and capability-building of members of agricultural cooperatives,
18   especially the empowerment of women and young farmers. Among others, these
19   programs may include:
20          a. Internship and apprenticeship programs with duly accredited agricultural
21             cooperatives, in partnership with the Committee on Higher Education
22             (CHED);
23          b. Certification programs for management and operation of agricultural
24             cooperatives, as well as related technical skills, in partnership with the
25             Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA);
26          c. Scholarships or student loan forgiveness, as may be necessary; and
27          d. Incentives for the discovery or development of innovative and sustainable
28             agricultural practices or techniques.

29   Sec. 32. Assessment and Ratings. - Upon the effectivity of this Act, the Bureau shall
30   establish an assessment and ratings system for agricultural cooperatives, which shall
31   measure the performance, including the social and community impact, of the latter using
32   both qualitative and quantitative metrics, and which shall be used to classify and
33   determine the best-performing agricultural cooperatives among various categories.
34   Thereafter, the Bureau and the CDA, in close coordination with NFAC shall review the
35   performance of registered agricultural cooperatives every three (3) years and update
36   their ratings accordingly.
37
38   The DA and CDA, in consultation with NFAC shall use the foregoing in the provision of
39   incentives and benefits to agricultural cooperatives under the law.
40
41   Sec. 33. Annual Trade Fair and Awards. - The Bureau, in coordination with the CDA
42   and the National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives (NFAC), shall establish the
43   Annual Agricultural Cooperatives Trade Fair and Awards to showcase exemplary
44   business achievement, latest innovations, and other emerging trends in the sector. The
 1   Bureau shall ensure that agricultural cooperatives are able to participate and exhibit
 2   their products during said Fair and shall encourage the latter's participation in other
 3   trade fairs and expos.

 5                                 CHAPTER III
 6                      AGRICULTURAL COOPERATIVES COUNCIL
 7
 8   Sec. 34. Creation of the Agricultural Cooperatives Council. - There is hereby
 9   created the Agricultural Cooperatives Council (hereafter, the "Council"), which shall be
10   the primary policy and planning body for the promotion, development, and
11   modernization of agricultural cooperatives and organizations.
12
13   Sec. 35. Composition of the Council. - The Council shall be composed of the following
14   members, or their duly authorized representatives:
15         a. Secretary of Agriculture, as Chairperson;
16         b. Chairperson of the CDA, Vice-Chairperson;
17         c. Undersecretary of the Department of Trade and Industry;
18         d. Undersecretary of the Department of Agrarian Reform;
19         e. Undersecretary of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources;
20         f. Undersecretary of the Department of Education;
21         g. Undersecretary of the Department of Science and Technology;
22         h. Director-General of the National Economic Development Authority;
23         i. Director-General of the TESDA;
24         j. Executive Director of the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management
25             Council;
26         k. Commissioner of the CHED;
27         l. Representative from the Philippine Statistics Authority;
28         m. One (1) Representative each from Cooperative Climate Resiliency Fund
29             private donor entities;
30         n. Representative from the National Federation of Agricultural Cooperatives
31             (NFAC) and
32         o. Two (2) representatives each, from the primary agricultural cooperatives
33             sector who are members of the National Federation of Agricultural
34             Cooperatives (NFAC), for Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao

35   Sec. 36. Powers and Functions of the Council. - The Council shall exercise the
36   following powers and functions:
37          a. Formulate and issue policies for the growth and development of the
38              agricultural cooperative sector, which shall encompass adoption of innovative
39              and ecological agricultural practices, access to resources and benefits,
40              market opportunities, and capacity-building, among others;
41          b. Formulate the Food Security Master Plan, in coordination with the Bureau;
42          c. Provide an integrated framework in order to optimize all government
43              resources for the modernization and empowerment of the sector;
 1         d. Issue guidelines, rules, and regulations for the development of the tangible
 2            assets, such as machineries, granaries, equipment, and lands, among
 3            others, of agricultural cooperatives;
 4         e. Issue guidelines, rules, and regulations, in coordination with the appropriate
 5            agencies, to ensure the rights and welfare of agricultural cooperatives and
 6            their members, especially women, youth, and family farmers, as well as their
 7            preferential treatment in government procurement as enshrined under the
 8            law;
 9         f. Formulate policies for the development of sustainable financial reform in the
10            agricultural cooperative sector, including the creation of a government
11            financial institution that will specifically cater to the sector, in coordination with
12            the relevant departments and agencies;
13         g. Develop policies and guidelines for the institutionalization of Cooperative
14            Resilience Mechanism (CRM) that will aid agricultural to adapt to and recover
15            from the effects of climate change or other unforeseen circumstances. The
16            CRM shall embody a holistic approach, with provisions for funding
17            assistance, addressing production and distribution issues, and use of new or
18            innovative technologies, production technologies using renewable energies,
19            regenerative and sustainable agriculture, among others; and
20         h. Advocate for policy reforms and programs that enhance social protection of
21            the agricultural cooperatives and their members, including access to
22            healthcare, insurance, and retirement benefits, among others.

23   Sec. 37. Policies Fostering Inclusivity and Sustainability. - Recognizing the
24   contributions of women and youth to the agricultural sector, as well as the need to
25   improve inclusivity, the Council shall prioritize policies that seek to improve women
26   empowerment and youth participation in the sector, and which shall foster sustainability
27   and further opportunities for economic growth.
28
29   Further, the Council shall prioritize the implementation of policies that enhance
30   biodiversity, sustainability, and agroecology, and the development of innovative farming
31   techniques and practices.
32
33   Sec. 38. Secretariat. - The Bureau shall serve as the Secretariat to the Council.
34
35   Sec. 39. Per Diem. - The members of the Council shall each receive a per diem
36   pursuant to existing laws, for each meeting of the Council actually attended; provided,
37   that in the case of representatives from the private sector, the Council is authorized to
38   provide funds to defray the cost of reasonable travel expenses, in addition to the per
39   diem, as may be necessary.

44   Sec. 40. Role of LGUs. - The Local Government Units shall:
 1          a. Ensure the appointment of CDOs in compliance with Republic Act NO.
 2             11535;
 3          b. Create a Committee on Agricultural Cooperatives Development in the Local
 4             Government Legislatives Council, which shall focus on addressing legislative
 5             concerns of agricultural cooperatives;
 6          c. Disseminate information and promote agricultural cooperatives in their
 7             respective localities;
 8          d. Facilitate the establishment and develop the management and operations of
 9             agricultural cooperatives within their jurisdictions;
10          e. Expedite the issuance of necessary permits for the implementation of projects
11             and programs of the DA, CDA, and other government agencies and
12             instrumentalities for the benefit of agricultural cooperatives;
13          f. Upon written request of the CDA, assist in the resolution of inter-cooperative
14             disputes among agricultural cooperatives operating within their localities;
15          g. Facilitate and conduct seminars, trainings, and conferences necessary for
16             the organization, registration, management, and operations of agricultural
17             cooperatives, in coordination with cooperative federations and unions; and
18          h. Provide technical assistance and skills training for farmers, farm workers, and
19             fisherfolk to engage in income-generating activities.

20   Sec. 41. Appropriation. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
21   Act shall be taken from the current fiscal year's appropriation of the DA and DTI.
22   Thereafter, the amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act shall be
23   appropriated in the General Appropriations Act of the year following its enactment into
24   law.

26   Sec. 42. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the

28   agencies, shall issue the necessary rules and regulations to implement this Act.

30   Sec. 43. Annual Reports. - The Council shall submit an annual report to the Congress

32   for possible legislation.

37   evaluation of the accomplishments and impact of this Act, as well as the performance
38   and organizational structure of its implementing entities, for purposes of determining
39   remedial legislation.
