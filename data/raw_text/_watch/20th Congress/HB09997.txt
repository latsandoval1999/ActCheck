                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Strong communities require strong barangays. As the government's most immediate
and accessible unit, the barangay is entrusted with responsibilities that extend far beyond
local administration. It serves as the first responder during emergencies, the primary venue
for community dispute resolution, the frontline implementer of national and local programs,
and the closest provider of basic public services to the Filipino people.

        In recent years, however, the functions assigned to barangays have significantly
expanded while the institutional support available to barangay officials and personnel has not
evolved at the same pace. They are expected to undertake responsibilities in public health,
disaster risk reduction, peace and order, social welfare, environmental protection, and
community development, often despite limited resources and inadequate compensation and
benefits. This disparity affects not only the welfare of barangay officials but also the
efficiency and quality of services delivered to local communities.

        The Constitution recognizes the importance of local autonomy and the role of local
government units in national development. Consistent with this policy, strengthening the
country's smallest political unit is essential to building more responsive, accountable, and
effective governance. Investing in barangays is ultimately an investment in better public
service, stronger citizen participation, and sustainable community development.

         This measure seeks to establish a comprehensive Magna Carta for Barangays by
institutionalizing mechanisms that enhance the welfare, protection, and capacity of barangay
officials and personnel while ensuring that barangays are better equipped to discharge the
functions entrusted to them by law. The objective is not merely to provide additional benefits
but to reinforce the institutional foundation upon which grassroots governance depends.

       The necessity of such legislation has long been acknowledged, as similar measures
have been introduced in previous Congresses seeking to establish a Magna Carta for
Barangays or to improve the rights and benefits of barangay officials. The repeated filing of
these measures demonstrates a continuing recognition that existing legal and institutional
frameworks remain inadequate to address the evolving demands placed upon barangays.
Their enactment, however, has yet to be realized.
        For these reasons, the passage of this bill is both timely and imperative in order to
strengthen local governance, improve the delivery of public services, and advance the
constitutional policy of genuine local autonomy.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.​

         AN ACT TO ESTABLISH THE MAGNA CARTA FOR BARANGAYS

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as “The Magna Carta for
Barangays.”

       Section 2. Declaration of Policy.- Pursuant to Article X, Sections 2 and 3 of the 1987
Constitution, which guarantee local autonomy and mandate the establishment of a responsive
and accountable system of local government through decentralization, it is hereby declared
the policy of the State to strengthen the barangay as the basic political unit and the primary
planning and implementing unit of government at the community level.

         The State shall promote the general welfare of barangay residents by enhancing the
institutional capacity of barangays, improving the economic and social welfare of barangay
officials and personnel, and ensuring that they are provided adequate support, resources, and
facilities necessary for the effective discharge of their duties. It shall likewise encourage
participatory governance, community empowerment, and citizen involvement in local
decision-making, consistent with the constitutional policy of genuine local autonomy and
inclusive national development.

     II. BARANGAY OFFICIALS AS REGULAR GOVERNMENT EMPLOYEES

        Section 3. The Barangay Officials as Regular Government Employees.- The Punong
Barangay, Sangguniang Barangay Members, the Sangguniang Kabataan Chairperson, the
Barangay Secretary, and the Barangay Treasurer are hereby declared regular government
employees and shall be entitled to compensation, allowances, insurance, medical and dental
benefits, retirement benefits, and such other benefits as may be provided by law.

        Section 4. Salaries of Barangay Officials. – Within six (6) months from the
effectivity of this Act, the appropriate city or municipal government, in coordination with the
concerned barangay, shall undertake the necessary measures for the implementation of the
salary scheme provided herein, subject to the enactment of the corresponding appropriation
ordinance.

       The barangay officials covered under Section 3 of this Act shall be entitled to the
following minimum salaries:

​       Punong Barangay- An amount equivalent to the salary of sangguniang bayan member
of the municipality or city concerned;

​       Six Sangguniang Barangay Members – an amount equivalent to eighty percent (80%)
of the salary of a Sangguniang Bayan Member; and

       Sangguniang Kabataan Chairperson, Barangay Secretary, and Barangay Treasurer –
for each, an amount equivalent to seventy-five percent (75%) of the salary of a Sangguniang
Bayan Member.

        Provided that no barangay official shall suffer any diminution of existing salaries or
benefits.

                     III. BASIC PRIORITIES IN THE BARANGAY

​        Section 5. Access to Safe and Potable Water.– It is the policy of the State that every
barangay shall have access to a regular, adequate, safe, and potable water supply. To achieve
this objective, the national government, in coordination with the appropriate local
government units and relevant government agencies, shall prioritize the development,
construction, rehabilitation, and maintenance of appropriate water supply systems and
facilities consistent with existing laws, technical standards, and local conditions.

        Section 6. Transportation Accessibility for Every Barangay. – The national
government, in coordination with local government units and the appropriate government
agencies, shall adopt measures to improve the accessibility and connectivity of barangays
through the development and enhancement of transportation infrastructure and services,
consistent with existing laws and available resources.

      Section 7. Basic Government Facilities and Services. – Every barangay shall
be provided reasonable access to:

      (a) basic education services;
      (b) primary healthcare services;
      (c) a functional barangay hall; and
      (d) such other public facilities as may be necessary for effective local
governance, subject to existing laws and available appropriations.

     IV. MEASURES TO ASSURE THE ENJOYMENT OF LOCAL AUTONOMY

        Section 8. Fiscal Support for Barangays. – Every barangay shall be entitled to
adequate fiscal support and to its lawful share in national taxes in accordance with existing
laws. The State shall ensure the timely release of such funds and adopt measures to
strengthen the financial capability of barangays in the effective delivery of public services.
        Section 9. Support for Barangay Infrastructure. – Barangays shall be provided
adequate financial and technical assistance for the construction, rehabilitation, and
maintenance of barangay roads, bridges, and other essential public infrastructure necessary
for effective local governance and community development, subject to existing laws and
available appropriations.
        Section 10. Fiscal Empowerment of Barangays. – Barangays shall be entitled to
adequate and sustainable financial resources necessary for the effective discharge of their
powers, duties, and responsibilities. The State shall adopt measures to enhance the fiscal
autonomy of barangays by expanding their access to lawful sources of revenue, ensuring the
timely release of funds, and providing appropriate financial support for local governance and
community development, subject to existing laws.

        Section 11. Equitable Share from the Utilization of Natural Resources. – Barangays
hosting or directly affected by the exploration, development, or utilization of natural
resources within their territorial jurisdiction shall be entitled to an equitable share of the
benefits and revenues derived therefrom, in accordance with existing laws. The State shall
adopt measures to ensure that such barangays receive appropriate financial and
developmental support, taking into consideration the environmental, social, and economic
impacts of such activities on the local community.

                                  V.SUNDRY MATTERS

        Section 12. Scholarship and Educational Assistance Program. – Every barangay is
encouraged to establish scholarship and educational assistance programs for deserving and
qualified residents in order to promote access to quality education and community
development, subject to the availability of funds and existing laws.

       The national government and local government units may provide technical, financial,
or other forms of assistance to support such programs and may encourage partnerships with
educational institutions, private organizations, and other stakeholders for this purpose.

        Section 13. Local Employment and Community Participation. – The State shall
promote the generation of employment opportunities for qualified residents of host barangays
in government-funded programs, projects, and activities implemented within their
communities. Implementing agencies and contractors are encouraged, whenever practicable
and consistent with existing laws and qualification standards, to prioritize the hiring of
qualified local residents and to provide skills development and training opportunities to
enhance local workforce participation.

        Section 14. Livelihood, Cooperative, and Enterprise Development. – Barangays shall
be supported in promoting sustainable livelihood opportunities and the establishment and
development of cooperatives, micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs), social
enterprises, and other community-based organizations that generate employment and local
economic growth.

        The national government, local government units, and appropriate government
agencies shall provide technical assistance, skills development, capacity-building programs,
market linkage, and access to appropriate financing and livelihood support programs, subject
to existing laws and available resources.
        Section 15. Capacity Development and Continuing Education. – The State shall
ensure that barangay officials and personnel are provided continuing education, leadership
development, governance training, financial management, disaster risk reduction, digital
literacy, and other capability-building programs necessary for the effective discharge of their
duties.

         The Department of the Interior and Local Government, in coordination with
appropriate government agencies, state universities and colleges, and other educational
institutions, shall develop and implement programs to strengthen the competencies and
professional development of barangay officials and personnel.

        Section 16. Legal Assistance and Protection.– Barangay officials and personnel who
are made parties to administrative, civil, or criminal actions arising from or in connection
with the lawful performance of their official duties shall be entitled to appropriate legal
assistance and representation, unless they are finally adjudged to have acted with bad faith,
malice, or gross negligence.

        Section 17. Digital Governance and Administrative Modernization. – The State shall
promote the modernization of barangay administration through the adoption of digital
governance systems and appropriate information and communications technology to improve
records management, financial accountability, transparency, and the efficient delivery of
public services.

        Section 18. Health and Wellness of Barangay Officials and Personnel. – The State
shall promote the physical, mental, and occupational well-being of barangay officials and
personnel by supporting access to health promotion, mental wellness, stress management, and
psychosocial support programs necessary for the effective performance of their duties.

        Section 19. Performance Incentives and Recognition. – The State shall encourage
excellence, innovation, transparency, and accountability in barangay governance by
establishing a system of incentives and recognition for outstanding barangays and barangay
officials.

       Section 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local Government, in
coordination with the appropriate government agencies and after consultation with relevant
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

       Section 21. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department of the Interior
and Local Government and other concerned agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be
necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

        Section 22. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.
         Section 23. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be
in full force and effect.

       Section 24. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
