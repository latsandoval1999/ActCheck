                              EXPLANATORY NOTE


      “… Truly I tell you, whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers
     and sisters of mine, you did for me.” – Matthew 25:40b

      “Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in your power
     to act.” – Proverbs 3:27


      This bill is a result of the consultations and dialogues this Representation has
made with various Filipino communities abroad, especially Overseas Filipino Workers
(OFWs). One of the persisting needs of OFWs is a support system where they can run
to in order to seek counselling, emotional upliftment, or spiritual strength – whether for
themselves or for their left-behind family members – in times of life challenges and
crises.

      Article XV, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution provides that “the State recognizes
the Filipino family as the foundation of the nation. Accordingly, it shall strengthen its
solidarity and actively promote its total development.” The Constitution affirms the
State’s duty to protect the integrity and well-being of Filipino families, even in
circumstances where they are vulnerable due to social, emotional, and psychological
challenges.

      OFWs, who are regarded as modern-day heroes, significantly contribute to
national development through their remittances which sustain millions of Filipino
households and support the national economy. However, prolonged family separation
due to overseas employment has been associated with increased risks of emotional
distress, weakened family cohesion, and psychosocial challenges among spouses
and children left behind.

    Existing government programs administered by the Department of Migrant
Workers (DMW), the Overseas Workers Welfare Administration (OWWA), and other

                                            1
relevant agencies primarily focus on labor protection, welfare assistance, and
reintegration. While these programs provide essential services, there remains a need
for an accessible government service that focuses on providing psychosocial support
for left-behind family members of OFWS, especially their spouses and children.
Though there were already efforts geared at providing support and focused attention
to this vulnerable sector, an institutionalized government program that focuses on this
area will greatly help in meeting the psychosocial needs of both OFWs and their left-
behind family members.

      Most of the time, OFWs or their left-behind family members during times of
distress and challenges seek help from extended family, church, or other communities
for emotional, mental, and spiritual support and direction. However, not all OFWs have
reliable support system which include members of their extended family, church, or
other communities. This is where the bill comes in. It mandates the government to be
a third-party alternative for afflicted OFWs or left-behind family members where they
can seek support and refuge in times of need, particularly, emotional, mental, social
and spiritual upliftment.

     This measure seeks to establish and institutionalize the OFW Life Crisis Support
System Program which aims to provide OFW or left-behind family members with
emotional support system, counseling, prayers and spiritual upliftment, hospital, jail,
and or other solitary facility visitations, peer groups, and other support services as may
be determined by the DMW consistent with the objectives of this Act.

       Further, the program aims to maximize the potential partnership and possible
contribution of the faith sector and care professionals in nation-building. The bill
harnesses the potential and volunteerism of faith-based groups and other
professionals with a heart of service for the needy to strengthen the social and
emotional resilience of OFWs and left-behind families. Their voluntary participation will
secure an abundant supply of loving hands who will reach out to needy OFWs and/or
their left-behind family members in times of need. Thus, the State still fulfills its duty of
preserving the solidarity and cohesion of Filipino families, even if separated by great
distance due to overseas employment.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                   AN ACT
     ESTABLISHING A LIFE CRISIS SUPPORT SYSTEM PROGRAM FOR OVERSEAS
     FILIPINO WORKERS AND THEIR LEFT-BEHIND FAMILIES

         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1        SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known “OFW Life Crisis Support
 2   System Act.”
 3
 4         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is a declared policy of the State to strengthen
 5   the solidarity of the Filipino family regardless of whether living together in the country
 6   or with other members residing abroad due to employment. Whether a co-residing or
 7   a left-behind family, the State shall take such actions as necessary to protect and
 8   strengthen the Filipino family as the basic unit of our nation.
 9
10        As such, the State shall:
11
12        (a) Protect and promote the welfare, stability, and resilience of Filipino migrant
13            workers and their left-behind families;
14
15        (b) Provide accessible services that provide mental, emotional, social, and
16            spiritual support systems to Filipino migrant workers and/or their left-behind
17            families especially in times of life distress, hardships and challenges to
18            preserve psychosocial well-being, family cohesion, and responsible
19            parenting; and
20
21        (c) Strengthen partnership of government and non-government organizations or
22            groups in providing support systems to overseas Filipino workers and their
23            left-behind families;
24

46   referred to as the “Program,” to be implemented nationwide and in countries where
