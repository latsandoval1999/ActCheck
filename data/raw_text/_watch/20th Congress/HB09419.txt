                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to institutionalize a mandatory bereavement leave benefit of
ten (10) days with full pay for employees in both the public and private sectors in
recognition of the emotional, psychological, and familial burden caused by the death
of an immediate family member. The measure aims to provide workers sufficient
time to grieve, attend a funeral, and support their families during periods of loss
without fear of losing income, employment, or workplace security.

        The loss of a loved one often creates profound emotional and mental distress
that directly affects an employee’s well-being and capacity to work. Despite this
reality, the Philippines still has no national law guaranteeing bereavement leave
benefits for all workers. Many employees rely solely on the discretion of employers
or are forced to consume vacation and sick leave credits during periods of
mourning. According to the Mayo Clinic1, grief can trigger emotional, physical, and
mental health effects, including anxiety, fatigue, depression, difficulty concentrating,
and sleep problems that may significantly impair daily functioning and work
performance. Meanwhile, the Cleveland Clinic2 explained that unresolved grief may
negatively affect emotional stability, productivity, and overall health, emphasizing
the importance of allowing individuals sufficient time and support during
bereavement.

      In the Philippines, employees who suddenly lose family members often
exhaust vacation leaves, incur debt to finance funeral expenses, or return to work
immediately despite emotional distress due to fear of salary deductions and job
insecurity. These realities reveal significant gaps in labor protection and employee
welfare policies.

availment of such leave as grounds for dismissal, demotion, or adverse employment
action. The bill also directs the Department of Labor and Employment and the Civil

welfare concern deserving of statutory protection.

       This measure responds to a deeply human reality that every Filipino family
may eventually face. No worker should have to choose between mourning a loved
one and preserving financial stability. During moments of grief, employees need
time to comfort their families, process loss, and fulfill cultural and familial
obligations without the additional burden of workplace insecurity. A compassionate
labor policy reflects not only economic priorities but also the values of solidarity,
dignity, and humanity that define Filipino society. This bill recognizes that behind
every worker is a family, and behind every family facing loss is a need for
understanding, protection, and time to heal.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
GRANTING A TEN-DAY BEREAVEMENT LEAVE WITH FULL PAY TO ALL
EMPLOYEES IN THE PRIVATE AND PUBLIC SECTORS, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Bereavement Leave Act”.

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State affirms that the Filipino family is the
foundation of the nation and recognizes that the death of an immediate family
member bears a significant emotional and psychological burden upon family
members. A bereavement leave benefit system is hereby institutionalized to support
employees in the public and private sectors who have recently lost relatives
belonging to their immediate family.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

          a. Bereavement Leave refers to a leave taken by an employee to grieve the
       death, or attend, or to make arrangements for the funeral of an immediate
       family member; and

           b. Immediate Family Member refers to an employee's spouse, parent or
       child, brother or sister, and relatives within the third degree of consanguinity
       or affinity.

       SEC. 4. Bereavement Leave for Immediate Family Members. - Employees in the
private and public sectors shall be entitled to a bereavement leave of ten (10) days
every year with full pay immediately following the death of an employee's
immediate family member.

      SEC. 5. Non-diminution of Benefits. - Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
reduce existing benefits granted under existing laws, decrees, executive orders, or
any contract, agreement, or policy between the employer and employee.

       SEC. 6. Security of Tenure. – No employee in the public and private sectors
who avails of bereavement leave provided under this Act shall be dismissed by
reason of the availment thereof and shall be assured of security of tenure. Availment
of the leave shall not be used as reason for misconduct, demotion, or termination in
employment, or for any form of unsatisfactory performance.

       SEC. 7. Penalties. – Any person, corporation, trust, firm, partnership,
association, or entity found violating this act or the rules and regulations
promulgated thereunder shall be punished by a fine not exceeding Twenty thousand
pesos (P20,000.00) or imprisonment of fifteen (15) days to one (1) month.

       If the violation is committed by a corporation, trust or firm, partnership,
association or any other entity, the penalty of imprisonment shall be imposed on the
entity’s responsible officers, including but not limited to, the president, vice
president, chief executive officer, general manager, managing director or partner
directly responsible therefor.

      SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE) and the Civil Service Commission (CSC), for the private sector
and public sector, respectively, shall issue the necessary rules and regulations of this
Act within thirty (30) days of its effectivity.

        SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part, section or provision
of this Act is held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected
thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

       SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, regulations and
other issuances or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

       Approved,
