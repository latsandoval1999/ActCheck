                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE


        Pursuant to the mandate of the 1987 Philippine Constitution that the State shall protect
local industries against unfair trade practices, this bill addresses the act of dumping, defined as
the importation of goods into the Philippines at prices lower than their normal value in the
exporting country for domestic consumption. Such practice places local producers at a
disadvantage and undermines fair competition.

        Recent comparative studies on anti-dumping regimes, including an analysis of
Indonesia’s legal framework, highlight that dumping remains a persistent global trade concern
capable of causing material injury to domestic industries and destabilizing national economies.
These studies show that both the Philippines and Indonesia, as developing economies under the
WTO system, face similar pressures in balancing trade liberalization with the need to safeguard
domestic industries from unfair import surges. They likewise reflect shared juridical challenges
such as prolonged investigation processes, evidentiary limitations in proving material injury, and
risks of anti-dumping measures being perceived as disguised protectionism, thereby underscoring
the need for stronger standards and more transparent procedures.

        The Indonesian experience further emphasizes that anti-dumping enforcement is most
effective when grounded on clear evidentiary rules, improved institutional transparency, and strict
causation analysis between dumping and injury. It also highlights a normative dimension of trade
protection, where anti-dumping measures are viewed as instruments of substantive justice aimed
at preventing economic harm, which is an approach consistent with the Philippines’ constitutional
policy of social justice and protection of domestic industries.

        This measure seeks to address the weaknesses in Republic Act No. 8752, which has been
viewed by several sectors as unfavorable to domestic industries. It restores the method for
determining the normal value of goods subject to a dumping complaint, a safeguard that was
removed when R.A. No. 8752 superseded Republic Act No. 7843. Comparative experience shows
that the proper determination of normal value is a critical element in establishing the existence of
dumping and ensuring that anti-dumping measures are grounded on objective and verifiable
standards. By reinstating this process, the bill ensures a clearer and more reliable basis for

        Moreover, the bill removes the requirement for the posting of a surety bond by an affected
industry or interested party upon filing a complaint. Considering that the filing of a dumping case
already entails significant time and resources, the removal of this requirement prevents the
imposition of additional burdens that may discourage legitimate claims. Evidence from other
jurisdictions shows that procedural complexity and cost barriers often weaken the capacity of
domestic industries to avail of trade remedies, thereby limiting the effectiveness of anti-dumping
laws.

       In addition, the bill amends provisions of the Tariff and Customs Code of the Philippines
by removing the authority of the Tariff Commission to withhold the imposition of definitive
anti-dumping duties when all legal requirements have been satisfied. While the welfare of
consumers and other industries remains important, international practice emphasizes that
anti-dumping determinations must primarily rest on the existence of dumping, material injury,
and causation, rather than on broader economic considerations that may dilute enforcement.

        Allowing such external considerations to influence anti-dumping cases may weaken
enforcement, as imports sold below normal value may temporarily benefit certain sectors while
causing long-term harm to domestic industries. Comparative experience further shows that
anti-dumping measures must function as corrective tools to restore fair competition, not as
discretionary instruments subject to competing policy preferences. The resolution of a dumping
case must therefore be anchored on the existence of the unfair trade act itself. Without these
amendments, the current framework may continue to hinder the successful prosecution of valid
complaints.

        Finally, the bill provides that fifty percent (50%) of the duties collected from a successful
dumping case shall be returned to the petitioning companies through their respective industry
associations. This measure is intended to encourage vigilance among industries in reporting
dumping practices, while also contributing to increased government revenue.


        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is respectfully sought.

   AN ACT PROVIDING GREATER PROTECTION TO LOCAL INDUSTRIES
THROUGH THE IMPOSITION OF ANTI-DUMPING DUTY, AMENDING FOR THE
  PURPOSE SECTION 301, PART 2, TITLE II, BOOK 1 OF THE TARIFF AND
CUSTOMS CODE OF THE PHILIPPINES, AS AMENDED BY REPUBLIC ACT NO.
                                8752

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

        SECTION 1. Section 301, Part 2, Title II, Book I of the Tariff and Customs Code of the
Philippines, as amended, is hereby further amended as follows:

“PART 2. SPECIAL DUTIES

“SEC. 301. Anti-Dumping Duty.-

         “(a) Whenever any product, commodity or article of commerce imported into the
Philippines at an export price less than its normal value in the ordinary course of trade for the like
product, commodity or article destined for consumption in the exporting country is causing or is
threatening to cause material injury to a domestic industry, or materially retarding the
establishment of a domestic industry producing the like product, the Secretary of Trade and
Industry, in the case of agricultural product, commodity or article, or the Secretary of Agriculture,
in the case of agricultural product , commodity or article (both of whom shall act upon the
findings of the Tariff Commission (hereinafter referred to as the Commission), shall cause the
imposition of an anti-dumping duty equal to the margin of dumping on such product, commodity
or article and on like product, commodity or article thereafter imported to the Philippines under
similar circumstances, in addition to ordinary duties, taxes and charges imposed by the law on
imported product, commodity or article. However, the anti-dumping duty may be less than the
margin if such lesser duty will be adequate to remove the injury to the domestic industry. [Even
when all the requirements for the imposition have been fulfilled, the decision on whether or not to
impose a definitive anti-dumping duty remains the prerogative of the Commission. It may
consider, among others, the effect of imposing an anti-dumping duty on the welfare of consumers
and/or the general public and other related local industries.]

​        “(b) Initiation of Action – An anti-dumping investigation may be initiated upon receipt of
a written application from any person whether natural or juridical, representing a domestic
industry, which shall include evidence of: (a) dumping, (b) injury, and (c) causal link between the
dumped imports and the alleged injury. Simple assertion, unsubstantiated by relevant evidence,
cannot be considered sufficient to meet the requirements of the paragraph. The application shall
contain such information as is reasonably available to the applicant on the following: 1) the
identity of the applicant and a description of the volume and the value of the domestic production
of the like product of the applicant; 2) a complete description of the alleged dumped product, the
name of the country of origin or export under consideration, the identity of each known exporter
of foreign producer and a list of known persons importing the product under consideration; 3)
information on the normal value of the product under consideration in the country of origin or
export; and 4) information on the evolution of the volume of the alleged dumped imports, the
effect of these imports on the price of the like products in the domestic market, and the
consequent impact of the imports on the domestic industry.

        SEC. 2. Repealing Clause. – Section 301, Part 2, Title II, Book I of the Tariff and
Customs Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 7843 and all other laws decrees, rules and
regulations, ordinances, executive or administrative orders, and such other presidential issuances
related to dumping which are inconsistent with any of the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended or otherwise modified accordingly.

        SEC. 3. Separability Clause. – If any of the provisions of this Act is declared invalid by a
competent court, the remainder of this Act or any provisions not affected by such declaration of
invalidity shall remain in full force and effect.

        SEC. 4. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following its publication
in the Official Gazette or in at least one (1) newspaper of general circulation.
