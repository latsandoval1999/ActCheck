                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

This bill aims to establish a comprehensive Philippine Ube Industry Development
Program, as well as declare Dioscorea alata or locally known as ‘Ube’ as a National
Priority Crop in the Philippines.

The Philippines is globally recognized for its rich biodiversity, culinary heritage, and
agricultural potential. Among its unique crops, Dioscorea alata or locally known as
‘Ube’ stands out as a distinctly Filipino ingredient with rapidly growing international
appeal in food, beverage, dessert, and specialty product markets. In the Philippines
alone, ube is highly used as ube jam that we regularly incorporate in food such as our
famous dessert - Halo-Halo, ice cream, cakes, cookies, and more. Recent data from
the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and related reports indicate that Philippine
ube and ube-based product exports have reached several million US dollars annually,
with significant demand from North America, Europe, the Middle East, and Asia.

In the Philippines, the Bicol Region is known for producing ube, especially in
Camarines Norte, Camarines Sur, Albay and Sorsogon, for both local consumption
and commercial markets. In fact, 35% of the country’s production is centered in the
Bicol Region. Ube and ube-based product exports earned the Philippines $3.06 million
in 2025.

Japan’s experience with matcha offers a clear blueprint—and a set of cautionary
lessons—on how a local agricultural product can become a multi-billion-dollar global
industry. Strategic government support, strong geographic branding, and high quality
standards enabled matcha’s rise; however, the global matcha boom has also exposed
vulnerabilities including supply shortages, aging farmer populations, climate impacts,
price spikes, counterfeit products, and competition from lower-quality substitutes.

Therefore, this proposed measure aims to learn from and surpass that experience by:

   •   Declaring ube as a national priority crop and strategic export under the High-
       Value Crops Development Act (RA 7900), the Export Development Act (RA
       7844), and related laws;
   •   Creating a Philippine Ube Industry Council to set standards, guide investments,
       coordinate agencies, and steward a long-term national strategy;

   •   Structuring the industry to avoid shortages, volatility, counterfeit risks, and
       sustainability issues now challenging the global matcha supply chain; and

   •   Building robust systems for geographic indication (GI) and intellectual property
       protection under RA 8293, ensuring that “Philippine Ube” and regional GIs such
       as “CamSur Ube” become globally protected origin brands.

Anchored on the constitutional mandate to promote rural development, equitable
distribution of opportunities, and self-reliant and independent national economy, this
bill envisions the Philippines as the undisputed global leader in ube—with a more
resilient, sustainable, and inclusive framework than that experienced by early matcha
development.

Therefore, in view of the foregoing, the early approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE FRAMEWORK FOR THE
DEVELOPMENT, PROMOTION, AND PROTECTION OF THE PHILIPPINE UBE
INDUSTRY, DECLARING UBE AS A NATIONAL PRIORITY CROP, CREATING THE
PHILIPPINE UBE INDUSTRY COUNCIL, PROVIDING FOR QUALITY STANDARDS
AND GEOGRAPHIC INDICATION PROTECTION, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

                         CHAPTER I: GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Philippine Ube Industry
Development Act".

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote
the preferential use of locally produced goods and enhance their competitiveness by
recognizing Dioscorea alata, or “ube,” as a strategically important agricultural product with
strong potential for high-value exports, rural income generation, and global market
positioning as a distinctive Philippine ingredient.

Toward this end, the State shall support the development of a competitive and sustainable
ube industry through coordinated efforts in production, processing, quality
standardization, branding, and international marketing. It shall establish the Philippines
as the premier global source of ube and ube-derived products, particularly ube powder,
by ensuring quality excellence, geographic indication protection, and unified brand
promotion.

The State shall likewise ensure that smallholder farmers and rural communities are
primary beneficiaries through improved market access, fair pricing, technical support, and
strengthened linkages to domestic and international value chains.
Furthermore, it is the policy of the State to create an enabling policy and institutional
environment that encourages private sector investment, public-private partnerships,
research and innovation, and local government participation, protect the authenticity,
quality reputation, and geographic origin of Philippine ube products through intellectual
property mechanisms, certification systems, and enforcement of standards, and align ube
industry development with national agricultural modernization, export promotion, rural
development, and food security objectives under exisitng legislative frameworks.

SEC. 3. Objectives. – It shall be the objectives of this Act to:

   1. Formally declare Dioscorea alata or locally known as ‘ube’ as a National Priority
      Crop entitled to preferential support under all relevant government agricultural
      development, export promotion, and rural livelihood programs;
   2. Establish a comprehensive national quality and certification framework for ube and
      ube products, including product grades, processing standards, traceability
      systems, and export certifications that enable premium marketing positioning;
   3. Create the Philippine Ube Industry Council as the central coordinating body for
      industry governance, standards-setting, certification, promotion, and public-private
      sector collaboration;
   4. Develop and secure Geographic Indication (GI) protection for Philippine ube and
      support regional original marks, such as the ‘Camsur Ube’ or the ‘Bohol Ube’, that
      recognize distinct terroir and production areas;
   5. Integrate ube industry development into the Philippine Export Development Plan
      and ensure coordinated international promotion through trade missions, buyer
      engagement, and market intelligence;
   6. Support local government units and private sector entities in establishing ube
      production zones, processing facilities, research centers, and market linkage
      programs;
   7. Promote research, innovation, and technology transfer in ube cultivation, post-
      harvest handling, processing technologies, product development, and market
      applications; and
   8. Establish financing mechanisms and incentives that facilitate farmer participation,
      processing facility development, export operations, and brand-building
      investments.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

   a) “Ube” refers to the purple yam, scientifically known as Dioscorea alata, a root crop
      cultivated in the Philippines and recognized for its distinctive color, sweet flavor,
      and nutritional properties;

   b) “Ube products” refer to all products derived from or containing ube as a primary
      ingredient, including but not limited to fresh ube tubers, frozen ube, ube puree, ube
      paste, ube powder, ube flour, ube-based food products, ube extracts, and ube
      colorants;
   c) “Ube powder” refer to dehydrated and milled ube processed into fine powder form
      suitable for use in food, beverage, bakery, nutraceutical, and other industrial
      applications;

   d) “National Priority Crop” refers to agricultural products designated by law or policy
      as meriting preferential government support, resource allocation, and development
      assistance;

   e) “Geographic Indication (GI)” refers to an indication which identifies good as
      originating in the territory of a country, or a region or locality in that territory, where
      a given quality, reputation, or other characteristic of the goods is essentially
      attributable to their geographic origin, as defined under Republic Act No. 8293,
      amended or the Intellectual Property Code;

   f) “Certification” refers to the official recognition that ube or ube products meet
      defined quality standards, production protocols, or origin requirements, issued by
      an authorized certifying body;

   g) “Contract farming” refers to agricultural production arrangements under written
      agreements specifying terms for production, quality standards, pricing
      mechanisms, delivery schedules, and support services between farmers and
      buyers;

   h) “Council” refers to the Philippine Ube Industry Council created under this Act;

   i) “Terroir” refers to the combination of soil, climate, topography, and traditional
      cultivation practices in a specific geographic area that impart distinctive
      characteristics to agricultural products;

   j) “Traceability” refers to the ability to track the production history, processing,
      distribution, and location of ube products through recorded identification systems.

SEC. 5. Coverage and Scope. – This Act shall apply to:

   1. All ube cultivation, production, processing, trading, marketing, and exportation
      activities conducted within the territory of the Republic of the Philippines;

   2. All persons, entities, cooperatives, corporations, and organizations engaged in or
      intending to engage in any stage of the ube value chain, from farm production to
      retail and export;

   3. The development and use of quality standards, certifications, and geographic
      indications related to Philippine ube and ube products;

   4. Government programs, appropriations, and support services directed towards ube
      industry development under this Act and related legislation;
   5. Public-private partnerships, local government initiatives, and private sector
      investments in ube industry development.

                               CHAPTER II
            NATIONAL PRIORITY CROP AND PROGRAM FRAMEWORK

SEC. 6. Ube as a National Priority Crop and Strategic Export. – Ube (Dioscorea alata)
is hereby declared a national priority crop in the Philippines, as well as a strategic export,
entitled to preferential support, resource allocation, and development assistance under
all relevant government agricultural and export promotion programs, including but not
limited to:

   1. The High-Value Crops Development Program (HVCDP) under Republic Act No.
      7900 otherwise known as the High-Value Crops Development Act of 1995, with
      ube designated as a priority high-value crop for production support, financing,
      market development, and technology transfer;

   2. The One Town, One Product (OTOP) Philippines Program under Republic Act
      No. 11960 otherwise known as the OTOP Philippines Act, with ube and ube
      products recognized as priority products for branding, marketing, and standards
      compliance support;

   3. The Philippine Export Development Plan (PEDP) under Republic Act No. 7844
      otherwise known as the Export Development Act of 1994, with ube products
      included as priority export commodities for trade promotion, market access
      facilitation, and export financing;

   4. Agricultural modernization programs, irrigation development, farm-to-market road
      construction, and rural infrastructure projects administered by the Department of
      Agriculture (DA) and other related agencies;

   5. Technology commercialization, processing equipment support, and quality
      infrastructure programs of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST);
      and

   6. Credit and financing programs of the Landbank of the Philippines (LBP),
      Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP), Philippine Crop Insurance
      Corporation (PCIC), and Agricultural Credit Policy Council (ACPC).
SEC. 7. Ube Production and Cultivation Support. – The DA, through its regional field
offices and in coordination with local government units, shall provide comprehensive
support for ube production and cultivation, including, but not limited to:

   1. Varietal Development and Planting Material Distribution

         a. Conduct research and selection of ube varieties, optimized for powder
            production, with emphasis on high anthocyanin content, high dry matter,
            yield stability, and processing quality;
         b. Establish and maintain mother farms, nurseries, and tissue culture
            laboratories for certified planting material propagation;

         c. Distribute certified, disease-free ube planting materials (seed tubers, vine
            cutting, etc.) to qualified farmers at subsidized rates or free of charge; and

         d. Register and protect superior ube varieties as Plant Variety Protection
            (PVP) under Republic Act No. 9168 otherwise known as the Philippine Plant
            Variety Protection Act of 2002 to preserve genetic resources.

   2. Technical Assistance and Extension Services

         a. Deploy agricultural extension workers trained in ube cultivation best
            practices to production areas;

         b. Conduct farmer field schools, training programs, and demonstration farms
            showcasing Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) for ube;

         c. Provide advisory services on soil preparation, planting density, nutrient
            management, pest and disease control, and post-harvesting handling; and

         d. Disseminate climate-smart and sustainable farming techniques adapted to
            ube production.

   3. Infrastructure and Input Support

         a. Prioritize ube-growing areas in irrigation development, farm-to-market road
            construction, and post-harvest facility establishment programs;

         b. Provide subsidized access to organic fertilizers, soil amendments, trellising
            materials, pest management inputs, and other farm inputs; and

         c. Facilitate access to agricultural machinery and equipment through
            mechanization programs and shared service facilities.
   4. Climate Resilience and Risk Management

         a. Develop and distribute climate-resilient ube varieties adapted to drought,
            flooding and temperature stress;

         b. Provide weather information services, early warning systems, and climate
            advisories to ube farmers; and

         c. Expand crop insurance coverage for ube under the PCIC with premium
            subsidies for smallholder farmers.

   5. Sustainability Mapping and Production Zoning

         a. Conduct and maintain national and regional suitability assessments
            identifying areas most conducive to ube cultivation based on soil, climate,
            topography, and water availability;

         b. Designate Ube Production Zones in suitable areas with concentrated
            support services, infrastructure development, and market linkage; and

         c. Publish and disseminate suitability maps and production guides to LGUs,
            farmers, and investors.

SEC. 8. Contract Farming and Market Linkage. – The following shall be the strategies
of the following implementing agencies for the effectiveness of this Act:

   1. The Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the Department of Trade
      and Industry (DTI) and the Council, shall promote and facilitate contract farming
      arrangements that link ube farmers directly to processors, exporters, and end-
      buyers through written agreements specifying the following:

         a. Production volumes and delivery schedules;
         b. Quality standards and grading criteria;
         c. Pricing mechanisms (fixed price, indexed to market, or negotiated) with
            provisions earning fair returns to farmers;
         d. Support services provided by buyers (planting materials, technical
            assistance, inputs, financing, etc.); and
         e. Risk-sharing arrangements and dispute resolution mechanisms.

      Furthermore, DA shall develop and disseminate model contract farming templates
      that shall protect farmer interests, ensure transparent pricing, and promote
      equitable partnerships.

   2. DTI shall maintain a database of certified ube processors and buyers to facilitate
      farmer-buyer matching and market linkage;
   3. Government financing institutions shall provide preferential credit terms for
      contract farming arrangements in ube, including lower interest rates, longer
      repayment periods, and collateral-free loans based on production contracts.

SEC. 9. Ube Research and Development. – The following implementing agencies shall:

   1. DA, through the Philippine Root Crops Research and Training Center
      (PhilRootcrops) and other research institutions, shall conduct comprehensive
      research and development programs on:

          a. Ube varietal improvement, genetic conservation, and breeding programs;
          b. Agronomic practices optimized for different agro-climatic zones;
          c. Integrated pest and disease management systems;
          d. Soil health, nutrient management, and organic production systems;
          e. Climate change adaptation and resilience strategies for ube cultivation; and
          f. Post-harvest handling, storage, and quality preservation techniques.

   2. DOST, through the Food and Nutrition Research Institute (FNRI) and the Industrial
      Technology Development Institute (ITDI), shall conduct research on:

          a. Ube processing technologies (drying, milling, extraction, stabilization);
          b. Nutritional profiling, anthocyanin content analysis, and health benefit
             studies;
          c. Product development and application research for food, beverage,
             nutraceutical, and cosmetic industries;
          d. Packaging technologies, shelf-life extension, and quality preservation; and
          e. Valorization of ube processing by-products and waste minimization.

   3. State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) located in ube-producing regions shall be
      encouraged to establish Ube Research and Innovation Hubs offering
      undergraduate and graduate research programs, technology demonstration
      facilities, and industry collaboration platforms.

   4. Research findings, improved varieties, and technologies developed under
      government-funded programs shall be disseminated freely to Filipino farmers and
      processors with priority given to smallholders and cooperatives.

                                  CHAPTER III
                       PHILIPPINE UBE INDUSTRY COUNCIL

SEC. 10. Creation of the Philippine Ube Industry Council. – There is hereby created
the Philippine Ube Industry Council, hereinafter referred to as the “Council”, as the
policy-making, coordinating, and advisory body under DA for administrative and
budgetary purposes, responsible for the governance, standards-setting, certification, and
promotion of the Philippine Ube Industry, in coordination with DTI, DOST, Intellectual
Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL), Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), Local
Government Units (LGU), and private sector.

SEC. 11. Composition of the Council. – The Council shall be composed of the following
members:

   1. The Secretary of Agriculture as the Chairperson;
   2. The Secretary of Trade and Industry as the Vice-Chairperson;
   3. The Director-General of the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines as a
       member;
   4. The Secretary of Science and Technology as a member;
   5. The Director of the Philippine Root Crops Research and Training Center as a
       member;
   6. The President of the Landbank of the Philippines as a member;
   7. The Administrator of the Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation as a member;
   8. The Chairperson of the Union of Local Authorities of the Philippines (ULAP) or a
       designated representative from ube-producing LGUs as a member;
   9. Three (3) representatives from the private sector, nominated by recognized ube
       farmers’ federations, processors’ associations, and exporters’ groups as
       members, which shall be appointed by the President of the Philippines for a term
       of three (3) years;
   10. Two (2) representatives from civil societies or academe with expertise in
       agriculture, food science, export marketing, or rural development as members,
       which shall be appointed by the President of the Philippines for a term of three (3)
       years; and
   11. One (1) representative from the Philippine Trade and Investment Centers abroad
       as members, which shall be designated by the Secretary of Trade and Industry.

The Council shall have an Executive Director appointed by the President of the
Philippines upon the recommendation of the Chairperson, who shall serve as the
Council’s Chief Operating Officer and Head of the Secretariat.

The DA shall provide the technical, administrative, and logistical support for the Council’s
operations.

SEC. 12. Powers and Functions of the Council. – The Council shall have the following
powers and functions:

   1. Policy Formulation and Planning:
         a. Formulate, review, and update a comprehensive National Ube Industry
            Development Plan aligned with the Medium-Term Philippine Development
            Plan, the Philippine Export Development Plan, and other relevant national
            strategies;

          b. Recommend policies, legislation, and administrative measures to support
             ube industry development to the President and Congress; and
       c. Set strategic directions, targets, and performance indicators for the ube
          industry.

2. Standards Development and Certification
      a. Develop, promulgate, and regularly update the Philippine National
         Standards for ube and ube products covering quality specifications,
         processing methods, food safety requirements, and labeling;
      b. Establish and maintain a product grading system for ube powder and other
         ube products that enables market differentiation and premium positioning;
      c. Create and implement a certification and accreditation system for ube
         farmers, processing facilities, and products, including audit protocols and
         conformity assessment procedures; and
      d. Issue certificates, quality seals, and marks for conformity to qualified entities
         meeting established standards.

3. Geographic Indication Protection and Brand Management
     a. Coordinate with the IPOPHL to secure Geographic Indication (GI)
        registration for “Philippine Ube” and support regional or local GI
        applications, such as the CamSur Ube or Bohol Ube;
     b. Develop, protect, and manage the “Philippine Ube” collective mark and
        related brand identities such as the logo, seal, tagline, and visual guidelines;
     c. License the use of the Philippine Ube brand and certification marks to
        qualified producers, processors, and exporters;
     d. Monitor and enforce proper use of Philippine Ube branding and take action
        against misuse, counterfeiting, or misrepresentation; and
     e. Commission terroir studies, biochemical profiling, and origin authentication
        research to support GI applications and brand differentiation.

4. Market Development and Export Promotion
     a. Develop and implement international marketing campaigns to promote
        Philippine ube products in targeted export markets;
     b. Coordinate Philippine participation in international food and ingredient trade
        fairs, buyer missions, and market development activities;
     c. Establish partnerships with Philippine Trade and Investment Centers,
        foreign distributors, food manufacturers, and retail chains;
     d. Conduct market research, gather trade intelligence, and disseminate
        market information to industry stakeholders;
     e. Facilitate business-to-business (B2B) matchmaking, product trials, and
        commercial negotiations between Filipino suppliers and international
        buyers; and
     f. Develop application guides, technical specifications, and recipe resources
        for industrial and commercial customers.
   5. Industry Coordination and Stakeholder Engagement
         a. Serve as the central coordinating platform for all government agencies,
            LGUs, private sector entities, research institutions, and civil society
            organizations involved in ube industry development;
         b. Convene regular consultations, industry forums, and multi-stakeholder
            dialogues;
         c. Mediate disputes and facilitate collaborative arrangements among industry
            participants; and
         d. Foster public-private partnerships and joint ventures in processing,
            marketing, and export operations.

   6. Capacity Building and Technical Assistance
        a. Organize or facilitate training program for farmers, processors, traders, and
           exporters on production, quality control, food safety, branding, and market
           access;
        b. Support the establishment of Ube Application and Innovation Centers for
           product development, technical training, and buyer demonstration; and
        c. Coordinate study tours, technology transfer programs, and knowledge
           exchange with international partners.

   7. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting
        a. Monitor the implementation of this Act and the National Ube Industry
            Development Plan;
        b. Collect and analyze data on production, processing, exports, market trends,
            and industry performance;
        c. Prepare and submit annual reports to the President of the Philippines and
            both houses of Congress through the Committees on Agriculture and Food
            and Trade and Industry, including achievements, challenges, financial
            statements, and policy recommendations; and
        d. Conduct periodic reviews and impact assessments of programs and
            interventions.

SEC. 13. Meeting and Operations of the Council. – The Council shall:

   1. Meet at least once every quarter, or more frequently as necessary, upon the call
      of the Chairperson or upon written request of at least one-third (1/3) of the overall
      members.

      A quorum shall consist of a majority of the members, and decisions shall be made
      by a majority vote of all present members.

   2. Create a Technical Working Group (TWG), committees, and expert panels for
      specific tasks such as standards development, GI applications, market research,
      or regional contribution; and
   3. May engage consultants, technical experts, and service providers as necessary to
      carry out its functions, subject to applicable procurement laws and regulations.

                          CHAPTER IV
 PRODUCTION DEVELOPMENT, CONTRACT FARMING, AND FARMER SUPPORT

SEC. 14. Establishment of a Farmer Registry. – There is hereby established a Farmer
Registry, which shall be updated annually, that shall identify the following:

   a. Farmer identity and land tenure; and
   b. Credit and insurance enrollment.

SEC. 15. Contract Farming Agreement (CFA). - All CFAs must be written, in a language
understood by the farmer, and include: parties’ details, crop/specifications, production
schedule, input commitments, delivery points, quality standards, volume obligations,
pricing formula, payment terms, termination and force majeure clauses, grievance
mechanism, and monitoring arrangements.

LGUs may supply seeds, planting materials, fertilizers, pesticides, technical advice, and
equipment on grant, loan, or cost-recovery basis as specified in the CFA.

SEC. 16. Buyback Arrangement. – The LGUs may buy the contracted quantities that
meet the agreed quality standards at the agreed pricing unless valid termination or force
majeure applies. Delivery locations, acceptance testing (sampling protocols), and
rejection criteria will be specified in the CFA.

If the LGU fails to take delivery without just cause, the LGU must compensate the farmer/s
for reasonable costs.

SEC. 17. Pricing Formulas Linked to Export Contracts. - Where LGUs have an Export
Contract that sets price benchmarks, CFA pricing may use formula linkage to such
benchmarks to ensure transparency and pass-through of export values to farmers. Pricing
formula shall be expressed in writing, which shall be determined by the Council.

SEC. 18. Training of Farmers in the Registry. – LGU, in partnership with the Council,
will provide or fund training on best agronomic practices, post-harvest handling, quality
standards, food safety, and record-keeping. Frequency and curriculum will be set in
annual plans. Priority shall be given to smallholders and new registrants.

SEC. 19. Credit Access and Insurance. – LGUs shall facilitate access to credit for
registered Farmers through: (a) on-lending; (b) linkages with formal financial institutions;
or (c) input-on-credit schemes with partnered financial institutions such as the Landbank
of the Philippines and the Development Bank of the Philippines. Terms must be
transparent and comply with law.
Furthermore, LGUs will facilitate access to suitable insurance products (weather-index,
multi-peril crop insurance, post-harvest insurance) and may subsidize premiums per a
defined schedule for registered farmers. CFAs must explain the claims procedure,
documentation required, timelines, and whether LGUs will assist farmers in filing claims.

SEC. 20. Mechanization. - LGUs may provide or arrange access to mechanization (land
preparation, planting, harvesting, processing) either directly, through service providers,
or rental schemes.

Terms for ownership, rental rates, maintenance responsibilities, and training on safe
operation must be explicit. If equipment is provided on finance, repayment and
repossession terms must be fair and disclosed.

                              CHAPTER V
          PROCESSING, QUALITY STANDARDS, AND VALUE ADDITION

SEC. 21. Philippine National Standards for Ube Products. – The Council, in
coordination with the Bureau of Philippine Standards under DTI, shall develop and
promulgate the Philippine National Standards for:

   1. Fresh ube tubers such as quality grades, size classifications, physical and
      chemical specifications;
   2. Ube powder such as its particle size, moisture content, color values, anthocyanin
      content, microbial limits, heavy metal limits, pesticide residues;
   3. Other ube products such as frozen ube, ube puree, ube paste, ube flour, ube
      extracts, ube-based food products; and
   4. Ube processing facilities and good manufacturing practices;

These standards shall consistent with, or exceed, international food safety and quality
standards, including Codex Alimentarius, ISO standards, and importing country
requirements, which shall be reviewed and updated at least every three (3) years, or as
necessary, to reflect technological advances, market requirements, and scientific
developments.

SEC. 22. Ube Powder Product Grading System. – To enable market differentiation and
premium positioning, the Council shall establish official product grades for Philippine
Ube Powder, including at minimum:

   1. Premium/Ceremonial Grade – characterized by extra-fine particle size, intense
      color, high anthocyanin content, and clean flavor profile, suitable for premium
      beverages, specialty foods, and direct-to-consumer retail;

   2. Food Service Grade – characterized by standard fine particle size and consistent
      color suitable for cafés, bakeries, restaurants, ice cream manufacturers, and food
      service applications;
   3. Industrial Grade – characterized by its suitability for large-scale food
      manufacturing, natural colorant applications, and ingredient blending.

Each grade shall have detailed technical specifications covering particle size distribution,
which shall be measured by laser diffraction or sieve analysis, moisture content, color
values, anthocyanin content, microbial limits, heavy metal limits, pesticide residue limits,
and sensory characteristics.

Products certified under this grading system shall be authorized to bear the Philippine
Ube Quality Seal and corresponding grade designation.

SEC. 23. Certification and Traceability System. – The Council shall establish and
operate a Philippine Ube Certification System that verifies compliance with quality
standards, production protocols, and origin requirements, which may be conducted by the
Council’s designated certification body, government laboratories and testing facilities
accredited for this purpose, and internationally recognized third-party certification bodies
accredited by the Council.

All ube products bearing the ‘Philippine Ube’ brand or certification marks intended for
export or premium domestic markets must undergo certification and testing before their
release.

A traceability system shall be implemented requiring:
    1. Registration of all participating farms and processing facilities;
    2. Batch coding and record-keeping throughout the supply chain;
    3. Documentation of inputs, processing methods, and handling procedures;
    4. Ability to trace products back to origin (farm or production zone) and forward to
       final buyers; and
    5. Digital traceability platforms wherever feasible.

The Council shall maintain a public registry of certified farms, processors, products, and
authorized brand users accessible to buyers, regulators, and consumers.

SEC. 24. Export Certifications and International Compliance. – The Council, in
coordination with the Bureau of Philippine Standards, Food and Drug Administration, and
relevant agencies shall support ube processing facilities in obtaining and maintaining
international export certifications including:

   1. Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP);
   2. ISO 22000 Food Safety Management System;
   3. Halal Certification;
   4. Organic Certification (USDA Organic, EU Organic, or equivalent); and
   5. Good Manufacturing Practices and other certifications required by target export
      markets.
The Council shall likewise secure government assistance for certification, which shall
include technical advisory and compliance support, subsidies or grants covering
certification fees and audit costs for qualified small and medium enterprises, and
cooperatives, training programs on food safety management systems and quality
assurance.

The DA and the Council shall coordinate with foreign regulatory authorities and standard-
setting bodies to facilitate market access and mutual recognition of standards.

                              CHAPTER VI
          SUPPLY SECURITY, BUFFER STOCKS, AND PRICE STABILITY

SEC. 25. National Ube Production and Capacity Targets.-

   a. The Council and DA shall set a ten (10)-year rolling production and processing
      capacity targets, reviewed at least every three (3) years;
   b. Targets shall consider domestic demand, export contracts, and available drying
      and milling capacity to avoid chronic shortages.

SEC. 26. Ube Buffer Stock Mechanism. –

   a. The DA, in consultation with the Council and the Department of Budget and
      Management (DBM), shall design an Ube Buffer Stock Mechanism for ube powder
      and/or other storable ube products;
   b. The mechanism shall:
         i.  Purchase surplus output in peak years;
        ii.  Release stocks during tight years to stabilize prices and supply for key
             domestic and export users; and
       iii.  Prioritize fulfillment of long-term contracts and strategic domestic needs.

SEC. 27. Tariff, Trade, and Logistics Optimization. –

   a. The DTI and DFA shall work to secure favorable tariff regimes and reduce non-
      tariff barriers for Philippine ube in priority export markets;
   b. The DA, DOTr, and relevant authorities shall develop logistics protocols, including
      cold chain where needed, to ensure product quality and reduce bottlenecks.

                               CHAPTER VII
            FOOD SAFETY, ANTI-COUNTERFEIT AND ENFORCEMENT

SEC. 28. Ube Food Safety Standards. - The FDA, in consultation with DA and DOST,
shall:
   a. Set maximum residue limits, contaminant thresholds, and microbiological
       standards for ube products in line with or stricter than major importing country
       standards; and
   b. Periodically review and upgrade these standards.
SEC. 29. Anti-Counterfeit and Misrepresentation Program. –

   a. The IPOPHL, DTI, DFA, and the Council shall establish a National Ube Anti-
      Counterfeit and Misrepresentation Program to monitor, investigate, and act
      against:
        i.   Misuse of “Philippine Ube” or GI names; and
       ii.   Adulterated or falsely labeled products in domestic and foreign markets.
   b. Philippine embassies and trade posts shall assist in enforcement and legal actions
      abroad.

                                 CHAPTER VIII
      GEOGRAPHIC INDICATION, BRANDING, AND GLOBAL POSITIONING

SEC. 30. Philippine Ube Geographic Indication and Collective Marks. – Philippine
Ube Geographic Indications and Collective Marks:
  a. The IPOPHL, in coordination with the Council, shall develop and process GI and
      collective mark applications for “Philippine Ube”;
  b. Regional GIs such as “CamSur Ube” and “Bicol Ube” and others as identified;
  c. GI specifications shall be closely harmonized with local ordinances such as the
      CamSur Ube Development and Global Promotion Ordinance, including defined
      boundaries and standards.

SEC. 31. Licensing and Use of GIs. - Only producers and processors located within the
GI area and compliant with GI specifications shall be allowed to use the GI names and
symbols. Violations shall be subject to penalties under this Act and existing IP laws.

SEC. 32. Global Brand Platform. –
  a. The DTI, Department of Tourism (DOT), Tourism Promotions Board, and the
      Council shall develop a global brand platform for Philippine ube, emphasizing its
      ambition to become an equal to or better than matcha in global recognition and
      value.
  b. Activities shall include:
         i.  Annual Philippine Ube Global Summit;
        ii.  Coordinated participation in major trade fairs; and
       iii.  Co-branding initiatives with domestic and global F&B brands.

                             CHAPTER IX
               MARKET DEVELOPMENT AND EXPORT PROMOTION

SEC. 33. Integration with Philippine Export Development Plan. – Ube and Ube
products shall be formally integrated into the Philippine Export Development Plan
prepared by DTI pursuant to Republic Act No. 7844 or the Export Development Act of
1994.

The DTI, in coordination with the Council and DA, shall:
   a. Identify and prioritize target export markets for Philippine ube products based on
      market size, growth potential, trade relations, and competitive positioning;
   b. Develop market-specific entry strategies, including tariff and non-tariff barrier
      mitigation, buyer identification, and distribution channel development;
   c. Allocate resources and coordinate government export promotion activities focused
      on ube products; and
   d. Monitor expert performance and adjust strategies based on market feedback and
      changing trade conditions.

SEC. 34. Trade Fair Participation and Buyer Missions. – DTI, through the Center for
International Trade Expositions and Missions and in coordination with the Council, shall
ensure regular Philippine participation in major international food and ingredient trade
fairs, which shall be determined by the implementing agencies under this Act. The
Department of Foreign Affairs, through embassies, consulates, and the Philippine Trade
Investment Centers abroad, shall actively promote Philippine Ube products through
diplomatic channels, trade events, cultural festivals, and commercial advocacy.

Trade fair participation and its promotion shall feature the following:
   a. coordinated ‘Philippine Ube Pavilion’ branding and exhibition design;
   b. product samples, technical specifications, and application demonstrations;
   c. B2B meeting facilities and matchmaking services;
   d. Participation of certified Philippine ube producers, processors, and exporters; and
   e. Collateral materials, digital content, and follow-up support for buyer inquiries.

Furthermore, DTI and the Council shall organize inbound buyer missions and trade
delegations, inviting international food manufacturers, ingredient distributors, café and
restaurant chains, retail buyers, and industry media to visit the Philippines, tour ube farms
and processing facilities, and conduct product trials and commercial negotiations.

SEC. 35. Ube Application and Innovation Centers. – The Council, in coordination with
the DOST, DA, LGUs, and the private sector shall support the establishment of Ube
Application and Innovation Centers (UAICs) in strategic locations, including Pili,
Camarines Sur, through direct government investments, public-private partnerships with
food companies, equipment suppliers, or hospitality groups, and existing government
facilities such as food processing centers or technology demonstration centers, which
shall serve as the following:

   a. Product development kitchens and laboratories for creating ready-to-use recipes,
      prototypes, and formulations using Philippine ube powder;
   b. Training and demonstration venues for chefs, baristas, bakers, food technologists,
      and manufacturers on ube applications in various product categories;
   c. Co-development spaces for potential buyers, brand partners, and R&D teams to
      experiment with formulations and conduct product trials; and
   d. Content creation hubs for producing photography, videography, and digital assets
      showcasing ube applications for marketing and social media.
UAICs shall develop and distribute technical application guides, formulation sheets,
recipe databases, and product concept libraries to support B2B customers and facilitate
commercial adoption of Philippine ube powder in diverse applications.

SEC. 36. Domestic Market Development. – The Council and DTI, while emphasizing
export development, shall likewise support domestic market development to build local
demand, create domestic anchor customers, and establish the Philippine as a reference
market for ube innovation.

Domestic market strategies shall include:
  a. Collaboration with major food service chains, hotel groups, café networks, bakery
     franchises and ice cream manufacturers to incorporate Philippine ube products
     into their menus and product lines;
  b. Integration of ube into the OTOP Philippines Program under Republic Act No.
     11960, promoting ube and ube-based products as signature local products with
     unified branding and quality standards;
  c. Development of ube tourism experiences such as farm tours, factory visits, and
     culinary festivals, that shall promote ube consumption and cultural appreciation;
  d. Consumer education campaigns highlighting the nutritional benefits, versatility,
     and Filipino heritage of ube;
  e. Support for Filipino entrepreneurs developing value-added ube products for
     domestic retail and online markets.

                              CHAPTER X
              PROCESSING INFRASTRUCTURE AND TECHNOLOGY

SEC. 37. Processing Facility Development. – The DA, DTI, and DOST shall provide
technical and financial assistance for the establishment, upgrading, and expansion of ube
processing facilities, including:

   a. Feasibility study support and investment planning;
   b. Technology assessment, equipment selection, and facility design assistance;
   c. Access to Shared Service Facilities (SSF) Program grants for processing
      equipment;
   d. Soft loans and credit guarantees for capital investment through government
      financial institutions;
   e. Training on food processing technology, quality control, and facility management;
      and
   f. Assistance in securing processing permits, food safety certifications, and export
      licenses.

Priority shall be given to farmers’ cooperatives and smallholder organizations seeking to
establish collectively-owned processing facilities, local government units developing
municipal or provincial processing infrastructure, SMEs investing in ube processing for
domestic and export markets, and public-private partnerships that ensure farmer
participation and benefit-sharing.
SEC. 38. Minimum Standards for Processing Facilities. – Processing facilities
producing certified Philippine Ube products shall be required to meet the minimum
standards as established by the Council, including:

   a. Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) compliance;
   b. Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point (HACCP) or equivalent food safety
      management system;
   c. Quality control laboratory or access to accredited testing services;
   d. Traceability systems for raw materials and finished products; and
   e. Appropriate certifications for target markets (domestic, export, organic, halal, etc.)

SEC. 39. Technology Transfer and Innovation Support. – DOST, through the
Industrial Technology Development Institute (ITDI), Food and Nutrition Research Institute
(FNRI), and the Philippine Council for Agriculture, Aquatic and Natural Resources
Research Development (PCAARRD), shall:

   a. Conduct applied research on ube processing technologies, including drying
      methods such as hot-air, freeze-drying, and spray-drying, milling technologies,
      color stabilization, and anthocyanin preservation;
   b. Develop and transfer cost-effective, scalable processing technologies suitable for
      Philippine conditions and small-to-medium scale operations;
   c. Pilot and demonstrate innovative ube applications; and
   d. Provide technology commercialization support, including intellectual property
      protection, licensing arrangements, and business incubation services for ube-
      based startups and innovators.

The Council may enter into international technology partnerships and collaborative
research agreements with foreign universities, research institutions, and companies to
access advanced processing technologies, quality testing methods, and market
application expertise.

SEC. 40. Involvement of State Universities and Colleges (SUCs). – SUCs, particularly
programs with food science, agricultural engineering, and chemical engineering shall be
encouraged to:

   a. Integrate ube processing and product development into academic curricula and
      research programs;
   b. Collaborate with industry on technology development, student internships, and
      faculty exchanges; and
   c. Establish pilot processing facilities for training and technology demonstration.

                                   CHAPTER XI
                            FINANCING AND INCENTIVES

SEC. 41. Ube Industry Development Fund. – There is hereby created under this Act
the Ube Industry Development Fund (UIDF), which shall be administered by the Council
for the implementation of this Act and likewise be used to support ube industry
development programs and projects, which shall be sourced from the following:

   a. General Appropriations Act (GAA);
   b. Grants, donations, and contributions from domestic and international sources;
   c. Proceeds from licensing fees, certification fees, and other revenues generated by
      the Council;
   d. Income from government-owned processing facilities, demonstration farms, and
      commercial operations related to ube; and
   e. Proceeds from public-private partnership arrangements.

The UIDF shall be utilized to the following, including, but not limited to, as the Council
may deem to enhance based on the necessity:

   a. Farmer support programs (planting materials, inputs, training, extension services,
      etc.)
   b. Processing facility development grants and subsidies;
   c. Research and development projects;
   d. Quality infrastructure (laboratories, testing equipment, certification systems)
   e. Brand development, trademark registration, and geographic indication
      applications;
   f. Marketing, trade fair participation, and export promotion activities;
   g. Capacity building and technical assistance programs; and
   h. Monitoring, evaluation, and administrative costs of the Council.

The Council shall submit an annual budget proposal to the DBM and a detailed financial
report to the Committees on Agriculture and Food, Trade and Industry, and Agrarian
Reform of House of Representatives and the Committees on Agriculture, Food, and
Agrarian Reform and Trade, Commerce, and Entrepreneurship of the Senate, showing
all revenues, expenditures, and program outcomes.

SEC. 42. Credit and Financing Programs. – The Landbank of the Philippines and the
Development Bank of the Philippines shall develop and offer preferential credit programs
for ube industry development programs, including:

   a. Production loans for ube farmers at concessional interest rates with flexible
      repayment terms aligned with crop cycles;
   b. Investment loans for processing facility establishment, equipment purchase, and
      facility upgrading;
   c. Working capital loans for processors, traders, and exporters; and
   d. Collateral-free or reduced-collateral lending based on contract farming
      agreements, cooperative guarantees, or certification under government programs.

Furthermore, the Agricultural Credit Policy Council (ACPC) shall likewise include ube in
its priority lending programs and allocate funds for credit guarantee coverage to reduce
risk and facilitate access to financing for smallholder farmers and SMEs, as well as the
PCIC who shall develop and offer crop insurance products specifically tailored to ube
production, covering production risks, and price volatility, with premium subsidies for
smallholder farmers enrolled in government programs.

SEC. 43. Tax Exemptions. – The following shall be exempt from tax and other forms of
taxation:

   a. Ube farmers registered under the CamSur Ube Growers Program or equivalent
      programs in other provinces, and complying with quality standards and traceability
      requirements, shall be exempt from local business taxes on their ube farming
      operations for a period of five (5) years from registration, subject to certification by
      the Council;
   b. Importation of specialized ube processing equipment, machinery, and technology
      not available locally and certified by the DOST, as necessary, for the ube industry
      shall be exempt from custom duties and value-added tax (VAT), as subject to
      approval by the Department of Finance; and
   c. Donations, grants, and contributions to the Ube Industry Development Fund or to
      qualified ube industry projects shall be deductible in full from the donor’s gross
      income for purposes of computing taxable income, pursuant to the provisions of
      the National Internal Revenue Code.

SEC. 44. Fiscal Incentives. – Ube processing facilities and export enterprises certified
by the Council and meeting the standards established under this Act may apply for fiscal
incentives under existing investment promotion laws, including, but not limited to:

   a. Income tax holidays, reduced corporate income tax rates, and duty-free
      importation of capital equipment under the Strategic Investment Priority Plan
      (SIPP) administered by the Board of Investments (BOI);
   b. Export incentives under the Philippine Export Development Plan and export
      promotion programs of the DTI.

              RESEARCH, DEVELOPMENT, INNOVATION, AND DATE

SEC. 45. National Ube R&D Agenda. - The DOST, DA-BAR, NIH, SUCs, and research
institutions shall formulate and implement a National Ube R&D agenda covering:

   a. Varietal improvement and disease resistance;
   b. Biochemical profiling and health-related research;
   c. Processing innovations and new products; and
   d. Non-food applications (cosmetics, nutraceuticals, etc.)

SEC. 46. Health Claims and Nutrition Information. –

   a. Evidence-based health and nutrition claims for ube products shall be reviewed by
      FDA and DOST; and
   b. Only claims approved under this system may be used in marketing materials, in
      compliance with domestic and foreign regulations.

SEC. 48. National Ube Data and Intelligence Platform. - The Council, in coordination
with the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), shall maintain a National Ube Data Platform
with updated information on:

   a. Area planted and production;
   b. Processing capacity and certification status;
   c. Domestic and export prices; and
   d. Market and trade statistics.

                          CHAPTER XIII
  INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION, TRADE DEFENSE, AND ALIGNMENT WITH
                       LOCAL MEASURES

SEC. 49. International Cooperation. - The DA, DTI, DFA, and the Council may enter
into international cooperation agreements on:

   a. R&D collaboration;
   b. Market access and promotion;
   c. Capacity building; and
   d. Co-branding, provided, That the Philippine origin and benefit to Philippine
      producers are preserved.

SEC. 50. Trade Defense and Early Warning. –

   a. The Council, Tariff Commission, and DTI shall establish an early warning system
      to detect dumping, unfair pricing, or misleading origin claims related to ube and
      ube products; and
   b. Appropriate trade remedies and diplomatic responses shall be recommended
      where necessary.

SEC. 51. Alignment with Local Legislation.-

   a. LGUs are encouraged to enact ordinances consistent with this Act, taking the
      Camarines Sur Ube Development and Global Promotion Ordinance of 2026 as a
      model; and
   b. The Council shall issue guidelines for aligning local ordinances and programs with
      the national framework.
                                  CHAPTER XIV
                          PENALTIES AND ENFORCEMENT

SEC. 52. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts shall constitute violations of this Act:

   1. Unauthorized use of the “Philippine Ube” brand, logo, seal, certification marks, or
      geographic indication on products, packaging, promotional materials, or
      commercial communications;
   2. False, misleading, or deceptive representation of the geographic origin of ube or
      ube products, including misrepresenting non-Philippine ube as “Philippine Ube” or
      misrepresenting products from one Philippine region as originating from another
      region with protected GI status;
   3. Sale, distribution, or export of ube powder or other ube products labeled or
      marketed as “Philippine Ube” or bearing Philippine Ube certification marks without
      valid certification from the Council;
   4. Mislabeling or misrepresentation of the quality, grade, or composition of ube
      products bearing Philippine Ube certification;
   5. Counterfeiting or reproduction of Philippine Ube certification marks, seals, or
      labels;
   6. Obstruction, interference, or non-cooperation with inspections, audits, or
      investigations conducted by the Council or authorized enforcement agencies;
   7. Failure of certified entities to maintain compliance with quality standards
      traceability requirements or reporting obligations after receiving written notice and
      reasonable opportunity to remedy; and
   8. Providing false of fraudulent information in applications for certification,
      registration, or use of Philippine Ube brands and marks.

SEC. 53. Administrative Sanctions. – Violations of this Act shall be subject to
administrative sanctions imposed by the Council after due notice and hearing, including:

   a) Written warning and directive to cease and desist, with a compliance period;
   b) Suspension of certification, license, or authorization for a period of three (3) to
      twelve (12) months;
   c) Permanent revocation of certification, license, or authorization;
   d) Administrative fines ranging from Twenty Thousand Pesos (Php 20,000.00) to One
      Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 100,000.00) depending on the nature and severity
      of the violation;
   e) Seizure and destruction of non-compliant products;
   f) Disqualification from government support programs and benefits for a period
      determined by the Council; and
   g) Public disclosure of violations and violators to warn consumers and protect market
      integrity.

Fines collected shall accrue to the Ube Industry Development Fund, Provided, That
Administrative sanctions shall be without prejudice to civil and criminal liabilities under
other applicable laws.
SEC. 54. Civil and Criminal Liability. – Violations of this Act involving unauthorized use
of geographic indications, trademarks, or certification marks, or false representation of
origin, shall also constitute violations of the following laws:

   a) Republic Act No. 8293 or the Intellectual Property Code of Philippines, subjecting
      violators to civil actions for damages and criminal prosecution;
   b) Republic Act No. 7394 or the Consumer Act of the Philippines, subjecting violators
      to penalties for misleading advertisements and fraudulent sales practices;
   c) Republic Act No. 10611 or the Food Safety Act of 2013, if violations involve food
      safety, labeling or misbranding issues;

Affected parties, including the Council, certified producers, government agencies, and
consumer protection groups, may file civil actions for:

   a) Injunctive relief to prevent continued violations;
   b) Actual damages suffered as a result of unfair competition or brand dilution; and
   c) Exemplary damages and attorney’s fees in cases of willful and malicious violations.

Criminal penalties under the Intellectual Property Code and other applicable laws may
include imprisonment and fines as provided by those statutes.

                              CHAPTER XV
          FINANCING, IMPLEMENTING RULES, AND FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 55. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the concerned agencies.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 56. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DA, in coordination with the DTI, DOST, DOT, IPOPHL, and
other concerned agencies, and in consultation with farmer and industry representatives,
shall promulgate the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) necessary for the
effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 57. Monitoring and Reporting. – The Council shall submit an annual report to
Congress, through the appropriate Committees, on the implementation of this Act,
including industry performance indicators, budget use, and policy recommendations..

SEC. 58. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.

SEC. 59. Repealing Clause. - All orders, issuances, rules and regulations or parts
thereof that are contrary to, or inconsistent with, the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, modified or amended accordingly. This Act is consistent with and shall not
supplant the other streamlining initiatives of the government covered by existing laws and
previously issued executive orders.

SEC. 60. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
