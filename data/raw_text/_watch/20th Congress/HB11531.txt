EXPLANATORY NOTE

The right to freedom of speech and expression is a
constitutionally-protected right and is therefore inviolable. Therefore, such
rights cannot be arbitrarily abridged or prohibited. Although the Constitution
guarantees the protection of the right to freedom of speech, the government
must likewise combat the misuse of new media, especially when it involves
spreading false information, promoting harassment, encouraging violence,
and insinuating hate or hostility.

One technology that contributes and further exacerbates these issues
is “deepfake.” Deepfakes use artificial intelligence (AI) to create new, realistic
media, in the form of altering a person’s speech, voice, or appearance in
videos. It should be clarified, however, that the use of deepfakes per se is not
illegal, as it may likewise be used for positive or useful purposes, like creating
digital voices for people with disabilities or for entertainment, as seen in
movies like “Rogue One.”

Despite their potential, the legality of deepfakes depends on their
content. They can lead to violations of copyright, data privacy, defamation,
and privacy intrusion. A major concern is that the vast majority (95-96%) of
online deepfakes are non-consensual pornography.’

Deepfake technology first appeared in 2017, during a period when
there was a proliferation of fake news aimed towards creating a more divisive
atmosphere towards different groups with varied opinions over different
topics. As can be surmised from that time, the deceptive power of deepfakes is
alarming. In the Philippines, several celebrities, including Liza Soberano,
Paulo Avelino, and Catriona Gray,” have been victims, highlighting the urgent

AN ACT
REGULATING THE USE OF SOCIAL MEDIA PLATFORMS
BY MINORS, IMPOSING AGE RESTRICTIONS, PROVIDING
PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Deepfake
Accountability and Transparency Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of State Policy. - It is the policy of the
State to protect the dignity, privacy, and security of individuals from the
harmful use of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies, particularly deepfake
media. The State shall promote transparency and accountability in the
creation and dissemination of such content.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the
following terms shall mean:

(a) Deepfake refers to any media that appears to depict, with realistic
likeness, a person’s speech or conduct that they did not engage in,
generally substantiated through technical means, including AI,
machine learning, or digital manipulation;

(b) Disclosure refers to a clear and conspicuous statement indicating that
content has been digitally altered; and

(c) Harmful Deepfake refers to a kind of deepfake created or distributed
with the intent to deceive, defraud, or harm an individual or the public.

===== [page 4] =====
SECTION 4. Mandatory Disclosure Requirements. — Any person
or entity that produces or distributes deepfake content shall ensure a clear,
visible, and/or audible disclosure is included, depending on the media format:

(a) Visual content - refers to a prominently displayed written disclosure;

(b) Audio content - refers to a verbal statement at the beginning of the
recording; and

(c) Audiovisual content - refers to both written and verbal disclosures,
where applicable.

The disclosure must state that the content is altered. Further, the nature
of the alteration must be specified.

SECTION 5. Prohibited Acts. - It shall be unlawful to:
(a) Create or distribute harmful deepfake without proper disclosure;
(b) Remove, alter, or tamper with required disclosures;

(c) Use deepfake technology to defame, deceive, commit fraud, or incite
violence;

(d) Obtain consent through coercion, deception, or misrepresentation for
creating a deepfake.

SECTION 6. Exemptions. - This Act shall not apply to:

(a) Content created for satire, parody, or artistic expression, provided it
contains appropriate disclosure;

(b) Deepfakes created for educational or research purposes, with
disclosure; and

(c) Content where the individual depicted has given explicit, verifiable, and
informed consent in written form.

SECTION 7. Penalties. - Any person who violates Sec. 5(a) or 5(b) shall
face a fine ranging from Two million pesos (P2,000,000.00) to Five million
pesos (P5,000,000.00), and/or imprisonment of six (6) to twelve (12) years.

===== [page 5] =====
Those who commit harmful deepfake acts under Sec. 5(c) or 5(d) shall face
a fine of Five million pesos (P5,000,000.00) to Ten million pesos
(P10,000,000.00) and/or imprisonment of twelve (12) to twenty (20) years.

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The
Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), in
coordination with the Department of Science and Technology (DOST),
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), and the Department of Justice
(DOJ) shall promulgate rules and guidelines to ensure the effective
implementation of this Act.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders, rules,
and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 11. Effectivity Clause. — This Act shall take effect fifteen
(15) days after its publication in a newspaper of general circulation in the
Philippines.
