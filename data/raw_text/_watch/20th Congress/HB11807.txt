EXPLANATORY NOTE

Mental health is an important part of the rights to health, human dignity, and social
development. It supports individuals' personal well-being, family stability, productivity,
and meaningful participation in society when they have access to timely, affordable,
appropriate, and high-quality mental health services.

As stated in Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution, the State must recognize
the right to health and promote consciousness; this constitutional policy provides the basis
for legislation that ensures access to mental health services and strengthens the national
health care system. Meanwhile, Republic Act No. 11036, or the Mental Health Act, is a
national policy framework that aims to promote mental health, provide care, and ensure
individuals’ rights.

Sections 15 and 16 of the same Act relate to the development of community-level
mental health services and provide that the national government, through the Department
of Health, should establish and operate community-based mental health care facilities.
Section 18 addresses psychiatric, psychosocial, and neurological services in regional,
provincial, and tertiary hospitals. Section 29 recognizes the role of the National Center for
Mental Health (NCMH)as the principal training and research center within the Department
of Health, and Sections 30, 37, and 38 specify the respective responsibilities of the
Department of Health and the local government units with regard to the delivery and
development of mental health services.

Republic Act No. 11223, the Universal Health Care Act, also demonstrates the
government's commitment to ensuring access to preventive, promotive, curative,
rehabilitative, and other essential health services, including mental health services through
an integrated health care system.

Even with these legal obligations, access to specialist mental health services still
depends on properly equipped facilities, qualified staff, necessary medicines, effective
referral systems, and sustained funding. Therefore, the legislation should address this
problem by strengthening the government's mental health capacity in far-flung and
underserved areas.

===== [page 2] =====
Zamboanga City is suitable for the proposed facility because it serves as an urban
and service center for several communities in south-western Mindanao. The presence of a
specialist facility there would provide the opportunity to improve access to psychiatric
consultations, psychological assessment, emergency mental health evaluation, appropriate
inpatient care, rehabilitation, community outreach, professional training, and research.

The new Zamboanga City Center for Mental Health will act as a satellite specialist
facility of the National Center for Mental Health and will be placed under the
administrative control of the Department of Health. This arrangement will enable the center
to make use of the National Center for Mental Health's institutional expertise, clinical
standards, training facilities, and research capabilities while at the same time offering
specialized services to a community which might otherwise have difficulty accessing such
care.

The Center must not limit itself to institutional treatment, rather, it should advocate
for a comprehensive continuum of care that includes early identification, outpatient
treatment, emergency assessment, appropriate referral, rehabilitation, family support,
community-based interventions, and ongoing care after discharge. Through cooperation
with local government authorities, hospitals, general practitioners, educational institutions,
social welfare agencies, and community organisations, the Center will assist in improving
mental health services in the area for which it is responsible.

The measure also emphasizes the importance of protecting the rights and dignity of
service users. It contains provisions relating to informed consent, confidentiality, data
privacy, non-discrimination, appropriate complaint procedures, and measures designed to
prevent involuntary treatment and restrictive interventions. Furthermore, it promotes
recovery-oriented, least-restrictive care, in accordance with Republic Act No. 11036 and
other pertinent laws.

In order to secure a responsible implementation, the suggested legislation requires
a thorough feasibility study and the preparation of an implementation plan. This plan
should specify the location that will be used, the expected level of service demand, the
necessary infrastructure, the staffing and equipment needed, the anticipated operational
costs, the funding required, and the schedule for implementation. The Department of
Health, via the National Center for Mental Health, will conduct the study in cooperation
with the Department of Budget and Management, the Department of Public Works and
Highways, the City Government of Zamboanga, the office of the First Legislative | istrict
Representative, and other appropriate agencies.

The measure also provides for coordination with the City Government of
Zamboanga and other local government units through legal agreements, without
automatically imposing any unfunded obligations on them. It also establishes a step-by-
step implementation procedure that accounts for the need for adequate resources, qualified
personnel, appropriate facilities, and the necessary licenses and authorizations before
specialized services can start.

The amount of funding required for the establishment of the Centre and for its
continued operation should be suggested in the annual General Appropriations Act. on the
basis of the feasibility study and the detailed project proposal. provided that it is approved
by Congress and conforms to the existing laws relating to budgeting, procurement,
accounting and auditing. The measure also includes the requirement for monitoring,
evaluation and annual reporting in order to promote transparency, accountability, patient
safety and the efficient use of public resources.

===== [page 3] =====
The legislation under consideration seeks to put into effect the principles of the
Mental Health Act and the Universal Health Care Act by setting up a specific institutional
arrangement which will improve access to specialized mental health services in
Zamboanga City and the areas around it. It is intended to strengthen the national mental
health care network by means of a facility that gives specialized treatment, promotes
community participation, provides professional development, carries out research, includes
rehabilitation, and ensures the protection of human rights.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

KATRINA REIKO CHUA-TAI

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Zamboanga City Center for

Mental Health Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right of every Filipino to the highest attainable standard of physical and mental
health and to ensure that mental health services are accessible, affordable, comprehensive,
equitable, culturally appropriate, and responsive to the needs of individuals, families, and

communities.

Pursuant to Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution, Republic Act No. 11036,
otherwise known as the “Mental Health Act,” Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the

“Universal Health Care Act,” and other relevant laws, the State shall strengthen the delivery of

mental health services through the establishment of accessible specialty facilities, the integration

The State recognizes the need to strengthen mental health services in geographically

Towards this end, the State shall establish the Zamboanga City Center for Mental Health

SEC. 3. Establishment of the Zamboanga City Center for Mental Health. — ‘here is
hereby established a national government mental health facility to be known as the Zamboanga

City Center for Mental Health, hereinafter referred to as the Center.

The Center shall be situated in Zamboanga City and shall function as a satellite specialty
facility of the NCMH. It shall form part of the national network of government mental health
services and shall be operated and administered by the NCMH under the administrative

supervision of the DOH.

The Center shall not constitute a separate government-owned or controlled corporation or
an independent corporate entity. It shall exercise only such administrative and operational
functions as may be delegated to it under applicable laws, rules, regulations, and duly approved

organizational arrangements.

The establishment and operation of the Center shall be subject to the feasibility study, site
and land-use assessments, availability of suitable government land or other lawful site
arrangements, environmental and engineering assessments, licensing requirements, staffing

standards, and appropriations provided under this Act.

SEC. 4. Feasibility Study and Implementation Plan. — Within one hundred eighty (180)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, through the NCMH and in coordination with the
Department of Budget and Management (DBM), the Department of Public Works and Highways
(DPWH), the City Government of Zamboanga, the DOH regional office concerned, and other
appropriate government agencies, shall undertake a comprehensive feasibility study and prepare

an implementation plan for the establishment and operation of the Center.
The feasibility study and implementation plan shall include:

(a) An assessment of the mental health needs of Zamboanga City and the proposed service
area, including existing facilities, service gaps, referral patterns, projected demand, and

available health workforce;

(b) Identification and evaluation ofa suitable site, including the availability and legal status
of government land, accessibility, public transportation, environmental suitability,
disaster resilience, utilities, and compliance with applicable land-use and building

requirements;

(c) The proposed service model, including outpatient care, emergency assessment, crisis
stabilization, inpatient services where justified, rehabilitation, community outreach,

tele-mental health, training, and research;

(d) The proposed organizational structure, staffing pattern. personnel requirements, bed
capacity where applicable, equipment, medicines, supplies, and other operational

requirements;

(e) A detailed capital expenditure and operating expenditure estimate, including
construction or renovation, land development where necessary, equipment, information
systems, personnel services, utilities, medicines, maintenance, and other recurring

costs;

(f) An assessment of the availability of national and local funding, potential lawful grants
and donations, applicable PhilHealth benefit packages, and other sustainable financing

arrangements;

SEC. 8. Personnel and Capacity Development. — The NCMH, in coordination with the
DOH and the DBM, shall determine the appropriate staffing pattern and personnel requirements
of the Center based on its approved service capacity, projected workload, clinical requirements,

and available appropriations.

The Center shall be staffed, as appropriate to its authorized services, by psychiatrists,
physicians, psychologists, psychiatric nurses, other nurses, social workers, occupational therapists,
pharmacists, medical technologists, rehabilitation personnel, administrative personnel, and other

qualified professionals.

The NCMH may detail qualified personnel to the Center in accordance with applicable

civil service rules, compensation standards, personnel regulations, and budgetary limitations.

Permanent plantilla positions may be created and filled in accordance with existing laws,

approved staffing standards, and available appropriations.

The NCMH shall implement continuing education, clinical supervision, personnel
wellness, patient safety, and professional development programs. All personnel shall receive
appropriate training on human rights, informed consent, confidentiality, suicide prevention,

emergency intervention, and the prevention of abuse and discrimination.

Republic Act No. 11036, its implementing rules and regulations, applicable DOH standards,

Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and all other relevant

laws.
In particular, the Center shall:

(a) Respect the dignity, autonomy, legal capacity, informed consent, and rights of service

users in accordance with applicable law;

(b) Prohibit discrimination based on mental health condition, disability. sex, gender, age,

religion, ethnicity, socioeconomic status, or other status protected by law;

SEC. 12. Research, Education, and Public Awareness. — The Center shall serve as a
