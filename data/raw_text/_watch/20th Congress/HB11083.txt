                       AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A VARSITY SPORTS AND PARA-SPORTS
PROGRAM IN ELEMENTARY AND SECONDARY SCHOOLS,
STRENGTHENING INCLUSIVITY IN BASIC EDUCATION, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article XIV, Section 19 of the 1987 Constitution provides that the State
shall promote physical education and encourage sports programs, league
competitions, and amateur sports, including training for international
competitions, to foster self-discipline, teamwork, and excellence for the
development of a healthy and alert citizenry. It further mandates all educational
institutions to undertake regular sports activities in cooperation with athletic clubs
and other sectors.

       Consistent with this mandate, Republic Act No. 9155, otherwise known as
the “Governance of Basic Education Act of 2001,” recognizes school sports and
physical fitness as integral components of the basic education curriculum.
Likewise, Republic Act No. 5708, otherwise known as the “Schools Physical
Education and Sports Development Act of 1969,” underscores the importance of
providing opportunities for the athletic development of children and youth.
Complementarily, Republic Act No. 7277, otherwise known as the “Magna Carta
for Disabled Persons,” as amended, affirms the State’s duty to promote the
rehabilitation, self-development, and full participation of persons with disabilities
in society, including through sports and physical development programs.
       Despite these policy directives, opportunities for systematic and sustained
athletic and para-athletic development at the basic education level remain limited.
While competitions such as the Palarong Pambansa and other school-based
meets provide exposure, they do not fully address the need for continuous
training, early talent identification, accessibility support, and structured
development pathways for young athletes and para-athletes.

       Many student-athletes and student-para-athletes lack access to organized
varsity sports and para-sports programs, competent coaching, adaptive training
systems, accessible facilities, adequate equipment, and regular competition at the
elementary and secondary levels. This results in missed opportunities to nurture
athletic potential at an early stage, which is critical in building a strong pipeline
of athletes and para-athletes for higher-level competition, including collegiate,
national, and international tournaments.

       This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize varsity sports and para-
sports programs in public and private elementary and secondary schools by
encouraging the organization of varsity teams, strengthening coaching systems,
and improving access to facilities, training, competitions, and adaptive sports
support systems. It also establishes mechanisms for athlete and para-athlete
identification, performance profiling, and the creation of a national registry of
student-athletes and student-para-athletes to support long-term athletic
development.

       The bill likewise promotes collaboration among schools, local
communities, local government units, and national sports institutions, including
the Philippine Sports Commission, National Sports Associations, the Philippine
Olympic Committee, and the Philippine Paralympic Committee, to ensure that
training programs align with national, international, and adaptive sports
standards. It further recognizes disparities in institutional resources and
establishes mechanisms for inter-school clustering and resource-sharing to
expand opportunities for learners from underserved and marginalized
communities.

       Ultimately, this measure aims to expand the base of Filipino student-
athletes and student-para-athletes, strengthen grassroots sports and para-sports
development, and enhance the country’s competitiveness in international sporting
events. By institutionalizing varsity sports and para-sports programs in basic
education, the State invests in the development of disciplined, healthy, resilient,
and high-performing youth, while promoting inclusivity and equal opportunities
in sports participation.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

     Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Varsity Sports
and Para-Sports Program in Basic Education Act.”

       Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to promote physical education and to ensure the development and promotion
of the health of the youth. It is also hereby declared the policy of the State to
encourage sports programs to foster self-discipline, teamwork, and excellence for
the development of the youth.
       Towards this end, the State shall provide opportunities for the development
of Filipino athletes and para-athletes by expanding the base of student-athletes
and student-para-athletes, increasing the time and frequency for their early
exposure, training, and competition through the institutionalization of varsity
sports and para-sports programs in the basic education sector.

      Section 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following
terms are hereby defined:
      a. Adaptive Sports refers to sporting disciplines and physical activities that
          are modified or re-engineered to accommodate the unique functional
          abilities and needs of individuals with disabilities;
      b. Student-Athlete refers to a K-12 learner officially enrolled in a basic
          education institution who actively participates in an organized,
          competitive school varsity sports program;
      c. Student-Para-Athlete refers to a K-12 learner with a verified physical,
          orthopedic, visual, speech, intellectual, or sensory impairment as
          defined under Republic Act No. 7277, otherwise known as the “Magna
          Carta for Disabled Persons,” as amended, who is officially enrolled in a
          basic education institution or the Alternative Learning System (ALS),
          and who actively participates in an organized school varsity para-sports
          or adaptive sports program;
      d. Varsity Para-Sports refers to competitive, structured adaptive sporting
          disciplines organized by a school administration for student-para-
          athletes, patterned after national and international paralympic standards;
      e. Varsity Sports refers to the official, structured, and competitive sports
          programs and teams organized, managed, and sanctioned by a basic
          education institution to represent the school in inter-scholastic leagues,
          tournaments, and meets.

       Section 4. Organization and Development of Varsity Sports and Para-
Sports Programs and Teams. – To ensure the optimal and long-term development
of Filipino athletes, public and private elementary and secondary schools shall, as
far as practicable, organize and develop varsity sports and para-sports programs
and teams.
       The school shall endeavor to gradually expand the number of sporting
events that it holds in accordance with its after-school sports program and the
interests of the students, coaches, and the community.
       Sports events that may be considered in the establishment of varsity teams
in elementary and secondary levels may include, but not be limited to, the
following:
       1. Archery;
       2. Arnis;

      The establishment of varsity teams in traditional and indigenous games,

      The organization of varsity teams shall consider the presence of competent
and committed teacher-coaches, the availability of standard facilities and
equipment, adequate administrative support, and the active involvement and
support of the community.

      Section 5. National Varsity Sports and Para-Sports Selection and
Identification Framework. – The Department of Education (DepEd) shall
formulate a National Varsity Sports and Para-Sports Selection and Identification
Framework to systematically scout, map, and identify athletic and para-athletic

                                                                                  6
talent in the basic education sector. The implementation of this framework shall
be categorized as follows:
       a. Public Basic Education Institutions – SDOs and public schools shall
           mandatorily implement the identification framework across all
           elementary and secondary levels, executed progressively based on local
           fiscal capacity, available infrastructure, adaptive facility accessibility,
           and regional sports specializations as determined by DepEd; and
       b. Private Basic Education Institutions – Private elementary and secondary
           schools shall retain administrative autonomy to adopt, modify, or
           integrate the identification framework within their respective
           institutional sports scouting and recruitment policies.

       Section 6. Baseline Qualification Requirements for Student-Athletes and
Student-Para-Athletes. – All K-12 learners officially enrolled in public or private
educational tracks, including Junior High School, Senior High School, Open High
School, and the Alternative Learning System (ALS), who meet the minimum
criteria set under the National Varsity Sports and Para-Sports Selection and
Identification Framework outlined in Section 5 of this Act, shall be eligible to
qualify for their school’s varsity or para-sports teams: Provided, That they satisfy
the following baseline requirements:
       1. Successful completion of standardized physical fitness, adaptive fitness,
          and sport-specific skill tests;
       2. Written parental or legal guardian consent; and,
       3. Maintenance of good academic standing, in accordance with the
          scholastic standards set by DepEd or the private school's academic
          policy.

       Section 7. Selection and Deployment of Competent Coaches. – It shall be
the responsibility of the school administration to secure the services of competent
coaches. Teachers, including sports coordinators, trained in sports coaching, shall
be the priority in the selection of coaches.
       Former outstanding athletes and para-athletes from among the parents or
community residents may be tapped to serve as volunteer coaches in the sports
where they excel and gain national or international recognition. Students and
graduates of the Sports Track in the Senior High School (SHS) may also be tapped
to provide assistance in the training of varsity and para-sports teams.

                                                                                    7
     The varsity teams may also serve as a venue for the practicum and/or
immersion of students in the Sports Track.

       Section 8. Recognition and Training of Coaches. – The DepEd shall
develop a mechanism to provide incentives and recognition to volunteer coaches.
It shall also establish a system of accreditation for coaches deployed to schools.
       The DepEd, in consultation with the Philippine Sports Commission (PSC),
National Sports Associations (NSAs), Philippine Olympic Committee (POC),
Philippine Paralympic Committee (PPC), and the private sector shall develop
programs to train varsity and para-sports coaches to become certified trainers
based on international and adaptive sports standards.

       Section 9. Time and Frequency of Training. – The implementing school
has the discretion to determine the time and frequency of training of the varsity
teams: Provided, That the training is conducted after the regular class hours and
shall not interfere with relevant academic activities of the students.

       Section 10. Competitions. – Aside from the calendar of regular sports
competitions conducted by the DepEd, private and public schools shall organize
varsity matches with neighboring schools. A league may be composed of public
and private schools.
       Elementary and secondary schools may also partner with existing stadiums
and sports centers in the community to host varsity matches.

       Section 11. Equipment and Infrastructure Support for Varsity Sports and
Para-Sports. – It shall be the responsibility of the respective school
administrations to secure the equipment necessary to sustain the training of their
varsity teams.
       For public schools, procurement of standard sports equipment shall be
supported by the regular budget of the DepEd, local school board funds, and
alternative public-private partnerships. Private schools shall source their athletic
equipment through internal institutional funds, tuition allocations, and private
donations.
       The DepEd, in consultation with the PSC and the PPC, shall maintain an
open-access databank of standard sports facilities, accessible infrastructure
specifications, and adaptive sports and equipment technical specifications to guide
both public and private schools in their procurement and safety management.
                                                                                  8
       Section 12. Inter-School Clustering and Resource-Sharing Mechanism.
– To address disparities in sports infrastructure and fiscal capacity among basic
education institutions, the DepEd, through its Schools Division Offices (SDOs)
and in coordination with respective LGUs, shall establish a Varsity Sports
Clustering Mechanism. Under this mechanism, neighboring public and private
schools may be organized into localized sports clusters to maximize the utilization
of limited resources.
       The operationalization of sports clusters shall be governed by the following
guidelines:
      a. Shared Training Facilities and Equipment – Schools within a designated
          cluster that lack standard athletic facilities, accessible infrastructure, or
          specialized sports and adaptive equipment may, through formal
          memoranda of agreement, utilize the training grounds, sports
          complexes, gymnasiums, swimming pools, and athletic equipment of
          better-equipped partner schools within the same cluster;
      b. Optimization of Local Government Assets – SDOs shall actively partner
          with their respective provincial, city, or municipal LGUs to secure
          priority, rent-free access to civic sports centers, community
          gymnasiums, and public stadiums for the training and competitive
          matches of clustered school varsity teams, in accordance with Republic
          Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the “Local Government Code of
          1991;”
      c. Pool of Itinerant Cluster Coaches – To mitigate the shortage of
          specialized sports trainers, certified coaches and sports coordinators
          assigned to a specific school may be designated as itinerant cluster
          coaches to provide technical training and oversight across multiple
          schools within the same cluster; and
      d. Collaborative Sports Leagues – Clusters may form internal sports
          leagues or combine talent pools to field unified teams in higher-level
          regional meets, ensuring that student-athletes from smaller or
          marginalized schools are provided an avenue for competitive exposure.

      Section 13. Performance Profiling of Student Athletes and Student Para-
Athletes. – There shall be a school-based profiling of student-athletes and student-
para-athletes. In case a student-athlete or student-para-athlete transfers to another
school, it shall be the responsibility of the former coaches to transmit the student’s
                                                                                     9
athletic or para-athletic profile, as the case may be, to the new school to ensure
proper recognition and continuous training and development, subject to applicable
data privacy laws and regulations. The performance profile shall show results of
previous competitions and improvement in clocking or classification metrics in
the case of measurable sports.
        All elementary and secondary schools shall register and update the profiles
of all student-athletes and student-para-athletes from time to time.

       Section 14. Registry of Student Athletes and Student Para-Athletes. – The
DepEd shall maintain a registry of student-athletes and student para-athletes and
shall make this accessible to colleges and universities, PSC, NSAs, POC, PPC,
and other private sport groups and organizations, subject to the requirements of
Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012.”
       The registry shall be used solely for identification of potential inclusion to
varsity teams in colleges and universities, or in the case of the PSC and PPC, for
the selection of national athletes and para-athletes who shall represent the
Philippines in international sporting events.

       Section 15. Recognition. – Recognitions shall be awarded to school
coaches and school heads for their support of the organization and registration of
their school varsity teams.
       Teacher-coaches shall be given service credits and/or additional
compensation or overtime pay for work performed in excess of eight (8) hours, in
accordance with the law.

       Section 16. Appropriations. – The funding necessary to implement the
provisions of this Act in public elementary and secondary schools, including the
progressive procurement of sports equipment and development of standard
training facilities, shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act
under the budget of the DepEd.
       Private basic education institutions shall bear the costs of their respective
varsity programs independently: Provided, That the DepEd and the PSC may
provide technical training, coaching accreditations, and institutional recognitions
to private schools free of charge to incentivize participation.

      Section 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days after the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd and PSC, upon consultation with
                                                                                  10
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations needed for the
implementation of this Act.

      Section 18. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      Section 19. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances,
decrees, rules, and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of
this Act are deemed amended, modified, or repealed accordingly.

       Section 20. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
