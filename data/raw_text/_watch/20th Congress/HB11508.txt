                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

Road safety is a pressing public health and education concern. The World Health
Organization’s Global Status Report on Road Safety (2023) highlights that road traffic
injuries remain the leading cause of death among people aged 5 to 29 worldwide. In the
Philippines, the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported over 11,000 road crash
fatalities in 2022, while Metro Manila alone recorded more than 65,000 road accidents in
the same year.

These alarming figures underscore the need for a preventive approach through early
education. By integrating Basic Road Safety and Comprehensive Drivers’ Education into
the K-12 curriculum, students will gain practical knowledge on traffic rules, pedestrian
rights, commuter etiquette, and defensive driving principles.

The inclusion of a Monitoring and Evaluation mechanism ensures accountability and
continuous improvement, allowing policymakers to track outcomes such as reduced
youth-related road incidents and improved safety awareness.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     AN ACT REQUIRING THE TEACHING OF BASIC ROAD SAFETY AND
  COMPREHENSIVE DRIVERS' EDUCATION AS PART OF THE CURRICULUM OF
           THE K-12 PROGRAM, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Basic Road Safety in K-12
Curriculum Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the duty of the State to promulgate policies that will
ensure a safe, viable and dependable road safety system in the country in order to protect
the welfare of the general public.

SEC. 3. Basic Road Safety and Comprehensive Drivers' Education. — Basic Road Safety
and Comprehensive Drivers' Education shall be integrated in the curriculum of all
educational institutions, both public and private, starting from elementary up to the senior
high school level.

SEC. 4. Monitoring and Evaluation. — The Department of Education (DepEd), in
coordination with the Land Transportation Office (LTO) and the Department of
Transportation (DoTr), shall submit an annual report to Congress on the implementation
and effectiveness of the Basic Road Safety and Comprehensive Drivers' Education
Program. The report shall include, but not be limited to, measurable indicators such as:

   a. Number of schools integrating the curriculum;
   b. Coverage of teacher and administrator training;
   c. Reported cases of road-related incidents involving students;
   d. Student literacy and awareness levels on road safety;
   e. Parental and community engagement in road safety education; and
   f. Recommendations for program improvement and policy enhancement.

SEC. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The Department of Education (DepEd),
in coordination with the Land Transportation Office (LTO), shall adopt the necessary rules
and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 6. Curriculum Design. — The DepEd, in coordination with the Department of
Transportation (DoTr), University of the Philippines National Center for Transportation
Studies and in consultation with road safety advocates and organizations, shall develop
a Basic Road Safety Curriculum conducive for students which shall include, but not limited
to, the rights and responsibilities of pedestrians and commuters in their use of all types of
road transportation, road awareness, road signage, and responsible commuting, as well
as, driving rules and regulations, driving courtesy, driving safety, road hazards, and
vehicle safety features.

SEC. 7. Appropriations. — The funds needed for the purpose of implementing this Act
shall be sourced from the Road Safety Fund which is 7.5% of the Motor Vehicles User's
Charge.

SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 9. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in at
least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
