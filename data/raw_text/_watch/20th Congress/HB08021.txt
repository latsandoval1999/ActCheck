                              AN ACT
STREAMLINING ALL PLANS, PROGRAMS, ACTIVITIES, AND PROJECTS TO
REHABILITATE, PROTECT, SUSTAINABLY MANAGE AND DEVELOP THE MANILA
BAY REGION, THEREBY CREATING THE MANILA BAY AUTHORITY, AND
PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR


                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE


Laguna de Bay and Manila Bay are not isolated water bodies. They form a single,
interconnected hydrological system linked by the Pasig River, the Napindan Channel,
and a network of tributaries whose flows are influenced by tides, seasonal rainfall, flood
control infrastructure, and human activity. What happens in one inevitably affects the
other.

Laguna de Bay—the country’s largest freshwater lake—serves as a critical resource for
fisheries, domestic water supply, flood regulation, and livelihoods of millions of Filipinos
living in its surrounding cities and municipalities, including San Pedro City, Laguna.
Manila Bay, on the other hand, functions as the nation’s premier coastal gateway for
commerce, fisheries, tourism, and maritime transport. Yet decades of pollution,
unregulated development, and fragmented governance have degraded both ecosystems,
threatening public health, food security, and climate resilience.




                                                                                            1
Scientific and technical studies consistently show that pollution and floodwaters
move both ways between Laguna de Bay and Manila Bay. During the rainy season,
excess water and contaminants from the Laguna de Bay watershed flow through the
Pasig River system toward Manila Bay. Conversely, during dry months and high tides,
polluted and saline waters from Manila Bay can intrude upstream into the Pasig River and
even into Laguna de Bay, adversely affecting water quality, aquatic life, and the
surrounding communities. Flood control structures such as the Manggahan Floodway
further reinforce this physical interdependence by diverting floodwaters into Laguna de
Bay to protect Metro Manila—waters that eventually influence Manila Bay conditions.

Despite this reality, governance remains fragmented. Multiple national agencies, local
government units, and special bodies exercise overlapping and sometimes conflicting
mandates over Manila Bay, the Pasig River, and Laguna de Bay. While the Laguna Lake
Development Authority (LLDA) governs the Laguna de Bay region, and various task
forces and coordinating offices oversee Manila Bay rehabilitation, the absence of
a single, permanent, and empowered institution to integrate planning, enforcement,
and investment across the entire Manila Bay watershed has undermined long-term
effectiveness.

The Supreme Court’s Writ of Continuing Mandamus issued in 2008 underscored the
urgency of restoring Manila Bay, yet compliance has been uneven precisely because
institutional arrangements remain temporary, fragmented, and dependent on inter-
agency coordination rather than clear authority and accountability.

This proposed measure seeks to address these systemic gaps by creating the Manila
Bay Authority, a Government-Owned or Controlled Corporation that will serve as the
primary entity to integrate, harmonize, and oversee all plans, programs, activities, and
projects for the rehabilitation, protection, sustainable management, and development of
the Manila Bay region and its watershed. The Authority institutionalizes a ridge-to-
reef approach that recognizes the inseparable link between upland watersheds, river
systems, Laguna de Bay, and Manila Bay.

Importantly, the bill explicitly recognizes the interconnectedness of Manila Bay and
Laguna de Bay and mandates close coordination and concurrent policy-making with the
Laguna Lake Development Authority. This ensures that measures taken to rehabilitate
Manila Bay will not undermine, but instead reinforce, the protection and sustainable
management of Laguna de Bay and its surrounding cities, including San Pedro City,
Laguna. The measure preserves the powers, functions, and jurisdiction of the LLDA under
its charter, while providing a unified regional framework to address shared environmental
threats such as pollution, flooding, climate change impacts, and unregulated coastal and
lakeshore development.

The proposed Authority is firmly anchored on the Manila Bay Sustainable Development
Master Plan (MBSDMP)—a comprehensive, science-based, and participatory 20-year
blueprint developed with the assistance of international and local stakeholders. By
consolidating existing coordinating bodies, enforcing the user-pays and polluter-pays



                                                                                       2
principles, strengthening water quality management, and aligning infrastructure, housing,
and environmental interventions, the Authority provides the long-term institutional
backbone necessary to transform Manila Bay and its connected water systems into
resilient, productive, and life-sustaining ecosystems.

For lakeshore communities such as San Pedro City, Laguna, this measure is not merely
about Manila Bay. It is about protecting freshwater resources, preventing recurrent
flooding, safeguarding public health, sustaining livelihoods, and ensuring that
environmental governance reflects scientific reality rather than administrative boundaries.

The creation of the Manila Bay Authority is therefore a decisive step toward integrated
watershed governance, climate resilience, and intergenerational environmental justice.
It affirms that the rehabilitation of Manila Bay and the protection of Laguna de Bay must
proceed together—or not at all.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                              AN ACT
STREAMLINING ALL PLANS, PROGRAMS, ACTIVITIES, AND PROJECTS TO
REHABILITATE, PROTECT, SUSTAINABLY MANAGE AND DEVELOP THE MANILA

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:
Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the Manila Bay Authority.


Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy.- It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect,
promote and accelerate the sustainable management and development of the Manila Bay
region, recognizing its importance as the country’s epicenter for commercial, industrial,
and tourism activities, as well as its cultural and environmental significance, while
focusing on the maintenance of ecological balance and the protection and enhancement
of its natural resources.




                                                                                       4
To this end, the State shall formulate and implement policies, plans, programs, activities,
and projects (PAPs), particularly adopting the user-pays and polluter-pays principles, to
ensure adequate provision for environmental management and control, the preservation
of ecological systems, and the prevention of human induced ecological disturbances,
resource degradation, and pollution, resulting in an improved quality of life.


The objectives shall be executed with utmost regard to appropriate provisions for the
conservation, rehabilitation, preservation, restoration, and maintenance of Manila Bay
waters, consistent with the plans and programs embodied in Presidential Decree (PD)
1586 of 1978 or the Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) System, Republic Act (RA)
No. 9275 known as the “Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004”, RA 8749 or the “Philippine
Clean Air Act of 1999, RA 9003 or the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000”,
RA 6969 known as the “Toxic Substances and Nuclear and Hazardous Waste Control
Act of 1990”, other pertinent laws, and the Writ of Continuing Mandamus issued by the
Supreme Court on 18 December 2008 and en banc resolution on 15 February 2011 (G.R.
Nos. 171947-48).


The State shall likewise ensure an efficient and effective utilization of the natural
resources of Manila Bay region, encourage the participation of all sectors of society, and
maximize public involvement in natural resource management, conservation, and
protection.


Sec. 3. Definition of Terms:


          a. Coastal Greenbelt shall refer to a strip of natural or planted coastal
              vegetation, typically composed of mangroves and beach forest species,
              designed to prevent erosion and mitigate the impacts of natural disasters
              like storm surges, tsunamis, and climate change. These zones generally
              stretch at least 100 meters from the sea towards land and serve as crucial,
              nature-based buffers for coastal communities.




                                                                                         5
         b. Manila Bay Sustainable Development Master Plan (MBSDMP) refers to
              the 20-year comprehensive and sustainable master plan for Manila Bay and
              the immediate coastal zone with the aim for inclusive growth, ecosystem
              protection, climate change adaptation and disaster risk reduction, water
              quality improvement, and upgrading informal settlements;


         c. Operational Plan for the Manila Bay Coastal Strategy (OPMBCS) refers
              to a framework of strategies identified to provide stakeholders what needs
              to be executed to achieve the shared vision of the future of the Manila Bay
              region, and defines long-term actions, as well as the necessary support and
              participation of all stakeholders;


         d. Polluter-pays Principle shall refer to the principle that those causing
              pollution should meet the costs to which it gives rise. The Authority shall
              endeavor to promote the internalization of environmental costs and the use
              of economic instruments, taking into account the approach that the polluter
              should, in principle, bear the cost of pollution, with due regard to the public
              interest and without distorting international trade and investment;


         e. Ridge to Reef Approach shall refer to a mechanism that aims to provide a
              holistic intervention for protecting the coastal area by targeting
              environmental degradation in the uplands (ridge) that impact the coastal
              ecosystems through sedimentation, by restoring the shoreline and
              protecting marine ecosystems (reef), thereby mitigating storm surges; and


         f.      User-pays Principle shall refer to the variation of the polluter-pays
              principle that calls upon the user of a natural resource to bear the cost of
              running down natural capital.


Sec. 4. Creation of Manila Bay Authority. - There is hereby created a Government-
Owned or Controlled Corporation (GOCC) to be known as the Manila Bay Authority,



                                                                                           6
hereinafter known as the Authority, to streamline all PAPs to preserve, rehabilitate,
preserve, manage, develop, and operate the Manila Bay region, in accordance with the
provisions of this Act. The Authority shall serve as the primary agency for integration and
oversight of implementation of plans, policies, PAPs of agencies and local government
units (LGUs), and shall be an attached agency of the Office of the President, but shall
maintain a close coordination with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources
(DENR), for policy and program harmonization.


The Manila Bay Coordinating Office (MBCO) created under DENR Administrative Order
(A.O.) No. 2011-01 and the Pasig River Coordinating and Management Office (PRCMO)
established under DENR AO No. 2020-02, both under the DENR, and the Manila Bay
Task Force (MBTF) created under Presidential AO No. 16, Series of 2019, composed of
various National Government Agencies (NGAs), also headed by the DENR, shall be
subsumed under the Authority, to ensure the continuity of the plans and PAPs for the
rehabilitation and development of the Manila Bay region.


The creation of the Authority shall be consistent with the MBSDMP which aims to deliver
an integral, sustainable and inclusive plan that will benefit the inhabitants around the bay
area.


The Authority shall prioritize the protection, preservation, and rehabilitation of the natural
resources and biodiversity of the Manila Bay region and shall be established into a self-
sustaining commercial, financial, industrial, leisure, tourism, and investment institution,
generating employment opportunities, and attracting and promoting local and foreign
investments: Provided, That the exercise of the Authority’s powers and functions shall in
no way diminish the autonomy of the LGUs concerning purely local matters within the
framework, mandate, and limitations of the Constitution and the pertinent provisions of
the Local Government Code of 1991. Provided further, That protected areas within the
classification of National Park shall be excluded from the provisions of this Act and
continue to be governed under RA 11038, known as “An Act Declaring Protected Areas
and Providing for Their Management, Amending for This Purpose Republic Act No. 7586,



                                                                                            7
Otherwise Known as the “National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS) Act of
1992” and For Other Purposes”.


Sec. 5. MBCO, PRCMO, and MBTF Offices, Functions, and Personnel, Subsumed
Under the Authority. - The Authority shall entirely subsume the Manila Bay Coordinating
Office (MBCO) and its Site Coordinating and Management Offices in the National Capital
Region (NCR), Region 3 (Central Luzon), and Region 4A (CALABARZON- Cavite,
Laguna, Batangas, Rizal, and Quezon), the Pasig River Coordinating and Management
Office (PRCMO), and the Manila Bay Task Force, with their applicable powers, functions,
personnel, funds, and appropriations, records, and equipment and property.


Sec. 6. Transfer of Powers and Functions of MBCO, PRCMO, and MBTF. - The
Authority shall integrate the functions and assume the current tasks of the three (3)
offices. To wit:


   a. Coordinate the full implementation of the OPMBCS with NGAs, LGUs, the NGOs
       and POs, Private Sector, Academe, Religious and Civil Society;


   b. Collaborate with relevant national and regional programs and projects, including
       the Manila Third Sewerage Project (MTSP) and the Regional Programme,
       “Building Partnerships in the Environmental Management of the Seas of East Asia
       (PEMSEA);


   c. Collaborate with the DENR regional offices in Regions III, IV-A and the NCR
       regarding related projects and activities with the provincial and other LGUs;


   d. Engage Protected Area Management Boards (PAMBs), and other relevant
       national and local bodies created by law, to ensure that the respective plans and
       PAPs of such authorities are consistent with and complement the objectives and
       targets of the OPMBCS;




                                                                                       8
e. Review and provide input on projects being planned, developed, and implemented
   in the Manila Bay region, for both foreign and locally funded by all offices, bureaus
   and attached agencies of various NGAs, to ensure alignment and complementarity
   to the objectives and targets of the OPMBCS;




f. Collaborate with the aforementioned stakeholder NGAs and NGOs, develop an
   annual work plan for the implementation of project activities, including
   accomplishments, counterpart budgets, timeframe, monitoring and reporting
   strategies;


g. Facilitate the development and implementation of a monitoring and tracking
   system to ensure compliance with agreed implementation and financing plans and
   annual accomplishment targets;


h. Prepare the regular report for submission to the Supreme Court;


i. Monitor and prepare a quarterly and annually progress report on the
   implementation of the OPMBCS, to be submitted to the OP;


j. Implement appropriate and sustainable waste and water quality management
   PAPs;


k. Explore and adopt water quality improvement strategies, interventions, and
   technologies that aim to improve the Manila Bay, its waterways and tributaries, and
   its environs;


l. Regularly monitor the water quality of the Manila Bay, its waterways and
   tributaries, through the conduct of water and sediment quality analysis together
   with partner agencies;




                                                                                      9
m. Facilitate PAPs in maintaining and sustaining the cleanliness of the Manila Bay, its
   waterways and tributaries, through regular clean up activities;


n. Provide policy improvements, recommendations, and guidelines that aim to
   address the solid waste-related issues and concerns, in coordination with agencies
   concerned, LGUs, and other stakeholders;


o. Enforce relevant provisions of PD No. 856 or the "Code on Sanitation of the
   Philippines," as amended, PD 1586, RA 8749, RA 9003, RA 6969, and other
   related environmental laws RA 9275, and RA 9003, to ensure the complete
   rehabilitation, restoration, and conservation of the Manila Bay region;


p. In accordance with PD No. 198 or the "Provincial Water Utilities Act," as amended,
   PD No. 856, as amended, and RA 9275, require all government facilities,
   subdivisions, condominiums, commercial centers, hotels, sports and recreational
   facilities, hospitals, market places, public buildings, industrial complex and other
   similar establishments, including households, to immediately connect existing
   sewage lines to available sewerage systems or sewerage treatment plants (STPs),
   or to construct individual STPs, whenever applicable, as well as to issue
   appropriate orders, impose fines, penalties and other administrative sanctions to
   compel compliance thereof;


q. Undertake remedial measures using engineering and technological interventions
   to improve the water quality of the Manila Bay, such as sustained and targeted
   reduction in the coliform level in all major river systems and tributaries within the
   Manila Bay region;


r. In collaboration with the National Anti-Poverty Commission (NAPC), Presidential
   Commission for the Urban Poor (PCUP), National Housing Authority (NHA) and
   affected LGUs, prepare and implement a comprehensive plan for massive
   relocation of Informal Settler Families (ISFs), especially in the priority areas of the



                                                                                       10
      NCR along the Manila Bay region, which shall include: (i) identification of suitable
      relocation sites; (ii) strategies for economic and social integration of ISFs in the
      area; and (iii) long term solutions to address ongoing migration into the Manila Bay
      region;


   s. Prepare a comprehensive plan for expediting the local sanitation program of LGUs
      within the Manila Bay region;


   t. Fast-track compliance with the Writ of Continuing Mandamus issued by the
      Supreme Court, including full implementation of the OPMBCS in accordance with
      its schedule;


   u. Ensure that the concerned agencies and LGUs undertake appropriate measures
      relative to violation of environmental laws such as, but not limited to, demolition,
      closure or cessation of business and filing of administrative action against erring
      persons or establishments;


   v. In collaboration with the Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher
      Education, Presidential Communications Operations Office and affected LGUs,
      and in partnership with the private sector and NGOs, facilitate a massive
      information, education and communication drive to gain public support on the
      Manila Bay region clean up, rehabilitation, and restoration efforts, as well as the
      preservation of the ecosystem in the Manila Bay region; and


   w. Improve resource management of the Manila Bay region and create models of
      inter- LGU cooperation in ecosystem management, with special focus on
      interconnecting major tributaries like Laguna Lake and Pasig River.
