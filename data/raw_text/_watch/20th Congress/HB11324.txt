                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Food waste remains a pressing challenge in the Philippines, with substantial
quantities of edible food discarded daily despite the continued prevalence of food insecurity
and hunger. Many Filipino students likewise attend classes with inadequate nutrition,
adversely affecting their learning, school participation, and overall development. Addressing
food waste not only promotes food security but also supports better educational outcomes
for learners.

       Schools play a vital role in shaping lifelong values and habits. Integrating food waste
reduction and prevention into the basic education system will enable learners to appreciate
the value of food, practice responsible consumption, and understand the link between food
waste, nutrition, environmental sustainability, and climate change. Equipping teachers with
the necessary knowledge and instructional resources will further ensure that these
principles are effectively integrated into classroom instruction and school activities.

       This measure seeks to institutionalize food waste reduction and prevention
education in all public and private basic education institutions through curriculum
integration, teacher capacity-building, and school-based initiatives that promote responsible
food management. By fostering a culture of responsible consumption among learners, this
bill advances the State's commitment to quality education, food security, environmental
protection, and sustainable development.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
    INSTITUTIONALIZING FOOD WASTE REDUCTION AND PREVENTION
EDUCATION IN ALL PUBLIC AND PRIVATE BASIC EDUCATION INSTITUTIONS,
 PROMOTING RESPONSIBLE FOOD CONSUMPTION AMONG LEARNERS, AND
                       FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:



      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Food Waste Reduction and
Prevention Act”.

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State recognizes every person’s right to an
adequate standard of living, including sufficient, safe, and nutritious food. It shall be the
policy of the State to attain food security, end hunger, promote the efficient use of the
country’s food resources, and protect the environment from adverse effects of greenhouse
gas emissions arising from food waste.
       The State further recognizes that schools play a vital role in developing lifelong habits
and values among learners. Through age-appropriate education and school-based initiatives,
learners shall be empowered to minimize food waste, appreciate the value of food, and
contribute to national efforts toward food security, environmental protection, and
sustainable development.

        SECTION 3. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all public and private educational
institutions under the supervision of the Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on
Higher Education (CHED), and Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA).
       SECTION 4. School-Based Food Waste Reduction and Prevention Program. – All public
and private basic education institutions shall establish and implement a School-Based Food
Waste Reduction and Prevention Program appropriate to their size, resources, and
circumstances.

The Program shall integrate food waste reduction and prevention into relevant school
activities and, where applicable, shall include:

(a) food waste awareness and education activities;

(b) responsible food consumption and portion management initiatives;

(c) proper segregation and management of food waste;

(d) measures to reduce food waste generated by school canteens and other school food
service areas;

(e) measures to prevent unnecessary food waste in school feeding programs;

(f) school gardening, composting, and other appropriate activities for the beneficial use of
unavoidable food waste;

(g) learner-led activities and campaigns; and

(h) partnerships with parents, local government units (LGUs), community organizations, and
other stakeholders.

The DepEd, TESDA, and CHED shall issue standards and guidelines for the establishment and
implementation of the Program, taking into consideration the varying circumstances and
capacities of schools.


      SECTION 5. Food Waste Reduction and Prevention Education and Training.- Food
waste reduction and prevention education shall be institutionalized as part of the
educational programs and activities of covered institutions.

The appropriate education agency shall develop age- and level-appropriate training and
learning resources for:

(a) DepEd, for learners, teachers, school heads, and school personnel in basic education
institutions;

(b) CHED, for students, faculty members, and personnel of higher education institutions; and
(c) TESDA, for trainees, trainers, and personnel of technical-vocational institutions.

The training shall cover, as appropriate:

   1.   the causes and impacts of food waste;
   2.   responsible food consumption;
   3.   food planning, preparation, storage, and portion management;
   4.   food safety and proper handling;
   5.   food recovery and the appropriate management of edible surplus;
   6.   composting and other appropriate management of unavoidable food waste;
   7.   sustainable food systems and resource conservation; and
   8.   practical strategies for preventing food waste within educational institutions,
        households, and communities.

        SECTION 6. Integration into Educational Programs. - The DepEd, CHED, and TESDA
shall each integrate, within their respective mandates and educational frameworks, age- and
level-appropriate concepts and activities on food waste reduction and prevention.

For basic education, DepEd shall integrate such concepts into the curriculum and relevant
co-curricular activities.

For higher education, CHED shall encourage and support the integration of food waste
reduction and prevention into relevant academic programs, general education courses,
research, extension, and student development activities.

For technical-vocational education and training, TESDA shall integrate relevant food waste
reduction and prevention competencies into appropriate technical-vocational programs and
training regulations, particularly those involving food preparation, food service, hospitality,
agriculture, and related fields.


      SECTION 7. Student Participation. - Covered educational institutions shall provide
opportunities for meaningful participation of learners and students in food waste reduction
and prevention activities through:

(a) food waste awareness campaigns;

(b) student-led advocacy programs;

(c) food waste monitoring activities;

(d) school garden projects;
(e) composting projects;

(f) food recovery initiatives, where appropriate;

(g) innovation competitions; and

(h) community extension activities promoting responsible food consumption.




      SECTION 8. Institutional Food Services. - All covered educational institutions with
canteens, cafeterias, food service facilities, or feeding programs shall adopt reasonable
measures to prevent and reduce food waste.

These measures may include:

(a) demand-based food preparation and procurement;

(b) appropriate serving portions;

(c) proper food storage and handling;

(d) monitoring of frequently wasted food items;

(e) appropriate management of safe and edible surplus food; and

(f) proper segregation and management of unavoidable food waste.

The DepEd, CHED, and TESDA shall each issue appropriate guidelines for institutions under
their respective jurisdictions.




       SECTION 9. Inter-Agency Coordination. - The Department of Education shall
coordinate with the Department of Agriculture (DA), Department of Environment and
Natural Resources (DENR), Department of Health (DOH), National Nutrition Council (NNC),
Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and local government units (LGUs) in implementing
this Act.

       SECTION 10. Recognition and Incentives. - The DepEd, CHED, and TESDA shall
establish appropriate recognition mechanisms for educational institutions that demonstrate
exemplary implementation of food waste reduction and prevention programs.
Recognition may be based on demonstrated reduction of avoidable food waste, learner and
student participation, innovative practices, institutional food service improvements, and
sustained community engagement.


       SECTION 11. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days after the
effectivity of this Act, the NNC, in coordination with all concerned agencies, shall promulgate
the necessary rules and regulations.

      SECTION 13. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part hereof is held invalid, or
34 unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provision not otherwise affected shall
35 remain valid and subsisting.

        SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. - All other laws, presidential decrees or issuances,
executive orders, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules and regulations contrary
to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or amended
accordingly.

       SECTION 15. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
