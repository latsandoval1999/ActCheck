                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article XIV, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution mandates the State to protect
and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and to take
appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all. Section 2(2) thereof further
directs the State to establish and maintain a complete, adequate, and integrated system
of education relevant to the needs of the people and society. Likewise, Section 10
recognizes science and technology as essential for national development and requires
the State to prioritize research and development, innovation, and technical education
and training to foster productivity, self-reliance, and global competitiveness.

       The SOCCSKSARGEN Region (Region XII) has emerged as one of the
country's major economic growth centers and among Mindanao's leading contributors
to agriculture, fisheries, manufacturing, logistics, tourism, and agro-industrial exports.
The region is recognized as the Tuna Capital of the Philippines and serves as a
strategic hub for the tuna industry, aquaculture, rubber, banana, pineapple, coconut,
coffee, and the rapidly expanding halal industry. It is likewise experiencing sustained
growth in construction, renewable energy, digital services, information and
communications technology, and advanced manufacturing.

        These developments have significantly increased the demand for highly skilled
workers possessing competencies in Industry 4.0 technologies, digital transformation,
automation, artificial intelligence, advanced manufacturing, sustainable agriculture,
logistics, entrepreneurship, and globally recognized technical qualifications.

        Despite the presence of several Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA) Technology Institutions across Region XII, technical-vocational
education and training remains fragmented. The existing institutions operate
independently with separate administrative systems, planning mechanisms, and
resource allocations. Such fragmentation has resulted in duplication of programs,
inefficient utilization of resources, uneven institutional development, limited
specialization, and inadequate industry integration.
                                                                                        1
       Recent labor market analyses likewise indicate persistent shortages of skilled
workers in priority sectors such as agriculture, fisheries, construction, tourism,
healthcare, manufacturing, digital technologies, logistics, renewable energy, and
emerging industries. Unless these structural deficiencies are addressed, Region XII
risks losing opportunities for higher productivity, greater investments, and inclusive
economic growth.

       This Bill seeks to establish the SOCCSKSARGEN Polytechnic Institute of
Technical Education (SPITE) as a unified, multi-campus, industry-driven regional
polytechnic institution under the supervision of TESDA. The proposed Institute shall
consolidate existing TESDA Technology Institutions in Region XII into a single
coordinated system governed by one administration, one development framework, and
one strategic vision.

       SPITE shall serve as the premier institution for competency-based technical
education, diploma programs, lifelong learning, applied research, technology
innovation, entrepreneurship, and industry collaboration in SOCCSKSARGEN. It
shall provide seamless educational pathways from National Certificates to diploma
programs and other higher-level technical qualifications consistent with the Philippine
Qualifications Framework.

       The Institute shall likewise establish specialized Centers of Innovation in
sectors where Region XII possesses strategic advantages, including tuna processing,
aquaculture, rubber technology, agri-processing, halal industry development,
renewable energy, construction technologies, digital innovation, tourism, hospitality,
and advanced manufacturing. Through strengthened partnerships with industries, local
government units, higher education institutions, and international organizations,
SPITE shall ensure that its programs remain responsive to present and future labor
market requirements.

       Beyond strengthening technical education, the establishment of SPITE will
contribute to regional industrialization, innovation, employment generation, poverty
reduction, entrepreneurship, and inclusive economic development. It will enhance
workforce competitiveness, promote lifelong learning, support technology transfer,
and serve as a catalyst for sustainable growth not only in SOCCSKSARGEN but
throughout Mindanao.

       The establishment of the SOCCSKSARGEN Polytechnic Institute of Technical
Education is therefore a strategic investment in human capital development that
advances the Constitutional mandate to provide quality, relevant, and accessible
technical education responsive to the demands of national development.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this Bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SEC. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "SOCCSKSARGEN
Polytechnic Institute of Technical Education (SPITE) Act."

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote and strengthen technical-vocational education and training as a strategic
instrument for national development, employment generation, poverty alleviation,
industrialization, technological advancement, entrepreneurship, and global
competitiveness.

       Toward this end, the State shall establish an integrated, accessible,
industry-responsive, innovation-driven, and competency-based technical education
system that produces highly skilled, productive, ethical, and globally competitive
Filipino workers capable of responding to the evolving demands of the local and
international labor markets.

       The State likewise recognizes the strategic role of the Region XII
(SOCCSKSARGEN) as a major center for agriculture, fisheries, agro-industrial
production, manufacturing, logistics, tourism, renewable energy, digital innovation,
and halal industry development. Accordingly, it shall strengthen institutional
capacities for technical education by integrating existing Technical Education and
Skills Development Authority (TESDA) Technology Institutions into a unified
regional polytechnic system that promotes efficiency, specialization, research,
technology development, and strong partnerships among government, industry,
educational institutions, and local government units.​

       SEC. 3. Establishment of the SOCCSKSARGEN Polytechnic Institute of
Technical Education. – There is hereby established a state-supported, industry-driven,
multi-campus technical-vocational and polytechnic institution to be known as the
                                                                                      3
SOCCSKSARGEN Polytechnic Institute of Technical Education (SPITE), hereinafter
referred to as the Institute.

​      The Institute shall operate under the administrative supervision and policy
oversight of the TESDA, through the TESDA Regional Office XII, and shall serve as
the premier regional institution for technical education, competency-based skills
training, applied research, technology innovation, entrepreneurship, and industry
collaboration in Region XII. TESDA shall continue to exercise policy direction,
quality assurance, standards setting, monitoring and evaluation, and performance
oversight to ensure alignment with national Technical-Vocational Education and
Training (TVET) policies, while the Institute shall enjoy operational autonomy in the
discharge of its functions, subject to TESDA rules and regulations.

​      The Institute shall possess perpetual succession and shall have the powers,
functions, privileges, and responsibilities provided under this Act and other applicable
laws.

SOCCSKSARGEN Polytechnic Institute of Technical Education:

          General Santos City;

       (d)​ Provincial Training Center, Malapatan, Sarangani Province;
       (e)​ Provincial Training Center, Kidapawan City, Cotabato Province;
       (f)​ Provincial Training Center, Pigcawayan, Cotabato Province;
       (g)​ Provincial Training Center, Lambayong, Sultan Kudarat; and
       (h)​ Provincial Training Center, Surallah, South Cotabato.

​      The General Santos National School of Arts and Trades shall serve as the Main
Campus of the Institute.

​      All campuses shall remain operational and shall function as constituent
campuses under a unified governance, academic, financial, administrative, and quality
assurance system established under this Act.
​
​      The integration of additional TESDA institutions within Region XII may be
authorized by the TESDA Board upon recommendation of the Board of Trustees of
the Institute and subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations.

​      SEC. 13. Meetings of the Board; Quorum. – The Board shall hold at least four
(4) regular meetings each year, one of which shall be designated as the annual
organizational meeting. Special meetings may be called by the Chairperson or upon
the written request of at least one-third (1/3) of all voting members of the Board. A
majority of all voting members shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of
business, and, unless otherwise provided by law, the affirmative vote of a majority of
the members present, there being a quorum, shall be required for the approval of any
action or resolution of the Board.

​      The Board may conduct its meetings through secure electronic or virtual
platforms in accordance with applicable laws and government regulations. The
Executive Director shall serve as the Secretary of the Board: Provided, That the Board
may designate another qualified official of the Institute to act as Board Secretary, who
shall have no voting rights.

​      The Board may convene a special meeting for the purpose of constituting the
Search Committee and evaluating the qualifications of nominees for Executive
Director.

​      SEC. 14. The Executive Director. – The Institute shall be headed by an
Executive Director, who shall be appointed by the Director General of the Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) from a shortlist of at least
three (3) qualified nominees submitted by the Board of Trustees through a duly
constituted Search Committee, in accordance with Republic Act No. 7796, civil
service laws, rules and regulations, and the provisions of this Act. The Board shall
promulgate rules governing the composition of the Search Committee and the conduct
of the search process, which shall be based on the principles of merit, competence,
                                                                                     11
integrity, transparency, and equal opportunity. The Executive Director shall serve for a
term of four (4) years, renewable for one (1) additional term, unless sooner removed
for cause in accordance with law.

​       No person shall be appointed Executive Director unless he or she is a
natural-born citizen of the Philippines; possesses unquestionable integrity,
competence, and administrative capability; holds at least a Master's Degree in
engineering, technology, education, public administration, business administration,
industrial management, or a related discipline recognized by the government:
Provided, That holders of a doctoral degree shall be given preference; has at least ten
(10) years of progressively responsible experience in technical education, higher
education, research, innovation, public administration, or industry management, at
least five (5) years of which shall have been in a managerial or executive position; has
not been convicted by final judgment of an offense involving moral turpitude; and
possesses such other qualifications as may be prescribed by the Board consistent with
existing laws.

​      The Executive Director shall receive compensation, allowances, and other
benefits in accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations. The position of
Executive Director shall have the equivalent rank of Director II with Salary Grade
(SG) 27, without prejudice to any subsequent adjustment authorized by law.

​       The Executive Director shall serve as the Chief Executive and Administrative
Officer of the Institute and shall exercise general supervision over its academic,
administrative, financial, research, extension, production, and support operations. He
or she shall implement the policies, plans, and resolutions of the Board; recommend
institutional policies, development plans, annual budgets, academic programs,
organizational reforms, and other initiatives; appoint faculty members, officials, and
employees in accordance with civil service laws and Board-approved policies, subject
to confirmation by the Board where required under this Act; execute contracts and
other legal instruments authorized by the Board; administer the funds, assets, and
properties of the Institute in accordance with existing accounting, auditing,
procurement, and budgeting laws; represent the Institute before government agencies,
local government units, educational institutions, industries, international
organizations, and other entities; submit to the Board an annual report on the
academic, administrative, financial, and operational performance of the Institute
together with recommendations for its continued development; exercise disciplinary
authority over faculty members, officials, employees, and students in accordance with
law and Board policies; and perform such other powers and functions as may be
assigned by the Board or provided under this Act.
​
​       Notwithstanding the integrated structure, SPITE shall remain under the
technical, administrative, and policy supervision of TESDA through the Regional
Director. The integration shall not diminish the Regional Director's authority to ensure
compliance with TESDA policies, quality standards, personnel administration, and
institutional performance requirements.

       (a)​ Academic Affairs;
       (b)​ Administration and Finance; and
       (c)​ Research, Innovation, Extension, Industry Partnerships, and Institutional
            Development.

​      Each constituent campus shall be headed by a Campus Chief, who shall have
the equivalent rank of Division Chief and shall exercise administrative, operational,
and supervisory authority over the assigned campus in accordance with this Act and
the approved organizational structure. The Campus Chief shall be appointed by the
Executive Director, subject to the confirmation of the Board of Trustees, based on
merit, competence, and the qualifications prescribed by the Board.

​     Each Campus Chief shall:
      (a)​ Exercise direct supervision over the academic and administrative operations
           of the campus;
      (b)​ Implement the policies, plans, and programs of the Institute within the
           campus;
      (c)​ Supervise faculty members, administrative personnel, and support staff;
      (d)​ Strengthen partnerships with local government units, industries, civil
           society organizations, and community stakeholders;
      (e)​ Oversee research, extension, innovation, production, and technology
           transfer initiatives of the campus; and
      (f)​ Perform such other duties and responsibilities as may be assigned by the
           Executive Director or the Board of Trustees.

​      The qualifications, compensation, duties, responsibilities, and performance
standards of the Deputy Executive Directors and Campus Chiefs shall be prescribed
by the Board of Trustees in accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations.

​      SEC. 16. Administrative Council. – There shall be an Administrative Council,
chaired by the Executive Director, which shall assist in the formulation and
implementation of policies relating to the administration, finance, human resource
management, infrastructure, procurement, planning, property management, and other
support services of the Institute.
                                                                                    13
​      The Administrative Council shall be composed of the Executive Director, as
Chairperson; the Deputy Executive Directors; the Campus Chiefs; the Chief
Administrative Officer; the Chief Finance Officer; the Chief for Planning and
Development; the Chief for Human Resource Management; the Chief for Procurement
and Supply Management; the Chief for Research, Innovation, and Extension; and such
other officials as may be designated by the Executive Director.

​       The Administrative Council shall recommend policies on administration,
budgeting, finance, procurement, infrastructure development, institutional planning,
personnel management, organizational development, records management, and
administrative reforms; review and recommend the annual budget and work and
financial plan of the Institute; monitor the implementation of Board policies and
institutional programs; evaluate institutional performance and recommend appropriate
corrective measures; and perform such other functions as may be assigned by the
Board or the Executive Director.

​     The Administrative Council shall meet at least once every quarter, or as often
as may be necessary.

​      SEC. 17. Academic Council. – There shall be an Academic Council, which
shall serve as the highest academic body of the Institute and shall formulate and
recommend policies governing academic affairs, technical education, research,
extension, and innovation.

​     The Academic Council shall be composed of the Executive Director, as
Chairperson; the Deputy Executive Director for Academic Affairs, as Vice
Chairperson; all Campus Chiefs; all Academic Directors; all permanent faculty
members holding plantilla positions; and such other academic officials as may be
designated by the Board.

​       The Academic Council shall recommend academic policies, curricula,
competency standards, diploma programs, training regulations, and academic and
training programs; prescribe admission, graduation, certification, and academic
standards consistent with TESDA policies; recommend the conferment of certificates,
diplomas, academic distinctions, and honorary awards; formulate policies governing
instruction, assessment, competency certification, student evaluation, and academic
integrity; promote excellence in teaching, applied research, innovation, extension, and
lifelong learning; recommend faculty development, scholarship, exchange, and
professional upgrading programs; and perform such other academic functions as may
be assigned by the Board.

​    All academic policies, standards, and recommendations adopted by the
Academic Council shall be subject to the approval of the Board of Trustees.

​      SEC. 18. Industry Advisory Council. – There is hereby created an Industry
Advisory Council, which shall serve as the principal advisory body of the Institute on
industry partnerships, workforce development, curriculum relevance, emerging
technologies, entrepreneurship, research collaboration, and labor market
responsiveness.

                                                                                    14
​      The Industry Advisory Council shall be composed of representatives from the
tuna processing and fisheries, agriculture and agro-processing, rubber, manufacturing,
construction, renewable energy, information and communications technology, logistics
and supply chain, tourism and hospitality, halal industry, banking and financial
services, local government, chambers of commerce, and employers' sectors, as well as
representatives from such other industries as may be determined by the Board.

​      The Industry Advisory Council shall recommend emerging skills requirements
and competency standards; assist in curriculum validation and development; facilitate
apprenticeship, dual training, internship, and employment opportunities; promote
technology transfer, innovation, and industry collaboration; recommend priority
research and extension programs; facilitate institutional partnerships and resource
sharing; and advise the Board on labor market trends, workforce requirements, and
industry priorities.

​     Members of the Industry Advisory Council shall serve without compensation
but may receive reimbursement for actual and necessary expenses incurred in the
performance of their official duties, subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations.

       (f)​ Office of Planning and Development;
       (g)​ Office of Administration;
       (h)​ Office of Finance and Resource Management;
       (i)​ Office of Human Resource Management;
       (j)​ Office of Student Affairs and Services;
       (k)​ Office of Quality Assurance and Accreditation;
       (l)​ Office of Information and Communications Technology;
       (m)​Office of Legal Affairs;
       (n)​ Office of Internal Audit;
       (o)​ Office of Procurement and Supply Management;
       (p)​ Office of Campus Development and Infrastructure;
       (q)​ Office of Community Extension and Public Relations; and
       (r)​ Such other offices as may be created by the Board to effectively carry out
          the purposes of this Act.

​       SEC. 20. Faculty, Personnel, and Human Resource Development. – The
Institute shall employ qualified faculty members, trainers, assessors, researchers,
extension specialists, administrative personnel, technical support staff, and such other
employees as may be necessary to carry out its mandate. Appointments to faculty and
administrative positions shall be made by the Executive Director in accordance with
the qualification standards prescribed by the Civil Service Commission (CSC), the
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and the Board of
Trustees.

​      Recruitment, selection, appointment, promotion, transfer, reassignment,
discipline, and separation from the service shall be governed by the principles of merit
and fitness and shall be subject to the Civil Service Law, its implementing rules and
regulations, and other applicable laws.

​      The Institute shall establish a comprehensive Faculty and Human Resource
Development Program to promote professional growth and institutional excellence.
Such program shall include graduate education scholarships, local and international
faculty exchange programs, industry immersion and apprenticeship, continuing
professional education, research grants and publication incentives, innovation and
technology commercialization incentives, performance-based recognition and awards,
leadership and executive development, and such other capacity-building initiatives as
may be approved by the Board.

​       Faculty members and personnel shall enjoy academic freedom, security of
tenure, opportunities for professional advancement, and such other rights, privileges,
and benefits as may be provided under existing laws, rules, and regulations. The
Institute shall likewise adopt a performance management system that promotes
excellence, accountability, innovation, integrity, and efficiency in the public service.

​       No employee of the TESDA Technology Institutions integrated into the
Institute shall be separated from the service solely by reason of the implementation of
this Act. All incumbent personnel shall be absorbed into the Institute without loss of
seniority, rank, salary, retirement benefits, leave credits, or other vested rights, subject
to existing laws, rules, and regulations.

​       Upon the effectivity of this Act, existing TESDA Specialist positions with
Salary Grade (SG) 22 assigned as heads of the integrated TESDA Technology
Institutions shall be reclassified as Campus Chief positions with the equivalent rank
and compensation of Salary Grade (SG) 24, subject to the applicable qualification
standards, staffing standards, position classification, and approval of the Department
of Budget and Management (DBM), the Civil Service Commission (CSC), and other
appropriate government authorities. Such reclassification is hereby recognized in view
of the expanded scope of functions, increased administrative responsibilities, and
integrated management of the campuses under the Institute's multi-campus
organizational structure, thereby ensuring parity in authority, accountability, and
compensation among campus heads.

​      SEC.        21.    Research, Extension, Innovation, and Technology
Commercialization. – The Institute shall serve as a regional center for applied
research, innovation, extension services, technology transfer, and entrepreneurship in
support of technical-vocational education and regional development. As such, it shall:
       (a)​ Undertake applied research responsive to industry needs and regional
            development priorities;
       (b)​ Establish research institutes, innovation hubs, fabrication laboratories,
            technology incubation centers, and extension service units;
       (c)​ Promote the development, protection, commercialization, and transfer of
            intellectual property, inventions, utility models, industrial designs, software,
            and other innovations generated by the Institute;
       (d)​ Encourage collaborative research with government agencies, higher
            education institutions, industries, local government units, and international
            organizations;
       (e)​ Provide technical assistance and extension services to micro, small, and
            medium enterprises (MSMEs), cooperatives, farmers, fisherfolk, indigenous
            peoples, and other community organizations; and
       (f)​ Formulate an institutional research agenda consistent with national and
            regional development plans.

​       Income derived from technology commercialization, consultancy services,
research grants, intellectual property rights, and similar activities shall accrue to the
Institute and shall be used exclusively for research, innovation, faculty development,
and institutional advancement, subject to existing laws.

​      SEC. 22. Industry Linkages and International Cooperation. The Institute
shall establish and maintain partnerships with industries, professional organizations,
local government units, educational institutions, development partners, and
international organizations to strengthen technical education, workforce development,
research, innovation, and technology transfer.

​      For this purpose, the Institute may enter into memoranda of agreement,
consortiums, and other collaborative arrangements; establish dual training systems,
apprenticeship, internship, and enterprise-based learning programs; implement faculty
and student exchange programs; participate in international research, innovation, and
technical cooperation initiatives; receive technical assistance, grants, scholarships,
equipment, and other forms of support from domestic and foreign sources; and
undertake joint certification, training, and competency assessment programs with
recognized local and international institutions.

​      All partnerships and agreements entered into pursuant to this Section shall be
subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations.

financial assistance program to promote equitable access to quality technical
education.

​      SEC. 27. Transfer of Assets, Properties, Records, Personnel, and Liabilities.
– Upon the effectivity of this Act, all lands, buildings, facilities, equipment, records,
appropriations, assets, liabilities, contractual obligations, rights, interests, and other
properties of the TESDA Technology Institutions integrated into the Institute under
this Act shall be transferred to and vested in the Institute.

​       All permanent personnel of the integrated TESDA Technology Institutions
shall be absorbed by the Institute without loss of seniority, rank, salary, retirement
benefits, leave credits, or other vested rights, subject to existing civil service laws,
rules, and regulations. The Institute shall likewise assume all pending administrative,
civil, contractual, and other lawful obligations of the integrated institutions.

​       The transfer of assets, properties, and records authorized under this Section
shall be exempt from all national and local taxes, transfer fees, registration fees,
documentary stamp taxes, and other lawful charges arising solely from the
implementation of this Act.

​       SEC. 28. Transition Committee and Five-Year Transition Plan. – Within sixty
(60) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Director General of the Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) shall constitute a Transition
Committee composed of representatives from TESDA, the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), the Civil Service Commission (CSC), the Commission on
Higher Education (CHED), the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development
(DEPDev), the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), the Department of Science
and Technology (DOST), the integrated campuses, and such other government
agencies as may be deemed necessary.
​
​       The Transition Committee shall formulate and implement a Five-Year
Transition Plan to ensure the orderly establishment and operationalization of the
Institute, including the institutional integration of the constituent campuses, transfer of
assets and liabilities, personnel placement, organizational restructuring, harmonization
of academic programs, quality assurance, financial integration, infrastructure
development, institutional branding, and such other transition activities as may be
necessary to carry out the purposes of this Act.

​      The Transition Committee shall submit annual progress reports to the Board of
Trustees and the TESDA Director General until the completion of the transition
period.

​       The Transition Committee shall cease to exist upon the approval by the Board

Technical Education and Skills Development Authority and the TESDA Technology
Institutions integrated under this Act. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for
the continued operation and development of the Institute shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.

​       SEC. 30. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the TESDA Director General, in consultation with the

Act.

​       SECTION 31. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and
effect.

regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
