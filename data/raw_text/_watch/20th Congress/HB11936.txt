                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Section 1 of Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that
the goals of the national economy are a more equitable distribution of
opportunities, income, and wealth, a sustained increase in the amount of goods
and services produced by the nation for the benefit of the people, and an
expanding productivity as the key to raising the quality of life for all, especially
the underprivileged. Complementing this mandate, Section 5 of Article XIII
recognizes the rights of farmers, farmworkers, cooperatives, and independent
farmers' organizations to participate in the planning, organization, and
management of agricultural development and directs the State to provide them
with support through appropriate technology and research, adequate financial,
production, marketing, and other support services.

       In fulfillment of these constitutional mandates, this measure seeks to
establish a comprehensive Agricultural Value Chain Development Program that
strengthens farm-to-market linkages through agricultural aggregation hubs,
cooperative-led logistics systems, digital agricultural trading platforms, and
value-adding enterprises. The proposed measure recognizes that agricultural
modernization extends beyond increasing farm productivity; it likewise requires
an efficient and integrated value chain that enables producers to participate
competitively in domestic and global markets while ensuring fair returns for their
labor.

       Agriculture remains fundamental to the country's food security, rural
employment, and economic development. However, despite decades of public
investment and policy reforms, the sector continues to face persistent structural
constraints arising from fragmented supply chains, inadequate post-harvest
infrastructure, inefficient logistics systems, weak market integration, and limited
participation of farmers in higher-value agricultural activities. These
longstanding inefficiencies contribute to significant post-harvest losses, unstable
farmgate prices, reduced market competitiveness, and persistent rural poverty.
The Philippine Development Plan (PDP) 2023–2028 recognizes these challenges
and identifies the modernization of agriculture and agribusiness through a whole-
of-value-chain approach, improved logistics, expanded market access, digital
transformation, and strengthened agricultural institutions as essential strategies
for achieving food security, increasing farmers' incomes, and promoting inclusive
economic growth.1
      More than twenty-five years after the enactment of the Agriculture and
Fisheries Modernization Act (Republic Act No. 8435), policy assessments
continue to show that while important gains have been achieved, substantial
implementation gaps remain in modernizing agricultural support services and
strengthening value chain integration. The Philippine Institute for Development
Studies (PIDS) emphasizes that the realization of agricultural modernization
requires stronger institutional coordination, greater investments in logistics and
post-harvest systems, and improved market linkages to enhance the
competitiveness of Filipino farmers and fisherfolk.2
       Similarly, PIDS research on Philippine agricultural value chains concludes
that the country's agricultural sector has yet to fully ascend the value-added ladder
due to weak forward and backward linkages among production, processing,
logistics, and marketing systems. The study underscores the need for integrated
value chain policies that facilitate product aggregation, logistics modernization,

       Recognizing these policy gaps, this proposed legislation institutionalizes a
coordinated national framework for agricultural value chain development by
establishing strategically located Agricultural Aggregation Hubs, strengthening
cooperative-led logistics systems, developing a National Digital Agricultural
Trading Platform, promoting value-adding enterprises, and enhancing market
development mechanisms. These interventions are intended to reduce post-
harvest losses, improve logistics efficiency, increase price transparency,
encourage agribusiness investments, strengthen farmers' bargaining power, and
expand access to domestic and international markets. Ultimately, the measure
seeks to transform Philippine agriculture into a resilient, technology-driven,
market-oriented, and globally competitive sector capable of generating
sustainable livelihoods, improving rural prosperity, and ensuring long-term
national food security.
      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                              CHAPTER I
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Agricultural
Value Chain Development Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to establish an integrated Agricultural Value Chain Governance Framework
that coordinates production, aggregation, post-harvest management, logistics,
processing, digital marketing, and market distribution in order to improve food
security, reduce post-harvest losses, increase farmers' and fisherfolk's incomes,
and promote rural industrialization.

      This Act shall supplement and strengthen, and shall not diminish or
supersede, the implementation of Republic Act No. 8435, otherwise known as the
Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization Act, Republic Act No. 11321, otherwise
known as the Sagip Saka Act, Republic Act No. 10611, otherwise known as the
Food Safety Act of 2013, and other existing agricultural laws by institutionalizing
an integrated national value chain governance system.

      SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act shall pursue the following objectives:

      (a) Increase the income, productivity, and competitiveness of Filipino
      farmers and fisherfolk;

      (b) Reduce post-harvest losses through modern storage, cold chain,
      transportation, and logistics systems;

      (c) Establish Agricultural Aggregation Hubs in strategic production areas;

      (d) Strengthen farm-to-market linkages through cooperative-led logistics
      systems;

      (e) Institutionalize a National Digital Agricultural Trading Platform;

      (f) Improve price transparency and market information systems;

      (g) Encourage value-added processing and agribusiness development;

      (h) Expand domestic and export market access for agricultural products;

      (i) Promote climate-resilient and disaster-responsive agricultural logistics
      systems; and

      (j) Encourage investments in agricultural infrastructure and digital
      innovation.

      SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall mean:

      (a) Agricultural Aggregation Hub refers to a centralized facility where
      agricultural commodities are collected, consolidated, sorted, graded,
      packed, stored, processed, and distributed before reaching wholesale or
      retail markets.

      (b) Agricultural Value Chain refers to the interconnected activities
      involved      in   the   production,   aggregation,       processing,   storage,
      transportation, marketing, distribution, and sale of agricultural products
      from producers to consumers.

      (c) Cooperative-led Logistics System refers to transportation, warehousing,
      cold storage, distribution, and delivery services managed by duly
      registered agricultural cooperatives or farmers' organizations.

      (d) Digital Agricultural Trading Platform refers to a government-
      supported electronic marketplace that enables direct trading between
      agricultural producers, cooperatives, processors, institutional buyers,
      exporters, and consumers.

      (e) Farmer Cooperative refers to a cooperative duly registered under
      Republic Act No. 9520 engaged in agricultural production, processing,
      marketing, logistics, or related agricultural services.

      (f) Institutional Buyers refer to supermarkets, hotels, restaurants, food
      processors, exporters, government agencies, educational institutions,
      hospitals, and similar entities purchasing agricultural products in bulk.

      (g) Value-Adding Enterprise refers to an enterprise engaged in processing,
      packaging, branding, preservation, or manufacturing              agricultural
      commodities into products with greater commercial value.


                                   CHAPTER II
   AGRICULTURAL VALUE CHAIN DEVELOPMENT PROGRAM

      SEC. 5. Establishment of the Agricultural Value Chain Development
Program. – The Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), shall establish the Agricultural Value
Chain Development Program, which shall serve as the national framework for
integrating agricultural production, aggregation, processing, logistics, marketing,
and distribution.

      SEC. 6. Agricultural Aggregation Hubs. – The DA, in partnership with
Local Government Units, State Universities and Colleges, agricultural
cooperatives, and the private sector, shall establish Agricultural Aggregation
Hubs in strategic agricultural production areas.

Each hub shall provide:

      (a) product consolidation;

      (b) sorting and grading;

      (c) quality inspection;

      (d) packaging;

      (e) warehousing;

      (f) cold storage;

                                                                                 4
      (g) digital inventory management;

      (h) logistics coordination;

      (i) market matching services; and

      (j) value-adding facilities.

      SEC. 7. Cooperative-Led Logistics Systems. – The government shall
strengthen accredited agricultural cooperatives by providing financial assistance,
technical support, and capacity-building programs to establish and operate
logistics systems, including transport vehicles, refrigerated trucks, mobile buying
stations, distribution centers, and delivery networks.

      The Cooperative Development Authority (CDA) shall assist in
strengthening the governance and operational capabilities of participating
cooperatives.

      SEC. 8. National Digital Agricultural Trading Platform. – The
Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), in
coordination with the DA and DTI, shall establish a National Digital Agricultural
Trading Platform that shall:

      (a) facilitate direct transactions between producers and buyers;

      (b) provide real-time market prices;

      (c) support electronic contracts and digital payments;

      (d) integrate logistics scheduling;

      (e) provide product traceability;

      (f) maintain digital inventories;

                                                                                 5
      (g) facilitate export documentation; and

      (h) generate market intelligence reports.

      SEC. 9. Cold Chain and Warehousing Facilities. – The DA shall
prioritize the construction and modernization of energy-efficient cold storage,
warehousing, and post-harvest facilities within Agricultural Aggregation Hubs to
reduce spoilage, preserve product quality, and strengthen supply chain resilience.

      SEC. 10. Market Information and Price Transparency System. – The
DA shall establish a real-time Agricultural Market Information System accessible
through digital platforms and mobile applications to provide registered farmers
with updated information on prevailing market prices, weather conditions,
production forecasts, and consumer demand.

      SEC. 11. Agricultural Product Traceability System. – The DA shall
establish a national digital traceability system enabling consumers and
institutional buyers to verify the origin, production practices, food safety
compliance, and quality standards of agricultural commodities.


                                CHAPTER III
          VALUE ADDITION AND MARKET DEVELOPMENT

      SEC. 12. Value-Adding Enterprises. – The DTI shall assist agricultural
cooperatives, micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs), and farmer
organizations in establishing value-adding enterprises through technical
assistance, product development, quality certification, branding, packaging, and
marketing support.

      SEC. 13. Market Linkage Agreements. – The DA and DTI shall facilitate
long-term market linkage agreements between agricultural cooperatives and
                                                                                6
institutional buyers, including supermarkets, hotels, restaurants, food processors,
exporters, government agencies, and school feeding programs.

      SEC. 14. Export Promotion Support. – The DTI, through the Board of
Investments (BOI), Center for International Trade Expositions and Missions
(CITEM), and Export Marketing Bureau (EMB), shall assist qualified agricultural
enterprises in complying with international standards and expanding export
opportunities.

      SEC. 15. Agricultural Financing and Credit Assistance. – The Land
Bank of the Philippines (LBP), Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP),
Agricultural Credit Policy Council (ACPC), and Philippine Crop Insurance
Corporation (PCIC) shall establish financing programs, credit facilities, crop
insurance, and guarantee mechanisms to support agricultural infrastructure,
logistics systems, aggregation hubs, processing facilities, and digital
technologies.


                                   CHAPTER IV
                 CAPACITY BUILDING AND INCENTIVES

      SEC. 16. Training and Technical Assistance. – The Agricultural Training
Institute (ATI), TESDA, CDA, and State Universities and Colleges shall conduct
continuing training programs on agribusiness management, logistics operations,
digital marketing, cooperative governance, value chain management, food safety
standards, and export readiness.

      SEC. 17. Incentives. – Qualified cooperatives, farmer organizations, and
agribusiness enterprises participating in the Program may receive:

      (a) matching grants;

                                                                                 7
      (b) equipment assistance;

      (c) logistics subsidies;

      (d) digitalization grants;

      (e) technical assistance;

      (f) research and innovation support; and

      (g) fiscal and non-fiscal incentives as may be provided under existing
      laws.

      SEC. 18. Public-Private Partnerships. – The DA may enter into Public-
Private Partnership (PPP) arrangements for the financing, construction,
operation, maintenance, and modernization of Agricultural Aggregation Hubs,
cold chain systems, logistics facilities, and digital infrastructure, subject to
existing laws.


                                   CHAPTER V
                                 ADMINISTRATION

      SEC. 19. Lead Implementing Agency. – The Department of Agriculture
shall serve as the lead implementing agency of this Act and shall coordinate with
all concerned government agencies and stakeholders.

      SEC. 20. Agricultural Value Chain Development Council. – There is
hereby created an Agricultural Value Chain Development Council chaired by the
Secretary of Agriculture and co-chaired by the Secretary of Trade and Industry.

The Council shall include representatives from:

      (a) Department of Information and Communications Technology;
                                                                               8
      (b) Department of Public Works and Highways;

      (c) Cooperative Development Authority;

      (d) Department of Science and Technology;

      (e) Department of the Interior and Local Government;

      (f) Land Bank of the Philippines;

      (g) Development Bank of the Philippines;

      (h) Agricultural Credit Policy Council;

      (i) Philippine Statistics Authority; and

      (j) three (3) representatives from nationally accredited farmers' and
      fisherfolk organizations.

      SEC. 21. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The Council shall establish a
Monitoring and Evaluation Framework and submit an annual report to Congress
containing:

      (a) the number of aggregation hubs established;

      (b) logistics systems operationalized;

      (c) cooperatives assisted;

      (d) reduction in post-harvest losses;

      (e) increase in farmers' income;

      (f) digital platform utilization;

      (g) investments generated; and

                                                                              9
       (h) recommendations for policy improvements.


                                   CHAPTER VI
                              FINAL PROVISIONS

       SEC. 22. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Department of Agriculture and other concerned agencies. Thereafter, such
sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.

       SEC. 23. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DA, in consultation with the DTI, DICT,
CDA, DOST, DILG, DPWH, and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate
the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

       SEC. 24. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 25. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
administrative issuances, rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 26. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
