                               AN ACT
DISQUALIFYING RELATIVES OF PUBLIC OFFICIALS WITHIN THE FOURTH CIVIL
DEGREE OF CONSANGUINITY AND AFFINITY IN ALL GOVERNMENT CONTRACTS

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

          Article XI, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution declares that public office is a
  public trust, mandating that public officers must at all times be accountable to the
  people and serve them with utmost responsibility, integrity, and efficiency.
  Furthermore, Section 27 of the Declaration of Principles and State Policies commands
  the State to maintain honesty and integrity in the public service and to take positive
  and effective measures against graft and corruption. In pursuit of these Constitutional
  mandates, the recently enacted Republic Act No. 12009, or the "New Government
  Procurement Act," seeks to strengthen the integrity of the bidding process by
  mandating the disclosure of relations up to the third civil degree of consanguinity or
  affinity. Despite these stringent benchmarks, public procurement remains vulnerable
  to the persistent challenges of patronage and perceived conflicts of interest.

         This measure seeks to institutionalize a robust "statutory firewall" by expanding
  the disqualification to include relatives of public officials within the fourth civil degree
  of consanguinity or affinity. This prohibition shall apply to all contracts with any
  government agency or instrumentality, including Government-Owned or -Controlled
  Corporations (GOCCs), Government Financial Institutions (GFIs), and Local
  Government Units (LGUs). By extending this safeguard, the bill aims to eliminate
  potential avenues for collusion, foster genuine competition, and ensure that the
  procurement process is governed solely by merit and the best interest of the
  government.

         In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1         SECTION 1. Disqualification on the Basis of Relations. - Notwithstanding any
 2     provision of the law to the contrary, all relatives of public officials within the fourth civil
 3     degree of consanguinity or affinity shall be barred from entering into any government
 4     contract. For the purposes of this Act, the term "public official" shall refer to heads of
 5     agency, heads of procuring entity, members of governing board, or any public officer or
 6     employee exercising policy-determining, supervisory, or managerial functions, whether
 7     in the career or non-career service, including military and uniformed personnel, whether
 8     or not they receive compensation, regardless of amount.
 9
10         Moreover, the term "government contract" shall refer to any contract between a
11     private entity and any government agency or instrumentality, including government
12     owned or controlled corporations (GOCCs) and local government units, that pertains to
13     a) supplies, materials, machinery, equipment, and services; (b) infrastructure projects;
14     (c) joint ventures; (d) public-private partnership projects; and (e) other similar or
15     analogous agreements or undertaking to which the latter is the procuring party, partner,
16     shareholder, implementing agency, end user, or a party contributing public funds or
17     property: Provided, That this shall exclude contracts that are highly technical,
18     proprietary, or confidential in nature.
19
20            SEC. 2. Mandatory Disclosure and Sworn Certification. - All bidders,
21     proponents, or contractors shall, as a mandatory requirement for eligibility, submit a
22     sworn certification under penalty of perjury, declaring that they are not related within
23     the fourth civil degree of consanguinity or affinity to any public official of the procuring
24     or contracting entity as defined in Section 1 of this Act. Failure to submit this certification

 4           SEC. 3. Penalties. - Any public official or employee who knowingly allows,

15   other government agencies concerned, shall jointly promulgate the rules and regulations
16   to effectively implement the provisions of this Act.
