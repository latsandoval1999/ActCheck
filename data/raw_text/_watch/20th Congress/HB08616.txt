 AN ACT INCREASING THE PENALTIES FOR PROHIBITED ACTS INIMICAL TO
THE PUBLIC INTERESTS AND NATIONAL SECURITY INVOLVING PETROLEUM

  PAMBANSA BLG. 33, AS AMENDED BY PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 1865

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

The recent conflict in the Middle East and its resulting effects in oil prices revealed the
necessity for strong protections against acts involving petroleum products that are
inimical to public interests. When the news broke of the impending oil price hike in the
country, there were several reports of hoarding and profiteering in the sale of
petroleum products.1In fact, as of 9 March 2026, the Department of Energy assessed
87 reports of excessive fuel price hikes, 26 of which are from Metro Manila, and issued
55 show cause orders to such reported gas stations.2 The Philippine National Police
has also increased police visibility in gas stations, to help in monitoring hoarding and
price manipulation.3

Batas Pambansa Blg. 33, first enacted in 1979, and later amended in 1983 by
Presidential Decree No. 1865 (PD 1865), was promulgated precisely to curb activities
involving petroleum and petroleum products that are inimical to the public interest and
national security, such as illegal trading, hoarding, overpricing, and misuse of
petroleum allocations. Under PD 1865, the penalties for these prohibited acts were

Since PD 1865 is a decades old law, the penalties prescribed are no longer at par with
more recently enacted laws involving petroleum products or those seeking to curb
similar behavior that seek to take advantage of Filipino consumers. For instance,
violations under the Downstream Oil Industry Deregulation Act of 1998, the law which
seeks to ensure a competitive market in the oil industry, are penalized with a fine
ranging from P50,000 to P300,000.5 Meanwhile, acts of cartelization and predatory
pricing are penalized with 3 to 7 years imprisonment, and a fine ranging from P1 million
to P2 million.

Further, under Republic Act No. 7581 or the The Price Act, illegal acts of price
manipulation of any basic necessity or prime commodity, such as hoarding and
profiteering, are punishable by imprisonment of not less than 5 years nor more than
15 years, and a fine of not less than P5,000 nor more than P2 million.

This measure seeks to increase the penalties under PD 1865, in recognition of the
abhorrent nature of these crimes, especially those that take advantage of Filipinos
during emergency situations or economic difficulties, for their own personal gain. To
curb these practices, this bill proposes to amend the fines from P20,000 - P50,000 to
P50,000 - P300,000, and raise the period of imprisonment from 2-5 years to 5-15
years. Strengthening these protections are necessary to deter individuals from these
exploitative practices.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Section 4 of Batas Pambansa Blg. 33, as amended by Presidential
Decree No. 1865, is hereby further amended as follows:

      Sec. 4. Penalties. — Any person who commits any act herein prohibited
      shall, upon conviction, be punished with a fine of not less than TWENTY
      FIFTY thousand pesos (P20,000) (P50,000) but not more than FIFTY
      THREE HUNDRED thousand pesos (P50,000) (P300,000), or
      imprisonment of at least two (2) FIVE YEARS but not more than FIVE
      FIFTEEN (5) (15) years, or both, in the discretion of the court. In cases
      of second and subsequent conviction under this Act, the penalty shall
      be both fine and imprisonment as provided herein. Furthermore, the
      petroleum and/or petroleum products subject matter of the illegal
      trading, adulteration, shortselling, hoarding, overpricing [and] OR
      misuse, shall be forfeited in favor of the Government: Provided, That if
      the petroleum and/or petroleum products have already been delivered
      and paid for, the offended party [the payment made] shall be
      indemnified twice the amount paid [the subject of forfeiture] and if the
      seller who has not yet delivered has been fully paid, the price received
      shall be returned to the buyer with an additional amount equivalent to
      such price; and in addition, if the offender is [a trader] an oil company,
