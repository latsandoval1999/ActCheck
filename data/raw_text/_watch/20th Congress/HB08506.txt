                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Education and skills development remain among the most critical
investments a nation can make in its people. A well-educated, highly skilled, and
adaptable workforce strengthens economic growth, promotes innovation, and
enhances a country’s global competitiveness. For developing economies such as
the Philippines, expanding access to quality education and training is essential
to preparing citizens for the evolving demands of the modern labor market.

      The State’s commitment to education is firmly anchored in the 1987
Constitution of the Philippines, which mandates under Article XIV, Section 1
that the State shall protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality
education at all levels and shall take appropriate steps to make such education
accessible to all. The Constitution likewise directs the State, under Article XIV,
Section 2(4), to encourage non-formal, informal, and indigenous learning
systems, as well as self-learning, independent, and out-of-school study programs
that respond to community needs.

       In pursuit of these constitutional objectives, Congress enacted Republic
Act No. 7796, which created the Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA). The law established a comprehensive framework for
technical education and skills development (TESD) in order to provide Filipinos
with practical competencies that support employment, entrepreneurship, and
national productivity. Section 3 of Republic Act No. 7796 expressly declares as
a policy of the State the promotion and strengthening of the quality of technical
education and skills development programs to attain international
competitiveness.

      Over the past decades, the role of technical and vocational education has
become even more crucial. Rapid technological advancements, digital
transformation, and evolving industry requirements have fundamentally altered
the nature of work. Emerging sectors such as advanced manufacturing,
information technology, renewable energy, and digital services increasingly
demand workers equipped with specialized technical skills and lifelong learning
capabilities.

      At the same time, the Philippines continues to face challenges relating to
job-skills mismatch, youth unemployment, and the need to upskill and reskill
workers in response to changing labor market conditions. Strengthening the
country’s technical education and skills development system is therefore
essential to ensuring that Filipino workers remain competitive both domestically
and in the global labor market.

       In this context, it is imperative that standards governing technical
education and skills development programs remain responsive to technological
advancements and industry needs. Curricula must be regularly reviewed and
updated, training programs must be aligned with evolving occupational
standards, and institutional mechanisms must be strengthened to ensure that
training providers deliver high-quality and relevant skills development programs.

       This measure seeks to strengthen the country’s technical education and
skills development framework by enhancing the capacity of relevant institutions
to update standards, improve curricula, and ensure that technical training
programs remain aligned with both domestic labor market needs and
international industry standards. The proposed reforms are intended to reinforce
the mandate of TESDA under Republic Act No. 7796 while complementing other
education and workforce development initiatives, including Republic Act No.
10533 and Republic Act No. 10968.

      Rather than conflicting with existing legislation, the proposed measure
strengthens the current legal and institutional framework by ensuring greater
responsiveness, quality assurance, and alignment between training programs
and the requirements of industry and emerging technologies.

      In an increasingly competitive global economy, the Philippines must
continue to invest in developing a workforce that embodies excellence,
adaptability, and a strong work ethic. By strengthening the country’s technical
education and skills development system, this measure contributes to building
a more productive, globally competitive, and future-ready Filipino workforce.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
 MANDATING THE TECHNICAL EDUCATION AND SKILLS DEVELOPMENT
 AUTHORITY (TESDA) TO UPDATE THE EXISTING TRAINING FOR WORK
SCHOLARSHIP PROGRAM (TWSP), SPECIAL TRAINING FOR EMPLOYMENT
 PROGRAM (STEP), AND TECHNICAL AND VOCATIONAL EDUCATION AND
                        TRAINING (TVET)

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “TESDA Skills
Training Act.”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to give
priority to education, science and technology, arts, culture, and sports to foster
patriotism and nationalism, accelerate social progress, and promote total human
liberation and development.

     SECTION 3. Scope. – This Act shall apply to the following programs of the
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority:

      a) Training For Work Scholarship Program (TWSP);
      b) Special Training For Employment Program (STEP); and
      c) Technical And Vocational Education And Training (TVET).

       SECTION 4. Roles and Responsibilities. – The Technical Education and
Skills Development Authority shall identify, prepare, and provide policy guidance
and technical resource assistance with regard to the inclusion of updates in the
existing programs and curricula of their Training For Work Scholarship Program
(TWSP), Special Training For Employment Program (STEP), and Technical And
Vocational Education And Training (TVET) in order to ensure globally competitive
programs of training for Filipinos enrolled in the same.
      SECTION 5. Socio-Civic Organizations and the Private Sector. – This Act
shall also empower the private sector in engaging and partnering with the
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority in promulgating these
programs. Non-governmental organizations, civic organizations, the academe,
people’s organizations, and the like shall be tapped by the Technical Education
and Skills Development Authority in updating such programs.
      SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. The Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority shall promulgate the implementing
rules and regulations of this Act within ninety (90) days from its effectivity.
       SECTION 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 9. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
