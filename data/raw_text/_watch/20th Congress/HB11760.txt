                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

       A substantial component of the government staﬃng operates under temporary
engagements or arrangements. As per the latest data from the Civil Service
Commission’s Inventory of Government Human Resources (IGHRS)1 as of June 30,
2026, there are 1,038,284 Job Order (JO), Contract of Service (COS), and Memorandum
of Agreement (MOA) [JO/COS/MOA] workers, and 178,850 Casual and Contractual
employees across the country. JO/COS/MOA workers account for 31.51% and Casual
and Contractual employees accounts 5.43% of the total workforce.

       It is a fact that there is strict legal limitation regarding their employment status.
Speciﬁcally, there is a lack of an employer-employee relationship based on the Civil
Service Commission, Commission on Audit, and Department of Budget and
Management Joint Circular No. 1, s. 2025. However, it is undeniable that they play an
important role in supporting government programs, projects, administrative
functions, and frontline services. The government must ensure that while they are
actively serving under their respective contracts, they still enjoy essential ﬁnancial
safety nets.

       To address this, this bill seeks to provide beneﬁts to JO, COS, Casual and
Contractual workers serving in the government. This measure lists beneﬁts which
include: 13th-month Pay, HMO coverage, Mid-year Bonus, Performance-based Bonus,
Sick Leave, PhilHealth Coverage, and Pag-IBIG Membership.

                             AN ACT
  GRANTING BENEFITS TO JOB ORDER, CONTRACT OF SERVICE, CASUAL,
      CONTRACTUAL WORKERS WORKING IN THE GOVERNMENT

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Government Contractual
Worker’s Benefits Act”.

        Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - To fully utilize its core human resources, the State
shall protect the rights of government workers and ensure and promote their welfare, and
recognize their hard work by granting them the necessary benefits to Job Order, Contract of
Service, Casual and Contractual employees.

        Section 3. Definition of terms – For the purposes of this Act, the following definitions
shall apply:

   a. Casual worker – refers to an individual working in government agency who was issued
      an appointment under casual status to perform essential and necessary services only
      where there are not enough regular staff to meet the demands of the service and for
      emergency cases for an intermittent period not exceeding one year;

   b. Contractual worker – refers to an individual working in a government agency refers
      to an individual working in government agency who was issued an appointment under
      contractual status to perform tasks under a special contract to undertake local or
      foreign-assisted projects or a specific work or job requiring special or technical skills
      not available in the employing agency to be accomplished within a specific period;
   c. Contract of Service (COS) – refers to the engagement of an individual was a
      consultant, learning service provider, or technical expert to undertake a special project
      or job within a specific period;

   d. Contractor or Service Provider – refers to an individual who is duly registered and
      recognized by an authorized government agency to provide consultancy services in
      their respective field of expertise;

   e. Job Order (JO) – refers to an individual directly hired or engaged by a government
      agency/office or through Human Resource recruitment agencies/firms to perform piece
      of work, i.e. “pakyaw”, or intermittent or emergency jobs such as clearing debris on the
      roads, canals, waterways, etc. after natural/man-made disasters/occurrences, and other
      manual or trades and crafts services such as carpentry, plumbing, electrical, and the
      like. These jobs are short duration and for a specific piece of work without an employer-
      employee relationship with the government.

        Section 4. Benefits for Long-Time Casual and Contractual Workers of the
Government . – It is hereby mandated that all incumbent Casual and Contractual Workers,
who have rendered a minimum five (5) aggregate years of service in government as of the
effectivity of this Act shall be entitled to the following privileges and benefits:

   a.   13th-Month Pay;
   b.   HMO Coverage;
   c.   Mid-Year Bonus;
   d.   Performance-Based Bonus;
   e.   Productivity Enhancement Incentives;
   f.   Sick leave;
   g.   PhilHealth Coverage; and
   h.   Pag-IBIG Membership.

        Section 5. Benefits for Job Order and Contract of Service workers of the
Government . – All JO and COS workers in the government shall be entitled to receive the
benefits as outlined in Section 3 of this Act, provided that they meet the following minimum
qualifications:

   a. Directly hired or engaged by the government through a contractor or service provider
      for a job or project and has been in service for at least six (6) months;
   b. Earning monthly compensation equivalent to or less than Salary Grade 7.

All Contractors or Service Providers are mandated to provide the abovementioned benefits to
qualified JO/COS workers. Failure to do so shall result in the cancellation of their accreditation
and imposition of applicable fines and/or penalties.

        Section 6. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this
Act shall be charged against the Contingent Fund, the Miscellaneous Personnel Fund, and other
applicable current appropriations. Thereafter, the amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be include in the General Appropriations Act.
        Section 7. Separability Clause. - If, for any reason, any provision of this Act is
declared to be unconstitutional or invalid, the other sections or provisions hereof which are not
affected thereby shall continue to be in full force or effect.

        Section 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations or parts
thereof which are inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended or modified accordingly.

       Section 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in a newspapers of general circulation or in the Official Gazette.
