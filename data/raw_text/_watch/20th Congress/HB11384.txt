21                                              EXPLANATORY NOTE
22
23   The country already has government agencies responsible for education, technical
24   training, employment, and industry. However, these efforts are handled separately.
25   There is no single body that specifically specializes in regularly bringing government,
26   academe, and industries together to monitor changes in the labor market, identify the
27   skills employers need, and determine whether the courses and training being offered
28   are keeping pace with those needs.
29
30   Without this coordination, gaps can remain between what students are taught and what
31   the labor market requires.
32
33   Hence, this proposed measure seeks to create a Tripartite Council composed of
34   representatives from government, academe, and industry. The Council will regularly
35   gather information on employment, unemployment, underemployment, and skills
36   requirements, review existing academic and technical training programs, and
37   recommend policies and programs based on the actual needs of the labor market.
38
39   Formal education and hands-on training should keep pace with the jobs available, and
40   the skills of Filipino workers should keep pace with the needs of the industries that
41   employ them.
42
43   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

21                                AN ACT
22       CREATING A TRIPARTITE COUNCIL TO ADDRESS UNEMPLOYMENT,
23     UNDEREMPLOYMENT, AND THE JOB-SKILLS MISMATCH PROBLEM IN THE
24             COUNTRY, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR
25
26   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
27   Congress assembled:
28
29           SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. – It is declared the policy of the State to
30   promote the sustained development of a skilled citizenry and maintain a reservoir of
31   national talents consisting of Filipino scientists, entrepreneurs, professionals,
32   managers, high-level technical manpower, and skilled workers and craftsmen in all
33   fields. Skills development can reduce unemployment and underemployment, increase
34   productivity, and improve the standards of living.
35
36          To this end, the State shall ensure the relevance and adequacy of academic
37   courses in the higher education curriculum and training programs offered in
38   technical-vocational institutions in relation to the needs and requirements of the
39   business and industry sectors and the over-all human resource requirement of the
40   country pursuant to its economic development goals.
41
42           SEC. 2. Creation of a Tripartite Council. – There is hereby established a
43   Tripartite Council, hereinafter referred to as the Council, that shall formulate policies
44   and programs to address the job-skills mismatch in the country. It shall be a
45   coordinating body among the government, academe and industry sectors to primarily
46   monitor economic trends in the global and domestic markets, including those pertaining
47   to business and commerce and the local labor market, and to generate information
48   relative      to     employment, unemployment, underemployment, and job-skills
49   mismatch. The Council shall be made up of representatives from the government,
50   academe, and industry.
51
52       The government representatives shall be composed of the following: a
53   Commissioner of the Commission on Higher Education (CHED); a Deputy Director-
 1   General of the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA); an
 2   Undersecretary of the Department of Education (DepEd); an Undersecretary of the
 3   Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE); and an Undersecretary of the
 4   Department of Trade and Industry (DTI).
 5
 6          The representatives of the academe shall be the presidents of the federations
 7   of public and private higher education institutions (HEIs), public and private
 8   technical-vocational institutions (TVIs), and accreditation bodies of higher and
 9   technical-vocational educational institutions, which include the following: Philippine
10   Association of State Universities and Colleges; Association of Local Colleges and
11   Universities; Coordinating Council of Private Educational Associations; Technical-
12   Vocational Schools and Associations of the Philippines; National Network of Quality
13   Assurance Agencies; and the Federation of Accrediting Agencies of the Philippines.
14
15          The representatives of the industry sector shall be the presidents of the
16   following business and industry federations: Employers Confederation of the
17   Philippines; Makati Business Club; Semiconductor and Electronics Industries in the
18   Philippines Foundation, Incorporated; Information Technology and Business Process
19   Association of the Philippines; Philippine Business for Education; Management
20   Association of the Philippines; Philippine Business for Social Progress; and the People
21   Management Association of the Philippines.
22
23         The Council shall be headed by a CHED Commissioner as Chairperson, and the
24   Deputy Director-General of the TESDA shall be its Vice-Chairperson. The members of
25   the Council, except for the ex officio members, shall receive per diem for every meeting
26   attended in accordance with existing rules and regulations.
27
28         The Council shall be attached to the CHED for administrative and budgetary
29   purposes. The CHED shall provide a secretariat to the Council to be manned by the
30   organic personnel of the CHED and may be augmented by the employees from the
31   member-agencies of the Council through detail or secondment.
32
33          SEC. 3. Powers and Functions of the Council. – The Council shall have the
34   following specific powers and functions:
35
36            a) Monitor the trends in the labor market, both domestic and international,
37            and the incidence of unemployment, underemployment and the job-skills
38            mismatch problem in the country;
39
40            b) Formulate short-term and long-term policies, plans and programs that shall
41            address job-skills mismatch, unemployment, and underemployment;
42
43            c) Conduct an inventory, review and evaluation of courses, academic
44            programs and curricula of public and private HEIs and training programs
45            implemented by TVIs;
46
47            d) Conduct an assessment of qualifications, skills and competencies of
48            students and graduates of higher education courses and technical-vocational
49            education and training and benchmark these with national and international
50            standards such as the Philippine Qualifications Framework, Association of
51            Southeast Asian Nations Qualifications Reference Framework, other
 1            frameworks, and international agreements to which the Philippines is a
 2            signatory;
 3
 4            e) Conduct an inventory of job specifications and skills requirement of various
 5            industries;
 6
 7            f) Recommend to the President of the Philippines, Congress and other
 8            government institutions policy measures that need to be undertaken in
 9            addressing the problems of unemployment, underemployment and job-skills
10            mismatch;
11
12            g) Hold regular quarterly meetings; and
13
14            h) Perform such other functions as may be necessary for its effective operation
15            to achieve the mandate of this Act.
16
17          SEC. 4. Annual Report and Assessment. – Upon the effectivity of this Act, the
18   Council shall submit an annual report to Congress on the status of employment of
19   college students and new graduates for the last five years, as well as an assessment of
20   the trends in the labor market and the skills required by various industries and business
21   establishments.
22
23          SEC. 5. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of
24   this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
25
26          SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
27   after the effectivity of this Act, the CHED shall, in coordination with the TESDA,
28   DepEd, DTI and DOLE, as well as other education stakeholders, issue the rules and
29   regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.
30
31          SEC. 7. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any portion or provision of
32   this Act shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected
33   thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.
34
35          SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
36   rules and regulations, issuances or parts thereof, which are inconsistent with the
37   provisions of this Act, are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.
38
39          SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
40   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
