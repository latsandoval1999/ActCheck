                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Constitution recognizes education as a fundamental instrument for national
development and mandates the State to promote quality education, advance science
and technology, and ensure that institutions of higher learning remain centers of
excellence, innovation, and public service. In an increasingly interconnected world, the
ability of Philippine higher education institutions to attract and retain globally
competitive Filipino scholars has become essential to strengthening the country's
academic and research capabilities.

      Republic Act No. 9225, otherwise known as the Citizenship Retention and Re-
acquisition Act of 2003, allows natural-born Filipinos who have reacquired Philippine
citizenship to enjoy full civil and political rights. However, Section 5(3) of the law
requires dual citizens appointed to public office to renounce their oath of allegiance to
a foreign state before assuming office. While this requirement is appropriate for
positions involving the exercise of sovereign governmental powers, its application to
faculty members, researchers, and academic administrators in public higher education
institutions has inadvertently discouraged many highly qualified Filipino professionals
abroad from serving in the country's state universities and colleges.

      Philippine public higher education institutions increasingly compete for world-
class educators, scientists, researchers, and academic leaders whose expertise has
been developed through international education, research, and professional
experience. Many of these individuals have acquired foreign citizenship while
maintaining strong ties to the Philippines and have subsequently reacquired their
Philippine citizenship under Republic Act No. 9225. Requiring them to renounce their
foreign allegiance as a condition for academic service often creates legal, professional,
and personal barriers that discourage their return, resulting in the loss of valuable
expertise that could otherwise strengthen the country's research, innovation, and
human capital development.

    This measure seeks to amend Section 5(3) of Republic Act No. 9225 by
exempting faculty members, researchers, and administrators of public higher
education institutions from the requirement of renouncing their foreign oath of

allegiance. The proposed amendment recognizes that these positions primarily
advance education, research, and knowledge generation rather than the exercise of
sovereign political authority. At the same time, these individuals remain bound by their
oath of allegiance to the Republic of the Philippines, the Constitution, and all applicable
laws governing public service.

      By removing an unnecessary barrier to academic engagement, this measure will
encourage more highly qualified Filipino scholars, scientists, innovators, and
academic leaders abroad to contribute their knowledge and experience to Philippine
higher education. Their participation will strengthen research collaboration, improve
instructional quality, expand international partnerships, enhance technology transfer,
and ultimately contribute to the country's scientific, economic, and social development.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
