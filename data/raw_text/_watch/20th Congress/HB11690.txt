EXPLANATORY NOTE

An affordable and reliable electricity is a prerequisite for household welfare, investment,
industrialization, and regional development. Twenty-five years after Republic Act No. 9136
reorganized the power industry, the legislative priority must shift from asset disposition toward
measurable consumer affordability, efficient service, fair competition, and responsible expansion of
generation and networks.

Ordinary households confront bills composed of generation, transmission, distribution, taxes
and authorized adjustments, while businesses face high delivered power costs and reliability risks. A
reform that merely changes the owner of an asset will not necessarily reduce these costs. This
measure therefore suspends further mandatory privatization of remaining government power assets
while preserving existing lawful contracts, and opens pathways for new public, cooperative,
local-government and private investment under competitive and transparent conditions.

The bill introduces targeted zero-rated VAT treatment for the first 300 kilowatt-hours of
monthly residential consumption; progressively stricter technical loss benchmarks and non-recovery
of avoidable nontechnical losses; itemized billing, automatic restitution and utility performance
standards; mandatory plans for objectively designated high-electricity-cost areas; stronger local
government participation in energy planning and investment facilitation; and accelerated, competitive
development of distributed generation, storage and grid connections.

The bill does not promise that electricity can be supplied without unavoidable network costs.
Instead, it prohibits charging consumers for systems loss, proven inefficiency and unlawful
expenditures while allowing the recovery of prudent, efficient and verifiable costs. It safeguards rural
electric cooperatives through transitional modernization assistance tied to results, not perpetual
subsidies. It similarly protects investment certainty through prospective application and respect for
vested contractual rights.

The measure amends specific provisions of Republic Act No. 9136 and makes a consequential
amendment to Section 108 of the National Internal Revenue Code. It complements Republic Act No.
11552 on lifeline rates, Republic Act No. 11646 on microgrids, Republic Act No. 9513 on renewable
energy, and applicable competition, procurement, and local government laws. The fiscal cost of tax
relief shall be reported annually, and the entire reform shall undergo independent evaluation.

===== [page 2] =====
The bill further creates a narrowly defined public-welfare emergency mechanism allowing an
affected local government unit, upon ERC approval and with NEA participation, to assume
temporary operational management of a seriously failing electric cooperative. An initial intervention
may last no more than six months, subject to justified extensions not exceeding twenty-four months
in aggregate absent a subsequent Act of Congress. This extraordinary remedy preserves
member-consumer ownership, requires notice and hearing or promptly reviewed emergency
measures, mandates professional management and audited accounts, and guards against political
interference. It expressly coordinates with the NEA step-in framework under Republic Act No.
10531.

In view of the foregoing, immediate passage of this bill is respectfully sought.

JERRY E. REREZ

Section 1. Short Title.

This Act shall be known as the “Electricity Affordability and Competitiveness Reform Act of
2027” or “Enhanced EPIRA Act.”

Section 2. Amendment to Section 2 of Republic Act No. 9136.

Section 2 is hereby amended by adding, after its existing declaration of policies, the following
paragraphs:

“The State shall make affordability to residential end-users, reliable service, efficient cost
recovery, and national and local investment competitiveness express objectives of electricity policy.
Consumers shall not be required to bear costs arising from proven fraud, imprudence, preventable
nontechnical losses, or unauthorized charges. Government shall promote additional generation and
storage capacity, fair competition, consumer choice, and local participation without making further
privatization of government-owned assets a condition for energy reform. These objectives shall be
implemented consistently with energy security, environmental protection, due process, and the
financial sustainability of efficient service providers.”

Section 3. Additional Definitions in Section 4.

Section 4 is hereby amended by inserting the following definitions, without renumbering or
deleting existing definitions:

|. “High-Electricity-Cost Area” means a geographic area or distribution franchise area
designated by the DOE pursuant to Section 37-A on the basis of objectively verified tariffs,
household affordability, business cost burden, reliability, and feasible alternatives.

2. “Avoidable Nontechnical Loss” means systems loss or energy not billed or recovered because
of theft, tampering, erroneous metering, deficient controls or accounting, or other nonphysical
causes, to the extent established through an ERC-approved independent audit.

===== [page 4] =====
3. “Efficient Technical Loss” means the minimum reasonably achievable physical loss under
prudent network design, maintenance and operating conditions, determined using an
ERC-approved benchmark.

4. “Local Energy Development Plan” means an LGU plan identifying local demand,
affordability concerns, generation and storage opportunities, resilience requirements, and
proposed investments, aligned with the Philippine Energy Plan.

Section 4. New Section 37-A: High-Electricity-Cost Areas.
A new Section 37-A is hereby inserted after Section 37, to read as follows:

“SEC. 37-A. High-Electricity-Cost Areas and Corrective Action. Within twelve (12) months
from effectivity, the DOE, in consultation with the ERC, DTI, NEDA or its successor agency, LGUs,
consumer groups and affected utilities, shall publish a transparent methodology for designating
High-Electricity-Cost Areas. The methodology shall consider comparable delivered tariffs,
purchasing power, industrial cost burden, service reliability, and supply alternatives. Within ninety
(90) days after designation, the DOE shall convene the affected LGU and utilities to prepare a
three-year corrective action plan identifying procurement, generation, storage, network, efficiency,
and demand-management options, with estimated costs and consumer savings. Plans and quarterly
progress reports shall be public. Designation shall not authorize a new consumer surcharge or
preferential procurement without competition.”

Section 5. New Section 37-B: Local Government Participation.
A new Section 37-B is hereby inserted, to read as follows:

“SEC. 37-B. Local Energy Development and Investment Facilitation. — Provinces, cities and
municipalities may adopt Local Energy Development Plans; identify suitable sites; coordinate lawful
permits; facilitate community generation, storage and efficiency projects; participate in qualified
projects or public-private partnerships; and aggregate their own eligible public-facility electricity
demand, subject to applicable market, procurement and competition rules. LGUs may participate as
microgrid system providers only as authorized under Republic Act No. 11646 and shall not, by
reason of this section alone, acquire a distribution franchise, retail-supply license, or authority to set
rates. The DOE and DILG shall provide model plans and technical assistance. LGU investments shall
require feasibility studies, public disclosure, conflict-of-interest safeguards, and separate accounts; no
local project loss shall be added to regulated electricity tariffs without independent ERC
authorization.”

Section 6. New Section 37-C: Distributed and Local Generation.
A new Section 37-C is hereby inserted, to read as follows:

“SEC. 37-C. Competitive Local Generation and Storage Program. — The DOE shall establish
a program supporting qualified small and medium generation, storage, demand response and
community energy projects in high-cost, underserved, industrial, island, urban, and rural areas.
Participation shall be open to qualified public entities, electric cooperatives, LGUs, community
enterprises and private investors. The ERC shall prescribe proportionate, published interconnection
standards and deadlines, subject to technical safety and grid capacity. Distribution utilities shall
evaluate eligible local resources on the basis of total efficient delivered cost and reliability through
transparent competitive procurement where required by law. No technology, project sponsor, or LGU
shall be guaranteed above-market tariffs solely by reason of locality. Existing rights under the
Renewable Energy Act and Microgrid Systems Act shall remain applicable.”

Section 8. New Section 43-A: Prohibition on Improper Pass-Through Charges.

===== [page 6] =====
A new Section 43-A is hereby inserted, to read as follows:

“SEC. 43-A. Consumer Cost Protection. No regulated electricity service provider shall
recover from end-users fines, penalties, fraud-related losses, imprudent affiliate transactions,
unauthorized expenditures, administrative inefficiencies, or costs disallowed under this Act or by a
final order of the Energy Regulatory Commission.

Any cost or system loss prohibited from recovery under Section 43(f) of this Act and Section
10 of Republic Act No. 7832, as amended, shall not be incorporated, allocated, capitalized, deferred,
transferred, or otherwise recovered through generation charges, transmission charges, distribution
charges, universal charges, subsidies, adjustments, regulatory assets, operating expenses, revenue
requirements, or any other tariff component.

No regulated entity shall circumvent this prohibition through affiliated transactions,
contractual arrangements, accounting reclassifications, or other mechanisms having the effect of
transferring prohibited costs to consumers.

Each electricity bill shall separately disclose the generation, transmission, distribution,
authorized technical system-loss, tax, subsidy, and adjustment components actually imposed, together
with their corresponding legal or regulatory basis.

The ERC shall conduct regular compliance audits, establish simplified consumer complaints
procedures, and order the refund or credit of unlawful collections, together with interest at the
applicable legal rate.

The recovery of prudent and efficiently incurred costs expressly authorized by law shall
remain permissible, provided that such costs do not include amounts prohibited under this Act.”

Section 9. New Section 43-B: Performance and Reliability.

A new Section 43-B is hereby inserted, to read as follows:

“SEC. 43-B. Utility Performance Standards. Within twelve (12) months from the effectivity of
this Act, the Energy Regulatory Commission shall prescribe and enforce utility-specific performance
standards covering service interruptions, restoration time, connection processing, billing accuracy,
complaint resolution, and technical and nontechnical system losses.

Every distribution utility, electric cooperative, and regulated transmission operator shall
prepare and implement a System-Loss Reduction and Network Efficiency Plan containing
independently verifiable baseline data, annual reduction targets, preventive maintenance measures,
metering improvements, anti-pilferage controls, and modernization requirements.

The ERC shall establish progressively declining technical-loss benchmarks based on
independently verified engineering and operational conditions. Compliance shall be monitored
through annual independent audits and publicly disclosed performance reports.

Failure to meet approved benchmarks shall require the submission and implementation of a
corrective action plan within a period prescribed by the ERC. Repeated or willful noncompliance
shall constitute grounds for administrative sanctions under existing law.

The ERC shall provide proportionate automatic service credits for material violations
attributable to the utility, subject to applicable due process, force majeure safeguards, and protection
against duplicate recovery.

No penalty, disallowed loss, or cost attributable to proven utility negligence, fraud, or
inefficiency shall be passed on to electricity consumers.”

Section 10. New Section 43-C: Competition and Affiliated Transactions.
A new Section 43-C is hereby inserted, to read as follows:

“SEC. 43-C. Competition and Procurement Integrity. The ERC shall, in coordination with the
Philippine Competition Commission, conduct periodic market concentration and pivotal-supplier
assessments, publish nonconfidential results, and review affiliate power-supply transactions for
prudence and competitive fairness. Competitive procurement rules shall provide transparent bid
criteria, conflict-of-interest disclosure, independent evaluation, and accessible complaints processes.
The DOE and ERC shall publish a phased plan for consumer aggregation and expanded supplier
choice, without exposing residential customers to unregulated or misleading retail offers.”

Section 11. Amendment to Section 47: Suspension of Mandatory Privatization.

===== [page 7] =====
Section 47 is hereby amended by inserting the following paragraph at its beginning, which
shall prevail over inconsistent mandatory disposition deadlines in that section:

“SEC. 47. NPC Asset Stewardship and Suspension of Mandatory Privatization.
Notwithstanding any provision of this Act requiring the sale, transfer or disposition of remaining

other competent agencies shall submit within one hundred eighty (180) days a public inventory of
remaining assets, liabilities, contracts and proposed stewardship arrangements. Nothing herein
prohibits new investment in generation by qualified public or private entities.”

SEC. 12. New Section 47-A: Public Asset Accountability.
A new Section 47-A is hereby inserted, to read as follows:

“SEC. 47-A. Government Power Asset Management. Government-owned power assets
retained under Section 47 shall be managed under published financial, reliability and operating
performance targets, independent annual audits, transparent procurement, and separate accounting.
No retained asset shall be used to confer discriminatory market advantages, and any public service
obligation shall be expressly funded and reported.”

Section 13. Addition to Section 73: Lifeline Administration.

Section 73, as amended by Republic Act No. 11552, is hereby further amended by adding the

“The DOE, DSWD, ERC and distribution utilities shall establish, subject to the Data Privacy

Section 14. Amendment to Section 108 of the National Internal Revenue Code.

Section 108(B) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, is hereby further
amended by adding the following zero-rated transaction:

“Sale of electricity to a residential end-user, including the associated regulated transmission
and distribution services billed to such end-user, to the extent attributable to the first three hundred
(300) kilowatt-hours of electricity consumed in each monthly billing cycle, or its pro-rated equivalent
for a shorter billing period. For consumption beyond such threshold, the applicable VAT rules shall
govern. The BIR, in consultation with the DOF and ERC, shall prescribe a method for allocation of
consideration, input tax treatment, invoicing and verification that prevents double benefit and ensures
full pass-through of the tax relief. The ERC shall require the corresponding reduction in billed
amounts and shall prohibit recovery of the forgone VAT through another rate component. The DOF
shall publish annually the fiscal cost and distributional incidence of this incentive.”

Section 15. Energy Affordability and Grid Modernization Support.
The Department of Energy (DOE), in coordination with the Energy Regulatory Commission,

National Electrification Administration, and other concerned agencies, shall establish, subject to
annual appropriations, a competitively administered program supporting electricity affordability,

distribution efficiency, system-loss reduction, grid modemization, energy storage, interconnection,
and qualified local generation projects.

Priority assistance shall be extended to High-Electricity-Cost Areas, financially distressed
electric cooperatives, underserved communities, and areas where excessive technical losses
materially contribute to high electricity tariffs.

Eligible projects may include network rehabilitation, replacement of inefficient transformers
and distribution lines, installation of advanced metering infrastructure, technical and financial audits,
anti-pilferage systems, energy storage, and other investments independently demonstrated to reduce
electricity costs or improve service reliability.

Financial assistance shall be subject to feasibility studies, transparent selection procedures,
measurable performance targets, milestone-based disbursements, public disclosure, and independent
verification of consumer or reliability benefits.

Recipients shall be required to implement approved system-loss reduction plans and
demonstrate measurable improvements as a condition for continued assistance. Funds obtained
through fraud, misrepresentation, or material nonperformance shall be subject to recovery in
accordance with applicable law.

No new universal charge, system-loss surcharge, or other additional consumer charge shall be
imposed to finance the program. Assistance shall be funded through lawful appropriations and other
legally available financing sources.

Nothing in this section shall authorize the recovery of nontechnical or excessive technical
system losses prohibited under Section 43(f) of Republic Act No. 9136, as amended, and Section 10
of Republic Act No. 7832, as amended.

Section 17. Insertion of Section 22-A in Republic Act No. 9136; Extraordinary Local
Government Intervention in Electric Cooperatives.

A new Section 22-A is hereby inserted immediately after Section 22 of Republic Act No.
9136, to read as follows:

“SEC. 22-A. Temporary Local Government Unit Management in Exceptional Public- Welfare
Circumstances. Where exceptional or extraordinary circumstances seriously endanger the continuous,
safe, reliable, or affordable distribution of electricity in the franchise area of an electric cooperative,
the concerned provincial, city, or municipal local government unit may, pursuant to the general
welfare mandate under Section 16 of Republic Act No. 7160, petition the Energy Regulatory
Commission (ERC) for authority to assume temporary operational management of the electric
cooperative, subject to the conditions herein prescribed.

a. Grounds. Intervention may be authorized only upon clear and convincing evidence of: (1)
sustained and material failure to deliver electricity or comply with essential reliability and
safety standards; (2) grave operational or financial distress creating an imminent risk of
widespread service interruption; (3) serious and persistent management failure or unlawful
practices materially prejudicing member-consumers; or (4) a calamity or comparable
emergency that renders ordinary remedial measures inadequate. High tariffs alone, absent
evidence of a qualifying failure and a feasible corrective plan, shall not justify intervention.

===== [page 9] =====
. Application and jurisdiction. Before any intervention under this Act may be authorized, the
Sanggunian of the concerned city or province shall adopt a resolution stating the factual
grounds for intervention, the public necessity thereof, the financial capacity of the Local
Government Unit, the proposed professional management structure, and a time-bound service
restoration plan. The Local Government Unit shall thereafter file a verified petition with the
ERC and furnish copies thereof to the NEA, the concerned electric cooperative, and its
member-consumers. The NEA shall be an indispensable party and shall submit its assessment
of the cooperative and any existing or proposed actions under Presidential Decree No. 269, as
amended by Republic Act No. 10531. The ERC shall determine the necessity, scope,
conditions, and duration of the proposed intervention, taking into account consumer welfare,
service reliability, and the public interest, while fully recognizing and coordinating with the
NEA's statutory supervision, disciplinary authority, and step-in powers.

Due process and emergency relief. Except where an imminent and grave threat to public
safety or widespread interruption requires immediate provisional relief, the ERC shall provide
notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard to the cooperative, NEA, affected
member-consumers, and other interested parties. A provisional order shall state specific
findings of urgency, shall be effective for no more than thirty (30) days unless confirmed after
an expedited hearing, and shall not dispense with subsequent due process. ERC approval shall
be by a reasoned, publicly available order.

. Duration. Temporary LGU management shall be authorized for an initial period not exceeding
twelve (12) months. The ERC may, after public hearing and upon a verified finding that the
original grounds persist and that continuation is necessary and proportionate, grant extensions
of not more than six (6) months each. In no case shall the aggregate period of intervention
exceed thirty-six (36) months. Any extension beyond thirty-six (36) months shall require
express authority under a subsequent Act of Congress. Successive petitions, substitutions of
LGUs, or changes in the management team shall not restart the aggregate period.

Scope and limitations. The authority shall be confined to day-to-day operational management
and corrective actions specified in the ERC order. It shall not transfer ownership, membership
rights, cooperative assets, or the congressional franchise; authorize sale or encumbrance of
material assets; permit unilateral modification of ERC-approved rates; or extinguish existing
lawful contractual obligations. All procurement and disbursements shall comply with
applicable law and conflict-of-interest safeguards. The intervention shall not be construed as
privatization or expropriation.

Professional management and safeguards. The LGU shall designate a qualified, independent
management team meeting NEA fit-and-proper standards and ERC technical requirements.
Incumbent elective local officials and persons with prohibited financial interests shall not
exercise day-to-day managerial functions. Separate accounts, an independently verified
opening inventory, quarterly financial and service-performance reports, and public disclosure
of material transactions shall be required. No intervention cost may be passed through to
electricity consumers without specific ERC authorization upon proof of prudence and
necessity.

. Coordination, monitoring, and exit. The ERC and NEA shall jointly monitor compliance with
the approved corrective plan. The ERC may terminate intervention earlier for failure to meet
its conditions, abuse, conflict of interest, or restoration of stable operations. At least sixty (60)
days before expiry, the management team shall submit an audited turnover and continuity
plan. Operational control shall revert to the lawful cooperative governing authorities or to an
otherwise lawfully designated NEA management arrangement, subject to applicable due
process and existing law.

. Accountability and judicial review. Officials and managers responsible for fraud, gross
negligence, misuse of funds, or unlawful interference shall be subject to administrative, civil,
and criminal liability under applicable laws. Aggrieved parties may seek judicial review under

===== [page 10] =====
the rules governing ERC orders and NEA actions. The exercise of powers under this section
shall respect the constitutional requirements of due process and protection of property rights.

Section 4-B of Presidential Decree No. 269, as amended, is hereby further amended by adding

“Notwithstanding the foregoing, where an extraordinary public-welfare intervention involving

Section 19. Consumer Impact and Fiscal Review.

Within one hundred eighty (180) days from effectivity, DOE, ERC and DOF shall establish a
baseline for real residential tariffs, household energy burden, commercial and industrial delivered
tariffs, outages, recoverable system losses, investment and competition. Annual public reports shall
state actual savings, forgone VAT revenues, appropriated subsidies and distributional impacts,
disaggregated by region and income where feasible. An independent evaluation shall be transmitted
to Congress after three (3) years and every five (5) years thereafter.

Section 20. Penalties and Enforcement.

Violations of Sections 43-A and 43-B and rules issued thereunder shall be subject to the ERC
enforcement powers and penalties under Section 46 of Republic Act No. 9136, without prejudice to
other civil, criminal and administrative liabilities. The ERC shall ensure notice, opportunity to be
heard, proportionate sanctions and judicial review as provided by law. No penalty imposed upon a
utility may be passed on to consumers.

Section 21. Transitory Provisions.

Existing lawful contracts, vested rights, and final regulatory orders shall remain effective,
subject to prospective regulation consistent with the Constitution and the express prohibitions
established under this Act.

Within one hundred eighty (180) days from effectivity, the Energy Regulatory Commission
shall establish and publish technical system-loss benchmarks, revised billing and accounting rules,
independent audit requirements, and performance standards necessary to implement Sections 43(f),
43-A, and 43-B of Republic Act No. 9136, as amended, and Section 10 of Republic Act No. 7832, as
amended.

Beginning upon the effectivity of this Act, no new tariff approval, rate adjustment, or
regulatory authorization shall permit the recovery of nontechnical system losses or technical losses
exceeding approved benchmarks.

Within six (6) months from effectivity, all regulated electricity service providers shall
discontinue the recovery of prohibited system losses, including recovery through indirect charges,
accounting reclassifications, or other tariff components. The ERC shall prescribe the necessary
transition arrangements without authorizing the continued recovery of costs expressly prohibited
under this Act beyond the prescribed period.

===== [page 11] =====
Any unlawful collection made after the applicable prohibition takes effect shall be refunded or
credited to affected consumers, together with applicable legal interest.

The implementation of the foregoing provisions shall not impair accrued rights or obligations
except to the extent expressly authorized by this Act and consistent with constitutional requirements.

The zero-rating of electricity under Section 14 shall commence on the first day of the taxable
quarter following the issuance of the implementing rules by the Bureau of Internal Revenue, which
shall be issued within ninety (90) days from effectivity.

Until replacement regulations take effect, existing rules shall remain applicable insofar as they
are not inconsistent with this Act.”

Section 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations.

Within one hundred eighty (180) days, the DOE, ERC, DOF, BIR, DILG and other concerned
agencies shall jointly or separately promulgate implementing rules within their respective statutory
mandates, after public consultation. Rules shall not create new consumer charges absent express
statutory authorization.

Section 23. Appropriations.

Amounts necessary for implementation shall be charged against available appropriations of
the implementing agencies, subject to existing budgeting laws. Thereafter, necessary sums shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act. No agency may obligate funds beyond lawful
appropriations.

Section 24. Separability Clause.

If any provision is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder not affected thereby
shall continue in force.

Section 23. Repealing and Amending Clause.

Section 2, Section 4, Section 43(f), Section 47, and Section 73 of Republic Act No. 9136,
otherwise known as the “Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001,” as amended; Section 4-B of
Presidential Decree No. 269, as amended by Republic Act No. 10531; and Section 108 of the
National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, are hereby amended as provided in this Act.

New Sections 22-A, 37-A, 37-B, 37-C, 43-A, 43-B, 43-C, and 47-A are likewise inserted into
Republic Act No. 9136 pursuant to this Act.

All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

Except as expressly amended herein, the remaining provisions of Republic Act No. 9136,
Republic Act No. 11552, Republic Act No. 11646, Presidential Decree No. 269, as amended, the
National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, and all other applicable laws shall remain in
full force and effect.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to impair vested rights, existing franchises, licenses,
permits, contracts, obligations, liabilities, pending proceedings, or lawful acts performed under prior
laws, nor to invalidate any order, decision, resolution, approval, authorization, or regulatory action
previously issued by the ERC, NEA, DOE, CDA, or any other competent authority. Any right
accrued, liability incurred, penalty attached, or proceeding commenced before the effectivity of this
Act shall remain valid and enforceable and shall continue to be governed by the law applicable at the
time of its accrual or commencement unless otherwise expressly provided in this Act.
