EXPLANATORY NOTE

Coffee cultivation represents both an integral part of Philippine cultural heritage and a vital
source of livelihood for tens of thousands of farming households and rural upland communities
across the country. From the heritage Liberica traditions of Batangas and Cavite, popularly
celebrated as Barako, to the diverse commercial plantings of Robusta, Arabica, and Excelsa in
Mindanao, Northern Luzon, and the Visayas, the domestic coffee sector holds immense economic
promise.

Growing local consumption, the rise of homegrown specialty coffee shops, and
government initiatives under the Philippine Coffee Industry Roadmap reflect the continued growth
and potential of the Philippine coffee industry. Yet while opportunities and profits expand across
coffee processing, branding, and retailing, many smallholder coffee farmers continue to struggle
to earn a fair and sustainable income from their harvests. The growing success of Philippine coffee
must also be felt by the farmers whose hard work makes it possible.

Despite the Philippines’ favorable climate and soil conditions for coffee cultivation,
domestic production has struggled to keep pace with growing local demand in recent years.
Although the Philippines produced 35,650 metric tonnes (MT) of coffee in 2025,' 11.5% higher
than 2024 production, the Philippine Coffee Board, Incorporated estimates that the country’s
annual demand sits at a minimum of 150,000 MT. This makes the Philippines highly dependent
on imports, while worsening climate conditions further constrain production.

While government initiatives have sought to improve coffee production, provide agricultural
assistance, and expand market opportunities, greater attention must also be given to protecting
farmers during the actual sale of their harvest. For small coffee farmers, selling their produce is
not always a straightforward transaction. Some may have limited means to verify the weight or
quality assessment of their coffee, understand deductions from the agreed price, or determine
whether promised premiums have been properly paid. Without clear buying terms or written
records, farmers may be unable to question discrepancies or establish the amount they should have
received.

These concerns are especially important for farmers who invest additional time and
resources to improve the quality of their coffee in the hope of earning better prices. When such
efforts do not translate into the compensation originally promised, it is not only their income that
suffers, but also their ability to provide for their families and sustain their livelihoods.

This bill seeks to protect Filipino coffee farmers by establishing basic standards of
transparency and fairness in transactions with commercial buyers. It requires the disclosure of
buying terms, allows farmers to witness the weighing of their produce, and ensures that they
receive purchase records showing how their earnings were computed. It likewise prohibits

AN ACT
PROTECTING FILIPINO COFFEE FARMERS AGAINST UNFAIR BUYING
PRACTICES, PROMOTING TRANSPARENCY AND FAIR DEALING IN COFFEE
PURCHASE TRANSACTIONS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in (Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Coffee larmers’
Fair Trade and Income Protection Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. —\t is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
the rights and welfare of agricultural producers, promote equitable opportunities in agricultural
trade, and enable coffee farmers to obtain fair and transparent compensation for their produce. The
State recognizes the economic and cultural importance of Philippine coffee and shall protect
farmers against deceptive, coercive, and unfair buying practices, without imposing government-
fixed purchase prices or impairing legitimate commercial negotiations.

SECTION 3. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to commercial purchases within the
Philippines of coffee cherries, parchment coffee, dried coffee, green coffee beans, and other
primary forms of coffee produce directly from coffee farmers or farmer organizations. It shall not
apply to ordinary retail purchases for personal consumption or to subsequent sales between
processors, wholesalers, roasters, and retailers where no farmer or farmer organization is the seller.
The implementing rules may provide simplified compliance for small-volume and occasional
buyers, without exempting fraudulent practices.

SECTION 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
be defined as follows:

(a) “coffee farmer” means a natural person cultivating or producing coffee for sale, whether
individually or as a member of an organization;

(b) “farmer organization” means a cooperative, association, or other duly organized group
authorized to market coffee on behalf of its members;

(c) “commercial buyer” means any person or entity purchasing primary coffee produce
directly from coffee farmers or farmer organizations for resale, processing, manufacturing,
or other commercial purposes;

(d) “quality-based premium” means an additional amount above the agreed base purchase
price expressly offered or agreed for specified quality, grade, origin, traceability, or other
objectively described attributes;

(e) “deduction” means any reduction from the gross amount otherwise payable to the seller;
and

(f) “purchase record” means a written or electronic document setting out the material
particulars of a covered purchase.

SECTION 5. Disclosure of Buying Terms. — Before a covered purchase is concluded, the
commercial buyer shall disclose to the coffee farmer or farmer organization, in a language or form
reasonably understood by the seller:

(a) the offered unit price and unit of measurement,

(b) the applicable weighing and quality assessment criteria, if these affect price;
(c) any proposed deductions and their basis;

(d) any quality-based premium offered, including the conditions for earning it, and
(e) the agreed payment terms.

Where purchases are concluded immediately upon delivery, disclosure may be made
through a clearly displayed buying schedule and an explanation upon request. No minimum
purchase price or disclosure of confidential downstream resale prices shall be required.

SECTION 6. Transparency in Weighing and Quality Assessment. — The commercial
buyer shall allow the farmer or an authorized representative a reasonable opportunity to observe
the weighing of coffee delivered for sale and shall disclose the recorded weight. Where a grade or
quality assessment affects the offered price or an agreed premium, the buyer shall communicate
the grade or assessment result and the criteria applied, upon request of the seller.

Where the buyer represents that a Philippine National Standard was applied, the applicable
standard shall be identified. This Act shall not establish a separate weighing, calibration, grading,
or certification system, and shall be implemented without prejudice to existing laws on weights
and measures.

SECTION 7. Purchase Records, Payment, and Protection of Agreed Premiums. — Upon
completion of a covered purchase, the commercial buyer shall furnish the coffee farmer or farmer
organization a written or electronic purchase record indicating the date of transaction, quantity and
recorded weight of coffee purchased, applicable grade or quality assessment, agreed unit price,
gross amount, itemized deductions, any agreed quality-based premium, net amount payable, and
payment status.

No deduction shall be imposed, nor shall any agreed quality-based premium be withheld
or reduced, except on grounds disclosed to and accepted by the seller before the purchase is
concluded, or as otherwise required by law. Any premium earned under the agreed conditions shall
form part of the purchase price payable to the seller.

Where payment or final pricing is subject to post-delivery quality assessment, the basis for
determining the final purchase price and the payment deadline shall be agreed upon and disclosed
before the transaction is concluded. The buyer shall furnish the seller a record of the final
computation. In the absence of an agreement for deferred payment, the purchase price shall be
payable upon acceptance of the produce, without prejudice to applicable provisions of the Civil
Code.

For purchases made through farmer organizations, a consolidated purchase record may be
furnished to the organization, which shall make the relevant information accessible to its member-
sellers. The Department of Agriculture (DA) shall provide a simple model purchase record,
including a handwritten format.

SECTION 8. Protection Against Unfair Buying Practices. —In purchases covered by this
Act, commercial buyers shall be prohibited from:

(a) Knowingly falsifying purchase records, weights, grades, or other material information
affecting the purchase price;

(b) Imposing deductions that were not disclosed and agreed upon before the purchase;

(c) Fraudulently withholding or reducing quality-based premiums earned under the agreed
purchase terms; and

(d) Threatening or retaliating against coffee farmers or farmer organizations for raising good-

A coffee farmer or farmer organization adversely affected by any prohibited practice under
this section may seek appropriate relief under the Civil Code and other applicable laws, including
the recovery of amounts wrongfully withheld or deducted, where legally warranted. Complaints
may be referred to the appropriate government authorities in accordance with their respective
mandates and existing laws.

Nothing in this Act shall prejudice the right of coffee farmers and farmer organizations to
pursue remedies available under the Civil Code and other applicable laws.

SECTION 9. Farmer Information and Accessible Compliance. — The DA, in
coordination with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), local government units, and
existing coffee industry stakeholders, shall integrate information on rights under this Act. sample
purchase records, and complaint referral channels into existing extension, market-linkage, and
farmer-information activities. No separate training institution, registration system, or coffee
market information platform shall be created under this Act.

SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the Department
of Trade and Industry (DTI), concerned local government units, and representatives of coffee
farmers and commercial buyers, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its
implementation.

The rules shall provide simplified documentation requirements for small-volume
transactions and procedures for referring complaints to appropriate government authorities,
consistent with their existing mandates.

SECTION 11. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the existing appropriations of the concerned agencies. Thereafter,
such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 12. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
and effect.

SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,

amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 14. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
