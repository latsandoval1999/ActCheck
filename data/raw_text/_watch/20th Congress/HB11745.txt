                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Section 13, Article II, of the 1987 Constitution recognizes the vital role of the
youth in nation-building and directs the State to protect their physical, moral, spiritual,
intellectual, and social well-being.

        The Constitution further commands the State to defend: “[t]he right of children
to assistance, including proper care and nutrition, and special protection from all forms
of neglect, abuse, cruelty, exploitation and other conditions prejudicial to their
development” (Section 3(2), Article XV).

        These constitutional commitments include the protection of the mental and
emotional well-being of children, adolescents, and young adults. Congress has already
taken important steps in this direction. Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the
“Mental Health Act,” recognizes the need to integrate mental health into educational
institutions. Section 24 thereof provides: “Educational institutions, such as schools,
colleges, universities, and technical schools, shall develop policies and programs for
students, educators, and other employees designed to: raise awareness on mental health
issues, identify and provide support and services for individuals at risk, and facilitate
access, including referral mechanisms of individuals with mental health conditions to
treatment and psychosocial support.”

       More recently, Republic Act No. 12080, or the “Basic Education Mental Health
and Well-Being Promotion Act,” strengthened the delivery of mental health services in
basic education. The law requires the development of a School-Based Mental Health
Program for learners in public and private basic education schools. It provides that the
Program shall: “promote mental health awareness and manage the mental health
concerns of all learners, including the prevention of suicide in schools” (Section 4).

       The Filipino youth are growing up in an environment that is very different from
that experienced by previous generations. School life now extends beyond the
classroom and into digital spaces where students study, communicate, socialize, seek
information, and form relationships. The same technologies that have made learning
and communication more accessible have also introduced new pressures on young
people, including excessive screen use, cyberbullying, harmful online content, social
comparison, and digital habits that may interfere with sleep, concentration,
relationships, and emotional well-being.

       While existing legislation has established an important institutional framework
for mental health care, the rapid growth of digital technology calls for a corresponding
public policy response. The mental health of students cannot be considered separately
from the environments in which they now spend a substantial part of their daily lives.

       This measure therefore seeks to strengthen early intervention, improve access to
appropriate mental health professionals, promote mental health literacy, and establish
mechanisms through which schools can respond to students who may be experiencing
emotional or psychological difficulties before these concerns develop into more serious
conditions.

        At the same time, the bill introduces a digital well-being component that is not
adequately addressed by existing mental health legislation. It directs government
agencies and educational institutions to promote responsible digital use among young
people and provides for an annual Digital Detox Week intended to encourage students
to reconnect with activities outside the digital environment. The objective is not to reject
technology or prevent young people from benefiting from digital platforms. Rather, it
is to encourage healthier and more deliberate patterns of technology use.

       Ultimately, this measure is founded on a straightforward principle: students
cannot be expected to learn, participate, and reach their potential when their mental and
emotional well-being is neglected. Mental health support should be available before a
young person reaches a point of crisis, and digital technology should remain a tool for
education, communication, and opportunity rather than becoming a source of
preventable harm.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                               AN ACT
  INSTITUTIONALIZING MENTAL HEALTH SERVICES AND PROMOTING DIGITAL
   WELL-BEING AMONG THE YOUTH, PROVIDING FOR SCHOOL-BASED MENTAL
    HEALTH PROGRAMS, DIGITAL WELL-BEING MEASURES, AND FOR OTHER
                             PURPOSES

Section 1. This Act shall be known as the “Mental Health and Digital Wellbeing for
Youth Act of 2026.”

Section 2. It is the policy of the State to protect the mental, emotional, and
psychological health of the Filipino youth. Recognizing the rising incidence of
depression, anxiety, self-harm, and digital addiction among students, the State shall
institutionalize responsive, school-based mental health services and regulate the
harmful effects of digital platforms on the well-being of minors.

Section 3. This Act aims to:
   a. Ensure access to early, preventive, and non-stigmatizing mental health support
       in all public schools and State Universities and Colleges (SUCs);
   b. Deploy trained mental health professionals to educational institutions
       nationwide;
   c. Regulate youth-targeted digital platforms to prevent algorithmic harm and
       addictive design;
   d. Promote digital literacy and emotional resilience among students;
   e. Create a nationwide platform for youth mental health support through
       technology.

Section 4. The Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) shall implement comprehensive mental health services in all public
secondary schools and SUCs, including:
    a. Deployment of at least one licensed mental health professional (psychologist,
       guidance counselor, or psychiatric nurse) per public high school and university
       campus within three (3) years from the effectivity of this Act;
    b. Annual mental health screenings and wellness mapping for students; and
       creation of safe spaces for counseling, peer support, and emotional processing;
    c. Confidential and non-punitive referral system for at-risk students;
    d. Training programs for teachers on mental health first aid, trauma-informed
       approaches, and empathy-based discipline.

Section 5. All schools shall observe an annual “Digital Detox Week,” a week-long
series of activities promoting offline connections, creative expression, nature
engagement, and digital self-regulation.

DepEd and CHED shall integrate Digital Detox Week into the official school calendar
starting School Year 2026-2027.

Section 6. DepEd, CHED, and the Department of Health (DOH) shall publish an annual
report on Youth Mental Health and Digital Behavior, covering:
    a. Trends in depression, anxiety, digital usage, and suicide risk among students;
    b. Regional disparities and emerging mental health issues;
    c. Policy recommendations and accountability of digital platforms.

Section 7. The necessary funds for the implementation of this Act shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA). The DepEd, CHED, DOH, and the
Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) shall coordinate
to ensure adequate and timely release of funds. Local government units (LGUs) may
also allocate funds to support this Act within their jurisdiction.

Section 8. Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, DepEd,
CHED, and DICT shall convene a Joint Task Force to draft the Implementing Rules and
Regulations (IRR).

Section 9. If any provision or part of this Act is declared unconstitutional, the remaining
provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

Section 10. All laws, executive orders, issuances, and rules and regulations inconsistent
with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

Section 11. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
