                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

Every Filipino deserves to see their taxes build sturdy bridges and safe communities, not vanish
into the pockets of crooked officials. The Philippine False Claims Act delivers on this by shifting
the focus from slow criminal trials to the swift recovery of stolen public funds. While current
laws like Republic Act No. 3019 require the standard of "proof beyond a reasonable doubt," this
Act introduces a civil framework designed to recover taxpayer money quickly.

By lowering the evidentiary threshold, the State can aggressively reclaim funds lost to ghost
projects, substandard infrastructure, and fraudulent schemes that hinder national development.

The Act holds any entity liable for submitting fraudulent claims, and ensures a powerful
deterrent through a "treble damages" provision, which holds perpetrators accountable by
requiring them to pay heavy civil penalties plus three times the government's total loss.Further, it
introduces a "qui tam" mechanism, which turns ordinary citizens into partners in justice by
rewarding whistleblowers with a portion of the recovered assets— effectively creating an
environment where corruption has nowhere to hide. In essence, this Act enforces public
accountability by providing a swift tool to recover losses, deter fraud, and ensure that public
funds directly serve the public good.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

    AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE RECOVERY OF PUBLIC FUNDS OBTAINED
   THROUGH FRAUD, IMPOSING CIVIL LIABILITY FOR FALSE CLAIMS, AND
      STRENGTHENING ACCOUNTABILITY IN GOVERNMENT PROJECTS

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Philippine False Claims Act."
SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect the integrity of
public funds and ensure that every peso allocated for government programs and projects,
including flood control and critical infrastructure, is utilized honestly, efficiently, and in
accordance with law.
The State recognizes that fraud in public contracting, manifested through ghost projects,
substandard implementation, false certifications, and related schemes, results in substantial loss
to the national treasury and undermines public safety and trust.
The State shall likewise recognize and encourage responsible civilian participation, subject to
government oversight, in the detection and reporting of fraud involving public funds.
SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
defined as follows:
       a) “Aggregation” refers to the combining of multiple individual claims, invoices, or
       fraudulent acts into a single civil action for the purpose of meeting jurisdictional or
       procedural requirements.
       b) “Civil Investigative Demand (CID)” refers to a pre-litigation administrative subpoena
       issued by the Secretary of Justice to compel the production of documents, written
       answers, or oral testimony relevant to a false claims investigation and issued prior to the
       unsealing of a complaint.
       c) “Claim” refers to any request or demand, whether under contract or otherwise, for
       money or property made to the Government, or to a contractor, grantee, or other recipient
       if the Government provides or reimburses any portion of the money or property
       requested.
d) “False or Fraudulent Claim” refers to any request or demand for money or property
submitted to the Government that is false, fictitious, or misleading, whether in whole or
in part, including claims arising from or supported by:

       (1) falsified, inaccurate, or incomplete statements, records, or certifications;

       (2) non-performance, partial performance, or substandard delivery of goods,
       works, or services;

       (3) concealment of material facts affecting eligibility for payment;

       (4) collusion, conspiracy, or any arrangement designed to improperly influence
       project selection, procurement, contract award, or payment;

       (5) conflicts of interest, kickbacks, or undisclosed financial relationships that
       materially affect the validity of the claim; or

       (6) any scheme, device, or course of conduct that results in the Government
       paying money that it would not have paid had it known the true facts.

e)“Document or Record” refers to any written, electronic, or graphic matter used to
support a claim for payment, including but not limited to vouchers, invoices, contracts,
"as-built" plans, certifications of completion, and similar records.
f) “Ghost Project” refers to any government contract, program, or procurement for which
payment is requested or received, but where the actual work, goods, or services were not
delivered, performed, or completed, notwithstanding records or certifications to the
contrary.
g) “Government” refers to the National Government, including all departments, bureaus,
local government units (LGUs), government-owned or controlled corporations (GOCCs),
and any entity or program where public funds are used or administered.
h) “Incapable of Pecuniary Estimation” refers to actions where the primary issue is the
vindication of public interest and the enforcement of transparency policies, rather than a
mere collection of a sum of money, thus conferring jurisdiction upon the Regional Trial
Court.
i) “Independent Knowledge” refers to information l that is not derived from public
disclosures and that materially adds to the Government’s ability to investigate or
prosecute the claim.
j) “Knowing and Knowingly” refers to a state of mind where a person has actual
knowledge of the information, acts in deliberate ignorance of the truth or falsity of the
information, or acts in reckless disregard of the truth or falsity of the informations, and no
specific intent to defraud is required.
k) “Material” refers to information having a natural tendency to influence, or being
capable of influencing, the payment or receipt of money or property.
      l) “Obligation” refers to an established duty, whether fixed or contingent, to pay or
      transmit money or property to the Government.
      m) “Original Source” refers to an individual who voluntarily disclosed the information to
      the Government prior to public disclosure, or who possesses knowledge that is
      independent of and materially adds to publicly disclosed information and has voluntarily
      provided such information to the Government before filing an action under this Act.
      n) “Person” refers to any natural or juridical person, including but not limited to
      individuals, corporations, partnerships, associations, and other legal entities.
      o) “Principal Planner or Initiator” refers to an individual who organized, managed, or
      directed the fraudulent scheme or false claim, as distinguished from a participant who
      played a minor or incidental role.
      p) “Private Individual” refers to any natural or juridical person, including but not limited
      to individuals, corporations, partnerships, associations, and other legal entities who
      initiates a civil action under the Qui Tam provision of this Act.
      q) “Proceeds” refers to the total amount recovered under this Act, including civil
      penalties and treble damages.
      r) “Public Disclosure” refers to the dissemination of information regarding fraud or false
      claims through government audits, reports, or investigations; proceedings in legislative or
      administrative bodies; or reports by the news media accessible to the general public.
      s) “Qui Tam Action” refers to a lawsuit brought by a private individual, acting on behalf
      of the Government, to recover damages and penalties.
      t) “Relator” refers to a private individual or entity who initiates a civil action on behalf of
      the Government under this Act, based on knowledge of a false or fraudulent claim, and
      who may be entitled to receive a share of any proceeds recovered from such action,
      subject to the provisions of this Act.
      u) “Reverse False Claims” refers to knowingly concealing or improperly avoiding or
      decreasing an obligation to pay or transmit money or property to the Government,
      including the improper retention of overpayments.
      v) “Substandard Property or Services” refers to any goods, materials, or services that fail
      to meet applicable quality, technical, engineering, or contractual standards, including
      latent defects that were knowingly concealed during inspection or certification, regardless
      of whether the project was accepted by the Government.
      w) “Substantially the Same Allegations or Transactions” refers to allegations or
      transactions that are materially similar in their essential elements of fraud to those already
      publicly disclosed, such that the same fraudulent scheme would have been apparent to the
      Government upon a reasonable investigation of such prior disclosure.
      x) “Treble Damages” refers to a statutory recovery equal to three (3) times the actual
      financial loss sustained by the Government.
SECTION 4. Liability for False Claims. – Any person who:
       (1) knowingly presents, or causes to be presented, a false or fraudulent claim for payment
       or approval;

       (2) knowingly makes, uses, or causes to be made or used, a false record or statement
       material to a false or fraudulent claim;

       (3) knowingly delivers or causes to be delivered less property, goods, or services than the
       amount or quality for which payment is requested;

       (4) knowingly conceals or improperly avoids or decreases an obligation to pay or
       transmit money or property to the Government;

       (5) knowingly engages in any collusion, conspiracy, or arrangement to defraud the
       Government through the submission, approval, or payment of a false or fraudulent claim;
       or

       (6) knowingly participates in a scheme, transaction, or course of conduct that results in
       the Government paying money that it would not have paid, or paying more than it should
       have paid, had it known the true facts,

       shall be liable to the Government for treble damages, plus the costs of the civil action.

       The knowledge, acquiescence, or approval of a government official regarding the falsity
       of a claim or the substandard nature of a project shall not, by itself, constitute a defense to
       liability under this Act. The State’s right to recover shall not be prejudiced by the
       connivance, participation, or negligence of its agents.

SECTION 5. Civil Penalties. – In addition to treble damages, any person found liable shall pay
a civil penalty of not less than ₱ 300,000.00 and not more than ₱1,000,000.00 for each false or
fraudulent claim.
SECTION 6. Reduced Liability for Cooperation. – Notwithstanding the provisions on treble
damages, the court shall reduce the liability of a person to not less than two (2) times the amount
of damages sustained by the Government, and may waive the imposition of civil penalties, if
such person:

       a) within thirty (30) days after the date on which the person first obtained credible
       information of a violation under this Act, the person voluntarily disclosed to the
       Department of Justice all information known to such person relating to such violation;

       b) the person fully cooperated with any investigation conducted by the Government,
       including the timely production of documents, provision of testimony, and identification
       of all individuals involved in the violation; and

       c) at the time of such disclosure, the person had no actual knowledge that the
       Government had commenced, or was conducting, any criminal, civil, or administrative
       investigation relating to such violation, and had not received any notice of a complaint,
       Civil Investigative Demand, subpoena, or similar Government action concerning the
       same.

Failure to satisfy any of the foregoing conditions shall render the person ineligible for reduced
liability under this Section.

The burden of proving entitlement to reduced liability shall rest upon the person invoking this
Section.

SECTION 7. Qui Tam Actions and Incentives. – A private individual may bring an action in the
name of the Government. If the Government intervenes, the individual receives 15% to 25% of
the proceeds. If the individual proceeds alone, they receive 25% to 30%.
SECTION 8. Government Intervention. - Upon receipt of the complaint and disclosure materials
under Section 13 of this Act, the Department of Justice shall evaluate the allegations and
determine whether to intervene in the action. If the Government elects to intervene, it shall have
primary responsibility for prosecuting the action and shall not be bound by the acts of the Private
Individual bringing the action.If the Government declines to intervene, the Private Individual
shall have the right to conduct the action in the name of the Government, subject to the rights of
the Government under this Act. The Government may, notwithstanding the objection of the
Private Individual, move to dismiss or settle the action, subject to notice and hearing before the
court, upon a showing that such dismissal or settlement is fair, adequate, and reasonable under
the circumstances.
SECTION 9. Disposition of Recovered Proceeds. – All proceeds recovered under this Act,
excluding the share awarded to a Private Individual under Section 7, shall accrue to the General
Fund of the National Treasury. The court shall order the payment of the remaining proceeds to
the Government, and such amounts shall be paid to the Bureau of the Treasury in accordance
with the rules, and regulations.
SECTION 10. Anti-Retaliation. – Any employee, agent, or contractor, whether in the private or
public sector, shall be protected from discrimination or discharge for acts done in furtherance of
this Act. Relief includes reinstatement, double back pay, and compensation for special damages.
Public sector whistleblowers shall be granted immunity from administrative disciplinary
proceedings related to the reported fraud, provided they were not the principal architect of the
scheme.
SECTION 11. Administrative Liability for Non-Reporting. – Any government official with
oversight of a project who becomes aware of a false claim and fails to report it to the Department
of Justice within thirty (30) days shall be liable for Gross Neglect of Duty. This is without
prejudice to criminal or civil liability if the official connived in the fraud.
SECTION 12. Debarment and Blacklisting. – Any person or entity found liable under Section 4
shall be barred from participating in all government procurement. A first offense shall merit a
suspension of five (5) to ten (10) years, while a subsequent offense shall result in a permanent
ban.
This disqualification extends to any related entity used to circumvent this provision, including
but not limited to entities owned or controlled by the same beneficial owners, immediate family
members of the incorporators, and entities sharing the same physical address or primary
equipment as the liable party. The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) shall maintain
a centralized registry of such barred individuals and entities.
SECTION 13. Filing Under Seal. – A complaint filed by a Private Individual shall be filed in
camera and shall remain under seal for a period of at least sixty (60) days. The complaint shall
not be served upon the defendant until the court so orders.

At the time of filing, the Private Individual shall serve upon the Department of Justice a copy of
the complaint together with a written disclosure of substantially all material evidence and
information in the possession of the Private Individual.

Within the period that the complaint remains under seal, the Department of Justice may move the
court for an extension of such a period upon a showing of good cause. Any extension granted
shall be for a definite period.

The total period during which the complaint remains under seal shall not exceed eighteen (18)
months; provided, that the court may grant further extension upon a showing of extraordinary
circumstances in a summary hearing.

The filing of the complaint under seal shall suspend the running of the prescriptive periods as
provided in Section 19.

SECTION 14. First-to-File Bar. – When a person brings a Qui Tam action under this Act, no
person other than the Government may intervene or bring a related action based on the same
underlying facts or transactions. Only the first person to file a complaint and notify the
Government shall be eligible to receive the incentives provided under Section 7 of this Act.
SECTION 15. Asset Preservation and Provisional Remedies. – The Government may apply for
a Writ of Preliminary Attachment or other provisional remedies against a defendant’s assets,
including those held by nominees, to secure the potential recovery of damages and penalties. The
court shall evaluate such applications in accordance with the Rules of Court and may grant the
remedy upon a prima facie showing of fraud or risk of asset dissipation.
SECTION 16. Jurisdiction and Venue. – The Regional Trial Court (RTC) shall have exclusive
original jurisdiction over all actions under this Act, regardless of the amount involved, as these
cases are deemed incapable of pecuniary estimation; provided, that the Sandiganbayan shall have
concurrent jurisdiction if the case involves public officials falling under its jurisdiction as
provided by law.
For purposes of meeting any jurisdictional or procedural threshold, the court shall allow the
aggregation of multiple false claims, invoices, or transactions if they arise from the same
government project, contract, or a related series of transactions by the same defendant.
Actions under this Act may be brought in the Regional Trial Court of the judicial region where:
(i) any defendant resides, is found, or transacts business; (ii) any act prohibited under Section 4
occurred; or (iii) the Government office or agency affected by the fraud is situated.
SECTION 17. Public Disclosure Bar. – The court shall dismiss an action or claim under this
Act if substantially the same allegations or transactions were publicly disclosed in government
audits, reports, legislative hearings, or the news media, unless the person bringing the action is
an Original Source.
SECTION 18. Tax Treatment of Awards. – Any award or share of proceeds received by a Private
Individual under Section 7 of this Act shall be subject to a final tax of ten percent (10%).
SECTION 19. Standard of Proof. – In any action brought under this Act, liability shall be
established by a preponderance of evidence. The standard of proof required in criminal
proceedings shall not apply.
SECTION 20. Statute of Limitations. – A civil action under this Act may not be brought more
than ten (10) years after the date on which the violation is committed, or more than six (6) years
after the date when facts material to the right of action are known or reasonably should have
been known by the official of the Government charged with responsibility to act, whichever
occurs last; provided, that in no event shall an action be brought more than twenty (20) years
after the date of the violation.
The running of these prescriptive periods shall be strictly tolled and suspended during the entire
period that the complaint remains under seal pursuant to Section 12.
SECTION 21. Pre-litigation Discovery. – The Secretary of Justice may issue a written Civil
Investigative Demand (CID) for documents, testimony, or written answers relevant to a false
claims investigation before a complaint is unsealed. No claim of trade secret, proprietary
information, or confidentiality agreement shall prevent the production of materials to the
Department of Justice; provided, that the recipient may petition the Regional Trial Court for a
protective order to limit the use or disclosure of such information to the public.
The recipient may, within fifteen (15) days of service, petition the Regional Trial Court to quash
or modify the demand if it is unreasonable, irrelevant, or issued in bad faith. All obtained
information shall remain confidential and exempt from disclosure during the investigation,
preserving the recipient’s right to counsel and protection against self-incrimination.
Non-compliance with a valid CID, not otherwise stayed by the court, shall be grounds for the
Department of Justice to seek a court order of enforcement or a citation of contempt.
SECTION 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the Department of Justice (DOJ), in coordination with the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM) and the Commission on Audit (COA), shall promulgate the necessary rules
and regulations.
SECTION 23. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid, the
remaining parts shall remain in full force and effect.
SECTION 24. Repealing Clause. – All laws or regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.
SECTION 25. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
