                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


Access to a fair, independent, and effective judiciary is a cornerstone of any
democratic society. Judges serve as the guardians of the rule of law, tasked with
resolving disputes, upholding rights, and ensuring that justice is administered without
fear or favor. However, the very nature of their duties exposes them to significant risks
to their safety and well-being, and these remain largely unaddressed in the current
system of compensation and protection.

By reason of their profession, members of the judiciary are often placed in situations
entailing dangerous, high-stakes cases, including those involving heinous crimes or
organized criminal activity. In the course of dispensing justice, judges inevitably
encounter disgruntled parties who may resort to threats, harassment, or even violence.
Throughout Philippine history, members of the judiciary have been subjected to
assaults and even assassination attempts, underscoring the real and present dangers
attached to their role.

Despite these realities, judges are not accorded hazard pay comparable to other
government personnel whose duties similarly expose them to occupational risks or
perils to life. Existing policies recognize the necessity of providing additional
compensation to public servants operating under hazardous conditions. Yet, the
judiciary remains excluded from such benefits, notwithstanding the high-risk
classification often attributed to their profession.

Therefore, this bill seeks to remove this disparity by granting hazard pay to members
of the judiciary. Such compensation acknowledges the inherent dangers of judicial
service and affirms the State’s commitment to safeguarding those who dedicate their
lives to the administration of justice.

Moreover, it aims to strengthen judicial independence by boosting morale and
ensuring that judges can perform their duties without undue concern for their personal
safety and security.

Ultimately, the measure is grounded on the principle that those who uphold the law
must themselves be protected by it. By institutionalizing hazard pay for judges, the
State reinforces its duty to promote the general welfare, protect its public servants,
and preserve the integrity of the justice system.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT MANDATING THE PAYMENT OF HAZARD PAY TO ALL JUDGES OF
  COURTS IN THE PHILIPPINES, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in the
Congress assembled:


SEC. 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Hazard Pay for Judges Act”.
SEC. 2. Hazard Pay for Regional Trial Court (RTC) Judges. – All duly appointed judges
in Regional Trial Courts (RTC) shall be granted a hazard pay during their incumbency
in the amount equivalent to thirty percent (30%) of their basic monthly salary
SEC. 3. Hazard Pay for MeTC, MCTC, and Shari’a Court Judges. – All duly appointed
judges in the Metropolitan Trial Courts (MeTC), Municipal Trial Courts in Cities
(MCTC), Municipal Circuit Trial Courts (MCTC), and Shari’a Courts shall be granted a
hazard pay during their incumbency in the amount equivalent to twenty five percent
(25%) of their basic monthly salary.
SEC. 4. Hazard Pay for Municipal Trial Court (MTC) Judges. – All duly appointed
judges in Municipal Trial Courts (MTC) shall be granted a hazard pay during their
incumbency in the amount equivalent to twenty percent (20%) of their basic monthly
salary.
SEC. 5. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the available appropriations of the Judiciary under the
current General Appropriations Act. Thereafter, such sums as maybe necessary for
its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.
SEC. 6. Tax Exemption and Non-Diminution of Benefits. – The hazard pay for the
judges herein shall not be subject to tax and nothing in this Act shall be construed as
to diminish in any manner, any benefit granted by existing laws, rules and regulations,
local ordinances, and other issuances especially favorable to duly appointed judges
of Philippine courts.
SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of Budget and
Management in consultation with the Judiciary shall, within sixty (60) days after the
effectivity of this Act, issue the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.
SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – Any provisions, laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed and
modified accordingly.
SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) national newspapers of general circulation.
