                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Rabies is a deadly but preventable disease transmitted through the bites, scratches,
or saliva of infected animals, commonly dogs and cats.1 In 2024, the Department of
Health (DOH) recorded 426 rabies-related deaths nationwide.2 Meanwhile, in the first
eight (8) months of 2025, 211 cases of rabies have been reported in the country.3
These figures reveal that rabies remains a serious public health concern in the
Philippines, despite the availability of preventive and treatment measures.

In Parañaque City alone, the cases of animal bites have been increasing over the
years, from over 8,000 cases in 2023 to over 12,000 in 2025, with one recorded rabies-
related death in 2024.4 Although more animal bite cases are receiving post-exposure
prophylaxis, access to and completion of treatment remain inconsistent. For instance,
of the 737 animal bite cases recorded in the first 3 quarters of 2022, only 576

According to the World Health Organization, rabies is preventable through three
interventions: (1) awareness; (2) post-exposure prophylaxis; and (3) mass dog
vaccination.5 The government has made efforts to control and prevent rabies, primarily
through the enactment of Republic Act No. 9482 or the Anti-Rabies Act of 2007. This
law requires local government units to enact ordinances to support the National
Rabies Prevention and Control Program and establish veterinary offices in every
province, city and first-class municipality. In addition, some hospitals, health centers,
or local government units have also established Animal Bite Centers, to administer
immediate post-exposure treatment.

However, access to free vaccinations and related services remain uneven across
localities. Not all communities have readily accessible or free post-exposure
treatments and many individuals remain unaware of existing rabies prevention and
control measures. Further, the high number of recorded cases of rabies and rabies-
related deaths reveal the gaps in the awareness, prevention, and treatment of this
preventable disease.

This bill aims to strengthen the implementation of R.A. No. 9482 by requiring each
provincial, city, and municipal government to establish a Rabies Prevention and
Control Committee, which shall implement policies on rabies awareness, prevention,
and treatment. Said Committee shall also ensure the establishment of local animal
bite centers and the implementation of free vaccination programs. With this proposed
measure, we aim to ensure that each local government is able to adopt an integrated
approach to controlling animal rabies and mitigating rabies-related deaths.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. A new Section 7-A shall be inserted after Section 7 of Republic Act No.
9482, otherwise known as the “Anti-Rabies Act of 2007”, which shall read as follows:

   SECTION 7-A. LOCAL RABIES PREVENTION AND CONTROL
   COMMITТЕЕ. — ЕACH PROVINCIAL, CITY, AND MUNICIPAL
   GOVERNMENT SHALL ESTABLISH A RABIES PREVENTION AND
   CONTROL COMMITTEE, TO BE COMPOSED OF THE FOLLOWING:

      (A) LOCAL CHIEF EXECUTIVE OR HIS/HER REPRESENTATIVE AS
          CHAIRPERSON;
      (B) ONE (1) REPRESENTATIVE FROM THE LOCAL SANGGUNIAN;
      (C) REPRESENTATIVE OF THE PROVINCIAL, CITY, OR MUNICIPAL
          HEALTH OFFICE;
      (D) REPRESENTATIVE OF THE PROVINCIAL, CITY, OR MUNICIPAL
          HEALTH OFFICE;
      (E) REPRESENTATIVE OF SCHOOL DIVISION OFFICE OF THE
          DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION;

       (G) AT LEAST ONE (1) REPRESENTATIVE FROM NON-GOVERNMENT
           AND CIVIL SOCIETY ORGANIZATIONS INVOLVED IN ANIMAL
           WELFARE OR PUBLIC HEALTH.

   THE RABIES PREVENTION AND CONTROL COMMITTEE SHALL
   IMPLEMENT POLICIES ON RABIES AWARENESS, PREVENTION, AND
   TREATMENT, ESTABLISH ANIMAL BITE CENTERS IN THEIR
   RESPECTIVE LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNIT, IMPLEMENT FREE
   VACCINATION PROGRAMS, AND COORDINATE ALL LOCAL
   GOVERNMENT EFFORTS TO REDUCE RABIES-RELATED DEATHS.

SECTION 2. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the appropriations of the respective local government
unit in the year following its enactment into law. Thereafter, sums necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SECTION 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of the Interior
and Local Government, in consultation with the Department of Health and other
relevant agencies, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations (IRR)
within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act. The non-issuance of the IRR
shall not hinder the implementation of this Act.

SECTION 4. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 5. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
letters of instruction, proclamations, or administrative orders, rules or regulations that
are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

SECTION 6. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
