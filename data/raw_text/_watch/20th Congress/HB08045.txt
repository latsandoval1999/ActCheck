                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to strengthen agricultural education, expand training and
scholarship opportunities, and create institutional support mechanisms that will
encourage the Filipino youth to actively participate in the agricultural sector.

       Agriculture remains a vital pillar of the Philippine economy, yet the sector
continues to face the twin challenges of an aging farmer population and
declining youth interest. The average age of Filipino farmers is estimated to be
over Fifty five (55) years old1, highlighting the urgent need to cultivate a new
generation of agriculturists who can sustain food production, adopt modern
technologies, and respond to climate-related risks. Without deliberate
intervention, these demographic and structural challenges threaten the country’s
food security, agricultural productivity, and long term rural development.

       This measure establishes a National Network of Agricultural Educational
Institutions to strengthen coordination among schools offering agriculture
programs and improve the delivery of agricultural education and training. It
mandates the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) to provide priority
scholarships for undergraduate and graduate studies in agriculture and to revise
higher education curricula to integrate agribusiness, leadership, management,
research, information and communications technology (ICT), and rural
development. These align with the evolving needs of a modern and competitive
agricultural sector.

      The bill also expands internship opportunities through coordination with
relevant national and international institutions and establishes Youth Resource
Centers for Agriculture in every province to provide training materials,
technology access, and learning support for young Filipinos pursuing careers in

                         AN ACT
PROMOTING    AGRICULTURE   AMONG   FILIPINO YOUTH   TO
STRENGTHEN NATIONAL FOOD SECURITY, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Kabataan Para sa
Pagsasaka Act”.

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — In recognition of the huge role of
agriculture and the youth in developing our country, it is the policy of the State
to promote and support endeavors that will facilitate sustainable agricultural
development and provide avenues for active involvement and dignified status
within the agricultural sector.

      Towards this end, the goals and objectives of this Act are to:

         a. Recognize the crucial role of the agricultural sector as a
      fundamental aspect of economic growth and societal development;

         b. Rebuild and recreate a new, lively, and positive image of agriculture,
      especially within the youth sector;

         c. Revitalize the youth’s interest in agriculture through various
      programs and institutional linkages and partnerships;

         d. Adapt and modernize the education curriculum on agriculture to
      include leadership, management, and business subjects; and

         e.​ Promote and support research and development within the
      agriculture sector to find ways to advance sustainable development and
      adapting to climate change
​
        SEC. 3. National Network of Agricultural Educational Institutions. — To
facilitate institutional connections for the implementation of the Act, there shall
be an establishment of a national network of higher education institutions that
offers agricultural courses.
​
        SEC. 4. Higher Education Scholarships. — The Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) shall establish and allocate funds for a priority scholarship
program for those who will be taking up undergraduate, Masters, and Doctoral
programs related to agriculture. CHED shall provide subsidies to higher
education institutions in order to increase enrollment on agriculture-related
programs.
​
        Further, CHED, in cooperation with the National Network of Agricultural
Educational Institutions in Section 3, shall coordinate and link with universities
abroad who are offering postgraduate programs on agriculture and create a
continuing education program primarily intended for instructors of higher
education institutions on agriculture to improve on teaching and research.
​
        SEC. 5. Revising Higher Education Curricula on Agriculture. — Education
curriculum on agriculture courses in higher education institutions shall undergo
revision to include other subjects that respond to the needs of the labor market
and capacitate enterprise and development in the agricultural sector.

      This will include subjects such as, but not limited to:

         a.​ Agribusiness and Entrepreneurship;

         b.​ Leadership and Management in Agriculture Industry;

         c.​ Agriculture and Information and Communication Technology (ICT);

         d.​ Agricultural Research and Technology;

         e.​ Legal Agricultural Framework; and

         f.​ Agriculture and Rural Development

      Further, CHED shall spearhead a consultative committee with members
from the Department of Agriculture (DA), Department of Agrarian Reform
(DAR), higher education institutions, and other relevant stakeholders in the
process of revising higher education curriculum pertaining to agriculture.
​

       SEC. 6. Vocational Training Programs. — To promote further enhancement of
agricultural skills of rural youth, the Department of Agriculture - Agricultural
Training Institute (DA-ATI) and the Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA) shall create enhanced training programs to include
leadership, capacity building, and business management aspects and training of
professional instructors to meet the needs of the labor market and improve
agribusiness management and opportunities.

       Further, the DA-ATI and TESDA, in partnership with the Department of
Trade and Industry (DTI), shall create apprenticeship opportunities for farmers,
especially the youth, with rural agricultural micro, small, and medium enterprise
(MSMEs) to enhance entrepreneurial skills.
​
       SEC. 7. Internship. — The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE)
shall connect with various institutions such as the DA, DAR, International Rice
Research Institute (IRRI), Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) of the
United Nations, to provide internship opportunities for individuals, especially
students, who are involved with the agriculture sector.
​
       SEC. 8. Establishment of Youth Resource Centers for Agriculture. — In order to
provide the youth with various resources on the study and practical application
of agriculture and other various agri-related programs, the DA, DAR, CHED, in
cooperation with the National Network of Agricultural Educational Institutions,
shall establish at least one (1) main Youth Resource Center for Agriculture in each
province and, if possible, an annex center in each higher education institution
providing agricultural courses.

        The Youth Resource Center for Agriculture shall contain training
materials, educational resources, applicable technological equipment, and other
relevant resources related to the study and further appreciation of the agriculture
sector.

       Further, the Department of Science and Technology (DOST) and the
Department of Information and Communication Technology (DICT) shall be
responsible for creating technological services in these Centers to capacitate ICT
learning for youth with regards to agriculture.
​
       SEC. 9. Youth for Agriculture Council. — There shall be a youth council
under the supervision of National Youth Council (NYC) made to monitor and
evaluate the implementation of the Act and propose further programs that
respond to the aim to invite the youth into the agriculture sector. There shall be
seven (7) members which shall include students-representatives of various
higher educational institutions offering agricultural courses.
​
       SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulation. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Chairperson of CHED, Director-General of TESDA,

       SEC. 11. Appropriations. — An initial fund of Five Hundred Million
(P500,000,000.00) shall be appropriated for the immediate implementation of this

Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

       SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general
circulation.
