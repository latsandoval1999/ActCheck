                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

Food security remains to be one of the significant challenges faced by our country
today.1 In 2023, 31.4% of households suffered from moderate to severe food
insecurity.2 Recent global difficulties, including the conflict in the Middle East and the
resulting rise in fuel prices, have further highlighted our country’s vulnerability to
external economic shocks and disruptions in the food supply chain. These
circumstances emphasize the need to strengthen our domestic food production
systems, and to build more resilience and localized sources of food.

The country’s increasing reliance on imported agricultural products further highlights
this vulnerability. In 2025, the country’s total agricultural imports was valued at $20.37
billion, representing a 15.2% share of the country’s total imports. This is an increase
of 4.7% from the agricultural imports in 2024.3 In fact, imports have consistently
outpaced exports by more than double, resulting in agricultural trade deficits since
2018.4

In this context, urban and peri-urban agriculture has emerged as a possible approach
to increasing reliable food sources in cities, thereby strengthening food security and
resilience. Urban farming is the growing, raising, processing, and distribution of food
and non-food products within or on the fringe of a town or city.5 This provides not only
food but also livelihood opportunities for people in the city. It can increase access to
fresh produce, empower communities, and reduce transportation costs.6

Seeing the importance and potential of urban and peri-urban agriculture, the
Department of Agriculture created the National Urban and Peri-Urban Agriculture
Program. A banner program established in 2022, this was initiated partly due to
unstable food supplies during the COVID-19 pandemic. This Program focuses on the
distribution of agricultural inputs to program beneficiaries, establishment of community
urban farms, start-up of corporate urban farms for commercial scale, and
establishment of urban agriculture training and technology centers.7

While this is a laudable effort from the Department of Agriculture, its implementation
remains uneven across regions and dependent on yearly budgeting constraints. As
such, this measure aims to institutionalize the National Urban And Peri-Urban
Agriculture Program, including activities to establish urban, outdoor, rooftop, or indoor
farms and greenhouses, and hydroponic, aeroponic, and aquaponic farm facilities.
Other urban and peri-urban agriculture modules may also be adopted as new
technologies develop. This bill also mandates the DOST and other pertinent agencies
to prioritize the research, development, and transfer of technologies necessary in
promoting integrated agriculture technologies.

Moreover, idle and abandoned government lots and buildings owned by either national
and local governments or available land resources in state universities and colleges
will be considered for growing crops, raising livestock, and producing food using urban
agricultural methods. Finally, this measure creates an Urban and Peri-urban
Agriculture Fund to be used for the implementation of the proposed program.

Ultimately, our goal is to ensure that every Filipino has greater access to affordable
and nutritious food, while promoting innovation and sustainability in local food
production systems.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Urban and Peri-Urban
Agriculture Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to promote the people’s
right to health, adequate nutrition, and a balanced and healthful ecology. It is likewise
State policy to promote agricultural development and growth. Recognizing that rapid
urbanization poses challenges to traditional agricultural methods and means of food
production, the State shall prioritize the use of diverse agricultural methods in order to
ensure the food security of the country.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms are defined as
follows:

   a. Peri-urban areas refer to zones of transition from rural to urban land uses
      located between the outer limits of urban and regional centers and the rural
      environment. A barangay can be classified as peri-urban if this barangay is
      adjacent to urban area(s) and if any of the following criteria are met:
         i. Population density in the barangay is at least 300 inhabitants per square
             kilometer or per 100 hectares; or
        ii. Poverty incidence in the barangay is 50% or lower.



                                            4
   b. Urban and Peri-urban Agriculture refers to food production that occurs in urban
      areas and peri-urban areas which includes, but not limited to community
      gardening, vertical farming, conventional farming, hydroponics, containerized
      gardening, greenhouse, backyard gardening, rooftop gardens, and edible
      landscaping.
   c. Urban Areas refer to areas in the National Capital Region (NCR) and all the
      other areas falling under any of the following categories pursuant to the
      Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA):
          i. Category 1- Barangays with a population size of 5,000 or more; or
         ii. Category 2 - Barangays with at least one establishment with a minimum
             of 100 employees; or
        iii. Category 3 - Barangays with five or more establishments with 10 to 99
             employees, and five or more facilities within the two-kilometer radius
             from the barangay hall. These facilities are as follows: municipal/city hall
             or provincial capitol; church, chapel or mosque where religious services
             are held at least once a month; public/town plaza or park for recreation;
             cemetery; market place or building where trading activities are carried
             out at least once a week; public school buildings (elementary, high
             school, and college/university); hospital, puericulture center, barangay
             health center/station or clinic; fire station or community waterworks
             system; post office or postal service; landline telephone system or calling
             station; cellular phone signal; and public street sweeper.

SEC. 4. National Urban and Peri-Urban Agriculture Program. – There is hereby
established a National Urban And Peri-Urban Agriculture Program (Program) under
the Department of Agriculture (DA).

Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DA shall integrate and align
all its existing programs, initiatives, and projects related to urban and peri-urban
agriculture into this single, comprehensive Program.

Activities under the Program shall include urban, outdoor, rooftop, or indoor farms and
greenhouses, hydroponic, aeroponic, and aquaponic farm facilities; and other
innovations in urban and peri-urban agriculture production. Other urban and peri-urban
agriculture modules may also be adopted as appropriate and as new technologies
develop.

For the purpose of this Act, the respective Secretaries of the Department of Agriculture
(DA) and the Department of Science and Technology (DOST) are hereby mandated
to promote the use of urban and peri-urban agriculture within the country's
metropolitan areas as an instrument to address food security concerns and regenerate
the ecosystem function of the Philippines.




                                            5
The DOST and other pertinent agencies shall also prioritize the research,
development, and transfer of technologies necessary in promoting integrated
agriculture technologies.

Idle and/or abandoned government lots and buildings owned by either national and
local governments or available land resources in state universities and colleges shall
be considered for growing crops, raising livestock, and producing food using the said
methods.

SEC. 5. Creation and Composition of the Urban and Peri-urban Agriculture
Council. – There is hereby established an Urban and Peri-urban Agriculture Council
(Council) which shall be attached to the Department of Agriculture. The Council shall
be composed of the following members:
   a. The Secretary of the Department of Agriculture (DA) who shall serve as Chair
      of the Council;
   b. The Secretary of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources
      (DENR);
   c. The Secretary of the Department of Finance (DOF);
   d. The Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST);
   e. Representative from the People's Organizations (PO);
   f. Representative from the Non-Governmental Organization (NGO);
   g. Representative from the National Urban Poor Sector Council of the National
      Anti-Poverty Commission (NUPSC-NAPC);
   h. The National President of the League of the Cities of the Philippines;
   i. The National President of the League of the Municipalities of the Philippines;
      and,
   j. Representative from the private sector who have expertise in urban and peri-
      urban agriculture.

The Council shall adopt a ten-year Urban and Peri-urban Agriculture Roadmap, which
shall undergo review every three (3) years.

SEC. 6. Urban and Peri-urban Agriculture Fund. – There is hereby created an
Urban and Peri-urban Agriculture Fund, which shall be used for the implementation of
the Program. The Fund shall be sourced from the General Appropriations Act,
donations and grants, and other sources as may be determined by the Council.

SEC. 7. Local Government Units Participation. - Local government units shall
formulate the policy and adopt enabling ordinances on the practice of urban farming
and the utilization of unused spaces and idle lands in their respective areas.

All homeowners associations, neighborhood associations and community
associations and/or people's organizations are encouraged to participate in urban
farming activities within their area of operations. The concerned LGU may grant

necessary incentives for the benefit of the members of the concerned associations or
people’s organizations who actively participate in urban farming activities.

SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within six (6) months from the date
of effectivity of this Act, the DA, in consultation with the relevant government agencies
and stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations
to implement the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – Should any provision of this Act be declared
unconstitutional, the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. - Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation contrary to, or
inconsistent with the provisions of this act is hereby repealed, modified or amended
accordingly.

SEC. 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
