                      AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF THE OUT-OF-SCHOOL
YOUTH

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article II, Section 11 of the 1987 Constitution provides that the State values
the dignity of every human person and guarantees full respect for human rights.
Consistent with this mandate, the State must ensure that out-of-school youth are
not left behind in the country’s education, employment, and social protection
systems.

       Out-of-school youth face intersecting barriers that limit their full
participation in society. Many are unable to complete formal education due to
poverty, family responsibilities, early pregnancy, lack of access to schools,
displacement, abuse, disability, conflict with the law, or other difficult
circumstances. Without adequate intervention, they are placed at greater risk of
unemployment, exploitation, violence, trafficking, and long-term poverty.

       While existing programs such as the Alternative Learning System,
technical-vocational education, livelihood assistance, youth development
programs, and social welfare services provide important support, these
interventions remain dispersed across different agencies and local government
units. There is a need for a comprehensive rights-based framework that will
clearly define the rights of out-of-school youth, harmonize government programs,
  and ensure coordinated delivery of education, employment, health, social
  protection, and reintegration services.

        This bill seeks to provide a Magna Carta for Out-of-School Youth by
  recognizing, protecting, and promoting their rights and by mandating government
  agencies to provide targeted assistance and support. It strengthens access to
  formal, non-formal, informal, and technical-vocational education; promotes
  decent work and livelihood opportunities; ensures health, psychosocial, and
  social welfare interventions; and provides special protection for those in
  especially difficult circumstances.

         The measure further mandates the creation of an updated database and
  profiling system for out-of-school youth, strengthens the role of the National
  Youth Commission, local government units, Sangguniang Kabataan, Local
  Youth Development Councils, and concerned agencies, and encourages
  partnership with the private sector and non-government organizations. Through
  these mechanisms, programs for out-of-school youth may be better coordinated,
  monitored, and made more responsive to their actual needs.

        Ultimately, this measure recognizes out-of-school youth not as liabilities,
  but as rights-holders and partners in nation-building. By providing them with
  meaningful access to education, skills training, employment, social services, and
  protection, the State invests in their potential and strengthens inclusive national
  development.

       This measure has been filed since the 16th Congress. During the 19th
  Congress, it was approved on the third and final reading.

        In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                              CHAPTER I
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta of
the Out-of-School Youth.”

      Section 2. Declaration of Policies. – Article II, Section 11 of the
Constitution provides that the State values the dignity of every human person and
guarantees full respect for human rights. Pursuant to the aforementioned mandate,
the State recognizes the rights of the out-of-school youth (OSY) to social
protection and to the development of their full potential as partners in nation-
building.

      Section 3. Objectives. – The objectives of this Act are as follows:
      (a) To recognize, protect, and promote the rights of the OSYs;
      (b) To empower and encourage the OSYs to contribute to nation-building;
(c) To give full support to the improvement of the total well-being of the
    OSYs by providing learning and employment opportunities as well as
    the needed social services and interventions; and,
(d) To recognize the important role of the private sector in improving the
    welfare of the OSY and to actively seek their partnership.

                        CHAPTER II
                   DEFINITION OF TERMS

Section 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
(a) Alternative Learning System refers to a parallel learning system in the
    Philippines that provides a practical option to the existing formal
    instruction and equivalent pathways to basic education. It includes both
    the non-formal and informal sources of knowledge and skills;
(b) Especially Difficult Circumstances refer to situations of extreme
    vulnerability, risk, or marginalization faced by an OSY, including but
    not limited to being a person with disability (PWD); a victim of sexual,
    emotional, or physical abuse, domestic violence, illegal recruitment,
    prostitution, or human trafficking; being displaced by armed conflict or
    natural disasters; being an out-of-school youth in conflict with the law
    (CICL); or being severely affected by pandemics, public health
    emergencies, or other socio-economic crises that cause functional
    incapacity or severe disruption to personal development and social
    integration.
(c) Local Social Welfare and Development Officer (LSWDO) refers to the
    officer designated as such under the Social Welfare and Development
    Offices in the local government unit (LGU);
(d) Out-of-School Youth refers to member of the population aged 15 to 30
    years old who is currently out of school, not gainfully employed, and
    has not finished secondary or college education;
(e) Social Protection refers to the systemic policies, programs, and safety
    nets implemented by the State pursuant to the constitutional mandate
    on social justice, aimed at humanizing the laws, reducing socio-
    economic inequalities, and mitigating the vulnerability of the OSY to
    systemic risks, poverty, and marginalization by actively promoting
    equity, social welfare, and secure employment opportunities.

                                                                          2
      (f) Substantive Equality refers to the progressive application of the Equal
          Protection Clause which demands that persons under different
          circumstances and conditions be treated differently in law to achieve
          equitable outcomes; it requires the State to provide preferential
          interventions, rights, and opportunities to disadvantaged groups like the
          OSY to correct structural inequalities, eradicate discrimination, and
          ensure actual parity in growth and development;
      (g) Technical-Vocational Education and Training (TVET) refers to the
          education or training process designed at post-secondary and lower
          tertiary levels, officially recognized as non-degree programs aimed at
          preparing technicians, paraprofessionals and other categories of
          middle-level workers by providing them with a broad range of general
          education, theoretical, scientific and technological studies, and related
          job skills training; and
      (h) TVET graduate refers to a student or trainee who has completed the
          requirements set for a Technical Education and Skills Development
          Authority (TESDA)-registered TVET course or program.

                     CHAPTER III
DUTIES RELATED TO THE HUMAN RIGHTS OF OUT-OF-SCHOOL
                       YOUTH

      Section 5. The State as the Primary Duty-Bearer. – The State shall,
through the National Youth Commission (NYC), Commission on Human Rights
(CHR), and other concerned youth-serving agencies, be guided by progressive
developments on the aspect of human rights of the OSY under existing laws,
including Republic Act No. 8044, otherwise known as the "Youth in Nation-
Building Act," Republic Act No. 11510, otherwise known as the "Alternative
Learning System Act," Republic Act No. 10917, otherwise known as the "Special
Program for Employment of Students Act," and Republic Act No. 10679,
otherwise known as the "Youth Entrepreneurship Act" and international law. The
State shall also endeavor to formulate policies, laws, and other regulatory
measures to fulfill these duties. It shall institute programs that will carry out the
objectives of this Act and promote:
      (a) The protection of the rights of the OSYs against discrimination of any
          kind by private corporations, entities, individuals, and from any
          institution, public or private; and
                                                                                   3
      (b) The promotion of substantive equality on the rights of the OSY in all
          aspects of growth and development.

       Section 6. Duties of the State Agencies and Instrumentalities. – The
duties of the State as stated in Section 5 of this Act shall extend to all state
agencies, offices, and instrumentalities at all levels of government, including
government-owned and -controlled corporations, subject to the Constitution and
pertinent laws, policies, and administrative guidelines that define specific duties
of concerned state agencies and entities.

                             CHAPTER IV
                      RIGHTS AND EMPOWERMENT

      Section 7. Human Rights of Out-of-School Youth. – An OSY shall enjoy,
without discrimination, all rights provided for in the Constitution, existing
Philippine laws, local ordinances, and those rights recognized under international
instruments duly ratified by the Philippines.

      Section 8. Protection from Violence. – The State shall ensure that all
OSYs shall be protected from all forms of violence, physical abuse, sexual
exploitation, and human trafficking as provided for under existing laws. The
Department of Justice, Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD),
and other concerned agencies shall give priority to the defense and protection of
the OSYs and help them in attaining justice and social equality.

      Section 9. Equal Treatment Before the Law. – The NYC and other youth-
serving agencies shall take active steps to review and, when necessary, submit
proposals to amend or repeal existing laws that are discriminatory to the OSYs,
and submit legislative proposals, as far as practicable, at the start of every
Congress.

       Section 10. Representative of Out-of-School Youth Issues and Concerns
in Media, Film, and Other Platforms. – The NYC and other concerned agencies
shall formulate policies and programs for the advancement of the OSYs in
collaboration with government and non-government organization (NGO) media-
related organizations.

                                                                                 4
      For this purpose, the State shall ensure the allocation of resources for the
production, publication, dissemination, and airing of all forms of information
materials on the rights of the OSYs and the various services and benefits provided
to them under this Act.

       Section 11. Recognition and Preservation of Cultural Identity and
Integrity. – The rights of out-of-school youth belonging to the Bangsamoro, non-
Moro indigenous communities, and all other Indigenous Cultural Communities
and Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) throughout the country to practice, promote,
protect, and preserve their own culture, traditions, and institutions, and to
consider these rights in the formulation and implementation of national policies
and programs, shall be upheld. To this end, the National Commission on Muslim
Filipinos (NCMF) and the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP)
shall, within their respective mandates and in close consultation with the sectors
concerned, protect their rights, indigenous knowledge systems and practices,
traditional livelihoods, and other cultural manifestations, and recommend
legislation and other appropriate measures to promote and respect them:
Provided, That these cultural systems and practices do not discriminate against
other OSYs who do not belong to the Bangsamoro or to any recognized ICCs/IPs.

      Section 12. Right to Decent Work. – The Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE) shall, in coordination with TESDA, ensure decent work
standards for OSYs who have graduated from TVET courses, including the
following minimum guarantees:
      (a) Local job generation and employment, economic opportunities, and
          providing strict regulations against forced and involuntary
          displacement;
      (b) Protection of the rights and promotion of the welfare of migrant TVET
          graduates regardless of work status and protection against
          discrimination in wages, conditions of work, and employment
          opportunities in host countries; and
      (c) Opportunities for work shall be productive and fairly remunerative as
          family living wage, security of tenure in the workplace, and better
          prospects for personal development and social integration.

                        CHAPTER V
             GOVERNMENT ASSISTANCE AND SUPPORT
                                                                                5
       Section 13. Government Assistance. – The National Government, through
its agencies and instrumentalities shall provide the following:
       a) Education – The Department of Education (DepEd), TESDA, and the
          Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall, in consultation with
          non-government organizations (NGOs), institute a program that will
          ensure access to formal, informal, and non-formal learning
          opportunities;
       b) Health – The Department of Health (DOH) shall, in coordination with
          LGUs and NGOs, institute a national health program and provide an
          integrated health service for OSYs: Provided, That the DOH shall
          provide the OSYs and their families with sexual and reproductive health
          education and information, as well as family counseling to curb the
          number of dropout cases due to early marriage and pregnancy, marital
          conflict, and other family problems;
       c) Social Services – The DSWD shall, in cooperation with the LGUs,
          NGOs, and other relevant stakeholders, develop and implement
          programs on social services for the OSYs, the components of which are:
              (1) Social Enhancement Services that provide the OSYs with
                  opportunities for socializing, organizing creative expression, and
                  self-improvement;
              (2) After Care Services that provide support for the OSYs who are
                  discharged from the homes or institutions of the DSWD, and
                  other private institutions duly accredited by the DSWD,
                  especially those who have problems of reintegration with family
                  and the community; and
              (3) Social Case Management that includes a comprehensive, updated
                  list and detailed profile of all known OSYs in the respective
                  localities for the purpose of providing age-appropriate social
                  service interventions.
       d) Employment – The DOLE shall, in coordination with other government
          agencies such as the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and
          TESDA, assess, design, and implement training programs that will
          provide jobs, entrepreneurial skills and facilitation, agricultural
          training, and livelihood opportunities for the OSYs.


                                                                                  6
       Section 14. Social Protection. – The National Anti-Poverty Commission
and concerned agencies shall institute for the OSYs policies and programs that
seek to reduce poverty and vulnerability to risks by mandating their employers to
provide mandatory life, accident, and health insurance coverage.

                                CHAPTER VI
                                EDUCATION

       Section 15. Technical Vocational Education. – It shall be the duty of the
State to provide technical or vocational education to the OSYs. The course shall
be provided and managed by TESDA subject to its rules and regulations:
Provided, That an officer of the TESDA shall be assigned as a TESDA adviser
who shall monitor the progress and development of the OSYs in the chosen
technical vocational course.

       Section 16. Alternative Learning System. – An ALS of education shall be
made available to OSYs.
       For OSYs who are under the care and custody of a home or an institution
managed or accredited by the DSWD, the latter shall coordinate with the DepEd
for the provision of an ALS within its premises.

      Section 17. Promotion to Formal Tertiary Education. – The LSWDO
may recommend an OSY to be a recipient of a scholarship grant from the CHED
under either Republic Act No. 10931, otherwise known as the “Universal Access
to Quality Tertiary Education Act” or Republic Act No. 10687, otherwise known
as the “Unified Student Financial Assistance System for Tertiary Education
(UniFAST) Act.”
      An OSY who is a recipient of any CHED scholarship grant shall execute a
scholarship bond, subject to the condition of rendering at least two (2) years of
service to the government upon completion thereof.

       Section 18. Entrepreneurial Education. – The DTI shall, in coordination
with TESDA and other relevant government agencies, conduct training programs
to provide the OSYs with the knowledge, skills, and motivation to engage in
entrepreneurial endeavors in a variety of settings. These programs shall include
small business management, new approaches to innovation, developing creativity
or the introduction of new products or services to the markets and providing aid
                                                                               7
to nascent entrepreneurial undertaking to grow and sustain the business of the
OSYs.
      Both public and private higher education institutions shall set a minimum
number of admissions of returning OSYs, scholarship grants, and other financial
support to OSYs.

      Section 19. Education Cost and Support. – The DepEd, CHED, and
TESDA shall provide formal, non-formal, informal, technical and vocational
education services to the OSYs free of charge. It shall likewise provide materials,
instruments, and tools needed to carry out and complete said programs.

                          CHAPTER VII
              ESPECIALLY DIFFICULT CIRCUMSTANCES

      Section 20. Especially Difficult Circumstances. – OSYs who are
determined to be under especially difficult circumstances, as defined under
Section 4 of this Act, shall be immediately provided by the State, through the
DSWD, LGUs, and other relevant youth-serving agencies, with comprehensive
and age-appropriate services and interventions as necessary, which shall include:
      (a) Temporary and protective custody;
      (b) Medical, dental, and physical therapy services;
      (c) Mental health care;
      (d) Psychological evaluation;
      (e) Counseling;
      (f) Psychiatric evaluation;
      (g) Legal services;
      (h) Productivity capability building;
      (i) Livelihood assistance;
      (j) Financial assistance;
      (k) Life skill training; and
      (l) Health education and information.

                          CHAPTER VIII
                ADMINISTRATION AND ENFORCEMENT

      Section 21. Role of the Advisory Council. – The Advisory Council created
under Section 14 of Republic Act No. 8044, otherwise known as the “Youth in
                                                                                 8
Nation-Building Act,” shall, in addition to its respective mandates, implement the
provisions of this Act which shall include the following duties and functions:
      (a) Formulate a National Comprehensive Multi-Stakeholder Plan of Action
          for the OSYs which shall include back-to-school transitional support,
          career pathing, counseling, mental and reproductive health education
          and services, skills training and livelihood assistance;
      (b) Coordinate, monitor, and ensure that the relevant agencies of the
          government have appropriate yearly work programs for the OSYs in
          accordance with the objectives of this Act;
      (c) Cause the dissemination of information and engender national public
          awareness of the plight of the OSYs in the country through social
          media, government media and broadcasting facilities, and other modern
          channels of communication;
      (d) Review and ascertain existing policies that are discriminatory to the
          OSYs as a subsector and recommend to Congress remedial legislative
          measures and interventions to eradicate all forms of discrimination to
          the OSYs; and
      (e) Furnish the NYC on a yearly basis a national situationer on OSYs,
          including the available programs, services, and interventions available
          for them.

      Section 22. Roles and Responsibilities of Concerned Agencies. – The

            (3) Capacitate the Sangguniang Kabataan (SK), Task Force on
                Youth Development, and Local Youth Development Council
                (LYDC) to implement the provisions of this Act at the local level;

            (4) Coordinate with concerned government agencies, private or
                NGOs, the necessary programs and projects designed to promote
                the general welfare, development, and empowerment of the
                OSYs as part of the multi-stakeholder plan of action for the
                OSYs.

      Section 23. Role of Non-Government Organizations. – NGOs or private
volunteer organizations dedicated to promoting, enhancing, and supporting the
welfare of OSYs are hereby encouraged to become government partners in
implementing programs and projects for the OSYs.

                                CHAPTER IX
                             FINAL PROVISIONS

      Section 24. Punishable Acts and Penalties. – The following acts shall be
deemed violations of this Act with the following corresponding penalties: (1)
Discrimination against OSYs by private corporations, entities, and individuals;
(2) Discrimination in wages, conditions of work, and employment opportunities;
and (3) Non-compliance with the mandatory coverage by employers of health,
accident, and life insurance.
      (a) For the first violation, imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but
          not more than two (2) years and a fine of not less than Fifty thousand

                                                                                  12
           pesos (P50,000.00) but not more than One hundred thousand pesos
           (P100,000.00);
       (b) For any subsequent violation, imprisonment of not less than two (2)
           years but not more than six (6) years and a fine of not less than One
           hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00) but not more than Two hundred
           thousand pesos (P200,000.00);
       (c) For any person who avails of any of the benefits and privileges under
           this Act but who is not qualified to avail of the same, imprisonment of
           not less than six (6) months and a fine of not less than Fifty thousand
           pesos (P50,000.00) but not more than One hundred thousand pesos
           (P100,000.00); and
       (d) If the offender is a corporation, organization, or any similar entity, the
           officials thereof directly involved in the violation or abuse shall be held
           liable.
       If the offender is an alien or a foreigner, the person shall be deported
immediately upon service of sentence without further proceedings.
       Upon filing of the appropriate complaint or information, the proper
authorities shall, in coordination with the concerned LGU, cause the cancellation
or revocation of the business permit, permit to operate, franchise, and other
similar privileges granted to any business, entity, or person that fails to abide by
or violates the provisions of this Act.

      Section 25. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s
appropriation of the departments and agencies concerned. Thereafter, such
amount shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
      The LGU concerned may set aside funds from any local revenue an amount
deemed appropriate for the implementation of this Act.

       Section 26. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The NYC, the
members of the Advisory Council created under Republic Act No. 8044, TESDA,
and CHED shall, not later than thirty (30) days upon the effectivity of this Act,
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation
of this Act: Provided, That failure to promulgate the rules and regulations shall
not prevent the implementation of this Act upon its effectivity.


                                                                                   13
      Section 27. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      Section 28. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances,
decrees, rules, and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of
this Act are deemed amended, modified or repealed accordingly.

       Section 29. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
