                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines continues to face a persistent mismatch between higher education outcomes and the
needs of the labor market. Many college graduates remain underemployed or work in positions that
do not require a college degree, while employers continue to report difficulty finding workers with
the skills needed in key sectors. This reflects a deeper disconnect between what higher education
produces and what the economy demands.

One major cause is the fragmentation of labor market information. Relevant data are collected
separately by government agencies, making it difficult for higher education institutions (HEIs),
policymakers, training providers, and employers to work from a common and updated understanding
of emerging skills needs. At the same time, partnerships between HEIs and industry remain largely
informal and inconsistent, limiting opportunities to align curricula, internships, research, and
training with actual workforce demand.

This measure therefore a Higher Education-Industry Partnership Framework to establish sustained
cooperation between HEIs and industry stakeholders. The bill creates a Higher Education-Industry
Coordination Council to consolidate labor market and skills information and produce a National
Skills Gap Inventory and an Annual National Skills and Labor Market Intelligence Report. It also
establishes Regional Industry-Higher Education Advisory Boards to ensure that regional industries,
local employment conditions, and the needs of state universities and colleges are reflected in higher
education planning.

By connecting education planning with reliable labor market intelligence and meaningful industry
participation, this measure seeks to ensure that Filipino graduates are equipped not only with
degrees, but with competencies relevant to the changing needs of the economy. It provides a more
coordinated and responsive approach to reducing skills gaps, improving graduate employability, and
strengthening the contribution of higher education to national and regional development.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Higher Education-Industry Partnership
 2   Framework Act."
 3   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to develop a higher
 4   education system that produces graduates equipped with the competencies, values, and adaptability
 5   necessary for productive employment, lifelong learning, and active participation in national
 6   development.
 7   It is likewise the policy of the State to ensure that higher education institutions, industry, and
 8   government coordinate in a sustained and structured manner in identifying skills requirements,
 9   sharing labor market intelligence, and jointly accounting for graduate outcomes, consistent with
10   academic freedom, institutional autonomy, and the distinct mandates of all agencies concerned.
11   Toward this end, the State shall institutionalize a national framework for higher education-industry
12   cooperation; establish a body mandated to generate, consolidate, and disseminate the labor market
13   and skills intelligence necessary for informed decision-making; create regional mechanisms for
14   localized industry engagement; and provide clear lines of accountability for the implementation of
15   this Act.
16   SEC. 3. Scope and Application. - This Act shall apply to:
17      (a) Public and private higher education institutions, duly recognized by the Commission on
18          Higher Education (CHED), including state universities and colleges, local universities and
19          colleges, and private higher education institutions offering degree and non-degree programs
20          within the tertiary education system;
21      (b) Industry partners, including enterprises, employer organizations, chambers of commerce,
22          professional associations, cooperatives, industry clusters, social enterprises, and other
23          entities engaged in economic activity, innovation, and employment generation that may
24          contribute to or benefit from higher education-industry collaboration; and
25      (c) Relevant government agencies, including those involved in higher education, workforce
26          development, skills formation, innovation, labor, trade, and economic planning, which shall
27          participate within their respective mandates, functions, and areas of responsibility.
28   Provided, That nothing in this Act shall be construed to mandate the participation of any HEI or
29   industry partner in any partnership arrangement; to alter or supersede existing accreditation, quality
30   assurance, or licensing requirements applicable to HEIs; to prescribe curriculum content or academic
31   standards; to interfere with the institutional governance or academic freedom of any HEI; or to
32   confer upon any body created under this Act any regulatory, supervisory, or enforcement power not
33   otherwise authorized by law.
34   SEC. 4. The Higher Education-Industry Partnership Framework. - There is hereby
35   institutionalized the Higher Education-Industry Partnership Framework, hereinafter referred to as
36   the Framework, as the national policy and governance architecture for structured and sustained
37   cooperation between HEIs and industry partners. The Framework shall govern the conduct of all
38   higher education-industry partnerships under this Act.
39          The Framework shall serve to:
40      (a) Define the principles, permissible modes, and institutional roles governing the engagement
41          of industry partners in higher education matters, consistent with academic freedom and
42          institutional autonomy;
43      (b) Provide formal and standing channels for dialogue, joint planning, and feedback between
44          HEIs and industry partners at the national, regional, and sectoral levels;
45      (c) Promote shared accountability among HEIs, industry partners, and government for graduate
46          relevance, skills utilization, research translation, and workforce readiness;
47      (d) Enable evidence-informed generation, publication, and use of labor market and skills
48          intelligence, and graduate outcomes information; and decision-making through the
49          systematic generation, publication, and use of labor market and skills intelligence, and
50          graduate outcomes information; and
51      (e) Ensure coherence between higher education outcomes, workforce development objectives,
52          and national and regional development strategies.
53   Provided, That the Framework shall function solely as a coordination, facilitation, and alignment
54   mechanism. It shall not exercise regulatory, supervisory, or enforcement powers, nor shall it
55   prescribe curriculum standards, confer academic or professional credentials, or interfere with the
56   governance or quality assurance processes of any HEI.
57   SEC. 5. Guiding Principles. - The implementation of the Framework shall be governed by the
58   following principles:
59      (a) Collaboration between HEIs and industry partners shall be structured, sustained, and
60          embedded in institutional policies and governance mechanisms, rather than ad hoc or project-
61          based;
62      (b) HEIs and industry partners shall jointly identify challenges and priorities, and shall
63          collaboratively design, implement, and review partnership initiatives;
64      (c) HEIs, industry partners, and government shall recognize their shared responsibility for
65          graduate relevance, employability, research translation, and workforce readiness, with the
66          end in view of advancing decent and dignified work for Filipino graduates;
67      (d) All partnerships shall at all times uphold academic freedom and the authority of HEIs over
68          academic matters and internal governance;
69      (e) The Framework shall promote equitable participation across institutional types, geographic
70          regions, and population groups, with particular attention to gender equity, the needs of
71          persons with disabilities, indigenous peoples, and learners from historically underserved
72          communities;
73      (f) Partnerships shall be designed and implemented in a manner that anticipates and responds to
74          structural changes in the labor market and emerging shifts in occupational demand. Such
75          changes include those arising from the digital transition, meaning the transformation of
76          industries, work processes, and occupations through digital technologies, automation, and
77          artificial intelligence; the green transition, meaning the shift of the economy toward
78          environmentally sustainable production, renewable energy, and low-carbon industries; the
79          care economy; and other evolving sectors, employing technology-neutral approaches where
80          applicable; and
81      (g) The Framework shall recognize the substantial differences in labor market conditions,
82          industry composition, and development priorities across the regions of the country. State
83          universities and colleges, as anchor institutions in their respective regional economies, shall
84          be supported in developing partnerships calibrated to local industry clusters, regional
85          workforce needs, and area-specific development priorities.
86   SEC. 6. Modes of Partnership. - Consistent with this Act, collaboration between HEIs and industry
87   partners may be undertaken through the following modes, subject to applicable laws, rules, and
88   regulations:
89      (a) Structured advisory arrangements, including the participation of industry representatives in
90          consultative or advisory bodies, committees, or forums established by an HEI for the purpose
 91          of providing inputs on program relevance, emerging skills requirements, occupational
 92          trends, and industry developments, without encroaching upon academic decision-making;
 93      (b) Joint planning and coordination for work-integrated learning, including internships,
 94          practicums, apprenticeships, on-the-job training, and other forms of supervised workplace
 95          exposure, whether conducted on-site, virtually, or in hybrid modes, undertaken in
 96          coordination with applicable enterprise-based education and training frameworks and
 97          designed to support graduate transition and employability;
 98      (c) Collaborative research, development, and innovation initiatives, including joint or co-funded
 99          research projects, applied research activities, technology development, innovation
100          challenges, and knowledge-transfer activities that respond to industry needs and societal
101          priorities, consistent with institutional research policies, applicable ethical standards, and
102          Republic Act No. 10055, otherwise known as the Technology Transfer Act;
103      (d) Faculty exposure, immersion, and professional development programs, including
104          opportunities for faculty members to engage with industry settings for the purpose of
105          updating professional knowledge, strengthening industry awareness, and enhancing the
106          relevance of instruction and research, subject to institutional policies and applicable
107          guidelines;
108      (e) Shared access to facilities, laboratories, equipment, and innovation spaces, including
109          arrangements for the joint use, development, or enhancement of physical or digital
110          infrastructure that support learning, research, and innovation, subject to agreed terms and
111          applicable regulations;
112      (f) Coordinated participation in regional and sectoral development planning, including
113          engagement in the formulation of industry roadmaps, regional workforce development plans,
114          innovation strategies, and other development initiatives that require inputs from both HEIs
115          and industry partners;
116      (g) Technology-mediated and digital partnership arrangements, including virtual and hybrid
117          advisory engagements, online and hybrid work-integrated learning programs, and digital
118          platforms for joint curriculum relevance mapping and the co-development and open sharing
119          of educational resources, subject to applicable data privacy, intellectual property, and quality
120          assurance requirements; and
121      (h) Lifelong learning and continuing education partnerships, including the joint design and
122          delivery of micro-credentials, stackable learning programs, upskilling and reskilling courses,
123          and recognition-of-prior-learning arrangements for employed workers and returning
124          overseas Filipinos.
125   Provided, That participation in any of the foregoing modes of partnership shall be voluntary and
126   shall be entered into by mutual agreement of the parties concerned; Provided, further, That academic
127   decision-making authority shall at all times remain exclusively with the HEI; Provided, finally, That
128   such partnerships shall not substitute for, preempt, or circumvent any accreditation, quality
129   assurance, program approval, licensing, or other regulatory process required by law or by the
130   appropriate government authorities.
131   SEC. 7. Creation of the Council. - There is hereby created the Higher Education-Industry
132   Coordination Council, hereinafter referred to as the Council, which shall serve as the principal
133   national body for the coordination of higher education-industry cooperation, the generation and
134   dissemination of labor market and skills intelligence relevant to tertiary education, and the
135   monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of this Act.
136   The Council shall be advisory and coordinative in nature. It shall exercise its functions exclusively
137   through recommendation, facilitation, and the publication of intelligence and reports. It shall not
138   exercise regulatory, accrediting, licensing, or enforcement powers, and shall not supplant,
139   supersede, or diminish the statutory mandates of CHED, the Technical Education and Skills
140   Development Authority (TESDA), the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), the
141   Department of Education (DepEd), the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), or any other agency
142   of government.
143   SEC. 8. Mandate of the Council. - The Council shall carry out the following functions and shall
144   produce the deliverables specified herein within the timelines prescribed:
145      (a) National Labor Market and Skills Intelligence System. The Council shall establish, maintain,
146          and continuously update a National Labor Market and Skills Intelligence System that
147          consolidates labor market and skills data from CHED, TESDA, DOLE, the Philippine
148          Statistics Authority, the Department of Migrant Workers, the Overseas Workers Welfare
149          Administration, and such other agencies and sources as the Council may determine.
150      The System shall disaggregate all data by administrative region, economic sector, gender,
151      disability status, and socio-economic background. It shall specifically monitor the effects of
152      the digital and green transitions on graduate occupations and skills demand; the competency
153      requirements of the care economy; and occupational data on deployed overseas Filipino
154      workers, skills acquired abroad, and return-migration employment outcomes, as a source of
155      global labor market intelligence unique to the Philippine context.
156      All data consolidated under the System shall be published as an open and publicly accessible
157      dataset through a dedicated online portal maintained by the Secretariat. The portal shall be
158      launched within one year from the constitution of the Council and shall be updated not less
159      than once per year.
160   (b) National Skills Gap Inventory. Within eighteen months from the effectivity of this Act, the
161       Council shall complete and publicly release the first National Skills Gap Inventory for
162       Tertiary Education, identifying current and emerging skills gaps, priority competencies, and
163       occupational mismatches at the national and regional levels across all major economic
164       sectors, including those affected by the green and digital transitions and the care economy.
165       The Inventory shall be updated every three years thereafter.
166   (c) Annual National Skills and Labor Market Intelligence Report. The Council shall publish and
167       transmit to the Senate, House of Representatives, and the Office of the President, an Annual
168       National Skills and Labor Market Intelligence Report. Each Report shall contain, at
169       minimum:
170         (i) Regional Employability Scorecard presenting graduate employment rates,
171              underemployment rates, and degree-to-job match rates for each administrative
172              region;
173        (ii)  A National Skills Gap Summary identifying the ten highest-priority skills shortages
174              at the national level and the three highest-priority shortages per administrative
175              region;
176       (iii)  A Green and Digital Transition Monitor tracking the emergence and growth of
177              occupations in green, digital, and care sectors relevant to HEI graduates;
178       (iv) An OFW Skills and Reintegration Dashboard presenting occupational data on
179              deployed overseas Filipino workers, skills acquired abroad, and return-migration
180              employment outcomes; and
181        (v)   Equity indicators disaggregating graduate outcomes by gender, disability status,
182              administrative region, and socio-economic background.
183   (d) National Coordination. The Council shall convene structured dialogue, joint planning
184       sessions, and coordination meetings among HEIs, industry partners, and relevant
185       government agencies at the national level. It shall receive, consolidate, and integrate regional
186       and sectoral intelligence submitted by Regional Advisory Boards into its Annual Report and
187       policy recommendations, ensuring that regional development differences and the particular
188       needs of state universities and colleges in provincial economies are systematically reflected
189       in national planning.
190   (e) Annual General Assembly. The Council shall convene an Annual General Assembly of HEIs,
191       industry partners, relevant government agencies, and other stakeholders. The Annual Report
192       shall be formally presented at the Assembly. The Secretariat shall publish a summary of the
193       proceedings, outcomes, and recommendations of the Assembly within thirty days from the
194       date of its convening.
195   (f) Periodic Review of the Framework. Every five years from the effectivity of this Act, the
196       Council shall conduct a comprehensive review of the Framework, inclusive of a public
197       consultation process, and shall submit its findings and recommendations for the updating of
198       the Framework to CHED and to Congress. Each review shall be completed within twelve
199       months from the date of its commencement.
200   (g) Capacity Development. The Council shall coordinate capacity development initiatives for
201       HEIs in resource-constrained regions, with particular attention to state universities and
202       colleges, covering partnership development, the use of labor market intelligence in
203       institutional planning, and industry engagement, drawing upon the technical expertise and
204       resources of its member agencies.

210            (i) The Chairperson of CHED, who shall serve as Chairperson of the Council;
211           (ii)  The Director-General of TESDA, who shall serve as Vice-Chairperson;
212          (iii)  The Secretary of the DOLE;
213          (iv) The Secretary of the DepEd; and
214           (v)   The Secretary of the DTI.
215          (vi) The higher education sector shall be represented by six members designated
216      (b) The higher education sector shall be represented by six members designated from nationally
217          recognized federations of public and private HEIs and accreditation bodies, in accordance
218          with guidelines to be issued by CHED. Designation shall ensure representation across
219          institutional types and geographic regions. At least two members shall represent state
220          universities and colleges located outside of Metro Manila.
221      (c) The industry sector shall be represented by six members designated from nationally
222          recognized employer, business, professional, and sectoral federations, in accordance with

224          industries operating outside of Metro Manila, and at least one member shall represent small
225          and medium enterprises.
226          CHED shall establish and maintain a permanent Secretariat to provide technical and
227   administrative support to the Council.
228   SEC. 10. Regional Advisory Boards. Within two years from the effectivity of this Act, at least one
229   Regional Industry-Higher Education Advisory Board shall be constituted in each administrative
230   region of the Philippines. Sectoral Advisory Boards may additionally be constituted to address the
231   needs of specific industry sectors.
232   Each Regional Advisory Board shall serve as the primary body for the gathering and consolidation
233   of industry-HEI intelligence at the regional level. It shall identify and document the skills
234   requirements of local industries; emerging workforce and occupational trends within the region; the
235   degree of alignment between regional HEI program offerings and local labor market demand; and
236   partnership opportunities at the regional level that merit consideration at the national level. Each
237   Regional Advisory Board shall submit its annual regional intelligence inputs to the Secretariat not
238   later than the thirty-first day of January of each year, for incorporation into the Annual National
239   Skills and Labor Market Intelligence Report.
240   Provided, That the composition, procedures, terms of office, and operations of Regional Advisory
241   Boards, including Sectoral Advisory Boards, shall be prescribed in the IRR, which shall ensure
242   representation from state universities and colleges, private HEIs, local government units, large
243   enterprises, and small and medium enterprises operating within the region or sector concerned.
244   SEC. 11. Institutional Partnership Units. - Every HEI is encouraged to establish or designate a
245   Higher Education-Industry Partnership Unit to serve as the institutional focal point for the planning,
246   coordination, and management of industry engagement consistent with this Act. Where constituted,
247   the unit shall facilitate the participation of the HEI in partnership activities under this Act; ensure
248   that all partnership arrangements comply with applicable ethical standards, academic integrity
249   requirements, and conflict-of-interest rules; maintain institutional records of partnership activities
250   and outcomes; and submit data to the Secretariat as may be required for the preparation of the Annual
251   Report.
252   SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - CHED, in consultation with TESDA, DOLE,
253   DTI, DepEd, and representatives of HEIs and industry partners, shall promulgate the IRR within
254   ninety days from the effectivity of this Act.
255   SEC. 13. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be
256   charged against the current appropriations of the Commission on Higher Education (CHED).
257   Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
258   Act.
