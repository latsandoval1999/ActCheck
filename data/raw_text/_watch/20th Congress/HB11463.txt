                                AN ACT
    UPGRADING THE SALARY GRADE OF DENTISTS EMPLOYED IN THE PUBLIC
      SECTOR, PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        A person’s overall health and well-being includes oral health. However, for many
Filipino families, dental care is not a priority. Oftentimes, people just go to the dentist
when the pain is no longer bearable. When a family’s financial resources are limited, it is
understandable that they would rather spend on food, medicine, and other basic needs
rather than preventive dental care. This is the reason why affordable and accessible
dental services in public health facilities are vital to the country’s healthcare system.

       The state of oral health among Filipinos shows the need to prioritize dental
services in the country. According to the DOH, in 2023, about 3 out of 10 Filipino children
aged 5 years and older have decayed, missing, or filled teeth.1 Meanwhile, the Philippine
Dental Association estimates that 7 out of 10 Filipinos have dental caries or tooth decay,
and this number includes school-age children.2 Oral health problems, when left untreated,
can cause pain and infection, and interfere with one’s daily life.

        Despite the importance of oral health, the country faces a shortage of dentists
within the public health system. This year, the DOH reports that the current dentist-to-
population ratio is at 1 to 53,000, which is below the ratio of 1 to 7,500 as recommended
by the World Health Organization.3 To bridge this gap, the government must be able to
attract and retain qualified dentists.

       Currently, the entry level position for dentists (Dentist I) is classified as Salary
Grade 14 while Dentist II is at Salary Grade 17.4 However, this compensation must be
evaluated in light of the years of education, licensure requirement, and technical expertise
required of dentists. The salaries for dentists must likewise be assessed against the
opportunities available to them in private practice, where dentists have greater control
over their professional fees and the volume of procedures performed.

        The adequacy of compensation is particularly important in ensuring that qualified
dentists are encouraged to enter and remain in government service. When there are too
few dentists serving in public health facilities, the burden of limited access to oral
healthcare falls most heavily on communities that have fewer resources and fewer
alternatives for obtaining dental services. Thus, addressing the shortage of public dentists
is not only a matter of strengthening the public health workforce, but also of ensuring that
underserved and low-income Filipinos have meaningful access to essential oral
healthcare.

       With this measure, we hope to attract and retain qualified professionals in the
public sector, and ultimately strengthen the government’s ability to deliver essential health
services to the Filipino people.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Public Dentist Salary
Modification Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared a policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them. In
line with this policy, and in recognition of the critical role of dentists in promoting oral
health among Filipinos, and the need to encourage qualified health care professionals to
join and remain in government service, the State shall ensure that the compensation
received by public dentists remain commensurate to the demands of the service, and that
their social and economic well-being, living and working conditions, and terms of
employment are continuously promoted and improved.

SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all dentists, regardless of their status of
employment, in all public health institutions, whether nationally or locally funded, as well
as those assigned for national government agencies, instrumentalities of government,
including, government-owned and -controlled corporations (GOCCs), state universities
and colleges (SUCs), and local government units (LGUs).

SEC. 4. Adjustment of Salary Grade of Public Dentists. – Upon the effectivity of this
Act, the salary grade levels for dentists employed in all government agencies and
instrumentalities shall be modified as follows:


          Position                  Old Salary Grade              New Salary Grade

           Dentist I                        14                             17

          Dentist II                        17                             20

          Dentist III                       20                             23

          Dentist IV                        23                             25

          Dentist V                         24                             26

          Dentist VI                        26                             28

         Dentist VII                        28                             30

Provided, That public dentists employed on part-time or casual basis under the Human
Resources for Health and other similar programs and projects of the government shall
receive remuneration in accordance with the modified salary grade schedule and shall be
paid proportionate to the actual service they have rendered: Provided, further, That
government agencies and instrumentalities that are not covered under Republic Act No.
11466, otherwise known as the Salary Standardization Law of 2019, shall continue to
receive their current remuneration so long as the salaries they receive correspond to or
are higher than the salary schedule provided under this Act: Provided, finally, That the
modified salaries of public dentists employed by LGUs shall correspond to the income
classification of the LGU concerned and shall not exceed the percentage of the applicable
salary schedule as provided in Section 10(a) of Republic Act No. 11466, otherwise known
as the Salary Standardization Law of 2019.

SEC. 5. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to effectively implement the provisions
of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act, in the respective
annual appropriations ordinances of the LGUs, and in the respective annual corporate
operating budgets of the GOCCs.

SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within thirty (30) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Budget and Management shall, in coordination
with other government agencies concerned, promulgate the rules and regulations to
effectively implement the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 7. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 9. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
