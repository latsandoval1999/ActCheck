EXPLANATORY NOTE

The 1987 Constitution commands the State to give priority to science and
technology! and to pursue the industrialization and full employment of the nation.?
For decades, these mandates have been pursued in a world where the Philippines
competed largely on the strength of its workforce. That world is changing. Today, the
capacity to make semiconductors, refine critical minerals, and power artificial
intelligence determines which nations will prosper and which will be left behind.

The global technology supply chain is being redrawn, and the decisions being
made now will shape where factories, research centers, and high-paying jobs will be
located for a generation. In December 2025, the United States and its partners
launched Pax Silica to build secure and resilient supply chains for the technologies of
the AI age, recognizing that “a reliable supply chain is indispensable to our mutual
economic security.” The Philippines joined in April 2026, alongside more than twenty
other countries and the European Union.* Membership opens the door, but it does not

Investors of this magnitude do not commit on promises alone. They look for
clear rules, efficient government, and the assurance that the terms they rely on will
not change with every administration. Our communities, in turn, deserve the
assurance that growth will respect their rights and benefit their families. Only a law
passed by Congress can provide both.

This measure therefore seeks to establish the Advanced Industries Zone in New
Clark City as the country’s national platform for strategic technology, advanced
manufacturing, and innovation, under a dedicated authority empowered to attract
investment and deliver government services efficiently. In return for competitive
incentives, enterprises in the Zone must create jobs for Filipinos, train Filipino workers,
and transfer skills and know-how, so that in time Filipinos will not only assemble these
technologies but design, build, and manage them.

The measure does not trade away our sovereignty or our people’s rights for
investment. The Zone remains fully under Philippine law and jurisdiction, and no
immunity is granted to any person. Our labor laws apply in full, and the rights of
Indigenous Peoples and the communities of Capas and Tarlac are protected.

The race for the industries of the future is already under way, and the window
to secure our place in it will not stay open forever. With this measure, the Philippines
can move up the value chain, process its own resources at home, and offer its people
world-class careers without leaving the country. It is a chance to ensure that the next
chapter of the technological revolution is written with Filipino hands, for the benefit of
the Filipino people.

In view of the foregoing, the immediat : ge of this-bill is earnestly sought.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Advanced
Industries Development Act."

SEC. 2. Dec/aration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State,
pursuant to Article XIV, Sections 10 and 12, and Article XII, Sections 1 and 12, and
Article XIII, Section 3 of the Constitution, to give priority to science and technology,
to promote industrialization and full employment, to afford full protection to labor and
promote the preferential use of Filipino labor, and to strengthen the industrial,
technological, and supply chain capabilities essential to national economic security.

Toward this end, the State shall establish a national platform for strategic
technology, advanced manufacturing, scientific research, and _ innovation,
administered by a single body enjoying fiscal and administrative autonomy,
empowered to coordinate government services, provide critical infrastructure, and
attract investments of national significance, consistent with the Constitution and
existing laws.

(c) Free Zone Status. — The Zone is hereby declared a free zone within the
meaning of Republic Act No. 10863, otherwise known as the "Customs
Modernization and Tariff Act," and shall be operated and managed by the
Authority as a separate customs territory, ensuring the free flow and movement
of goods, capital, and equipment within, into, and exported out of the Zone.

(d) Entry and Treatment of Foreign Goods. — Goods brought into the Zone
from a foreign country shall not be subject to customs duties, value-added tax,
excise tax, or other national internal revenue taxes upon entry, and shall not
be deemed imported into the Philippines for so long as they remain within the
Zone. Such goods may be stored, warehoused, broken up, repacked,
assembled, sorted, graded, cleaned, mixed with foreign or domestic goods,
processed, manufactured, tested, refurbished, or otherwise manipulated within
the Zone without the payment of duties and taxes, and may be exported
directly from the Zone free of duties and taxes. The treatment under this
subsection shall apply only to goods admitted for storage, processing,
manufacturing, testing, research, or use in the operations of a Locator or
Qualified Enterprise. Motor vehicles for personal use, consumer goods,
foodstuffs, and other goods for the personal consumption of residents,
employees, or visitors of the Zone shall be deemed imported into the Philippines
upon their entry into the Zone and shall be subject to the duties and taxes
imposed under Republic Act No. 10863 and the National Internal Revenue Code
of 1997, as amended, unless admitted under a specific exemption provided by
law. No hazardous waste, including electronic waste, shall be admitted into the
Zone except as permitted under Republic Act No. 6969 and the Basel
Convention.

(e) Removal to the Customs Territory. — The removal of goods from the Zone
into the customs territory shall constitute importation, and the duties and taxes
due thereon shall accrue at the time of removal. Such duties and taxes shall be
assessed upon the foreign components of the goods removed, on the basis of

their tariff classification, valuation, and the rates of duty in force at the time of
removal: Provided, That the value added within the Zone, including the cost of
labor, utilities, services, and domestic materials incorporated therein, shall not
form part of the dutiable value.

(f) Goods and Services from the Customs Territory. — The sale and delivery of
goods and the rendition of services from the customs territory into the Zone
shall be treated as constructive exportation and shall be subject to value-added
tax at the rate of zero percent (0%). Domestic goods brought into the Zone

(g) Customs Administration and Enforcement. — The Bureau of Customs shall
retain jurisdiction over the points of entry into and exit from the Zone and over
the enforcement of customs and tariff laws with respect to goods entering or
leaving the Zone, and shall establish and maintain an office within the Zone,
for which the Board shall provide the necessary facilities. The Board shall
maintain a secure perimeter, controlled points of entry and exit, and an
electronic inventory and tracking system for all goods admitted into the Zone,
and shall furnish the Bureau of Customs with access thereto. The Board and
the Bureau of Customs shall jointly issue the rules necessary to implement this
Section within one hundred twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act.

(h) Defense and Security. — The defense and the security of the Zone's
perimeter fence shall be the responsibility of the national government in
coordination with the Authority. Military forces sent by the national government
for the purpose of defense shall not interfere in the internal affairs of the Zone
and expenditure for these military forces shall be borne by the national
government. The Authority may provide and establish internal security and
firefighting forces. Such forces shall be subject to Article 279(d) of the Labor
Code, and shall not deny entry into the Zone to labor inspectors of the
Department of Labor and Employment or to duly authorized representatives of
labor organizations in the exercise of their lawful functions, subject to
reasonable security and access protocols applicable within the Zone.

(i) Prohibition and Suppletory Application. — No goods admitted into the Zone
free of duties and taxes shall be sold, transferred, or otherwise disposed of in
the customs territory except upon payment of the duties and taxes due thereon.
Republic Act No. 10863 shall apply suppletorily to all matters relating to the
customs character of the Zone, including its provisions on seizure, forfeiture,
and penalties.

(j) Stability of Customs Character. — The customs status and treatment granted
under this Section shall not be diminished by any rule, regulation, or
administrative issuance with respect to a registered project during its
registration period, except as otherwise provided by law.

SEC. 5. The Zone to be a Decentralized Advanced Industrial Zone. — The Zone
shall be developed, as much as possible, into a decentralized, self-reliant and self-
sustaining industrial zone, with minimum government intervention. The Zone shall

CHAPTER II
THE ADVANCED INDUSTRIES DEVELOPMENT AUTHORITY

SEC. 6. Creation of the Advanced Industries Development Authority. — There
is hereby established an independent government instrumentality vested with
corporate powers, to be known as the Advanced Industries Development Authority
(AIDA), which shall enjoy fiscal, administrative, operational, and regulatory autonomy,
except as otherwise provided by the Constitution. The Authority shall be headed by a
Board of Governors, which shall exercise the powers and discharge the functions
vested in the Authority by this Act. As used in this Act, Board refers to the Board of
Governors of the Authority.

SEC. 7. Composition of the Board. — The Board shall be composed of eleven
(11) Governors, as follows:

(a) A Chairperson, appointed by the President;
(b) The Secretary of Finance, or a duly authorized Undersecretary;
(c) The Secretary of Trade and Industry, or a duly authorized Undersecretary;

(d) The Secretary of Information and Communications Technology, or a duly
authorized Undersecretary

(e) The President and Chief Executive Officer of the Bases Conversion and
Development Authority;

(f) Three (3) Independent Governors, appointed by the President from persons
possessing competence and integrity, in one or more of the following fields:

(1) Semiconductor manufacturing;
(2) Science and engineering;

(3) Technology commercialization;
(4) Finance and venture capital;

(5) International business;

(6) Infrastructure development; and
(7) Law; and

(g) Two (2) representatives from Locators within the Zone, to be appointed by
the Board; and

(h) One (1) representative of the labor sector, appointed by the President from
among the nominees of labor organizations.

===== [page 9] =====
All Governors shall be citizens of the Philippines. The Chairperson and the
Independent Governors shall be at least thirty-five (35) years of age and of good moral
character and integrity.

The Chairperson, the Independent Governors, the representatives of the
Locators, and the representative of the labor sector shall serve for a term of six (6)
years. The Chairperson first appointed shall serve the full term of six (6) years. Of the
Independent Governors and representatives of Locators first appointed, two (2)
Independent Governors and one (1) representative of Locators shall serve for three
(3) years, and the rest for six (6) years, as determined in their respective
appointments. Upon the expiration of a term, a Governor shall serve as such until a
successor shall have been appointed and qualified. A Governor appointed to fill a
vacancy shall serve only the unexpired term.

The Chairperson, the Independent Governors, the representatives of the
Locators, and the representative of the labor sector shall not be removed except for
cause, after notice and hearing, on the ground of subsequent disqualification, physical
or mental incapacity, conviction of an offense involving moral turpitude, or grave
misconduct in office.

No Governor shall participate in the deliberation or decision of any matter in
which such Governor, or any entity in which such Governor holds a material interest,
has a pecuniary interest, and such Governor shall not be counted for purposes of the
quorum thereon.

No Governor, officer, or employee of the Authority shall be held liable for any

act performed or omitted in the discharge of official duties, unless the act or omission
constitutes a willful violation of this Act or is attended by bad faith, malice, or gross

negligence.
SEC. 8. Powers and Functions. — The Authority shall have the following
powers:

(a) To administer, develop, and promote the Zone, and to formulate and issue
rules for its operation;

(b) To register Locators and certify Qualified Enterprises, regulate and
supervise the Locators and Qualified Enterprises in an_ efficient and
decentralized manner, and to suspend or revoke the certification of Qualified
Enterprises in accordance with Sections 34 to 36;

(c) To administer fiscal and non-fiscal incentives granted under this Act;

(d) To develop, operate, and maintain infrastructure and utilities within the
Zone, directly or through subsidiaries or private operators;

(e) To lease, sub-lease, and administer land and improvements within the
Zone;

(f) To exercise the right of eminent domain;

(g) To fix, impose, and collect fees, rentals, and charges for services and
facilities;

(h) To enter into contracts, incur obligations, and acquire and dispose of
property in the exercise of its corporate powers;

(i) To exercise regulatory jurisdiction over solid, liquid, and hazardous waste
management within the Zone, in accordance with Section 20;

(j) To act, through the Chairperson or a duly designated officer, as the Building
Official within the Zone, and to issue all building, occupancy, and related
permits;

(k) To be fully responsible and accountable for the enforcement of Republic Act

(1) To exercise exclusive control and jurisdiction over the issuance,

(m) To exercise such other powers as may be necessary to carry out the
purposes of this Act.

SEC. 9. Meetings, Quorum, and Voting. — The Board shall meet at least twice
each month and may hold special meetings upon the call of the Chairperson or upon
the written request of not less than four (4) Governors.

The presence of six (6) Governors shall constitute a quorum. Any action or
decision of the Board shall require the affirmative vote of at least six (6) Governors.
In case of a tie, the Chairperson shall cast the deciding vote.

SEC. 10. Chairperson. — The Chairperson shall preside over the meetings of
the Board and shall serve as the Chief Executive Officer of the Authority, exercising
executive and administrative supervision over the operations of the Zone and
implementing the policies of the Board.

The Chairperson shall be appointed by the President from among persons of
recognized competence, integrity, and experience in strategic industries, technology,
infrastructure, finance, or economic development. The term of office and the grounds
and procedure for removal of the Chairperson shall be as provided in Section 7.

The Chairperson shall sign contracts, certificates of registration, and other
instruments on behalf of the Authority, and shall represent it in all dealings with other
agencies and in judicial and administrative proceedings. The Chairperson may
delegate any of these functions to officers of the Authority, subject to such conditions
as the Board may prescribe.

SEC. 11. Fiscal and Administrative Autonomy. — In the discharge of its
responsibilities under this Act, the Authority shall enjoy fiscal and administrative
autonomy. It shall determine its own organizational structure and staffing pattern and
shall adopt its own compensation and position classification system, which shall be
exempt from Republic Act No. 6758: Provided, That the same shall not exceed the
compensation levels of the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas for positions of comparable
rank; that it shall be published and reported annually to the Congress; and that all
disbursements thereunder shall be subject to audit by the Commission on Audit.

the exchange of information on registered projects.

SEC. 13. Classification and Patrimonial Character of Lands within the Zone. —
Lands of the State within the Zone that are owned by, titled in the name of, or under
the administration of the Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) and
transferred to the Authority under Section 12 are hereby declared alienable and
disposable and classified as patrimonial property of the State, to be held and

This declaration shall not apply to lands covered by a Certificate of Ancestral

The Register of Deeds concerned shall issue the corresponding titles in the name of
the Authority.

CHAPTER III
ADMINISTRATION AND IMPLEMENTATION

SEC. 14. Lead Administrative Agency. — The Authority is hereby designated
as the lead administrative agency for all projects, activities, investments, and
government transactions within the Zone.

SEC. 15. Jntegrated Government Services Center. — The Authority shall
establish and maintain an Integrated Government Services Center (IGSC), which shall
be staffed by officers and employees of the Authority and shall serve as the single
point of intake, assistance, filing, payment, tracking, and release for all government
transactions relating to activities within the Zone.

The IGSC shall receive applications from Locators and Qualified Enterprises,

Filing with the IGSC shall constitute filing with the agency or local government

CHAPTER IV
INFRASTRUCTURE, UTILITIES, AND LAND

SEC. 17. Franchise for Utility Services Within the Zone. — The Authority is
hereby granted a franchise, for a period of twenty-five (25) years from the effectivity
of this Act, renewable for another twenty-five (25) years, to construct, operate, and
maintain systems for the distribution of electricity and the supply and distribution of
water within the Zone. The Authority may exercise the franchise directly, through a
subsidiary, or through a private operator selected through a competitive process. Any
private operator exercising the franchise shall comply with the nationality and other
requirements applicable to public utilities under Section 11, Article XII of the
Constitution.

Electricity services shall comply with Republic Act No. 9136, the Philippine
Distribution Code, and applicable Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) standards.

ia

===== [page 14] =====
Notwithstanding the threshold requirements under Republic Act No. 9136 and
applicable ERC rules, all end-users within the Zone shall be deemed contestable
customers.

Water services shall comply with Presidential Decree Nos. 1067 and 1206 and
applicable National Water Resources Board (NWRB) standards.

The franchise shall constitute the Certificate of Public Convenience and
Necessity or Certificate of Public Convenience required by law for electricity and
waterworks systems within the Zone. Each system shall be registered with the ERC or
NWRB, as applicable, within sixty (60) days from commencement of operations. The
ERC and NWRB shall retain their respective regulatory authority to monitor compliance
and impose penalties under applicable law.

Notwithstanding Republic Act No. 9136, the Authority shall fix rates for the
distribution of electricity and the supply of water within the Zone, applying the rate-
setting methodologies of the ERC and the NWRB as minimum standards. Where the
Authority exercises the franchise directly, such rates shall be subject to review by the
ERC or the NWRB, as the case may be, upon complaint of an end-user.

SEC. 18. Certificates of Authority. — The Authority may issue Certificates of
Authority for the construction and operation of transmission-voltage lines and water
facilities wholly within the Zone upon finding that the applicant is legally, technically,
and financially qualified and that the proposed facility is necessary or beneficial to the
efficient operation of the Zone: Provided, That no certificate shall be issued except to
a citizen of the Philippines or to a corporation or association organized under Philippine
law at least sixty percent (60%) of whose capital is owned by such citizens; and that
no certificate shall be exclusive or exceed fifty (50) years.

A facility shall be deemed to form part of the electric power system within the
Zone, notwithstanding its voltage level, where the facility: (1) is located wholly within
the Zone, except for a dedicated connection segment necessary to reach an approved
point of interconnection; (2) primarily serves loads, generation, or storage facilities
within the Zone; and (3) is not used to convey electric power to customers outside
the Zone. No Certificate of Authority issued under this Section shall authorize the
operation or control of any facility forming part of the national transmission system
governed by Section 19.

SEC. 19. Wational Transmission Grid. — Nothing in this Act shall affect the
national transmission grid, which shall continue to be governed by Republic Act No.
9136, Republic Act No. 9511, the concession agreement entered into pursuant to
Section 21 of Republic Act No. 9136, and other applicable laws and regulations. The
cost of interconnection facilities between the Zone and the grid may be funded by the
National Government, subject to appropriation by the Congress.

The Authority may defray, out of its retained income, the transmission charges
payable by Locators: Provided, That the grid operator shall be paid its full approved
rate, and that no such payment shall constitute a cross-subsidy under Republic Act
No. 9136.

SEC. 20. Environment and Waste Management. — The Authority shall
undertake and regulate the establishment, operation, and maintenance of an

integrated waste management system within the Zone, including the collection,
segregation, storage, transport, recovery, treatment, and disposal of waste.

The Authority may construct, acquire, own, lease, operate, and maintain waste

private partnership or other lawful arrangement. It may regulate service providers and
impose reasonable fees and charges.

The Authority may adopt and enforce supplemental waste management,
pollution control, and environmental standards within the Zone, which shall not be
inconsistent with applicable national standards.

SEC. 21. Land Tenure. — Patrimonial land under Section 13 may be
leased to any Locator for a period not exceeding ninety-nine (99) years:
Provided, That land within the Zone remaining part of the public domain may

Within any portion of the Zone covered by a Master Development Lease,
occupancy shall be by sublease from the Master Developer and Estate Operator.
Elsewhere, leases shall be entered into directly with the Authority.

No lease or sublease shall be assigned or further subleased without the prior
written approval of the Authority.

For this purpose, the Authority may grant a Master Development Lease

management, and commercial rights as may be necessary to implement the
Master Development Plan. The Authority may likewise enter into development,
usufruct, management, operations and maintenance, or other commercial
arrangements, separately or in combination with such lease.

(b) Se/ection. — The Master Developer and Estate Operator, which shall have

The consideration for the lease may consist of fixed or variable rent,

(e) Infrastructure and Alternative Estate Management. — The Board shall

SEC. 23. Registration of Locators; Certificate of Endorsement. — Any person

intending to occupy, operate, or conduct business within the Zone shall register with
the Board. Registration shall entitle the Locator to the administrative services and one-
stop processing established under this Act.

Upon registration, the Board shall issue a Certificate of Endorsement stating

the proposed project, the investment capital committed, and the project location. The
Certificate shall issue in the name of the entity undertaking the project, and all permits

SEC. 24. Qualified Enterprises. — A Locator may apply for certification as a
Qualified Enterprise prior to the commencement of construction or operations. The
Board may certify a registered Locator as a Qualified Enterprise upon determining that
the project or activity in which it is engaged, or proposes to engage in, falls within any
of the following:

(a) The design, fabrication, assembly, testing, or advanced packaging of
semiconductors and integrated circuits;

(b) Advanced manufacturing employing automation, robotics, precision
engineering, or advanced materials;

(c) The establishment and operation of data centers and computing
infrastructure for artificial intelligence and high-performance computing;

(d) The processing of critical minerals and the production of specialty materials,
gases, and equipment used in the activities enumerated in this Section;

(e) Scientific research, development, and technology commercialization in
support of the foregoing;

(f) The development, construction, and operation of energy facilities and
systems located within the Zone, including the generation, storage, conversion,
and distribution of electricity, and the production, storage, and supply of fuels
and other energy carriers, from any conventional, renewable, or emerging
energy source or technology;

(g) Such other activities as the Board may determine to be directly related to,
in support of, or necessary for the establishment and operation of the activities
mentioned in paragraphs (a) to (e), including supplier, logistics, and technical
service activities forming part of their supply chain: Provided, That such
activities shall be performed exclusively for Qualified Enterprises within the
Zone.

Any determination under paragraph (g) shall be made by resolution stating the
basis therefor, shall be published in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation, and shall be transmitted to the Congress within fifteen (15) days from
publication.

The Board shall act on a complete application within thirty (30) working days
and shall, upon approval, issue a Certificate of Registration stating the registered
project, the activity under which it qualifies, and the conditions of availment.

SEC. 25. Standards. — The Board shall exercise its authority under Section 24
and shall make a determination under Section 24(g) only upon a written finding, stated
in its resolution, that the activity:

(a) Applies, produces, or materially depends upon advanced technological,
scientific, or engineering capability that is not widely available in the Philippines;

(b) Is necessary to the establishment, operation, or continuity of one or more
activities enumerated in Section 24(a) to (e), or forms part of their supply chain;

18

===== [page 19] =====
(c) Generates employment requiring technical, scientific, or professional
qualifications, and provides for the training of Filipino personnel therein;

(d) Involves substantial capital investment; and

(e) Provides for the transfer of skills and technical know-how to Filipino
personnel under a Technology Transfer and Training Plan forming part of its
Investment Agreement, without prejudice to intellectual property rights, and
results in the transfer of technology, the conduct of research and development
in the Philippines, or the development of Filipino suppliers, subject in all cases
to Republic Act No. 10697 and other export control laws applicable to the
technology concerned, and to the protection of sensitive technologies.

SEC. 26. Minimum Investment. — No Locator shall be certified as a Qualified
Enterprise unless its registered project involves an investment capital of at least
Fifteen Billion Pesos (P15,000,000,000.00), exclusive of the value of the land.

The Authority shall, every five (5) years, adjust the foregoing amount to reflect
the cumulative change in the Consumer Price Index as published by the Philippine
Statistics Authority. No adjustment shall reduce the amount below that first prescribed,
and no adjustment shall affect a project already registered.

Investment capital shall be reckoned as the aggregate amount committed
under the Investment Agreement and shall be deployed within the period stipulated
therein.

An expansion of the registered project of a Qualified Enterprise involving an
additional investment capital of at least Five Billion Pesos (?5,000,000,000.00),
exclusive of the value of the land, may be registered as a separate registered project,
which shall be entitled only to the incentives under Section 27(c), (d), and (e).

SEC. 27. Incentives. — Notwithstanding Title XIII of the National Internal
Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, and any other law granting or regulating fiscal
incentives, the fiscal incentives of Qualified Enterprises within the Zone shall be
governed exclusively by this Act: Provided, That Qualified Enterprises shall be entitled
to all deductions, exclusions, and credits allowed to taxpayers generally under the said
Code. A Qualified Enterprise shall be entitled to the following incentives:

(a) Income Tax Holiday (ITH). — A Qualified Enterprise shall be entitled to an
ITH of seven (7) years from the start of commercial operations of the registered
project, but not later than three (3) years from the date of its Certificate of
Registration;

(b) Election. — In lieu of, or upon expiration of, the income tax holiday, a
Qualified Enterprise may avail itself, at its option, of either the Special Corporate
Income Tax (SCIT) under subsection (c) or the Enhanced Deductions Regime
(EDR) under subsection (d), for a period not exceeding twenty (20) years:
Provided, That the aggregate period of availment under subsections (a), (c),
and (d) shall in no case exceed twenty (20) years from the date of the
Certificate of Registration of the registered project. The election shall be
exercised in the manner prescribed by the Authority and, once exercised, shall
be irrevocable for the registered project concerned;

19

===== [page 20] =====
(c) Special Corporate Income Tax. — A Qualified Enterprise shall pay a tax
equivalent to five percent (5%) of its gross income earned from the registered
project, in lieu of all national and local taxes, fees, and charges, except real
property tax on land: Provided, That customs duties and the value-added tax
on importation shall be governed by Section 4 and by subsection (e) of this
Section.

For this purpose, gross income earned refers to gross sales or receipts
derived from the registered project, less sales discounts, sales returns and
allowances, and cost of sales or direct costs, but before any deduction for
administrative, marketing, selling, operating, or financing expenses;

(d) Enhanced Deductions Regime (EDR). — A Qualified Enterprise shall be
subject to an income tax rate of twenty percent (20%) on its taxable income
from the registered project, and shall be entitled to deduct from gross income,
in addition to the deductions otherwise allowed under the Code:

(1) An additional depreciation allowance of ten percent (10%) for
buildings and twenty percent (20%) for machinery and equipment;

(2) Fifty percent (50%) additional deduction on the direct labor expense
incurred in the taxable year for Filipino personnel directly engaged in the
registered project;

(3) One hundred percent (100%) additional deduction on research and
development expense incurred in the taxable year;

(4) One hundred percent (100%) additional deduction on training
expense incurred in the taxable year for Filipino personnel;

(5) Fifty percent (50%) additional deduction on domestic input expense
incurred in the taxable year;

(6) One hundred percent (100%) additional deduction on power
expense incurred in the taxable year;

(7) Enhanced Net Operating Loss Carry-Over (NOLCO). — The net
operating loss of the registered project during the first three (3) years
from the start of commercial operation, which had not been previously
offset as a deduction from gross income, may be carried over as a
deduction from gross income within the next five (5) consecutive taxable
years immediately following the last year of the ITH entitlement period,
or, where no income tax holiday was availed of, immediately following
the third year from the start of commercial operations; and

(8) Reinvestment Allowance. — A Qualified Enterprise that reinvests its
undistributed profits or surplus in the expansion of its registered project
within five (5) years shall be entitled to a reinvestment allowance of up
to fifty percent (50%) of the amount reinvested, in accordance with the
rules applicable to the reinvestment allowance under Republic Act No.
12066;

(e) Value-Added Tax and Customs Duty. — In addition to, and without prejudice
to, the customs character of the Zone under Section 4 of this Act, a Qualified

===== [page 21] =====
Enterprise shall, from the date of its Certificate of Registration and for the
duration of its incentive period, be entitled to:

(1) Exemption from Value-Added Tax (VAT) and customs duties on the
importation of capital equipment, raw materials, supplies, spare parts,
accessories, and other goods directly attributable to its registered
project;

(2) VAT at the rate of zero percent (0%) on local purchases of goods
and services directly attributable to its registered project, regardless of
the location of the seller; and

(3) Exemption from VAT and customs duties on goods transferred to

(h) Limitations. — Incentives shall be granted on a per-project basis. No

Commission on Higher Education, the Department of Education, and the local
government units concerned, shall formulate a Zone Workforce Development Plan
identifying the skills required by Qualified Enterprises and the programs to supply
them, consistent with the Trabaho Para sa Bayan Plan under Republic Act No. 11962.

(b) Other qualifications being equal, Locators shall give preference in hiring,
under criteria published in accordance with the rules of the Department of
Labor and Employment, to: (1) residents-of the Municipality of Capas and the
Province of Tarlac; (2) members of Indigenous Cultural Communities whose
ancestral domains or ancestral lands are within or adjacent to the Zone; and
(3) persons displaced by the development of the Zone, wherever they reside.

CHAPTER VI
CORRECTIVE MEASURES, SUSPENSION, AND REVOCATION

SEC. 33. Corrective Measures. — Where the Board determines that a Qualified
Enterprise has failed to comply with this Act or the conditions of its certification, it may
require the enterprise to undertake appropriate corrective measures within a
reasonable period before the imposition of any sanction, unless immediate action is
necessary to protect national security, public safety, public order, or other compelling
public interests.

SEC. 34. Suspension of Certification. — The Board may suspend the
certification of a Qualified Enterprise, after notice and opportunity to be heard, where
the Qualified Enterprise:

(a) Has materially failed to comply with the provisions of this Act or the
conditions of its certification;

(b) Has failed to implement the corrective measures required by the Board
within the prescribed period; or

24

===== [page 25] =====
(c) Has failed to maintain the qualifications required under this Act, where such
deficiency is capable of being remedied.

Notwithstanding Section 33, the Board shall, after notice and opportunity to be
heard, suspend the certification of a Qualified Enterprise found liable, in a final and
executory order or decision of the Department of Labor and Employment, the National
Labor Relations Commission, or a court, for child labor, forced labor, trafficking in
persons, unfair labor practice under Article 259(a) to (e) of the Labor Code, or a
violation of occupational safety and health standards resulting in death or serious
injury. A second such finding against the same Qualified Enterprise, for a distinct
violation, within five (5) years from the date the first finding became final and
executory, shall constitute repeated violations under Section 35(c). Violations by its
contractors and subcontractors shall be dealt with under Sections 30(f) and 33.

The order of suspension shall specify the grounds therefor, the period of
suspension, and the conditions for the restoration of the enterprise's certification.

SEC. 35. Revocation of Certification. — The Board may revoke the certification
of a Qualified Enterprise, after notice and an opportunity to be heard, upon a finding
that the enterprise:

(a) Obtained certification through fraud, material misrepresentation, or
concealment of material facts;

(b) No longer satisfies the qualifications required under this Act and such
deficiency is no longer capable of being remedied;

(c) Has committed repeated or willful violations of this Act or the conditions of
its certification;

(d) Has abandoned or permanently ceased the registered project for which
certification was granted without justifiable cause;

(e) Has failed to deploy the committed investment capital within the period
stipulated in its Investment Agreement; or

(f) Has engaged in activities that materially threaten national security, public
safety, or public order.

SEC. 36. Effects of Suspension or Revocation. — During the period of
suspension, a Qualified Enterprise may be denied access to such incentives, privileges,
or administrative services as the Board may determine to be appropriate under the
circumstances. The order of suspension shall specify the incentives, privileges, or
services affected and the effective date thereof. The Authority shall promptly furnish
the Bureau of Internal Revenue, the Bureau of Customs, and other concerned agencies
a copy of the final order.

Upon revocation of certification, the enterprise shall cease to be entitled to the
incentives, privileges, and benefits granted under this Act, without prejudice to any
liabilities or obligations incurred prior to such revocation.

The suspension or revocation of certification shall not relieve the enterprise
from compliance with applicable laws or from any civil, criminal, or administrative
liability arising from its acts or omissions.

CHAPTER VII
FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 37. Arbitration of Investment Disputes. — Any dispute between the
Authority and a Locator or a Qualified Enterprise arising from or in connection with an
Investment Agreement shall be resolved by arbitration conducted in accordance with
Republic Act No. 9285 and the arbitration rules agreed upon by the parties. An
Investment Agreement may provide for a seat of arbitration outside the Philippines
and for administration by a recognized international arbitral institution.

The arbitral award shall be final and binding upon the parties and shall be
recognized and enforced by the courts of the Philippines in accordance with Republic
Act No. 9285 and the Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign
Arbitral Awards. The agreement of the Authority to arbitrate shall constitute an
express waiver of its immunity from suit in respect of such disputes, without prejudice
to its immunity from execution over property devoted to a public purpose: Provided,
That no regulatory, licensing, permitting, tax, or labor matter, including the
enforcement under Chapter VI of the employment and training commitments in an
Investment Agreement, and no matter within the jurisdiction of the Energy Regulatory
Commission, the Court of Tax Appeals, the Department of Labor and Employment, or
the National Labor Relations Commission, shall be the subject of arbitration: Provided,
further, That any award shall be satisfied only out of the retained income of the
Authority and in accordance with law.

SEC. 38. Funding. — The amount of One Billion Pesos (#1,000,000,000.00) a
year for five (5) years, or a total of Five Billion Pesos (?5,000,000,000.00), is hereby
appropriated out of any funds in the National Treasury not otherwise appropriated, as
the cash contribution of the National Government to the capital of the Authority under
Section 11 and for the initial implementation of this Act. Thereafter, such sums as may
be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.

The Authority may likewise receive grants, donations, endowments,
contributions, and other lawful sources of funding from domestic or foreign sources,
subject to existing laws.

SEC. 39. Annual Report; Congressional Review. — The Authority shall submit
to the President and to the Congress, not later than the thirty-first of March of each
year, a report on its operations, finances, registrations, incentives granted, and
performance, including the employment generated in the Zone by sex, nationality,
employment status, and position level; the foreign nationals employed and the
positions they hold; and the findings of violations of labor and occupational safety and
health laws against Locators and their contractors. The Congress shall undertake a
comprehensive review of this Act every five (5) years from its effectivity.

SEC. 40. 7ransitory Provisions. — Enterprises registered with the Bases
Conversion and Development Authority, the Philippine Economic Zone Authority, the
Board of Investments, or any other Investment Promotion Agency and operating
within the Zone at the effectivity of this Act shall continue to enjoy the incentives
granted under their existing registration for the remaining period thereof, and may
register as Locators under Section 23. Any such enterprise certified as a Qualified

===== [page 27] =====
Enterprise shall avail itself of the incentives under this Act only with respect to a new
registered project, subject to Section 27(h).

The Board shall be constituted within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of
this Act. Pending its organization, the Bases Conversion and Development Authority
shall continue to administer the Zone under its existing authority.

SEC. 41. Jmplementing Rules and Regulations. — The Department of Finance
and the Department of Trade and Industry shall formulate the implementing rules and
regulations of this Act within sixty (60) days after its approval. Such rules and
regulations shall take effect fifteen (15) days after their publication in a newspaper of
general circulation in the Philippines: Provided, That upon its organization the
Authority may amend or supplement the same pursuant to Section 8(a): Provided,
further, That failure to formulate the rules shall not prevent the implementation of this
Act.

SEC. 42. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

SEC. 43. Repealing and Amending Clause. — Republic Act No. 7227, as
amended, and all proclamations, executive orders, and other issuances that place the
lands within the Zone under the ownership, administration, or jurisdiction of the Bases
Conversion and Development Authority, or within the Clark Special Economic Zone or
the Clark Freeport Zone, are hereby amended accordingly, and such lands are hereby
excluded from their coverage. The transfer of these lands to the Authority under
Section 12 shall be without compensation, and shall not be subject to the provisions
of Republic Act No. 7227, as amended, on the disposition of the assets of the Bases
Conversion and Development Authority and the distribution of the proceeds thereof.
All other laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations inconsistent with this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 44. Fffectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
