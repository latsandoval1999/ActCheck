                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to provide timely and forward-looking information on
vegetable production, supply, demand, and market conditions. It aims to strengthen
production planning and enable the government and agricultural stakeholders to
identify emerging imbalances and respond before these translate into significant
losses for farmers and disruptions in the market.

       In April 2026, cabbage farmers in Atok, Benguet reportedly received as little
as Four pesos (Php 4.00) to Eight pesos (Php 8.00) per kilogram, while some opted not
to harvest as prices fell below production and harvesting costs1. At the La Trinidad
Vegetable Trading Post, adequate supply coincided with weaker demand, fewer
buyers, and high transport costs, leaving some produce unsold2. Farmers and
traders subsequently sought temporary floor prices for selected vegetables after
cabbage prices fell to about Five pesos (Php 5.00) per kilogram against production
costs of approximately Twenty pesos (Php 20.00) per kilogram3. These show the need
for reliable information on what farmers intend to plant, where production will
occur, and when harvests are expected, so that potential supply and market
imbalances can be identified earlier.

       The proposed measure institutionalizes the National Vegetable Planting
Intentions Survey under the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), which shall
regularly collect information on intended crops, planting areas, locations,
production schedules, expected output, and intended markets. The PSA shall

indicators, weather and climate information, and geospatial data. Digital reporting
platforms, Geographic Information Systems, remote sensing, and statistical and

       For vegetable farmers, timely information can mean the difference between a
profitable harvest and a crop that is left to rot. When vegetables are sold below
production cost or abandoned in the fields, farmers lose not only their expected
income but also the capital, labor, and months of work invested in every planting
season. These losses also affect consumers, traders, transporters, and the broader
agricultural economy.

       The State should not wait for farmgate prices to collapse, trading posts to be
filled with unsold produce, or crops to spoil before responding. Farmers deserve a
government that can see emerging risks, act ahead of market disruptions, and use
public resources where they can have the greatest impact.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING  A   NATIONAL CONTINUOUS VEGETABLE
PLANTING INTENTIONS SURVEY AND MODERNIZING AGRICULTURAL
STATISTICAL FORECASTING THROUGH REAL-TIME DATA AND
GEOSPATIAL TECHNOLOGIES, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND
FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Vegetable
Planting Intentions and Agricultural Forecasting Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote food security, stable agricultural markets, and sustainable farmer incomes
through timely, accurate, and forward-looking agricultural information. Towards
this end, the State shall develop and maintain an agricultural statistical system that
captures farmers’ planting intentions and other forward-looking indicators, in
addition to historical production data, to support evidence-based production
planning, market coordination, logistics management, and timely government
intervention.

       SEC. 3. National Vegetable Planting Intentions Survey. – The Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA) shall institutionalize and regularly conduct a National Vegetable
Planting Intentions Survey (NVPIS) as part of the Philippine Statistical System. The
NVPIS shall generate timely estimates on farmers’ planting intentions and related
production indicators for vegetables, including, as appropriate:

          a. Type and variety of vegetable to be planted;

          b. Area intended for planting;

          c. Location of the farm or production area;

          d. Expected planting and harvesting periods;


                                           1
          e.​ Expected volume of production;

           f.​ Intended use or destination of production, including local markets,
       institutional buyers, processors, and other channels; and

          g.​ Such other indicators or variables necessary for agricultural supply
       and market forecasting.

        The PSA shall determine the appropriate statistical methodology, including
the statistical design, sample size, sampling technique, sampling frame, frequency of
the survey, geographic coverage, data gathering procedure, survey instruments, and
statistical treatment, taking into account regional production patterns and the
production cycles of different vegetables.

       SEC. 4. Agricultural Data Integration and Forecasting System. – The PSA shall, in
coordination with the Department of Agriculture (DA), establish and maintain an
integrated agricultural data and forecasting system that consolidates, processes, and
analyzes available survey, administrative, market, weather, production, and other
relevant agricultural datasets.

       The System shall make use, where appropriate, of digital data collection and
reporting platforms, Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and other geospatial
technologies, remote sensing and earth observation data, weather and climate
information, historical production and price records, market and demand
information, and statistical, econometric, and other predictive modeling techniques.

      These technologies and datasets shall be integrated to generate timely and
geographically disaggregated estimates and forecasts of agricultural production,
supply, demand, and market conditions, which shall serve as statistical inputs for
production planning, market coordination, logistics management, and the
formulation of appropriate agricultural interventions by national government
agencies, local government units, farmers’ organizations, cooperatives, and other
stakeholders.

       The System shall also provide for the periodic assessment of planting
intentions against actual planting, harvested area, production, and relevant market
outcomes to improve the accuracy of agricultural forecasts and statistical estimates.

       SEC. 5. Inter-Agency and Multi-Stakeholder Coordination. – The PSA shall lead
the implementation of this Act, in coordination with the DA and other concerned
government agencies. The PSA and DA shall establish mechanisms for collaboration
with local government units, state universities and colleges, research institutions,
farmers’ organizations, cooperatives, industry groups, market intermediaries, and
civil society organizations, private sector, and other institutions with relevant
agricultural, statistical, research, or development expertise.

       Local government units and participating organizations shall, when

requested by the PSA and consistent with their respective mandates, assist in the
collection, validation, and timely submission of agricultural information in
accordance with the statistical standards, methodologies, and protocols prescribed
by the PSA.

       SEC. 6. Data Dissemination and Early Warning. – The PSA shall, in coordination
with the DA, periodically disseminate aggregated planting intentions, supply
forecasts, and other relevant agricultural indicators through accessible digital
platforms. Where the data indicate a significant projected imbalance between
agricultural supply and market demand, the PSA shall promptly transmit the
relevant statistical information and forecasts to the DA and other concerned agencies
to support appropriate interventions, including but not limited to production
advisories, market linkage, logistics coordination, processing, storage, and other
lawful measures.

       No planting-intention estimate or agricultural forecast generated under this
Act shall be construed as a directive requiring farmers to plant or refrain from
planting any particular crop.

        SEC. 7. Data Governance and Protection. – All personal information and
confidential farm-level or commercially sensitive information collected or processed
under this Act shall be protected and handled in accordance with Republic Act (RA)
No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”, RA No. 10625,
otherwise known as the “Philippine Statistical Act of 2013”, and other applicable laws,
rules, and regulations.

       Public dissemination shall primarily use aggregated, anonymized, or
otherwise appropriately protected data that cannot identify individual farmers,
farms, or respondents, except when disclosure is authorized by law.

        SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the PSA, in coordination with the DA and other concerned
government agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations
necessary for its effective implementation.

      The implementing rules shall provide for the survey methodology, data
standards, reporting protocols, GIS and technology standards, inter-agency data
sharing, validation procedures, dissemination mechanisms, and other matters
necessary to implement this Act.

       SEC. 9. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the PSA and DA,
subject to existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of
