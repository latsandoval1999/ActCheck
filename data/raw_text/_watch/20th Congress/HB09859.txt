                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Article XIV, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution provides that, “[t]he State shall
protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and shall
take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all.” Article 28 of the
United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) provides that, “States
Parties recognize the right of the child to education, and with a view to achieving this
right progressively and on the basis of equal opportunity.” These emphasize not only
the right to quality education but also the necessity of actively making it accessible on
an equal basis to all.

        In today’s digital age, equal access to quality education is inextricably linked to
digital connectivity or access to internet services. This reality was affirmed and acutely
felt during the COVID-19 pandemic when schools had to shift their teaching modality
to online arrangement, permanently expanding learning space even in the present
post-pandemic setting. Even as most schools and classes in the primary, secondary,
and tertiary education levels have returned to the face-to-face class modality, the
digital tools, resources, and platforms used during the pandemic have remained
integral to the delivery of education. However, the increasingly significant role of digital
accessibility in education and learning has created a new barrier to access to quality
education in the country.

        In the Philippines, the deepening digital divide exacerbates deep-rooted
injustices in the education sector that leave students from underprivileged families and
communities behind as they struggle to keep up with various learning expenses not
covered by existing public education, scholarship, and learning subsidy programs.

Among these learning expenses is the access to mobile and internet services that
allow students to make use of online tools, resources, and platforms to enhance their
learning experience and school performance.

       This bill seeks to provide a 20% discount on mobile load, text, call, and internet
services to all Filipino students currently enrolled in a duly authorized elementary,
secondary, technical-vocational, or higher education institution, excluding post-
graduate degree courses. This bill seeks to bridge the digital accessibility gap and
address the emerging hindrance to equally accessible, quality education by easing the
financial burden on Filipino students for whom internet access is now a basic
educational necessity.

      In the 20th Congress, similar bills were filed by Representatives Walfredo
“Arman” Dimaguila, Jesus “Bong” Suntay, Antolin Oreta III, and Javier Benitez, and
Senators Bam Aquino, JV Ejercito, and Mark Villar. This bill fully adopts Senator Bam
Aquino’s Senate Bill No. 126.

       Early approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

 1         SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Student Discount
 2   Para Sa Load Act.”
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall give priority to education,
 5   science and technology, arts, culture, and sports to foster patriotism and nationalism,
 6   accelerate social progress, and promote total human liberation and development. It
 7   also recognizes the vital role of communication and information in nation-building.
 8
 9          Pursuant to these policies, the State ensures equitable access to education by
10   providing necessary support to students, particularly in their communication and online
11   learning needs. To achieve this, the State shall:
12
13           (a) Establish a mechanism that ensures the mandatory grant of a student load
14   discount privilege;
15           (b) Enlist the cooperation of telecommunication companies (TELCOs) in
16   extending assistance to students through this privilege; and
17           (c) Encourage students, particularly the poor and underprivileged, to utilize
18   digital tools for education.
19
20         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
21




                                               1
22          (a) Student refers to any Filipino citizen currently enrolled in a duly authorized
23   elementary, secondary, technical-vocational, or higher education institution, excluding
24   post-graduate degree courses;
25
26         (b) Telecommunication Companies (TELCOs) refer to companies providing
27   mobile, text, call, and internet services in the Philippines;
28
29          (c) Retailers refer to individuals or business establishments, whether physical
30   or online, duly authorized to sell or distribute prepaid mobile load, mobile data, call
31   and text services, and other internet services to consumers on behalf of TELCOs;
32
33         (d) Student Load Discount refers to a twenty percent (20%) discount on prepaid
34   and postpaid mobile load, text, call, and internet services availed by eligible students.
35
36          SEC. 4. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all telecommunication services,
37   including but not limited to:
38
39          (a) Prepaid mobile load for text, call, and data;
40          (b) Postpaid mobile plans; and
41          (c) Broadband and internet services used for educational purposes.
42
43          SEC. 5. Student Load Discount Privilege. – A student shall be entitled to a
44   twenty (20%) discount on mobile load, text, call, and internet services, subject to the
45   following conditions:
46
47          (a) The discount shall be applied upon presentation of a valid school ID or proof
48   of enrollment;
49          (b) The discount shall be available year-round, including weekends and
50   holidays; and
51          (c) The discount shall apply only to personal educational use and may not be
52   transferred.
53
54          SEC. 6. Prohibition on Double Discounts. – Students shall not be allowed to
55   avail of multiple discounts at the same time. If a promotional discount from a TELCO
56   is higher than twenty (20%), the student may choose which discount to apply.
57
58          SEC. 7. Tax Deduction for TELCOs. – TELCOs may claim tax deductions
59   equivalent to the cost of the discount granted, subject to the rules to be promulgated
60   by the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR).
61
62         SEC. 8. Implementation and Monitoring. – The National Telecommunications
63   Commission (NTC) and the Department of Information and Communications
64   Technology (DICT) shall oversee the implementation of this Act. TELCOs shall submit
65   quarterly reports on their compliance with this Act.

 67          SEC. 9. Penalties. – The following penalties shall be imposed for violation of
 68   this Act:
 69
 70            (a) TELCOs that refuse to grant the discount provided under this Act shall be
 71   fined:
 72               1. Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000) for the first offense;
 73               2. One Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000) for the second offense; and
 74               3. Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱200,000) for the third and subsequent
 75                  offenses.
 76
 77          (b) Retailers who, without justifiable reason, fail or refuse to provide the student
 78   load discount, or who impose conditions contrary to the intent of this Act, shall be
 79   subject to the following penalties:
 80
 81               1. Twenty-Five Thousand Pesos (₱25,000) for the first offense;
 82               2. Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000) for the second offense; and
 83               3. One Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000) and possible disqualification
 84                  or blacklisting from being an accredited load or service retailer for the
 85                  third and subsequent offenses.
 86
 87   Provided, That the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination with the
 88   National Telecommunications Commission (NTC), shall maintain a list of erring
 89   retailers and may issue orders of disqualification for habitual non-compliance.
 90   “Habitual non-compliance” shall be defined in the implementing rules and regulations
 91   with appropriate parameters that are aligned with the intent of this Act.
 92
 93          (c) Any student found using fraudulent documents to avail of the discount shall
 94   be disqualified from the privilege without prejudice to the filing of appropriate criminal
 95   charges for such act.
 96
 97          SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
 98   the effectivity of this Act, the NTC, DICT, DTI, Department of Education (DepEd), and
 99   the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall promulgate the rules and
100   regulations to effectively implement the provisions of this Act.
101
102          SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any section or provision of this Act is declared
103   invalid or unconstitutional, the sections or provisions not affected thereby shall
104   continue to be in full force and effect.
105
106         SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive and
107   administrative orders, letters of instruction, rules and regulations, or parts thereof
108   inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or amended
109   accordingly.
