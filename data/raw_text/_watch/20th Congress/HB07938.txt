                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Many children under State care grow up abandoned, neglected, or voluntarily
committed, and while their basic needs are provided for during childhood, they often
leave care at adulthood with no inancial support to help them start independent lives.
This sudden transition exposes them to poverty, exploitation, and instability, despite
years of public effort to protect and nurture them.

This bill establishes a national program and special trust fund that provides modest,
regular government contributions for each covered child, to be held in trust and
accessed upon reaching eighteen (18) years of age. The measure promotes
transparency and accountability through a national registry, strict fund management,
and audit safeguards, while ensuring that family reuni ication, foster care, and
adoption remain the preferred permanent solutions.

By equipping vulnerable youth with a small but meaningful inancial foundation, the
State ful ills its duty to protect children and act in their best interest, helping them
transition with dignity, opportunity, and hope for a better future.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                              AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL PROGRAM AND SPECIAL TRUST FUND FOR THE
BENEFIT OF ABANDONED, NEGLECTED, AND VOLUNTARILY COMMITTED
CHILDREN UNDER STATE CARE, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Trust Fund for the
Abandoned, Neglected, or Voluntarily Committed Children.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to ensure special
protection and assistance to children who are abandoned, neglected, or voluntarily
committed, including those under the care of the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD) and licensed/accredited child-caring institutions, consistent
with the best interest of the child and the State’s role as parens patriae.

The State shall institutionalize a national program to (a) identify and monitor
covered children, and (b) build a modest inancial resource for their transition to
independent living upon reaching majority age, without undermining family-
based care, foster care, and adoption as preferred permanent solutions.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
de ined:

   (a) “Child” refers to a person below eighteen (18) years of age;

                                                                           2|Page
   (b) “Abandoned Child” refers to a child who has no proper parental care or
       guardianship, or whose parents have deserted him/her for a period of at least
       three (3) continuous months, and has been declared abandoned with
       applicable laws, rules and issuances;

   (c) “Neglected Child” refers to a child whose basic needs (including food, shelter,
       clothing, education, health care, and emotional support) have been
       deliberately unattended or inadequately attended, resulting in harm or risk of
       harm, as determined by DSWD through social case study and applicable
       standards.

   (d) “Voluntarily Committed Child” refers to one whose parents or legal guardian
       knowingly and willingly relinquished parental authority to the Department of
       Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) or to any duly accredited child-
       placement or child-caring agency, in accordance with law;

   (e) “Child-Caring Agency/Institution” refers to a duly licensed and accredited
       agency by the DSWD that provides twenty-four (24)-hour residential care
       services for the abandoned, orphaned, neglected, or voluntarily committed
       children.

   (f) “Covered child” refers to an abandoned, neglected, or voluntarily committed
       child who is (i) under the care/custody of the DSWD or a DSWD-licensed and
       accredited child-caring agency/institution, and (ii) included in the of icial
       registry established under this Act.

   (g) “Trust entity” refers to a government inancial institution or a regulated
        inancial institution authorized to perform trust and iduciary functions under
       Philippine law, duly selected by the DSWD pursuant to the standards and
       procurements/selection rules provided in this Act and its IRR;

   (h) “Trust Fund Account” refers to the individual trust/ iduciary account created
       for each covered child under this Act, administered by the trust entity under a
       trust agreement approved by the DSWD.

SECTION 4. Coverage. — This Act shall cover all covered children as de ined in
Section 3(f), including those placed in DSWD-licensed and accredited child-caring
agencies/institutions, subject to veri ication and inclusion in the registry under
Section 5.

SECTION 5. National Registry and Monitoring System. — The DSWD, shall
establish and maintain a National Registry and Monitoring System for covered
children, in coordination with relevant agencies and local government units (LGUs),
for the purpose of:

   (a) maintaining updated, veri ied data on the number, location, and status of
       covered children;

   (b) pro iling and monitoring           licensed    and    accredited     child-caring
       agencies/institutions; and

   (c) providing an accountable mechanism for the establishment, funding,
       management, and release of bene its under this Act.

The registry shall be administered with due regard to con identiality, data privacy, and
child protection standards.

SECTION 6. Establishment of Trust Fund Accounts; Trust Agreement. — Within a
period to be speci ied in the IRR, the DSWD shall cause the opening of a Trust Fund
Account in the name of each covered child with the selected trust entity.

The DSWD and the trust entity shall execute a trust agreement or memorandum of
agreement that, at a minimum, provides for: (a) iduciary duties, (b) allowable trust
fees (if any), (c) investment parameters prioritizing capital preservation and liquidity,
(d) reporting and statement of account delivery, (e) audit access, and (f) safeguards
against unauthorized withdrawals.

SECTION 7. Contributions; In lation Indexing. —

   (a) Regular Contributions. Subject to appropriations, the National Government
       shall contribute Twelve Thousand Five Hundred Pesos (PHP
       12,500.00) quarterly to each Trust Fund Account of a covered child during the
       period of coverage, under rules set by the DSWD.

   (b) Period of Coverage. Contributions shall accrue only for quarters during which
       the child remains a covered child under Section 3(f), and until the child
       reaches eighteen (18) years of age, unless earlier terminated under this Act
       and the IRR.

   (c) Indexing. To maintain real value, the DSWD shall review the contribution
       amount every three (3) years and may recommend adjustments based on the
       average in lation rate for the period as certi ied by the Philippine Statistics
       Authority (PSA), subject to iscal sustainability and appropriations.

SECTION 8. Effect of Foster Care, Adoption, or Reuni ication. — Consistent with
the State policy favoring family-based care and the role of foster care as an alternative
family arrangement, a child who is placed under foster care, reuni ied with family, or
adopted shall:

   (a) retain ownership of contributions already credited to the Trust Fund Account
       during the period the child was covered; and

   (b) be governed, as to future contributions (if any), by eligibility rules in the IRR
       to ensure that this Act does not create incentives for prolonged
       institutionalization.

SECTION 9. Release of Bene its; Tranches; Permitted Uses. —

   (a) General Rule. Bene its shall be released starting when the bene iciary reaches
       eighteen (18) years of age.

   (b) Tranche Release. Release shall be in tranches under the IRR to reduce
       dissipation risk and to support transition needs (e.g., education, skills training,
       livelihood, rental deposits, basic needs, health care). The IRR shall provide
       minimum documentary requirements and counseling/transition support.

   (c) Direct Payment Option. The IRR may allow direct payment to schools,
       training centers, health providers, or other service providers where this better
       protects the bene iciary’s interest.

SECTION 10. Ineligibility, Suspension, and Disquali ication; Due Process. — The
DSWD shall issue guidelines on ineligibility, suspension, or disquali ication, provided
that:

   (a) no disquali ication shall be imposed without written notice and an
       opportunity to be heard;

   (b) the bene iciary shall have an administrative remedy/appeal within the DSWD;
       and

   (c) the standards shall be consistent with the best interest of the child and child
       protection principles.

SECTION 11. Death of Bene iciary; Unclaimed and Forfeited Funds. — The DSWD
shall incorporate the budgetary requirements of this Act in their annual budget
proposal.

   (a) Death Before Majority. If a covered child dies before reaching eighteen (18),
       the Trust Fund Account shall be closed and the balance shall be disposed of
       pursuant to the IRR, with preference for rechanneling to the program’s pool
       fund for the bene it of other covered children, subject to audit rules.

   (b) Death After Majority. If the bene iciary dies after reaching eighteen (18),
       disposition shall follow applicable succession laws unless otherwise provided
       by the trust agreement and consistent with law.

   (c) Unclaimed Bene its. Unclaimed bene its after a reasonable period to be
       provided in the IRR shall be transferred to a program pool fund, without
       prejudice to the bene iciary’s right to claim subject to conditions in the IRR.

SECTION 12. Program Administration; Reporting; Audit. — The DSWD shall
administer the program and shall:

   (a) submit an annual report to Congress on program coverage, fund utilization,
       and outcomes;

   (b) ensure transparency and accountability measures, including publication of
       aggregated statistics (without personally identifying information); and

   (c) ensure that the program and trust arrangements are subject to Commission
       on Audit (COA) rules and audit.

SECTION 13. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary to implement this Act shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. The DSWD shall incorporate
the budgetary requirements of this Act in its annual budget proposal.

SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, in consultation with the Department of the
Interior and Local Government (DILG), the Council for the Welfare of Children (CWC),

SECTION 15. Liberal Construction. — The provisions of this Act and its IRR shall be
liberally construed in favor of the best interest of the child.

SECTION 16. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 17. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 18. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
