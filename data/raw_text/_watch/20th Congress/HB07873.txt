AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE EXEMPTION OF REFINED PETROLEUM
IMPORTERS FROM THE BOND REQUIREMENT UNDER SECTION 160
OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED


                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

Refined petroleum products are indispensable to the country’s energy security and
economic stability. They power industries, transportation networks, and households
nationwide. Given the strategic importance of this sector, fiscal regulations must be
responsive to modern compliance systems and existing safeguards.

Section 160 of the National Internal Revenue Code requires manufacturers and
importers of excisable goods to post a bond as security for tax compliance. This
provision traces its origins to earlier tax regimes designed for a regulatory
environment lacking today’s monitoring and enforcement mechanisms.

In the case of refined petroleum importations, multiple safeguards already exist:

    1. Customs Control – Imported refined petroleum products cannot be released
       from customs custody without proof of payment of customs duties and excise
       taxes.

    2. Prepayment Practice – Industry practice reflects advance payment of excise
       taxes even before vessel arrival.

    3. Fuel Marking System (TRAIN Law) – The mandatory fuel marking program
       under Republic Act No. 10963 ensures that petroleum products entering the
       domestic market have paid the proper excise taxes.




                                                  1
These layers of enforcement render the bond requirement duplicative and unnecessary
for refined petroleum importers. Unlike other excisable goods, petroleum imports are
subject to real-time tracking, strict customs oversight, and chemical fuel marking
mechanisms that substantially reduce tax leakage risks.

Moreover, the annual premiums paid for such bonds form part of the importers’ cost
structure. These costs are eventually embedded in pump prices and passed on to
consumers. At a time when inflationary pressures are highly sensitive to fuel prices,
eliminating redundant regulatory burdens is both economically prudent and socially
responsive.

This measure proposes to exempt importers of refined petroleum products from the
bond requirement under Section 160, provided that they have no outstanding customs
duties or excise tax assessments arising from prior importations. The exemption is
therefore conditional and preserves government authority to enforce compliance.

By recognizing the sufficiency of existing enforcement systems and eliminating
outdated requirements, the bill:

   ● Promotes regulatory efficiency;

   ● Reduces unnecessary financial burdens on compliant importers;

   ● Supports price stability in the fuel market; and

   ● Maintains robust tax enforcement safeguards.


The measure modernizes the tax code in light of present-day compliance frameworks
while ensuring that revenue protection remains intact.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE EXEMPTION OF REFINED PETROLEUM

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

Section 1. Section 160 of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC), as amended, is
hereby further amended to read as follows:

        Sec. 160. Manufacturers’ and Importers’ Bonds. Manufacturers and importers
        of articles subject to excise tax shall post a bond subject to the following
        conditions:

        (A)​Initial Bond. – For the initial bond, the amount shall be PHP 100,000.
            HOWEVER, if after six (6) months of operation the initial bond is less
            than the total excise tax paid during that period, the bond shall be
            ADJUSTED TO TWICE THE AMOUNT OF TAX ACTUALLY
            PAID.

        (B) Bond for the Succeeding Years of Operation. – The bonds for the
            succeeding years of operation shall be based on the actual total excise tax
            paid during the period the year immediately preceding the year of
            operation.

The bond is CONDITIONED on the business FAITHFULLY COMPLYING
WITH ALL LAWS AND REGULATIONS and PAYING ANY FINES OR
PENALTIES imposed under this Code while it operates.

Importers of refined petroleum products ARE NOT COVERED BY THIS
SECTION, PROVIDED that there are NO UNPAID CUSTOMS DUTIES OR
EXCISE TAXES from the importation of such products in any previous year.

Sec. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance (DOF), in consultation with the
Bureau of Customs (BOC) and the appropriate government agencies, shall issue the
rules and regulations needed to implement this Act effectively.

Sec. 3. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.

Sec. 4. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations, or other
issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed and modified accordingly.

Sec. 5. Effectivity. -This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
