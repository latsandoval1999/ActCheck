                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 25, Article II of the 1987 Constitution provides that the State shall
ensure the autonomy of local governments. Likewise, Section 3, Article X provides
that the Congress shall enact a local government code which shall provide for a more
responsive and accountable local government structure instituted through a system
of decentralization with effective mechanisms of recall, initiative, and referendum,
allocate among the different local government units their powers, responsibilities,
and resources, and provide for the qualifications, election, appointment and removal,
term, salaries, powers and functions and duties of local officials, and all other matters
relating to the organization and operation of the local units.1 Thus, the Constitution
entrusts to Congress the task of giving effect to local autonomy by law, including

      However, the Code took effect in 1992 and has not kept pace with a changing
country. Local governments now carry larger responsibilities3 and receive more
resources4, yet studies show that devolution remains incomplete5 and that
development remains concentrated in Metro Manila, leaving other local
governments with uneven capacity.6 Hence, this measure creates a Congress-led
review commission, with the Executive Department and LGUs at the table, to
recommend the reforms needed to give full effect to local autonomy.

       To this end, this act creates the Decentralization and Local Autonomy Review
Commission, composed of five (5) Senators and five (5) Members of the House of
Representatives, co-chaired by the Chairpersons of the Committees on Local
Government of both Houses, which shall complete its work within three (3) years.
In carrying out its mandate, the Commission is empowered, among others, to review
the Code and outcomes of decentralization, formulate legislative and administrative
recommendations, and require data from government offices, together with such
other powers provided in this Act to achieve its objectives, and, in turn, it shall report
periodically to Congress with copies to the President. To support this work, the bill
provides a Technical Secretariat and the Philippine Institute for Development
Studies (PIDS) as research arm. Furthermore, to ensure that the Executive
Department and LGUs take part, a twelve-member Decentralization Policy Advisory

        In sum, the Constitution mandates genuine local autonomy, and the Code of
 1991 was enacted to give it effect. However, research on devolution and the data on
 regional development show that the mandate has been only partly fulfilled.
 Therefore, a single, time-bound, and recommendatory review, led by Congress and
 shared with the Executive Department and LGUs, is the most practicable way to turn
 scattered reform efforts into one legislative agenda that reaches beyond Metro
 Manila.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

in Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the                     D   D
Local Autonomy Review Act´


      Section 2. Declaration of Policy.      Pursuant to Section 25, Article II of the
1987 Constitution, the State shall ensure the autonomy of local governments.



                                                                                     1
Furthermore, Section 3, Article X directs Congress to enact a local government code
providing for a more responsive and accountable local government structure
instituted through a system of decentralization.
      Towards this end, the State shall review Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise
known as the "Local Government Code of 1991," and the outcomes of
decentralization, to ensure that the powers, responsibilities, and resources are
properly allocated among the different local government units, and that the local
government structure is responsive and accountable to the people.


      Section 3. Objectives. Guided by the operative principles of decentralization
under the Local Government Code, the Commission shall evaluate the outcomes of
decentralization, build a more integrated and well-coordinated system of local
governance, enhance the institutional capacity, financial management, and
accountability of local government units (LGUs), and the mechanisms for local
participation by setting specific, measurable, and time-bound recommendations,
including the amendments to the Local Government Code of 1991 and related laws
necessary to give full effect to local autonomy.


      Section 4. Creation of the Commission.          There is hereby created a
Decentralization and Local Autonomy Review Commission, hereinafter referred to
as the Commission, to review the Local Government Code of 1991 and the outcomes
of decentralization, and to recommend specific and time-bound reforms.


      Section 5. Composition.      The Commission shall be composed of ten (10)
members, five (5) of whom shall come from the Senate and five (5) from the House
of Representatives.



                                                                                 2
      The Chairpersons of the Committees on Local Government of the Senate and
of the House of Representatives shall act as Co-Chairpersons.
      The remaining four (4) members from each chamber shall be designated by
the Senate President and the Speaker of the House of Representatives, respectively.
      The members shall serve without compensation, but shall be entitled to
reimbursement of actual and necessary travel, communication, and other expenses,
subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations.


      Section 6. Duration. The Commission shall organize itself within thirty (30)
days from the effectivity of this Act, and shall complete its mandate within three (3)
years from its organization.


      Section 7. Powers and Functions.              The Commission shall have the
following powers and functions:
             (a) To review and evaluate the outcomes of decentralization and the
      Local Government Code of 1991 in accordance with Section 8 of this Act, and
      to identify the provisions of the Code and related laws that should be amended,
      clarified, or repealed;
             (b)   To formulate      short-term     and long-term    legislative   and
      administrative recommendations, including additional legislation, that are
      specific, measurable, and time-bound, and consistent with the existing
      policies and programs of the government;
             (c) To approve its work plan for the conduct of the review, assessment,
      and harmonization, as well as the budget for its programs and all
      disbursements therefrom, including the compensation of its personnel;

      (d) To hire and appoint employees and personnel on a temporary,
contractual, or consultancy basis;
      (e) To conduct hearings and receive testimonies, reports, and expert
advice;
      (f) To issue subpoenas ad testificandum and duces tecum to any public
official or private person, in accordance with its duly published rules of
procedure and with due respect for the rights of persons appearing before it;
      (g) To require any department, bureau, office, or instrumentality of the
government, including LGUs, to furnish data, reports, and other assistance,
subject to existing laws on confidentiality and data privacy;
      (h) To conduct multi-stakeholder consultations, through appropriate
mechanisms and guidelines, in each administrative region, with LGUs,
regional development councils, the private sector, civil society and people's
organizations, and the academe;
      (i) To build upon, and avoid duplication of, existing studies, reports,
and plans;
      (j) To review and act upon the recommendations of the Technical

      (k) To report to Congress, with copies furnished to the President, on a

      (l) To formulate the necessary rules and guidelines for the effective
implementation of this Act; and
      (m) To perform such other acts as may be necessary to carry out the
provisions of this Act.

      Section 8. Scope of Review.     Pursuant to Section 4 hereof, the Commission
shall evaluate the outcomes of decentralization, particularly in the delivery of basic
services and the equitable treatment of LGUs, and shall review the Local
Government Code of 1991 in light of its operative principles of decentralization.


      Section 9. Technical Secretariat.     The Commission shall be assisted by a
Technical Secretariat headed by an Executive Director, who shall be a recognized
expert in local governance and public finance and shall be appointed by the
Commission.


      Section 10. Research Arm.         The Philippine Institute for Development
Studies (PIDS) shall serve as the research arm of the Commission and, for the
duration thereof, shall provide the data-based research and analysis necessary for the
formulation of its recommendations.


      Section 11. Decentralization Policy Advisory Council.          There is hereby
created a Decentralization Policy Advisory Council, hereinafter referred to as the
Advisory Council, to provide expert advice to the Commission and to ensure the
participation of the Executive Department and LGUs in the review. The members of
the Advisory Council shall attend all meetings of the Commission.
      The Advisory Council shall be composed of twelve (12) members, as follows:
             (a) The Secretaries of the Interior and Local Government, of Budget
      and Management, of Finance, and of Economy, Planning, and Development,
      each of whom may designate a representative with a rank not lower than
      Undersecretary;



                                                                                    5
             (b) The national presidents of the Liga ng mga Barangay, the League
      of Provinces of the Philippines, the League of Cities of the Philippines, and
      the League of Municipalities of the Philippines; and
             (c) Four (4) experts chosen by the Senate President and the Speaker of
      the House of Representatives, two (2) from the academe and two (2) from
      civil society and the private sector.


      Section 12. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial operations
of the Commission shall be charged against the available appropriations of the
Senate and the House of Representatives. Thereafter, the amount necessary for the
remainder of its term, including any deficiency in the current year, shall be included
in the annual General Appropriations Act, subject to existing budgeting, accounting,
and auditing rules and regulations.


      Section 13. Separability Clause.        If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.


      Section 14. Repealing Clause.           All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees, administrative orders, rules and regulations, issuances, or parts thereof
inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
amended accordingly. Nothing in this Act shall be construed as an amendment of
Local Government Code of 1991.


      Section 15. Effectivity.   This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
