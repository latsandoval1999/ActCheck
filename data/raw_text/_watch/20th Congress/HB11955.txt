14                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE
15
16   In the Philippines, companion animals are widely regarded as cherished members of the family.
17   According to a 2023 Social Weather Stations (SWS) survey, 64% of Filipino households own at
18   least one pet, reflecting a growing culture of responsible pet ownership across the country.
19
20   Despite the deep emotional bond between Filipinos and their pets, there remains a severe lack of
21   accessible, dignified, and environmentally safe burial spaces for deceased companion animals.
22   Many families, particularly in densely populated urban areas, lack private yard space to bury their
23   pets. Private cremation and memorial services are often prohibitively expensive, leaving pet owners
24   with very few viable options.
25
26   In late October 2026, nationwide outrage erupted following the discovery of dozens of deceased
27   dogs and cats stuffed inside sacks and abandoned in a vacant lot in San Jose del Monte, Bulacan—
28   remains that investigators later traced to a Cavite-based facility that had improperly discarded the
29   animals instead of giving them a proper send-off. Alongside such incidents, deceased pets continue
30   to be discarded in waterways, roadsides, and municipal garbage bins by individuals who lack
31   affordable burial options, posing serious public health, sanitation, and environmental hazards.
32
33   This bill seeks to address this gap by mandating every city and municipality to establish, operate,
34   and maintain a dedicated public pet cemetery or memorial park. By providing transparent,
35   affordable, and sanitary burial grounds managed directly by Local Government Units (LGUs), this
36   measure ensures accessible after-care options for all pet owners, promotes public hygiene,
37   safeguards the environment, and honors the dignity of companion animals.
38
39   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 73
 74         AN ACT REQUIRING ALL CITIES AND MUNICIPALITIES TO ESTABLISH,
 75        OPERATE, AND MAINTAIN PUBLIC PET CEMETERIES, PROVIDING FUNDS
 76                     THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
 77
 78         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
 79   assembled:
 80
 81   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Public Pet Cemetery Act."
 82
 83   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote
 84   animal welfare, protect public health and sanitation, and preserve environmental integrity. Toward
 85   this end, the State shall ensure that deceased companion animals are accorded dignified, sanitary,
 86   and environmentally sound burial services accessible to all citizens.
 87
 88   SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall mean:
 89        (a) Companion Animal or Pet refers to any domesticated animal kept primarily for
 90        companionship, emotional support, or service, including dogs, cats, birds, rabbits, and other
 91        small household animals, excluding livestock and wildlife regulated under separate laws.
 92        (b) Local Government Unit (LGU) refers to any province, city, or municipality.
 93        (c) Public Pet Cemetery refers to a designated parcel of land owned, leased, or managed by
 94        an LGU exclusively dedicated to the sanitary interment, burial, or memorialization of
 95        deceased companion animals.
 96
 97   SECTION 4. Establishment of Public Pet Cemeteries. – Every city and municipality shall
 98   establish, operate, and maintain at least one (1) public pet cemetery within its territorial jurisdiction
 99   within two (2) years from the effectivity of this Act.
100
101   SECTION 5. Site Selection, Sanitation, and Environmental Standards. – The site selection,
102   design, and operation of public pet cemeteries shall comply with existing zoning ordinances,
103   sanitation laws, and environmental regulations. Specifically:
104           (a) The cemetery shall be located at a safe distance from residential water sources, rivers,
105           creeks, and flood-prone areas to prevent groundwater contamination, in accordance with
106           Presidential Decree No. 856 (Code on Sanitation of the Philippines).
107           (b) Graves shall meet minimum depth requirements and soil cover standards prescribed by
108           the Department of Health (DOH) and the Department of Environment and Natural
109           Resources (DENR).
110           (c) Remains of animals that died from highly contagious or zoonotic diseases, such as rabies,
111           shall be handled and disposed of strictly in accordance with biosecurity protocols set by the
112           Department of Agriculture – Bureau of Animal Industry (DA-BAI).
113
114   SECTION 6. Administration, Transparency, and Record-Keeping. – The City or Municipal
115   Veterinary Office, in coordination with the City or Municipal Environment and Natural Resources
116   Office (ENRO) and Health Office, shall oversee the day-to-day management, maintenance, and
117   record-keeping of the public pet cemetery.
118   To ensure proper management and accountability, the LGU shall:
119          (a) Maintain an official registry recording the animal's name, species, breed, date of burial,
120          exact plot coordinates, and owner details for every interred pet; and
121          (b) Issue an official Certificate of Interment to the pet owner and permit owners or their
122          authorized representatives to witness the burial process.
123
124   SECTION 7. Fees and Indigent Exemption. – LGUs may impose reasonable, minimal
125   maintenance fees for burial plots and memorial markers to sustain the operations of the facility.
126   Provided, that indigent pet owners, as certified by the City or Municipal Social Welfare and
127   Development Office (CSWDO/MSWDO), as well as registered animal rescue groups and LGU
128   animal pounds, shall be exempted from paying burial fees.
129
130   SECTION 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial establishment and operation
131   of public pet cemeteries shall be charged against the respective local development funds and annual
132   budgets of the cities and municipalities. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for their
133   continued maintenance shall be included in the annual budget of the concerned LGU.
134
135   SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within ninety (90) days from the
136   effectivity of this Act, the DILG, in coordination with the DA-BAI, DOH, DENR, and leagues of
137   cities and municipalities, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
138   implementation of this Act.
139
140   SECTION 10. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
141   invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.
142
143   SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and regulations
144   inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
145
146   SECTION 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
147   Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
