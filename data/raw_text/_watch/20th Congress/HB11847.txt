                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Constitution declares that public office is a public trust and directs the Civil Service
Commission to strengthen merit, integrity, responsiveness, and accountability in government
service. Promotion to higher responsibility must therefore reflect not only minimum qualifications
and technical competence, but also demonstrated fitness for public trust.

        Under Section 73 of the 2025 Rules on Administrative Cases in the Civil Service,
promulgated through Civil Service Commission Resolution No. 2500357, the penalty of
suspension already carries disqualification from promotion for a period corresponding to the
period of suspension. In practice, an employee may therefore become eligible for promotion
shortly after serving the penalty, even when the administrative offense reflects a serious lapse in
honesty, discipline, or integrity. This measure establishes a longer statutory post-suspension
accountability period, ranging from three (3) to six (6) years, for officials and employees who are
finally penalized with disciplinary suspension. The period is calibrated according to the length and
nature of the penalty, with the longest period applying only where suspension was lawfully
imposed for an integrity-related offense not punishable by dismissal under applicable law or rules,
or where the respondent has a qualifying prior disciplinary suspension.

         The bill protects due process by applying only when a final and executory decision actually
imposes disciplinary suspension. It expressly excludes preventive suspension and the mere
pendency of a complaint, neither of which constitutes a finding of guilt. Dismissal, perpetual
disqualification, and any other heavier penalty provided by law or rules remain controlling. The
bill also requires personnel officers and appointing authorities to verify eligibility before issuing a
promotional appointment.

       By linking advancement in public service to a sustained record of integrity and
accountability, the measure seeks to preserve public confidence while allowing disciplined
employees a clear and proportionate path toward rehabilitation and renewed eligibility.
In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
 ESTABLISHING AN EXTENDED DISQUALIFICATION FROM PROMOTION FOR
  GOVERNMENT OFFICIALS AND EMPLOYEES FINALLY PENALIZED WITH
  SUSPENSION FOR ADMINISTRATIVE OFFENSES, PROVIDING GRADUATED
    PERIODS OF THREE (3) TO SIX (6) YEARS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

                                       CHAPTER I
                                   GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title.
       This Act shall be known as the “Public Service Promotion Accountability Act of 2026.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.
        It is the policy of the State to uphold the constitutional principles that public office is a
public trust and that appointments in the civil service shall be made according to merit and fitness.
Toward this end, advancement to higher positions shall be conditioned upon a sustained record of
integrity, discipline, and accountability, without prejudice to due process and the rehabilitative
purpose of administrative discipline.

SEC. 3. Coverage.
       This Act shall apply to all appointive public officials and employees in the civil service
who are subject to administrative disciplinary jurisdiction, regardless of status of appointment, in
the National Government, local government units, government-owned or controlled corporations
with original charters, state universities and colleges, and other government instrumentalities,
subject to the Constitution and the constitutional independence or fiscal autonomy of the office
concerned.
SEC. 4. Definition of Terms.
       For purposes of this Act:

       a. “Final disciplinary suspension” means a suspension actually imposed as an
       administrative penalty under a decision that has become final and executory after the lapse
       or exhaustion of the applicable period for reconsideration, appeal, or review. It does not
       include preventive suspension, dismissal, or any heavier penalty;
       b. “Promotion” means the advancement of an official or employee from one position to
       another position with an increase in duties and responsibilities as authorized by law, and
       usually accompanied by an increase in salary or rank. It includes a temporary, coterminous,
       or acting appointment or designation to a higher position when used to confer substantially
       the same benefits, authority, or status as a promotion;
       c. “Dishonesty” includes serious dishonesty, less serious dishonesty, and simple
       dishonesty, as classified under applicable civil service laws and rules; and
       d. “Administrative offense” means an act or omission punishable under civil service laws,
       the Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public Officials and Employees, the rules
       of the Office of the Ombudsman, or other applicable administrative disciplinary laws and
       regulations.

                                CHAPTER II
                 EXTENDED DISQUALIFICATION FROM PROMOTION

SEC. 5. Extended Disqualification from Promotion.
        Notwithstanding any law, rule, or regulation to the contrary, an appointive public official
or employee against whom a final and executory decision actually imposes disciplinary suspension
for an administrative offense shall, after completing the service of the suspension, be disqualified
from promotion for the applicable period prescribed in the succeeding section. Preventive
suspension shall not trigger the disqualification under this Act, and dismissal, perpetual
disqualification, or any other heavier penalty imposed under applicable law or rules shall remain
controlling.

        A suspension lawfully converted to a fine or otherwise satisfied through an authorized
alternative mode shall carry the same disqualification based on the duration of the original
suspension, unless the official or employee is exonerated or the suspension is set aside on appeal
or review.

SEC. 6. Graduated Periods of Disqualification.
       The period of disqualification from promotion shall be determined as follows:

       a. Three (3) years, when the final disciplinary suspension does not exceed three (3) months;
       b. Four (4) years, when the final disciplinary suspension exceeds three (3) months but does
       not exceed six (6) months;
       c. Five (5) years, when the final disciplinary suspension exceeds six (6) months but does
       not exceed one (1) year; and
       d. Six (6) years, regardless of the duration of suspension, where the final and executory
       decision lawfully imposes suspension for an integrity-related offense involving dishonesty,
       graft or corruption, fraud, falsification, abuse of authority, or a comparable breach of public
       trust, provided that the offense is not punishable by dismissal under applicable law or rules;
       or when the respondent has previously been finally penalized with disciplinary suspension
       for another administrative offense within the ten (10) years immediately preceding the
       finality of the latest decision.


       When more than one classification applies, the longer period shall govern. Nothing in this
Act shall reduce or supersede dismissal, perpetual disqualification, or any heavier penalty imposed
under existing law or rules.

SEC. 7. Reckoning of the Period.
        The disqualification shall commence on the day immediately following the official
completion of the suspension. When the suspension is converted to a fine or another authorized
mode, the period shall commence upon full satisfaction of the converted penalty. Time during
which the official or employee unlawfully occupies a promotional position in violation of this Act
shall not be credited toward the disqualification period.

SEC. 8. Preventive Suspension and Pending Cases.
       No person shall be disqualified from promotion under this Act solely by reason of
preventive suspension, the filing or pendency of an administrative or criminal complaint, or an
administrative decision that has not become final and executory. This section shall not limit any
temporary restriction expressly authorized under another law or lawful civil service rule.

                                CHAPTER III
               ADMINISTRATION, ENFORCEMENT, AND OVERSIGHT

SEC. 9. Certification Before Promotion.
        Before a promotional appointment, designation, or personnel action is issued, the human
resource management officer or equivalent personnel officer shall certify that the proposed
appointee is not subject to an unexpired disqualification under this Act. The appointing authority
shall rely on and preserve such certification as part of the official personnel record.
       The Civil Service Commission shall establish a secure and updated mechanism for
recording and verifying disqualifications, consistent with data privacy laws and the right of the
concerned official or employee to inspect and correct erroneous personnel information.

SEC. 10. Effect of a Prohibited Promotion.
        A promotional appointment or designation issued in violation of this Act shall be subject
to disapproval, invalidation, or recall by the Civil Service Commission or the competent
constitutional, judicial, legislative, or administrative authority, without prejudice to lawful
compensation for services actually rendered and to the administrative liability of any person who
willfully circumvents this Act.

SEC. 11. Restoration of Eligibility.
        Upon expiration of the applicable period, the official or employee shall no longer be
disqualified under this Act and may be considered for promotion in accordance with merit and
fitness requirements. Expiration of the period shall not create a vested right to promotion or
appointment.

       If the decision imposing suspension is reversed or the respondent is exonerated, the
disqualification shall be immediately cancelled and the personnel record corrected, without
prejudice to remedies available under existing law.

SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations.
        Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Civil Service Commission, in
consultation with the Office of the Ombudsman, the Department of Budget and Management, the
Department of the Interior and Local Government, and representatives of government human
resource management offices and employee organizations, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for its implementation.

       Independent constitutional bodies and the Judiciary may promulgate complementary rules
consistent with their respective constitutional powers and the minimum standards established in
this Act.

SEC. 13. Congressional Oversight and Report.
       The Civil Service Commission shall include in its annual report to the President and
Congress anonymized and aggregated data on the implementation of this Act, including the
number and classification of active disqualifications, promotions disapproved or recalled, and
disqualifications cancelled following exoneration or reversal.
                                         CHAPTER IV
                                      FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 14. Appropriations.
       The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be charged against
the current appropriations of the Civil Service Commission. Thereafter, such sums as may be
necessary shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 15. Separability Clause.
        If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions
not affected thereby shall continue in full force and effect.

SEC. 16. Repealing Clause.
        All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly: Provided, That
existing penalties or disqualifications that are more severe shall remain in force.

SEC. 17. Effectivity.
       This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in
a newspaper of general circulation.
