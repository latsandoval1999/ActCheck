                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      In every economy, there is a quiet line that separates stability from
vulnerability. It is the line where income, though earned honestly and steadily, is
no longer enough to keep pace with the cost of living. For millions of Filipino
families, particularly those in the low-income and middle-class sectors, that line
has been crossed not because of idleness or failure, but because prices have risen
faster than wages, and essential needs have become increasingly unaffordable.

      Electricity, water, healthcare, education, and housing are not discretionary
expenses. They are the fixed costs of dignity. When these become too expensive,
households are forced into painful trade-offs—between food and medicine,
tuition and rent, work and care. Over time, these trade-offs accumulate into
deeper inequality, poorer health outcomes, educational attrition, and economic
fragility.

       This proposed measure seeks to correct that imbalance by institutionalizing
a Maginhawang Buhay Support Framework, grounded on the principle that every
Filipino household is entitled to a basic social floor of economic security,
regardless of income level, so long as they remain within the broad low-income
and middle- class spectrum. In this sense, the measure draws from a universal
income perspective—not by distributing cash, but by protecting real income at
the point where it matters most: the price of essentials.

       Persistent inflation in recent years has eroded household purchasing power,
disproportionately affecting families whose incomes are fixed or slow-moving.
While emergency subsidies and conditional assistance programs have provided
temporary relief, these measures are often reactive, fragmented, and dependent
on discretionary political action. Moreover, existing social protection
mechanisms are largely targeted at the poorest households, leaving a growing
segment of the middle class exposed—too “rich” to qualify for aid, yet too
financially constrained to absorb rising costs.

       This condition has created a silent crisis. Middle-income households
increasingly rely on debt, reduce preventive healthcare, delay education, or accept
substandard housing conditions. The long-term cost of these decisions is borne
not only by families, but by the State itself, through higher healthcare
expenditures, lower educational attainment, reduced productivity, and social
instability.



                                                                                 2
      There is therefore a clear need for a permanent, automatic, and inflation-
responsive mechanism that stabilizes household welfare before economic stress
turns into economic damage.

      This bill establishes a Maginhawang Buhay Support Framework that grants
qualified low-income and middle-class households a twenty percent (20%)
discount on essential needs, specifically basic utilities, healthcare, education, and
housing expenses. The discount is designed as a baseline social protection
measure that operates continuously, rather than episodically.

      The measure is carefully integrated with existing lifeline rates, PhilHealth
benefits, free tuition and scholarship programs, and housing subsidies, ensuring
that it supplements rather than replaces current entitlements. It introduces a
national beneficiary registry, anchored on official statistics and interoperable
government databases, to ensure accurate targeting, transparency, and protection
against leakage.

      Recognizing the cyclical nature of inflation, the bill further provides for an
automatic, time-bound expansion of benefits when inflation breaches defined
thresholds, thereby avoiding the delays and uncertainty of ad hoc emergency
legislation.

      To ensure fiscal sustainability, the measure adopts a cost-sharing
mechanism with the private sector, requiring covered service providers to grant
discounts upfront, while allowing reimbursement solely through tax credits and
incentives, subject to fiscal ceilings, audit, and verification. No direct cash
reimbursement is allowed, and no new or special appropriation is mandated.




                                                                                   3
       Oversight is institutionalized through an inter-agency council attached to
the Office of the President, supported by a permanent secretariat, and
complemented by congressional oversight and sunset review provisions.

       The immediate impact of this measure is the protection of real household
income. By reducing the price of essentials, the bill functions as a form of
universal income stabilization, ensuring that earned income retains its purchasing
power even during inflationary periods. Unlike cash transfers, which may be
inflationary, this approach directly lowers effective prices and moderates second-
round inflation effects. Economically, the measure is expected to stabilize
consumption, particularly among low- and middle-income households with high
marginal propensity to spend. This sustains local demand, supports small and
medium enterprises, and mitigates economic contraction during periods of price
volatility.

       Socially, the bill reduces the risk of preventable health crises, educational
dropouts, housing insecurity, and debt accumulation. Over time, these effects
translate into lower public health costs, higher educational completion rates, a
more resilient workforce, and a broader tax base.

       From a governance perspective, the measure shifts social protection from
discretion to design—from reactive relief to automatic response—thereby
strengthening public trust and policy credibility.

       At its core, this measure affirms a simple but powerful idea: income is only
meaningful if it can meet the cost of living. By institutionalizing a Maginhawang
Buhay Support Framework, the State acknowledges that protecting dignity in
times of economic stress is not charity, but responsibility.

      This bill does not promise abundance. It promises stability. And in an
economy shaped by uncertainty, stability is the foundation upon which
opportunity, growth, and shared prosperity are built.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

SECTION. 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Maginhawang
Buhay Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote social justice, equity, shared prosperity, and household economic
resilience, and to protect and enhance the purchasing power of Filipino
households, particularly those belonging to the low-income and economically
vulnerable middle-class sectors.

The State recognizes that sustained inflationary pressures, rising costs of living,
and economic shocks disproportionately burden households whose incomes are
fixed, vulnerable, or gradually eroded by price increases.

The State further recognizes that access to essential goods and services, including
basic utilities, healthcare, education, and housing, is indispensable to human
dignity, family stability, economic mobility, and inclusive national development.

Accordingly, the State shall institutionalize a Maginhawang Buhay Support
Framework that shall provide predictable, targeted, transparent, and fiscally
responsible relief to qualified households.

Toward this end, the State shall:

(a) protect households from excessive erosion of purchasing power;

(b) prevent low-income and economically vulnerable middle-class households
from falling into deeper poverty or financial distress;

(c) integrate and harmonize existing subsidies, statutory discounts, and social
protection programs;

(d) establish an automatic, objective, and time-bound response to sustained
inflation;

(e) ensure that benefits are accurately targeted and subject to reasonable statutory
ceilings;




                                                                                  2
(f) prioritize poorer and more economically vulnerable households whenever
fiscal resources are constrained;

(g) minimize fraud, duplication, exclusion, and leakage through interoperable and
secure government systems; and

(h)   ensure   transparency,   accountability,   measurable    outcomes,    fiscal
sustainability, and continuing congressional oversight.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

(a) Annual Fiscal Envelope refers to the maximum annual aggregate fiscal
exposure authorized for implementation of this Act, including direct
appropriations, authorized funding sources, and tax expenditures arising from
reimbursements, credits, or other incentives granted hereunder;

(b) Annual Household Benefit Ceiling refers to the maximum aggregate amount
of Maginhawang Buhay benefits that may be availed of by a qualified household
during a calendar year;

(c) Covered Service Providers refers to public or private entities, utilities,
institutions, facilities, schools, healthcare providers, housing providers, or
establishments delivering essential needs covered under this Act;

(d) Eligible Net Amount refers to the remaining amount actually payable by the
qualified beneficiary after all applicable statutory discounts, exemptions,
insurance benefits, subsidies, vouchers, scholarships, grants, lifeline rates, and
other government assistance have first been applied;

(e) Essential Needs refers to goods and services indispensable to decent living
and human dignity, including basic utilities, healthcare services and medicines,
education-related expenses, and housing-related costs;
                                                                                3
(f) Inflation-Triggered Enhancement refers to the temporary increase in the
value or applicable ceiling of Maginhawang Buhay benefits authorized under
Section 11 of this Act;

(g) Low-Income Households refers to households whose total gross annual
income falls within the poverty threshold or low-income classifications officially
determined, defined, and periodically updated by the Philippine Statistics
Authority, taking into account household size, geographic location, and
prevailing cost-of-living indicators;

(h) Maginhawang Buhay Discount refers to the mandatory twenty percent
(20%) reduction in the Eligible Net Amount for covered essential needs, subject
to the limitations and safeguards established under this Act;

(i) Middle-Class Households refers to households whose total gross annual
income falls within income brackets officially classified by the Philippine
Statistics Authority as middle class, including vulnerable, emerging, and
pressured middle-income groups whose economic security is materially affected
by inflation or economic volatility;

(j) Qualified Beneficiaries refers to low-income and middle-class households
duly identified, registered, validated, and included in the National Maginhawang
Buhay Registry pursuant to this Act; and

(k) Tax Expenditure refers to revenues otherwise collectible by the National
Government but lawfully forgone through tax credits, deductions, exemptions, or
other tax-based incentives granted pursuant to this Act.

SEC. 4. Maginhawang Buhay Support Framework. — There is hereby
established a Maginhawang Buhay Support Framework, which shall serve as the
comprehensive, coordinated, and institutional mechanism for the delivery of
discounts on essential needs to qualified beneficiaries.

The Framework shall provide, as a baseline benefit, a twenty percent (20%)
discount on the Eligible Net Amount for covered essential needs, subject to:

(a) beneficiary eligibility;

(b) annual and sector-specific benefit ceilings;

(c) consumption thresholds;

(d) beneficiary prioritization;

(e) verification procedures;

(f) the Annual Fiscal Envelope; and

(g) safeguards against fraud, duplication, abuse, and leakage.

The Framework shall be guided by the principles of accurate targeting,
administrative simplicity, proportionality, fiscal sustainability, transparency,
shared public-private responsibility, data protection, accountability, and
measurable social impact.

SEC. 5. Annual Household Benefit Ceiling. — The aggregate base
Maginhawang Buhay benefit that may be availed of by a qualified household
during a calendar year shall not exceed an amount equivalent to twenty percent
(20%) of the applicable annual poverty threshold for the household size and



                                                                               5
geographic area concerned, as officially determined by the Philippine Statistics
Authority.

The Council may prescribe lower sector-specific ceilings for utilities, healthcare,
education, and housing, taking into account:

(a) prevailing prices and expenditure patterns;

(b) household size;

(c) geographic differences in cost of living;

(d) beneficiary income classification;

(e) available fiscal space;

(f) actual utilization;

(g) program performance; and

(h) equity among beneficiaries.

No adjustment made by the Council may exceed the aggregate statutory ceiling
provided herein except during an Inflation-Triggered Enhancement authorized
under Section 11 of this Act.

SEC. 6. Scope of Maginhawang Buhay Discounts. — Qualified beneficiaries
shall be entitled to the Maginhawang Buhay Discount on the following essential
needs:

(a) Basic Utilities. — Electricity, water, basic telecommunications, and internet
services, subject to lifeline consumption caps, service classifications, and
regulatory parameters prescribed by the appropriate regulatory agencies;

                                                                                 6
(b) Healthcare Expenses. — Hospital services, professional fees, diagnostic and
laboratory services, medicines, medical devices, and outpatient care in public and
private healthcare facilities;

(c) Education Expenses. — Tuition and authorized miscellaneous fees in public
and private basic education schools, higher education institutions, and technical-
vocational institutions; and

(d) Housing Expenses. — Rent, monthly amortizations, and basic housing-
related charges for socialized and affordable housing.

The Council shall establish sector-specific ceilings and consumption thresholds
consistent with Section 5 of this Act.

SEC. 7. Integration with Existing Subsidy Programs and Order of Application
of Benefits. — The Maginhawang Buhay Discount shall supplement and not
replace existing statutory discounts, exemptions, lifeline rates, insurance benefits,
subsidies, vouchers, scholarships, grants, and other social protection programs.

For purposes of determining the Eligible Net Amount, benefits shall generally be
applied in the following order, subject to specific governing laws:

(a) statutory discounts and exemptions;

(b) mandatory social insurance and benefit packages;

(c) lifeline rates and regulated subsidies;

(d) scholarships, vouchers, grants, and other government financial assistance; and

(e) the Maginhawang Buhay Discount on the remaining amount actually payable.

Accordingly:
                                                                                   7
(1) for utilities, the discount shall be applied after applicable lifeline rates and
regulated subsidies;

(2) for healthcare, the discount shall apply to the remaining co-payments and out-
of-pocket expenses after PhilHealth, other mandatory benefit packages, and
applicable statutory discounts;

(3) for education, the discount shall apply only to the net amount payable after
free tuition, vouchers, scholarships, subsidies, and other student financial
assistance have been deducted; and

(4) for housing, the discount shall align with existing socialized and affordable
housing programs and shall not impair or diminish existing contractual subsidies.

No expense, transaction, or portion thereof may be claimed, reimbursed, credited,
or discounted more than once under the same government benefit.

The Council shall establish uniform rules on benefit stacking and sequencing to
prevent duplication while preserving lawful entitlements.

SEC. 8. Beneficiary Identification, Income Tiers, and Prioritization. — The
Department of Social Welfare and Development, in coordination with the
Philippine Statistics Authority, the Department of Economic Planning and
Development, and concerned local government units, shall establish, maintain,
and regularly update a National Maginhawang Buhay Registry.

Qualified beneficiaries shall be classified into the following priority tiers:

Tier I — Poor and Low-Income Households;

Tier II — Vulnerable and Emerging Middle-Class Households; and


                                                                                  8
Tier III — Pressured Middle-Class Households.

       Tier I beneficiaries shall be entitled to up to one hundred percent (100%)
of the applicable Annual Household Benefit Ceiling.

       Tier II beneficiaries may be entitled to up to seventy-five percent (75%) of
the applicable Annual Household Benefit Ceiling.

       Tier III beneficiaries may be entitled to up to fifty percent (50%) of the
applicable Annual Household Benefit Ceiling.

The twenty percent (20%) discount rate shall remain applicable to the Eligible
Net Amount until the corresponding annual ceiling is reached.

Subject to the Annual Fiscal Envelope, assistance shall be funded and
implemented in the following order of priority:

(a) Tier I;

(b) Tier II; and

(c) Tier III.

No qualified Tier I household shall be denied an otherwise available benefit
solely to preserve funding for a lower-priority tier.

The Council may refine income bands within the foregoing tiers, taking into
account household size, geographic cost of living, dependency ratio, disability,
catastrophic expenditure, and other objectively verifiable indicators of economic
vulnerability.




                                                                                 9
SEC. 9. National Maginhawang Buhay Registry. — The Registry shall utilize
interoperable government databases, secure digital platforms, and verification
systems to ensure:

(a) accurate targeting;

(b) single household registration;

(c) timely updating of beneficiary status;

(d) prevention of duplicate registration;

(e) verification of benefit utilization;

(f) data integrity;

(g) prevention of fraud and leakage; and

(h) efficient interoperability among participating agencies and providers.

The collection, processing, storage, use, and sharing of personal data shall strictly
comply with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and other applicable laws.

Only information reasonably necessary for beneficiary identification, eligibility
verification, benefit delivery, audit, program evaluation, and prevention of fraud
may be collected or processed.

SEC. 10. Grievance, Correction, and Appeals Mechanism. — The DSWD shall
establish an accessible, free, and time-bound grievance and appeals mechanism
for:

(a) wrongful exclusion from the Registry;

(b) incorrect beneficiary classification;
                                                                                  10
(c) suspension or termination of benefits;

(d) disputed transactions;

(e) non-recognition of lawful benefits; and

(f) other errors affecting beneficiary entitlement.

Initial complaints shall be resolved within fifteen (15) working days from receipt
of complete supporting information.

An aggrieved party may appeal an adverse determination to the appropriate
regional office or such body designated by the Council within fifteen (15)
working days.

Final administrative appeals shall be resolved within thirty (30) working days.

No beneficiary shall be permanently removed from the Registry without notice
and reasonable opportunity to contest the basis for removal, except in cases
involving fraud or identity theft requiring immediate protective action.

SEC. 11. Inflation-Triggered Automatic Enhancement of Benefits. — An
Inflation-Triggered Enhancement shall be activated when the national inflation
rate reaches or exceeds four percent (4%) for three (3) consecutive months, as
officially reported by the Philippine Statistics Authority.

Upon activation, and subject to the Annual Fiscal Envelope and certifications
required under Section 12:

(a) the Maginhawang Buhay Discount may be increased by not more than five
(5) percentage points, provided that the total discount shall in no case exceed
twenty-five percent (25%); and

                                                                                  11
(b) the applicable Annual Household Benefit Ceiling may be increased by not
more than twenty- five percent (25%).

No inflation-triggered enhancement may:

(1) create a new category of essential need not authorized under this Act;

(2) expand eligibility beyond the classes contemplated herein;

(3) require a new or special appropriation outside the existing Annual Fiscal
Envelope; or

(4) create a vested right to continuation of the enhanced benefit.

Any enhancement shall automatically terminate once national inflation falls
below four percent (4%) for three (3) consecutive months.

Within thirty (30) days after activation or termination, the Council shall submit a
report to Congress stating the fiscal impact, number of beneficiaries affected, and
measures implemented.

SEC. 12. Annual Fiscal Envelope and Fiscal Safeguards. — The Department
of Finance and the Department of Budget and Management shall jointly
determine and certify the proposed Annual Fiscal Envelope for the
implementation of this Act.

The Annual Fiscal Envelope shall include:

(a) appropriated funds;

(b) authorized savings and unobligated balances;

(c) other authorized funding sources; and

                                                                                12
(d) the maximum allowable tax expenditure arising from provider
reimbursement.

The proposed Annual Fiscal Envelope shall be submitted as part of the annual
national budget process and shall be separately disclosed in the National
Expenditure Program or accompanying fiscal documents.

No implementation, benefit enhancement, tax credit, or other fiscal commitment
under this Act shall exceed the Annual Fiscal Envelope.

If projected claims exceed available fiscal capacity, the Council shall:

(a) first apply statutory and sector-specific benefit ceilings;

(b) preserve benefits for Tier I households;

(c) thereafter prioritize Tier II households; and

(d) reduce or defer benefits for Tier III households as may be necessary.

No unpaid, deferred, or disallowed claim arising solely from exhaustion of the
Annual Fiscal Envelope shall constitute a vested right against the National
Government.

No provision of this Act shall be construed as creating an automatic
appropriation.

SEC. 17. Responsibilities of Covered Service Providers. — Covered service
providers shall:

(a) honor the Maginhawang Buhay Discount upon verification of beneficiary
eligibility;

(b) apply the discount only to the Eligible Net Amount;

(c) clearly reflect the original charge, prior discounts or subsidies, Eligible Net
Amount, Maginhawang Buhay Discount, and final amount payable in billing
statements, official receipts, or electronic records;

(d) maintain transaction records sufficient for audit and verification;


                                                                                18
(e) submit periodic compliance and reimbursement reports;

(f) cooperate with regulatory, tax, and auditing authorities;

(g) protect beneficiary information; and

(h) refrain from passing on the cost of the discount through hidden charges,
unjustified rate increases, reduced service quality, discriminatory treatment, or
other forms of circumvention.

SEC. 18. Cost-Sharing with Private Service Providers and Government
Reimbursement. — The implementation of the Maginhawang Buhay Discount
shall be undertaken through a mandatory cost-sharing mechanism between the
National Government and covered private service providers.

Covered private service providers shall grant the full applicable discount at the
point of service or billing.

Of the verified Maginhawang Buhay Discount actually granted:

(a) eighty percent (80%) shall constitute the maximum reimbursable government
share; and

(b) twenty percent (20%) shall be borne by the covered private service provider
as its cost- sharing contribution.

The reimbursable government share shall be recognized exclusively through tax-
based mechanisms, primarily through tax credits against national internal revenue
tax liabilities, subject to the Annual Fiscal Envelope.

Tax credits:

(1) shall be supported by verified transaction-level documentation;
                                                                              19
(2) shall not exceed eighty percent (80%) of the lawful discount actually granted;

(3) shall not be claimed more than once;

(4) shall be subject to audit, valuation, reconciliation, and post-utilization review
by the Bureau of Internal Revenue;

(5) may be carried forward for a period not exceeding three (3) taxable years
when the provider has insufficient current tax liability, subject to implementing
rules; and

(6) shall not be convertible into direct cash reimbursement.

The Department of Finance and Bureau of Internal Revenue shall establish a
uniform electronic claims and verification system.

No provider shall acquire a vested right to reimbursement exceeding the Annual
Fiscal Envelope.

For public service providers, the cost attributable to discounts shall be
implemented within their authorized appropriations and other lawful funding
sources.

SEC. 21. Fraud Prevention, Prohibited Acts, and Penalties. — The following
acts are prohibited:

(a) willful refusal by a covered provider to grant a lawful discount;

(b) collection of hidden charges or imposition of unjustified price or rate increases
intended to recover the cost of the discount;

(c) reduction of service quality or discriminatory treatment of beneficiaries;

(d) filing of false, fictitious, inflated, or duplicate reimbursement claims;

(e) use of another person’s beneficiary identity or credentials;

(f) collusion between beneficiaries and providers to generate fictitious
transactions;

(g) falsification or manipulation of Registry or transaction data;

(h) unauthorized access, disclosure, sale, or misuse of beneficiary information;

(i) claiming reimbursement for an amount already reimbursed, subsidized, or
otherwise compensated by government; and

(j) any other fraudulent act intended to evade, defeat, or unlawfully benefit from
this Act.

Violations shall be subject to administrative fines, disallowance and recovery of
improper claims, suspension or revocation of accreditation or licenses when
authorized by law, exclusion from the program, and applicable civil or criminal
liability.




                                                                                  23
Any provider found to have filed a fraudulent reimbursement claim shall return
the full amount improperly credited, with applicable interest and penalties,
without prejudice to prosecution under existing laws.

SEC. 22. Data Privacy, Security, and System Integrity. — All agencies and
covered providers processing personal information under this Act shall
implement appropriate organizational, physical, and technical security measures.

Access to the Registry shall be role-based and auditable.

Every material access, modification, validation, or benefit transaction shall
generate an electronic audit trail.

Data-sharing agreements shall clearly specify:

(a) purpose;

(b) lawful basis;

(c) categories of information exchanged;

(d) authorized users;

(e) retention periods;

(f) security standards; and

(g) accountability for unauthorized disclosure or misuse.

No data gathered pursuant to this Act shall be used for purposes unrelated to
implementation, evaluation, audit, law enforcement involving fraud, or other
purposes expressly authorized by law.



                                                                             24
SEC. 23. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Council, in consultation with the Department of
Finance, Department of Budget and Management, Bureau of Internal Revenue,
concerned regulators, local government units, consumer representatives, private
service providers, and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing
rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

The IRR shall include:

(a) detailed beneficiary classification rules;

(b) sector-specific benefit ceilings;

(c) consumption thresholds;

(d) benefit sequencing and anti-duplication rules;

(e) Registry procedures;

(f) provider verification and reimbursement procedures;

(g) grievance and appeals mechanisms;

(h) audit and anti-fraud systems;

(i) pilot evaluation methodology;

(j) fiscal monitoring and reporting rules; and

(k) data protection and cybersecurity standards.




                                                                                25
SEC. 24. Funding. — Subject to the Annual Fiscal Envelope and applicable
laws, implementation may be funded from:

(a) duly authorized appropriations;

(b) reprogrammed savings and unobligated balances of concerned agencies, when
legally available;

(c) excess dividends from government-owned or -controlled corporations;

(d) incremental revenues from excise taxes on tobacco, alcohol, sweetened
beverages, and similar products, consistent with existing earmarking laws;

(e) contingent and standby funds authorized under the General Appropriations
Act for social protection and inflation mitigation; and

(f) such other sources as may be authorized by law.

Tax expenditures arising from provider reimbursement shall be separately
accounted for and disclosed.

No provision of this Act shall be construed as creating a permanent entitlement
or automatic appropriation.

SEC. 25. Periodic Program Review. — Every three (3) years from the
commencement of nationwide implementation, the Department of Economic
Planning and Development, in coordination with the Department of Finance and
the Council, shall conduct a comprehensive review of the program.

The review shall determine:

(a) whether the twenty percent (20%) baseline discount remains appropriate;


                                                                              26
(b) whether benefit ceilings remain equitable and fiscally sustainable;

(c) whether beneficiary classifications remain responsive to household
vulnerability;

(d) whether the cost-sharing mechanism remains workable;

(e) whether the program produces measurable improvements in household
welfare; and

(f) whether any provision should be continued, modified, expanded, reduced, or
terminated.

The findings shall be submitted to the President and Congress and made publicly
available.

SEC. 26. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

SEC. 27. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

SEC. 28. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
