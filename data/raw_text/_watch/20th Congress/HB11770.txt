                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article XIV, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution provides that the State
shall protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all
levels and shall take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to
all. Corollary to this mandate is the responsibility of the State to ensure that
the learning environment in schools and classrooms is conducive for learning.

      In May 2026, the Second Congressional Commission on Education
(EDCOM 2) heard from junior high school students in Manila whose regular
classes hold about sixty (60) learners, and whose school starts classes as early
as 5:30 in the morning to accommodate them.1 An article from The Asia
Foundation reported that studies show that overcrowded classrooms not only
make it difficult for learners to concentrate on their lessons, but also limit the
amount of time teachers can spend on improving their teaching methods.
Teachers in congested classrooms are also generally over-stretched and are
more likely to suffer from burnout.2 EDCOM 2 likewise found that
overcrowded classrooms fuel bullying, disruption, and absenteeism.3

      The scale of the problem is documented. The Department of Education
(DepEd) placed the classroom backlog at more than 144,000 as of January
2026.4 Figures released in October 2026 show that 68,147 additional
classrooms are needed to decongest 4,492 schools with more than fifty (50)
learners per classroom, and 52,871 more to end double and triple shifting in

      Yet no law sets how many learners a single teacher may be made to
handle. The Magna Carta for Public School Teachers, enacted in 1966, limits
teaching hours but is silent on class size. Without a legal yardstick, there is
no fixed measure against which classroom construction and teacher hiring
can be planned, funded, and held to account.

        The problem on school congestion is multi-faceted and requires various
solutions on different fronts. In 2026, the national budget gave the DepEd a
record one trillion fifteen billion pesos (PHP 1.015 trillion), including sixty-five
billion pesos (PHP 65 billion) for new classrooms,7 and the DepEd targets the
hiring of 32,916 new teachers in 2026.8 This bill seeks to standardize the
number of learners in a class to serve as the guidepost in formulating plans
and strategy for the country's education sector. It sets a standard of thirty-
five (35) learners and a maximum of fifty (50), to be attained within five (5)
school years under a National Classroom and Teacher Complement Plan that
puts the most congested schools and the poorest municipalities first. It also
guarantees that no learner will be turned away because a class is full. This
bill also seeks to compensate public school teachers who handle large classes
through Large Class Allowance that increases with every learner above the
standard, while the government simultaneously addresses other aspects of
the school congestion problem.

     It is for the foregoing premises that the approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
  PROVIDING FOR THE STANDARD AND MAXIMUM CLASS SIZES IN
PUBLIC BASIC EDUCATION SCHOOLS, PROVIDING FOR THEIR PHASED
    ATTAINMENT, AND INSTITUTIONALIZING THE LARGE CLASS
 ALLOWANCE FOR PUBLIC SCHOOL TEACHERS, AND APPROPRIATING
                     FUNDS THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Class Size
Act.”

        SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of
the State to protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education
at all levels and to take appropriate steps to make such education accessible
to all. It is likewise declared a policy of the State to promote and pursue a
classroom environment conducive for learning in all stages of education. The
State shall ensure and safeguard the welfare of both the learners and teachers
in the classroom. Toward this end, the State shall progressively reduce class
sizes in public schools by expanding the supply of classrooms and teachers,
and shall not, in doing so, deny or delay the enrollment of any learner. Lastly,
the State shall ensure that public school teachers shall be properly
compensated in unavoidable cases of large class size.

        SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

           (a) Class refers to a group of learners officially enrolled in the same
               grade level and section who receive instruction from a single
               teacher at the same time and in the same learning space;
           (b) Class size refers to the number of learners in a class as reflected
               in the official enrollment records of the Department of Education
               (DepEd) as of the reckoning date set by the DepEd for each school
               term;
         (c) Public school refers to any school offering basic education from
             Kindergarten to Grade 12 that is owned and operated by the
             national government through the DepEd, or by a local
             government unit (LGU); and
         (d) Teacher refers to any person engaged in actual classroom
             instruction in a public school, whether holding a permanent,
             provisional, or substitute appointment, and whether paid by the
             national government or by an LGU.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all classes in all public
kindergarten, elementary, junior high, and senior high schools. This Act shall
cover all public-school teachers, whether paid by the national government or
LGUs. The DepEd shall issue appropriate learner-to-facilitator standards for
the Alternative Learning System consistent with the purposes of this Act.

      SECTION 5. Standard Class Size. – The standard class size to be
handled by a single teacher in public schools shall be thirty-five (35) learners
for Grades 1 to 12, and twenty-five (25) learners for kindergarten. A class at
or below the applicable standard shall be considered standard for purposes
of this Act. The DepEd shall prescribe lower standard class sizes for
multigrade classes and for classes that include learners with disabilities,
consistent with Republic Act No. 11650, which shall in no case exceed the
standards set in this Section.

     SECTION 6. Large Class. – A class whose size exceeds the applicable
standard class size but does not exceed the applicable maximum class size
under Section 7 of this Act shall be considered a large class.

      SECTION 7. Maximum Class Size. – Upon full implementation of this
Act under Section 9, no class in public schools shall exceed fifty (50) learners
for Grades 1 to 12, or thirty-five (35) learners for Kindergarten.

      After full implementation, the maximum class size may be exceeded
only when a calamity, armed conflict, or other emergency destroys or renders
unusable school facilities or causes a sudden influx of displaced learners,
upon written authority of the DepEd Regional Director concerned, and for a
period not exceeding one (1) school year. Every such authority shall be
reported to the Secretary of Education and addressed in the next update of
the Plan under Section 9 of this Act.

      SECTION 8. Protection of Learners’ Access to Education. – No learner
shall be refused enrollment, made to wait for enrollment, or transferred to
another school without the consent of the learner’s parent or guardian, on the
ground that a class has reached the standard or maximum class size.

     Shifting schedules adopted to comply with this Act shall observe the
minimum instructional time and the safe school hours prescribed by the
DepEd, taking into account the age, travel, and security of learners.
       SECTION 9. Phased Implementation; National Classroom and Teacher
Complement Plan. – The maximum class size under Section 7 of this Act shall
be attained in all public schools not later than the fifth (5th) school year
following the effectivity of this Act.

       For this purpose, the DepEd, in coordination with the Department of
Budget and Management (DBM), the Department of Public Works and
Highways (DPWH), the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development
(DEPDev), the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and
the leagues of LGUs, shall formulate, within six (6) months from the effectivity
of this Act, a National Classroom and Teacher Complement Plan, hereinafter
referred to as the Plan, which shall contain:

         (a) A school-by-school inventory of class sizes, classrooms, teachers,
             and shifting arrangements;
         (b) Annual targets for classroom construction and for the creation
             and filling of teaching positions needed to attain the maximum
             class size within the period provided in this Section, and to
             progressively attain the standard class size thereafter;
         (c) Interim decongestion measures that do not reduce instructional
             time below the minimum prescribed by the DepEd; and
         (d) The estimated annual cost of each target.

       In allocating classrooms and teaching positions under the Plan, priority
shall be given to schools with classes exceeding the maximum class size;
schools operating on double or multiple shifts or using makeshift learning
spaces; Kindergarten to Grade 3 classes; and schools in geographically
isolated and disadvantaged areas and in lower-income municipalities as
classified under Republic Act No. 11964.

       The Plan shall be updated annually, made publicly available, and
reflected in the annual budget proposal of the DepEd.

      SECTION 10. Large Class. – A teacher who handles a large class, or a
class exceeding the maximum class size, shall be entitled to a Large Class
Allowance equivalent to one percent (1%) of the teacher’s daily rate for every
learner in excess of the applicable standard class size, for every day of actual
instruction. Where a teacher handles a class for only part of the day, the
allowance for that class shall be prorated according to the share of the
teacher’s six (6)-hour daily teaching load spent with that class.

      The Large Class Allowance shall be paid monthly. It shall be in addition
to the salary, the additional compensation under Republic Act No. 4670, the
teaching allowance under Republic Act No. 11997, and all other benefits to
which the teacher is entitled, and shall be exempt from income tax. It shall
not be treated as an honorarium.

      The allowance of teachers paid by LGUs shall be charged against the
Special Education Fund of the LGU and Local School Board (LSB) concerned.
Large classes shall be distributed equitably among the teachers of a school,
and no teacher shall be assigned a large class as a penalty or reprisal.

       SECTION 11. Monitoring and Public Reporting. – The DepEd shall
maintain, through its learner information system, current data on the size of
every class in every public school. Not later than the end of the first term of
every school year, the DepEd shall publish a report on class sizes by school,
division, and region, including the number of large classes, classes exceeding
the maximum class size, and schools on shifting schedules, and the progress
of the Plan. The report shall be submitted to the appropriate committees of
both Houses of Congress and made available to school governing councils and
parent-teacher associations.

      SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DepEd, in
coordination with the DBM, the Civil Service Commission, the DPWH, and the
DILG, and in consultation with teachers’ organizations, parent-teacher
associations, and learners’ organizations, shall formulate the implementing
rules and regulations within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act.

       SECTION 13. Appropriations and Initial Implementation. The amount
necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be charged against
the current appropriations of the DepEd. Thereafter, the amount necessary
for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act. The Large Class Allowance under Section 10 of this Act
shall first be granted in the school year beginning after the effectivity of this
Act.

      SECTION 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act, or part
hereof, is held invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or
provision not otherwise affected shall remain valid and subsisting.

      SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, presidential decrees,
orders, and issuances, including DepEd issuances on the organization of
classes, or portions thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
