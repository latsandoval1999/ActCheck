                            AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING AN EMERGENCY MEDICAL SERVICES SYSTEM (EMSS),
      CREATING FOR THE PURPOSE THE NATIONAL EMSS COUNCIL,
              AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The right to health is a fundamental right guaranteed under Section 15, Article II of the
1987 Constitution of the Philippines. However, access to timely and effective emergency
medical care remains uneven across the country. In many communities—particularly in
rural areas, geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs), and densely
populated urban centers—emergency response services are fragmented, inadequately
resourced, or entirely unavailable. Delays in emergency response often mean the
difference between life and death for victims of accidents, disasters, sudden illness, and
other life-threatening situations.

At present, emergency medical services (EMS) in the Philippines are provided by a mix
of local government units (LGUs), hospitals, private ambulance providers, volunteer
organizations, and non-government institutions. While many of these entities provide
invaluable services, the absence of a unified national framework results in inconsistent
standards, insufficient coordination, varying levels of training among responders, and
unequal access to emergency medical care across localities.

This bill seeks to address these gaps by institutionalizing a comprehensive Emergency
Medical Services System (EMSS) that will standardize and strengthen emergency
response throughout the country. The proposed measure aims to establish a National
Emergency Medical Services Systems Council (EMSSC) under the Department of the
Interior and Local Government (DILG), which will serve as the central policymaking and
coordinating body for EMS nationwide. The Council will develop national standards,
treatment protocols, training guidelines, and operational requirements for emergency
medical personnel, ambulance services, dispatch centers, and related systems.
The measure also aims to professionalize and expand the EMS workforce by creating
plantilla positions for emergency medical services personnel in government hospitals and
local government units. Through standardized training and accreditation systems, the bill
ensures that emergency responders possess the necessary competencies to provide both
basic and advanced life support during medical emergencies.

Furthermore, the bill institutionalizes the use of 911 as the single nationwide emergency
hotline to streamline public access to emergency assistance. It mandates local
governments to establish dispatch centers and maintain adequate emergency transport
vehicles with trained personnel to ensure prompt response and safe patient transport to
appropriate medical facilities.

In recognition of the Philippines’ vulnerability to natural disasters and other emergencies,
the proposed EMSS framework also strengthens coordination among government
agencies, private institutions, and civil society organizations. This integrated approach
will ensure a faster, more efficient, and more reliable response during disasters,
accidents, and public health emergencies.

Ultimately, the institutionalization of a national Emergency Medical Services System will
improve survival rates, reduce preventable deaths and disabilities, and promote equitable
access to life-saving emergency care for all Filipinos. By creating a standardized,
coordinated, and well-resourced EMS network, this measure advances the State’s
commitment to protecting the health, safety, and welfare of the people.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 3
 4   SECTION 1. Short Title. —This Act shall be known as the "Emergency Medical Services
 5   System (EMSS) Act".
 6
 7   Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
 8   and promote the right to health of the people. Pursuant to this policy, the government
 9   shall institutionalize a comprehensive, accessible, integrated and standardized system of
10   emergency medical services and provide an environment that will maximize the capability
11   and potential of emergency medical services personnel.
12
13   Sec. 3. Objectives. - This Act has the following objectives:
14      a. To create a national Emergency Medical Services System Council;
15      b. To encourage and promote the active participation of the private sector in the
16         provision of emergency medical services;
17      c. To encourage and promote the active participation of the private sector in the
18         provision of emergency medical services;
19      d. To establish a national standard for emergency medical services including, but not
20         limited to, accreditation of EMS institutions and/or training of EMS personnel,
21         establishing standards for design, accreditation and regulation of emergency
22         medical vehicles;
23      e. To institutionalize the use of 911 as the Nationwide Emergency Hotline Number;
 1      f. To adopt an emergency medical service protocol in times of natural and man-made
 2         disasters;
 3      g. To promote public safety and accessible emergency medical services to the people
 4         especially those in the under-served and unserved communities; and
 5      h. To establish and provide support services, both internal and external, to emergency
 6         medical services.
 7
 8   Sec. 4. Definition of Terms - As used this Act:
 9      a. Accredited Training and Assessment institutions - refer to organizations, in good
10         standing with the Emergency Medical Service System Council (EMSSC), accredited
11         to offer training programs, courses, continuing education and competency-based
12         assessment in emergency medical services for MS personnel that meet the
13         standards established by the EMSSC, hereinafter referred to as the Council created
14         under this Act and, in case of uniformed personnel, in coordination with the
15         Philippine Public Safety College (PPSC).
16
17      b. Ambulance/Emergency Medical Vehicle - refers to a vehicle designed and equipped
18         for transporting sick or injured patients to, from, and between places of treatment
19         by land, water or air, affording safety and comfort to the patients and avoiding the
20         aggravation of illness or injury.
21
22      c. Basic Life Support (BLS) - refers to a group of actions and interventions used to
23         resuscitate and stabilize victims of cardiac and respiratory arrest. These BLS
24         actions and interventions include the recognition of a cardiac or a respiratory
25         emergency or stroke, activation of the emergency response system, CP and relief
26         of foreign-body airway obstruction.
27
28      d. Competency-Based Assessment - refers to the confirmation of an EMS personnel's
29         capability and capacity to perform one's duties and responsibilities, through the
30         process of collective evidence and judgment, in accordance with the standards and
31         guidelines established by the Council.
32
33      e. Emergency Medical Dispatch - refers to the immediate identification and
34         prioritization of emergency situations, the timely dispatch of appropriate resources,
35         providing essential pre-arrival medical instructions and full endorsement to the
36         receiving hospital. Dispatch encompasses all aspects of communication including
37         request processing, coordination and support, documentation and monitoring.
38
39      f. Emergency Medical Services (ENS) refer to:
40
41         1) Emergency Care - refers to the independent delivery of pre-hospital emergency
42            medical services by appropriately trained and certified MS personnel, usually in
43            a mobile or community setting, in full accordance with the Emergency Medical
44            Services Treatment Protocols established by the Emergency Medical Services
45            System Council (EMSSC), hereinafter referred to as the Council, created under
46            this Act.
47
 1      2) Advance Life Support - refers to a set of life saving protocols and skills that
 2         extend beyond BLS to further support the circulation and provide an advanced
 3         airway and adequate ventilation.
 4            i. Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ALS) refers to a group of interventions
 5               used to treat and stabilize adult victims of life-threatening
 6               cardiorespiratory emergencies and to resuscitate victims of cardiac
 7               arrest.
 8           ii. Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS) refers to a group protocol for
 9               managing trauma victims and designed to maximize management in the
10               first hour after trauma and ensure an optimal long-term outcome.
11          iii. Pediatric Advanced Life Support (PALS) refers to a system of critical care
12               procedures and facilities, such as intensive care nursery, for the basic
13               and advanced treatment of seriously ill or injured infants and children.
14          iv. Neonatal Advanced Life Support (NALS) refers to a group of
15               interventions for the basic and advanced treatment of injured and
16               critically ill newborn infants.
17
18   g. Emergency Medical Services Personnel refers to a trained and certified personnel
19      engaged in the provision of emergency medical services during emergencies.
20
21   h. Emergency Medical Services (EMS) System refers to the arrangement and
22      coordination of personnel, facilities, and equipment for the effective delivery of
23      EMS required in the management of medical emergencies; for the management
24      and prevention of further incidents or accidents and the broad range of emergency
25      care from emergency care to transport in an intensive care setting.
26
27   i. Emergency Medical Technician refers to the EMS personnel who provide basic
28      emergency medical care and transportation for critical and emergent patients who
29      access emergency medical system.
30
31   j. Emergency Response and Care refers to the arrival of resources at the scene and
32      the timely initiation and provision of appropriate medical interventions.
33
34   k. Emergency Transport refers to the transporting of a patient to the most appropriate
35      and definitive health facility with continued provision of care and appropriate
36      interventions en route to the appropriate health facility.
37
38   l. Inter-Facility Referral and Transport refers to the transport of patient with medical
39      escort, from one referring facility or agency to another receiving facility or agency
40      for definitive care, as the patient requires, in an event that further appropriate
41      services are not available in the referring facility.
42
43   m. Medical Direction refers to the communication between an EMS personnel and a
44      physician from the field via radio or other means to obtain instruction on further
45      care of a patient.
46
 1      n. Medical Emergency refers to any acute or life-threatening condition that requires
 2         immediate intervention by competent medical personnel.
 3
 4      o. National Emergency Medical Services Treatment Protocols refer to emergency
 5         medical procedures outlining approved clinical practices and therapies to be
 6         observed by EMS personnel, as established by the Council created under Section
 7         5 hereof.

 9                                CHAPTER II
10                 EMERGENCY MEDICAL SERVICES SYSTEMS COUNCIL
11
12   Sec. 5. Creation of the Emergency Medical Services Systems Council. - A body to be
13   known as the Emergency Medical Services Systems Council (EMSSC) is hereby created
14   under the Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG).
15
16   Sec. 6. Powers and Functions of the EMSSC - The Council shall perform the following
17   functions:
18      a. To formulate policies on EMS systems;
19      b. To develop national standards of emergency medical services to include, among
20          others, the skills and competencies required for EMS personnel and the
21          development of mandatory national emergency medical services treatment
22          protocols to be observed by EMS personnel and such other entities as it may
23          consider appropriate;
24      c. To promulgate a Code of Ethics for EMS personnel;
25      d. To develop appropriate standards of operation for EMS personnel;
26      e. To develop standards and protocols for the design, accreditation, construction,
27          outfitting and operations of emergency medical vehicles; and
28      f. To ensure the establishment of a system of networking and coordination among
29          all existing government health agencies, LGUs, and private and non-government
30          medical institutions for the effective implementation of this Act;
31      g. To establish a Secretariat, pursuant to Section 9 of this Act, under an Executive
32          Director for the administrative and day to day operations of the Council;
33      h. To create committees and other mechanisms to help expedite the implementation
34          of plans and strategies;
35      i. To      generate     resources     from     local,  national     and  international
36          organizations/agencies, whether government or private sector, for its operation;
37          and receive and accept donations and other conveyances including funds,
38          materials and services by gratuitous title: Provided, That not more than thirty
39          percent (30%) of said funds shall be used for administrative expenses;
40      j. To prepare an annual budget of the Council, through the DILG, and submit the
41          same to the President for inclusion in the annual General Appropriations Act;
42      k. To advise the President on matters pertaining to EMS;
43      l. To investigate complaints of motu propio and impose penalties for the violation
44          against any of the provisions of this Act;
45      m. To request any department, instrumentality, office, bureau or agency of the
46          government, including LGUs, to render such assistance as it may require in order
47          to carry out, enforce or implement the provisions of this Act;
 1      n. To regulate activities inimical to the delivery of emergency medical services; and
 2      o. To promulgate rules and regulations and policies and enforce the provisions of this
 3         Act.

 6   the following:
 7

 9         Council;
10      b. The Secretary of the Department of Health (DOH) or duly authorized

12      c. The Secretary of the Department of Information and Communications Technology
13         (DICT) or duly authorized representative;

21         technicians (EMT's) who will undergo a nomination and selection process by the
22         Council;
23

25   No. 56-2018, and the Director General of Technical Education and Skills Development
26   Authority (TESDA), shall be required to participate in all meetings of the Council. Relevant
27   stakeholders from the different government offices, private entities and civil society
28   organizations (CSOs) who are involved in the practice and implementation of emergency
29   medical services can be invited as resource persons or representatives in the Council's
30   meetings.
31
32   Sec. 8. Compensation and Remuneration. - The Secretaries of the DILG, DOH and DICT
33   shall perform their duties as such without compensation or remuneration, subject to
34   reasonable per diem allowances as approved by the Council and subject to existing rules
35   and regulations of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM). Members thereof
36   who are not government officials or employees shall be entitled to necessary traveling
37   expenses, per diem and representation allowances chargeable against funds of the DILG,
38   as approved by the Council, subject to existing rules and regulations of the DBM.
39
40   Sec. 9. The Secretariat. - The Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP), an attached agency of the
41   DILG, shall act as the Secretariat of the Council. The BFP shall be strengthened by
42   augmenting its additional manpower and budget for its operations.
43
44   Sec. 10. Meetings. - The Council shall meet at least once every quarter.
45
46   Sec. 11. Program Plans. - The Council shall within six (6) months after having been
47   officially constituted and finally staffed, adopt and immediately cause to be implemented,
 1   in coordination with medical and related agencies, a short-range program in support of
 2   relevant existing projects and activities and, within one (1) year, a long-range five (5)-
 3   year development program. This development program shall be developed and subjected
 4   to annual review and revision by the Council, in coordination with relevant public and
 5   private medical agencies and organizations.
 6
 7   Sec. 12. Accreditation. - The Council shall issue authorization for the following:
 8      a. Accreditation of training institutions for EMS personnel; and
 9      b. Accreditation of s emergency medical vehicle providers.

13                     EMERGENCY MEDICAL SERVICES PERSONNEL
14
15   Sec. 13. Creation of Plantilla Positions for Emergency Medical Services Personnel. – There
16   shall be created a minimum number of plantilla positions for MS personnel in the following
17   government hospitals/health facilities within the next five (5) years upon approval of this
18   Act:
19      a. Level 3 Hospitals - Five (5) EMS personnel and at least one (1) Ambulance
20          Assistant;
21      b. Level 1 and 2 Hospitals - Three (3) EMS personnel and at least one (1) Ambulance
22          Assistant;
23      c. Local Government Units —
24            1) Highly-urbanized city (HUC) - Ten (10) MS personnel and at least one (1)
25               Ambulance Assistant
26            2) Component city - Eight (8) EMS personnel and at least one (1) Ambulance
27               Assistant;
28            3) First to third class municipality - Four (4) EMS personnel and at least one (1)
29               Ambulance Assistant;
30            4) Fourth to sixth class municipality - Two (2) EMS personnel and at least one (1)
31               Ambulance Assistant; and
32      d. Other Health Facilities - as may be deemed necessary by the Council.
33
34   Upon the approval of the necessary plantilla positions for EMS personnel, the Council shall
35   submit a proposed qualification standard for positions to the Civil Service Commission
36   (CSC) for its approval, pursuant to E.O. No. 292 entitled, "The Revised Administrative
37   Code of 1987 on the Civil Service Commission". The annual financial requirements needed
38   to pay for the salaries of EMS personnel shall be included in the annual general
39   appropriations of the respective hospitals, health facilities and LGUs.
40
41   Sec. 14. Authorized Training institution. - Training programs, courses and continuing
42   education for an EMS personnel shall be conducted by an institution that has been granted
43   a Certificate of Program Registration (COPR) by the TESDA, in case of technical non-
44   degree courses falling under the TESDA jurisdiction, and the Philippine Public Safety
45   College (PPSC) for uniformed personnel. The requirements prescribed by the Council shall
46   serve as the minimum requirement for program registration. The DOH can provide
 1   training programs for EMS personnel: Provided, That these shall be in accordance with
 2   the standards set by the Council.
 3
 4                                   CHAPTER IV
 5                        EMERGENCY MEDICAL SERVICES SYSTEM
 6
 7   Sec. 15. Emergency Medical Vehicles. - The Council shall develop minimum requirements
 8   for the design, construction, performance, equipment, testing and appearance of
 9   emergency medical vehicles. As such, only emergency medical vehicles shall be allowed
10   to display the word "Ambulance" and the universally-accepted "Star of Life" symbol. It
11   shall also provide for the operation protocols of said vehicles. The Council shall design a
12   specification and an accreditation systems and procedures, subject to the existing
13   regulations of the DOH on licensure of medical vehicles.
14
15   Sec. 16. Emergency Dispatch. - All local government units are mandated to establish
16   their dispatch centers where constituents can call for all cases of emergencies, with
17   adequate and qualified personnel. The dispatch centers shall follow the prescribed
18   guidelines on dispatch protocol as determined by the Council.
19
20   Sec. 17. Emergency Response, Care, and Transport. - All LCDs shall ensure the
21   availability of adequate emergency transport vehicles or ambulances with qualified EMS
22   personnel. All emergency transport vehicles and the procedures to be undertaken in
23   responding and caring for patients shall follow the prescribed guidelines of the Council
24   on ambulance services and on emergency response and care.
25
26   Sec. 18. Inter-facility Referral and Transport. - The Council shall establish the prescribed
27   protocols/guidelines on inter-facility referral and transport, in accordance with appropriate
28   medical direction.

33   Sec. 19. Adoption of a Free National Emergency 911 Hotline Number. – There shall only
34   be one (1) free national emergency 911 hotline number to enable the public to access
35   emergency medical services. The operations of the national emergency 911 hotline
36   number shall be under the DILG, in coordination with the Council.
37
38   Sec. 20. Compliance. - It shall be the duty of every telecommunication company to
39   provide its subscribers with access to the national emergency 911 hotline number in
40   accordance with the implementing rules and regulations to be adopted pursuant to this
41   Act.
42
43   Sec. 21. Prohibited Acts and Penalties on Emergency Communications. —
44      a. Any person making a telephone call to an emergency hotline number who annoys,
45          abuses, threatens or harasses any person who is answering the telephone call
46          shall be guilty with the first offense and, subject to subsection (c) of this section,
47          shall be compelled to attend a seminar on the proper use of the nationwide
 1            emergency hotline number. Upon commission of the offense for the second time,
 2            the offender shall, upon conviction, be imposed with a fine of not less than Five
 3            Hundred Pesos (Php 500.00) but not more than One Thousand Pesos (Php
 4            1,000.00). Upon commission of the offense for the third and succeeding times,
 5            the offender shall, upon conviction, be imposed with a penalty of imprisonment
 6            of not less than one (1) month but not more than six (6) months or a fine of not
 7            less than Two Thousand Pesos (Php 2,000.00) but not more than Five Thousand
 8            Pesos (Php 5,000.00), or both, at the discretion of the court.
 9
10       b. Any person making a telephone call to an emergency hotline number and, upon
11          the call being answered, making or soliciting any comment, request, suggestion,
12          proposal or sound which is obscene, lewd, lascivious, filthy or indecent, shall be
13          mandated to attend a seminar on the proper use of the nationwide emergency
14          hotline number for the first offense. Upon commission of the offense for the
15          second time, the offender shall, upon conviction, be imposed with a fine of not
16          less than Five Hundred Pesos (Php 500.00) but not more than One Thousand
17          Pesos (Php 1,000.00). Upon commission of the offense for the third and
18          succeeding times, the offender shall, upon conviction, be imposed with a penalty
19          of imprisonment of not less than one (1) month but not more than six (6) months
20          or a fine of not less than Two Thousand Pesos (Php 2,000.00) but not more than
21          Five Thousand Pesos (Php 5,000.00), or both, at the discretion of the court.
22
23       c.   A person giving false report of a medical emergency or giving false information
24            in connection with a medical emergency, or making false alarm of a medical
25            emergency, knowing the report or information or alarm to be false; or making a
26            false request for ambulance service to an ambulance service provider, knowing
27            the request to be false, shall be mandated to attend a seminar on the proper use
28            of the nationwide emergency hotline number for the first offense. Upon
29            commission of the offense for the second time, the offender shall, upon
30            conviction, be imposed with a fine of not less than Two Thousand Pesos
31            (Php 2,000.00) but not more than Five Thousand Pesos (Php 5,000.00) and
32            payment of damages. Upon commission of the offense for the third and
33            succeeding times, the offender shall, upon conviction, be imposed with a penalty
34            of imprisonment of not less than one (1) year but not more than three (3) years
35            or a fine of not less than Five Thousand Pesos (Php 5,000.00) but not more than
36            Ten Thousand Pesos (Php 10,000.00) and payment of damages, or both, at the
37            discretion of the court.
