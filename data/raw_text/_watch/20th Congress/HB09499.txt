                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Food security, rural poverty reduction, and agricultural modernization remain
among the most pressing development priorities of the Philippines. While the
country is endowed with rich agricultural and fishery resources, many small farmers
and fisherfolk continue to face structural challenges such as limited market access,
weak bargaining power, high post-harvest losses, and dependence on layers of
intermediaries that significantly reduce farmgate incomes.

       The “Farm-to-Table Food Hubs Act” seeks to address these long-standing
inefficiencies by establishing an integrated, localized, and institutionalized system
of agricultural aggregation, processing, distribution, and market linkage through the
creation of Farm-to-Table Food Hubs (FTFHs) in 5th and 6th class municipalities.
These hubs are envisioned as strategic facilities that directly connect local producers
with institutional buyers such as government feeding programs, schools, hospitals,


                                                                                       1
 and private sector markets, thereby shortening the supply chain and improving both
 producer income and consumer access to affordable food.

        The bill adopts a whole-of-government and multi-sectoral approach by
 designating the Department of Agriculture as the lead implementing agency, in
 coordination with key government institutions such as the Department of the Interior
 and Local Government, Department of Trade and Industry, Department of
 Education, Department of Health, Department of Social Welfare and Development,
 and the Cooperative Development Authority, among others. It likewise recognizes
 the critical role of Local Government Units, cooperatives, associations, civil society
 organizations, and private sector partners in ensuring the effective implementation
 and sustainability of the program.

        The bill further strengthens market access for local producers by encouraging
 institutional procurement through accredited food hubs, consistent with existing
 procurement laws and regulations. It also supports the development of value-adding
 activities, logistics systems, and post-harvest infrastructure to reduce losses and
 improve competitiveness in agricultural and fishery sectors.

        By modernizing agricultural market systems and institutionalizing farm-to-
 market integration at the local level, the State affirms its commitment to food
 security, poverty reduction, and sustainable rural development.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title - This Act shall be known as the “Farm-to-Table Food
Hubs Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote food security, inclusive economic growth, rural development, and



                                                                               1
sustainable agricultural modernization by strengthening direct market access for
local farmers and fisherfolk.


The State recognizes the vital role of farmers, fisherfolk, agrarian reform
beneficiaries, rural workers, and local agricultural enterprises in ensuring a stable
and sufficient food supply, reducing poverty in rural communities, and supporting
national economic resilience. Toward this end, the State shall develop efficient,
accessible, and community-based food aggregation and distribution systems that
enable local producers to directly supply institutional buyers and consumers.


The State shall likewise promote equitable and transparent agricultural supply
chains, reduce excessive intermediary costs, minimize post-harvest losses, improve
farmgate prices, and encourage value-adding activities that increase the income and
competitiveness of local producers.


Consistent with the principles of local autonomy and participatory governance, the
State shall empower Local Government Units (LGUs), in partnership with
cooperatives, associations, civil society organizations, and the private sector, to
establish and manage Farm-to-Table Food Hubs that strengthen local economies and
enhance community food systems.


The State further recognizes the importance of sustainable food systems and shall
support innovations in food logistics, storage, processing, marketing, and
institutional procurement to improve the efficiency, resilience, and long-term
viability of local agricultural and fishery industries.




                                                                                   2
SEC. 3. Objectives. - This Act seeks to:


(a) Establish and strengthen LGU-managed or LGU-designated Farm-to-Table Food
Hubs in 5th and 6th class municipalities as centralized aggregation, storage,
processing, distribution, and marketing centers for locally produced agricultural and
fishery products;


(b) Improve direct market access and income opportunities for farmers, fisherfolk,
agrarian reform beneficiaries, cooperatives, and other local producers by facilitating
sustainable linkages with institutional buyers, local markets, and consumers;


(c) Promote local food security and supply chain resilience by strengthening
community-based food distribution systems and encouraging localized sourcing of
agricultural and fishery products;


(d) Reduce post-harvest losses, excessive intermediary costs, and supply
inefficiencies through improved logistics, storage, handling, and market
coordination;


(e) Encourage institutional procurement and long-term market arrangements
between local producers and public or private buyers, consistent with existing
procurement laws and regulations;


(f) Support value-adding activities, product quality improvement, food safety
compliance, and agricultural competitiveness through technical assistance, training,
and infrastructure support; and


                                                                                    3
(g) Strengthen rural development and local economic growth by promoting inclusive
participation of cooperatives, associations, civil society organizations, and private
sector partners in the operation and sustainability of Farm-to-Table Food Hubs.


SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
hereby defined:


(a) Farm-to-Table Food Hub (FTFH) refers to an LGU-established or LGU-
designated aggregation, storage, processing, distribution, and marketing center that
facilitates the direct linkage of local farmers and fisherfolk with institutional buyers
and consumers;


(b) Local Producers refer to individual farmers, fisherfolk, agrarian reform
beneficiaries, rural workers, and duly registered cooperatives, associations, or
community-based organizations engaged in the production of agricultural and
fishery products within the municipality or adjacent localities;


(c) Institutional Buyers refer to public and private entities purchasing agricultural
and fishery products in bulk or on a regular basis, including schools, hospitals,
hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, public markets, government feeding programs,
and similar establishments;


(d) Food Safety Standards refer to the applicable sanitation, handling, storage,
transport, and quality control requirements prescribed by the Department of




                                                                                      4
Agriculture (DA), the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and other relevant
government agencies;


(e) Farmgate Price refers to the price received by farmers or fisherfolk for their
products at the point of production, prior to transportation, trading, or retail
distribution;


(f) 5th and 6th Class Municipalities refer to municipalities classified as such
pursuant to existing laws, rules, and regulations issued by the Department of Finance
(DOF) or other competent government authorities;


(g) Cooperative or Association refers to any duly registered farmers’ or fisherfolk
cooperative, association, organization, or federation recognized by the Cooperative
Development Authority (CDA), the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE),
or other relevant government agencies; and


(h) Institutional Procurement refers to the sourcing or purchasing of agricultural and
fishery products by government agencies, government-owned or controlled
corporations, private institutions, and other entities for feeding programs,
commercial operations, or organizational use.


SEC. 5. Lead Implementing Agency and Inter-Agency Coordination. - The
Department of Agriculture (DA) shall serve as the lead implementing agency of this
Act and shall oversee the planning, coordination, implementation, monitoring, and
evaluation of the Farm-to-Table Food Hubs program nationwide. In carrying out its




                                                                                    5
functions under this Act, the DA shall coordinate with the following agencies and
entities:


   1. The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), for
       coordination with Local Government Units (LGUs) and local development
       planning;
   2. The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), for market linkage
       development, enterprise support, and institutional buyer engagement;
   3. The Department of Education (DepEd), Department of Health (DOH),
       Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), and other
       government agencies implementing feeding, nutrition, and institutional
       procurement programs;
   4. The Cooperative Development Authority (CDA), for cooperative organization
       strengthening and capacity-building support;
   5. State universities and colleges (SUCs), technical-vocational institutions, and
       other relevant entities for training, research, and technical assistance; and
   6. Farmers’ and fisherfolk cooperatives, associations, civil society organizations,
       and private sector stakeholders for program implementation and
       sustainability.


The DA may establish an inter-agency technical working group or coordinating
council to ensure harmonized implementation of this Act and to facilitate policy
coordination, capacity-building, and market integration initiatives.


The DA shall develop and maintain a national monitoring and information system
for Farm-to-Table Food Hubs, including data on production volume, market

transactions, participating producers, institutional procurement, and other relevant
indicators necessary for program assessment and policy development.


SEC. 6. Establishment of Farm-to-Table Food Hubs. -
(a) Subject to feasibility assessments and the availability of funds, every 5th and 6th
class municipality shall, individually or jointly with adjacent municipalities,
establish, designate, or operationalize at least one Farm-to-Table Food Hub (FTFH)
to serve as an aggregation, storage, processing, distribution, and marketing center
for locally produced agricultural and fishery products.


(b) Existing public markets, trading posts, agricultural terminals, warehouses, cold
storage facilities, or other suitable government-owned facilities may be upgraded,
designated, or integrated as Farm-to-Table Food Hubs for purposes of this Act.


(c) Each FTFH shall:


   1. Serve as a centralized collection and consolidation point for agricultural and
      fishery products sourced from local producers;
   2. Facilitate post-harvest handling and related activities, including cleaning,
      sorting, grading, packaging, labeling, storage, and basic processing to
      maintain product quality and reduce losses;
   3. Facilitate direct marketing, negotiated supply agreements, institutional
      procurement arrangements, and other market linkage activities between local
      producers and institutional buyers;
   4. Support food safety compliance, pricing transparency, and efficient logistics
      and distribution systems.


                                                                                     7
(d) The Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the Department of the
Interior and Local Government (DILG) and other relevant agencies, shall prescribe
minimum standards and operational guidelines for the establishment, accreditation,
management, and operation of Farm-to-Table Food Hubs to ensure food safety,
logistical efficiency, accessibility, and long-term sustainability.


SEC. 7. Role of Local Government Units. - The Local Government Units (LGUs)
shall:


   1. Identify, provide, or facilitate access to land, facilities, utilities, and other
         infrastructure necessary for the establishment and operation of Farm-to-Table
         Food Hubs;
   2. Maintain an updated registry of local farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives,
         associations, agrarian reform beneficiaries, and other eligible producers
         within their jurisdiction;
   3. Facilitate coordination between local producers and institutional buyers to
         ensure stable and timely supply arrangements;
   4. Provide administrative, technical, and logistical support necessary for the
         effective operation and sustainability of the food hubs.


Preference shall be given to duly registered farmers’ and fisherfolk cooperatives,
associations, and community-based organizations in the management, operation, and
administration of Farm-to-Table Food Hubs, subject to applicable laws, rules, and
regulations.




                                                                                     8
The LGUs may enter into partnerships, joint ventures, or other cooperative
arrangements       with   cooperatives,   associations,   accredited   non-government
organizations, civil society organizations, educational institutions, and private
enterprises for the development, operation, financing, and sustainability of the food
hubs.


The LGUs shall encourage the participation of local producers, cooperatives, and
community stakeholders in planning and decision-making processes related to the
operation and management of Farm-to-Table Food Hubs.


SEC. 8. Institutional Procurement and Market Linkages. -


(a) Government agencies, government-owned or controlled corporations (GOCCs),
state universities and colleges (SUCs), public hospitals, schools, feeding programs,
and other government institutions procuring agricultural and fishery products shall,
whenever practicable and consistent with existing procurement laws, rules, and
regulations, prioritize the sourcing of locally produced agricultural and fishery
products through accredited Farm-to-Table Food Hubs established under this Act.


(b) Local Government Units (LGUs) are encouraged to adopt local procurement
policies and market linkage programs that support farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives,
and associations participating in Farm-to-Table Food Hubs, subject to existing laws
and regulations.


(c) The Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the Department of
Budget and Management (DBM), the Government Procurement Policy Board


                                                                                    9
(GPPB), the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), and other relevant agencies,
shall formulate guidelines to facilitate institutional procurement arrangements,
community-based sourcing mechanisms, negotiated procurement modalities where
authorized by law, and simplified accreditation procedures for eligible local
producers and cooperatives.


(d) The DA and DTI shall assist Farm-to-Table Food Hubs, cooperatives, and
participating producers in developing long-term supply agreements, market
contracts, pricing transparency mechanisms, and buyer-seller matching programs
with public and private institutional buyers.


(e) Nothing in this Act shall be construed to exempt any procuring entity from
compliance with applicable procurement, auditing, food safety, and competition
laws and regulations.


SEC. 9. Accreditation, Food Safety, and Quality Standards. -


(a) All Farm-to-Table Food Hubs established or designated under this Act shall
comply with applicable food safety, sanitation, quality assurance, post-harvest
handling, storage, transport, and traceability standards prescribed by the Department
of Agriculture (DA), the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the Department of
Health (DOH), and other relevant government agencies.


(b) The DA, in coordination with the FDA, DTI, DILG, and other concerned
agencies, shall establish an accreditation system for Farm-to-Table Food Hubs,




                                                                                  10
participating cooperatives, associations, and producers to ensure compliance with
operational, food safety, and product quality standards.


(c) Accreditation standards shall include, but shall not be limited to:


   1. Proper sanitation and hygiene practices;
   2. Appropriate storage, cold chain, and transport systems where necessary;
   3. Product grading, packaging, labeling, and traceability measures;
   4. Compliance with applicable environmental and waste management
      regulations;
   5. Record-keeping and inventory management systems; and
   6. Other standards necessary to ensure product quality, consumer protection, and
      efficient market operations.


(d) The DA shall provide technical assistance, training, and capacity-building
programs to assist Local Government Units (LGUs), cooperatives, associations, and
participating producers in complying with accreditation and food safety
requirements.


(e) No Farm-to-Table Food Hub shall engage in institutional procurement
arrangements under this Act unless duly accredited in accordance with the standards
and procedures established by the DA.


(f) The DA may periodically inspect and evaluate Farm-to-Table Food Hubs and
may suspend or revoke accreditation for substantial noncompliance with the
standards prescribed under this Act and its implementing rules and regulations.


                                                                                  11
SEC. 10. Digital Market Systems and Information Platform. -


(a) The Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT), the Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI), Local Government Units (LGUs), and other relevant agencies, shall
develop, establish, or support digital market systems and information platforms for
Farm-to-Table Food Hubs established under this Act.


(b) The digital market systems may include, but shall not be limited to:


   1. Product listing and inventory management systems;
   2. Market matching platforms connecting producers, cooperatives, food hubs,
      institutional buyers, and consumers;
   3. Price monitoring and market information services;
   4. Supply forecasting and demand coordination mechanisms;
   5. Digital ordering, scheduling, and logistics coordination systems;
   6. Traceability and quality assurance monitoring systems; and
   7. Electronic record-keeping and data management systems for operational and
      reporting purposes.


(c) The DA and DICT shall promote accessible, user-friendly, and low-cost digital
technologies appropriate for rural communities and small-scale producers, including
mobile-based platforms and offline-compatible systems where practicable.




                                                                                12
(d) LGUs and Farm-to-Table Food Hubs shall encourage the registration and
participation of local producers, cooperatives, and institutional buyers in the digital
market systems established under this Act.


(e) The DA, in coordination with concerned agencies, shall provide capacity-
building programs, technical assistance, and digital literacy training to participating
LGUs, cooperatives, associations, and local producers to support the effective use
of digital market systems.


(f) All data collection and processing activities conducted pursuant to this Act shall
comply with applicable data privacy, cybersecurity, and information management
laws, rules, and regulations.


SEC. 11. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting. -


(a) The Department of Agriculture (DA) shall establish and implement a
comprehensive monitoring and evaluation (M&E) system for all Farm-to-Table
Food Hubs established under this Act.


(b) The M&E system shall track and assess, among others, the following indicators:


   1. Volume and value of agricultural and fishery products consolidated,
      processed, and distributed through Farm-to-Table Food Hubs;
   2. Income levels and farmgate price improvements of participating farmers,
      fisherfolk, and cooperatives;
   3. Reduction in post-harvest losses attributable to food hub operations;


                                                                                    13
   4. Number and type of institutional buyers engaged and volume of procurement
      facilitated through the hubs;
   5. Operational efficiency and financial sustainability of Food Hubs;
   6. Participation rates of local producers, cooperatives, and associations; and
   7. Compliance with food safety, accreditation, and quality standards.


(c) All LGUs operating Farm-to-Table Food Hubs shall submit regular reports to the
DA in accordance with guidelines to be issued in the Implementing Rules and
Regulations (IRR), including data on operations, procurement activities, financial
performance, and program outcomes.


(d) The DA shall prepare and submit an annual consolidated report to the President
of the Philippines, the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the
Congress of the Philippines, detailing the implementation status, accomplishments,
challenges, and recommendations for the improvement of the program.


(e) The DA shall maintain a centralized database of all Farm-to-Table Food Hubs,
participating producers, accredited cooperatives, and institutional buyers, which
shall be updated regularly and used as a basis for policy formulation and program
improvement.


(f) The Commission on Audit (COA) shall have access to all records, reports, and
databases relevant to the implementation of this Act for audit and evaluation
purposes in accordance with existing laws and regulations.


SEC. 12. Appropriations and Funding Sources. -

   4. Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs), joint ventures, and other lawful private

      related facilities;

   5. Loans, grants, or technical assistance from government financial institutions,

   6. Donations, endowments, and other lawful contributions from private entities

(d) The Department of Agriculture, in coordination with the Department of Budget

farmer and fisherfolk organizations, and LGU leagues, shall formulate the
implementing rules and regulations (IRR) necessary to carry out the provisions of
this Act.


SEC. 14. Separability Clause. - If any part or provision of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.
