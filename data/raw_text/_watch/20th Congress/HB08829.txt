                           AN ACT
CREATING THE NATIONAL COMMISSION ON DISABILITY AFFAIRS, DEFINING
ITS POWERS, FUNCTIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES, AND APPROPRIATING
FUNDS THEREFOR AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article XIII, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution provides that “[t]he Congress
shall give highest priority to the enactment of measures that protect and enhance the
right of all the people to human dignity, reduce social, economic, and political
inequalities, and remove cultural inequities by equitably diffusing wealth and political
power for the common good.” Moreover, the Philippines is a signatory to the United
Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD), which
obligates that “States Parties undertake to ensure and promote the full realization of
all human rights and fundamental freedoms for all persons with disabilities without
discrimination of any kind on the basis of disability.”

        Based on the Department of Health (DOH) data as of June 2025, the National
Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA) reported that over 2.5 million Filipinos are now
registered as persons with disabilities (PWD).1 Of the 2.5 million Filipinos with
disabilities, 827,189 have physical disabilities that limit the function of their motor
abilities, while others have invisible disabilities, including speech impairment
(335,763), psychosocial disability (314,210), visual disability (251,302), mental
disability (152,762), intellectual disability (137,491), deaf/hard of hearing (129,021),
cancer (110,880), learning disability (102,623), and rare disease (49,112). 2 Across 17
regions in the country, the number of registered persons with disabilities increased
from 2024 to 2025, with a national increase of 35% from 1.8 million to 2.5 million.3

Government agencies attribute this increase in registered persons with disabilities to
better reporting mechanisms and greater accessibility of health services primarily
provided by the local government units (LGUs),4 with the NCDA in recent years striving
to create a centralized national database of persons with disabilities through
collaborations with the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) and
LGUs. 5

        However, despite the efforts of the NCDA and the numerous laws aimed at
improving the welfare of persons with disabilities in the country, the reality remains
that many Filipinos with disabilities still do not have adequate access to basic needs,
such as healthcare, education, employment, and proper interventions required for their
disabilities. Among the reasons for this is the NCDA’s need for stronger mechanisms
to address complaints and ensure the rights of persons with disabilities are upheld, as
admitted by NCDA Executive Director Glenda Relova.6 According to Relova, “persons
with disabilities are among the most vulnerable sectors—together with senior citizens
and children—in many aspects such as health, well-being, and economic
opportunity.”7 As such, there is an urgent need for stronger collaboration among
agencies and a robust mechanism to ensure that programs and services effectively
reach the sector.8

       This bill seeks to establish the National Commission on Disability Affairs,
replacing the National Council on Disability Affairs. As a Commission, the National
Commission on Disability Affairs shall be strengthened in its role as the primary
agency responsible for the nationwide implementation of policies aimed at fulfilling the
rights and improving the welfare of the PWD sector.

        Compared to the present Council that merely serves as the lead agency in
policymaking and in addressing issues and concerns related to disability affairs but
ultimately does not directly implement programs, the Commission shall lead in the
implementation of the policies, programs, and services concerning persons with
disabilities across the country. The Commission shall also lead the conduct of a
National Registration Program for Persons with Disabilities through the Philippine
Statistics Authority to gather comprehensive data on persons with disabilities vital for
policy formulation, and serve as the main research arm of the government on PWD
concerns and central repository of data on the sector. Furthermore, the Commission
shall oversee and recommend measures for the effective conduct of a comprehensive
assessment program on a provincial, city, and municipal level.

       In the 17th and 18th Congresses, this Representation filed similar bills in the

 1         SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as “National Commission
 2   on Disability Affairs Act of 2026”.
 3
 4           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State guarantees full respect for the rights
 5   of every human being, regardless of their status in life. Likewise, the State recognizes
 6   that persons with disabilities (PWDs) are entitled to the same rights as persons without
 7   disabilities. It is also the policy of the State to give highest priority to the enactment of
 8   measures that will protect, support, and promote the rights of persons with disabilities.
 9
10          SEC. 3. Creation of the National Commission on Disability Affairs. – There is
11   hereby created the National Commission on Disability Affairs, hereinafter referred to
12   as the Commission. The Commission shall be independent, autonomous, and shall
13   have the same status as that of a national government agency attached to the Office
14   of the President.
15
16           SEC. 4. Mandate. – The Commission shall be the central national government
17   agency that attends to the needs of persons with disabilities, providing them with the
18   necessary services, supervision, regulation, and guidance in the pursuit of their
19   welfare and well-being. Likewise, the Commission shall be the lead policy-making,
20   planning, monitoring, and coordinating body that develops, formulates, and
21   implements programs for the prevention of the causes of disability, rehabilitation, and
22   equalizing of opportunities in the concept of a rights-based society for persons with
23   disabilities.

 2         SEC. 5. Powers and Functions. – The Commission shall exercise the following
 3   powers and functions to carry out its mandate:
 4
 5            (a) Ensure that the status, rights, welfare, and interests of persons with
 6                disabilities are upheld, in accordance with the Constitution, the UN
 7                Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD) and
 8                applicable laws;
 9            (b) Formulate policies and propose legislation, as well as regulations,
10                concerning the rights and well-being of persons with disabilities, and lead
11                in the implementation of programs and services concerning the same;
12            (c) Conduct consultative meetings and symposia, including summits and
13                dialogues, with all stakeholders, and undertake program evaluation and
14                monitoring to ensure that comprehensive, relevant, and timely programs
15                and services are adequate and accessible to persons with disabilities;
16            (d) Identify and address gaps in the implementation and enforcement of
17                domestic laws and international commitments benefiting persons with
18                disabilities;
19            (e) Conduct relevant researches and studies for policy formulation to
20                promote and enhance, at all levels, the rights of persons with disabilities;
21            (f) Establish, develop, and maintain a data bank on disability through a
22                National Registry of PWDs which shall be obtained through the National
23                Registration Program for PWDs, on a provincial, city, and municipal-wide
24                level, in partnership with concerned government agencies and non-
25                governmental organizations, and strengthen referral services to ensure
26                availability of data on stakeholders and services to persons with
27                disabilities, including provision of assistive devices;
28            (g) Establish and maintain linkages and networking with local and
29                international organizations, including organizations of and for persons
30                with disabilities, to generate resources, maximize utilization of existing
31                resources, and achieve a level of convergence most beneficial to
32                persons with disabilities;
33            (h) Assist in the establishment of self-help organizations and the setting up
34                of specific projects through the provision of technical and financial
35                assistance to draw out the active participation of persons with disabilities
36                for the social and economic development of the country;
37            (i) Create, organize, restructure, and otherwise manage such offices of the
38                Commission;
39            (j) Provide guidance to Persons with Disability Affairs Offices (PDAO) of
40                local government units for the expeditious and effective fulfillment of their
41                mandate and for specific thematic and functional matters;
42            (k) Submit annual reports to both Houses of Congress on the processes and
43                implementation of policies; and
44            (l) Exercise such powers or perform such other functions as may be
45                delegated or assigned by the President.

18         SEC. 7. Duties and Responsibilities of a Chairperson. – The Chairperson shall
19   have the following duties and responsibilities:

21             (a) Direct and supervise the affairs of the Commission in accordance with
22                 its policies, programs, rules and regulations;

24                 of the Commission;

32             (g) Execute contracts in the pursuit of the Commission’s objectives as may
33                 be authorized by the Commission, in accordance with government rules

35             (h) Organize permanent or ad-hoc committees, consisting of members of
36                 the Commission or such other experts as are deemed necessary for the
37                 full execution of the functions and objectives of the Commission.

20           SEC. 10. Qualifications. – No person shall be appointed Chairperson, Executive
21   Director, or Deputy Executive Director of the Commission unless they are a citizen and
22   resident of the Philippines, of good moral character, of known probity, of proven
23   integrity, and with at least seven (7) years of understanding, expertise, competence,
24   and experience in delivering services to persons with disabilities.

26          SEC. 11. Divisions. – To achieve the mandates stated above, the Commission
27   shall have the following Divisions:
28
29             (a) Finance and Administrative Division – This Division shall be responsible
30                 for providing efficient and effective services relating to personnel,
31                 information, records, supplies, equipment, collection, disbursement,
32                 security, and other matters.
33             (b) Policy Formulation and Research Division – The Policy Formulation and
34                 Research Division shall be responsible for the formulation of policy
35                 recommendations based on surveys, researches, and studies relating to
36                 disability-related issues and concerns. In line with this, the Division shall
37                 undertake continuing research, study and statistics updating on issues
38                 related to persons with disabilities, with the purpose of recommending
39                 reliable policy reforms, development programs, and administrative and
40                 legislative measures that will address the economic, social, and cultural
41                 rights of persons with disabilities and further enhance their quality of life.
42                 This Division shall be the repository of all processed data concerning
43                 disability topics and shall maintain a National Registry for PWDs.
44             (c) Program and Project Division – The Programs and Project Division shall
45                 plan, conduct, and evaluate national programs on the prevention of the
46                 causes of disabilities, rehabilitation, and equalization of opportunities.
47                 The Division shall provide technical assistance through training and
48                 other related activities concerning disability. It shall likewise develop
49                 strategic framework, guidelines, and standards on program/project
50                 development, management, monitoring and evaluation. The Division

19           SEC. 12. Establishment of Regional Offices. – There shall be Regional Offices
20   for the NCDA. Each Regional Office shall be headed by a Regional Manager who shall
21   supervise the official activities of all provinces, cities, and municipalities under the
22   jurisdiction of the concerned Regional Office. They shall be the primary coordinating
23   and overseeing authority over the Persons with Disability Affairs Office (PDAO), duly
24   established by local government units (LGUs).
25

32                 Commission through coordination with local government units;
33             (c) Coordinate with the LGUs and ensure that each has its established
34                 PDAO or has designated Focal Persons for the fourth (4th), fifth (5th),
35                 and sixth (6th) class municipalities; and

 8          SEC. 14. Annual Report. – The Commission shall prepare an annual report on
 9   the findings of its monitoring activities with regard to the state of the country’s
10   compliance with its international obligations concerning persons with disabilities. The
11   report shall include, but not be limited to the following:
12
13             (a) Identification of causes that result in the increase or decrease in the
14                 number of persons with disabilities;
15             (b) Assessment of the legal mechanisms of existing institutions in providing
16                 adequate protection and services for persons with disabilities;
17             (c) Recommendations for legal, legislative, and institutional reforms for
18                 greater promotion and protection of the rights of persons with disabilities;
19             (d) Statistics and data indicating the total number of persons with disabilities
20                 in the country as culled from the National Registry for PWDs, in
21                 coordination with other government agencies.
22
23           SEC. 15. Disability Support Fund. – A Disability Support Fund shall be
24   established under this Act. It shall make funds available for individuals with disabilities
25   to access rehabilitation and disability support services, and to support disability-related
26   activities in the local community through programs and projects of the local
27   government units and civil society organizations.
28
29          The fund shall be sourced from the operations of the Philippine Charity
30   Sweepstakes Office (PCSO), Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation
31   (PAGCOR), one (1%) percent from the collections arising from the Sin Tax and
32   amusement taxes collected by other national and local government agencies, and
33   contributions from the private sector. The shares from the collections in sin and
34   amusement taxes shall be automatically remitted to the Disability Support Fund,
35   subject to regulations and guidelines of the Department of Budget and Management
36   and the Department of Finance (DOF).
37
38          SEC. 16. Transitory Provisions. – The National Council on Disability Affairs
39   (NCDA) is deemed abolished upon the creation of the Commission. All the powers,
40   functions, assets, liabilities, capital, accounts, contracts, equipment, and facilities of
41   the NCDA which are owned by the government shall be transferred to the
42   Commission.
43
44           All regular or permanent employees of the NCDA shall be absorbed or
45   transferred to the Commission: Provided, that they possess the necessary
46   qualifications, and shall not suffer any loss of seniority or rank or decrease in salaries
47   and emoluments.
48
49         Employees separated from the service as a result of the abolition of the NCDA
50   under the provisions of this Act shall, within three (3) months from their separation
