                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       It has been more than two decades since the enactment of Republic Act No. 8485, or
the Animal Welfare Act of 1998, which laid down the foundation for the protection and
promotion of animal welfare in the country. It was then further amended by Republic Act No.
101631 in 2012.

        Unfortunately, there is an apparent disconnect between the intention behind the law, i.e.
to deter acts of cruelty, maltreatment and negligence toward animals, and the penalties
prescribed therein. Some may argue that the fines, which ranges from P30,000 to P100,000,
are merely a slap on the wrist as they fail to effectively discourage repeat offenses.

         This bill seeks to strengthen our policy on animal welfare protection by providing
stricter penalties for animal cruelty. It also introduces an accessory penalty of a perpetual ban
on owning any kind of animal for convicted offenders, to prevent recidivism.

        In relation to the accessory penalty of perpetual ban, this bill also proposes the
establishment of a National Animal Offender Registry. This will ease the monitoring of the
implementation of this Act.

        Animal welfare is not just a secondary concern; it is a reflection of our societal values.
By enacting these stricter measures, we send a clear message that cruelty will no longer be
tolerated and that the law will finally possess the necessary power to champion the lives of
those who cannot speak for themselves.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
