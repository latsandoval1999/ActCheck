                            AN ACT
PROVIDING A ONE-TIME AMNESTY FOR OTHERWISE VALID PHILHEALTH
BENEFIT CLAIMS DENIED SOLELY DUE TO LATE FILING, PRESCRIBING
SAFEGUARDS AGAINST FRAUDULENT CLAIMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

PhilHealth was created to help Filipinos get the medical care they need at the lowest
possible cost. Yet many valid PhilHealth claims are denied simply because they were
 iled late, even when the treatment was properly given and clearly covered. These
delays are often caused by paperwork issues, system changes, or other practical
problems beyond the control of hospitals or patients. When claims are denied for
technical reasons alone, hospitals may hesitate to accept PhilHealth patients without
advance payment, and ordinary Filipinos end up shouldering costs they should not
have to pay.

This bill recognizes that strict deadlines should not defeat the very purpose of health
insurance. The Supreme Court has already ruled that claim- iling deadlines are
administrative rules, not requirements set by law, and should be applied fairly and
 lexibly when public welfare is at stake. The Universal Health Care law also makes
clear that doubts in implementation must be resolved in favor of patients and that
existing bene its should not be reduced. Consistent with these principles, the bill
provides a one-time, limited amnesty for valid claims that were denied solely because
they were iled late.

By allowing these claims to be paid, while excluding fraudulent or improper claims,
the measure helps hospitals stay inancially stable and continue serving PhilHealth

restores trust in the system, and ensures that PhilHealth rules serve people, not
paperwork. This is a fair, practical solution that upholds the spirit of universal health

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Late-Filed PhilHealth
Claims Amnesty Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to ensure equitable
access to quality and affordable health care and to strengthen inancial risk protection
through the National Health Insurance Program (NHIP). Technical rules on claims
 iling should not, when rigidly applied, defeat the purpose of health insurance
coverage and frustrate payment for health services actually and properly rendered to
members.

At the same time, the State shall protect the National Health Insurance Fund by
preventing, detecting, and penalizing fraudulent claims and violations of NHIP rules.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
de ined:

   (a) “Bene its Claim” refers to a claim for payment or reimbursement of health
       services, goods, or bene its covered by PhilHealth, iled by an accredited health
       care provider or an eligible member, in accordance with PhilHealth rules.

   (b) “Late-Filed Claim” refers to a Bene it Claim that was denied solely because it
       was iled or re iled beyond the iling period prescribed under the

   (e) “Amnesty Period” refers to the one-time period within which eligible Late-

   (f) “Fraudulent Claim” includes any claim involving misrepresentation,

       regulations, including the Universal Health Care Act;

SECTION 4. Coverage; Eligible Claims. — This Act shall cover Late-Filed Claims that
satisfy all of the following:

   (a) The health services were actually rendered to a PhilHealth member who was
       eligible at the time of service;

   (b) The claim was denied solely on the ground of late iling;

   (c) The claim does not involve fraud, prohibited schemes, or violations of
       PhilHealth rules that render the services non-compensable; and

   (d) The claim pertains to discharges or services rendered within a period to be
       speci ied by Congress: Provided, That such period shall be clearly stated in the
       implementing rules and regulations, and shall in no case extend beyond the
       date immediately preceding the effectivity of this Act.

SECTION 5. One-Time Amnesty; Payment of Eligible Late-Filed Claims. —

   (a) Grant of Amnesty. — PhilHealth shall implement a one-time amnesty and shall
       receive, evaluate, and pay Eligible Claims under this Act, subject to veri ication
       and audit safeguards.

   (b) Filing Window. — Eligible claims shall be re iled or reactivated within one
       hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of the implementing rules and
       regulations (IRR). Claims not submitted within the iling window shall be
       barred.

   (c) Veri ication and Audit. — Payment shall be subject to:
       (i)     validation of member eligibility and service delivery;
       (ii)    clinical and claims evaluation; and
       (iii) post-audit under PhilHealth and Commission on Audit (COA) rules,
               with authority to suspend payment pending veri ication when red lags
               exist.

   (d) Mode of Payment. — Claims shall be paid in accordance with the bene it rates
       and payment rules applicable at the time the services were rendered, unless a
       more favorable rule to members is required under existing law and
       regulations.

SECTION 6. Exclusions. — The following shall not be covered by the amnesty:

   (a) Claims with indings or indicators of fraud, falsi ication, misrepresentation,
       padding, or participation in prohibited schemes;

   (b) Claims for services that are non-compensable under PhilHealth rules,
       including services performed in violation of PhilHealth policies;

   (c) Claims previously paid, partially paid, or already settled through compromise
       or inal adjudication on the merits other than late iling; and

   (d) Claims subject of pending criminal, administrative, or civil actions involving
       fraud or fund misuse, until inal resolution, without prejudice to payment if
       exonerated and otherwise eligible.

SECTION 7. Administrative Process; Due Process. —

   (a) Claims Resolution. — PhilHealth shall establish an expedited but fair process
       for receiving and resolving claims under this Act, including documentary
       requirements and timelines.

   (b) Appeal. — Denials under this Act shall be appealable within PhilHealth in
      accordance with its rules, provided that the IRR shall ensure speedy
      disposition.

SECTION 8. Reporting to Congress; Transparency. — Within one (1) year from the
effectivity of the IRR, and every six (6) months thereafter until completion of the
amnesty program, PhilHealth shall submit to Congress a report containing: (i) the
number and amount of claims received; (ii) the number and amount paid; (iii) the
number and amount denied and the grounds; (iv) lagged claims and actions taken;
and (v) fund impact.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, PhilHealth, in coordination with the Department of Health
and after consultation with accredited health care providers and stakeholders, shall
promulgate the IRR necessary to implement this Act.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
