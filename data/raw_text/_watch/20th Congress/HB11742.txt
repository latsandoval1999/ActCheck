                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Letter carriers, couriers, messengers, delivery workers, and other workers
engaged in the movement and delivery of letters, documents, parcels, goods, and
other lawful articles perform essential functions in sustaining commerce,
communication, government transactions, and the country's logistics and
delivery systems. Yet, despite the essential nature of their work, they are
routinely exposed to traffic accidents, extreme weather, fatigue, robbery, theft,
cargo-related risks, unsafe working conditions, and other occupational hazards.

       The growth of app-based and platform-enabled delivery services, as well
as the increasing participation of logistics companies and logistics service
providers in delivery and fulfillment operations, has further changed the nature
of this work. Many workers now perform substantially similar delivery and
messenger functions under different employment or contractual arrangements,
including through contractors, subcontractors, fleet operators, and digital
platforms. These arrangements should not result in the denial of basic safety,
welfare, and protection to workers performing essential delivery and logistics
functions.

       This measure therefore seeks to establish a comprehensive framework for
the safety, welfare, and protection of letter carriers, couriers, messengers,
delivery workers, platform-based delivery workers, in-house messengers,
logistics workers, and similarly situated workers.

       The proposed “Kartero Law of 2026” seeks to provide covered workers with
appropriate occupational safety and health measures, safety training, protective
equipment, access to social protection and insurance, protection against unfair
deductions and unreasonable financial liability, safeguards in the handling of
cash and cargo, protection against robbery and fraud, reasonable access to
delivery facilities, and appropriate mechanisms for addressing adverse actions
and workplace concerns.
       The measure likewise recognizes the realities of modern platform-based
work by promoting transparency in task assignment, compensation, incentives,
ratings, suspension and deactivation policies, and other automated systems that
materially affect a worker's ability to earn a livelihood. At the same time, it
respects existing laws and jurisprudence governing employment and contractual
relationships and does not, by itself, determine the employment status of any
covered worker.

       Recognizing the growing role of logistics companies and logistics service
providers, the bill expressly includes workers engaged or deployed by such
entities, including those performing delivery, courier, messengerial, sorting,
handling, dispatch, fulfillment, distribution, fleet, and related functions. It
likewise prevents employers, principals, contractors, subcontractors, logistics
service providers, platform operators, and other intermediaries from shifting
regulatory obligations to individual workers or using contracting arrangements
to circumvent the protections granted by the measure.

       The bill does not seek to deregulate the logistics or transportation sectors.
In particular, it preserves the existing jurisdiction of the appropriate government
agencies over transportation and logistics activities that are independently
subject to regulation. It merely clarifies that the performance of delivery, courier,
messengerial, parcel, last-mile delivery, fulfillment, dispatch, or related logistics
activities does not, by itself, constitute passenger transportation requiring an
LTFRB franchise or Certificate of Public Convenience. Existing requirements
applicable to truck-for-hire, freight or cargo transportation for hire, passenger
transportation, and other regulated transportation services remain unaffected.

       Finally, the measure seeks to strengthen coordination among the
Department of Labor and Employment, Department of Information and
Communications Technology, Department of Transportation and its attached
agencies, Philippine Postal Corporation, local government units, and other
concerned agencies, while ensuring that its provisions are harmonized with
existing labor, occupational safety and health, transportation, social protection,
data privacy, and other applicable laws.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                                       AN ACT
             PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF LETTER
             CARRIERS, COURIERS, MESSENGERS, AND DELIVERY
             WORKERS,        PROVIDING      FOR      THEIR      SAFETY,
             WELFARE, AND PROTECTION, AND FOR OTHER
             PURPOSES
      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

                                 CHAPTER I
                             GENERAL PROVISIONS

     Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Kartero Law of
2026.”

       Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to recognize
the essential role of letter carriers, couriers, messengers, delivery workers,
logistics workers, and app-based delivery personnel in sustaining commerce,
public communication, government transactions, and national logistics.

      Towards this end, the State shall:

      a. Protect covered workers from occupational hazards, traffic risks,
         extreme weather, excessive fatigue, unsafe working conditions, and
         other risks inherent in delivery, logistics, and messengerial work;

      b. Promote equal opportunity and non-discrimination in the workplace
         and in the performance of delivery, logistics, and messengerial services;

      c. Ensure reasonable protection for covered workers in relation to task
         assignment, compensation, performance evaluation, suspension,
         deactivation, and termination of work arrangements;
d. Promote access to appropriate training in road safety, occupational
   safety and health, data privacy, cargo handling, and emergency
   procedures;

e. Strengthen access to social protection and insurance programs
   consistent with the employment or contractual status of covered
   workers;

f. Protect covered workers from unfair financial liability arising from
   cash-on-delivery collections, cargo handling, robbery, theft, fraud,
   force majeure, and other circumstances beyond their reasonable
   control;

g. Promote safe and reasonable access to commercial and residential
   premises and appropriate facilities for workers engaged in delivery,
   logistics, and messengerial activities; and

h. Establish a coordinated framework among concerned government
   agencies for the effective implementation of this Act.

Section 3. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to:

a. Letter carriers;

b. Couriers and messengers;


c. Delivery workers, including personnel engaged in the delivery of food,
   groceries, medicines, documents, parcels, and other lawful goods;

d. In-house or corporate messengers and employees who are regularly or
   periodically assigned to perform messengerial, delivery, or field errands;

e. Platform-based or app-based delivery workers, subject to the provisions
   applicable to their employment or contractual status;

f. Workers affiliated with Private Express and/or Messengerial Delivery
   Service (PEMEDES) operators;

g. Workers engaged or deployed by logistics companies or logistics service
   providers in the performance of delivery, courier, messengerial,
   warehousing, dispatch, sorting, handling, transport, fulfillment,
   distribution, fleet, or other related logistics functions;
      h. Workers performing functions covered by this Act through contractors,
         subcontractors, manpower providers, fleet operators, logistics service
         providers, or other intermediaries; and

      i. Employers, principals, platform operators, logistics companies,
         logistics service providers, PEMEDES operators, contractors,
         subcontractors, and other entities engaging, contracting, or deploying
         covered workers, insofar as they have duties or obligations under this
         Act.

      Government personnel performing official messengerial or delivery
functions shall be covered by the applicable safety and welfare provisions of this
Act, subject to civil service laws, rules, and regulations.

      Section 4. Definition of Terms. –

      For purposes of this Act:

      a. Letter carrier refers to a person whose principal function involves the
         collection, transport, and delivery of letters, correspondence, or similar
         communications;

      b. Messenger refers to a person engaged in the collection, transport, filing,
         submission, delivery, or retrieval of documents, parcels, or other lawful
         articles on behalf of an employer, principal, organization, or client;

      c. Courier refers to a person engaged in the transportation and delivery of
         documents, parcels, goods, or other lawful articles for compensation;

      d. Delivery worker refers to a person engaged in the delivery of goods, food,
         groceries, medicines, documents, parcels, or similar lawful articles;

      e. In-house or corporate messenger refers to an employee whose regular
         or incidental duties include external messengerial, delivery, collection,
         filing, or field errands for the employer;

      f. Platform-based delivery worker refers to a person who performs delivery
         services through or by means of a digital platform or application that
         facilitates the matching, dispatch, assignment, or completion of
         delivery services;

      g. Digital labor platform refers to a digital service or application that
         facilitates the provision of delivery or other work services by connecting
         workers with customers, merchants, businesses, or other users;
      h. PEMEDES operator refers to a duly authorized private express and/or
         messengerial delivery service operator subject to applicable laws, rules,
         and regulations;

      i. “Logistics company” refers to a person or juridical entity principally
         engaged in logistics, supply-chain, freight, transportation of goods,
         warehousing, fulfillment, distribution, dispatch, or related services
         involving the movement, handling, storage, or delivery of goods,
         documents, parcels, or other lawful articles; and

      j. “Logistics service provider” refers to a person or entity engaged or
         contracted to provide one or more logistics or supply-chain services,
         including transportation, trucking, freight forwarding, warehousing,
         fulfillment, sorting, handling, dispatch, distribution, last-mile delivery,
         or related services, whether directly or through employees, contractors,
         subcontractors, or other service providers.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed as determining or altering the
existence of an employer-employee relationship. Such determination shall
continue to be governed by applicable laws, regulations, and jurisprudence.

                             CHAPTER II
           FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS AND WORKPLACE EQUALITY

      Section 5. Prohibition Against Discrimination. - No covered worker
shall be discriminated against in hiring, compensation, task allocation, access
to incentives, training, promotion, performance evaluation, suspension,
deactivation, or termination on account of age, sex, gender identity or
expression, sexual orientation, ethnicity, religion, disability, marital status,
union affiliation, or lawful organizational participation, consistent with existing
laws.

      Section 6. Fair Task Assignment and Platform Transparency. - Digital
labor platforms and other entities utilizing automated systems for task
assignment, performance evaluation, rating, or account management shall adopt
reasonable and transparent policies governing such systems.

      Covered workers shall be informed of the material grounds for suspension,
deactivation, or other adverse action affecting their ability to work and shall be
provided a reasonable mechanism to seek review or appeal.

      No covered worker shall be penalized solely for refusing a delivery or
assignment that presents a substantial and imminent risk to life, health, or
safety, subject to reasonable verification procedures.
       Section 7. Equal Access to Premises. - Property owners, building
managers, residential associations, establishments, and security personnel shall
not subject covered workers to discriminatory or degrading access requirements
solely because of their status as delivery personnel, messengers, or couriers.

      Legitimate and uniformly applicable security, identification, inspection,
and safety protocols shall remain valid.

                         CHAPTER III
     IN-HOUSE CORPORATE MESSENGERS AND FIELD ASSIGNMENTS

      Section 8. Protection of In-House Corporate Messengers. - Employees
who are regularly or periodically assigned to perform external messengerial or
field duties, including bank deposits, document filing, check collection,
government transactions, and similar activities, shall be entitled to the
applicable safety, training, insurance, and expense protections under this Act.

       Section 9. Personal Vehicle Use for Official Duties. - No employer shall
require an employee to use a personal motorcycle, bicycle, automobile, or other
private vehicle for official messengerial or delivery duties without reasonable
reimbursement of authorized transportation and field expenses actually incurred
in the performance of such duties.

      The employer may instead provide a company vehicle or an appropriate
transportation allowance pursuant to applicable law, company policy, or
employment agreement.

      Section 10. Field-Related Risks and Insurance. - Employers shall take
reasonable measures to protect employees performing regular external
messengerial duties against work-related accidents and risks.

      Where an employee is authorized to use a personal vehicle in the
performance of official duties, the employer shall provide or arrange appropriate
accident or third-party liability protection, subject to applicable insurance laws
and regulations.

                             CHAPTER IV
          SAFETY, TRAINING, AND OCCUPATIONAL PROTECTION

       Section 11. Mandatory Safety and Compliance Training. - Employers,
principals, digital platforms, logistics companies, logistics service providers, and
PEMEDES operators shall provide or facilitate appropriate initial and refresher
training for covered workers at no cost to the worker when such training is
required as a condition for the performance of the work.
      Training may include:

      a. Road safety and defensive driving;

      b. Accident prevention and emergency roadside procedures;

      c. Occupational safety and health;

      d. Data privacy and customer information handling;

      e. Proper handling and transport of packages and cargo;

      f. Recognition and reporting of hazardous or suspicious packages; and

      g. Other training requirements prescribed by the appropriate government
         agencies.

      Training required under this Act shall, whenever practicable, be integrated
with existing government-accredited training programs and shall be
implemented consistently with Republic Act No. 11058, otherwise known as the
“Occupational Safety and Health Standards Act,” and its implementing rules and
regulations.

      Section 12. Protection of Personal Information. - Covered workers shall
be trained and protected in the proper handling of personal information
appearing on delivery labels, documents, receipts, orders, and digital
applications in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the
“Data Privacy Act of 2012,” as amended.

    Digital platforms and employers shall, whenever practicable, use official
communication channels or privacy-protective mechanisms when facilitating
communication between workers and customers.

       Section 13. Handling of Hazardous or Suspicious Cargo. - Covered
workers shall not be required to knowingly transport articles that are prohibited
by law or that present a substantial and imminent danger to persons or property.
A worker who reasonably suspects that a package contains prohibited,
dangerous, or unlawful contents may decline its transport without adverse
employment or contractual consequences, subject to reasonable reporting and
verification procedures.

      Section 14. Inspection of Sealed Packages. - No covered worker shall be
compelled to open or inspect a sealed package except as expressly authorized by
law, applicable regulations, or with the consent of the person lawfully entitled to
authorize such inspection.
     Nothing in this Act shall prevent lawful inspection by competent
authorities pursuant to applicable laws and regulations.

      Section 15. Hours of Work and Fatigue Protection. - For covered
workers who are employees, hours of work, overtime, rest periods, and related
matters shall be governed by the Labor Code of the Philippines and other
applicable labor laws and regulations.

       For platform-based workers who are not employees under applicable law,
digital platforms and principals shall adopt reasonable safety measures to
prevent excessive fatigue and prolonged continuous delivery activity that may
endanger the worker or the public.

      No platform or principal shall design or implement an incentive scheme
that knowingly encourages covered workers to disregard applicable traffic,
safety, or occupational health requirements.

       Section 16. Night Work. - Employees who are legally entitled to night
shift differential shall receive the applicable benefit under existing labor laws.

      For platform-based workers who are not employees under applicable law,
any additional fee, premium, or compensation specifically provided for deliveries
during high-risk periods shall be transparently disclosed, including the amount
or portion payable to the worker where applicable.

       Section 17. Extreme Weather and Hazardous Road Conditions. -
Employers, principals, and digital platforms shall adopt appropriate procedures
during typhoons, floods, severe weather, dangerous road conditions, or other
officially recognized hazards.

      No covered worker shall be penalized, suspended, deactivated, or adversely
rated solely for refusing or discontinuing a delivery when continuing the activity
would pose a substantial and imminent risk to life, health, or safety.

       Section 18. Safety Gear and Protective Equipment. - Employers,
principals, logistics companies, logistics service providers, and digital platforms
shall provide appropriate safety equipment and protective gear necessary for the
safe performance of delivery and messengerial duties, consistent with
occupational safety and health and traffic regulations.

      Where personal protective equipment is required by applicable
occupational safety and health standards or by reason of the hazardous work
process or environment, such equipment shall be provided by the responsible
employer, contractor, subcontractor, principal, or other legally responsible entity
in accordance with Republic Act No. 11058 and its implementing rules and
regulations, without cost to the covered worker.
      Section 19. Emergency Medical and Roadside Assistance. - Employers,
principals, logistics companies, logistics service providers, and digital platforms
shall maintain reasonable mechanisms for emergency assistance and
coordination in cases of work-related road accidents, injuries, or other
emergencies occurring during the performance of authorized delivery or
messengerial duties.

                              CHAPTER V
                   SOCIAL PROTECTION AND INSURANCE

      Section 20. Social Security and Statutory Benefits. - Employees
covered by this Act shall continue to receive all mandatory benefits and statutory
coverage under existing labor and social protection laws.

      Employers shall comply with their obligations concerning SSS, PhilHealth,
Pag-IBIG, Employees' Compensation, and other mandatory programs.

      Section 21. Social Protection for Platform-Based Workers Who Are Not
Employees. - Digital platforms and principals engaging platform-based workers
who are not employees under applicable law shall facilitate, in accordance with
applicable laws and regulations, access to SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and other
government social protection programs in accordance with the applicable
membership, contribution, coverage, and registration rules of such programs.

      Digital platforms may provide mechanisms for the voluntary or legally
required deduction and remittance of contributions from worker earnings,
subject to applicable laws and regulations.

       Nothing in this Act shall be interpreted as imposing an employer-employee
relationship where none exists under law.

       Section 22. Accident Insurance. - Employers shall comply with their
obligations under existing Employees' Compensation and other applicable laws
for covered employees.

      For platform-based workers who are not employees under applicable law,
the responsible platform or principal shall provide or facilitate reasonable
accident insurance or equivalent accident protection in connection with delivery
work, subject to applicable laws and regulations.

      For platform-based workers, the responsible platform or principal shall
disclose the existence, scope, exclusions, and claims procedure of any accident
insurance or similar protection provided in connection with delivery work.
                             CHAPTER VI
       FAIR LIABILITY, CASH HANDLING, AND CARGO PROTECTION

       Section 23. Cash-on-Delivery and Remittance Procedures. -
Employers, principals, logistics companies, logistics service providers, and
platforms shall establish clear and secure procedures for cash-on-delivery
collections, petty cash, checks, documents, and other funds entrusted to covered
workers.

      Whenever practicable, digital payment, bank deposit, or other secure
remittance mechanisms shall be made available.

      Section 24. Protection Against Unfair Deductions and Holdbacks. - No
covered worker who is an employee shall be subjected to unauthorized or
unlawful wage deductions.

      For platform-based and other covered workers who are not employees
under applicable law, deductions, chargebacks, penalties, or holdbacks shall be
governed by transparent contractual or platform rules consistent with applicable
laws and shall not be imposed arbitrarily.

      A worker shall be given a reasonable opportunity to contest disputed
deductions arising from alleged cash shortages, delivery disputes, or similar
incidents.

      Section 25. Liability for Cash, Cargo, and Documents. - A covered
worker shall not be held personally liable for loss, damage, or non-remittance
arising from robbery, theft, fraud by third parties, force majeure, accident, or
other circumstances beyond the worker's reasonable control, provided that the
worker exercised reasonable care and promptly reported the incident.

       Nothing in this Act shall exempt a worker from liability arising from fraud,
willful misconduct, or gross negligence as established under applicable law and
after due process.

       Section 26. Protection Against Theft, Robbery, and Fraud. -
Employers, principals, logistics companies, logistics service providers, and
platform operators shall establish reasonable procedures for reporting and
addressing robbery, theft, fraudulent delivery instructions, scam transactions,
fake addresses, and similar incidents occurring during authorized delivery
activities.

     Appropriate insurance or risk protection for cargo, funds, and documents
may be required in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
      Section 27. Package Weight and Safety Standards. - The weight,
dimensions, packaging, and loading of goods transported by motorcycles,
bicycles, or other delivery modes shall comply with applicable transportation,
occupational safety, postal, and industry standards.

      The appropriate government agencies may establish or update standards
to prevent vehicle instability, excessive loading, and physical injury.

                              CHAPTER VII
                 ACCESS, REST, AND DELIVERY FACILITIES

       Section 28. Delivery Waiting and Rest Areas. - Commercial
establishments, malls, office complexes, residential condominiums, and other
establishments with substantial and regular delivery activity are encouraged to
provide, where reasonably practicable and consistent with available space and
safety requirements, designated waiting or receiving areas for delivery personnel.

        Where feasible, such areas may include access to drinking water, sanitary
facilities, charging facilities, and weather protection.

      Section 29. Reasonable Access to Premises. - Covered workers
performing legitimate delivery or messengerial duties shall be granted reasonable
access to premises subject to lawful security, identification, safety, and visitor-
management procedures.

      No establishment shall impose discriminatory access fees or degrading
treatment solely on account of a worker's occupation.

       Section 30. Loading and Unloading Areas. - Local government units and
appropriate public authorities are encouraged to establish reasonable short-term
loading and unloading areas for delivery vehicles and messenger motorcycles in
locations with significant delivery activity, subject to traffic management, road
safety, and local conditions.

       Private establishments may likewise designate appropriate loading and
receiving areas.

                            CHAPTER VIII
               PEMEDES AND OPERATOR RESPONSIBILITIES

      Section 31. Compliance with Existing PEMEDES Regulation. –
PEMEDES operators shall comply with all applicable laws, rules, regulations,
and licensing requirements governing their operations.
       The registration, licensing, identification, and authority-to-operate
requirements applicable to PEMEDES operators and persons required by law or
regulation to obtain PEMEDES authorization shall remain under the jurisdiction
of the appropriate government agency.

       Nothing in this Act shall be construed to create a separate or duplicative
licensing system for PEMEDES operators, logistics companies, or logistics
service providers.

      Section 32. Responsibilities of PEMEDES Operators. - PEMEDES
operators shall:

      a. Ensure that covered workers engaged in their operations satisfy
         applicable registration and licensing requirements;

      b. Provide or facilitate required safety and compliance training;

      c. Maintain appropriate procedures for social protection and insurance
         coverage;

      d. Establish secure procedures for COD collections and remittances;

      e. Adopt reasonable mechanisms for addressing worker complaints and
         disputes;

      f.   Refrain from shifting corporate licensing obligations, administrative
           penalties, or regulatory compliance costs to workers where such
           obligations are legally imposed upon the operator; and

      g. Comply with applicable labor, occupational safety, consumer
         protection, data privacy, transportation, and other laws.

      Logistics companies and logistics service providers shall, insofar as
applicable to their operations and their relationship with covered workers,
comply with the worker-protection, safety, training, insurance, cash-handling,
and other obligations imposed under this Act.

       Section 33. No Shifting of Regulatory Liability. - An employer,
principal, logistics company, logistics service provider, platform operator,
PEMEDES operator, contractor, subcontractor, or other intermediary shall not
transfer to an individual worker a regulatory obligation that is imposed by law
upon such employer, principal, logistics company, logistics service provider,
platform operator, PEMEDES operator, contractor, subcontractor, or other
entity.
       The engagement of a contractor, subcontractor, logistics service provider,
fleet operator, or other intermediary shall not be used for the purpose of
circumventing the rights and protections granted to covered workers under this
Act.

     This shall not prevent the recovery of amounts properly attributable to a
worker's fraud, willful misconduct, or gross negligence in accordance with law.

                            CHAPTER IX
             PLATFORM AND DIGITAL DELIVERY OPERATIONS

      Section 34. Transparency in Platform Policies. - Digital labor platforms
engaging covered delivery workers shall provide clear and accessible information
regarding:

      a. Compensation and applicable fees;

      b. Incentives and deductions;

      c. Task assignment and acceptance policies;

      d. Rating and performance systems;

      e. Suspension and deactivation policies;

      f. Procedures for contesting adverse decisions; and

      g. Insurance and social protection arrangements applicable to the worker.

      Section 35. Protection Against Arbitrary Suspension or Deactivation.
- A platform-based delivery worker shall not be arbitrarily suspended or
permanently deactivated without reasonable notice and an opportunity to seek
review, except when immediate action is reasonably necessary to protect public
safety, prevent fraud, or comply with law.

     Platforms shall establish reasonable procedures for the review of disputed
suspensions and deactivations.

       Section 36. Data Privacy and Algorithmic Accountability. - Digital
platforms shall process personal information of covered workers in accordance
with Republic Act No. 10173 and applicable regulations.

      Where automated systems materially affect task allocation, compensation,
rating, suspension, or deactivation, platforms shall maintain reasonable
measures to ensure that such systems are not used in a discriminatory or
manifestly arbitrary manner.
      Nothing in this Section shall be construed to require the disclosure of
source code, trade secrets, proprietary algorithms, or confidential business
information, except as may be lawfully required by a competent government
authority under applicable law.

                           CHAPTER X
             GOVERNMENT AGENCIES AND IMPLEMENTATION

      Section 37. Department of Labor and Employment. - The Department
of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall:

      a.   Exercise its existing jurisdiction over labor standards, occupational
           safety and health, and employee welfare;

      b.   Monitor compliance with applicable labor standards affecting covered
           employees;

      c.   Coordinate with relevant agencies regarding safety and welfare
           concerns affecting platform and delivery workers; and

      d.   Provide appropriate mechanisms for receiving and addressing
           complaints within its jurisdiction.

      Section 38. Department of Information and Communications
Technology. - The Department of Information and Communications Technology
(DICT) shall continue to exercise its existing regulatory functions over Private
Express and/or Messengerial Delivery Services (PEMEDES) and other matters
within its lawful mandate.

       The DICT shall coordinate with concerned agencies on digital registration,
verification, platform accountability, data protection, and other matters falling
within its legal mandate.

      Section 39. Department of Transportation and Attached Agencies. –
The Department of Transportation (DOTr), through its appropriate attached
agencies, shall continue to exercise its existing jurisdiction over road safety,
vehicle standards, driver's licensing, traffic-related matters, and other
transportation concerns.

       Nothing in this Act shall be construed to exempt any logistics company,
logistics service provider, employer, principal, platform operator, contractor,
subcontractor, or covered worker from any franchise, Certificate of Public
Convenience, accreditation, permit, registration, or other requirement applicable
to a transportation activity that is independently subject to the lawful
jurisdiction of the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board
(LTFRB).

        The provision of delivery, courier, messengerial, parcel, last-mile delivery,
fulfillment, dispatch, or related logistics services shall not, by itself and solely by
reason of the nature of such services, be construed as requiring a franchise,
Certificate of Public Convenience, or similar authority from the LTFRB where the
activity does not involve the operation of a transportation service independently
subject to LTFRB regulation under existing law.

      Nothing in this Section shall affect requirements applicable to truck-for-
hire operations, freight or cargo transportation for hire, passenger
transportation, or other transportation services that are otherwise subject to the
jurisdiction of the LTFRB under existing laws and regulations.

      Nothing herein shall likewise exempt any person or entity from motor
vehicle registration, driver's licensing, road safety, traffic, insurance,
occupational safety, local permits, or other requirements imposed by law.

      The DOTr and its attached agencies may coordinate with DOLE, DICT, and
local government units in developing safety standards applicable to delivery
operations, consistent with their respective mandates.

       Section 40. Local Government Units. - Local government units shall
continue to exercise their powers under existing law concerning traffic
management, loading and unloading areas, access to local roads, and regulation
of establishments within their respective jurisdictions.

      Section 41. Philippine Postal Corporation. - The Philippine Postal
Corporation (PHLPost) may provide technical assistance and expertise on postal
security, handling of correspondence, package classification, and other matters
within its mandate.

      Nothing in this Act shall transfer to PHLPost regulatory powers vested by
law in other government agencies.

      Section 42. Inter-Agency Coordination. - DOLE, DICT, DOTr, PHLPost,
concerned attached agencies, local government units, and other appropriate
government agencies shall coordinate, within their respective mandates, in the
implementation of this Act.

       An inter-agency body may be created or designated through the
implementing rules and regulations to facilitate coordination, consultation, and
policy development.
      The body shall include, where appropriate, representatives of covered
workers, recognized worker organizations, industry associations, and other
stakeholders.

                            CHAPTER XI
          APPROPRIATIONS, PENALTIES, AND FINAL PROVISIONS

     Section 43. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act of the concerned implementing agencies, subject to existing
budgeting, accounting, auditing, and procurement laws, rules, and regulations.

       Nothing in this Act shall require the creation of a new office or plantilla
position unless authorized by law and supported by an appropriate
appropriation.

      Section 44. Administrative Sanctions. – Violations of this Act shall be
subject to the administrative, civil, or other sanctions and remedies provided
under applicable laws.

      Enforcement of the provisions of this Act shall be undertaken by the
government agency having jurisdiction over the subject matter and the person
or entity concerned, consistent with existing law.

        The implementing rules and regulations may establish procedures for the
filing, investigation, referral, and resolution of complaints and for the imposition
of sanctions authorized by law, but shall not create new offenses or penalties not
provided by this Act or other applicable law.

     Nothing in this Section shall be construed to confer upon any government
agency jurisdiction beyond that provided by law.

      Repeated or serious violations may, where authorized by law, constitute
grounds for suspension or revocation of the appropriate license, accreditation,
or authority to operate.

      Section 45. Existing Rights and Remedies. - Nothing in this Act shall
be construed to diminish any right, benefit, protection, or remedy available to
covered workers under the Labor Code, social legislation, occupational safety
and health laws, data privacy laws, civil law, or other applicable statutes.

      Section 46. Harmonization with Existing Laws and Regulations. -
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to repeal, diminish, or modify the lawful
regulatory authority of the DOLE, DICT, DOTr and its attached agencies, local
government units, PHLPost, or other government agencies.
       The provisions of this Act shall be interpreted in a manner consistent with
existing laws and regulations governing employment, independent contracting,
platform work, transportation, postal services, PEMEDES, logistics, social
protection, occupational safety and health, data privacy, insurance, and other
applicable matters.

     Where another law provides a specific protection applicable to a covered
worker, such protection shall continue to apply.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed as deregulating the logistics,
transportation, courier, delivery, freight, or related industries, or as creating a
general exemption from any license, registration, permit, franchise, Certificate of
Public Convenience, or other regulatory requirement otherwise imposed by law.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed to repeal, amend, or modify any
special law or regulatory authority expressly preserved under this Act, except to
the extent expressly provided herein.

      Section 47. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOLE, DICT, and DOTr, in coordination
with PHLPost and other concerned agencies, worker organizations, industry
representatives, and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules
and regulations for its effective implementation.

      The implementing rules and regulations shall ensure that the
requirements of this Act are harmonized with existing laws, regulations, and
government programs.

       Section 48. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

      Section 49. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly:
Provided, that laws, rules, regulations, or issuances providing greater rights,
benefits, protections, or remedies to covered workers shall remain in full force
and effect.
      Nothing in this Section shall be construed to repeal, amend, or modify any
special law or regulatory authority expressly preserved under this Act.

      Section 50. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.

Approved.
