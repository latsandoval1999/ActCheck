28                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
29
30          This Bill amends Section 31 of Presidential Decree No. 1177, otherwise known
31   as the “Budget Reform Decree of 1977,” issued during the martial law period which
32   provides for automatic appropriation for public debt service. It likewise amends Section
33   26, Chapter 4, Book VI of Executive Order No. 292, otherwise known as the
34   “Administrative Code Of 1987,” which reiterates in toto Section 31 of PD 1177. These
35   laws provide for a no-questions-asked priority for debt servicing, a policy implemented
36   during martial law and throughout the post-EDSA 1 administrations, thereby limiting
37   the budget available for education as well as other social services.
38
39          The effective repeal of automatic appropriation for debt service remedies a long-
40   standing defect in the government’s policies on the budget—the indiscriminate payment
41   of all public debts including indefensible loans, regardless of the grave diversion of
42   finances from basic services. It affirms and asserts the obligation of the State under
43   Article II, Section 9 of the 1987 Constitution to “promote a just [social order]…through
44   policies that provide adequate social services.”

 1           The Bill’s passage will pave the way for a more rational and humane budgetary
 2   process. It will bring, annually, hundreds of billions of pesos within greater control of
 3   the people, rather than being paid for debt servicing. The excesses of past
 4   administrations—from the Marcos Sr. dictatorship until the Marcos Jr. administration—
 5   will no longer hold the country hostage, and only legitimate loans, not onerous and
 6   fraudulent debts, will be serviced. The government can begin to prioritize badly needed
 7   social services.
 8
 9           Since 1986, cumulative debt servicing has been consuming almost a third of the
10   annual national government budget. Meanwhile, the country’s public schools,
11   universities and colleges perennially suffer from decrepit facilities, teachers, textbook
12   and classroom shortages. Government hospitals are being closed, public health services
13   are a pittance, and vital medicines and treatments, especially for the poor, have become
14   inaccessible. Public provision for social goods and services are steadily being crippled,
15   with increasing reliance on the private sector, to the detriment of vulnerable sectors such
16   as the poor, senior citizens, and persons with disabilities.
17
18           The need to repeal the law on automatic appropriation for debt servicing has
19   become more urgent as the public debt has become an albatross for the nation. The
20   country’s debt is bloated by revenue losses from trade liberalization, overly generous
21   fiscal incentives for foreign investors, and the guarantee of profits as the government
22   assumes contingent liabilities. Debt-to-GDP ratio is still high at 65.2% as of March
23   2026.1 The government’s continued dependence on borrowing means that the debt
24   service burden is getting heavier and heavier.
25
26           The large share of debt servicing in the national budget has exacerbated the
27   government’s worsening neglect of social services since 1997. Education, health and
28   social welfare budgets remain insufficient while a large share of the people’s taxes is
29   allocated for debt payment. In 2026, debt servicing amounted to ₱2.006 trillion, of
30   which ₱950 billion is for interest payment and ₱1.056 trillion is for payment for
31   principal amortization of foreign and domestic debts. The amount for debt servicing is
32   higher than the 2026 allotment for the Department of Education, which is ₱961.32
33   billion, and the Department of Health with ₱297.86 billion.
34

 4          Despite the government’s huge debt servicing, the government is still borrowing
 5   heavily. In nominal terms, the total debt added by the Duterte administration is over
 6   two and a half times that of the Arroyo administration and five times that of the B.

11           The automatic appropriation for debt service at the expense of basic services is
12   unjust. It preempts the people’s prerogative by giving absolute and unquestioned
13   priority to debt servicing and placing so much of the national budget outside normal
14   budget processes. It has caused economic policy to systematically favor narrow private
15   creditor and corporate interests over the needs of the people. It even encourages gross
16   fiscal irresponsibility by making loans depend less on their merits than on the capacity
17   of the government to squeeze the people for repayment funds.

28   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
29   assembled:
30
31   SECTION 1. Section 31 of Presidential Decree No. 1177 of July 30, 1977, entitled
32   “Revising the Budget Process in Order to Institutionalize the Budgetary Innovations of
33   the New Society,” is hereby amended to read as follows:
34
35         “Section 31. Automatic Appropriations. All expenditures for [(a)] personnel
36         retirement premiums, government service insurance, and other similar fixed
37         SOCIAL SERVICE expenditures [(b) principal and interest on public debt,
38         (c) national government guarantees of obligations which are drawn upon,]
39         are automatically appropriated: Provided, that no obligations shall be
40         incurred or payments made from funds thus automatically appropriated
41         except as issued in the form of regular budgetary allotments.”
42
43   SEC. 2. Section 26, Chapter 4, Book VI of Executive Order No. 292, otherwise known
44   as the “Administrative Code of 1987,” which reiterates in toto Section 31 of Presidential
45   Decree No. 1177 and similar laws, decrees, executive orders, administrative orders, or
46   circulars are likewise hereby amended.
