                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

The high cost of hospitalization continues to push many Filipino families into debt,
especially when a loved one needs to be con ined in a hospital. Even patients admitted
to basic or ward rooms, who are often low-income or vulnerable, still end up paying
large amounts out of pocket. This happens because PhilHealth bene its are often too
low, essential medicines and tests are not fully covered, and some hospitals still
charge patients extra despite existing rules.

This bill seeks to make hospital care truly affordable for ward patients. It requires
PhilHealth to increase in-patient ward bene its by ifty percent (50%) and to strictly
enforce a “zero out-of-pocket” policy for covered services. In simple terms, this means
patients in ward or basic accommodation should no longer be asked to pay for
services, medicines, or tests that PhilHealth already covers. The measure strengthens
existing health laws that promise no co-payments for ward patients, and makes that
promise real in everyday hospital experience.

By improving PhilHealth coverage and stopping extra charges, the bill protects
families from inancial hardship, especially the poor, the elderly, and the sick. It
ensures that PhilHealth funds are used for their true purpose: to help members when
they need medical care the most. This measure promotes fairness, eases the burden
on ordinary Filipinos, and moves the country closer to genuine universal health care.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Expanded PhilHealth Ward
Accommodation Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to pursue universal
and compulsory health insurance coverage and to ensure equitable access to quality
and affordable health care, with priority for the underprivileged, sick, elderly, persons
with disability, women, and children. The State shall strengthen inancial risk
protection by reducing out-of-pocket payments, particularly for patients admitted in
ward accommodations in accredited health facilities.

SECTION 3. Coverage. — This Act shall cover all members of the National Health
Insurance Program (NHIP) and their quali ied dependents under Republic Act No.
7875, as amended.

SECTION 4. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
de ined:

   (a) “Accredited Health Facility” refers to a hospital, in irmary, clinic, or other
       health care institution accredited by the Philippine Health Insurance
       Corporation (PhilHealth) to provide health services covered by the NHIP;

   (b) “Diagnostic Procedures” refers to clinically indicated laboratory, imaging,
       and other diagnostic tests ordered by a licensed health professional in
       connection with an in-patient admission;

   (c) “Drugs and Medicines” refers to clinically indicated pharmaceutical products
       prescribed by licensed health professionals and dispensed by licensed
       pharmacies, whether within or outside the health facility, in connection with
       an in-patient admission;

   (d) “In-patient” refers to a person admitted to an accredited health facility for
       treatment and observation;

   (e) “Ward Accomodation” or “Ward” refers to an in-patient room con iguration
       within an accredited health facility that is shared by three (3) or more in-
       patients per room, as certi ied and classi ied by the Department of Health
       (DOH) and PhilHealth pursuant to their standards;

   (f) “Ward In-patient Bene it Package” refers to the PhilHealth bene it package
       applicable to ward accommodation admissions, as de ined and updated by
       PhilHealth, including case-based payments and/or itemized bene it
       components as may be applicable.

SECTION 5. Mandatory Increase in Ward In-patient Bene its. —

   (a) Increase. — Within six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act, PhilHealth
       shall increase by ifty percent (50%) the total PhilHealth coverage amounts for
       the Ward In-patient Bene it Package, relative to the immediately preceding
       coverage amounts in effect at the time of this Act’s effectivity.

   (b) Scope of Increase. — The ifty percent (50%) increase shall apply to PhilHealth
       payments for in-patient ward admissions, including professional fees and
       facility fees covered under the applicable ward bene it package, subject to
       standards on reasonableness and anti-fraud safeguards.

   (c) Regular Review. — PhilHealth, in coordination with the DOH, shall thereafter
       review ward bene it adequacy at least every two (2) years, considering
       in lation, disease burden, and utilization, and submit a report to Congress.

SECTION 6. No Balance Billing and Zero Out-of-Pocket for Ward Admissions. —

   (a) No Balance Billing Rule for Ward. — For ward admissions in accredited health
       facilities, PhilHealth shall implement and enforce a No Balance Billing policy
       such that no additional charges shall be collected from the member or
       quali ied dependent for services, drugs and medicines, and diagnostic
       procedures that are:
       (i)      clinically indicated;
       (ii)     included in the ward bene it package or otherwise covered under
                PhilHealth rules; and
       (iii) provided in connection with the admission.

   (b) Coverage of Essential Goods and Services. — For purposes of this Act, “zero
       out-of-pocket” refers to the absence of any co-payment, balance billing, or
       extra billing for covered items and services as determined under PhilHealth
       rules, subject to the limitations and exclusions that shall be expressly
       enumerated in the implementing rules and regulations.

   (c) Facility Accountability. — Any accredited health facility found to have collected
       prohibited charges in violation of this section shall be subject to administrative
       sanctions by PhilHealth, including suspension, downgrading, or termination of
       accreditation, and the imposition of restitution/refund mechanisms,
       consistent with due process.

SECTION 7. Funding; Actuarial Safeguards. —

   (a) Appropriations. — The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
       charged against the funds of PhilHealth, subject to actuarial soundness and to
       such supplemental appropriations as Congress may provide.

   (b) Actuarial Study. — Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act,
       PhilHealth shall submit to Congress and the DOH an actuarial assessment on
       the iscal impact of the mandated increase and the no balance billing
       expansion, together with proposed premium, payment, and anti-fraud
       measures to ensure sustainability.
