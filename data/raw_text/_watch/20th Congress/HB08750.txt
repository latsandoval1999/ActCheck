                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Senior High School (SHS) program under the Enhanced Basic Education Act of
2013 (K-12) provides specialized academic tracks that are designed to prepare students either
for higher education or for direct entry into the workforce. However, recent data indicate gaps
in outcome and readiness, which this bill aims to address through the establishment of Trabaho
Centers.

       According to the Philippine Statistics Authority, the youth unemployment rate (ages 15-
24) for 2024 was about 6.59%, down from 6.99% in 2023, yet still revealing challenges for
young people entering the labor force. 1

       Among SHS graduates who sought employment, the employment rate stood at 78.3%,
which is lower than the 84.5% recorded for graduates of Grade 10. Underemployment among
SHS graduates was 15.5%, compared to 13.4% for Grade 10 completers. 2

        Senior high school graduates tend to receive higher wages than those who only
completed junior high. A study by the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS)
found that SHS graduates earn approximately 19% more than those who completed only junior
high, likely because they are more often employed in medium-skill occupations requiring
advanced          literacy,      numeracy,         and          interpersonal      skills. 3

         Despite these gains, there are significant unmet needs: around 32% of Filipinos aged 16
to 18 were not enrolled in senior high school during the 2022-2023 school year (approximately
1.9 million out of an eligible 6 million). 4 This indicates both a participation gap in senior high
and a lost opportunity to shape students’ skills and employability earlier. Moreover, employers
still tend to prefer college-degree holders over SHS graduates for many entry-level jobs; most
opportunities open to SHS graduates are concentrated in service, sales, and other lower-entry
sectors. 5

graduates into gainful employment. This bill establishes Trabaho Centers as institutional
mechanisms for employment facilitation, capacity building, and career guidance, ensuring
access to job databases, employer linkages, and training opportunities to enhance both technical
and soft skills.

       Through these Trabaho Centers, graduates will be better prepared for productive work,
reducing youth unemployment and underemployment while improving earning potential and
enabling greater contribution to national development.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

             AN ACT ESTABLISHING JOB PLACEMENT OFFICES IN PUBLIC HIGH
                SCHOOLS TO PROVIDE CAREER SERVICES TO THE YOUTH,
              APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

1             SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Trabaho Center in Schools
2    Act.”

3            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote
4    full employment and equality of employment opportunities for all, and to afford full protection to
5    the youth entering the labor force. In line with the current reforms in the education sector, the State
6    recognizes the need to prepare the youth in choosing the career tracks that they intend to pursue
7    and to aid in matching senior high school students with career opportunities after graduation.

8             SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purpose of this Act, the following terms shall refer
9    to:

10                   a) Career Advocacy Activities refer to a series of activities intended to prepare
11                      students for productive career decisions that include, but are not limited to,
12                      career assessment, coaching, trainings and job-matching;
13                   b) Career Development Plan refers to an annual career development tool that
14                      outlines the intended outcomes and strategic management of
15                      programs/services to collectively achieve the end goals of the Trabaho Centers;
16                   c) Labor Market Information (LMI) refers to any information concerning the size,
17                      composition, functions, problems or opportunities of the labor market or any
18                      part thereof, including but not limited to employment-related intentions or
19                      aspirations of the labor market clients;
20                   d) National Career Assessment Examination (NCAE) refers to a system of
21                      assessing or evaluating the potential or aptitude of each and every high school
22                      student to guide their decisions in pursuing further education or employment;



                                                                                                          1
1                   e) Skills Registry System (SRS) refers to an existing system utilized by the
2                      Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) containing potential applicant
3                      and employment information.

4            SEC. 4. Establishment of Trabaho Centers in Schools. – Job Placement Offices (JPOs),
5    hereinafter referred to as "Trabaho Centers," shall be established in all public high schools.

 6           Trabaho Centers shall work in coordination with the Provincial, City, or Municipal Public
 7   Employment Service Offices (PESOs) and shall be supervised by the Trabaho Center Coordinators
 8   in Department of Education (DepEd) Division Offices. The Trabaho Center coordinators shall
 9   encourage partnerships among Educational Institutions (EI), Non-Government Organizations
10   (NGOs), Industry Associations and Members, and Local Government Units (LGUs) in the
11   establishment and management of the Trabaho Centers.

12          In applicable areas, existing JPOs in educational institutions shall continue to operate as
13   Trabaho Centers subject to the provisions of this Act, including partnering with the private sector
14   or NGOs for an online platform. The Trabaho Centers shall be responsible for preparing the youth
15   in choosing career tracks fit for their talents, skills and preferences and in matching these to the
16   needs of the current and future labor market.

17           SEC. 5. Trabaho Center Career Advocates. – All Trabaho Centers shall have at least one
18   (1) Trabaho Center Career Advocate with the following functions:

19                  a) Keep an up-to-date database of job vacancies from employers to facilitate the
20                     exchange of labor market information between young job seekers and
21                     employers by providing employment information services specific to Senior
22                     High School, both for local and overseas employment;
23                  b) Provide job linkages and networks to employers in coordination with the
24                     provincial, city or municipal PESOs;
25                  c) Develop and administer testing and evaluation instruments for effective job
26                     selection, training, and coaching specific to young jobseekers currently enrolled
27                     in educational institutions. Provided, That examinations are administered by
28                     qualified professionals;
29                  d) Organize or coordinate career enhancement trainings or seminars for young job
30                     seekers, as well as those who would like to pursue further education, livelihood
31                     or self-employment programs;
32                  e) Provide career advocacy and coaching, mass motivation and values
33                     development activities integrated in career programs specifically designed for
34                     Enhanced Basic Education Curriculum students;
35                  f) Connect and partner with concerned NGOs to expand opportunities in
36                     improving the quality of employment facilitation programs offered in their
37                     school;
38                  g) Lead the teachers within their school in the administration of the annual
39                     National Career Assessment Examination (NCAE) and assist the students in
40                     understanding how to use their test results as guide in choosing their career
41                     tracks;

1                   h) Prepare and submit to the Trabaho Center Coordinator a school specific annual
2                      career development plan and budget, including other regular funding sources
3                      and budgetary support; and
4                   i) Prepare and submit a quarterly progress report on students assisted and coached
5                      to the Trabaho Center Coordinator.

6           SEC. 6. Trabaho Center in Division Offices – Each DepEd Division Office shall appoint
7    a Trabaho Center Coordinator who shall:

 8                  a) Coordinate with the LGU, PESO, the Local School Board (LSB), and Parent
 9                     Teacher Association (PTA) in the creation of the annual key career planning
10                     tool for their division, taking into consideration track preferences of students in
11                     their respective divisions and the industry most relevant to their area;
12                  b) Monitor the planning, implementation and evaluation of career facilitation
13                     programs and materials of Trabaho Centers in Schools;
14                  c) Convene Career Advocates from the different Trabaho Centers under their
15                     respective divisions for the creation of modules or guides contextualized to
16                     cover their local industry;
17                  d) Share best practices among divisions and other Trabaho Centers in the country
18                     in a modality that would be most efficient to the institution;
19                  e) Train Career Advocates in the administration of testing and evaluation
20                     instruments for effective job selection, counseling, career guidance, mass
21                     motivation and values development activities to be implemented for the career
22                     programs;
23                  f) Utilize the DOLE's Skills Registry System (SRS) to manage the records of
24                     transactions in relation to career facilitation and relay information to Trabaho
25                     Centers in Schools;
26                  g) Consolidate necessary information technology structures and systems to
27                     improve the collection, processing, analysis, report development and
28                     dissemination of youth career preferences and existing labor market
29                     information; and
30                  h) Provide linkages to training providers needed by the schools in relation to local
31                     industry.

32           SEC. 7. Qualifications of Trabaho Center Career Advocates. – Notwithstanding the
33   provisions of Section 27 of Republic Act No. 9258, otherwise known as the "Guidance and
34   Counseling Act of 2004", career and employment advocates, who are not registered and licensed
35   guidance counselors, shall be allowed to conduct career advocacy activities for students of the
36   school where they are currently employed: Provided, That they undergo training programs related
37   to career advocacy.

38            Career advocates shall hold a non-teaching position and shall focus on activities for youth
39   employability programs in coordination with the DOLE and other relevant agencies and partner
40   institutions, both public and private.

41          SEC. 8. Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) and Technical Vocational Institutions
42   (TVIs). – Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) and Technical Vocational Institutions (TVIs) are
                                                                                                        3
1    encouraged to establish Trabaho Centers in their schools to prepare their students in choosing career
2    tracks suited for their talents, skills and preferences and in matching these to the needs of the current
3    and future labor market, in accordance with the provisions of this Act.

4           SEC. 9. Information Dissemination. – The Philippine Information Agency, in
5    coordination with the DepEd, DOLE and the DILG, shall ensure proper and adequate information
6    dissemination of the contents and benefits of this Act to pertinent entities in all provinces, cities,
7    municipalities and barangays.

 8           SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement the provisions of this Act
 9   shall be charged against the current year's appropriations of the DepEd. Thereafter, such sums as
10   may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
11   Appropriations Act.

12           SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
13   effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, in coordination with Department of Labor and Employment
14   (DOLE), Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), Commission on Higher
15   Education (CHEd), Department of Trade, Industry (DTI) and Technical Education and Skills
16   Development Authority (TESDA) and other relevant agencies shall formulate the necessary rules
17   and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

18          SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
19   unconstitutional, the same shall not affect the validity and effectivity of other provisions hereof.

20           SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, and rules and regulations
21   contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
22   accordingly.

23           SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days from the date of its
24   publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
