                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

Marine pollution caused by untreated sewage discharge from ships and floating facilities
poses a serious threat to the Philippines’ coastal waters, fisheries, and public health. The
country, being an archipelagic state with one of the largest maritime jurisdictions in the
world, is highly vulnerable to the cumulative effects of ship-generated waste. While the
Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) has issued Memorandum Circulars, including Circular No.
10-14 (2014) and the updated Circular No. 03-25 (2025), compliance remains
inconsistent, and enforcement mechanisms require stronger legislative backing.

International conventions such as MARPOL Annex IV, together with domestic laws
including Republic Act No. 9275 (Philippine Clean Water Act), Republic Act No. 9993
(Philippine Coast Guard Law), and Republic Act No. 12064 (Philippine Maritime Zones
Act), already provide a framework for sewage management. However, without
institutionalized penalties, monitoring systems, and clear authority vested in the PCG,
ship operators often evade responsibility, resulting in untreated sewage being discharged
into Philippine waters until now.

This bill seeks to institutionalize the compliance, monitoring, and penal provisions for
sewage treatment and disposal in Philippine-registered vessels. It strengthens the
enforcement powers of the PCG by authorizing stop-sail orders, suspension of
accreditation, and denial of departure clearance for non-compliant vessels. It also
mandates MARINA to integrate sewage compliance into vessel registration, DENR to
oversee effluent testing through accredited laboratories, and DOH to certify sewage
treatment facilities and personnel training.

By imposing stricter fines, requiring regular inspections, and establishing a Marine
Sewage Compliance Database, this measure ensures transparency, accountability, and
deterrence against violations. It also aligns Philippine maritime practices with international
standards, thereby protecting marine biodiversity, sustaining fisheries, and safeguarding
the health of our coastal communities.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                 AN ACT
     INSTITUTIONALIZING THE COMPLIANCE, MONITORING, AND PENAL
     PROVISIONS FOR SEWAGE TREATMENT AND DISPOSAL IN PHILIPPINE-
     REGISTERED VESSELS, STRENGTHENING THE POWERS OF THE PHILIPPINE
     COAST GUARD, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1        SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Ship Sewage
 2   Compliance and Enforcement Act of 2026.”

 3        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to protect the marine
 4   environment and public health by ensuring strict compliance with international and
 5   domestic standards on sewage management from Philippine registered vessels, all fixed
 6   or floating platforms, offshore platforms, floating crafts and floating facilities.

 7   The State recognizes the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) as the primary enforcement
 8   agency in preventing sewage pollution, in coordination with the Maritime Industry
 9   Authority (MARINA), the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR),
10   and the Department of Health (DOH).

11        SEC. 3. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all Philippine-registered vessels of four
12   hundred gross tonnage and above, vessels below four hundred gross tonnages certified
13   to carry fifteen or more passengers, fixed or floating platforms, offshore facilities, and for
14   accredited sewage collectors.

15        SEC. 4. Mandatory Requirements. - All covered vessels shall be fitted with sewage
16   treatment plants, holding tanks, or other approved marine sanitation devices and
17   appropriately approved related equipment. Effluent discharged from such systems shall
18   conform to internationally recognized standards, including coliform counts not exceeding
19   one hundred MPN per one hundred milliliters, biochemical oxygen demand not exceeding
20   twenty-five milligrams per liter, and total suspended solids not exceeding thirty-five
21   milligrams per liter.
 1   Vessels shall maintain a duly registered Sewage Discharge Record Book, properly
 2   accomplished and available for inspection. Sewage collectors accredited by the PCG
 3   shall submit monthly reports to both the Coast Guard and DENR detailing the quantity
 4   and type of waste collected and treated.

 5

 6        SEC. 5. Monitoring and Enforcement. - The PCG shall conduct annual and
 7   random surprise inspections of vessels and port facilities, and shall exercise authority to
 8   issue stop-sail orders, suspend or revoke sewage collector accreditation, and deny
 9   departure clearance to vessels found non-compliant.

10   MARINA shall integrate sewage compliance into vessel registration and dry-docking
11   requirements. Failure to do so shall result to non-issuance of the requisite Certificate of
12   Public Convenience and permits.

13   DENR shall oversee effluent testing through accredited laboratories, while DOH shall
14   certify sewage treatment facilities and ensure the appropriate training of personnel
15   engaged in sewage collection and disposal to issue proper management and handling of
16   this facility and sewage disposal.

17           SEC. 6. Incident Reporting, Sampling, and Testing. - Any malfunction, leakage,
18   bypass, overflow, suspected illegal discharge, or failure of a sewage treatment plant,
19   holding tank, or related equipment shall be immediately reported by the shipowner,
20   operator, master, or accredited sewage collector to the PCG and MARINA. Failure to
21   report such incident within twenty-four (24) hours from its occurrence shall
22   constitute a violation of this Act and shall subject the liable shipowner, operator,
23   master, or accredited sewage collector to an administrative fine of not less than
24   Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) but not more than Five Hundred Thousand
25   Pesos (₱500,000.00), and/or the suspension or revocation of the vessel's Authority
26   to Operate (ATO), Certificate of Public Convenience (CPC), or the collector’s
27   accreditation, without prejudice to other civil or criminal liabilities under existing
28   laws.

29           The PCG, DENR, or their authorized representatives may take water samples,
30   sewage samples, photographs, videos, records, and other evidence necessary to
31   determine compliance with this Act. Laboratory testing shall be conducted by DENR-
32   accredited or government-recognized laboratories. Test results, inspection findings, and
33   discharge records may be used as basis for compliance orders, stop-sail orders,
34   penalties, suspension, or other enforcement action under this Act.

35           SEC. 7. Inspection and Monitoring Unit. – The PCG shall hire qualified
36   personnel that has the appropriate training that will handle the inspection and monitoring
 1   of all vessels covered under this Act. These personnel shall be deployed in all regional
 2   offices and in such areas with high vessel traffic.

 3        The budgetary requirements for this unit will be included in the Personnel Services
 4   of the PCG in their annual budget.

 5        SEC. 8. Penalties. - Violations of this Act shall carry administrative fines of one
 6   hundred thousand pesos per day for first offenses, automatically adjusted by ten percent
 7   every three years to account for inflation.

 8   Provided, however, for repeat violations, it shall incur fines of up to five hundred thousand
 9   pesos per day, together with revocation of accreditation and denial of renewal. These
10   penalties shall be without prejudice to other sanctions under existing laws. The imposition
11   of administrative penalties shall likewise serve as grounds for the non-issuance of
12   departure clearance for vessels until compliance is achieved.

13        SEC. 9. Strengthening PCG Powers. - The PCG shall establish and maintain a
14   Marine Sewage Compliance Database, accessible to regulators and the public, to ensure
15   transparency and accountability in enforcement. It shall be empowered to coordinate with
16   MARINA, DENR, and DOH for integrated enforcement, and to exercise expanded
17   authority in the inspection, certification, and sanctioning of vessels and sewage operators.

18   It shall coordinate with concerned local government units to determine compliance and
19   encourage their participation in monitoring those vessels plying the routes of their LGU’s.

20   The PCG shall continue to improve and update existing standards compliant with
21   international conventions like the MARPOL 73/78, existing laws, and policies.

22        SEC. 10. Appropriations. -               Funding requirements necessary for the
23   implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

24        SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the
25   effectivity of this Act, the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG), in coordination with the
26   Department of Transportation (DOTr), Maritime Industry Authority (MARINA), and the
27   Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), shall promulgate the
28   necessary rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

29        SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
30   unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall not be affected and shall remain
31   in full force and effect.

32        SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, and circulars inconsistent with this
33   Act, including PCG Memorandum Circular No. 10-14, are hereby repealed.

34        SEC. 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen days after its publication in a
35   newspaper of general circulation.

36          Approved.
