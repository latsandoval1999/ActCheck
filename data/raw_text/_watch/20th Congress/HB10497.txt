                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       For decades, the Filipino people have rightly recognized the invaluable
contributions of our senior citizens by granting them discounts and other privileges
under the law. These benefits are a reflection of our nation's gratitude and commitment
to ensuring that our elderly live with dignity and security.

       While the objective of these benefits remains unquestioned, the manner in which
they are financed has changed over time. When Republic Act No. 7432 was enacted in
1992, private establishments were granted a 100% tax credit for the mandatory
discounts extended to senior citizens. This recognized that while businesses served as
the mechanism for delivering the benefit, the cost of the government's social welfare
policy should ultimately be borne by the State.

       However, Republic Act No. 9257 replaced the tax credit with a tax deduction, a
policy that was later retained under Republic Act No. 9994. As a result, businesses are
no longer fully reimbursed for the discounts they are legally required to grant.
This has placed a significant financial burden on many establishments, particularly
micro, small, and medium enterprises, which continue to comply with the law while
absorbing much of its cost.

       This measure does not seek to diminish or remove the benefits enjoyed by
senior citizens. On the contrary, it seeks to preserve these benefits while restoring the
original principle that government-funded social welfare programs should ultimately be
financed by the government itself.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT RESTORING THE TAX CREDIT FOR MANDATORY SENIOR CITIZEN
DISCOUNTS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9994,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "EXPANDED SENIOR CITIZENS ACT OF 2010,"
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

        Section 1. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and promote
the rights and welfare of senior citizens and to ensure their full enjoyment of the benefits and
privileges granted by law.

        The State likewise recognizes that private enterprises are essential partners in nation-
building, economic development, and employment generation. Accordingly, while private
establishments shall continue to implement the mandatory discounts provided under existing
laws, the State shall ensure that the cost of such government-mandated social welfare measures
is equitably borne through an appropriate tax credit mechanism.

        Section 2. Amendment. – Section 4(a)(8) of Republic Act No. 9994 is hereby amended
to read as follows:

         "(8) Tax Credit. – Private establishments granting the mandatory twenty
         percent (20%) discount and value-added tax (VAT) exemption provided under
         this Act shall be entitled to claim, as a tax credit, an amount equivalent to
         one hundred percent (100%) of the total mandatory discounts actually granted
         to qualified senior citizens: Provided, That such tax credit shall be creditable
         against any national internal revenue tax liability of the establishment and may
         be carried over to succeeding taxable years until fully utilized, but shall not be
         refundable or transferable: Provided, further, That the amount of the tax credit
         claimed shall be supported by proper documentation and shall be subject to the
         provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC), as amended, and the
         pertinent rules and regulations issued by the Bureau of Internal Revenue."

        Section 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance (DOF), the Bureau of Internal Revenue
(BIR), the National Commission of Senior Citizens (NCSC), the Department of Trade and
Industry and other concerned agencies shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations
for the effective implementation of this Act.

       In the formulation of the Implementing Rules and Regulations, the DOF and BIR shall
consult with at least two (2) representatives from duly recognized national business
organizations representing micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and the broader
business community.

       The implementing rules shall provide for a simplified and digitalized procedure for
claiming tax credits, particularly for micro, small, and medium enterprises.

       Section 4. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

        Section 5. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, rules and regulations, or
parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

        Section 6. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
