                        AN ACT
REQUIRING THE FORTIFICATION OF RICE, FLOUR, AND CANNED
GOODS DISTRIBUTED THROUGH GOVERNMENT FOOD AND
NUTRITION PROGRAMS WITH ESSENTIAL MICRONUTRIENTS,
STRENGTHENING PUBLIC NUTRITION INTERVENTIONS, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies continue to pose serious public
health challenges in the Philippines, particularly among children, pregnant women,
senior citizens, indigenous peoples, and low-income households. Deficiencies in
essential micronutrients such as iron, vitamin A, iodine, zinc, and folic acid
contribute to stunting, weakened immune systems, poor cognitive development,
maternal health complications, anemia, and increased vulnerability to disease.

       Despite existing nutrition programs, many Filipino families relying on
government feeding initiatives, relief assistance, school nutrition programs,
institutional feeding, and food subsidy programs continue to have limited access to
nutrient-rich foods. In times of economic hardship, emergencies, and disasters,
vulnerable populations often depend heavily on government-distributed staple food
products that may lack adequate nutritional value.
       Food fortification is a globally recognized, cost-effective, and evidence-based
public health intervention that enhances the nutritional quality of staple foods
without significantly altering consumption habits or increasing costs to consumers.
The Philippines has previously enacted Republic Act No. 8976, or the “Philippine
Food Fortification Act of 2000,” which established mandatory fortification policies
for selected food products. However, gaps remain in ensuring that all food products
procured, distributed, or subsidized through government programs consistently meet
enhanced nutritional standards.

        This proposed bill seeks to institutionalize mandatory micronutrient
fortification standards for rice, flour, canned goods, and other designated staple food
items distributed through all government feeding, relief, subsidy, and public food
assistance programs. The measure aims to strengthen public nutrition interventions,
reduce micronutrient deficiencies, improve health outcomes, support child
development, and advance the State’s commitment to food security and public
health.

      The bill likewise establishes clear regulatory standards, inter-agency
coordination mechanisms, monitoring systems, and accountability measures to
ensure the effective implementation of fortified food distribution across all levels of
government.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Food
Fortification for Public Distribution Act”


      SEC 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to protect and promote the right to health and nutrition of the people and adopt
sustainable, science-based, and equitable nutrition interventions to address hunger,
malnutrition, and micronutrient deficiencies, particularly among vulnerable and
underserved populations.


      The State shall strengthen food fortification measures within government
feeding, relief, subsidy, and public food distribution programs to improve nutritional
outcomes, enhance public health, and support national food and nutrition security
objectives.


      SEC 3. Objectives. – This Act aims to:


      (a) Reduce micronutrient deficiencies among vulnerable populations;
      (b) Improve the nutritional quality of staple food products distributed through
      government programs;
      (c) Strengthen national nutrition and food security interventions;
      (d) Institutionalize mandatory food fortification standards in public food
      procurement and distribution systems;
      (e) Enhance child growth, cognitive development, maternal health, and
      disease prevention; and
      (f) Promote coordinated and evidence-based nutrition governance.


      SEC 4. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:


      (a) Food Fortification refers to the addition of essential micronutrients to
      food products to improve nutritional quality and public health outcomes;
      (b) Micronutrients refer to essential vitamins and minerals including iron,
      vitamin A, iodine, zinc, folic acid, and other nutrients identified by the
      Department of Health (DOH);

      (c) Government Food Distribution Programs refer to feeding, relief,
      nutrition, subsidy, institutional feeding, emergency response, school feeding,
      and other publicly funded food assistance programs implemented by
      government agencies or local government units;

      (d) Fortified Food Products refer to food items enhanced with micronutrients
      in accordance with standards prescribed under this Act;

      (e) Public Distribution refers to procurement, storage, transport, and
      distribution of food products funded or administered by government agencies.

      SEC 5. Mandatory Fortification of Government-Distributed Food Products.
— All rice, flour, canned goods, and other staple food products identified by the
Department of Health (DOH) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) that are
procured, distributed, subsidized, or funded through government food assistance and
nutrition programs shall be fortified with essential micronutrients in accordance with
standards established under this Act.


      Mandatory fortification shall apply to food products distributed through,
including but not limited to:


      (a) School-based feeding programs;

      (b) Supplemental feeding and nutrition programs;
      (c) Disaster relief and emergency food assistance operations;

      (d) Conditional cash transfer-related feeding interventions;

      (e) Public hospitals, shelters, and institutional feeding programs;

      (f) Food subsidy and social protection programs; and

      (g) Other government-funded food distribution initiatives.

      The Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Food and Drug
Administration (FDA), National Nutrition Council (NNC), Department of
Agriculture (DA), Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD),
Department of Education (DepEd), Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), and other concerned agencies, shall determine the appropriate
micronutrient composition, fortification levels, and food coverage based on
scientific evidence, nutritional needs, and public health priorities.


      SEC 6. Nutritional Standards and Technical Guidelines. — The DOH, in
coordination with the FDA, shall formulate, adopt, and periodically update national
nutritional standards, food fortification specifications, labeling requirements, quality
assurance mechanisms, and other technical guidelines governing all food products
covered under this Act.


      In developing and revising such standards and guidelines, the DOH and FDA
shall take into account the prevalence and severity of micronutrient deficiencies, the
nutritional requirements of target populations, internationally recognized health,
nutrition, and food safety standards, product stability and quality assurance
considerations, the cost-effectiveness and feasibility of implementation, and relevant
scientific, technological, and public health developments to ensure that food
fortification interventions remain safe, effective, evidence-based, and responsive to
the nutritional needs of the population.


      SEC 7. Procurement and Supply Requirements. — All national government
agencies, government-owned and controlled corporations, local government units,
and government contractors involved in food procurement and public distribution
shall ensure compliance with the fortification standards prescribed under this Act.


      Government procurement contracts involving covered food products shall
include mandatory fortification and quality compliance requirements as conditions
for eligibility and award. Suppliers, manufacturers, millers, importers, distributors,
and contractors participating in government food programs shall secure the
necessary certifications and comply with inspection and testing requirements
imposed by the FDA and relevant agencies. Provided that, preference may be
given to locally produced and fortified food products consistent with existing
procurement laws and food security policies.


      SEC 8. Monitoring, Inspection, and Quality Assurance. – The Food and
Drug Administration (FDA), in coordination with the Department of Health (DOH),
National Nutrition Council (NNC), Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), and
local government units, shall establish comprehensive inspection, monitoring,
product testing, and quality assurance systems to ensure compliance with this Act.
      Government agencies implementing food distribution programs shall
maintain records of procurement, testing, distribution, and compliance and shall
submit periodic reports to the DOH. Random product testing, facility inspections,
and post-distribution nutritional evaluations may be conducted to ensure product
safety, nutrient compliance, and program effectiveness.


      SEC 9. Public Nutrition Education and Awareness. — The Department of
Health (DOH) and the National Nutrition Council (NNC) shall conduct nationwide
public information and nutrition education campaigns on the importance of food
fortification, micronutrient intake, maternal and child nutrition, and healthy dietary
practices.


      Local government units, schools, healthcare facilities, and community
organizations shall be encouraged to participate in nutrition promotion and public
awareness activities under this Act.


      SEC 10. Incentives and Technical Support. — The Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI), Department of Agriculture (DA), and relevant government agencies
may provide technical assistance, training support, technology access, and capacity-
building programs for local food manufacturers, millers, cooperatives, and suppliers
to facilitate compliance with fortification standards.


      Subject to existing laws and regulations, the government may also provide
fiscal or non-fiscal incentives for qualified local producers participating in public
fortified food supply programs.
      SEC 11. Penalties. — Any manufacturer, supplier, contractor, distributor,
importer, or government official who knowingly distributes non-compliant food
products, falsifies fortification compliance records, misrepresents nutrient content,
or violates the provisions of this Act shall, upon conviction, be subject to appropriate
administrative, civil, and criminal penalties under existing food safety, procurement,
consumer protection, and anti-graft laws.


      The FDA may suspend or revoke licenses, impose administrative fines,
recommend blacklisting from government procurement, or order product recalls for
violations of this Act.


      SEC 12. Role of the Department of Health (DOH). — The Department of
Health (DOH) shall serve as the lead implementing agency of this Act and shall:


      a. Formulate policies, standards, and implementation guidelines;

      b. Determine priority micronutrients and food coverage;

      c. Coordinate with relevant government agencies and stakeholders;

      d. Conduct monitoring and program evaluations;

      e. Establish nutrition outcome indicators and reporting systems; and

      f. Submit annual reports to Congress and the Office of the President on the
      implementation of this Act.
      SEC 13. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


      SEC 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred
twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health, in
coordination with concerned agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of
this Act.


      SEC 15. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


      SEC 16. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.


      SEC 17. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
