                             AN ACT
EXPANDING THE COVERAGE OF THE MALASAKIT PROGRAM TO LOCAL
GOVERNMENT UNIT-CONTROLLED AND SUPERVISED HOSPITALS AND
HEALTH FACILITIES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO.
11463, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “MALASAKIT CENTERS ACT”

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

The 1987 Constitution recognizes the right of the people to health and mandates the State to
adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to health development, making essential
health and social services available to all Filipinos at affordable cost. This constitutional duty
is especially important for indigent and financially incapacitated patients who often face
difficulty in securing timely medical care due to the high cost of hospitalization, medicines,
diagnostics, and other health-related expenses.

With the enactment of the Local Government Code of 1991, significant powers,
responsibilities, and functions were devolved to local government units. Among the major
services devolved were health services previously administered by the Department of Health.
As a result, the operation and supervision of many provincial, district, city, and municipal
hospitals, as well as rural health units and other local health facilities, were transferred to local
government units. This framework brought health services closer to communities, but it also
resulted in a public health system where not all government health facilities are directly
controlled or supervised by the DOH.

In 2019, Republic Act No. 11463, otherwise known as the Malasakit Centers Act,
institutionalized Malasakit Centers as one-stop shops for medical and financial assistance in
DOH hospitals and the Philippine General Hospital. The law brought together key government
agencies such as the DOH, Department of Social Welfare and Development, Philippine Health
Insurance Corporation, and Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office to streamline the processing
of assistance for indigent and financially incapacitated patients. Through this mechanism,
patients are given a more accessible, coordinated, and compassionate means of securing
support for their medical needs.

However, despite the gains of the Malasakit Program, its mandatory establishment remains
limited to DOH hospitals and the PGH. This creates a gap in access for patients who seek care
in LGU-controlled and supervised hospitals or health facilities, especially in provinces, cities,
and municipalities where such facilities are often the nearest or most accessible points of care.
In many communities, district hospitals, rural health units, infirmaries, and similar local health
facilities serve as the first and sometimes only available health service providers. Patients in
these facilities should not be placed at a disadvantage merely because the facility they access
is under local, rather than national, supervision.

This proposed measure seeks to address this gap by expanding the coverage of the Malasakit
Program to LGU-controlled and supervised hospitals or health facilities. It recognizes the
realities of devolved health service delivery and strengthens the connection between national
medical assistance programs and local health facilities. By allowing these facilities to adopt,
implement, or provide access to the Malasakit Program through centers, help desks, satellite
desks, referral systems, digital platforms, or other mechanisms prescribed by the DOH, the bill
ensures that assistance reaches patients more efficiently and equitably.

The measure is consistent with the objectives of Universal Health Care, which calls for an
integrated and people-centered health system. Expanding the Malasakit Program to local health
facilities will improve coordination, reduce delays, assist patients in navigating documentary
requirements, and lessen the financial burden on vulnerable families.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Amendment to Section 3 of Republic Act No. 11463. — Section 3 of Republic
Act No. 11463, otherwise known as the “Malasakit Centers Act,” is hereby amended by adding
a new paragraph to read as follows:

       Section 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following
       terms shall mean:

       xxx

       (j) LGU-CONTROLLED AND SUPERVISED HEALTH FACILITY
       REFERS TO ANY PUBLIC HOSPITAL, RURAL HEALTH UNIT,
       DISTRICT HOSPITAL, INFIRMARY, OR OTHER SIMILAR
       HEALTH    FACILITY   OWNED,  OPERATED,   MANAGED,
       CONTROLLED, OR SUPERVISED BY A LOCAL GOVERNMENT
       UNIT, WHETHER PROVINCIAL, CITY, OR MUNICIPAL, AND
       PROVIDING HEALTH CARE, MEDICAL, OR RELATED SUPPORT
       SERVICES TO THE PUBLIC.

       Program that all DOH hospitals, LGU-CONTROLLED AND
       SUPERVISED HEALTH FACILITIES, and the PGH shall adopt and
       implement. xxx

SEC. 4. Section 9 of Republic Act No. 11463 is hereby amended to read as follows:

       Section 9. Medical and Financial Assistance.

       xxx

       In cases of patients who are admitted in [LGU-and] other public hospitals

SEC. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with PhilHealth, DSWD, PCSO, and DILG, shall
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 6. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain valid and
subsisting.

SEC. 7. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, ordinances,
rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 8. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
