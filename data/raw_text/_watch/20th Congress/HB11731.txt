EXPLANATORY NOTE

Smoking and the use of tobacco and nicotine products remain significant
public health concerns in the Philippines. Despite the government’s continuing
efforts to reduce tobacco consumption, the prevalence of smoking remains
substantial, particularly among adults, while the continued use of cigarettes and
emerging nicotine products among adolescents raises serious concerns about
future tobacco dependence and its associated health consequences.

Based on the 2025 Updating Survey conducted by the Department of
Science and Technology - Food and Nutrition Research Institute (DOST-FNRI),
smoking prevalence among adolescents aged 10 to 19 decreased slightly from
4.8% in 2023 to 4.5% in 2025. However, smoking prevalence among adults aged
20 years and above increased from 24.4% in 2023 to 24.9% in 2025. The survey
further showed that manufactured cigarettes remain the most commonly used
tobacco product among adolescent and adult smokers, followed by electronic
cigarettes.}

These figures underscore the continuing need to strengthen the country’s
tobacco control policies and to address factors that may contribute to the
accessibility and affordability of tobacco and nicotine products. These factors
include the ease of access to such products, the proliferation of illicit trade,

The health consequences of tobacco use are far-reaching. In 2026, the
Philippine Statistics Authority reported that, from January to October of 2025, the
three leading causes of death in the country were ischemic heart diseases,
neoplasms, and cerebrovascular diseases*. Tobacco smoking is an established risk
factor for these diseases, as well as for numerous other serious and life-threatening
conditions. Consequently, the continued prevalence of tobacco use imposes not
only a substantial health burden on individuals and families but also significant
economic costs on the country’s healthcare system.

There is therefore a need to strengthen existing measures aimed at reducing
tobacco and nicotine consumption. One effective means of achieving this objective
is through appropriate and responsive excise taxation. Increasing the excise taxes
imposed on tobacco and related products can help make these products less
affordable, discourage initiation and continued consumption, which will in turn
lessen the incidence of smoking-related diseases, and reduce the long-term
medical and economic costs borne by Filipino families and the government.
Further, increasing the excise taxes can also generate additional revenues that may
be used to support essential government programs and services.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

SEC. 5. A new Section 263-B shall be introduced in the National Internal
Revenue Code of 1997, as amended. The new Section 263-B shall read as follows:

SEC. 263-B. MANUFACTURING, IMPORTING, SELLING, OR
DISTRIBUTION OF FLAVORED VAPOR PRODUCTS. - ANY PERSON WHO
MANUFACTURES, IMPORTS, SELLS, OR DISTRIBUTES VAPOR
PRODUCTS WITH FLAVORING OTHER THAN PLAIN TOBACCO OR
PLAIN MENTHOL IN VIOLATION OF SECTION 144 (C) OF THIS CODE
SHALL BE PUNISHED WITH A FINE OF NOT LESS THAN ONE MILLION
FIVE HUNDRED THOUSAND PESOS (P1,500,000.00) BUT NOT MORE
THAN FIFTEEN MILLION PESOS (P15,000,000.00) AND
IMPRISONMENT FOR A TERM OF NOT LESS THAN SIX (6) YEARS AND
ONE (1) DAY BUT NOT MORE THAN TWELVE (12) YEARS.

SEC. 6. A new Section 263-C shall be introduced in the National Internal
Revenue Code of 1997, as amended. The new Section 263-C shall read as follows:

SEC. 263-C. UNLAWFUL POSSESSION OF FLAVORED VAPOR PRODUCTS.
- IT SHALL BE UNLAWFUL FOR ANY PERSON TO HAVE IN HIS
POSSESSION VAPOR PRODUCTS WITH FLAVORING OTHER THAN
PLAIN TOBACCO OR PLAIN MENTHOL WHICH ARE PROHIBITED
UNDER SECTION 144 (C) OF THIS CODE. ANY PERSON WHO HAS BEEN
FOUND GUILTY UNDER THIS SECTION SHALL BE SUBJECT TO THE
FOLLOWING PENALTIES:
