                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE


          In 2018, when Republic Act No. 10963 or the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion
(TRAIN) Law was passed, the personal income tax exemption was set at Two Hundred FIfty
Thousand Pesos (Php250,000.00). Over the past eight years cumulative inflation significantly
eroded the purchasing power of Filipinos. As a result, One Hundred Pesos (Php100.00) in 2018
would only be able to buy an equivalent of Seventy-Four Pesos (Php74.00) to Seventy-Eight
Pesos (Php78.00) worth of goods and services now. Thus, almost a decade after the passage of
the law, it is now imperative to review whether the personal income tax exemption remains
responsive to the needs of the majority of Filipinos.


          This proposed measure seeks to increase the personal tax exemption to Three Hundred
Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php350,000.00). President Ferdinand “Bongbong” R. Marcos Jr. has
emphasized the urgency of enacting this measure in his 2026 State of the Nation Address
(SONA). The proposal is expected to benefit at least 3.13 Million Filipino workers, including an
additional 1.3 Million workers who will no longer be mandated to pay personal income tax. The
Department of Finance estimates that this will increase the number of tax-exempt Filipino
workers from at least 5.1 Million to 6.3 Million.1


          Amid heightened global economic uncertainties arising from international geopolitical
tensions, this legislative proposal seeks to provide tangible relief to millions of Filipino families
by allowing them to retain a larger part of their wages and increasing their disposable income.


          In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                (b) Rate of Tax on Income of Purely Self-employed Individuals and/or
        Professionals Whose Gross Sales or Gross Receipts and Other Non-operating Income
        Does Not Exceed the Value-added Tax (VAT) Threshold as Provided in Section 109(BB). –

        SEC. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the

implement the provisions of this Act.

        SEC. 3 Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations or parts

invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

        SEC. 5. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
