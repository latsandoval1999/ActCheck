                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to strengthen the protection of Overseas Filipino Workers
(OFWs) and their dependents against fraudulent schemes. It defines and penalizes
fraud specifically targeting OFWs and establishes stronger enforcement and
reporting mechanisms.

       Government agencies have repeatedly warned that OFWs are among the
sectors most vulnerable to fraudulent activities. In 2024, the Department of Migrant
Workers issued advisories against investment scams masquerading as legitimate
financial platforms and targeting OFWs and their families through misleading
advertisements, fabricated testimonials, and promises of extraordinary returns. The
agency likewise reported the continuing proliferation of online recruitment scams
and other fraudulent schemes that prey on Filipinos seeking economic opportunities
abroad.

       The growing digitalization of financial services has also expanded the
avenues through which scammers operate. While existing laws, like Republic Act
No. 12010, otherwise known as the anti-Financial Account Scamming Act, address
fraud in general, there remains a need for a more focused framework that recognizes
the unique vulnerabilities of OFWs and their dependents, who are frequently
targeted because of their remittance income, limited access to immediate legal
remedies, and geographic separation from their families.

       This proposed measure establishes a comprehensive framework that
specifically protects OFWs and their dependents from fraudulent practices. It
imposes stronger penalties against perpetrators, including those who engage in
investment fraud, cyber fraud, hidden-fee schemes, and agency collusion. It likewise
establishes dedicated reporting and assistance mechanisms through a 24/7 hotline
and online portal, as well as provides for a mandatory financial literacy and fraud
prevention modules in pre-departure and post-arrival orientation programs.

      Our OFWs endure separation from their families, uncertainty, and countless
personal sacrifices to provide a better future for their loved ones and contribute to
the country's development. The money they earn abroad represents years of hard
work and dedication that should never be stolen through deception and fraud.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
STRENGTHENING THE PROTECTION OF OVERSEAS FILIPINO WORKERS
AND THEIR DEPENDENTS AGAINST FRAUDULENT PRACTICES, AND
PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Overseas Filipino
Workers’ Fraud Protection Act.”

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the vital contributions of
Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) to national development and acknowledges their
inherent vulnerability to various forms of exploitation, particularly fraudulent
schemes. ​

       It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect OFWs and their
dependents from fraudulent activities pertaining to investments, purchases, and
financial transactions; ensure that OFWs and their dependents have access to
comprehensive financial literacy programs and effective redress mechanisms,
through the coordinated efforts of relevant government agencies; and promote
accountability by imposing stringent penalties on perpetrators of fraud, including
individuals, corporations, and intermediaries found to be involved.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:​

   a. Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) refers to a person who is to be engaged, is
      engaged, or has been engaged in a remunerated activity in a foreign country
      in which they are not citizens or permanent residents, as defined in Republic
      Act No. 11641, otherwise known as the “Department of Migrant Workers Act,”
      including those who have been contracted but are not yet deployed.

   b. Dependent refers to a legal spouse, unmarried children under twenty-one
      (21) years of age, or parents aged sixty (60) years or older who are

      demonstrably reliant on the OFW's remittances for their financial support.​

   c.​ Fraudulent Activity refers to any act or series of acts involving deceit,
       misrepresentation, false pretense, or omission of material facts, committed
       with intent to cause financial harm or gain undue advantage, including but
       not limited to:​

         i.​   Investment Scams: Schemes designed to defraud individuals through
               false promises of high returns on investments, such as Ponzi schemes,
               pyramid schemes, or fictitious cryptocurrency ventures.​

        ii.​   False Sales: The selling of non-existent, defective, or grossly overpriced
               goods or services, including but not limited to "ghost" real estate
               properties, fraudulent insurance policies, or non-delivery of purchased
               items.​

       iii.​   Cyber Fraud: Acts committed through the use of computer systems or
               networks, such as phishing, smishing, vishing, pharming, or other
               social engineering tactics aimed at acquiring personal or financial
               information, or engaging in money muling as defined under Republic
               Act No. 12010, also known as the “Anti-Financial Account Scamming
               Act.”​

   d.​ Financial Institution refers to banks, quasi-banks, trust entities, nonbank
       financial institutions, lending companies, financing companies, payment
       systems operators, virtual asset service providers, and other entities
       performing similar functions, which are supervised by the Bangko Sentral ng
       Pilipinas (BSP) or other relevant government regulatory bodies.

       SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts are hereby declared unlawful and
shall be subject to the penalties provided in this Act:​

          a.​ Investment Fraud: Soliciting, offering, or selling investments to OFWs
      or their dependents by misrepresenting material facts regarding the risks,
      returns, or legitimacy of such investments.​

          b.​ False Sales of Goods or Services: Selling or offering for sale to OFWs or
      their dependents goods or services that are non-existent, materially defective,
      or grossly overpriced, with the intent to defraud.​

          c.​ Imposition of Hidden Fees or Coercion: Imposing or charging
      undisclosed fees, commissions, or charges, or using high-pressure tactics,
      intimidation, or undue influence to compel OFWs or their dependents to
      enter into financial transactions, contracts, or agreements against their will or
      best interest.​

         d.​ Financial Account Scamming: Engaging in acts defined under Republic
      Act No. 12010 (Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act), including but not


                                            2
      limited to:​

                  i.​ Money Muling: Allowing or renting out one's financial account,
               whether physical or digital, for illicit transfers of funds.
                 ii.​ Social Engineering: Manipulating individuals through deceptive
               communication to disclose confidential financial information or
               credentials, or to perform actions that result in financial harm.​

         e.​ Agency Collusion: Any act where a recruitment agency, manning
      agency, or any intermediary involved in the deployment or welfare of OFWs
      conspires with or facilitates the commission of any fraudulent activity defined
      under this Act.

      SEC. 5. Penalties. – Any person found guilty of committing any of the
prohibited acts under this Act shall suffer the following penalties:​

   a.​ For Individuals:​

         i.​   First Offense: Imprisonment of twelve (12) years to twenty (20) years
               and a fine of Five Million Pesos (P5,000,000.00) to Ten Million Pesos
               (P10,000,000.00).​

        ii.​   Repeat Offense: Imprisonment of life and a fine of Fifteen Million
               Pesos (P15,000,000.00).

   b.​ For Corporations, Partnerships, Associations, or Other Juridical Entities:​

         i.​   First Offense: A fine of Twenty Million Pesos (P20,000,000.00) and the
               revocation of its license to operate. The president, managing partner, or
               any officer or person responsible for the commission of the offense
               shall also be held liable as an individual.​

        ii.​   Repeat Offense: A fine of Fifty Million Pesos (P50,000,000.00) and
               permanent closure of the entity. The president, managing partner, or
               any officer or person responsible for the commission of the offense
               shall also be held liable as an individual and shall suffer the penalty of
               life imprisonment.

   c.​ Restitution: In addition to the foregoing penalties, the convicted individual or
       entity shall be ordered to make full restitution to the victims of the fraudulent
       activity, which shall include the total amount defrauded plus an annual
       interest of ten percent (10%) from the date of the fraudulent transaction until
       full payment.

       SEC. 6. Enforcement Mechanisms. – To ensure the effective implementation of
this Act, the following mechanisms shall be established:​




                                            3
   a.​ Inter-Agency Council for OFW Fraud Protection: There is hereby created an
       Inter-Agency Council, hereinafter referred to as the "Council," which shall be
       primarily responsible for the coordination of efforts in combating OFW fraud.
       The Council shall be composed of the following:​

         i.​   The Secretary of the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) as
               Chairperson;​

        ii.​   A representative from the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI); * A
               representative from the Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating
               Center (CICC) under Republic Act No. 10175, otherwise known as the
               “Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012”;​

       iii.​   A representative from the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC),
               particularly for cases involving investment scams; and​

       iv.​    A representative from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP),
               particularly for cases involving financial institutions.​

       The Council shall formulate and implement a national action plan against
OFW fraud, conduct regular intelligence sharing, and facilitate inter-agency
investigations.

         a.​ OFW Fraud Hotline and Online Portal: The Department of Migrant
      Workers (DMW) shall establish and maintain a 24/7 dedicated hotline and an
      online portal accessible to OFWs and their dependents for reporting
      fraudulent activities and seeking assistance.​

          b.​ Mandatory Financial Literacy and Fraud Prevention Modules:
      Pre-departure orientation seminars (PDOS) and post-arrival orientation
      seminars (PAOS) for OF Ws shall include comprehensive modules on
      financial literacy, investment awareness, and fraud prevention strategies,
      developed and updated in coordination with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas
      and the Securities and Exchange Commission.

       SEC. 7. Liability Under Other Laws. – Prosecution under this Act shall be
without prejudice to any criminal or civil action that may be instituted under
existing laws, including but not limited to:​

           a.​ Republic Act No. 3815, otherwise known as the “Revised Penal Code”;​

         b.​ Republic Act No. 10175, otherwise known as the “Cybercrime Prevention
      Act of 2012”;​

         c.​ Republic Act No. 11967, otherwise known as the “Internet Transactions
      Act of 2023”; and​
