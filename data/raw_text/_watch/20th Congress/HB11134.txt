                               AN ACT

  REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9946, TO INCLUDE OTHER QUALIFIED DEPENDENTS


                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The passage of Republic Act No. 910, as amended by Republic Act No. 9946, otherwise
known as “An Act Granting Additional Retirement, Survivorship, and Other Benefits to
Members of the Judiciary,” has long been recognized as a significant measure acknowledging
the invaluable contributions of judges and justices to the administration of justice. By granting
retirement, survivorship, and related benefits, the law not only provides financial security to
members of the Judiciary and their families, but also reinforces judicial independence by
ensuring that judges and justices are accorded proper protection even after their service. These
benefits likewise encourage competent and distinguished members of the Bar to join the
Judiciary despite the financial sacrifices and opportunity costs associated with leaving private
practice.

        However, the present provisions of the law on survivorship benefits require
reconsideration and expansion in light of evolving family structures and the realities faced by
members of the Judiciary today. At present, the law primarily limits survivorship benefits to
the surviving legitimate spouse and heirs or those who are entitled to succeed in the estate of
the deceased Judge or Justice under the law on succession. Such limitation fails to account for
situations where a judge or justice, although unmarried at the time of death, leaves behind
dependents who relied upon them for support and who would otherwise qualify as their lawful
heirs.

        In modern society, the concept of “heirs” and “dependents” has become more nuanced
and should not be confined to a restrictive interpretation. The law must recognize that members
of the Judiciary may have other qualified dependents, who remain financially and emotionally
dependent upon them regardless of marital status. Extending survivorship benefits to these
dependents promotes fairness and ensures that judges and justices who are differently situated
in life are not unjustly excluded from the full protection intended by the law.

        Such an expansion is neither novel nor unprecedented. Section 1 of Republic Act No.
11643, otherwise known as “An Act Granting Survivorship Benefits to the Surviving
Legitimate Spouse and Dependent Children of a Deceased Retired Member of the National
Prosecution Service,” adopts a broader and more inclusive definition of dependents. Under the
said law, dependents include legitimate, illegitimate, or legally adopted children who are
chiefly dependent upon the deceased member of the National Prosecution Service, provided
they are unmarried, not gainfully employed, and not more than twenty-one (21) years of age,
or regardless of age if incapable of self-support due to mental or physical incapacity.

        Under similar considerations, this Bill seeks to expand the coverage of heirs and
dependents entitled to survivorship benefits under Republic Act No. 910, as amended, in order
to reflect the present realities faced by members of the Judiciary and their families. By doing
so, the proposed measure strengthens the State’s continuing recognition of the sacrifices and
service rendered by judges and justices, while ensuring equal and compassionate treatment
regardless of their civil status or family circumstances.

       In view of the foregoing, , the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       SECTION 1. Amendment of Section 2. – Section 2 of Republic Act No. 9946, is
hereby further amended to read as follows:

       “SEC. 2. In case a Justice of the Supreme Court or Court of Appeals, the
       Sandiganbayan or of the Court of Tax Appeals, or a Judge of the regional trial
       court, metropolitan trial court, municipal trial court in cities, municipal trial
       court, municipal circuit trial court, shari'a district court, shari'a circuit court, or
       any other court hereafter established, dies while in actual service, regardless of
       his/her age and length of service as required in Section 1 hereof, his/her
       surviving legitimate spouse and dependents shall receive a lump sum of five (5)
       years’ gratuity computed on the basis of the highest monthly salary plus the
       highest monthly aggregate of transportation, representation and other
       allowances such as personal economic relief allowance (PERA) and additional
       compensation allowance received by him/her as such Justice or
       Judge: Provided, however, That where the deceased Justice or Judge has
       rendered at least fifteen (15) years either in the Judiciary or in any other branch
       of Government, or both, his/her surviving legitimate spouse and dependents
       shall instead be entitled to a lump sum of ten (10) years gratuity computed on
       the same basis as indicated in this provision: Provided, further, That the lump
       sum of ten (10) years gratuity shall be received by the surviving legitimate
       spouse and dependents of the Justice or the Judge who was killed because of
       his/her work as such: Provided, That the Justice or Judge has served in
       Government for at least five (5) years regardless of age at the time of death.
      When a Justice or Judge is killed intentionally while in service, the presumption
      is that the death is work-related.”

      SECTION 2. Amendment of Section 3. – Section 3 of the same Act, is hereby further
amended to read as follows:

      “SEC. 3. Upon retirement, a Justice of the Supreme Court or of the Court of
      Appeals, the Sandiganbayan or of the Court of Tax Appeals, or a Judge of the
      regional trial court, metropolitan trial court, municipal trial court in cities,
      municipal trial court, municipal circuit trial court, shari'a district court, shari'a
      circuit court, or any other court hereafter established shall be automatically
      entitled to a lump sum of five (5) years’ gratuity computed on the basis of the
      highest monthly salary plus the highest monthly aggregate of transportation,
      representation and other allowances such as personal economic relief allowance
      (PERA) and additional compensation allowance he/she was receiving on the
      date of his/her retirement and thereafter upon survival after the expiration of
      five (5) years, to further annuity payable monthly during the residue of his/her
      natural life pursuant to Section 1 hereof: Provided, however, That if the reason
      for the retirement be any permanent disability contracted during his/her natural
      life pursuant to Section 1 hereof: Provided, however, That if the reason for the
      retirement be any permanent disability contracted during his/her incumbency in
      office and prior to the date of retirement, he/she shall receive a gratuity
      equivalent to ten (10) years' salary and the allowances
      aforementioned: Provided, further, That should the retirement under Section
      1(a) hereof be with the attendance of any partial permanent disability contracted
      during his/her incumbency and prior to the date of retirement, he/she shall
      receive an additional gratuity equivalent to two (2) years lump sum that he/she
      is entitled to under this Act. Provided, furthermore, That if he/she survives after
      ten (10) years or seven (7) years, as the case may be, he/she shall continue to
      receive a monthly annuity as computed under this Act during the residue of
      his/her natural life pursuant to Section 1 hereof: Provided, finally, That those
      who have retired with the attendance of any partial permanent disability five (5)
      years prior to the effectivity of this Act shall be entitled to the same benefits
      provided herein.

      Upon the death of a Justice or Judge of any court in the Judiciary, if such Justice
      or Judge has retired, or was eligible to retire optionally at the time of death, the
      surviving legitimate spouse and dependents of the Judge or Justice shall be
      entitled to receive all the retirement benefits that the deceased Justice or Judge
      would have received had the Justice or Judge would have received had the
      Justice or Judge not died. The surviving spouse and the dependents shall
      continue to receive such retirement benefits until the surviving spouse’s death
      or remarriage.
       As used in this Act, the term “dependent” refers to the Judge’s or Justice’s
       legitimate, legitimated, legally adopted, or illegitimate child who is unmarried,
       not gainfully employed, and either: (a) below the age of majority; or (b) over
       the age of majority but incapacitated and incapable of self-support due to a
       mental or physical disability acquired prior to reaching the age of majority.

       The term likewise includes the Judge’s or Justice’s parents who are senior
       citizens, mentally incapacitated, physically disabled, or suffering from chronic
       or degenerative illnesses or conditions that substantially impair their capacity
       for self-support, such as Parkinson’s disease, muscular dystrophy, diabetes, and
       similar conditions.”

       SECTION 3. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, parts of provisions of this Act which are not affected shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

       SECTION 4. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, presidential
decrees, Implementing Rules and Regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

       SECTION 5. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
