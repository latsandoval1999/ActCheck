                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Section 28, Article VI of the 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates that the rule of
taxation shall be uniform and equitable, and the Congress shall evolve a progressive
system of taxation. While taxation remains indispensable to funding government
programs and public services, it must not impose an undue burden on workers whose
purchasing power has steadily declined due to inflation.

In line with this, Republic Act No. 10963, also known as the Tax Reform for
Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) Law, has significantly reduced the tax burden on
individual taxpayers by exempting annual taxable income not exceeding Two Hundred
Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php 250,000.00) from income tax. At the time of its enactment,
this threshold provided meaningful tax relief for low- and middle-income earners.
However, persistent inflation, rising consumer prices, and increasing costs of basic
necessities—including food, transportation, housing, education, healthcare, and
utilities—have substantially eroded the real value of the existing exemption threshold.

In recent years, inflation has consistently diminished the purchasing power of Filipino
households, compelling workers to devote a greater proportion of their income to
essential expenditures. As a consequence, many taxpayers whose nominal incomes
have increased merely to keep pace with inflation are nevertheless subjected to higher
income tax liabilities despite experiencing little or no corresponding improvement in
their real standard of living. This phenomenon undermines the objective of
maintaining a fair and progressive tax system.

This measure seeks to increase the annual income tax exemption threshold from Two
Hundred Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php 250,000.00) to Five Hundred Thousand Pesos
(Php 500,000.00). By adjusting the exemption to better reflect current economic
realities, the proposed measure aims to preserve the purchasing power of Filipino
workers, provide meaningful tax relief to low- and middle-income earners, and allow
households to allocate a greater portion of their income toward essential goods,
education, healthcare, housing, savings, and productive investments.

By modernizing the income tax exemption threshold, this bill reaffirms the State's
commitment to a just, progressive, and responsive tax system that protects the
welfare of Filipino workers while promoting inclusive economic growth.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SEC. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty (60) days after the
effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance, upon the recommendation of the
Commissioner of Internal Revenue, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 3. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
and effect.

SEC. 4. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
presidential proclamations, letters of instruction, rules and regulations, or parts
thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or
amended accordingly.

SEC. 5. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following its publication
in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation in the
Philippines.
