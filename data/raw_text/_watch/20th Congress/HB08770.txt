13                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE
14
15   The unprecedented conflict that erupted on February 28, 2026 in the Middle East has
16   fundamentally altered the global energy landscape and exposed the fragility of supply
17   chains that underpin modern economies. The subsequent closure of the Strait of
18   Hormuz on March 4, 2026—one of the most critical chokepoints in global energy
19   trade—has severely disrupted the flow of crude oil and petroleum products
20   worldwide. This corridor, which ordinarily facilitates the transit of approximately
21   twenty percent (20%) of global oil consumption, has been reduced to a near standstill,
22   triggering immediate and sustained supply constraints across international markets.
23
24   Compounding this disruption are sustained attacks on key energy infrastructure in
25   the region, including refineries, export terminals, and gas processing facilities in Iran
26   and Qatar. These developments have significantly curtailed production and export
27   capacity, with Middle Eastern producers reportedly reducing output by at least ten
28   (10) million barrels per day. This has been characterized as the largest supply
29   disruption in modern history. As a direct consequence, benchmark Brent crude prices
30   have surged dramatically, peaking at nearly One Hundred Twenty United States
31   Dollars (US$120) per barrel, with continuing volatility driven by uncertainty in both
32   supply restoration and geopolitical stability.
33
34   The ongoing conflict involving the United States, Israel, and Iran serves as a stark and
35   urgent reminder of the risks inherent in heavy dependence on external energy sources
36   and volatile global markets. As a net oil-importing country, the Philippines is
37   uniquely vulnerable to these external shocks. The country’s exposure is not merely a
38   matter of higher fuel prices, but a broader macroeconomic risk that threatens
39   inflation stability, fiscal balance, and overall economic growth.
40
41   Economic indicators already reflect the severity of the situation. Rising import costs
42   for petroleum products have placed downward pressure on the Philippine peso, which
43   has weakened to an all-time low of ₱60.10 per United States Dollar as of March 2026.
44   This depreciation further amplifies the cost of fuel imports, creating a compounding
45   effect on domestic prices. Economists warn that prolonged instability in global energy
46   markets could significantly dampen the country’s gross domestic product (GDP)
47   growth for the first half of 2026, given the Philippines’ high energy import-to-GDP
48   ratio.
49




                                                1
 1   At the household level, the impact is immediate and profound. Increased fuel prices
 2   translate directly into higher transportation costs, elevated food distribution
 3   expenses, and increased production costs across key industries such as agriculture,
 4   fisheries, manufacturing, and logistics. These cost pressures inevitably cascade into
 5   higher prices for basic goods and services, disproportionately affecting low-income
 6   households and vulnerable sectors. Without timely and decisive intervention, there is
 7   a real risk that these developments will push more Filipinos into poverty and
 8   undermine recent gains in economic recovery and development.
 9
10   In light of these circumstances, the primary objective of this bill is to establish a
11   comprehensive and forward-looking policy framework that will enable the
12   Philippines to withstand and mitigate the adverse effects of global oil price shocks,
13   supply disruptions, geopolitical tensions, and related economic fallout. This measure
14   recognizes that while the country cannot control external events, it can and must
15   strengthen its internal capacity to respond effectively and protect its national
16   interests.
17
18   Central to this proposal is the establishment of a National Strategic Fuel
19   Reserve, which shall serve as a buffer against sudden supply interruptions and price
20   spikes. By maintaining a government-managed reserve of petroleum products, the
21   State will be able to augment domestic supply during periods of crisis, stabilize
22   market conditions, and ensure the continuity of essential economic activities.
23
24   Complementing this is the creation of a Targeted Fuel Relief Program, which
25   provides for a structured and immediate response mechanism to support sectors
26   most affected by rising fuel costs. This includes public transport operators, farmers,
27   fisherfolk, and other fuel-dependent economic actors whose livelihoods are directly
28   impacted by fuel price volatility. By delivering targeted subsidies during periods when
29   global prices exceed defined thresholds, the State can cushion the economic shock on
30   these sectors and prevent broader inflationary spillovers.
31
32   The bill further strengthens domestic resilience by requiring oil companies to
33   maintain adequate minimum inventory levels, thereby ensuring baseline fuel
34   availability within the country. At the same time, it authorizes the development of
35   government-owned and controlled fuel storage facilities, strategically located to
36   support national distribution and emergency response.
37
38   Taken together, these measures form an integrated approach to energy security—one
39   that combines physical reserves, regulatory safeguards, and targeted economic
40   intervention.
41
42   This bill is not merely a reactive response to the current crisis. It is a strategic
43   investment in long-term national resilience. It seeks to institutionalize mechanisms
44   that will allow the Philippines to anticipate, absorb, and respond to future disruptions
45   in the global energy market.
46
47   In an increasingly uncertain world where geopolitical conflicts can abruptly disrupt
48   critical supply chains, the ability of a nation to safeguard its energy security is
49   inseparable from its economic sovereignty. This measure affirms the State’s
50   responsibility to protect its people, sustain its economy, and ensure that, even in
51   times of global instability, the welfare of the Filipino people remains paramount.

13   AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL STRATEGIC FUEL RESERVE
14    AND THE TARGETED FUEL RELIEF PROGRAM, AND PROVIDING
15                       FUNDS THEREFOR

18   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
19   Congress assembled:
20
21   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Fuel Security Act.”
22
23   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
24   to promote a just and dynamic social order that will ensure the prosperity and
25   independence of the nation and free the people from poverty through policies that
26   provide adequate social services, promote full employment, economic development
27   and sustainability, improved standard of living, and quality of life for all. Towards this
28   end, in recognition of the country's dependence on imported oil, and the volatility of
29   global fuel markets and their vulnerability to price shocks, supply disruptions, and
30   geopolitical tensions, the State shall establish a system to stabilize domestic fuel
31   supply and prices, strengthen the country's energy security, and sustain economic
32   development
33
34   Towards this end, the State shall:
35
36   (a) Establish a National Strategic Petroleum Reserve System (NSPRS);
37   (b) Provide rules-based mechanisms for the release of petroleum reserves;
38   (c) Implement targeted and time-bound fuel relief measures; and
39   (d) Ensure transparent, accountable, and fiscally responsible management of
40   petroleum reserves.
41
42
43   SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
44
45      (a) Crude oil refers to oil in its natural state before the same has been refined or
46          otherwise treated, but excluding water, bottom substances and foreign
47          substances.
48      (b) Days supply refers to the equivalent number of days of in-country stocks of
49          petroleum crude oil and products based on the average daily sales or liftings
50          for the past six (6) months.




                                                 4
 1      (c) Petroleum refers to a naturally occurring mixture of compounds of hydrogen
 2          and carbon with a small proportion of impurities and shall include any mineral
 3          oil, petroleum gas, hydrogen gas, bitumen, asphalt, mineral wax, and all other
 4          similar or naturally associated substances, except for coal, peat, bituminous
 5          shale, and/or other stratified mineral fuel deposits.
 6      (d) Petroleum products refers to products formed in the course of refining crude
 7          petroleum through distillation, cracking, solvent refining and chemical
 8          treatment coming out as primary stocks from the refinery such as, but not
 9          limited to, liquified petroleum gas, naphtha, gasolines, solvent, kerosene,
10          aviation fuels, diesel oils, fuel oils, waxes and petrolatums, asphalts, bitumen,
11          coke and refinery sludges, or such refinery petroleum fractions which have not
12          undergone any process or treatment as to produce separate chemically-defined
13          compounds in a pure or commercially pure state and to which various
14          substances may have been added to render them suitable for particular uses:
15          Provided, That the resultant product contains not less than fifty percent (50%)
16          by weight of such petroleum products.
17      (e) Fuel inventory refers to the quantity of crude oil, petroleum, and petroleum
18          products.

20   SECTION 4. National Strategic Fuel Reserve. – There is hereby established a
21   National Strategic Fuel Reserve, hereinafter referred to as the Reserve, under the
22   Department of Energy (DOE), which shall be composed of a stockpile of crude oil,
23   petroleum, and petroleum products to be maintained for the purpose of mitigating
24   the adverse effects of severe oil market shocks and supply disruptions and ensuring
25   the long-term stability of domestic fuel supply and prices
26
27   The NSPRS shall consist of a hybrid reserve system, including:
28   (a) Government-owned physical petroleum reserves;
29   (b) Leased storage capacity;
30   (c) Contractual stockholding arrangements; and
31   (d) Emergency supply agreements with domestic and international suppliers.
32
33   The NSPRS shall be administered by the DOE through the Philippine National Oil
34   Company (PNOC) or its designated subsidiary.
35
36
37   SECTION 5. Fuel Inventory. – The Department of Energy (DOE) shall be
38   responsible for the formulation of policies for the establishment, buildup,
39   management, utilization and replenishment of the Reserve, including the
40   determination of the optimal level of fuel inventory to be maintained by the
41   government: Provided, That such level shall not be less than the equivalent of ninety
42   (90) days supply nor more than one hundred eighty (180) days supply, as may be
43   determined by the DOE, taking into account supply risks, prevailing fuel prices,
44   current storage capacity, and national energy requirements.
45
46   The strategic petroleum reserve shall be built in phases:
47   (a) Phase I (Years 1–2): Minimum of fifteen (15) to thirty (30) days of national net
48   import cover;
49   (b) Phase II (Years 3–5): Minimum of thirty (30) to forty-five (45) days;
50   (c) Phase III: Expansion subject to fiscal capacity, infrastructure readiness, and
51   energy security assessment.

15   SECTION 6. Acquisition of Fuel Inventory. – The acquisition of fuel inventory

26   (g) Swap arrangements, stockholding agreements, or similar business undertakings;

32   (a) Best value procurement
33   (b) Price risk management
34   (c) Supply diversification
35   (d) Transparency and audit compliance

38   (DOF) and Department of Budget and Management (DBM).

41   SECTION 7. Storage of Fuel Inventory. – The DOE, through the PNOC or its
42   designated subsidiary, shall be responsible for the construction, management,
43   maintenance, and operation of fuel storage facilities, and the procurement, handling,
44   storage, quality maintenance, rotation and transport of fuel inventory for the Reserve.
45
46   Further, the DOE, through the PNOC or its designated subsidiary, shall develop,
47   establish, operate, and maintain a national network of strategic petroleum storage
48   facilities necessary for the implementation of the National Strategic Petroleum
49   Reserve System (NSPRS). For this purpose, the DOE shall, within one hundred eighty
50   (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, formulate and publish a National Petroleum




                                               6
 1   Reserve Infrastructure Plan, which shall identify and prioritize strategic locations for
 2   storage facilities based on the following criteria:
 3
 4   (a) Proximity to major ports of entry and import terminals;
 5   (b) Accessibility to primary fuel distribution corridors and consumption centers;
 6   (c) Geographic distribution across Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao to ensure supply
 7   resilience;
 8   (d) Suitability of land, including availability, zoning classification, and hazard risk
 9   profile;
10   (e) Compliance with environmental, safety, and disaster-resilience standards;
11   (f) National security considerations, including protection of critical infrastructure.
12
13   Storage facilities may be developed and operated through any of the following
14   modalities:
15
16   (1) Government-owned and operated facilities;
17   (2) Public-private partnership arrangements, including build-operate-transfer or
18   joint venture agreements;
19   (3) Lease or utilization of existing storage facilities owned by private entities;
20   (4) Co-location with existing oil depots or energy infrastructure, subject to
21   regulatory compliance.
22   All facilities established under this Act shall comply with:
23   (a) Environmental compliance requirements, including the issuance of
24   Environmental Compliance Certificates (ECC);
25   (b) Fire safety, occupational safety, and hazardous materials handling standards;
26   (c) Structural and engineering standards for resilience against natural disasters;
27   (d) Security protocols for critical energy infrastructure.
28
29   The DOE shall ensure that all storage facilities are equipped with appropriate systems
30   for inventory monitoring, quality control, leak detection, and emergency response.

32
33   SECTION 8. Utilization of Fuel Inventory. – The fuel inventory in the Reserve
34   may be released, utilized or otherwise disposed of by the President of the Philippines,
35   upon the recommendation of the Secretary of Energy, under any of the following
36   Circumstances:
37
38       (a) In the event of a severe fuel supply disruption as evidenced by at least a twenty
39           percent (20%) decline in the national fuel inventory maintained by the
40           government and private sector from their average levels for a sustained
41           period of seven (7) days, or such other threshold or period as may be
42           determined by the DOE based on the prevailing market conditions and
43           national energy requirements;
44
45       (b) In the event of a surge in international fuel prices, as evidenced by a twenty
46           percent (20%) increase in the weekly average Dubai Crude Oil Price based on
47           the Mean of Platts Singapore compared to the preceding week, or such other
48           threshold or period as may be determined by the DOE based on the prevailing
49           market conditions and national energy requirements;
50

 1       (c) In the event of a State of Calamity or State of Emergency as may be declared
 2           by the President of the Philippines or the local sanggunian concerned, in
 3           accordance with Republic Act No. 10121, otherwise known as the “Philippine
 4           Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010;” Republic Act No.
 5           11332, otherwise is known as the “Mandatory Reporting of Notifiable
 6           Diseases and Health Events of Public Health Concern Act;” and other relevant
 7           laws, rules, and regulations; and
 8
 9       (d) For stock rotation, quality management, replacement of aging fuel inventory,
10           and such other activities to ensure usability and readiness of the Reserve.
11
12   The PNOC or its designated subsidiary shall implement the release, utilization,
13   disposition and distribution of fuel inventory in accordance with the policies,
14   guidelines, and standards promulgated by the DOE.

16   SECTION 9. Fuel Industry Participation. – The DOE may engage oil

23        (c) Transport and distribution of crude oil, petroleum and petroleum products;

27   The DOE may enter into agreements with petroleum industry participants, including
28   importers, refiners, distributors, and storage providers, to support the
29   implementation of this Act. Such participation may include:
30
31   (a) Provision of storage capacity through lease or contractual arrangements;
32   (b) Stockholding agreements to maintain specified reserve levels on behalf of the
33   government;
34   (c) Logistics and distribution support during reserve release operations;
35   (d) Emergency supply contracts to ensure continuity of fuel supply.

37   All agreements shall include provisions ensuring:
38   (1) Fair and transparent pricing mechanisms;
39   (2) Compliance with applicable competition laws and regulations;

49   existing laws, including minimum inventory requirements, licensing regulations, and

 2   SECTION 10. Targeted Fuel Relief Program. – There is hereby established a
 3   Targeted Fuel Relief Program (TFRP) to provide temporary financial assistance to
 4   vulnerable sectors affected by extraordinary increases in fuel prices. The program
 5   shall be activated upon the occurrence of any of the triggering conditions for reserve
 6   release under Section 8 of this Act.
 7
 8   The following shall be eligible beneficiaries, subject to verification through existing
 9   national registries:
10
11   (a) Public transport drivers and operators registered with the Land Transportation
12   Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB);
13   (b) Farmers and fisherfolk registered under the Registry System for Basic Sectors in
14   Agriculture (RSBSA) or equivalent databases;
15   (c) Accredited logistics providers and supply chain operators essential to food and
16   commodity distribution;
17   (d) Qualified micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) engaged in fuel-
18   dependent economic activities, as determined by the Department of Trade and
19   Industry (DTI).
20
21   The TFRP shall adhere to the following principles:
22
23   (1) Targeted — limited to verified beneficiaries;
24   (2) Time-bound — effective only during crisis periods;
25   (3) Non-duplicative — coordinated with existing subsidy and social protection
26   programs;
27   (4) Efficiently delivered — through digital or direct payment mechanisms where
28   practicable.
29
30   The DOE, in coordination with relevant agencies, shall establish:
31   (a) Beneficiary validation and registration procedures;
32   (b) Benefit computation and distribution mechanisms;
33   (c) Safeguards against fraud, duplication, and abuse;
34   (d) A grievance and appeals mechanism for beneficiaries.
35

48   (1) Appropriations under the General Appropriations Act;
49   (2) Proceeds from the rotation or disposition of petroleum reserves;
50   (3) Other lawful sources as may be authorized by law.

13   SECTION 12. Transparency, Audit, and Anti-Corruption Safeguards. – All
14   activities, transactions, and operations undertaken pursuant to this Act shall be
15   subject to strict standards of transparency, accountability, and audit, consistent with
16   existing laws, rules, and regulations. The Department of Energy (DOE), through the
17   Philippine National Oil Company (PNOC) or its designated subsidiary, shall ensure
18   that:
19
20   (a) All procurement, acquisition, storage, and disposition of petroleum reserves are
21   conducted in accordance with applicable procurement laws, including Republic Act
22   No. 9184, or the Government Procurement Reform Act, and its implementing rules
23   and regulations, except in cases of emergency procurement as may be allowed by law;
24   (b) All emergency procurement undertaken pursuant to this Act shall be properly
25   documented, justified, and subject to post-audit by the Commission on Audit (COA),
26   and shall be disclosed to Congress in accordance with reporting requirements under
27   this Act;
28   (c) A system of independent inventory verification shall be established to ensure the
29   accuracy of reserve volumes, quality standards, and storage conditions, including
30   periodic physical inspections and third-party certification where appropriate;
31   (d) Losses due to shrinkage, evaporation, contamination, or other causes shall be
32   monitored, recorded, and reported, and shall be subject to allowable thresholds to be
33   defined in the implementing rules and regulations;
34   (e) All contracts entered into with private sector participants shall include provisions
35   on transparency, audit access, performance standards, and penalties for non-
36   compliance;
37   (f) The DOE shall maintain records of all reserve-related transactions, including
38   procurement costs, storage fees, logistics expenses, and distribution records, which
39   shall be made available for audit and congressional oversight;
40   (g) Aggregate, non-sensitive information on reserve levels, utilization, and
41   expenditures shall be made available to the public in a manner consistent with
42   national security considerations.
