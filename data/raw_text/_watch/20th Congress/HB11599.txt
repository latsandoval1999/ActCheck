                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution affirms that the state shall promote full employment, afford full protection to
labor and to promote equality of employment opportunities for all, and ordains that all regions of
the country shall be given optimum opportunity to develop.

Notwithstanding these constitutional mandates, economic activity remains heavily concentrated in
the capital. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority, the NCR accounted for 31.2 percent
of the national economy in 2024. This is despite of the fact that based on the 2020 Census of
Population and Housing conducted by the same agency, the NCR is home to only 12.37 percent
of the Philippine population. As a consequence, workers from the provinces are constrained to
leave their homes and families in order to secure gainful employment.

Existing fiscal incentives do not sufficiently address this disparity. According to the Fiscal
Incentives Review Board, the incentives under the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as
amended by Republic Act No. 11534 or the CREATE Act and Republic Act No. 12066 or the
CREATE MORE Act, including the additional deduction of fifty percent (50%) on labor expense,
are available only to registered business enterprises. No corresponding incentive is extended to the
ordinary employer that establishes operations in the provinces and employs the residents thereof.
The present measure seeks to fill this gap by attaching the incentive to the employment itself.

The proposed measure is patterned after two (2) foreign models. The first is the Work Opportunity
Tax Credit or WOTC under Section 51 of the United States Internal Revenue Code. According to
the United States Internal Revenue Service, the WOTC, enacted in 1996, allows an employer to
claim, as a credit against federal income tax, forty percent (40%) of up to US$6,000 of the first-
year wages paid to a newly hired individual belonging to a targeted group, among them the
residents of designated empowerment zones and rural renewal counties. From the WOTC, this
bill adopts the form of the incentive, namely, a tax credit computed on the wages of a newly hired
employee and granted by reason of the place of residence of such employee.

The second is the Jobs Growth Incentive or JGI of Singapore. According to the Inland Revenue
Authority of Singapore, which administered the program, the government co-funded up to twenty-

five percent (25%) of the first S$5,000 of the gross monthly wages of every new local hire for
twelve (12) months, and up to fifty percent (50%) in the case of mature workers, persons with
disabilities and ex-offenders, on the condition that the local workforce of the employer had
increased over its baseline. From the JGI, this bill adopts its safeguards, to wit: that the incentive
be granted only for employment that is genuinely new, and that a higher rate be accorded to
workers who face greater difficulty in securing employment.

Accordingly, the proposed measure grants the employer a credit against income tax equivalent to
twenty-five percent (25%) of the first-year wages of every resident of the provinces hired for work
in the provinces, and forty percent (40%) in the case of priority hires, such as workers aged forty
(40) years and above, persons with disabilities, and members of households covered by the Pantawid
Pamilyang Pilipino Program. In order to protect government revenues, the credit shall be allowed only
for employment in excess of the existing workforce of the employer, and only after the employee
has rendered at least six (6) months of service.

It bears stressing that both models were instituted as temporary measures. As stated by the Inland
Revenue Authority of Singapore, the JGI covered new hires from September 2020 to March 2023
only. According to the United States Department of Labor, the WOTC is authorized until 31
December 2025, and as reported by CPA Practice Advisor in January 2026, a measure for its
further extension has been filed in both chambers of the United States Congress. Consistent
therewith, the credit herein proposed shall apply only to hiring made within five (5) years from the
effectivity of the Act, after which Congress shall review its results.

The creation of employment in the provinces will keep Filipino families together and retain wages
within the local economy.

In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
PROMOTING EMPLOYMENT IN THE PROVINCES BY GRANTING A TAX
CREDIT TO DOMESTIC AND FOREIGN ENTERPRISES THAT HIRE
RESIDENTS OUTSIDE THE NATIONAL CAPITAL REGION, APPROPRIATING
FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Provincial Jobs Tax Credit
Act of 2026."

   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to promote full employment and
equality of employment opportunities for all, and to give all regions of the country optimum
opportunity to develop. Toward this end, the State shall:

    a. Encourage enterprises to create jobs where Filipinos live, so that workers need not leave
       their home provinces to find gainful employment;
    b. Reduce the concentration of employment and economic activity in the National Capital
       Region;
    c. Grant fiscal incentives for employment that are performance-based, targeted, time-bound
       and transparent; and
    d. Accord domestic and foreign enterprises equal access to such incentives, consistent with
       the Constitution and existing laws.

    SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, and unless indicated otherwise, the
following terms shall be defined as:

    a. Baseline headcount refers to the average monthly number of Filipino employees of a
       covered employer during the twelve (12) months immediately preceding the month in
       which it hires its first qualified provincial employee, as shown in its contribution reports
       to the Social Security System (SSS). The baseline headcount, once determined, shall remain
       fixed for the entire period of availment under Section 13 of this Act. The baseline
       headcount of a newly organized covered employer shall be zero: Provided, That a covered
       employer that succeeds to or continues the business of another person, in whole or in part,


                                                                                                     1
    shall     assume        the      baseline       headcount        of       its     predecessor.

b. Covered employer refers to any person engaged in trade or business or in the practice of a
   profession in the Philippines and subject to income tax under Title II of the National
   Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended (NIRC), including domestic corporations,
   resident foreign corporations, partnerships and individuals, regardless of the nationality of
   ownership. The term excludes the national government, its agencies and instrumentalities,
   local government units, and government-owned or -controlled corporations.

c. Qualified provincial employee refers to a Filipino citizen who meets all of the following:

       i.   The individual has been a bona fide resident of a city or municipality outside the
            NCR for at least twelve (12) consecutive months immediately preceding the date
            of hiring;
      ii.   The individual is hired on or after the effectivity of this Act, directly by the covered
            employer, under an employer-employee relationship;
     iii.   The individual's principal place of work is outside the NCR. Work performed
            under a telecommuting arrangement pursuant to Republic Act No. 11165, or the
            Telecommuting Act, from a residence outside the NCR shall be deemed work
            performed outside the NCR;
     iv.    The individual is paid not less than the applicable minimum wage and is reported
            by the covered employer for coverage under the SSS, the Philippine Health
            Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) and the Home Development Mutual Fund
            (HDMF); and
      v.    The individual is not excluded under Section 6 of this Act.

d. Priority hire refers to a qualified provincial employee who, on the date of hiring, is any of
   the following:
       i. Forty (40) years of age or older;
      ii. A person with disability as defined in Republic Act No. 7277, or the Magna Carta
           for Persons with Disability, as amended;
     iii. A member of a qualified household-beneficiary under Republic Act No. 11310, or
           the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) Act;
     iv. An individual who has not been employed for at least six (6) consecutive months
           immediately preceding the date of hiring, as shown in the records of the SSS; or
      v. A resident of a geographically isolated and disadvantaged area, or of a municipality
           belonging to either of the two (2) lowest income classes under the prevailing
           income          classification       of       local       government            units.

e. Qualified wages refers to the basic salary or wage actually paid by a covered employer to
   a qualified provincial employee for services rendered during the first twelve (12) months
   of employment, as reported to the SSS and the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), not
   exceeding Twenty-five thousand pesos (₱25,000.00) for any month. The Secretary of
   Finance shall adjust this ceiling every three (3) years to its present value using the consumer



                                                                                                  2
       price     index      published      by     the      Philippine     Statistics    Authority.

   f. Related party refers to any person related to the covered employer in the manner described
      in Section 36(B) of the NIRC, and any entity that controls, is controlled by, or is under
      common control with the covered employer.

        SEC. 4. Provincial Jobs Tax Credit. – A covered employer shall be allowed a credit against
its income tax due under Title II of the NIRC equal to twenty-five percent (25%) of the qualified
wages paid to each qualified provincial employee. The rate shall be forty percent (40%) of the
qualified wages paid to each priority hire.

        SEC. 5. Conditions for Entitlement. – The credit under Section 4 of this Act shall be
subject to the following conditions:

     a. Net increase in employment. – Qualified wages paid for a given month shall be creditable
        only if the number of Filipino employees of the covered employer in that month, as
        shown in its contribution reports to the SSS, exceeds its baseline headcount, and only for
        a number of qualified provincial employees not exceeding that excess. Where the
        qualified provincial employees outnumber the excess, the covered employer shall
        designate those for whom the credit is claimed.
     b. Minimum period of service. – No credit shall be allowed for wages paid to an employee
        who has rendered less than six (6) months of service with the covered employer. Qualified
        wages paid before the completion of the six-month period shall be credited in the taxable
        year in which that period is completed.
     c. Compliance with labor and social legislation. – For the taxable year in which the credit is
        claimed, the covered employer must have paid not less than the applicable minimum
        wage and all benefits required by law, remitted the contributions due to the SSS,
        PhilHealth and the HDMF, and withheld and remitted the taxes due on compensation.
     d. Certification. – The qualified provincial employee must be covered by a certificate issued
        under Section 7 of this Act.

       SEC. 6. Excluded Individuals. – The following shall not be considered qualified
provincial employees:

     a. A relative within the third civil degree of consanguinity or affinity of the individual
        employer, of any partner, or of any stockholder who owns, directly or indirectly, more
        than fifty percent (50%) of the outstanding capital stock of the covered employer;
     b. An individual who was employed by the covered employer or by any of its related parties
        at any time within the twelve (12) months immediately preceding the date of hiring;
     c. An individual transferred to the covered employer from a related party, or absorbed by
        reason of a merger, consolidation, acquisition of a business, or change in business form
        or name;
     d. An individual deployed to the covered employer by a contractor or subcontractor, insofar
        as the covered employer, as principal, is concerned; and
     e. An individual engaged for a fixed period, project or season of less than six (6) months.

  SEC. 7. Certification and Verification. –

a. Within thirty (30) days from the date of hiring, the covered employer shall file a notice
   of hiring with the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), through its regional
   or field office having jurisdiction over the place of work, the Public Employment Service
   Office concerned, or an online facility established for the purpose. The notice shall be
   accompanied by the sworn declaration of the employee as to residence and, where
   applicable, status as a priority hire, and by proof of residence in the form of a record
   under the Philippine Identification System, a barangay certification, or such other proof
   as the implementing rules may allow.
b. The DOLE shall issue or deny a Certificate of Qualified Provincial Employment within
   twenty (20) working days from receipt of a complete notice. A notice not acted upon
   within that period shall be deemed approved, consistent with Republic Act No. 11032,
   or the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018.
c. The baseline headcount, monthly headcount and qualified wages of a covered employer
   shall be determined from the contribution and withholding records held by the SSS,
   PhilHealth, the HDMF and the BIR. These agencies and the DOLE shall share the data
   necessary to implement this Act, subject to Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data Privacy
   Act of 2012. No covered employer shall be required to submit information that is already
   in the possession of these agencies.
d. The certificate attests only to the residence and status of the employee. It shall not limit
   the authority of the Commissioner of Internal Revenue to examine the returns of the
   covered employer.

SEC. 8. Manner of Claiming the Credit; Limitations. –

a. The credit shall be claimed in the annual income tax return for the taxable year in which
   the qualified wages were paid, subject to Section 5(b) of this Act.
b. The credit shall be neither refundable nor transferable. Any amount in excess of the
   income tax due for the taxable year may be carried over and applied against the income
   tax due for the three (3) succeeding taxable years.
c. The deduction for salaries and wages otherwise allowable under Section 34(A) of the
   NIRC shall be reduced by the amount of the credit allowed for the taxable year.
d. Wages for which the credit is claimed shall not be the basis of any additional deduction,
   tax credit or similar incentive under Republic Act No. 7277, as amended, Republic Act
   No. 9994, Title XIII of the NIRC, or any other law. The covered employer shall elect
   which incentive to avail of for the same wages.
e. A registered business enterprise under Title XIII of the NIRC shall not claim the credit
   on wages attributable to a registered project or activity for any period in which that
   project or activity enjoys an income tax holiday or the special corporate income tax. A
   registered business enterprise under the enhanced deductions regime may claim the credit
   in lieu of, and not in addition to, the additional deduction on labor expense for the same
   wages.

SEC. 9. Recapture and Anti - Avoidance Policies. –


                                                                                             4
     a. The credit previously allowed for a qualified provincial employee shall be added to the
        income tax due of the covered employer, with interest under Section 249 of the NIRC,
        in the taxable year in which any of the following is established:
            i. The employee was dismissed without just or authorized cause within twenty-four
               (24) months from the date of hiring, as finally determined by the competent
               authority;
           ii. The covered employer terminated existing employees, or reduced their hours of
               work, in order to replace them with qualified provincial employees; or
          iii. The certificate under Section 7 of this Act was obtained through a false or
               misleading statement.
     b. The Commissioner of Internal Revenue may disregard any arrangement whose principal
        purpose is to obtain the credit without a real increase in employment outside the NCR,
        including the shifting of employees or the splitting of wages among related parties.

         SEC. 10. Equal Treatment of Domestic and Foreign Enterprises. – The credit shall be
available on equal terms to all covered employers regardless of the nationality of their ownership,
including resident foreign corporations and domestic corporations with foreign equity, provided
they are duly registered and licensed to do business in the Philippines. Nothing in this Act shall be
construed to allow foreign ownership or participation in any activity reserved or limited by the
Constitution or by law, or to dispense with the requirements for the employment of foreign
nationals. The Board of Investments and the investment promotion agencies shall include the
credit in their investment promotion programs.

         SEC. 11. Complementary Local Incentives. – Provinces, cities and municipalities outside
the NCR may, by ordinance and pursuant to Section 192 of Republic Act No. 7160, or the Local
Government Code of 1991, grant tax exemptions, incentives or reliefs from local taxes, fees and
charges to covered employers that hire qualified provincial employees within their territorial
jurisdiction.

        SEC. 12. Monitoring, Reporting and Evaluation. –

     a. Credits claimed under this Act shall be covered by Republic Act No. 10708, or the Tax
        Incentives Management and Transparency Act.
     b. Not later than June 30 of each year, the BIR and the DOLE shall jointly submit to
        Congress, through the Congressional Oversight Committee on the Comprehensive Tax
        Reform Program, and to the Fiscal Incentives Review Board, a report for the preceding
        year stating the number of covered employers that claimed the credit, the number of
        qualified provincial employees and priority hires by region and province, the qualified
        wages paid, the credits claimed, and the revenue foregone. The report shall be published
        on the websites of both agencies.
     c. Not later than the end of the fourth (4th) year from the effectivity of this Act, the
        Department of Finance and the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development
        shall submit to Congress an evaluation of the net employment created, the cost to the
        government of each job created, and the effect of the credit on the regional distribution
        of employment.


                                                                                                   5
         SEC. 13. Period of Availment and Congressional Review. – The credit shall apply to
qualified provincial employees hired within five (5) years from the effectivity of this Act. Credits
arising from such hiring may be claimed and carried over in accordance with Section 8 of this Act
even after that period. Before the period ends, Congress shall review the evaluation submitted
under Section 12(c) of this Act to determine whether the credit shall be extended, modified or
allowed to lapse.

        SEC. 14. Penalties. – Any person who willfully makes a false or misleading statement in
any notice, declaration or return required under this Act, or who willfully claims a credit to which
that person is not entitled, shall be subject to the penalties provided under Title X of the NIRC
and other applicable laws, without prejudice to liability for perjury under the Revised Penal Code.
A covered employer found to have claimed the credit through fraud shall, in addition to recapture
under Section 9 of this Act, be disqualified from the credit for the remainder of the period of
availment.

        SEC. 15. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act, including the online facility and the data-sharing arrangements under Section 7 hereof, shall
be charged against the current appropriations of the DOLE and the BIR. Thereafter, such sums
as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance, upon the recommendation of the
Commissioner of Internal Revenue and in coordination with the Secretary of Labor and
Employment, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary to implement this Act, after
consultation with the Department of Trade and Industry, the Department of the Interior and Local
Government, the SSS, PhilHealth, the HDMF, and representatives of employers and workers.

        SEC. 17. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any provision of this Act, or any part
thereof, is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions hereof not otherwise affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

        SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations or parts
thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 19. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

       Approved.
