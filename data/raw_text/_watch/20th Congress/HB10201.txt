                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines is home to diverse Indigenous Cultural
Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) whose traditional farming systems
and agricultural knowledge have long contributed to biodiversity conservation,
sustainable land management, food security, and cultural preservation. However,
many indigenous farming practices and knowledge systems are increasingly
threatened by modernization, environmental degradation, cultural erosion, and
unauthorized commercial exploitation.

      Despite the importance of indigenous agricultural knowledge, there
remains a need for stronger institutional mechanisms to protect traditional
farming practices, preserve indigenous seed systems and biocultural heritage, and
ensure that ICCs/IPs retain ownership and equitable benefits from the use of their
knowledge and resources.

       This bill seeks to protect, preserve, and promote indigenous farming
practices and traditional agricultural knowledge systems through documentation
with Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC), protection against
misappropriation and biopiracy, support for indigenous farming communities,
  and the integration of sustainable indigenous practices into agricultural, climate
  resilience, and biodiversity conservation programs.

         The measure likewise recognizes the collective rights of ICCs/IPs over
  their traditional knowledge and establishes safeguards against unauthorized
  commercial or scientific use.

         By strengthening support for indigenous farming systems and cultural
  heritage, this measure aims to promote environmental sustainability, cultural
  integrity, and inclusive rural development.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine
Indigenous Farming Practices Protection Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Consistent with the provisions of the 1987
Constitution and Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997, it is hereby declared
the policy of the State to recognize, respect, protect, preserve, and promote the
indigenous farming practices, traditional agricultural knowledge systems,
cultural expressions, and biocultural heritage of Indigenous Cultural
Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs).
The State recognizes indigenous farming systems as vital contributors to
biodiversity conservation, climate resilience, sustainable land management,
agroecology, food security, cultural identity, and intergenerational knowledge
transmission.


Toward this end, the State shall safeguard indigenous agricultural knowledge and
practices from unauthorized appropriation, exploitation, misuse, and cultural
erosion, while supporting the sustainable development and economic
empowerment of traditional farming communities.


SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act aims to:
   a) Identify, document, preserve, and protect indigenous and traditional
      farming knowledge, agricultural techniques, and cultural expressions
      related to farming and natural resource management;
   b) Promote intergenerational transmission of indigenous agricultural
      knowledge systems to younger generations;
   c) Protect ICCs/IPs from unauthorized commercial exploitation, biopiracy,
      misappropriation, or misuse of indigenous farming knowledge and genetic
      resources;
   d) Ensure equitable benefit-sharing arising from the use of indigenous
      agricultural knowledge, products, or biological resources;
   e) Integrate sustainable indigenous farming practices into agricultural
      development, climate adaptation, biodiversity conservation, and food
      security programs where appropriate;
   f) Strengthen community-based conservation of indigenous seeds, heirloom
      crops, and traditional farming landscapes; and
   g) Support sustainable livelihood opportunities linked to indigenous
      agricultural heritage and ecological stewardship.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
hereby defined:
   a) “Indigenous Farming Practices” refer to traditional agricultural systems,
      cultivation methods, land-use practices, seed management systems,
      ecological knowledge, and cultural agricultural practices developed,
      preserved, and transmitted by ICCs/IPs through generations;
   b) “Traditional Agricultural Knowledge Systems” refer to collective
      knowledge, innovations, technologies, practices, and cultural traditions
      relating to agriculture, biodiversity, natural resource management, and
      food systems maintained by ICCs/IPs;
   c) “Biocultural Heritage” refers to the interconnected relationship between
      indigenous communities, their cultural traditions, ancestral lands,
      biodiversity, farming systems, and ecological knowledge;
   d) “Benefit-Sharing” refers to the equitable sharing of monetary and non-
      monetary benefits arising from the use, research, commercialization, or
      application of indigenous agricultural knowledge or genetic resources;
   e) “Indigenous Seeds” or “Heirloom Varieties” refer to traditional, locally
      adapted, and culturally significant crop varieties preserved and cultivated
      by indigenous and traditional farming communities;
   f) “Agroecology” refers to sustainable farming approaches that integrate
      ecological principles, biodiversity conservation, and traditional knowledge
      systems into agricultural production;
   g) “Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC)” refers to the consensus of
      ICCs/IPs obtained in accordance with their customary laws and practices,
      free from manipulation, coercion, and undue influence, after full disclosure
      of the intent and scope of any activity affecting them;

   h) “Community Knowledge Registry” refers to a protected inventory or
      documentation system containing indigenous agricultural knowledge and
      practices subject to community-defined access and disclosure protocols.


SEC. 5. Recognition and Documentation of Indigenous Farming Practices. –
(a) The Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the National
Commission on Indigenous Peoples, the National Commission for Culture and
the Arts, Local Government Units (LGUs), State Universities and Colleges
(SUCs), accredited indigenous peoples’ organizations, and relevant stakeholders,
shall conduct a systematic identification, documentation, and inventory of
indigenous and traditional agricultural practices, including but not limited to:
   1. Traditional rice terracing and indigenous irrigation systems;
   2. Indigenous pest management and organic farming practices;
   3. Indigenous seed conservation and heirloom crop varieties;
   4. Agroforestry, shifting cultivation, and multi-cropping systems rooted in
      cultural traditions;
   5. Traditional soil and water conservation techniques;
   6. Indigenous food systems and agricultural biodiversity practices; and
   7. Other traditional ecological and agricultural knowledge systems.


(b) All documentation activities shall strictly comply with FPIC requirements and
customary laws of the concerned ICCs/IPs.


(c) Documentation under this Act shall not diminish, transfer, waive, or
extinguish the collective ownership rights of ICCs/IPs over their traditional
knowledge, cultural expressions, biological resources, or farming systems.




                                                                                   4
(d) Sacred, culturally sensitive, confidential, or community-restricted knowledge
shall not be disclosed, published, digitized, or publicly accessed without the
express consent of the concerned ICCs/IPs.


SEC. 6. Community Knowledge Registry and Protection Mechanisms. – The
DA, in coordination with the NCIP and NCCA, shall establish a protected
Community Knowledge Registry for indigenous farming practices and
agricultural heritage systems.


The Registry shall:
   a) Recognize       community ownership and control over documented
      knowledge;
   b) Establish varying levels of confidentiality and restricted access consistent
      with customary laws and community protocols;
   c) Prevent unauthorized commercial appropriation or public disclosure of
      protected knowledge;
   d) Serve as reference for conservation, educational, and cultural preservation
      purposes; and
   e) Support recognition of indigenous agricultural systems as important
      cultural and ecological heritage sites.


SEC. 7. Protection Against Misappropriation, Biopiracy, and Unauthorized
Use. – (a) Indigenous farming knowledge, traditional agricultural systems,
indigenous seeds, and related biological resources shall be protected from
unauthorized exploitation, commercial use, patenting, or scientific appropriation.




                                                                                 5
(b) Any research, publication, commercial activity, product development, or
scientific use involving indigenous agricultural knowledge, genetic resources, or
cultural expressions shall require:
   1. Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC) of the concerned ICCs/IPs;
   2. Equitable benefit-sharing agreements; and
   3. Compliance with applicable laws, including the Indigenous Peoples'
      Rights Act of 1997 and environmental and intellectual property laws.


(c) The State shall recognize that indigenous communities retain collective
ownership and control over their traditional agricultural knowledge and practices.


SEC. 8. Indigenous Seed Sovereignty and Biodiversity Conservation. – The
State shall protect indigenous seed systems, heirloom crop varieties, and
traditional seed exchange practices of ICCs/IPs.


The DA and relevant agencies shall support:
   a) Community seed banks and indigenous seed conservation initiatives;
   b) Protection and propagation of heirloom and climate-resilient crop
      varieties;
   c) Conservation of traditional farming landscapes and agro-biodiversity
      areas; and
   d) Community-led biodiversity conservation programs.


SEC. 9. Integration into Agricultural Extension, Education, and Research. –
(a) The DA, SUCs, and relevant educational institutions, in collaboration with
the NCIP and NCCA, shall develop culturally appropriate programs integrating
indigenous agricultural knowledge into:



                                                                                 6
   1. Agricultural extension services;
   2. Community-based trainings;
   3. Agroecology and climate resilience programs;
   4. Educational curricula where appropriate; and
   5. Sustainable agriculture and biodiversity conservation initiatives.


(b) Indigenous farming systems that have demonstrated sustainability, ecological
resilience, and cultural significance shall be promoted alongside science-based
and modern agricultural approaches.


(c) Research involving indigenous agricultural knowledge shall comply with
ethical   standards, FPIC requirements,       and community       benefit-sharing
mechanisms.


SEC. 10. Support for Traditional Farming Communities. – The DA, in
partnership with other relevant agencies and LGUs, shall develop support
programs for indigenous and traditional farming communities, including:
   a) Technical assistance and capacity-building;
   b) Support for sustainable and climate-resilient farming systems;
   c) Marketing assistance and value-chain development for culturally
      significant agricultural products;
   d) Geographical indication (GI), heritage branding, and product certification
      initiatives;
   e) Sustainable livelihood and agricultural tourism programs;
   f) Infrastructure and post-harvest support; and
   g) Programs supporting youth engagement and intergenerational knowledge
      transmission.



                                                                                7
Priority shall be given to community-led and cooperative-based initiatives.


SEC. 11. Indigenous Participation and Governance. – ICCs/IPs shall be
meaningfully represented and consulted in the planning, implementation,
monitoring, and evaluation of programs under this Act.


The DA, NCIP, and LGUs shall establish mechanisms to ensure direct
participation of indigenous elders, knowledge holders, women, farmers, and
community leaders in decision-making processes.


SEC. 12. Annual Report to Congress. – The DA, in coordination with the NCIP
and NCCA, shall submit an annual report to Congress containing:
     a) Documented indigenous farming systems and conservation initiatives;
     b) Programs and assistance provided to ICCs/IPs;
     c) Benefit-sharing agreements and research activities conducted;
     d) Cases of unauthorized use or exploitation addressed under this Act;
     e) Biodiversity and cultural preservation outcomes; and
     f) Recommendations for policy and program improvements.


SEC. 13. Penalties. – Any person, corporation, institution, or entity found guilty
of    unauthorized   commercial     exploitation,   misappropriation,   disclosure,
patenting, falsification, or misuse of indigenous farming knowledge, biological
resources, or protected cultural agricultural practices shall, without prejudice to
liabilities under other laws, be subject to:
     a) A fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00) but
        not more than Five Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00);
     b) Imprisonment of not less than six (6) years but not more than twelve (12)
        years;

                                                                                  8
   c) Cancellation of permits, licenses, or research authorizations; and
   d) Payment of restitution or damages to affected ICCs/IPs.


SEC. 14. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department
of Agriculture, NCIP, and NCCA. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


The Program may also receive grants, donations, and technical assistance from
domestic and international partners, subject to existing laws, rules, and
regulations.


SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DA, in consultation with the NCIP, NCCA, DENR,
LGUs, indigenous peoples’ councils, farmer organizations, SUCs, and other
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations
necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.


SEC. 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.


SEC. 18. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
