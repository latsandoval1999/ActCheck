                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to expand the coverage of Republic Act (RA) No. 11165,
otherwise known as the “Telecommuting Act”, to expressly include employees in the
public sector and to strengthen the implementation of telecommuting arrangements
in both government and private workplaces.

       While RA 11165 institutionalized telecommuting as a voluntary, output-based
work arrangement in the private sector, its application to public sector employees
remains limited and uneven. In light of technological advancements, evolving
workplace practices, and the demonstrated viability of remote work arrangements,
there is a need to clarify and broaden the law’s scope to ensure that qualified
government employees may likewise benefit from flexible work arrangements where
physical presence is not necessary for the performance of their duties.

       The bill defines telecommuting as an output-based work arrangement
available to employees in both the private and public sectors, reinforcing
performance-based standards rather than mere physical attendance. It authorizes
employers and government agencies to offer telecommuting programs on mutually
agreed terms, provided that such arrangements comply with existing labor
standards, civil service rules, and applicable laws on work hours, overtime, rest
days, and leave benefits.

       By institutionalizing flexible work arrangements across sectors and providing
modest but meaningful support for telecommuting employees, this measure
promotes productivity, work-life balance, digital transformation, and more resilient
public and private institutions.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
EXPANDING THE COVERAGE OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11165, OTHERWISE
KNOWN AS THE "TELECOMMUTING ACT," TO INCLUDE PUBLIC SECTOR
EMPLOYEES,   PROVIDING    INCENTIVES FOR   TELECOMMUTING
EMPLOYERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “FLEX WORK (Flexible
Labor & Employment eXpansion for WORKforce) Act”.

       SEC. 2. Section 3 of the Republic Act No. 11165, otherwise known as the
“Telecommuting Act”, is hereby amended to read as follows:

                   “Sec. 3. Telecommuting Defined - As used in this Act, the
             term ‘telecommuting’ refers to AN OUTPUT-BASED WORK
             ARRANGEMENT THAT ALLOWS EMPLOYEES IN THE
             PRIVATE OR PUBLIC SECTOR TO WORK FROM AN
             ALTERNATIVE          WORKPLACE        with    the    use     of
             telecommunication and/or computer technologies.”

      SEC. 3. Section 4 of RA No. 11165 is hereby amended to read as follows:

                    “Sec. 4. Telecommuting Program - An employer in the
             private or public sector may offer a telecommuting program to
             its employees voluntarily, WHOSE PHYSICAL PRESENCE IN
             THE WORKPLACE IS NOT NECESSARY FOR THE
             PERFORMANCE OR COMPLETION OF THEIR JOB, and
             upon such terms and conditions as they may mutually agree
             upon: Provided, That such terms and conditions shall not be less
             than the minimum labor standards set by law, and shall include
             work hours, minimum number of work hours, overtime, rest
             days, and entitlement to leave benefits. In all cases, the employer
             shall provide the telecommuting employee with relevant written

       SEC. 4. Two (2) new sections are hereby inserted after Section 8 of RA No.
11165, which shall read as follows:

                 “SEC. 9. INCENTIVES FOR TELECOMMUTING
            EMPLOYEES. — ALL EMPLOYEES WORKING UNDER A
            TELECOMMUTING PROGRAM SHALL BE ENTITLED TO A
            NON-TAXABLE TELECOMMUTING ALLOWANCE OF AT
            LEAST ONE THOUSAND PESOS (P1,000) FOR EVERY
            MONTH SERVED UNDER SUCH AGREEMENT. PROVIDED,
            THAT, NOTHING IN THIS PROVISION SHALL BE
            CONSTRUED TO ELIMINATE OR IN ANY WAY DIMINISH
            SUPPLEMENTS, ALLOWANCES, OR OTHER EMPLOYEE
            BENEFITS SIMILAR PURPOSE BEING ENJOYED AT THE
            TIME OF THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THIS LAW.
            PROVIDED FURTHER, THAT, NOTHING IN THIS
            PROVISION SHALL BE INTERPRETED TO PROHIBIT THE
            EMPLOYERS OR THE COLLECTIVE BARGAINING
            AGREEMENT FROM PROVIDING TERMS HIGHER OR
            MORE FAVORABLE THAN THE RATE MENTIONED
            ABOVE. PROVIDED, FINALLY, THAT SUCH ALLOWANCES
            SHALL BE CONSIDERED AS ALLOWABLE DEDUCTIONS
            FROM GROSS INCOME UNDER THE NATIONAL
            INTERNAL REVENUE CODE, AS AMENDED.”

                 “SEC. 10. PENALTIES. — ANY VIOLATION OF THE
            PROVISIONS OF THIS ACT SHALL BE PUNISHED IN
            ACCORDANCE WITH EXISTING LABOR LAWS, CIVIL
            SERVICE RULES, AND APPLICABLE REGULATIONS.
            EMPLOYERS WHO FAIL TO PROVIDE THE MANDATED
            TELECOMMUTING         ALLOWANCE      OR     WHO
            UNREASONABLY       DENY     ELIGIBLE   EMPLOYEES
            PARTICIPATION IN A VOLUNTARY TELECOMMUTING
            PROGRAM MAY BE SUBJECT TO ADMINISTRATIVE
            FINES, PENALTIES, OR OTHER SANCTIONS AS MAY BE
            DETERMINED BY THE DEPARTMENT OF LABOR AND
            EMPLOYMENT (“DOLE”) OR THE CIVIL SERVICE
            COMMISSION (“CSC”), AS APPLICABLE.”
​
