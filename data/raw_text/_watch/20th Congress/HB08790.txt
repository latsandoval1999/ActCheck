                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Party-List System was enshrined in the 1987 Constitution as a mechanism to give
meaningful representation to marginalized and underrepresented sectors of society. Republic Act No.
7941 was enacted to operationalize this constitutional mandate and to ensure that sectors historically
excluded from political power would have a voice in the House of Representatives.

         However, more than two decades since its enactment, the party-list system has increasingly
been subjected to abuse. Groups and nominees that do not genuinely belong to, nor meaningfully
represent, marginalized and underrepresented sectors have been able to participate and secure seats in
Congress. The entry of political dynasties, vested economic interests, and nominees with conflicts of
interest has diluted the very purpose of the party-list system and eroded public trust in its integrity.

        This bill, to be known as the “Genuine Party-List Representation and Nomination Act,” seeks
to restore the original intent of the party-list system by strengthening the standards for registration,
nomination, and substitution of party-list groups and their nominees.

        First, the bill amends the registration process by requiring party-list groups to prove, in an
evidentiary public hearing conducted by the Commission on Elections (COMELEC), that they
genuinely represent marginalized and underrepresented sectors, and that their nominees actually belong
to the sectors they seek to represent. This reform introduces transparency, due process, and public
scrutiny into party-list accreditation.

        Second, the bill tightens the rules on nomination by extending filing periods, requiring approval
of nominees by the highest decision-making body of the party-list organization, and increasing the
minimum number of nominees. It also limits substitutions strictly to cases of death or incapacity,
thereby curbing the practice of strategic or opportunistic nominee replacement.

         Third, the bill strengthens the qualifications of party-list nominees by prohibiting individuals
who are closely related to incumbent elected officials or who have interests as government contractors
from becoming nominees. These safeguards are intended to prevent political dynasties and conflicts of
interest from undermining sectoral representation.

      Finally, the bill provides a clear mechanism within the House of Representatives for party-list
nominees who wish to contest or dispute their removal or substitution, ensuring respect for due process
and for the institution, while preserving the right of party-list organizations to discipline or remove their
members on lawful and reasonable grounds.

        In sum, this measure reaffirms that the party-list system is not a mere alternative electoral route
for elite and traditional politics, but a constitutional instrument for social justice and inclusive
governance. The immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
