EXPLANATORY NOTE

One in every five children, or roughly fifteen to twenty percent of the
world’s population is neurodivergent. This encompasses conditions like Autism
Spectrum Disorder (ASD), ADHD, dyslexia, and dyspraxia, among others.

In the Philippines, the population of neurodivergent is_ still
undetermined due to underreporting, misdiagnosis, and limited screening.
Nevertheless, albeit fragmented, available local health data and advocacy
suggest that ASD affects 1 in every 100, which translates to 1.2 million
Filipinos; ADHD affects up to 80% of adolescents; and specific learning
disabilities such as dyslexia affect about 10% of the population.

It remains a challenge for children and families to seek proper
assessment and care for neurodivergent individuals due to the limited pool of
developmental pediatricians in the country. Nationwide, there are only about
80-100 actively practicing developmental and behavioral pediatricians, fifty
percent of whom are concentrated in Metro Manila, while others are spread
mostly across major cities like Cebu and Davao!. Aside from the high cost of
professional fees, parents find it hard to secure consultation schedules from
developmental and behavioral pediatricians - that is, if they find one nearby.

To bridge this gap and encourage more Filipino pediatricians to take
the path of becoming a full-fledged developmental and_ behavioral
pediatrician, this bill establishes the Developmental-Behavioral Pediatrics

SECTION 1. Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Developmental
Pediatrics Fellowship Sponsorship Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right of health of the people through the
formulation and implementation of policies and strategies for the appropriate
generation, recruitment, retraining, regulation, retention and reassessment of the
health workforce making it more attuned to the needs of the population. It is also
the declared policy of the State to promote social justice by expanding access to
quality education and creating opportunities for underprivileged individuals.

Towards this end, the State shall establish a fellowship sponsorship
program and return service that will help deserving medical students and

physicians pursue medical education and training in the field of developmental and

recognized as a specialist in Pediatrics by the appropriate regulatory and
professional authorities; and

d. Return Service refers to the mandatory professional service rendered by
a grantee pursuant to the terms and conditions of this Act.

SEC. 7. Conditions for the Grant of Fellowship Sponsorship. — Those
who are accepted to the DPFS Program shall be subject to the following
conditionalities:

a. Must sign an agreement stating the terms and conditions of the
Fellowship Sponsorship Program on a form prescribed by the DOH
pursuant to the provisions of this Act:

b. Must be a full-time Developmental-Behavioral Pediatrics Fellow within
the prescribed timeframe of the DOH where the fellow is enrolled:
Provided, That he/she is accepted to the DPFS program, before the
commencement of the subspecialty training: Provided, further, That
he/she may be allowed for valid and justifiable reasons, to defer
subspecialty training or file a leave of absence;

c. Must render return of service as provided under Section 9 of this Act.

SEC. 8. Disqualifications. ~ The Fellowship Sponsorship shall terminate,
and the grantee shall repay the full cost of the sponsorship and related benefits
received, including all expenses incurred during participation in the sponsorship
program, in case of the following circumstances:

a. If the grantee accepts another scholarship and/or sponsorship from other
government or private agency or entity while enjoying the benefits
under this Act;

b. If the grantee fails to complete the subspecialty training within the
prescribed period without valid cause as may be determined by the
DOH, through appropriate regulations, such as, but not limited to,
absence without notice, willful neglect, or other causes within the
control of the grantee; and

c. While being a grantee, he/she commits gross misconduct in a manner
that would bring significant damage to DOH or to the community as a

whole.

SEC. 9. Mandatory Return Service and Integration into the Public Health
and Medical Service System. — Upon completion of Developmental-Behavioral
Pediatrician Training, the grantee shall be integrated into the public health and
medical service system through the DOH, and shall receive the appropriate civil
service rank, salary, and related benefits. The grantee shall serve in a government
public health office, government hospital, or any accredited government health
facility, for at least one (1) year for every fellowship sponsorship year availed
thereof.

The grantee shall render the mandatory return service within six (6) years
after the completion of Developmental-Behavioral Pediatrician Training:
Provided, however, that in times of pandemic or a public health emergency, the
DOH may require the scholars to serve in any public health office or government
hospital where their services may be needed: Provided, further, That the
Developmental-Behavioral Pediatrician shall receive appropriate salaries and other
benefits for service rendered under the mandatory return service.

SEC. 10. Sanctions. ~ A Developmental-Behavioral Pediatrician who has
availed of the DPFS Program but fails or refuses to comply with the mandatory
return service and integration provided under this Act shal] be required to pay two
(2) times the full cost of fellowship sponsorship, including other benefits and
expenses incurred by reason of participation in the DPFS Program.

In case of nonpayment, as provided in the preceding paragraph, the
Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) shall deny the renewal of the
physician’s license: Provided, That the abovementioned penalties shall not apply
to physicians who fail to comply with the required return service on account, or by
reason of, severe or serious illness.

SEC. 11. Training While Serving the Mandatory Return Service. — The
DOH shall develop programs for the continuous training of grantees while serving

the mandatory return service under this Act.

SEC. 12. Role of the DOH. — The DOH shall perform the following

functions in the implementation of the DPFS Program:

a.

Determine the number and distribution of slots for the DPFS Program,
which detemination shall be made every three (3) years from the
effectivity of this Act;

Ensure the integration of grantees into the public health and medical
service system, and monitor their performance during the mandatory
return service period;

Conduct the regular information dissemination on the DPFS Program
and in the recruitment of applicants;

Craft a career pathway for Developmental-Behavioral Pediatricians who
are beneficiaries of the DPFS Program in the public health and medical
service system to incentivize scholar-pediatricians to continue serving
therein;

Coordinate with the Local Government Units for the mandatory
integration of scholar-pediatricians into the public health and medical
service system; and

Recommend to the Department of Budget and Management the creation
of Plantilla positions for scholar-pediatricians with salary grades
commensurate to their educational achievement, training, and other

qualifications.

SEC. 13. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the implementation

of this Act shall be included in the General Appropriations Act of the year

following its enactment into law and thereafter.

SEC. 14. Joint Congressional Oversight Committee on DPFS Program. —

There is hereby created a Joint Congressional Oversight Committee to oversee,

monitor, and evaluate the implementation of this Act.
