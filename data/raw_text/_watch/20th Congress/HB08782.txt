                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Political parties are indispensable to a healthy democratic system. They aggregate public
interests, develop policy platforms, recruit and train leaders, and provide voters with meaningful
choices. However, legislative research and policy studies submitted in support of this measure
consistently show that the Philippine political party system remains weakly institutionalized,
personality-driven, and heavily dependent on private financing.

        Research indicates that a substantial majority of political parties in the Philippines rely
almost entirely on private contributions for both campaign and non-campaign activities. Public or
membership-based funding accounts for only a minimal share of party resources. Campaign
expenditures are highly concentrated, with a small number of wealthy donors and political
financiers providing a disproportionate share of reported political funding. In recent electoral
cycles, research shows that a limited group of top donors accounts for more than half of total
declared political contributions, resulting in unequal competition and reinforcing elite dominance.

        The same research highlights that institutional party development is chronically
underfunded. Political parties devote most resources to short-term electoral activities, while
spending on policy research, political education, grassroots organization, internal capacity-building
and merit-based endorsement processes remains marginal. This structural weakness contributes to
unstable party identities, frequent party switching, limited policy coherence, unprofessionalized
candidate selection reliant on personal loyalties rather than expertise, and declining public trust in
political institutions.

        Comparative regional analysis included in the research further underscores the urgency of
reform. Several Southeast Asian democracies and emerging democratic systems in the Asia-Pacific
region have adopted structured and conditional public funding mechanisms to strengthen political
parties as institutions. These systems generally provide limited public support tied to party
development, policy formation, and political education, and professionalized endorsement
mechanisms such as merit-based candidate selection criteria emphasizing qualifications, track
records, and alignment with party platforms, while imposing strict transparency, reporting, and
audit requirements.

        The research shows that in jurisdictions where public party funding is institutional rather
than candidate-centered, political parties demonstrate stronger organizational stability, greater
programmatic coherence, merit-driven leadership recruitment, and reduced dependence on private
money. Importantly, these systems do not eliminate private donations but rebalance political
financing by ensuring that parties are not wholly dependent on private interests for their survival

        In contrast, the Philippines remains one of the few democratic systems in the region where
no comprehensive framework exists for institutional party funding, resulting in continued reliance
on private financing and ad hoc alliances, and personality-based endorsements. This gap places
Philippine political parties at a comparative disadvantage in terms of long-term development,
internal discipline, and accountability.

        This bill seeks to address these findings by establishing a framework for Critical Party
Funding, which is a system of measured, conditional, and transparent public support designed to
strengthen political parties as democratic institutions. Rather than financing individual candidates
or electoral campaigns, the proposed framework prioritizes support for core party functions such as
policy development, political education, organizational strengthening, merit-based and
professionalized endorsement processes, and compliance with transparency standards.

         Consistent with both domestic research and regional experience, the bill incorporates strict
safeguards. These include eligibility requirements, clearly defined allowable uses of funds,
mandatory disclosure and auditing mechanisms, and graduated penalties for misuse. These
safeguards ensure that public resources are used responsibly and exclusively for legitimate party
development purposes, including the establishment of objective criteria for party endorsements that
prioritize competence over patronage.

        This measure does not seek to comprehensively reform campaign finance laws, regulate
party-list representation, or impose general prohibitions on political party switching. Any references
to campaign finance rules or party loyalty are included solely as conditions for eligibility and
accountability in the use of public funds, and do not amend or supplant existing laws governing
elections and political participation.

         By addressing the documented over-reliance on private financing, the concentration of
political money, the chronic underinvestment in party institutions, and the lack of merit-based,
professionalized endorsements, while drawing from comparative regional experience. Thus, this
bill aims to promote fairer political competition, strengthen democratic accountability, and
encourage the development of stable, program-based political parties. In doing so, it advances
democratic governance while remaining consistent with constitutional principles and the existing
legal framework.

        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      AN ACT ESTABLISHING A FRAMEWORK FOR CRITICAL PARTY FUNDING,
        POLITICAL PARTY DEVELOPMENT, AND TRANSPARENCY IN PARTY
                   FINANCING, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

1           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Political Party Development
2    and Critical Funding Act.”

3            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to strengthen
4    democratic governance by promoting a stable, programmatic, and accountable political party
5    system.

6            The State recognizes that political parties are essential institutions of representative
7    democracy and that political parties are essential institutions of representative democracy and that
8    their institutional weakness, excessive reliance on private money, and lack of transparency
9    undermine electoral integrity, public trust, and equal access to political participation.

10            Accordingly, the State shall provide critical and regulated public support to political parties
11   as democratic institutions. As well as reduce undue influence of money in politics. It also promotes
12   transparency and accountability in party financing. Moreover, it promotes anti-turncoatism. And
13   finally, encourage internal party democracy, inclusiveness and long-term party development.

14           SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

15            a)      Anti‑Turncoatism – refers to the principle and policy of promoting political party
16   institutionalization, organizational discipline, and programmatic consistency by discouraging the
17   systematic or opportunistic switching of political party affiliation by candidates or elected officials,
18   solely for purposes of determining eligibility for or accountability in the use of public party funding,
19   and without establishing a general prohibition against party switching.
20            a)      Critical Party Funding – refers to limited, conditional, and regulated public
21   financial support provided by the State to qualified political parties to support party development
22   and institutional strengthening.
23            b)      Election Year and Non-Election Year – shall have the meanings defined by
24   COMELEC.

11            SEC. 4. Nature and Scope of Critical Party Funding. - Critical party funding under this
12   Act shall be understood as supplemental public support provided in recognition of the democratic
13   function of political parties. Such funding shall not constitute an entitlement or permanent source
14   of income; it shall be subject to strict eligibility, transparency, and audit requirements; it shall be
15   provided solely for purposes consistent with political party development and lawful political
16   activities; and it shall supplement, and not replace, lawful private funding.

17           SEC. 5. Establishment of the Political Party Development and Democracy Fund. – There
18   is hereby established a Political Party Development and Democracy Fund, to be administered by
19   the Commission on Elections.

20            The Fund shall be used exclusively to provide critical party funding to qualified political
21   parties in accordance with this Act and its implementing rules and regulations.

22            SEC. 6. Objectives of the Fund. – The Political Party Development and Democracy Fund
23   shall be utilized to strengthen political parties as stable, programmatic, and accountable democratic
24   institutions rather than temporary electoral vehicles. It shall reduce excessive dependence on
25   private money and mitigate the undue influence of wealth in politics by providing regulated public
26   support tied to transparency and accountability.

27           The Fund shall promote fair and competitive political participation, support long‑term
28   political party development beyond election cycles, encourage internal party democracy,
29   inclusiveness, and organizational discipline, and enable smaller and program‑based political parties
30   to build sustainable institutional capacity consistent with democratic principles.

32           SEC. 7. Eligibility for Access to the Fund. – To qualify for access to the Fund, a political
33   party must be duly registered and accredited by COMELEC; maintain a national organizational
34   structure and internal governing rules; comply with all reporting, disclosure, and audit requirements
35   under this Act; and meet minimum organizational or electoral performance standards as may be
36   determined by COMELEC solely for purposes of funding eligibility. Eligibility requirements under
37   this Act shall apply only to access to public funding and shall not be construed as regulating
38   political party existence, accreditation, or participation beyond the scope of this Act.

1            SEC. 8. Allocation of the Fund. – The Fund shall be allocated using a hybrid allocation
2    formula, which may include a base amount equally distributed among qualified political parties; a
3    performance-based component proportional to votes obtained or seats won in the most recent
4    election; and a matching component that encourages small donor contributions. COMELEC shall
5    ensure that allocation mechanisms promote fairness, inclusivity, and party institutionalization
6    while avoiding excessive concentration of public funds.

 7           SEC. 9. Allowable Uses of the Fund. - Public funds provided under this Act shall be used
 8   exclusively to advance the objectives of political party institutionalization, organizational
 9   strengthening, transparency, and accountability as set forth in Section 6 of this Act, subject to the
10   limitations and safeguards herein provided, as follows:

11           (a) Party Development Activities. – During non‑election years, the Fund may be used solely
12   for Party Development Activities, including but not limited to party administration and
13   organizational strengthening; membership recruitment, training, and political education; policy
14   research and platform development; civic education and voter engagement; internal democratic
15   processes; leadership development; and communication and constituency outreach consistent with
16   the party’s declared principles and programs.

17            (b) Election‑Related Activities. – During election years, a limited portion of the Fund may
18   be used for lawful election‑related activities of the political party, subject to existing election laws,
19   expenditure limits, reporting requirements, and such additional safeguards as may be prescribed
20   under this Act and its implementing rules. In all cases, the use of public funds shall prioritize
21   institutional party development and shall not be used primarily for individual candidate promotion.

22           (c) Prohibited Uses. – The Fund shall not be used for the personal enrichment of party
23   officers, members, or candidates; for activities unrelated to legitimate political party functions; for
24   the support of candidates not officially nominated or endorsed by the political party; for
25   vote‑buying or any unlawful electoral activity; or for any expenditure not properly reported or
26   accounted for in accordance with this Act.

27           (d) Administrative Consequences and Relation to Other Laws. – Any use of public funds
28   inconsistent with this Section shall constitute grounds for administrative action under this Act,
29   including the reduction, suspension, or withdrawal of access to public funding.

30           Nothing in this Act shall be construed as creating new criminal offenses or penalties. Any
31   act involving the misuse, diversion, or unlawful disbursement of public funds, or any violation of
32   election, audit, or anti‑corruption laws, shall be governed by and subject to the applicable
33   provisions of existing laws, including election laws, auditing rules, and statutes on public
34   accountability, without prejudice to administrative, civil, or criminal liability under such laws.

35            SEC. 10. Transparency and Accountability in the Use of Public Funds. – Political parties
36   receiving public funds under this Act shall maintain separate accounting for public funds and
37   private contributions; submit itemized reports on the receipt and use of public funds; comply with
38   periodic reporting requirements prescribed by COMELEC; and ensure public disclosure of fund
39   utilization through COMELEC-maintained platforms. These requirements shall apply only to
40   political parties receiving public funding under this Act.

1           SEC. 11. Relation to Existing Campaign Finance Laws. – Nothing in this Act shall be
2    construed as establishing a general campaign finance regime or as amending contribution limits,
3    expenditure ceilings, or disclosure requirements under existing laws.

4            Financial reporting requirements under this Act apply exclusively to public funds provided
5    herein and to private contributions insofar as they affect eligibility for or accountability in the use

 7            SEC. 12. Funding Eligibility in Relation to Party Loyalty. - For purposes of determining
 8   eligibility for access to the Fund, and in furtherance of the objectives of party institutionalization,
 9   organizational discipline, and institutional integrity set forth in Section 6 of this Act, political
10   parties shall demonstrate consistency in party affiliation and adherence to their internal
11   organizational rules.

12           A political party that, as a matter of policy or repeated practice, systematically
13   accommodates candidates who have recently switched party affiliation in a manner that undermines
14   party institutionalization and organizational discipline may be subject to the reduction, suspension,
15   or withdrawal of access to public funding under this Act.

16          This provision shall apply solely as a condition for access to public funding and shall not
17   be construed as establishing a general prohibition against political party switching, impairing the
18   freedom of association, or imposing penalties beyond the scope of this Act.

19           SEC. 13. Non-Interference with the Party-List System. – Nothing in this Act shall be
20   construed as amending, repealing, or modifying Republic Act No. 7941 or any law governing the
21   party-list system. Political parties participating in the party-list system shall be subject to the Act
22   only to the extent that they qualify for and receive public funding under this framework.

23           SEC. 14. Oversight and Audit. – The Commission on Elections shall administer and
24   monitor compliance with this Act. The Commission on Audit shall conduct regular audits of the
25   use of public funds provided under this Act. The Office of the Ombudsman shall have jurisdiction
26   over acts involving fraud, misuse of public funds, or corruption.

27           SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
28   effectivity of this Act, the Commission on Elections, in consultation with the Commission on Audit
29   and relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
30   implementation.

31           SEC. 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
32   invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

33           SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and regulations
34   inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

35           SEC. 18. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
36   the Official Gazette or on a newspaper of general circulation.
