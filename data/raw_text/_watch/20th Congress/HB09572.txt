                               AN ACT
   INSTITUTIONALIZING AND MAINSTREAMING SCIENCE DIPLOMACY IN
     PHILIPPINE GOVERNANCE AND FOREIGN POLICY, CREATING THE
   PHILIPPINE SCIENCE DIPLOMACY OFFICE UNDER THE OFFICE OF THE
                 PRESIDENT, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The Constitution commands the State to give priority to science and technology and
to recognize them as essential for national development and progress (Article II, Section 17;
Article XIV, Section 10). In an interdependent world, the great challenges confronting the
Philippines — climate change, disasters, pandemics, food and water security, energy
transition, and the governance of emerging technologies — are transboundary in character
and cannot be addressed by any nation acting alone. They demand that scientific evidence
and international scientific cooperation be placed at the center of how the country conducts
its foreign relations and plans its development.

        The discipline now widely known as “science diplomacy” was articulated in its
modern form by the American Association for the Advancement of Science and the Royal
Society in their 2010 report, New Frontiers in Science Diplomacy, which set out three
mutually reinforcing dimensions: science in diplomacy, diplomacy for science, and science
for diplomacy. Since then, numerous States have adopted national strategies that harness
science as an instrument of cooperation and soft power. The Philippines has engaged in this
practice through the Department of Science and Technology, the Department of Foreign
Affairs, the academic community, and its active participation in ASEAN scientific
cooperation. Filipino scientists and practitioners have helped advance the field in the regional
context; among them, Science Diplomat Glenn Banaguas, through Climate Smart Philippines
and his work with the ASEAN science diplomacy community, has contributed to
popularizing the practice and to building a network of science diplomats across Southeast

                                                       1
Asia. These efforts, however, remain fragmented and have not been organized under a single,
coherent legal framework.

        This bill addresses that gap. It institutionalizes science diplomacy as a
whole-of-government policy and mainstreams it across foreign policy, development planning,
disaster risk reduction, public health, education, and environmental governance. It creates the
Philippine Science Diplomacy Office under the Office of the President to coordinate,
mainstream, and monitor the implementation of this policy — expressly in coordination with,
and without supplanting, the mandates of the Department of Foreign Affairs over the conduct
of foreign relations and of the Department of Science and Technology over science and
technology.

        By embedding scientific evidence into the conduct of its foreign affairs, the
Philippines will strengthen its capacity to respond to shared regional and global challenges,
deepen its cooperation within ASEAN, and assert a constructive, evidence-based voice in
international affairs. The measure will help ensure that the country’s development is informed
by the best available knowledge and that its diplomacy advances the welfare and resilience of
the Filipino people.

       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

                               AN ACT
   INSTITUTIONALIZING AND MAINSTREAMING SCIENCE DIPLOMACY IN
     PHILIPPINE GOVERNANCE AND FOREIGN POLICY, CREATING THE

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Science
Diplomacy Mainstreaming Act.”

        SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to give priority to
science and technology and to harness scientific knowledge, innovation, and international
scientific cooperation as instruments of national resilience, sustainable development, and
peace. Toward this end, the State shall institutionalize science diplomacy as a coherent,
whole-of-government approach that integrates scientific evidence and collaboration into the
formulation of foreign policy, development planning, disaster risk reduction, public health,
education, and environmental governance — consistent with the Constitution, with the
conduct of foreign relations vested by law in the Department of Foreign Affairs, and with the
commitments of the Philippines under the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN)
and other international agreements.

        SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

        (a)    Science diplomacy refers to the use and exchange of scientific knowledge,
expertise, and collaboration to advance the foreign policy and development objectives of the


                                                       3
State, to foster international cooperation, and to inform evidence-based decision-making. It
encompasses three mutually reinforcing dimensions:
         (1)​   Science in diplomacy — the integration of scientific advice and evidence into
the formulation and conduct of foreign policy;
         (2)​   Diplomacy for science — the use of diplomatic relations and instruments to
facilitate international scientific cooperation, including research agreements, exchange
programs, and shared facilities; and
         (3)​   Science for diplomacy — the use of scientific cooperation to build and
strengthen relations between and among States;

       (b)​      Office refers to the Philippine Science Diplomacy Office created under this
Act;
       (c)​     DFA refers to the Department of Foreign Affairs;
       (d)​     DOST refers to the Department of Science and Technology; and
       (e)​     Thematic areas refers to the priority fields of cooperation enumerated in
Section 5 of this Act.

        SECTION 4. Mainstreaming of Science Diplomacy. — All departments, bureaus,
offices, agencies, and instrumentalities of the national government shall, within their
respective mandates and existing appropriations, integrate science diplomacy into their plans,
programs, and international engagements. The DFA, the DOST, and NEDA shall ensure that
science diplomacy objectives are reflected, as appropriate, in the country’s foreign policy, in
the Philippine Development Plan, and in the national science and technology agenda.

       SECTION 5. Priority Thematic Areas. — In mainstreaming science diplomacy, the
Office and the concerned agencies shall give priority to, among others, the following
thematic areas:

              (a)​ Human Security, Gender Equality, Peace, and Diplomacy — the
                   application of scientific evidence to human security, gender equality, conflict
                   prevention, peacebuilding, and the protection of vulnerable populations;

              (b)​Water Governance and Resource Diplomacy — cooperation on
                  transboundary water systems and shared natural resources, including their
                  equitable allocation and sustainable management;

              (c)​ Food Systems and Nutritional Security Diplomacy — agricultural
                   innovation, food security, and nutritional equity through scientific research
                   and cooperation;

              (d)​Health Security and Pandemic Preparedness Diplomacy — pandemic
                  preparedness, disease surveillance, and equitable access to medicines and
                  vaccines;

              (e)​ Infrastructure and Manufacturing Diplomacy — sustainable infrastructure,
                   industrial innovation, technology transfer, and the harmonization of standards;

              (f)​ Energy Transition and Sustainable Systems Diplomacy — clean energy
                   technologies, climate mitigation, and equitable access to energy;’


                                                 4
           (g)​Climate Resilience and Disaster Diplomacy — disaster risk reduction,
               climate resilience, and adaptive governance;

           (h)​Environmental Stewardship and Biodiversity Diplomacy — conservation,
               the sustainable use of natural resources, and the cross-border protection of
               biodiversity;

           (i)​ Digital Transformation and Artificial Intelligence Diplomacy — the setting
                of ethical standards, the fostering of innovation, and inclusive digital
                transformation;

           (j)​ Cybersecurity and Digital Trust Diplomacy — the building of digital trust,
                the prevention of cybercrime, and cooperation on cybersecurity;

           (k)​Sustainable Finance Diplomacy — sustainable investment, green finance,
               and economic resilience through evidence-based cooperation;

           (l)​ Space, Satellite, and Frontier Science Diplomacy — peaceful cooperation in
                outer space, satellite technology, and the frontier sciences;

           (m)​Education and Knowledge Diplomacy — academic mobility, knowledge
               exchange, and collaborative research;

           (n)​Transport Connectivity Diplomacy — sustainable transport, logistics, and
               mobility systems; and

           (o)​Marine and Ocean Science Diplomacy — sustainable ocean governance,
               marine biodiversity, oceanographic research, and maritime cooperation,
               consistent with international law and with the rights and interests of the
               Philippines as an archipelagic and maritime State.

       SECTION 6. Creation of the Philippine Science Diplomacy Office. — There is
hereby created the Philippine Science Diplomacy Office, hereinafter referred to as the Office,
under the Office of the President, which shall serve as the primary body responsible for
coordinating, mainstreaming, and monitoring the implementation of this Act. The Office shall
coordinate with, and shall not supplant or diminish, the powers and functions of the DFA, the
DOST, and other concerned agencies.

        SECTION 7. Composition and Term. — The Office shall be headed by a
Director-General, who shall have the rank, salary, and privileges of a Secretary, and who shall
be assisted by two (2) Deputy Directors-General, namely: a Deputy Director-General for
Scientific and Technical Affairs, who shall coordinate with the DOST, CHED, the
Department of Education, and academic and research institutions; and a Deputy
Director-General for Diplomatic and International Affairs, who shall coordinate with the
DFA, ASEAN bodies, and international partners. The Director-General and the Deputy
Directors-General shall be appointed by the President. The Director-General shall serve for a
term of five (5) years: Provided, That nothing in this Act shall be construed to limit the power
of control and the power of removal of the President over the Office and its officials as
executive officers under the Constitution.


                                               5
        SECTION 8. Qualifications. — The Director-General and the Deputy
Directors-General shall be natural-born citizens of the Philippines, at least thirty-five (35)
years of age, of recognized integrity, competence, and probity, holders of at least a master’s
degree in science, technology, engineering, public policy, law, international relations, or a
related field, and shall possess at least ten (10) years of aggregate experience in science,
technology, research, foreign affairs, public policy, or international cooperation..

       SECTION 9. Powers and Functions of the Office. — The Office shall have the
following powers and functions:

           (a)​ Formulate, and periodically update, a Philippine Science Diplomacy Agenda,
                and integrate its objectives into the country’s foreign policy, the Philippine
                Development Plan, and the national science and technology agenda, in
                coordination with the DFA, the DOST, and the Department of Economy,
                Planning, and Development (DEPDev);

           (b)​Serve as the principal body for coordinating international scientific
               cooperation, agreements, and partnerships, without prejudice to the
               treaty-making and foreign-relations functions vested by law in the DFA;

           (c)​ Provide scientific and technical advice to the President, the Cabinet, and the
                Congress on matters of foreign policy and international cooperation;

           (d)​In coordination with the DFA, support the representation of the Philippines in
               ASEAN and in other international fora on science diplomacy;

           (e)​ Develop and implement capacity-building programs to train science
                diplomats, scholars, and practitioners, in coordination with the Commission on
                Higher Education (CHED), the DOST, and the Foreign Service Institute;

           (f)​ Establish monitoring and evaluation mechanisms to assess the impact of
                science diplomacy initiatives;

           (g)​Convene and chair the inter-agency coordination mechanism under Section 10
               of this Act; and

           (h)​Submit an annual report to the President and to the Congress on the
               implementation of this Act.

       SECTION 10. Inter-Agency Coordination Mechanism. — The Office shall
convene and chair an inter-agency coordination mechanism composed of representatives, at
the rank of at least Undersecretary, of the DFA, the DOST, DEPDEV, CHED, the Department
of Education, the Department of Environment and Natural Resources, the Department of
Health, the Department of Agriculture, the Department of Information and Communications
Technology, the Department of Energy, the Department of Trade and Industry, and such other
agencies as may be necessary, in order to harmonize science diplomacy initiatives across the
government and to avoid duplication of functions

      SECTION 11. Non-Derogation Clause. — Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
amend, repeal, or diminish the powers and functions vested by law in the DFA over the

                                              6
conduct of the foreign relations of the Philippines, in the DOST over science and technology,
or in the President as the chief architect of the foreign policy of the State. In case of conflict
between this Act and the laws governing the conduct of foreign relations, the latter shall
prevail insofar as the conduct of foreign relations is concerned.

       SECTION 12. Annual Report and Congressional Oversight. — The Office shall
submit to the President and to the Congress, within the first quarter of each year, a report on
the implementation of this Act, including the status of its programs, the funds utilized, and its
recommendations. The appropriate committees of the Senate and the House of
Representatives shall conduct a regular review of the implementation of this Act.

        SECTION 13. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Office of
the President. Thereafter, the amount necessary for the continued implementation of this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Office, in consultation with the DFA, the DOST,
DEPDEV, and other concerned agencies, and after public consultation, shall promulgate the
rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

        SECTION 15. Transitory Provision. — The Office shall be organized and made
operational within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act. Pending its
full organization, the Office of the President shall designate an office or official to perform
the functions of the Office on an interim basis.

        SECTION 16. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

        SECTION 17. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules, regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof that are inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 18. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
