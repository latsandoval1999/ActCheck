                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Rights of Nature Act of 2026 seeks to strengthen environmental protection
by recognizing natural ecosystems as holders of legally enforceable rights to exist,
regenerate, and maintain their ecological integrity. It establishes a unified framework
that complements existing environmental laws by integrating ecosystem integrity
standards into environmental permitting, land-use planning, and development
decision-making.

      The measure requires government agencies and covered industries to adopt
science-based ecological safeguards, promotes accountability for environmental
harm, and provides legal mechanisms for the protection and restoration of
ecosystems. It likewise ensures that these rights complement existing human rights,
indigenous peoples' rights, property rights, and sustainable livelihoods.

      Ultimately, the bill aims to promote sustainable development by balancing
economic growth with the long-term protection and resilience of the country's natural
ecosystems for present and future generations.


      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     AN ACT TO RECOGNIZE AND PROTECT THE RIGHTS OF NATURE
      IN THE PHILIPPINES, INTEGRATING ECOSYSTEM INTEGRITY
          STANDARDS INTO THE NATIONAL ENVIRONMENTAL
     MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORK, AND PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR
                       VIOLATIONS THEREOF

     Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
     in Congress assembled:


1    SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Rights of Nature
2    Act of 2026."

 3   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy — Article II, Section 16 of the 1987
 4   Philippine Constitution mandates the State to protect and advance the right of the
 5   people to a balanced and healthful ecology in accord with the rhythm and
 6   harmony of nature. While the State has enacted comprehensive, sector-specific
 7   environmental laws to regulate human activities, existing legal statutory
 8   frameworks focus primarily on resource management, conservation thresholds,
 9   and the mitigation of anthropogenic utility. Consequently, distinct and
10   ecologically significant natural ecosystems are not recognized as entities
11   possessing legally enforceable rights, and there is an absence of a unified
12   statutory mechanism requiring public and private actions to explicitly safeguard
13   basic ecosystem integrity.
14
15   To address this legislative gap, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to
16   adopt a holistic approach to environmental governance by recognizing and
17   protecting natural ecosystems as legal entities possessing fundamental rights to
18   exist, persist, and regenerate their vital cycles. This Act shall supplement,
19   strengthen, and guide the interpretation and implementation of all existing
20   environmental statutes. The enforcement of the rights of nature shall be guided
21   by the following principles:
22
23     (a) Ecosystem Integrity — The interconnectedness of all biotic and abiotic
24         components within an ecosystem must be respected, ensuring that human
25         activities do not disrupt the functional capacity of land, air, water, and
26         biological diversity to sustain life.

27     (b) Dynamic Balance and Harmony — Human activities and developmental
28         projects must achieve a sustainable balance with the inherent natural cycles
29         and regenerative capacities of ecosystems.

30     (c) Intergenerational and Eco-centric Equity — The utilization of natural
31         resources must ensure both the fulfillment of human society's collective
32         good and the long-term preservation of ecological systems, safeguarding
33         the rights of future generations and the environment itself.

34     (d) Complementarity   — The recognition of the rights of nature shall
35         complement, and not diminish, human rights, indigenous peoples' rights,
36         and the socio-economic mandates guaranteed under the Constitution.

37     (e) Institutional Stewardship — The State, through its instrumentalities, shall
38         serve as the primary guardian of these rights, in active collaboration with
39         indigenous cultural communities, local government units, and civil society.

40    SECTION 3. Definition of Terms — As used in this Act, the following terms
41   shall be defined as follows:
42
43     (a) Nature refers to the collective physical and biological systems of the earth,
44         encompassing all living organisms, non-living elements, ecological
45         processes, and the interconnected networks they form.

46     (b) Natural Ecosystems refer to distinct, dynamic, and identifiable spatial units
47         composed of communities of plants, animals, microorganisms, and their
48         abiotic environment, interacting as a functional unit governed by climatic,
49         physiographic, and geological factors.

50     (c) Ecological Restoration refers to the intentional, scientifically grounded
51         process of assisting the recovery of an ecosystem that has been degraded,
52         damaged, or destroyed, with the objective of re-establishing its structural
53         integrity, species composition, and ecological functionality.
54      (d) Environmentally Critical Projects (ECPs) refer to projects or activities high
55            in magnitude or intensity that have a significant potential to cause adverse
56            environmental impacts, as defined under Presidential Decree No. 1586 and
57            its implementing rules and regulations.

58      (e) Environmentally Critical Areas (ECAs) refer to areas that are ecologically,
59            socially, or geologically sensitive, requiring special protection and
60            management as defined under Presidential Decree No. 1586 and its
61            implementing rules and regulations.

62      (f) Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) refer to
63            a group of people or homogenous societies identified by self-ascription and
64            ascription by others, who have continuously lived as an organized
65            community on communally bounded and defined territory, as defined
66            under Republic Act No. 8371, otherwise known as "The Indigenous
67            Peoples' Rights Act of 1997."

68   SECTION 4. The Fundamental Rights of Nature — Natural ecosystems, or
69   distinct and scientifically identifiable portions thereof, are hereby recognized as
70   holders of the following fundamental, measurable, and legally enforceable rights:
71
72      (a)   The right to exist and persist within their natural geographic boundaries;

73      (b)   The right to maintain and regenerate their vital cycles, structural functions,
74            evolutionary processes, and ecological balance;

75      (c)   The right to clean water, clean air, and the maintenance of critical
76            biogeochemical cycles necessary to sustain ecosystem health;

77      (d)   The right to comprehensive ecological restoration in the event of
78            degradation caused by anthropogenic activities or industrial accidents; and

79      (e)   The right to adequate and effective legal representation in all
80            administrative, quasi-judicial, and judicial proceedings affecting their
81            integrity.

82   These rights shall supplement, and shall not impair or limit, any other right, duty,
83   or remedy available under existing environmental laws, administrative
84   regulations, or Philippine jurisprudence.
85
86    A final administrative or judicial finding of a violation of any existing
87   environmental statute—including but not limited to Republic Act No. 8749
 88   (Clean Air Act), Republic Act No. 9275 (Clean Water Act), Republic Act No.
 89   9147 (Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act), Republic Act No.
 90   7586 as amended by Republic Act No. 11038 (Expanded National Integrated
 91   Protected Areas System Act of 2018), and Presidential Decree No. 1586
 92   (Environmental Impact Statement System) shall constitute a prima facie violation
 93   of the rights of nature recognized under this Act.
 94
 95    SECTION 5. Legal Personality of Natural Ecosystems — The legal personality
 96   of natural ecosystems, including distinct and identifiable river basins, marine
 97   protected areas, critical habitats, old-growth forests, and similar ecological units,
 98   is hereby recognized solely for the purpose of enforcing the rights, protections,
 99   and remedies provided under this Act. To qualify for protection and enforcement
100   under this Act, the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR),
101   in consultation with recognized scientific institutions, concerned Local
102   Government Units (LGUs), the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples
103   (NCIP) where applicable, and other relevant government agencies, shall establish
104   clear, objective, scientific, and ecological criteria for identifying and validating
105   eligible ecologically significant natural ecosystems.
106
107   In any administrative or judicial action filed for the protection, enforcement, or
108   restoration of the rights recognized herein, the natural ecosystem concerned shall
109   be designated as the real party-in-interest. The recognition of legal personality
110   under this Section shall not be interpreted to confer any liabilities, civil
111   obligations, duties, or financial responsibilities upon the natural ecosystem itself,
112   nor shall it affect the proprietary rights of the State or private landowners over
113   real property except as necessary to enforce the ecological protections mandated
114   herein.
115
116    SECTION 6. Obligations of the Government — The State, through its
117   departments, bureaus, offices, and local government units (LGUs), shall ensure
118   that the rights of nature are integrated into all aspects of public administration,
119   resource management, and development planning.
120
121      (a) The DENR shall, within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act,
122         develop and incorporate ecocentric evaluation criteria into the review
123         processes for resource extractions, land-use conversions, and
124         environmental permitting. The DENR shall implement early-warning and
125         monitoring systems to prevent irreversible ecological degradation.

126      (b) The  National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) shall
127         integrate ecosystem-integrity standards and the rights of nature into the
128         Philippine Development Plan (PDP) and national spatial frameworks.
129      (c) LGUs shall, in coordination with the DENR and the Department of the
130         Interior and Local Government (DILG), align their Comprehensive Land
131         Use Plans (CLUPs) and Local Development Plans with the preservation
132         requirements of ecosystems within their territorial jurisdictions. LGUs
133         shall submit annual compliance reports to the DILG regarding the
134         ecological status of critical ecosystems within their boundaries.
135
136      (d) The Department of Energy (DOE) shall prioritize long-term ecological
137         sustainability by accelerating the transition toward clean and renewable
138         alternative energy sources, implementing strict mitigation protocols for
139         energy projects situated within or near critical ecosystems.

140    SECTION 7. Obligations of Juridical Persons — The obligations under this
141   Section shall apply strictly to juridical persons engaged in, or applying to engage
142   in, Environmentally Critical Projects (ECPs) or projects located within
143   Environmentally Critical Areas (ECAs), as determined by the DENR pursuant to
144   Presidential Decree No. 1586.
145
146         (a)   Corporate Incorporation and Reorganization — Juridical persons
147         planning to operate ECPs or operate within ECAs shall include a specific
148         corporate policy committing to the respect and protection of the rights of
149         nature in their articles of incorporation or bylaws, upon submission to the
150         Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).

151         (b)    Ecosystem Integrity Assessment — Juridical persons operating
152         ECPs or operating within ECAs shall conduct a specialized Ecosystem
153         Integrity Assessment as an integrated component of the existing
154         Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) process under Presidential Decree
155         No. 1586, avoiding unnecessary duplication of environmental permitting
156         procedures. This assessment must scientifically demonstrate that the
157         proposed project or activity will not cause irreversible damage to the vital
158         cycles or regenerative capacity of the host ecosystem. The approval of this
159         assessment by the DENR Environmental Management Bureau (EMB) shall
160         be a mandatory prerequisite for the issuance of an Environmental
161         Compliance Certificate (ECC).

162         (c)   Annual Ecological Reporting. — Covered juridical persons shall
163         submit an annual Ecological Compliance and Impact Report to the DENR-
164         EMB, detailing the measurable impacts of their operations on the host
165         ecosystem and the specific mitigation and restoration measures undertaken.
166         Non-compliance shall be grounds for the suspension or revocation of their
167         ECC or environmental permits, after due notice and hearing.

168    SECTION 8. Representation and Standing — Pursuant to the constitutional
169   right to a balanced and healthful ecology, any Filipino citizen, acting as a steward
170   and public representative of a natural ecosystem, may file an administrative, civil,
171   or criminal action to enforce the rights or obligations recognized under this Act.
172   Legal representation and standing under this Section shall be harmonized and
173   exercised in full compliance with the Rules of Procedure for Environmental
174   Cases promulgated by the Supreme Court.
175
176   Upon the filing of a civil action under this Act, the court shall issue an order
177   containing a summary of the cause of action and the specific reliefs prayed for,
178   requiring the publication thereof in a newspaper of general circulation or through
179   official digital platforms. Any interested party, including local communities, non-
180   governmental organizations, or indigenous cultural communities situated within
181   the affected ecosystem, may manifest their intention to intervene within thirty
182   (30) days from such publication, subject to the Rules of Court and the Rules of
183   Procedure for Environmental Cases.
184
185    SECTION 9. Reliefs — If the court finds that a violation of the rights of
186   nature has occurred, it shall render a judgment in favor of the real party-in-
187   interest ecosystem. The judgment may include the following reliefs:
188
189         (a)  Permanent or Temporary Environmental Protection Orders
190         (TEPO/EPO) directing the immediate cessation of the damaging activity;

191         (b)   Mandatory injunctions requiring the implementation of a
192         comprehensive, scientifically validated Ecological Restoration Plan at the
193         expense of the defendant;

194         (c)   Monetary awards for environmental rehabilitation, representing the
195         actual and quantifiable cost necessary to fully restore the degraded
196         ecosystem to its pre-damage baseline condition; and

197         (d)    The payment of attorney's fees, litigation expenses, and costs of suit
198         to the citizen-representative or public interest group that prosecuted the
199         case.

200    SECTION 10. Trust Fund. — Any monetary awards or damages granted by an
201   administrative body or court for the restoration or rehabilitation of an ecosystem
202   shall not accrue to the personal benefit of the citizen-representative or the filing
203   organization. Such funds shall be deposited directly into a dedicated Ecosystem
204   Restoration Trust Fund established specifically for the affected ecosystem.
205
206   The Ecosystem Restoration Trust Fund shall be maintained in an authorized
207   government depository bank under the administrative accountability of the
208   Conservation Committee created under Section 11 of this Act. The fund shall be
209   utilized exclusively for financing the ecological restoration, monitoring, and
210   preservation measures ordered by the court or administrative body. Any
211   legitimate, verified out-of-pocket expenses and litigation costs incurred by the
212   citizen-representative shall constitute a first lien on the monetary award, subject
213   to judicial approval and accounting.
214
215   SECTION 11. Appointment of Conservation Committee — To administer the
216   Ecosystem Restoration Trust Fund and oversee the implementation of the court-
217   ordered Ecological Restoration Plan, the court shall appoint a specialized, ad-hoc
218   Conservation Committee for the specific natural ecosystem involved.
219
220   The Conservation Committee shall be composed of five (5) members, giving
221   preference to:
222
223         (a)   A representative from the host Indigenous Cultural Community or
224         Indigenous Political Structure (IPS), where applicable;

225         (b)   A representative from a local public interest non-governmental
226         organization or peoples' organization accredited by the LGU or DENR;

227         (c)   A qualified environmental scientist or ecologist nominated by an
228         accredited academic institution;

229         (d)    A representative from the DENR regional office; and

230         (e)    A representative from the host Local Government Unit.

231   Under no circumstances shall any person, officer, or entity responsible for, or
232   complicit in, the violation of the rights of nature be appointed to or participate in
233   the Conservation Committee. The Committee shall submit quarterly progress and
234   financial liquidation reports to the oversight court.
235
236    SECTION 12. Rulemaking and Courts. — Actions brought under this Act shall
237   be governed by the Rules of Procedure for Environmental Cases and shall be
238   heard and resolved by appropriate courts designated by the Supreme Court
239   pursuant to its constitutional authority over pleading, practice, procedure, and
240   judicial administration.
241
242    SECTION 13. Strategic Lawsuits against Public Participation (SLAPP). — A
243   Strategic Lawsuit against Public Participation (SLAPP) is any legal action—
244   whether civil, criminal, or administrative—filed against a person, group, or
245   juridical entity with the primary intent to harass, vex, exert undue pressure, or
246   stifle the enforcement of the rights of nature or existing environmental laws. The
247   defendant in an alleged SLAPP may raise this defense by filing a special motion
248   to dismiss at any stage of the proceedings. The court shall set the motion for
249   hearing and direct the plaintiff to show cause why the case should not be
250   dismissed as a SLAPP. The special motion to dismiss shall be resolved by the
251   court within a non-extendible period of thirty (30) days from the conclusion of
252   the hearing. If the court determines the action to be a SLAPP, it shall dismiss the
253   case with prejudice and award exemplary damages, attorney's fees, and full costs
254   of suit to the defendant.
255
256   SECTION 14. Territories of Life — The ancestral domains of Indigenous
257   Cultural Communities and Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs), formally recognized
258   under Republic Act No. 8371, may be acknowledged as Territories of Life
259   consistent with the provisions of the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997
260   (IPRA), customary indigenous governance, and the strict observance of the
261   principle of Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC). Ancestral domains so
262   acknowledged shall enjoy the protections and rights granted under Section 4 and
263   Section 5 of this Act, without overriding or diminishing indigenous self-
264   governance, customary laws, or traditional resource management systems.
265   Furthermore, all protected areas established pursuant to Republic Act No. 7586,
266   as amended by Republic Act No. 11038 (E-NIPAS Act), as well as locally
267   declared conservation areas established by LGUs through ordinances, are
268   designated as Territories of Life under this Act.
269
270   The Protected Area Management Boards (PAMBs) of E-NIPAS areas and the
271   local development councils of LGUs shall ensure that their respective Protected
272   Area Management Plans, Ancestral Domain Sustainable Development and
273   Protection Plans (ADSDPPs), and local zoning ordinances are fully aligned with
274   the ecocentric standards established by this Act.
275
276    SECTION 15. Role of the Indigenous Political Structure (IPS). — The
277   Indigenous Political Structures (IPS) of recognized ICCs/IPs, as mandated by
278   existing law, shall exercise the following duties and functions within their
279   ancestral domains to ensure the protection of the rights of nature:
280
281         (a)   Represent the ecological interests of the natural ecosystems situated
282         within their ancestral domains in local development councils and regional
283         planning bodies;
284         (b)    Ensure that the processes for Free, Prior and Informed Consent
285         (FPIC) strictly evaluate potential impacts on the vital cycles and structural
286         integrity of local ecosystems before any development project is authorized;
287         and

288         (c)   Collaborate with the Department of Education (DepEd) and the
289         National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) to integrate
290         indigenous ecological knowledge, sustainable resource management
291         systems, and conservation values into the localized educational curriculum.

292   SECTION 16. Allocation of Funds from Other Sources. — The DENR is hereby
293   authorized and mandated to allocate a specific portion of its retained collections
294   from existing statutory funds to support the implementation of this Act. This shall
295   include, but is not limited to, allocations from the Integrated Protected Area Fund
296   (IPAF) established under the E-NIPAS Act, and the environmental fees collected
297   under the Forestry Code and the Philippine Environmental Impact Statement
298   System, subject to standard accounting and auditing rules and regulations.
299
300   SECTION 17. Protection of Livelihoods Dependent on the Environment. —
301   Nothing in this Act shall be interpreted, construed, or applied to diminish, limit,
302   or impair the traditional, subsistence, and customary livelihood rights of artisanal
303   fisherfolk, small-scale farmers, and indigenous peoples, provided that their
304   activities are carried out using sustainable, non-destructive, and traditional
305   methods.
306
307   These sectors are recognized as primary stewards of nature within their ancestral
308   domains and traditional settlement areas. Any public or private development
309   project affecting their environment or subsistence areas must strictly comply with
310   the mandatory consultation and public hearing requirements set forth under
311   Sections 26 and 27 of Republic Act No. 7160 (Local Government Code of 1991).
312   The affected individuals and community representatives shall be deemed
313   indispensable parties in all consultations, environmental impact studies, and
314   permitting processes relating to the proposed projects.
315
316   SECTION 18. Review of the Environmental Impact Assessment System. —
317   Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DENR,
318   through the Environmental Management Bureau (EMB), shall conduct a
319   comprehensive review and revision of the Philippine Environmental Impact
320   Assessment System (PEIASS). This review shall modernize the system to
321   explicitly integrate ecosystem-integrity standards, cumulative impact
322   assessments, climate resilience profiles, and the rights of nature into the
323   evaluation guidelines for all project categories, ensuring that no project is granted
324   an ECC if it meets any of the following scientifically established thresholds:
325
326         (a)    Causes irreversible ecological degradation;

327         (b)    Significant impairment of fundamental ecosystem functions;

328         (c)    Substantial loss of critical biodiversity; or

329         (d)    Breaches scientifically established ecological carrying capacities.

330   SECTION 19. Education — The Department of Education (DepEd) and the
331   Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall, in coordination with the DENR,
332   integrate the concept of ecocentric environmental governance, sustainability
333   science, and the recognition of the rights of natural ecosystems into the core
334   curricula of all primary, secondary, and tertiary educational institutions within
335   one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act.
336
337    SECTION 20. Enforcement and Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The

352   SECTION 22. Prohibited Acts. — It shall be unlawful for any person, public
353   officer, or juridical entity to:
354
355         (a)    Knowingly undertake, authorize, or execute any activity or project
356         that directly causes irreversible ecological degradation, systemic collapse,
357         structural disruption, or permanent alteration of the vital cycles and
358         regenerative capacity of a natural ecosystem;
359         (b)   Execute, approve, or issue administrative permits, clearances, or
360         ECCs for projects in knowing violation of the ecosystem integrity
361         standards established under Section 7 and Section 18 of this Act;

362         (c)   Obstruct, prevent, or harass citizen-representatives, indigenous
363         communities, or public officials lawfully enforcing the rights of nature
364         under this Act;

365         (d)    Falsify, conceal, or misrepresent environmental data, assessments,
366         or information submitted under the regulatory procedures of this Act;

367         (e)   Knowingly violate specific terms, conditions, or mitigation rules
368         imposed under
369          Environmental Compliance Certificates (ECCs) issued pursuant to this
370         Act;
371         (f)    Fail or refuse to comply with lawful restoration, rehabilitation, or
372         cease-and-desist orders issued by competent administrative bodies or
373         courts; or

374         (g)    Retaliate against, threaten, or penalize citizen-representatives,
375         whistleblowers, indigenous communities, or environmental defenders
376         acting in good faith to uphold or implement the provisions of this Act.

377   SECTION 23. Penalties — The following penalties are hereby imposed for
378   violations of Section 22 of this Act, subject to due process and constitutional
379   protections:
380
381         (a)    Criminal Penalties — Any natural person who commits any of the
382         prohibited acts defined under Section 22 shall, upon conviction by final
383         judgment, be punished by imprisonment of not less than one (1) year nor
384         more than three (3) years, or a fine of not less than Five Million Pesos
385         (P5,000,000.00) nor more than Ten Million Pesos (P10,000,000.00), or
386         both, at the discretion of the court.

387         (b)   Liability of Juridical Persons and Corporate Officers. — If the
388         offender is a juridical person, it shall be sanctioned with an administrative
389         fine of not less than Twenty Million Pesos (P20,000,000.00) and the
390         immediate revocation of its business permits, licenses, and operational
391         clearances. The penalty of imprisonment prescribed in subsection (a) of
392         this Section shall be imposed directly upon the directors, officers,
393         managers, or responsible corporate personnel who authorized, tolerated, or
394         knowingly permitted the unlawful act or omission.

395         (c)     Liability of Public Officials. — If the offender is a public officer
396         who issues permits or approvals in knowing violation of this Act, or who
397         fails to execute mandatory enforcement duties, they shall, in addition to the
398         criminal penalties prescribed herein, suffer the penalty of perpetual
399         absolute disqualification from holding public office and forfeiture of all
400         administrative benefits.

401         (d)   Foreign Nationals. — If the offender is a foreign national, they shall
402         be immediately deported after serving the sentence of imprisonment and
403         paying the applicable fines, without need of further deportation
404         proceedings.
