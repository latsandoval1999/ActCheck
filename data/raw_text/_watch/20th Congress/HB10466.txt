                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines continues to rank among the most active internet users in
the world. As of 2025, there are approximately 87 to 89 million internet users in
the country, representing over 75% of the population, with Filipinos spending an
average of 8 to 9 hours online daily, among the highest globally. This reflects a
significant increase from earlier estimates of 76.7 million users and over 10
hours of daily usage.

      The rapid expansion of internet access has greatly improved connectivity,
enabling advancements in education, commerce, communication, and public
services. Increasing broadband penetration, mobile connectivity, and digital
infrastructure continue to drive the country’s digital transformation.

       However, the growing digital landscape has also intensified cybersecurity
risks. Recent data from the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT) and global cybersecurity firms indicate that the Philippines
experiences millions of cyber threat attempts annually, including phishing,
online scams, identity theft, and cyber-enabled exploitation. Notably, the country
remains among those with the highest reported cases of online sexual
exploitation of children (OSEC), underscoring the urgent need for stronger public
awareness and digital safety measures.
       In addition, reports from organizations such as We Are Social,
DataReportal, and Kaspersky highlight that Filipino users are among the most
vulnerable to social engineering attacks due to high social media usage and
digital engagement. These evolving threats affect not only individuals but also
institutions, making cybersecurity a growing national concern.

      Given these developments, there is a pressing need to strengthen public
awareness and promote responsible digital behavior. Educating citizens—
especially children and young people—on safe internet use is a critical first step
in reducing exposure to online risks.

        This bill seeks to declare the 10th of February of each year as National
Safer Internet Day in the Philippines. By institutionalizing this annual
observance, the State aims to promote awareness, encourage responsible online
behavior, and foster a culture of cybersecurity and digital responsibility among
all Filipinos.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Safer Internet Day
(SID) Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Principles. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to recognize the vital role of information and communications technology
in nation-building and to ensure a safe, secure, and responsible digital
environment for all Filipinos.

Toward this end, the State shall promote cybersecurity awareness, digital
responsibility, and the protection of internet users—especially children and
young people—against online threats, including cybercrime, exploitation, and
abuse.

SECTION 3. National Safer Internet Day. Every 10th day of February of each
year is hereby declared as National Safer Internet Day to be observed across the
country, especially in government offices, higher education institutions,
technical vocational institutions, high schools, and elementary schools.

SECTION 4. Implementing Agency. – The Department of Transportation and
Communications in close coordination with the Department of Social Welfare
and Development shall plan, design, and implement programs and activities in
celebration of the National Safer Internet Day every year. Other national
government agencies and local government units shall actively participate in the
activities of this celebration.

SECTION 5. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in two (2) newspapers of general circulation or the Official Gazette.
