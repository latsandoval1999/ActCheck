                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The right of the people to information on matters of public concern is
enshrined in Article III, Section 7 of the 1987 Constitution. This right is a
cornerstone of a functioning democracy, enabling citizens to meaningfully
participate in governance, hold public officials accountable, and make
informed decisions. However, despite this constitutional guarantee, access to
government information in the Philippines remains uneven, inconsistent, and
often dependent on internal policies rather than a uniform statutory
framework.


       At present, transparency mechanisms rely heavily on executive
issuances, particularly Executive Order No. 2 (2016), which applies only to the
Executive Branch. This fragmented approach leaves significant gaps in
coverage, especially in the Legislative and Judicial branches, as well as in local
government units and government-owned or controlled corporations. The
absence of a comprehensive Freedom of Information (FOI) law has resulted in
delays, denials, and procedural inconsistencies that undermine the public’s
right to know.


      This proposed Freedom of Information Act seeks to institutionalize and
standardize access to information across all branches and instrumentalities of



                                                                                 1
government. It establishes a clear legal framework governing the disclosure of
public records, the procedures for requesting information, and the
responsibilities of government agencies to proactively publish key data. By
mandating uniform timelines, clear grounds for denial, and accessible appeal
mechanisms, the measure ensures that the right to information is not merely
theoretical but fully enforceable.


      The bill likewise introduces a system of proactive disclosure, requiring
government agencies to regularly publish critical information such as budgets,
procurement activities, contracts, development plans, and performance
indicators. This reduces the need for formal requests and promotes a culture
of openness. Complementing this is the institutionalization of digital
platforms such as the FOI website and the Open Data Philippines portal,
ensuring that information is accessible, searchable, and usable by the public.


       At the same time, the measure carefully balances transparency with
legitimate state interests. It provides narrowly tailored exceptions to
disclosure, including matters involving national security, foreign relations,
law enforcement, trade secrets, and personal privacy. These safeguards are
aligned with existing laws such as Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data
Privacy Act of 2012, and jurisprudential standards on privileged
communication and due process. Importantly, the bill adopts a presumption
in favor of disclosure, placing the burden on government agencies to justify
any denial of access.


       The proposed measure also strengthens institutional accountability by
imposing administrative and criminal liabilities for unjustified refusal to
disclose information, deliberate concealment, or destruction of public records.
It further mandates the establishment of FOI units within agencies, the
adoption of records management systems, and the submission of annual
reports on FOI compliance. These mechanisms ensure that transparency is not
only declared as policy but operationalized in practice.



                                                                             2
      In addition, the bill recognizes the importance of building a culture of
transparency beyond government processes. It mandates capacity-building
programs for public officials and integrates the principles of freedom of
information, accountability, and good governance into the education
curriculum. By doing so, it fosters a more informed and engaged citizenry that
understands both its rights and responsibilities in a democratic society.


      This measure is likewise consistent with the Philippines’ commitments
under international frameworks such as the Open Government Partnership
and global standards on transparency and access to information.
Strengthening access to information is not only a matter of domestic
governance but also a key component of promoting investor confidence,
improving public service delivery, and combating corruption.


      Ultimately, this proposed Freedom of Information Act affirms that
transparency is not a privilege granted at the discretion of government, but a
fundamental right of the people. By institutionalizing open access to
information, establishing clear rules and safeguards, and promoting a culture
of accountability, this measure seeks to bridge the gap between constitutional
principle and everyday practice.


     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

25           multilateral negotiation or seriously jeopardize the diplomatic relations of

13          SEC. 14. Procedure of Access. – The following procedure shall govern the
14   filing and processing of a request for access to information:
15          a)   Any person who requests access to information shall submit an FOI

17   contact information of the requesting party, provide valid proof of the
18   identification of the requesting party, reasonably describe the information
19   requested and the reason for, or purpose of, the request for information: Provided,
20   That no request shall be denied or refused acceptance unless the reason for the
21   request is contrary to law or existing rules and regulations or the information

23   the request shall provide reasonable assistance to enable all requesting parties,
24   particularly those with special needs, to comply with the necessary requirements
25   under this section;
26          b)   The receiving government office shall indicate on the request the date
27   and time of receipt, and the name, rank, title, and position of the receiving public
28   officer or employee who shall correspondingly sign the request. A copy of the




                                                                                        13
 1   request shall be furnished to the requesting party. Each government office shall
 2   establish a tracking system to monitor the status of all requests for information
 3   received by it;
 4          c)   Unless an earlier period is provided under existing laws, the
 5   government office shall respond to a request that is fully compliant with
 6   requirements of sub-section (a) hereof as soon as practicable, but not exceeding
 7   fifteen (15) working days from receipt of the request. The agency's response may
 8   either be to grant or deny access to the information requested; and
 9          d)   The period to respond may be extended whenever the information
10   requested requires extensive search of the government office's records facilities,
11   examination of voluminous records, the occurrence of fortuitous events or other

13   request of the extension, setting forth the reasons for such extension. In no case
14   shall the extension go beyond twenty (20) working days unless exceptional

16          Once a decision is made to grant the request, the requesting party shall be
17   notified of such decision and directed to pay a reasonable fee.
18          The same procedure applies when requesting through the FOI website,

20          SEC. 15. Access and Processing Fees – Government offices shall not

22   however, charge a reasonable fee to reimburse necessary costs, including actual
23   costs of reproduction and copying of the information requested, subject to existing
24   rules and regulations. In no case shall the applicable fees be so onerous as to defeat
25   the purpose of this Act.

27   request in whole or in part, it shall as soon as practicable, and in any case within
28   fifteen (15) working days from the receipt of the request, notify the requesting

 1      party of the denial in writing. The notice shall clearly state the ground or grounds

 3             SEC. 17. Remedies in Cases of Denial. – a) In all government agencies
 4      other than the Judicial Branch:
 5                   1) The requesting party shall be informed in writing of the decision
 6              within fifteen (15) working days from the receipt of the request. Denial of
 7              any request for access to information may be appealed to the person or
 8              office next higher in authority following the procedure provided in the FOI
 9              Manual as required in Section 25, paragraph (h) of this Act: Provided, That
10              the written appeal must be filed by the same person making the request
11              within fifteen (15) calendar days from the notice of denial, or from the
12              lapse of the period to respond to the request;
13                   2) The appeal shall be decided by the person or office next higher in
14              authority within thirty (30) working days from the filing of said written
15              appeal. If the appeal is granted, the requested information shall be released
16              within fifteen (15) working days to the requesting party from the date of the
17              decision. Failure to decide within the period shall be deemed an approval
18              and will always be considered in favor of access unless prohibited due to
19              exceptions. Denial should also be in writing; and
20                   3) Upon the denial of the appeal, the requesting party may file the
21              appropriate case in the proper courts in accordance with the Rules of Court.
22              b) In the Judicial Branch – The Judiciary shall be governed by remedies as
23              promulgated by the Supreme Court.
24            The remedies under this section shall be sought or granted without prejudice

 1                           IV.LIABILITIES AND PENALTIES
 2          SEC. 18. Administrative Liability. – Depending on the gravity and
 3   repetition of the act, the following acts shall be tantamount to neglect of duty,
 4   refusal to perform official duty, or some other appropriate charge under existing
 5   rules of the Civil Service Commission, and shall constitute grounds for
 6   administrative and disciplinary sanction against any public official or employee:
 7          a) refusal to promptly forward the FOI request, pursuant to Section 14
 8   herein, to the public officer within the same office or agency responsible for
 9   officially acting on the request when such is the direct cause of the failure to
10   disclose the information within the periods required under this Act;
11          b) failure to act on the request within periods required under this Act;
12          c) claim of an exception under Section 10 of this Act when the claim is
13   manifestly devoid of factual or legal basis;
14          d) refusal to comply with the decision of an immediate supervisor or of any
15   court ordering the release of information; and
16          e) approval of policies, rules and regulations manifestly contrary to the
17   provisions of this Act, and which policies, rules and regulations are the direct
18   cause of the denial of a request for information.
19          SEC. 19. Criminal Liability – a) The commission of the following acts by a
20   public officer or employee shall be subject to the penalty of imprisonment of not
21   less than one (1) month, but not more than six (6) months, with the accessory
22   penalty of dismissal from the service:
23                (1) falsely denying or concealing the existence of information
24         mandated for disclosure under this Act; and
25                (2) destroying, or causing to be destroyed, information or documents
26         being requested under this Act for the purpose of frustrating the requesting
27         party's access thereto.




                                                                                         16
 1         (b) Any private individual who knowingly induces or causes the commission
 2         of the foregoing acts shall be liable as principal by inducement in the
 3         prosecution of public officials or employees under this section.
 4          SEC. 20. Denial in Good Faith Not a Ground for Liability. – A denial in
 5   good faith of a request for access to information made pursuant to the provisions of
 6   this Act shall not constitute a ground for administrative or criminal liability.
 7          SEC. 21. Act Not a bar to Claim of Right to Information Under the
 8   Constitution. – No provision of this Act shall be interpreted as a bar to any claim
 9   of the right to information under Article III, Section 7 of the 1987 Constitution.
10                    V. ADMINISTRATIVE DUTIES OF AGENCIES
11          SEC. 22. One-Stop Shop. – Each government office is mandated to have a
12   one-stop shop for the services made available by this Act.
13          SEC. 23. Release to One, Release to All. – For purposes of streamlining
14   requests of information, an information that has been made available to an
15   individual through a request for information shall also be published in an
16   appropriate website such as the FOI website, the Open Data Philippines website,
17   Official Gazette Online, departmental website, or local government website, thus
18   allowing the public access to all information that has been requested.
19          SEC. 24. Tracking of Requests for Information. – Websites of government
20   agencies shall contain a matrix of requests made, their status, and the decision
21   regarding the request. The matrix shall also contain links to uploaded information
22   from approved requests. In such cases where requests are denied, the matrix shall
23   contain the reasons for denial and the status of the appeal of such is made.
24          SEC. 25. Freedom       of   Information    Manual.     –   For    the   effective
25   implementation of this Act, all government agencies shall prepare an FOI Manual,
26   setting forth of the following:

 8             SEC. 26. Records Management. – Subject to existing laws, rules, and
 9      regulations, government offices shall create and maintain an accurate and complete
10      record of important information in appropriate formats, and implement a records
11      management       system      that   facilitates   easy   identification,   retrieval   and
12      communication of information to the public.
13             SEC. 27. Annual Reports on Actions Taken on Requests for Access to
14      Information. – All government agencies shall prepare, for each fiscal year, a report
15      on the number of requests for information received, processed, granted and denied;
16      of appeals made from denials of such requests; and of pending court actions they
17      are a party to as a result of such requests. These reports may be integrated in the
18      main annual reports of government agencies and may be posted and published in
19      their respective websites.

21             SEC. 28. Capacity-Building and Roles of the Department of Information
22      and Communications Technology (DICT). – Every government agency shall
23      ensure the provision of adequate training for its officials and employees to improve
24      awareness of the people's right to information on matters of public concern and the
25      provisions of this Act. Similarly, every government agency shall endeavor to study
26      and adopt best practices in relation to information disclosure, records maintenance,
27      and archiving.
