                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE


Republic Act 3753 or the Philippine Civil Registry Act of 1930, which governs the registration of
vital events such as births, deaths, and marriages, is long overdue for modernization to reflect
current demographic realities and technological advancements. Outdated framework results in
inefficiencies and limits the inter-operability of vital systems necessary for inclusive
governance and national development. It does not adequately provide mechanisms for civil
registration in crisis settings—such as natural disasters, displacement, or pandemics—nor does
it include sufficient safeguards against the falsification of civil documents. The absence of
timely and complete data undermines the effectiveness of government interventions,
particularly for the most vulnerable. The data from the 2020 Census illustrates the scale of this
challenge.

Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority released in 2020, there are at least 3.7 million
Filipinos – 1.4 million of them children - remain unregistered. The Bangsamoro Autonomous
Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) is particularly affected, with a birth registration rate of
only 77%, far below the national average of 96.6%. This means approximately 1.1 million
unregistered individuals are located in BARMM alone. Low medical certification of deaths is
another concern in BARMM, where only 10–15% of deaths are certified, compared to the 40–
50% national average. The prevalence of home deaths, limited access to health services, and
cultural practices—such as requiring burial within 24 hours—contribute to underreporting and
inaccuracies in death data. The absence of accurate, timely mortality statistics also stems from
underutilization of the Medical Certificate on the Cause of Death (MCCOD), especially in
conflict-affected or remote areas. There is a shortage of physicians and accredited facilities,
and families often rely on informal documentation, further compromising the quality of
national health and mortality data. This significant gap is attributed to a combination of cultural
norms, geographic inaccessibility, and inadequate infrastructure, all of which hinder timely and
universal civil registration.

The passage of Republic Act 11055, or the Philippine Identification System Act, introduced a
national ID system that aids in streamlining demographic data collection. However, a cohesive


                                                                                      Page 1 of 40
and integrated CRVS framework is critical in harmonizing CRVS with health and social
protection information systems to improve data-driven policymaking and service delivery.

To address these long-standing systemic challenges, a modern, inclusive, responsive, and
comprehensive civil registration and vital statistics system bill is central to this reform agenda.
This proposed legislation envisions a fully digitized and modernized CRVS system that ensures
universal and inclusive registration. It responds to evolving social norms and structures while
enabling the use of real-time data to guide policymaking and development planning. The new
system will integrate existing government databases such as the national ID registry and align
with international standards. Its key innovation lies in its child rights-based and equity-centered
approach, prioritizing inclusion of every Filipino, especially the marginalized. By
institutionalizing a rights-based, gender- and culture-sensitive framework for civil registration
and ensuring interoperability with other government systems, the new CRVS law will enhance
the State’s capacity to provide legal identity for all—laying the foundation for inclusive
development, better governance, and stronger protection of human rights.

With the aforementioned backdrop of the issue, immediate passage of this bill is sought.

 5   SECTION 79.              Authority to Correct Clerical or Typographical Error and Change of First
 6   Name or Nickname – No entry in a civil register shall be changed or corrected without a judicial
 7   order, except for clerical or typographical errors and change of first name or nickname, the day
 8   and month in the date of birth or sex of a person where it is patently clear that there was a clerical
 9   or typographical error or mistake in the entry, which can be corrected or changed by the
10   concerned city or municipal civil registrar or consul general in accordance with the provisions of
11   this Act and its implementing rules and regulations. Provided that no correction shall involve
12   substantial alterations that would affect the nationality, citizenship, age and status unless it can be
13   proven by other sufficient supporting documents that the said error is due to inadvertence and
14   that it is merely clerical or typographical error.
15
16   SECTION 80.            Grounds for Change of First Name or Nickname. – The petition for change
17   of first name or nickname may be allowed in any of the following cases:
18
19   (1) The petitioner finds the first name or nickname to be ridiculous, tainted with dishonor or
20   extremely difficult to write or pronounce.
21
22   (2) The new first name or nickname has been habitually and continuously used by the petitioner
23   and he has been publicly known by that by that first name or nickname in the community: or
24
25   (3) The change will avoid confusion.
26
27   SECTION 81.             Who May File the Petition and Where. – Any person having direct and
28   personal interest in the correction of a clerical or typographical error in an entry and/or change of
29   first name or nickname in the civil register may file, in person, a verified petition with the local
30   civil registry office of the city or municipality where the record being sought to be corrected or
31   changed is kept.
32
33   In case the petitioner has already migrated to another place in the country and it would not be
34   practical for such party, in terms of transportation expenses, time and effort to appear in person
35   before the local civil registrar keeping the documents to be corrected or changed, the petition
36   may be filed, in person, with the local civil registrar of the place where the interested party is
37   presently residing or domiciled. The two (2) local civil registrars concerned will then
38   communicate to facilitate the processing of the petition.
39
40   Citizens of the Philippines who are presently residing or domiciled in foreign countries may file
41   their petition, in person, with the nearest Philippine Consulates.
42
43   The petitions filed with the city or municipal civil registrar or the consul general shall be
44   processed in accordance with this Act and its implementing rules and regulations.
45
46   All petitions for the clerical or typographical errors and/or change of first names or nicknames
47   may be availed of only once.
48
49   SECTION 82. Form and Contents of the Petition. – The petition shall be in the form of an
50   affidavit, subscribed and sworn to before any person authorized by the law to administer oaths.
                                                                                             Page 32 of 40
 1   The affidavit shall set forth facts necessary to establish the merits of the petition and shall show
 2   affirmatively that the petitioner is competent to testify to the matters stated. The petitioner shall
 3   state the particular erroneous entry or entries, which are sought to be corrected and/or the change
 4   sought to be made.
 5
 6   The petition shall be supported with the following documents:
 7
 8   (1) A certified true machine copy of the certificate or of the page of the registry book containing
 9   the entry or entries sought to be corrected or changed.
10
11   (2) At least two (2) public or private documents showing the correct entry or entries upon which
12   the correction or change shall be based; and
13
14   (3) Other documents which the petitioner or the city or municipal civil registrar or the consul
15   general may consider relevant and necessary for the approval of the petition.
16
17   In case of change of first name or nickname, the petition shall likewise be supported with the
18   documents mentioned in the immediately preceding paragraph. In addition, the petition shall be
19   published at least once a week for two (2) consecutive weeks, in accordance with Section 92 of
20   this Act.
21
22   No petition for correction of erroneous entry concerning the date of birth or the sex of a person
23   shall be entertained except if the petition is accompanied by earliest school record or earliest
24   school documents such as, but not limited to, medical records, baptismal certificate and other
25   documents issued by religious authorities; nor shall any entry involving change of gender
26   corrected except if the petition is accompanied by a certification issued by an accredited
27   government physician attesting to the fact that the petitioner has not undergone sex change or
28   sex transplant. The petition for change of first name or nickname, or for correction of erroneous
29   entry concerning the day and month in the date of birth or the sex of a person, as the case may be,
30   shall be published at least once a week for two (2) consecutive weeks, in accordance with Section
31   92 of this Act.
32
33   Furthermore, the petitioner shall submit a certification from the appropriate law enforcement
34   agencies that he has no pending case or no criminal record.
35
36   The petition and its supporting papers shall be filed in three (3) copies to be distributed as
37   follows: first copy to the concerned city or municipal civil registrar, or the consul general; second
38   copy to the Office of the Civil Registrar General; and third copy to the petitioner.
39
40   SECTION 83. Duties of the City or Municipal Civil Registrar or the Consul General. – The city
41   or municipal civil registrar or the consul general to whom the petition is presented shall examine
42   the petition and its supporting documents. He shall post the petition in a conspicuous place
43   provided for that purpose for ten (10) consecutive days after he finds the petition and its
44   supporting documents sufficient in form and substance.
45
46   The city or municipal civil registrar or the consul general shall act on the petition and shall render
47   a decision not later than five (5) working days after the completion of the posting and/or
48   publication requirement. He shall transmit a copy of his decision together with the records of the
49   proceedings to the Office of the Civil Registrar General within five (5) working days from the
50   date of the decision.

 2   SECTION 84. Duties and Powers of the Civil Registrar General. – The civil registrar general
 3   shall, upon receipt of the decision granting a petition, exercise the power to impugn such decision
 4   by way of an objection based on the following grounds:
 5
 6   (1) The error is not clerical or typographical;
 7
 8   (2) The correction of an entry or entries in the civil register is substantial or controversial as it
 9   affects the civil status of a person; or
10
11   (3) The basis used in changing the first name or nickname of a person does not fall under Section
12   80.
13
14   The civil registrar general shall immediately notify the city or municipal civil registrar or the
15   consul general of the action taken on the decision. Upon receipt of the notice thereof, the city or
16   municipal civil registrar or the consul general shall notify the petitioner of such action.
17
18   The petitioner may seek reconsideration with the civil registrar general or file the appropriate
19   petition with the proper court.
20
21   If the civil registrar general fails to exercise his power to impugn the decision of the city or
22   municipal civil registrar or of the consul general within the period prescribed herein, such
23   decision shall become final and executory.
24
25   Where the petition is denied by the city or municipal civil registrar or the consul general, the
26   petitioner may either appeal the decision to the civil registrar general or file the appropriate
27   petition with the proper court.

31   indigent petitioner shall be exempt from the payment of the said fee.

39   SECTION 86. Civil Registry Documents as Prima Facie Evidence of Vital Events. -The registry
40   books making up the civil register, the office files in hard copies and its corresponding digital
41   files of the civil registrar's offices, certifications or certified copies from these files and all other
42   documents relating thereto shall be considered public documents and shall be prima facie

46   to be removed from his office, except upon lawful order of the court, in which case the proper
47   receipt shall be secured.

26   SECTION 93. Offenses and Penalties. -
27
28   a)   It shall be prohibited for any natural or juridical person or entity to commit or facilitate the
29        commission of any of the following acts:
30
31         1)   forging civil registration documents, certificates, and registers;
32         2)   willful falsification, alteration, or tampering of civil registration documents,
33              certificates, and registers;
34         3)   submitting false information in registering vital events;
35         4)   using, abetting to use, or possessing falsified or illegally obtained certificates and
36              security papers;
37         5)   profiteering and unauthorized facilitation of registration and copy issuance of civil
38              registry documents.
39
40        Any person who commits the foregoing violations shall, upon conviction, be punished with
41        imprisonment of six (6) years but not more than twelve (12) years or a fine of not less than
42        One hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00) but not more than One million pesos
43        (Phpl,000,000.00), or both, at the discretion of the court.
44
45   b)   It is prohibited for any government employee or official, solemnizing officers, or for any
46        medical practitioner, attendant, or health facility officer authorized to prepare civil registry
47        documents to:
48
49        1)    intentionally prepare/aid or abet in the preparation of a certificate containing false or
50              fraudulent information;
                                                                                           Page 36 of 40
 1         2)   intentionally and knowingly issue certificates containing false information;
 2         3)   make any unauthorized alteration in civil registration documents, certificates, and
 3              registers; and
 4         4)   intentional withholding of information or refusing to inform the LCR of a vital event.
 5
 6        Any government employee or official, solemnizing officers, or for any medical practitioner,
 7        attendant, or health facility officer who commits the foregoing violations shall, upon
 8        conviction, be punished with imprisonment of six (6) years but not more than twelve (12)
 9        years or fine of not less than One hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00) but not more than
10        One million pesos (P1,000,000.00), or both, at the discretion of the court.
11
12        In all instances, if the violation was committed by a government official or employee, the
13        penalty shall include perpetual absolute disqualification from holding any public office or
14        employment in the government, including any GOCCs, and their subsidiaries.
15
16        The above violations shall be without prejudice to the filing of criminal cases in accordance
17        with the Revised Penal Code and other special penal laws.
18
19   c)   Non-payment of hospital fees not bar to registration, it shall be unlawful for health facilities
20        and responsible personnel, and medical practitioners, to refuse to report or cause the
21        registration of a vital event due to non-payment of hospital fees.
22
23        Any government employee or official, or any medical practitioner or health facility who
24        commits the foregoing violations shall, upon conviction, be punished with imprisonment of
25        six (6) years but not more than twelve (12) years or fine of not less than One hundred
26        thousand pesos (P100,000.00) but not more than One million pesos (P1,000,000.00), or
27        both, at the discretion of the court.
28
29        This provision shall be without prejudice to other penalties and fines that may be imposed
30        on government employees and officials for misfeasance, malfeasance, and other acts and
31        omissions prohibited by law.
32
33        In case of acts committed by private individuals, the penalties imposed in this Section shall
34        be in addition to those imposed on acts or omissions punishable by existing penal and other
35        laws.
36
37        In case of acts committed by juridical persons, the penalty shall be imposed on its president,
38        manager or most senior officer.

25              Government, Bureau of Immigration and Justice with a rank of Undersecretary or
