                              AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING STANDARDS FOR RELATED LEARNING EXPERIENCES
(RLE) IN NURSING EDUCATION, PROHIBITING THE COLLECTION OF RLE FEES
FROM NURSING STUDENTS IN STATE UNIVERSITIES AND COLLEGES (SUCs)
AND LOCAL UNIVERSITIES AND COLLEGES (LUCs), AND PROVIDING
FUNDING SUPPORT THROUGH THE COMMISSION ON HIGHER EDUCATION
(CHED)

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Under the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) Memorandum Order No.
14, Series of 2009, students of the Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BSN) program are
mandated to complete at least 46 units or 2,346 hours of Related Learning
Experiences (RLE), which may be extended until expected competencies are
satisfactorily achieved. RLE are integral to nursing education as teaching-learning
opportunities designed to develop the competencies of nursing students utilizing the
nursing process in varying health situations. Through RLE, Filipino nursing students
are provided with hands-on clinical training and essential exposure to real-world
healthcare settings.

        However, despite the existence of Republic Act No. 10931, otherwise known as
the Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act (UAQTE), that provides for free
tuition and institutional fees in State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) and Local
Universities and Colleges (LUCs) and Tertiary Education Subsidies (TES) in private
higher education institutions (HEIs), RLE impose substantial financial burdens on
nursing students through various fees, including affiliation charges, hospital training
fees, transportation expenses, and allowances for clinical instructors. These RLE fees
and related expenses, which are mostly shouldered by the nursing students
themselves, range from P20,000 to P80,000 per year,1 thus creating significant
barriers and placing underprivileged nursing students at a disadvantage.

       In recognition of the noble contribution and significant role of nurses to the
country’s healthcare sector, and in fulfilling the State’s mandate to make quality
education accessible to all citizens, this bill seeks to institutionalize the non-collection
of RLE fees from students in SUCs and LUCs. Under this bill, all RLE-related expenses
shall be considered part of the tuition or institutional costs charged to the government
through the Unified Student Financial Assistance System for Tertiary Education
(UniFAST). As such, this bill mandates that all SUCs and LUCs offering nursing
programs submit projected RLE expenses to CHED for funding through the UniFAST
or other designated CHED mechanisms. For nursing students enrolled in accredited
private HEIs, this bill also expands eligibility for the TES program to cover RLE fees.

      In the 20th Congress, similar bills were filed by Representatives Walfredo
“Arman” Dimaguila, Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo, Antolin Oreta III, and Javier Benitez in
the House of Representatives, and Senator Bam Aquino in the Senate. This bill fully
adopts Senator Bam Aquino’s Senate Bill No. 123.

       In view of the foregoing, early approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

                                   AN ACT

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:


 1          SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Libreng RLE Act.”
 2
 3          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to protect
 4   and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and to take
 5   appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all, pursuant to Article XIV,
 6   Section 1 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution. Consistent with this mandate, and
 7   recognizing the crucial role of Related Learning Experiences (RLE) in nursing
 8   education, the State shall institutionalize standards for RLE and ensure that no
 9   additional financial burden shall be imposed on nursing students in the country for the
10   conduct of such activities.
11
12          SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to:
13
14          (a) All SUCs and LUCs offering nursing education programs duly recognized
15   by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED);
16          (b) All private higher education institutions (HEIs) with nursing programs
17   recognized by CHED; and
18          (c) All nursing students enrolled in recognized nursing education programs in
19   the Philippines.

20
21           SEC. 4. Standards for Related Learning Experiences (RLE). – CHED shall
22   institutionalize and enforce standards for the conduct of RLEs. These standards shall
23   guarantee the provision of quality clinical instruction and learning environments,
24   adequate clinical partnerships, and competent supervision of nursing students.
25
26          SEC. 5. Prohibition on Collection of RLE Fees in SUCs and LUCs. – No SUC
27   or LUC shall impose or collect any fee, charge, or assessment from nursing students
28   for the conduct of RLEs that are part of their approved academic program. All RLE-
29   related expenses shall be considered part of the tuition or institutional costs charged
30   to the government through the Unified Student Financial Assistance System for
31   Tertiary Education (UniFAST).
32
33          SEC. 6. Budgeting and Funding of RLE in SUCs and LUCs. – All SUCs and
34   LUCs offering nursing programs shall submit projected RLE expenses to CHED
35   annually. CHED shall evaluate and consolidate these for inclusion in its annual budget
36   proposal to the Department of Budget and Management (DBM). Approved funding
37   shall be disbursed to SUCs and LUCs through UniFAST or other designated CHED
38   mechanisms.
39
40         SEC. 7. Tertiary Education Subsidy (TES) for Nursing Students in Private HEIs.
41   – Nursing students enrolled in accredited private HEIs may apply for assistance for
42   RLE-related expenses under the Tertiary Education Subsidy (TES) program pursuant
43   to Republic Act No. 10931. TES guidelines shall be updated to include RLE fees as
44   allowable costs.
45
46           SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
47   the effectivity of this Act, CHED, in coordination with DBM and UniFAST, shall
48   promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to effectively implement the provisions
49   of this Act.
50
51           SEC. 9. Appropriations. – The amounts necessary to implement the provisions
52   of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA) under the
53   CHED budget and UniFAST Fund.
54
55          SEC. 10. Separability Clause. – If any section or provision of this Act is declared
56   invalid or unconstitutional, the sections or provisions not affected thereby shall
57   continue to be in full force and effect.
58
59         SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive and
60   administrative orders, letters of instruction, rules and regulations, or parts thereof
61   inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or amended
62   accordingly.
