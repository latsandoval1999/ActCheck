                                THEREFOR

                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Constitution imposes upon the State the obligation to ensure that essential
health services, facilities, and resources are not only available and accessible, but are
also responsive to the needs of the population.1 Among these essential health
services is the availability of safe and adequate affordable blood and blood products,
which are critical in saving lives during medical emergencies, surgical procedures,
childbirth, trauma care, and the treatment of chronic and life-threatening conditions.

      In recognition of this need, Republic Act No. 7719, or the National Blood
Services Act of 1994, was enacted to promote voluntary blood donation, regulate
blood banks, and establish a coordinated national blood services program. The law
sought to ensure a safe blood supply in the country by prohibiting commercial blood
donation and encouraging altruistic blood donation as a public health measure.
Pursuant to the authority granted under this law, the Department of Health (DOH)
issued Administrative Order No. 2005-002, which established the Philippine Blood
Center (PBC) as the national blood service facility tasked with blood collection, testing,
processing, storage, and distribution, as well as donor recruitment and training.

         Despite the critical role performed by the Philippine Blood Center, it continues
to operate without a specific legislative charter. Its existence, authority, organizational
structure, and funding remain dependent on administrative issuances and executive
priorities.

        As a result, the PBC lacks the institutional permanence and autonomy
necessary to pursue infrastructure expansion, long-term planning, and sustained
nationwide blood donor mobilization. This situation further limits its capacity to
proactively address the problem of low blood availability in the country, especially
during public health emergencies and other periods of increased medical demand.
Additionally, sans a clear statutory mandate, the PBC’s programs and operational
capabilities are vulnerable to changes in administration, budgetary limitations, and
shifting policy priorities.

        Moreover, the problem confronting the country’s blood services system is not
limited to insufficiency of supply alone. Equally pressing is the issue of access. Blood
collection and distribution facilities remain highly concentrated in urban centers,
particularly in Metro Manila. Our kababayans in far-flung and underserved
communities often face delays or difficulties in obtaining blood due to weak
coordination among blood service units and logistical constraints. These barriers
contribute to preventable morbidity and mortality, especially among vulnerable
populations.

        This bill seeks to address these concerns by institutionalizing the Philippine
Blood Center which shall be a permanent, chartered body that will serve as the
country’s central authority for blood services. By granting the PBC its own charter, the
bill aims to strengthen its administrative autonomy and financial stability, enabling it to
function as a truly national institution capable of leading blood services across the
country. Institutionalization will also allow the PBC to develop long-term strategies and
invest in modern facilities and technologies, as well as implement nationwide donor
education and recruitment programs.

       Moreover, the bill proposes the establishment of regional blood centers
strategically located throughout the country. These regional centers shall operate
under a decentralized but fully coordinated framework, ensuring that blood services
are brought closer to communities while remaining aligned with national standards
and policies. This approach promotes equity in access by reducing regional
disparities, improving response times during emergencies, and strengthening local
capacities for blood collection, testing, storage, and distribution. A coordinated
regional network also enhances preparedness for disasters and public health crises,
when rapid mobilization and redistribution of blood supply are critical.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in the
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Title – This set shall be known as the “Philippine Blood Center Act of 2026."
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right to health of every patient requiring blood transfusion by ensuring
adequate supply of blood that is safe, affordable, equitable, effective and efficient at all
times when the need arises
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
          a. Blood – refers to the human blood for transfusion
          b. Blood component – refers but not limited to whole blood. red cells,
             granulocytes, plasma, platelets, cryoprecipitate and cryosupernate
             prepared in a Blood Center
          c. Blood product – a therapeutic substance derived from whole blood or
             plasma
          d. Voluntary Non-remunerated Blood Donor – a donor who gives blood freely
             and voluntarily without receiving money or any form of payment
SEC. 4. Philippine Blood Center – There shall be established a Philippine Blood Center
(PBC) which shall address the safe and equitable supply of blood and blood products to
all hospitals and non-hospital based facilities doing blood transfusion. It shall be under
the direct control and supervision of the Department of Health (DOH), and shall be located
at the Lung Center of the Philippines (LCP) Compound, Quezon Avenue, Quezon City.



                                           4
The Philippine Blood Center shall be the lead blood center of the Philippine National
Blood Services (PNBS), a group of DOH-retained, Philippine Red Cross and LGU
regional blood centers all over the Philippines.
SEC. 5. Purposes and Objectives. -The PBC shall have the following purposes and
objectives:
   a. Centrally recruit, motivate, and screen blood donors and care for voluntary blood
      donors;
          b. Centrally collect blood from qualified voluntary blood donors;
          c. Test units of blood for the five (5) transfusion-transmissible infections (HIV
             1 and 2, Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, Malaria and Syphilis)
          d. Process whole blood into blood components;
          e. Collect blood components through Apheresis:
          f. Store, issue, transport and distribute whole blood and blood components to
             end-user hospitals and non-hospital health facilities;
          g. Implement and monitor quality assurance to selected regional blood
             centers;
          h. Conduct training and research related to blood bank technology and blood
             transfusion services;
          i. Perform special confirmatory and reference immunohematologic
             examinations such as: identification of red cell antibodies, phenotyping of
             patients and donors, and provision of antigen-negative red cell units;
          j. Manufacture and provide typing sera and panel of reagent red cells;
          k. Prepare special plasma derivatives;
          I. Ensure that safe supply of blood and blood products to all hospitals and
             other health facilities are adequate in terms of distribution and delivery; and
          m. Perform other functions as may be assigned by the National Council of
             Blood Services.
SEC. 6. Scope of Services. – Consistent with its purposes and objectives, the PBC shall
provide the following services:
          a.   Advocate, disseminate information and promote voluntary blood donation
               including healthy lifestyle in the community as an effective way to recruit,
               retain and care for qualified volunteer blood donors:
          b.   Coordinate human resource and logistics assistance to mobile blood
               donations within the Blood Services Network especially during disease
               outbreak like Dengue infection, natural calamities and disasters where
               blood and blood products are critical as a form of life -saving measure;



                                           5
           c.    Engage in social mobilization activities like conduct of mobile blood
                 donation activities in partnership with the Local Blood Council, Philippine

           Red Cross, Blood Service Facilities, Local Government Executives, Blood
           Collection Units and other institutional blood donor organizations;
           d.    Collect Apheresis – derived blood products from qualified volunteer
                 donors.
           e.    Test blood units for infectious agents, blood component processing and
                 leuko-reduction/depletion of units of blood for issuance and distribution of
                 safe, quality blood and blood products;
           f.    Standardize and regularly monitor all Blood Service Facilities and
                 hospitals in its network;
           g.    Provide on-the-job training for BSF staff, technical assistance as well as
                 technical resource persons in the education and training of medical
                 technologists and other technical personnel of other regional blood
                 centers; and
           h.    Conduct research in the field of blood transfusion and blood bank
                 technology.
SEC. 7. Organizational Structure and Staffing Pattern. – The DOH, through the Health
Human Resource Development Bureau (HHRDB) shall determine the organizational
structure and staffing pattern of the PBC subject to the evaluation by the Civil Service
Commission and of the Organization Position Classification and Compensation Bureau
of the Department of Budget and Management. The DOH may reorganize said structure.
modify the staffing pattern and create or abolish divisions, sections or units in the PBC.
SEC. 8. Appointment by the DOH. – The DOH, through the HHRDB shall have the
authority to appoint, promote, transfer, remove, suspend or otherwise discipline the
officers and employees of the PBC upon the recommendation of the Director, subject to
Civil Service laws, rules and regulations.
SEC. 9. Director. – The PBC shall be headed by a Director who shall be appointed by
the President of the Philippines upon the recommendation of the Secretary of Health.
      The Director shall exercise the following powers and functions:
            a.    Execute the policies, guidelines, standards, projects, programs and
                  other activities approved by the Secretary of Health, and be responsible
                  for the efficient discharge of management and operational functions;
            b.    Submit for consideration and approval of the Secretary of Health
                  proposed measures, policies, guidelines and programs as may be
                  deemed necessary or proper for the effective implementation of the
                  purposes and objectives of this Act;



                                            6
            c.   Direct and supervise the management, operation and administration of
                 the PBC, and may delegate this power and any or some of his
                 administrative responsibilities and duties to the other officers of the
                 PBC;
            d.   Execute, on behalf of the Secretary of Health, all contracts and
                 agreements which the latter may enter into, and to execute, accomplish
                 and deliver any and all documents relative to such contracts and
                 agreements;
            e.   Represent the PBC in all dealings with other offices, agencies and
                 instrumentalities of the government, and all other persons or entities.
                 whether domestic or foreign and whether government or private:
            f.   Recommend to the Secretary of Health the appointment. promotion,
                 transfer. dismissal and suspension of the officers and employees of the
                 PBC; and
            g.   Exercise such other powers and perform such duties as may be vested
                 or reposed by the Secretary of Health.
SEC. 10. Program for Indigents. – The DOH shall ensure that the PBC shall adopt and
enforce a program for indigents needing blood and blood products in accordance to the
implementing guidelines on classification of patients and on availment of Medical Social
Services in Government Hospitals.
SEC. 11. Tax Exemptions and other Privileges. – The provision of any general or
special law to the contrary notwithstanding, all donations, grants contributions, gifts or
endowments received by the PBC pursuant hereto, shall be exempt from income. gift and
all other kinds of taxes, and shall be further considered as allowable deductions from the
gross income of the donor, in accordance with the provisions of the National Internal
Revenue Code of 1997 as amended.
SEC. 12. Annual Report – The PBC shall submit an annual report to the President of
the Philippines, the Senate Committee on Health and Demography and the House
Committee on Health, on its activities. accomplishments and recommendations to better
address and improve the safe supply of blood in the Philippines.
SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations – The Secretary of Health in
coordination with the Secretary of Budget and Development, shall promulgate such rules
and regulations necessary for the implementation of this Act
SEC. 14. Appropriations. – The amount of One Hundred Million (Php 100,000,000.00)
is hereby appropriated for the initial operation and maintenance of the PBC. Thereafter,
such sum as may be necessary for the continued operation of the PBC shall be included
in the annual General Appropriations Act.
SEC. 15. Non-Profit Operation – The PBC shall operate on a non-profit basis provided
that it may collect blood service fees not greater than the maximum prescribed by the



                                          7
DOH which shall be limited to the necessary expenses entailed in collecting, processing
and testing of blood. Blood shall be collected from healthy voluntary donors only.
SEC. 16. Establishment of a Trust Fund. – A trust fund shall be established out of the
donations generated by the PBC and its retained income from the collected blood service
fees. The funds shall be used for the twenty-four hour operation, additional manpower,
purchase of additional blood bank equipment and for the augmentation of Maintenance
and Other Operating Expenses (MOOE) specifically for the purchase of reagents,
medical and laboratory supplies.
SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, proclamations. rules and
regulations, or parts thereof, which are inconsistent with the Act are hereby repealed.
amended, or modified accordingly.
SEC. 18. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication
in the Official Gazette or in two (2) national newspapers of general circulation.
