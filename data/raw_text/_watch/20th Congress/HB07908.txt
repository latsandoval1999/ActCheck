                             AN ACT
SIMPLIFYING THE  DOCUMENTARY      REQUIREMENTS   FOR   ACCESSING
GOVERNMENT FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

     The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to promote a just
and dynamic social order and to prioritize measures that protect the dignity
and welfare of the underprivileged, sick, elderly, women, and children. 1
Access to timely financial assistance for medical and burial needs is a
concrete expression of this constitutional duty. However, despite this mandate,
many Filipinos in distress continue to face burdensome and excessive
documentary requirements before they can avail themselves of urgently
needed government assistance.

       At present, agencies such as the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD), the Department of Health (DOH), the Philippine Charity
Sweepstakes Office (PCSO), and the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
(PhilHealth) provide various forms of medical, burial, and related financial
assistance. While these programs are essential social protection mechanisms,
the absence of a uniform statutory ceiling on documentary requirements has
resulted in procedural inconsistencies, documentary proliferation, and delays
that defeat the very purpose of emergency assistance. Beneficiaries are
frequently required to secure multiple certifications, social case study reports,
barangay clearances, and other supporting documents that entail additional
travel, expense, and lost time—burdens that weigh heaviest on indigent
families during medical emergencies or bereavement.

       This proposed measure institutionalizes a uniform and minimal set of
documentary requirements for availing immediate financial assistance, limiting
submissions to a valid government-issued identification card, a billing
statement from the hospital, clinic, funeral parlor, or related entity, and an
original or certified true copy of a medical certificate or medical abstract, or

a death certificate, as applicable. By codifying these requirements into law,
the bill removes discretionary authority to impose additional documents not
strictly necessary for verification, thereby preventing administrative overreach
while maintaining safeguards against fraud.

       Consistent with the government’s digital transformation agenda, the bill
mandates coordination with the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT) for the utilization of the eGov PH Super
App or similar platforms as mechanisms for submitting requests for medical,
burial, and similar financial assistance. Digital integration enhances
transparency, traceability, and efficiency in public service delivery and aligns
with ongoing e-governance initiatives.2 The measure likewise requires
coordination with the Anti-Red Tape Authority to ensure compliance with
processing standards and to provide a mechanism for addressing complaints
involving undue delays or excessive documentary requirements.

       In addition, the bill mandates same-day processing of requests
submitted with complete documentary requirements during official working
hours, subject to fund availability. This provision reinforces the statutory
timelines under Republic Act No. 11032 and affirms that emergency financial
assistance must be responsive, not merely procedural. In matters of medical
treatment and burial, time is not a mere administrative variable; it is often a
determinant of health outcomes and human dignity.

       Ultimately, the Direkta at Agad na Lingap at Interbensiyon (DALI) Act
transforms bureaucratic compassion into institutional efficiency. It reduces
transaction costs for the poor, curtails opportunities for rent-seeking and fixers,
harmonizes agency practices, strengthens accountability through ARTA
oversight, and accelerates the release of life-saving and dignity-preserving
assistance. By simplifying access to government financial assistance, the State
affirms that social protection must be direct, immediate, and humane.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

1         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Direkta at Agad na

2    Lingap at Interbensiyon (DALI) Act.”


3         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall promote the general welfare

4    of Filipino people by ensuring that individuals and families in difficult situations

5    are provided with timely and accessible financial assistance. To this end, the

6    government shall adopt measures to remove excessive documentary

7    requirements and streamline procedures for availing aid from government

8    agencies.


9         SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all national and

10   local government agencies and instrumentalities that provide medical, burial,

11   and similar forms of financial assistance, including the Department of Social

12   Welfare and Development (DSWD), Department of Health (DOH), Philippine

3         SEC. 4. Standardized Requirements. – To avail of immediate financial

4    assistance, beneficiaries shall be required to submit the following documents:


5         (a) Valid government-issued identification card (ID);

6         (b) Billing statement from the hospital, clinic, funeral parlor, or related

7            entities; and

8         (c) Original or certified true copy of medical certificate or medical

9            abstract with date of issuance, complete name, signature and license

10           number of the attending physician, or death certificate issued by the

11           hospital or the city/municipal health office, whichever is applicable.

12        Hospitals, clinics, and other authorized medical facilities issuing such

13   medical certificates or medical abstracts shall, in coordination with the DOH,

14   ensure that the foregoing information is clearly indicated therein.


15        Where the request for assistance is made through a representative, such

16   representative shall present an authorization letter duly signed by the

17   beneficiary, or, where the beneficiary is unable to sign due to age, illness,

18   disability, or illiteracy, such other reasonable proof of authorization as may be

19   prescribed by the implementing rules and regulations, along with a valid

20   government-issued ID of both the beneficiary and the representative. The

21   authorized representative must be a relative of the beneficiary within the fourth

22   degree of consanguinity or affinity, or a legal guardian: Provided, That no

23   individual shall act as a representative for more than two (2) beneficiaries.

1         SEC. 5. Digital Requests for Government Aid. – The Department of

2    Information and Communications Technology (DICT) shall be primarily tasked

18   Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018”. They shall likewise work with

19   ARTA to address complaints involving undue delays, excessive documentary

20   requirements, or other forms of non-compliance with the provisions of this Act.
