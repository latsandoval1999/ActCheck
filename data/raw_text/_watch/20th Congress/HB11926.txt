                        AN ACT
GRANTING FINANCIAL AND OPERATIONAL AUTONOMY TO SELECT
PUBLIC HOSPITALS, STRENGTHENING THEIR GOVERNANCE,
MODERNIZING HEALTH SERVICE DELIVERY, ESTABLISHING
PERFORMANCE-BASED ACCOUNTABILITY MECHANISMS, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Philippine public healthcare system continues to face persistent
challenges involving underfunding, bureaucratic inefficiencies, delayed
procurement, inadequate facility modernization, limited hospital flexibility,
workforce shortages, and constrained fiscal authority among government hospitals.
Despite the competence and dedication of healthcare professionals, many public
hospitals remain hindered by centralized administrative controls that prevent timely
decision-making, responsive service delivery, and efficient utilization of locally
generated revenues.

      At present, many tertiary and specialty government hospitals are unable to
independently reinvest their earnings, procure urgently needed medicines and
equipment in a timely manner, expand services according to local demands, or
competitively retain skilled medical professionals due to rigid budgetary and
administrative structures. This results in overcrowding, delayed patient care,
outdated infrastructure, and operational inefficiencies that directly affect the quality
of healthcare services provided to Filipinos.

      At the same time, several public hospitals have demonstrated exemplary
performance in fiscal management, patient outcomes, service innovation, and
healthcare delivery. These institutions possess the capability to manage greater
operational responsibilities and financial resources if provided with appropriate
autonomy, accountability safeguards, and performance oversight mechanisms.

       This proposed bill seeks to establish a modern governance framework for top-
performing public hospitals by granting them financial and operational autonomy
while maintaining public ownership, universal access principles, and government
oversight. The measure aims to empower qualified public hospitals to directly
manage revenues, modernize procurement systems, enter into strategic partnerships,
improve compensation and retention mechanisms for healthcare workers, expand
digital health systems, and reinvest earnings into healthcare improvements.

       The bill further institutionalizes performance-based accountability standards,
transparent auditing systems, patient welfare protections, anti-corruption safeguards,
and equitable healthcare access mechanisms to ensure that autonomy translates into
better healthcare outcomes and not commercialization of public health services.

       By enabling qualified public hospitals to operate with greater flexibility and
efficiency while remaining accountable to the State and the Filipino people, this
measure seeks to strengthen the national healthcare system, improve patient services,
reduce administrative delays, and promote sustainable hospital modernization
consistent with the RA 11223 otherwise known as the Universal Health Care Act
and the constitutional mandate to protect and promote the right to health of the
people.
    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      SEC 2. Declaration of Policy.         It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
consciousness among them. The State shall strengthen the public healthcare system
through responsive governance, institutional modernization, efficient resource
utilization, transparency, accountability, and improved healthcare delivery
mechanisms.
      Towards this end, the State shall grant qualified public hospitals greater
financial and operational autonomy to enable them to efficiently deliver accessible,
equitable, affordable, and quality healthcare services while maintaining public
ownership, public accountability, and universal healthcare principles.


      SEC 3. Objectives.     This Act aims to:
      (a). Improve the efficiency, responsiveness, and quality of public healthcare
      services;
      (b). Grant financial and operational autonomy to top-performing public
      hospitals;
      (c). Strengthen hospital governance and administrative flexibility;
      (d). Improve healthcare infrastructure, equipment, and digital systems;
      (e). Enhance retention, welfare, and professional development of healthcare
      workers;
      (f). Institutionalize performance-based management and accountability
      systems;
      (g). Promote innovation, research, and modernization in public healthcare
      institutions;
      (h).   Ensure   transparency,    anti-corruption   safeguards,     and    public
      accountability in hospital operations; and
      (i). Strengthen alignment with the RA 11223 otherwise known as the
      Universal Health Care Act and national health priorities.
      SEC 4. Definition of Terms.      For purposes of this Act, the following terms
are hereby defined:
      (a). Autonomous Public Hospital refers to a government-owned and operated
      hospital granted financial and operational autonomy under this Act.
      (b). Top-Performing Public Hospital refers to a public hospital that meets the
      performance standards established under this Act and by the Department of
      Health (DOH).
      (c). Financial Autonomy refers to the authority to retain, manage, allocate,
      and utilize internally generated revenues subject to applicable laws and
      auditing rules.
      (d). Operational Autonomy refers to delegated authority over procurement,
      staffing, organizational restructuring, partnerships, digital systems, and
      internal management.
      (e). Hospital Governing Board refers to the policy-making body established
      under this Act.


      SEC 5. Coverage.       This Act shall apply to national government hospitals,
specialty hospitals, regional hospitals, medical centers, and selected provincial or
city public hospitals that qualify under the standards established by the Department
of Health (DOH).


      SEC 6. Qualification Standards for Hospital Autonomy.        The Department
of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Department of Budget and Management
(DBM), the Civil Service Commission (CSC), the Commission on Audit (COA), the
Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), and other relevant agencies,
shall establish and periodically update a transparent and evidence-based framework
for determining the eligibility of public hospitals for financial and operational
autonomy.
      Only hospitals demonstrating institutional capacity, sound financial
management, effective governance, quality healthcare delivery, adequate human
resources, operational digital systems, public accountability mechanisms, and
compliance with applicable laws, regulations, and DOH standards shall be granted
autonomous status. The DOH may adopt a phased or tiered autonomy system based
on hospital classification, service capability, geographic location, and institutional
readiness, and may prescribe additional qualification standards consistent with this
Act. Autonomous status shall be valid for five (5) years, subject to annual
performance monitoring and periodic reassessment.


      SEC 7. Grant of Financial Autonomy.              Qualified autonomous public
hospitals shall be authorized to retain, manage, and utilize internally generated
revenues and other lawful funds for the improvement of healthcare services,
including infrastructure development, procurement of medicines and equipment,
workforce development, digitalization, research, emergency preparedness, and other
health-related expenditures.
      They may establish trust funds and reserve funds, receive grants, donations,
and development assistance, undertake multi-year capital investments, and
implement income-generating activities consistent with their public health mandate,
subject to existing budgeting, accounting, auditing, and procurement laws. Nothing
in this Act shall be construed to authorize the privatization or transfer of ownership
of any public hospital, and all financial transactions shall remain subject to COA
oversight and other applicable accountability mechanisms.
      SEC 8. Operational Autonomy.            Autonomous public hospitals shall be
granted operational autonomy to enable them to respond effectively and efficiently
to healthcare demands, institutional challenges, technological developments, and
evolving public health needs, while remaining subject to national health policies,
civil service laws, procurement laws, labor standards, and government
accountability mechanisms.


      Under this section, autonomous public hospital shall have the authority to:
      (a). Streamline procurement processes consistent with existing procurement
      laws;
      (b). Establish hospital-specific staffing patterns subject to CSC standards;
      (c). Hire contractual, technical, and specialized personnel as necessary;
      (d). Implement hospital management information systems and digital health
      technologies;
      (e). Enter into research collaborations, academic partnerships, and training
      agreements;
      (f). Establish specialized medical centers and service units;
      (g). Improve patient referral systems and telemedicine services;
      (h). Adopt performance-based management systems; and
      (i). Establish internal operational policies consistent with national health laws.


      SEC 9. Hospital Governing Board.         Each autonomous public hospital shall
establish a Hospital Governing Board which shall serve as the highest policy-making
and strategic oversight body of the institution. The Governing Board shall ensure
that the exercise of hospital autonomy remains consistent with the objectives of this
Act, national healthcare priorities, principles of public accountability, and the
constitutional mandate to provide accessible and affordable healthcare to the people.
      Under this Act, Hospital Governing Board shall be composed of the
following;
      (a). The Hospital Chief, as Chairperson;
      (b). A representative from the Department of Health;
      (c). A representative from the local government unit, where applicable;
      (d). A representative from PhilHealth;
      (e). One healthcare professional representative;
      (f). One private sector or healthcare management expert;
      (g). One patient or civil society representative; and
      (h). One academic or research representative.
      The Governing Board shall exercise policy direction and institutional
oversight over hospital operations, including approval of strategic development
plans, annual budgets, investment programs, infrastructure modernization projects,
performance targets, healthcare quality standards, financial management systems,
workforce development programs, research initiatives, and institutional reforms.
      The Board shall likewise ensure compliance with applicable laws, ethical
standards, anti-corruption safeguards, transparency measures, and public
accountability mechanisms. It shall establish systems for risk management, internal
audit, citizen engagement, patient grievance response, and institutional performance
monitoring.


      SEC 10. Performance Accountability Framework.                All autonomous
hospitals shall be subject to annual performance evaluation by the Department of
Health using measurable indicators including:
      (a). Patient outcomes and quality metrics;
      (b). Financial efficiency;
      (c). Hospital utilization rates;
      (d). Procurement efficiency;
      (e). Patient satisfaction;
      (f). Digitalization progress;
      (g). Healthcare worker retention;
      (h). Emergency responsiveness; and
      (i). Compliance with anti-corruption and transparency standards.
      The Department of Health shall conduct regular monitoring, inspections,
technical evaluations, and institutional audits to assess continued compliance with
performance standards and determine whether hospitals remain qualified to retain
autonomous status. Failure to comply with prescribed standards, serious
deterioration in healthcare performance, misuse or diversion of funds, persistent
adverse audit findings, gross negligence, corruption, or acts detrimental to patient
welfare may result in the suspension, downgrading, or revocation of autonomy status
after due process and administrative proceedings consistent with existing laws and
regulations.


      SEC 11. Transparency and Anti-Corruption Safeguards.             Autonomous
public hospitals shall establish and maintain comprehensive transparency, integrity,
and accountability systems to ensure the lawful, efficient, and ethical use of public
resources. All hospitals granted autonomy under this Act shall comply with existing
auditing, procurement, anti-graft, civil service, and public financial management
laws and shall adopt institutional safeguards against corruption, fraud, wastage,
overpricing, conflict of interest, and misuse of funds. They shall maintain publicly
accessible transparency mechanisms containing updated information on budgets,
expenditures, procurement activities, contracts, audit findings, hospital performance
indicators, and other matters of public interest consistent with applicable laws on
access to information and data privacy.
       Each autonomous hospital shall establish internal audit and compliance units,
risk management systems, digital financial controls, procurement monitoring
mechanisms, and citizen feedback and grievance redress systems to strengthen
institutional accountability and public trust. Whistleblowers, healthcare workers,
patients, and other individuals who report corruption or irregularities in good faith
shall be afforded protection in accordance with existing laws and regulations. Any
public official, employee, contractor, or private individual found liable for
corruption, falsification, procurement anomalies, embezzlement, or misuse of
hospital funds shall be subject to the appropriate administrative, civil, and criminal
liabilities under existing laws.


       SEC 12. Protection of Universal Access to Healthcare.            The grant of
autonomy under this Act shall not diminish the constitutional duty of the State to
ensure accessible, affordable, equitable, and quality healthcare services for all
Filipinos, particularly indigent, marginalized, and vulnerable populations.
Autonomous public hospitals shall continue to provide emergency medical treatment

maintain charity care programs, subsidized services, and socialized pricing
mechanisms consistent with the Universal Health Care Act and other existing health

      SEC 13. Healthcare Workforce Development, Protection, and Incentives.
   Autonomous public hospitals shall adopt comprehensive workforce development
and protection programs designed to strengthen institutional capacity, improve
healthcare delivery, and promote the welfare, retention, and professional growth of
healthcare workers. Subject to existing civil service, compensation, labor, and
budgeting laws, hospitals may implement merit-based incentives, continuing
professional development programs, specialized medical training, scholarship and
research grant initiatives, leadership development systems, and employee wellness
and mental health support programs to enhance workforce competence and
sustainability.
      Hospitals shall ensure safe working conditions, adequate staffing standards,
occupational health protections, and fair and ethical employment practices consistent
with existing labor and healthcare laws. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
diminish existing salaries, benefits, hazard pay, allowances, security of tenure,
collective rights, and other statutory entitlements of healthcare workers under
current laws, regulations, and collective agreements. The Department of Health, in
coordination with the Civil Service Commission, Department of Labor and
Employment, and other relevant agencies, shall ensure that workforce reforms
implemented under this Act remain aligned with principles of decent work, public
service professionalism, and healthcare service continuity.


      SEC 14. Digital Health Systems and Hospital Modernization.
Autonomous public hospitals shall prioritize the modernization of healthcare
infrastructure, medical technologies, and digital health systems to improve
healthcare efficiency, patient safety, institutional resilience, and continuity of public
health services. Hospitals shall progressively establish interoperable electronic
medical records systems, integrated hospital information management systems,
telemedicine platforms, digital procurement and inventory systems, data analytics
capabilities, and cybersecurity protection measures consistent with the Data Privacy
Act and other applicable laws and regulations.
      The Department of Health (DOH) shall formulate national interoperability
and digital health standards to ensure compatibility, data security, patient
confidentiality, and integration with broader national healthcare information
systems. Autonomous hospitals may invest in modern diagnostic technologies,
disaster-resilient and climate-resilient infrastructure, renewable energy systems,
emergency preparedness technologies, and evidence-based healthcare innovations,
provided that all modernization initiatives remain consistent with public
accountability standards, ethical healthcare principles, and equitable access to
healthcare services.


      SEC 15. Public-Private Partnerships, Academic Collaboration, and
Research Development          Autonomous public hospitals may enter into public-
private partnerships, academic affiliations, technical cooperation agreements, and
research collaborations with local or international institutions for purposes related to
healthcare modernization, medical education, infrastructure development, scientific
research, healthcare innovation, and institutional capacity-building. All such
agreements shall be governed by principles of transparency, competitiveness,
accountability, public interest, and value for money, and shall remain subject to
applicable procurement laws, auditing regulations, and government oversight
mechanisms.
      No partnership or contractual arrangement entered into pursuant to this Act
shall result in the privatization of public hospitals, transfer of ownership of
government healthcare facilities, reduction of government control over essential
healthcare services, or impairment of public access to affordable healthcare.
Research activities involving patients, clinical trials, medical data, or emerging
healthcare technologies shall comply with applicable ethical standards, data privacy
laws, bioethics regulations, and institutional review processes.
      The Department of Health (DOH) shall establish guidelines to ensure that all
partnerships and collaborative arrangements remain aligned with national healthcare
priorities and the public service mandate of government hospitals.


      SEC 16. Powers and Responsibilities of the Department of Health (DOH).
    The Department of Health (DOH) shall serve as the principal oversight,
regulatory, and policy-making authority responsible for the implementation and
supervision of this Act. In the exercise of its mandate, the DOH shall ensure that the
grant and exercise of hospital autonomy remain consistent with national healthcare
objectives, universal healthcare principles, public accountability standards, and the
constitutional duty of the State to provide accessible and affordable healthcare
services to all Filipinos.


      For this purpose, the Department of Health shall:
      (a). Formulate, promulgate, and periodically update the implementing rules,
      operational guidelines, governance frameworks, and institutional safeguards
      necessary for the effective implementation of this Act;
      (b). Establish and administer the qualification standards, evaluation criteria,
      performance indicators, and monitoring systems for autonomous public
      hospitals;
(c). Conduct regular monitoring, inspections, technical assessments,
institutional audits, and performance evaluations of autonomous hospitals to
ensure continued compliance with applicable laws, healthcare standards, and
accountability mechanisms;
(d). Recommend corrective measures, technical interventions, suspension of
privileges, downgrade of autonomy status, or revocation of autonomous status
in cases of serious noncompliance, gross mismanagement, corruption,
persistent adverse audit findings, or substantial deterioration in healthcare
performance, subject to due process;
(e). Provide technical assistance, institutional capacity-building, digital
transformation support, healthcare planning assistance, and governance
advisory services to hospitals seeking or maintaining autonomous status;
(f). Develop national standards and policies on hospital modernization, digital
health systems, interoperability, healthcare quality assurance, patient safety,
workforce development, and institutional governance consistent with existing
laws and international best practices;
(g). Ensure harmonization of this Act with the Universal Health Care Act,
public health emergency frameworks, healthcare financing reforms, and other
national health policies and programs;
(h). Coordinate with the Department of Budget and Management,
Commission on Audit, Civil Service Commission, PhilHealth, local
government units, and other relevant agencies for the effective
implementation of this Act;
(i). Maintain a centralized database and public reporting system on the
performance, financial status, healthcare outcomes, and institutional
compliance of autonomous public hospitals; and
      (j). Submit an annual report to Congress and the Office of the President
      containing the implementation status of this Act, healthcare performance
      outcomes, fiscal impacts, institutional assessments, and recommendations for
      legislative or policy reforms.


      SEC 17. Performance-Based Incentives, Institutional Support and
Expanded Authorities for High-Performing Autonomous Hospitals.                     To
promote excellence, innovation, accountability, and continuous improvement in
public healthcare delivery, the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
the Department of Budget and Management, PhilHealth, and other relevant
government agencies, shall establish a performance-based incentive and institutional
support framework for autonomous public hospitals that consistently demonstrate
exceptional performance in healthcare delivery and institutional governance.
      Hospitals recognized under this Section may be classified as centers of
excellence, specialty referral institutions, research and training hospitals, or pilot
implementation sites for healthcare modernization initiatives. Subject to existing
budgeting, auditing, procurement, civil service, and compensation laws, high-
performing autonomous hospitals may likewise be granted priority access to
government support mechanisms and expanded institutional authorities, including
but not limited to:
      (a). National government modernization and infrastructure funds;
      (b). Medical equipment upgrading and digital health assistance programs;
      (c). Research and development grants and innovation support;
      (d). Disaster resilience and emergency preparedness financing;
      (e). Authority to establish specialized medical centers and advanced
      healthcare programs;
      (f). Expanded workforce development, scholarship, and retention initiatives;
      and
      (g). Participation in national and international healthcare research, training,
      and modernization collaborations
      Provided, however, that all incentives, privileges, and expanded authorities
granted under this Section shall remain subject to government oversight, public
accountability standards, and the constitutional mandate that public healthcare
services remain accessible, affordable, equitable, and primarily public-oriented.


      SEC 18. Congressional Oversight.            A Joint Congressional Oversight
Committee is hereby created to monitor the implementation of this Act, assess
healthcare outcomes, review autonomy performance metrics, and recommend
legislative improvements. The Committee shall submit an annual report to Congress.


      SEC 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).          Within one hundred
twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH),
in coordination with the DBM, COA, CSC, PhilHealth, and other relevant agencies,
shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.


      SEC 20. Appropriations.             The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
Department of Health (DOH). Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).
      SEC 21. Separability Clause.            If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


      SEC 22. Repealing Clause.           All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.


      SEC 23. Effectivity.         This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
