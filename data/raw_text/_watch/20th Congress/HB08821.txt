                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Philippines is confronting an energy shock with immediate consequences
for transport costs, food distribution, business operations, and household welfare.
Executive Order No. 110, issued on 24 March 2026, already declared a state of
national energy emergency and activated the Unified Package for Livelihoods,
Industry, Food, and Transport as the government’s coordinated response framework.
This bill does not duplicate that executive action. It provides what only legislation can
firmly establish: a standing statutory framework for automatic relief, market
stabilization, logistics continuity, labor protection, and targeted public support during
any declared national energy emergency.

        The urgency of this measure is confirmed by official energy data. The
Department of Energy launched a Strategic Fuel Program targeting up to two million
barrels of additional supply to support domestic requirements and mitigate the impact
of fuel supply disruptions and market volatility.
       The DOE also reported on 31 March 2026 that 1.042 million barrels of diesel,
equivalent to 165,678,000 liters, had already been secured for delivery through April
2026. These are concrete stabilization measures taken in response to a real and
continuing threat to domestic fuel security.

        The inflation risk is equally clear. In its February 2026 Monetary Policy Report,
the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas stated that crude oil prices were revised upward
because of geopolitical tensions and that if Dubai crude averages US$80 per barrel in
2026 and 2027, headline inflation would breach the upper end of the target range. The
same BSP report shows headline inflation at 4.1 percent in both 2026 and 2027 under
that scenario, and states that these estimates exclude second-round effects. In plain
terms, a fuel shock does not stop at the pump. It spills into fares, food, electricity,
logistics, and the cost of keeping businesses open.

       Existing law already allows the President to respond to critically low energy
supply. Republic Act No. 7638 authorizes the declaration of a critically low energy
supply or imminent danger thereof, while Republic Act No. 11285 establishes the
State’s framework on energy efficiency and conservation. What is still missing is a
permanent law that automatically connects an energy-emergency declaration to
enforceable relief for households, workers, tenants, utility consumers, transport
operators, farmers, fisherfolk, and vulnerable enterprises, together with statutory tools
for anti-hoarding enforcement, logistics continuity, and accountable use of public
funds.

       Congress has adopted this kind of emergency architecture before. Republic Act
No. 11469 required covered institutions to implement grace periods for loans during
the pandemic emergency, while Republic Act No. 11494 later provided a one-time 60-
day grace period for loans and minimum grace periods for residential and commercial
rents and utility payments. The lesson is straightforward: when the nation faces a
large-scale external shock, the law must be ready to shield ordinary Filipinos from
sudden economic injury.

       This bill answers that need. It institutionalizes emergency cash assistance for
affected households, support for displaced and terminated workers, wage subsidy and
payroll preservation, grace periods for covered loans, rents, and utility payments, a
temporary moratorium on certain foreclosure and repossession actions, targeted
support for transport, agriculture, fisheries, MSMEs, mining, and construction, anti-
hoarding and anti-profiteering safeguards, anti-inflation and logistics continuity
measures, local contingency planning, and an Energy Emergency Relief Fund, all
subject to reporting, oversight, and audit.
      Ito ay isang praktikal na panukalang batas para sa mahihirap na panahon.
Layunin nitong mapanatiling tuloy-tuloy ang daloy ng fuel, ang pagdating ng pagkain,
ang trabaho ng mga manggagawa, ang operasyon ng mga negosyo, at ang
proteksiyon ng mga pamilya kapag ang volatility sa global energy markets ay direktang
nararamdaman na sa tahanan ng bawat Pilipino. Sa panahon ng energy crisis, hindi
sapat na ang batas ay basta na lamang tumugon. Dapat itong tumindig kasama ng
taumbayan, panatilihing matatag ang mahahalagang serbisyo, at tumulong sa
pagbuhat ng bigat na pasan ngayon ng napakaraming pamilyang Pilipino.

      In view of the foregoing, the swift passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

 1           Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect the
 2   Filipino people from the severe social, economic, inflationary, employment, transport,
 3   and food-security effects of extraordinary increases in oil and fuel prices, major
 4   petroleum supply disruptions, loss, insufficiency, or severe disruption of electrical
 5   power, logistics bottlenecks, fuel scarcity risks, transport paralysis, industrial
 6   disruption, work stoppages, and related shocks arising from instability in global energy
 7   markets, prolonged international armed conflict, grid unreliability, generation
 8   deficiency, transmission or distribution failure, or other events that threaten national
 9   energy security.
10
11           The State shall prioritize: (a) the continuity of public transportation, logistics,
12   agriculture, fisheries, mining, construction, power generation, power transmission,
13   power distribution, water supply, healthcare, education, telecommunications, fuel
14   distribution, ports, airports, and other essential sectors; (b) the uninterrupted
15   movement of food, medicine, fuel, construction materials, mineral outputs, fertilizers,
16   feeds, and other essential goods; (c) the protection of jobs, income, housing security,
17   and access to credit; (d) the prevention of hoarding, profiteering, cartelization, artificial
18   shortages, and abusive market practices; (e) the stabilization of prices of fuel,
19   electricity, and other essential goods and services materially affected by the declared
20   national energy emergency; and (f) the adoption of temporary, targeted, transparent,
21   accountable, and time-bound emergency interventions.
22

27   law, including emergencies arising from critically low fuel supply, critically low
28   electricity supply, or imminent danger thereof.

 7           Sec. 6. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:
 8           (a) “Declared national energy emergency” refers to a declaration made by the
 9   President under existing law, including Section 25 of Republic Act No. 7638, upon the
10   determination and recommendation of the Secretary of Energy, that a critically low
11   energy supply, including fuel supply, electricity supply, or imminent danger thereof,
12   exists and requires temporary emergency measures to protect public welfare and
13   maintain essential services;
14           (b) “Qualified affected household” refers to a low-income or otherwise
15   vulnerable household suffering substantial income loss, severe transport burden, or
16   serious economic dislocation directly attributable to the declared national energy
17   emergency;
18           (c) “Qualified displaced worker” refers to any worker who suffers involuntary
19   separation, closure-related termination, temporary layoff, forced reduction of
20   workdays, no-work-no-pay status, suspension of operations, severe diminution of
21   earnings, or similar livelihood loss directly attributable to the declared national energy
22   emergency;
23           (d) “Covered loans” shall include salary, personal, housing, motor vehicle,
24   agricultural, fisheries, business, credit card, and similar loans, whether granted by
25   banks or by other regulated or supervised lending institutions, as may be further
26   defined in the implementing rules;
27           (e) “Qualified commercial rent” refers to rent due on commercial premises
28   occupied by MSMEs or enterprises engaged in essential services materially affected
29   by the declared national energy emergency;
30           (f) “Essential sectors” include public transport, trucking, shipping, logistics, food
31   production and distribution, agriculture, fisheries, health services, education, power
32   generation, power transmission, power distribution, water supply systems, mining,
33   construction, manufacturing of essential goods, fuel distribution, ports, airports,
34   telecommunications, sanitation, waste management, hospitals, cold-chain facilities,
35   warehousing, and other electricity-dependent critical infrastructure, as well as such
36   other sectors as may be identified in the implementing rules.

38   the Secretary of Energy published on the official website of the Department of Energy
39   and promptly furnished to Congress.
40            (c) No sector or enterprise shall be denied access to fuel entirely; allocation
41   shall be proportionate and non-discriminatory within each priority tier;
