                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

Schools are meant to be sanctuaries of learning where students, teachers, and school
personnel can thrive without fear of violence or disruption. However, educational
institutions remain vulnerable to various threats that may compromise safety, disrupt
learning, and undermine the right of every learner to a secure educational environment.

This bill seeks to strengthen and institutionalize school security nationwide by mandating
the installation of Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) cameras and walk-through metal
detectors in all schools. CCTV cameras will enhance monitoring, incident documentation,
and crime prevention, while metal detectors will serve as a preventive measure against
the entry of firearms, bladed weapons, and other dangerous objects into school premises.

The measure also requires coordination among the Department of Education (DepEd),
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), Philippine National Police
(PNP), and Local Government Units (LGUs) to ensure the proper deployment,
maintenance, and operation of these security measures. Schools may likewise be
prioritized based on risk assessment and other relevant security considerations.

By institutionalizing these measures, the State affirms its responsibility to protect the lives
and welfare of students, teachers, and school personnel. Ensuring a safe and secure
school environment is essential to sustaining quality education and allowing learners to
study, grow, and develop without fear.

This legislation is a proactive and long-term measure to strengthen school safety and
prevent violence and other security threats within educational institutions.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     AN ACT INCREASING CAMPUS SECURITY AND CRIME AWARENESS BY
   INSTALLING CLOSED-CIRCUIT TELEVISION (CCTV) CAMERAS AND WALK-
   THROUGH METAL DETECTORS IN ALL SCHOOLS AND PROVIDING FUNDS
                             THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Campus Security Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It shall be the policy of the State to provide a safe and
healthy environment for students by requiring educational institutions to disclose crime
statistics that occur within the campus, its security policies and procedures, and to adopt
modern technology in apprehending and preventing crimes to enhance security and
maintain peace and order in every school all over the country.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act: a) Campus refers to any establishment
or property, grounds and buildings of an institution for educational purposes. b) Closed-
Circuit Television (CCTV) refers to any device or system that could be used to capture
and record photo or still and moving or video images. c) Walk-Through Metal Detector
refers to a security device designed to detect metallic weapons or dangerous objects
carried by individuals entering school premises.

SEC. 4. Installation of Closed-Circuit Television Cameras. — It is hereby mandated that
every school nationwide shall install Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) cameras in
strategic areas in their premises so as to capture photos and/or videos of possible crimes
or untoward incidents that may occur.
SEC. 5. Installation of Walk-Through Metal Detectors. — All public schools nationwide
shall install walk-through metal detectors at main entrances and other strategic access
points, subject to guidelines issued by the Department of Education (DepEd) and the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG).

The purpose of these metal detectors is to: a) Prevent the entry of firearms, bladed
weapons, and other dangerous items into school premises; b) Strengthen deterrence
against violent incidents; and c) Provide an added layer of protection for students,
teachers, and staff.

The DepEd, in coordination with the Philippine National Police (PNP) and Local
Government Units (LGUs), shall determine priority schools for immediate installation
based on risk assessment, population size, and security needs.

SEC. 6. Disclosure of Campus Security Policy and Campus Crime Statistics. — Upon
effectivity of this Act, schools must annually collect and disseminate campus crime
statistics and security policies to current students, employees, and prospective enrollees
upon request. The annual security report should include information on current campus
procedures and facilities for students, reports of on-campus criminal actions or
emergencies, and the institution's response policies to such reports.

SEC. 7. Appropriation. — The amount necessary for the effective implementation of this
Act shall be included in the current annual budget of the Department of Education
(DepEd) and the Commission on Higher Education (CHED). Thereafter, such sum
needed for the maintenance of the CCTV devices and metal detectors shall be included
in the annual General Appropriations Act.

For private schools and facilities, funding shall be included in their respective annual
corporate budget.

SEC. 8. Data Privacy. — The covered establishments shall ensure protection of data used
and processed in the implementation of this Act. Persons handling sensitive personal
information shall be educated on all relevant laws concerning data privacy and protection.
For this purpose, the provisions of the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173)
shall have suppletory effect.

SEC. 9. Implementation. — The Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on
Higher Education (CHED), and the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the proper implementation of the
provisions of this Act.

SEC. 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision or portion of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not thereby affected shall
remain in full force and effect.
SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, ordinances, rules,
regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof, which are inconsistent with any
provision of this Act, are hereby repealed, amended, and/or modified accordingly.

SEC. 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
