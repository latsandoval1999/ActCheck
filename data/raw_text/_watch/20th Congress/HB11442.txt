                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Constitution recognizes the vital role of our youth in nation-building and directs the
State to promote and protect their physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being. It
likewise mandates the right of children to assistance and to special protection from all forms of
neglect, abuse, cruelty, exploitation, and other conditions prejudicial to their development.

        As a result, the Republic has enacted an extensive body of laws protecting children and
learners from violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation, trafficking, bullying, discrimination, child
marriage, online sexual abuse or exploitation, child sexual abuse or exploitation materials, and
other threats to their safety and development. These include Republic Act No. 7610, as amended;
Republic Act No. 9344, as amended by Republic Act No. 10630; Republic Act Nos. 10173, 11476,
11510, 11596, 11650, 11930, 12080, and 12199; and other child-protection, education, mental-
health, disability, and data-privacy laws.

        These laws provide important substantive protections. Yet a legal right cannot fully protect
a child who does not know, in an age-appropriate manner, that such right exists, how to recognize
a situation requiring protection, whom to approach, and how to obtain lawful assistance. Child
protection must therefore include not only response and enforcement, but also prevention through
practical, inclusive, and developmentally appropriate education.

        Children need not be of legal age to understand their rights, recognize danger, seek
assistance, respect the rights of others, and appreciate the responsibilities of citizenship. Legal
literacy enables children and young people to understand the protections available to them,
recognize situations requiring assistance, use lawful reporting and referral mechanisms, and
appreciate the responsibilities that accompany the exercise of rights.

       This measure draws, among other sources, upon the policy proposal advanced by Atty.
Robie P. Quiño in the legal research paper entitled “Not of Legal Age, but of Legal Mind.” It is
likewise anchored on Articles 29 and 42 of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the

                                                                                                    1
Child, which relate education to respect for human rights and require the principles and provisions
of the Convention to be made widely known to adults and children alike.

        Republic Act No. 11476 already institutionalizes Good Manners and Right Conduct
(GMRC) as a separate subject from Grades 1 to 6, integrates GMRC into kindergarten learning
activities, and establishes Values Education for the higher grade levels. Its full promise, however,
depends upon faithful classroom implementation, trained teachers, adequate and relevant learning
resources, meaningful character-building activities, school-family-community partnership, and
continuing review of actual learner outcomes.

       This measure therefore strengthens, rather than duplicates or displaces, the GMRC and
Values Education framework. It integrates age-appropriate child-rights and rule-of-law
competencies with the formation of human dignity, respect, honesty, self-discipline, responsibility,
peaceful conflict resolution, obedience to law, and concern for others, while preserving the
separate-subject requirements and time allotments mandated under Republic Act No. 11476.

        The proposed National Child Rights and Rule-of-Law Education Program does not create
an additional stand-alone academic subject where existing curricula and learning areas can
adequately deliver the required competencies. Instead, it establishes a national framework through
which existing education, child-protection, GMRC, values-education, mental-health, digital-
safety, juvenile-justice, alternative-learning, technical-vocational, higher-education, and youth-
development programs may be mapped, harmonized, strengthened, and connected. This
integration shall not diminish the teaching of GMRC and Values Education as separate subjects
where required by Republic Act No. 11476.

        The Program is age-progressive. For young children, it emphasizes dignity, personal safety,
respectful relationships, personal boundaries, trusted adults, safe and unsafe situations, and help-
seeking. For older learners, it progressively introduces children’s rights and responsibilities, digital
citizenship, privacy, crime prevention, due process, restorative justice, civic participation, the
functions of public institutions, and lawful mechanisms for obtaining protection and assistance.

         The measure also protects learners from retraumatization, compelled disclosure, stigma,
retaliation, and misuse of personal information. Teachers and other education personnel are trained
to recognize concerns, secure immediate safety, comply with applicable reporting duties, and make
appropriate referrals, but they are not converted into investigators, forensic interviewers, social
workers, counselors, prosecutors, or lawyers. Nothing in the measure creates a new criminal
offense, increases criminal liability, or lowers the minimum age of criminal responsibility.

       The bill further provides measurable implementation standards, accessible and legally
accurate learning resources, local referral directories, inter-agency coordination within existing
mandates, phased implementation, administrative accountability through existing lawful
mechanisms, monitoring, periodic review, and reporting to Congress.

  INSTITUTIONALIZING A NATIONAL CHILD RIGHTS AND RULE OF LAW
EDUCATION PROGRAM, STRENGTHENING THE IMPLEMENTATION OF GOOD
MANNERS AND RIGHT CONDUCT AND VALUES EDUCATION UNDER REPUBLIC
  ACT NO. 11476, PROVIDING FOR THEIR HARMONIZED INTEGRATION IN
      EDUCATIONAL AND YOUTH DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMS, AND

                                        CHAPTER I
                                    GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title.
       This Act shall be known as the “Child Rights and Rule of Law Education Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.
        It is the policy of the State to protect and promote the rights, safety, dignity, participation,
and holistic development of every child; to make the principles and provisions of the United
Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child widely known to children and adults; and to foster
a culture of lawfulness founded on human rights, justice, accountability, equality, and respect for
the rule of law.

        Toward these ends, the State shall provide learners with age-appropriate, practical,
inclusive, evidence-informed, and accessible education that enables them to understand their rights
and corresponding responsibilities, recognize and avoid harm, seek timely assistance through safe
and lawful channels, respect the rights and dignity of others, understand the basic functions of



                                                                                                      4
public institutions and justice processes, resolve conflicts peacefully and lawfully, and participate
responsibly in family, school, workplace, digital, civic, and community life.

        The State shall implement this policy consistently with the best interests of the child,
academic freedom, lawful school autonomy, the rights and responsibilities of parents and
guardians, cultural and religious diversity, gender equality, disability inclusion, child participation,
due process, data privacy, and existing child-protection and safeguarding laws. Nothing in this Act
shall diminish any protection, right, duty, remedy, or service available under existing law.

SEC. 3. Objectives.
       a. Institutionalize a coherent and age-progressive program on children’s rights, child
       protection, crime prevention, criminal justice, and the rule of law;
       b. Equip learners with practical competencies to recognize violence, abuse, neglect,
       exploitation, bullying, trafficking, child marriage, online harm, and other threats and to
       seek assistance through safe and lawful channels;
       c. Develop respect for human rights, due process, restorative justice, accountability,
       peaceful conflict resolution, and responsible citizenship;
       d. Strengthen the preventive and educational dimensions of the country’s child-protection
       and juvenile-justice systems without stigmatizing child victims or witnesses, children at
       risk, or children in conflict with the law;
       e. Strengthen the faithful and measurable implementation of GMRC and Values Education
       under Republic Act No. 11476, including teacher preparation, learning resources,
       character-building activities, school-family-community partnership, supervision, and
       accountability;
       f. Integrate relevant competencies into appropriate existing subjects, learning areas,
       programs, and activities without unnecessary curricular duplication or overload;
       g. Train educators and service providers to deliver accurate content and respond safely and
       lawfully to child-protection concerns and disclosures;
       h. Ensure that instruction and learning resources are developmentally appropriate,
       culturally and linguistically responsive, gender-responsive, trauma-informed, disability-
       inclusive, and available in accessible formats;
       i. Strengthen coordination among education, child-protection, social-welfare, health,
       justice, digital-safety, youth, and local government institutions;
       j. Establish measurable standards for implementation, accessibility, personnel capacity,
       referral functionality, learner competency, and institutional compliance; and
       k. Promote evidence-based continuous improvement through monitoring, evaluation,
       consultation, and periodic review.



                                                                                                      5
SEC. 4. Definition of Terms.
       a. “Age-appropriate” or “developmentally appropriate” means content, methods,
       language, activities, and assessment suited to the learner’s age, evolving capacities,
       developmental stage, circumstances, and individual needs;
       b. “Child” means a person below eighteen (18) years of age, or a person eighteen (18) years
       of age or older who, because of a physical or mental disability or condition, is unable to
       fully take care of or protect oneself from abuse, neglect, cruelty, exploitation, or
       discrimination, consistent with applicable law;
       c. “Child rights and rule-of-law education” means structured learning that develops
       practical knowledge, values, attitudes, and competencies concerning children’s rights and
       responsibilities, child protection, crime prevention, justice institutions and processes, due
       process, restorative justice, civic participation, and lawful help-seeking;
       d. “Child safeguarding” means policies, procedures, and practices intended to prevent and
       respond to abuse, neglect, exploitation, discrimination, bullying, retaliation, and other harm
       involving a child in an education or youth-program setting;
       e. “Covered institution or program” means an educational institution, training institution,
       early-childhood program, alternative learning program, or government-supported youth
       education or training program covered by this Act;
       f. “Learner” means any person participating in early childhood, basic, alternative,
       technical-vocational, higher education, or government-supported out-of-school youth
       education or training covered by this Act;
       g. “Referral mechanism” means an established process through which a learner or another
       person may be connected to an appropriate child-protection, social-welfare, health, mental-
       health, legal, law-enforcement, education, or other competent service;
       h. “Rule of law” means the principle that all persons and institutions, public and private,
       including the State itself, are accountable to laws that are publicly promulgated, equally
       enforced, independently adjudicated, and consistent with human rights
       i. “Good Manners and Right Conduct” or “GMRC” means the universally accepted basic
       social values, etiquette, and proper modes of behavior that convey respect for oneself and
       others, as defined and institutionalized under Republic Act No. 11476; and
       j. “Trauma-informed” means an approach that recognizes the possible effects of trauma,
       seeks to avoid retraumatization, promotes physical and emotional safety, and connects the
       learner to appropriate professional support.

                               CHAPTER II
            CHILD RIGHTS AND RULE-OF-LAW EDUCATION PROGRAM

SEC. 5. Establishment of the Program.
       There is hereby established a National Child Rights and Rule-of-Law Education
Program, hereinafter referred to as the “Program,” which shall be implemented through existing
early-childhood, basic, alternative, technical-vocational, higher-education, and government-
supported out-of-school youth education and training systems in accordance with this Act.

        The Department of Education (DepEd) shall serve as the lead agency for the Program in
basic education and the Alternative Learning System (ALS), without prejudice to the statutory
mandates of the Early Childhood Care and Development (ECCD) Council, Commission on Higher
Education (CHED), Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA),
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Department of Health (DOH), Council
for the Welfare of Children (CWC), Juvenile Justice and Welfare Council (JJWC), Department of
Justice (DOJ), Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT), National Youth Commission (NYC), local
government units (LGUs), and other concerned agencies.

        It shall convene an interagency technical working group composed of the agencies
identified in Section 19 of this Act for curriculum mapping, standards development,
implementation coordination, and monitoring. The technical working group shall operate within
existing structures and shall not constitute a new permanent council or bureaucracy.

       The Program shall not create a new permanent council, commission, bureau, or office.
Coordination shall be undertaken through existing inter-agency mechanisms and, where necessary,
a technical working group convened by DepEd for matters within its mandate.

SEC. 6. National Program Framework.
        Within the period prescribed under Section 24 of this Act, the responsible agencies shall
jointly establish a National Child Rights and Rule-of-Law Education Framework containing the
following:

       a. National learning objectives and minimum competencies;
       b. Age- and development-level progression standards;
       c. Minimum safeguarding and referral standards;
       d. Minimum accessibility and inclusion standards;
       e. Personnel competency and training standards;
       f. Standards for learning-resource development, legal-accuracy review, and updating;
       g. Referral and local-service directory requirements;

                                                                                               7
       h. Monitoring and evaluation indicators;
       i. Data-protection and confidentiality safeguards; and
       j. Procedures for periodic review and updating.
        The Framework shall serve as the common reference for integrating the Program into
existing education and training systems while allowing each responsible agency to issue
appropriate standards within its lawful mandate.

SEC. 7. Core Learning Content and Competencies.
      The Program shall develop age-appropriate knowledge, values, attitudes, and practical
competencies on the following:

       a. The inherent dignity of every person; children’s rights and corresponding
       responsibilities; equality and non-discrimination; and respect for the rights of others;
       b. Rights and protections under the Constitution, the United Nations Convention on the
       Rights of the Child, and relevant domestic laws, presented in plain and understandable
       language;
       c. Recognition and prevention of violence, abuse, neglect, cruelty, bullying, discrimination,
       trafficking, child marriage, economic and sexual exploitation, online sexual abuse or
       exploitation of children (OSAEC), child sexual abuse or exploitation materials (CSAEM),
       grooming, and other forms of harm;
       d. Personal safety, bodily integrity, personal boundaries, an age-appropriate understanding
       of consent, safe and unsafe situations, trusted adults, bystander responsibility, and lawful
       help-seeking;
       e. Digital citizenship, privacy, media and information literacy, responsible internet use,
       cybersecurity awareness, online fraud and manipulation, and safe reporting through official
       protocols without opening, downloading, copying, screenshotting, or redistributing
       unlawful or harmful material;
       f. Basic crime prevention, personal and community safety, lawful behavior, responsible
       decision-making, peaceful conflict resolution, and the consequences of unlawful conduct;
       g. The functions of barangay institutions, schools, social-welfare offices, health and
       mental-health services, law-enforcement agencies, prosecutors, courts, child-protection
       bodies, and other referral institutions;
       h. The rights and appropriate treatment of child victims and witnesses, children at risk, and
       children in conflict with the law, including diversion, due process, restorative justice,
       rehabilitation, reintegration, and freedom from stigma;
       i. Mental-health awareness, emotional safety, crisis help-seeking, and available referral
       services, consistent with Republic Act No. 12080 and other applicable laws;



                                                                                                  8
      j. Citizenship, accountability, integrity, anti-corruption, civic participation, respect for
      diversity, and peaceful and lawful resolution of disputes; and
      k. Other emerging child-protection, public-safety, and rule-of-law concerns as may be
      determined through evidence-based review and consultation.

SEC. 8. Curriculum and Instructional Standards.
      The Program shall be implemented in accordance with the following standards:

      a. Content shall follow a spiral progression that begins with personal safety, dignity, trusted
      adults, and help-seeking and advances toward rights, responsibilities, justice processes,
      civic participation, and the rule of law;
      b. Instruction shall be learner-centered, evidence-informed, inclusive, developmentally
      appropriate, culturally and linguistically responsive, gender-responsive, disability-
      inclusive, and trauma-informed;
      c. Content shall, whenever practicable, be integrated into appropriate existing subjects,
      learning areas, guidance and counseling activities, co-curricular programs, community
      learning, and digital-citizenship initiatives. Nothing in this Act shall require the creation of
      an additional separate subject for the Program where integration will achieve the intended
      competencies: Provided, That this shall not diminish the separate teaching of GMRC in
      Grades 1 to 6, Values Education in Grades 7 to 10, or other requirements established under
      Republic Act No. 11476;
      d. Responsible agencies shall conduct curriculum mapping to avoid duplication and
      unnecessary overload and to identify where the competencies under this Act will reinforce
      GMRC, Values Education, Araling Panlipunan, Health, or successor learning areas and
      existing child-protection and digital-safety programs, without reducing statutory subject
      requirements or prescribed instructional time;
      e. Teaching strategies may include stories, play, role-playing, case simulations, guided
      discussion, community engagement, and other participatory methods, provided that no
      activity exposes a learner to unnecessary risk or compels disclosure of a personal or
      traumatic experience;
      f. Materials shall distinguish legal information from individualized legal advice and shall
      direct learners to qualified professionals and proper agencies when case-specific assistance
      is required; and
      g. Assessment shall focus on practical understanding, safe decision-making, help-seeking,
      respect for rights, and application of competencies and shall not penalize a learner for
      declining to disclose personal or traumatic information.

SEC. 9. Integration in Basic Education.
       DepEd shall integrate the Program into the K to 12 Basic Education Curriculum in all
public and private basic education institutions, consistent with Republic Act No. 10533, as
amended, Republic Act No. 11476, and other applicable laws. DepEd shall prescribe grade-level
standards and learning competencies, identify appropriate learning areas and activities, develop or
approve learning resources, and issue differentiated and proportionate implementation guidance
for public and private schools.

        For kindergarten and the early elementary grades, instruction shall emphasize dignity,
personal safety, respect, trusted adults, safe and unsafe situations, personal boundaries, help-
seeking, and peaceful conflict resolution through play-based, story-based, and concrete learning
activities. Technical legal terminology and detailed descriptions of violence or criminal conduct
shall not be used where developmentally inappropriate.

        For learners in the later elementary and secondary grades, instruction may progressively
include legal rights and responsibilities, digital safety, privacy, crime prevention, due process,
restorative justice, justice institutions, civic participation, and lawful reporting and referral.

SEC. 10. Strengthening the Implementation of Good Manners and Right Conduct and Values
Education.
        Nothing in this Act shall diminish, replace, or reduce the implementation of Republic Act
No. 11476. GMRC shall remain a separate subject from Grades 1 to 6 with the time allotment
prescribed for core subjects and shall remain integrated in daily learning activities in kindergarten.
Values Education shall remain a separate subject from Grades 7 to 10, with GMRC integrated
therein, and shall remain integrated into appropriate subjects in Grades 11 and 12, in accordance
with Republic Act No. 11476.

        DepEd shall harmonize the Program with GMRC and Values Education through
curriculum mapping and grade-level competency standards. In the elementary grades, GMRC shall
serve as one of the principal learning areas for appropriate competencies under this Act concerning
human dignity, respect for oneself and others, honesty, self-discipline, responsibility, concern for
others, peaceful conflict resolution, obedience to law, responsible citizenship, safe decision-
making, and lawful help-seeking: Provided, That competencies requiring specialized child-
protection, health, digital-safety, legal, or referral instruction shall also be delivered through the
appropriate learning areas, personnel, and mechanisms.

        Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of the implementing rules and
regulations of this Act, DepEd shall complete a national implementation review of GMRC and
Values Education under Republic Act No. 11476 and shall issue or update standards and guidance
concerning:


                                                                                                   10
       a. Compliance with statutory grade-level coverage and prescribed time allotments;
       b. Clear and developmentally appropriate learning competencies and progression
       standards;
       c. Teacher qualifications, assignment, pre-service preparation, induction, in-service
       training, and continuing professional development;
       d. Adequate, relevant, culturally responsive, disability-inclusive, and accessible learning
       resources;
       e. Concrete and experiential character-building activities, including age-appropriate role-
       playing, guided discussion, school-family collaboration, community engagement, and
       other lawful participatory methods;
       f. A whole-school approach and meaningful partnership among schools, families, and
       communities;
       g. Assessment of practical understanding, decision-making, conduct, and application of
       values, without compelling disclosure of personal beliefs or traumatic experiences and
       without discriminatory, stigmatizing, or degrading treatment; and
       h. Supervision, technical assistance, monitoring, corrective action, and public
       accountability for implementation gaps.
        School division offices shall periodically assess implementation, provide technical
assistance, and require proportionate corrective action plans where material deficiencies are found.
Monitoring shall focus on actual curriculum delivery, instructional time, trained personnel,
learning resources, character-building activities, learner competencies, and school-family-
community participation, and shall not be based solely on disciplinary statistics or subjective moral
judgments.

SEC. 11. Integration in the Early Childhood Care and Development System.
        The ECCD Council, in coordination with DepEd, DSWD, DOH, LGUs, parents, and child-
development experts, shall integrate developmentally appropriate concepts on dignity, personal
safety, respectful relationships, trusted adults, personal boundaries, safe and unsafe situations,
emotional safety, and help-seeking into existing ECCD programs, learning experiences, parent
education, and community-awareness activities under Republic Act No. 12199.

        ECCD content shall be delivered through play, stories, routines, caregiver-child
interaction, and other developmentally appropriate practices and shall not include technical
criminal-justice instruction. Parents and parent-substitutes shall be provided practical information
on positive caregiving, child safeguarding, recognizing possible signs of harm, responding
appropriately, and accessing referral services.




                                                                                                  11
       Implementation under this Section shall observe the respective statutory responsibilities of
the ECCD Council and DepEd for children below five (5) years of age and the formative years
from five (5) to eight (8) years of age.

SEC. 12. Alternative Learning System and Out-of-School Youth Programs.
        DepEd, through the Bureau of Alternative Education, shall integrate relevant competencies
into the ALS K to 12 Basic Education Curriculum, learning standards, modules, learning resources,
functional education and literacy programs, and assessment mechanisms under Republic Act No.
11510, consistent with the flexible and learner-centered nature of ALS.

         The NYC, DILG, DSWD, and LGUs shall, within their respective mandates, support or
facilitate community-based, mobile, modular, broadcast, and digital delivery for out-of-school
youth, particularly those in geographically isolated, underserved, conflict-affected, disaster-
affected, and emergency settings.

       Modules for older out-of-school youth shall include practical information on rights and
responsibilities at home, in training, at work, and online; protection from trafficking and
exploitation; lawful reporting and referral; community crime prevention; restorative justice; and
access to legal, social-welfare, health, mental-health, and livelihood support. Nothing in this
Section shall expand the jurisdiction of any agency beyond its existing statutory mandate.

SEC. 13. Technical-Vocational Education and Training.
        TESDA shall integrate appropriate child safeguarding, workplace rights, digital safety,
ethical conduct, and rule-of-law competencies into relevant training regulations, common
competencies, and programs serving young people or preparing trainees to work with children.
Particular attention shall be given to apprenticeships, workplace exposure, community-based
training, and occupations in which trainees may interact with children or other vulnerable persons.

SEC. 14. Higher Education.
       CHED shall develop and promote model modules, faculty-development programs,
reference materials, and appropriate competency frameworks for teacher education, social work,
psychology, criminology, health and allied professions, legal management, law-related education,
public administration, and other programs whose graduates are likely to work with children or
perform public-facing functions involving child protection.

        CHED may support voluntary or institutionally appropriate integration of relevant content
into general education, civic engagement, service-learning, and National Service Training Program
activities. Implementation shall respect constitutional academic freedom, institutional autonomy,
and applicable higher-education laws. Nothing in this Act shall authorize CHED to prescribe
academic content or a course in a manner inconsistent with those guarantees.

       DepEd, the ECCD Council, CHED, TESDA, and other responsible agencies shall provide
pre-service, induction, in-service, and continuing professional development opportunities for

personnel, school administrators, trainers, faculty members, and relevant youth-program
personnel.

       Training shall cover:

       a. Good Manners and Right Conduct and Values Education content, pedagogy, experiential
       character-building activities, and the whole-school approach required under Republic Act
       No. 11476;
       b. Child rights and safeguarding;
       c. Age-appropriate and trauma-informed pedagogy;
       d. Cultural, linguistic, gender, and disability inclusion;
       e. Digital safety and privacy;
       f. Recognition of possible harm;
       g. Confidentiality and data protection;
       h. Applicable mandatory-reporting duties;

       j. Protection against retaliation, intimidation, discrimination, victim-blaming, and other

       k. The limits of the role of education personnel in investigations and professional

       Nothing in this Act shall require teachers, child-development workers, or other education

providing appropriate immediate support within their competence, and facilitating referral.
Nothing in this Section shall expand or diminish a mandatory-reporting obligation under existing
law.

SEC. 16. Learning Resources; Localization, Accessibility, and Legal Accuracy.
        DepEd and the other responsible agencies shall develop, review, and periodically update
learning resources for their respective areas of responsibility, in consultation, as appropriate, with
children and youth, parents and guardians, educators, child-development specialists, child-
protection practitioners, persons with disabilities and their representative organizations,

                                                                                                   13
indigenous cultural communities, Muslim education stakeholders, civil society organizations,
professional associations, and other relevant groups.

       Learning resources shall be available in languages understood by learners and in accessible
formats, including Filipino Sign Language, Braille, large print, audio, captioned, easy-read, and
compatible digital formats, as appropriate and consistent with Republic Act No. 11650 and other

       Legal provisions, agency names, referral procedures, reporting mechanisms, and digital-
safety information in learning resources shall be reviewed periodically to ensure continuing
accuracy.

                             CHAPTER III
          SAFEGUARDING, IMPLEMENTATION, AND ACCOUNTABILITY

SEC. 17. Child Safeguarding, Disclosure, and Referral.
        Every covered institution or program shall maintain and implement clear, child-friendly,
confidential, and accessible procedures for receiving and responding to a request for help,
disclosure, or report of possible harm. Such procedures shall be consistent with existing child-
protection, anti-bullying, juvenile-justice, mental-health, data-privacy, and mandatory-reporting
laws and rules and shall:

       a. Prioritize the immediate safety, dignity, and best interests of the child;
       b. Avoid repeated, suggestive, public, or unnecessarily detailed questioning and prevent
       retaliation, intimidation, discrimination, and victim-blaming;
       c. Limit access to information to persons with lawful and legitimate functions, without
       prejudice to mandatory reporting and lawful investigation;
       d. Refer the child and, when appropriate, the family to qualified social-welfare, health,
       mental-health, legal, and law-enforcement services;
       e. Provide reasonable accommodation and communication support for children with
       disabilities and language needs;
       f. Ensure that parent or guardian participation does not delay immediate protection or
       compromise the child’s safety, particularly when the parent, guardian, or household
       member is the alleged source of harm; and
       g. Ensure that school or program personnel do not determine the truth or legal sufficiency
       of an allegation where such determination is reserved by law to competent authorities.




                                                                                               14
SEC. 18. Protection of Personal Information and Confidentiality.
        All covered institutions and government agencies shall comply with Republic Act No.
10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and other applicable privacy and
confidentiality laws and rules. Personal information concerning a learner shall be collected,
processed, stored, accessed, used, retained, and disclosed only for lawful and legitimate purposes
and to the extent necessary and proportionate to such purposes.

        Nothing in this Act shall prevent or restrict a disclosure expressly authorized or required
by law, including mandatory reporting or lawful investigation. Responsible agencies shall establish
safeguards against unauthorized access, disclosure, copying, retention, or use of information
concerning child victims, witnesses, children at risk, and children in conflict with the law.

SEC. 19. Duties of Government Agencies.
       The following agencies shall perform their respective duties under existing mandates and
in coordination with the lead agency:

       a. DepEd shall lead curriculum mapping, standards development, basic education and ALS
       implementation, personnel training, guidance for public and private schools, and
       consolidated monitoring;
       b. The ECCD Council shall lead development and implementation of the early-childhood
       and parent-education components;
       c. The CWC shall ensure consistency with national child-rights and child-protection
       policies and facilitate meaningful child participation;
       d. The JJWC and DOJ shall assist in ensuring the accuracy and child-sensitive presentation
       of juvenile justice, criminal justice, due process, diversion, restorative justice, and legal-
       referral content;
       e. DSWD shall support safeguarding standards, social-welfare referral protocols, case-
       management coordination, and capacity building within its mandate;
       f. DOH shall support health, mental-health, psychosocial, and referral content in
       coordination with structures established under Republic Act No. 12080 and other
       applicable laws;
       g. CHED and TESDA shall perform the functions specified in Sections 13 and 14 of this
       Act;
       h. DICT shall assist in digital citizenship, privacy, cybersecurity, online safety, and
       accessible digital-learning content;
       i. DILG shall support LGU and barangay participation, local referral directories,
       community dissemination, and programs for out-of-school youth;
       j. NYC shall promote youth participation and support out-of-school and community-based
       youth education;

                                                                                                  15
       k. The Presidential Office for Child Protection, or its successor office as may be designated
       by the President, may participate in policy harmonization and inter-agency coordination
       consistent with applicable law and executive issuances; and
       l. The Philippine National Police, National Bureau of Investigation, prosecution service,
       Public Attorney’s Office, and other justice-sector bodies may provide technical assistance
       on legal accuracy and referral mechanisms within their respective mandates.
        Participation of law-enforcement and justice-sector personnel in education activities shall
be child-sensitive, non-intimidating, and separated from intelligence gathering or enforcement
activities, except where immediate action is required by law to protect a child.

SEC. 20. Local Referral and Service Directories.
       Each covered basic education institution, ALS program, and other covered government-
supported youth program shall maintain or have access to an updated local referral directory
containing, as applicable:

       a. School child-protection and safeguarding focal points;
       b. Local social-welfare offices;
       c. Health and mental-health services;
       d. Law-enforcement and emergency services;
       e. Prosecutorial and legal-aid services;
       f. Barangay and local child-protection mechanisms;
       g. Services for children with disabilities;
       h. Services addressing trafficking, online sexual abuse or exploitation of children, child
       sexual abuse or exploitation materials, child marriage, child labor, bullying, and other
       child-protection concerns; and
       i. Other competent and appropriate services available within the locality.
        The directory shall be maintained in an accessible and child-friendly format and updated
at least annually and whenever there is a material change in a relevant service or referral
mechanism. No institution shall be required to create a new service where an appropriate existing
government or accredited service is available.

SEC. 21. Participation of Children, Parents, and Communities.
        Children shall be consulted in the design, testing, periodic review, and evaluation of
learning resources and reporting mechanisms in a manner consistent with their age, evolving
capacities, safety, and right to be heard. Parents, guardians, families, educators, community
leaders, civil society, child-protection practitioners, and youth organizations shall be encouraged
to support the Program through orientation, positive caregiving, community education, and referral
awareness.

                                                                                                 16
        Parent and community participation shall respect the privacy, safety, autonomy, and best
interests of the child and shall not restrict a child’s access to lawful protection, counseling, social-
welfare services, or reporting mechanisms.

SEC. 22. Compliance and Administrative Accountability.
        Covered institutions shall comply with applicable standards issued by the agency having
jurisdiction over them. Compliance shall be assessed through existing accreditation, licensing,
recognition, supervision, quality-assurance, monitoring, inspection, or administrative
mechanisms, as applicable.

        Where a covered institution materially fails to comply with mandatory standards under this
Act or its implementing rules, the responsible agency may require a corrective action plan, provide
technical assistance, conduct follow-up monitoring, or impose administrative measures authorized
under existing law, rules, regulations, or the terms of the institution’s license, permit, recognition,
accreditation, or authority to operate, subject to due process.

        No administrative measure shall be based solely on the number of disclosures or reports
received by an institution. Nothing in this Section authorizes suspension or cancellation of a
license, permit, recognition, accreditation, or authority to operate without compliance with
applicable procedural and substantive safeguards.

SEC. 23. Private Education and Training Institutions.
         Private basic education institutions, private technical-vocational institutions, and other
private programs covered by this Act shall comply with applicable minimum standards issued by
the agency having jurisdiction. Implementing rules shall provide reasonable, phased, and
proportionate requirements, technical assistance, model resources, and lawful flexibility for
institutions with limited personnel or financial resources without reducing minimum child-
safeguarding, accessibility, privacy, or legal-accuracy standards.

        Nothing in this Act shall impair the lawful autonomy of private educational institutions or
require creation of a separate academic subject where the competencies can be effectively
integrated into existing programs.

SEC. 24. Phased Implementation.
       Implementation of this Act shall proceed as follows:

       a. Within one hundred eighty (180) days from its effectivity, the implementing rules and
       regulations shall be promulgated;
       b. Within one (1) year from effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations, the
       responsible agencies shall complete curriculum mapping, the National Program


                                                                                                     17
       Framework, minimum competency standards, model learning resources, safeguarding and
       referral guidance, and the initial personnel-training framework;
       c. Implementation shall begin not later than the first full school, training, or program year
       following completion of the requirements under paragraph (b); and
       d. Nationwide implementation of the Program in basic education and ALS shall be
       completed within three (3) school years from commencement of implementation.
       Existing programs, learning resources, training programs, and institutional mechanisms
that substantially comply with this Act may be recognized, adapted, or integrated to prevent
duplication and promote efficient use of public resources.

SEC. 25. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Continuous Improvement.
        DepEd, in coordination with the agencies identified in Section 19 of this Act, shall establish
a national monitoring and evaluation framework for the Program. The framework shall measure,
at a minimum:

       a. Curriculum and program coverage;
       b. Availability and accessibility of learning resources;
       c. Personnel trained and competency attainment;
       d. Learner knowledge and practical competency;
       e. Availability and functionality of referral mechanisms;
       f. Participation of children, parents, and communities;
       g. Inclusion of underserved learners and learners with disabilities;
       h. Implementation gaps and corrective actions;
       i. Compliance with safeguarding and privacy standards;
       j. Compliance with the grade-level coverage, prescribed time allotments, teacher
       preparation, learning-resource, character-building, whole-school, annual-review, and
       reporting requirements for GMRC and Values Education under Republic Act No. 11476;
       and
       k. Resource utilization and funding adequacy.
         The framework shall not require unnecessary collection, publication, or disclosure of
personally identifiable information concerning child victims, witnesses, children at risk, or
children in conflict with the law. An increase in appropriate disclosures or reports shall not, by
itself, be treated as evidence of increased abuse or institutional failure, and report counts shall not
be used as a stand-alone performance indicator in a manner that discourages reporting, conceals
harm, or penalizes proper compliance.




                                                                                                    18
SEC. 26. Report to Congress.
         Not later than two (2) years after the start of implementation and annually thereafter for the
first five (5) years, DepEd shall submit a consolidated public report to the President and the
appropriate committees of the Senate and the House of Representatives. The report shall contain
findings, expenditures, implementation challenges, agency compliance, corrective action taken,
and recommended legislative or administrative action while protecting confidential and personal
information and without identifying individual child victims, witnesses, children at risk, or
children in conflict with the law. The report shall contain a distinct section on the implementation
of GMRC and Values Education under Republic Act No. 11476, including compliance with grade-
level coverage and time allotments, teacher preparation, learning resources, character-building
activities, learner outcomes, implementation gaps, and corrective action. This consolidated
reporting shall complement, and shall not excuse or replace, the annual review and report required
under Section 8 of Republic Act No. 11476.

                                         CHAPTER IV
                                      FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 27. Appropriations.
        The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be charged against
the current appropriations of the concerned agencies, subject to existing budgeting, accounting,
auditing, and procurement laws, rules, and regulations. Thereafter, the amounts necessary for
continued implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act and the
respective budgets of concerned agencies, subject to the usual budgetary process.

        Within their respective mandates, agencies and LGUs may utilize lawful existing funds for
education, child protection, youth development, training, digital safety, mental health, social
welfare, and related programs, provided that such utilization is consistent with the purposes for
which the funds were appropriated. Responsible agencies shall identify and submit their annual
funding requirements, including accessible learning resources, personnel training, monitoring, and
referral mechanisms, as part of the regular budget preparation process.

SEC. 28. Non-Creation of Criminal Liability.
       Nothing in this Act shall be construed to:

       a. Create a new criminal offense;
       b. Increase criminal liability or impose a criminal penalty on any learner;
       c. Lower the minimum age of criminal responsibility;
       d. Modify the rules on criminal responsibility, legal capacity, age of consent, statutory rape,
       or other protections under existing law;


                                                                                                    19
       e. Authorize an educational institution or education personnel to conduct a criminal or
       forensic investigation except to the extent expressly authorized by law;
       f. Create a new search, surveillance, intelligence-gathering, or police power;
       g. Diminish, replace, or reduce the separate teaching, grade-level coverage, time
       allotments, whole-school approach, annual review, or other requirements for GMRC and
       Values Education under Republic Act No. 11476; or
       h. Diminish rights, protections, remedies, confidentiality requirements, mandatory-
       reporting duties, professional standards, or procedural safeguards under existing child-
       protection, juvenile-justice, mental-health, disability, education, data-privacy, or other
       applicable laws.

SEC. 29. Implementing Rules and Regulations.
        Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, DepEd, in
coordination with the ECCD Council, CWC, JJWC, DOJ, DSWD, DOH, CHED, TESDA, DICT,
DILG, NYC, LGUs, and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations necessary for its effective implementation, insofar as their respective mandates are
concerned.

        The implementing rules shall be developed through meaningful consultation with
representatives of public and private education institutions, teachers, parents and guardians,
children and youth, persons with disabilities and their representative organizations, indigenous
cultural communities, Muslim education stakeholders, civil society organizations, professional
associations, and other relevant stakeholders and shall establish the National Program Framework,
minimum competencies, age- and development-level standards, curriculum-integration
procedures, safeguarding and referral standards, training requirements, accessibility standards,
learning-resource procedures, data-protection safeguards, monitoring indicators, local referral-
directory requirements, phased implementation, lawful administrative-compliance mechanisms,
and procedures for periodic review and updating.

       The implementing rules shall not create new criminal offenses or impose criminal penalties
not authorized by law.

SEC. 30. Separability Clause.
        If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions
not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 31. Repealing Clause.
        All laws, decrees, executive orders, administrative issuances, rules, and regulations or parts
thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly. Nothing
in this Act shall be construed to repeal or diminish Republic Act No. 11476 or any protection,

                                                                                                   20
right, duty, remedy, or service under existing child-protection, education, disability, mental-health,
data-privacy, juvenile-justice, or other applicable laws unless expressly provided herein.

SEC. 32. Effectivity.
       This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in
a newspaper of general circulation.
