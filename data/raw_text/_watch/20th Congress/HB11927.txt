                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Constitution recognizes that sovereignty resides in the people and that
public office is a public trust. As representatives of the people, Members of Congress
are expected not only to enact laws but also to maintain meaningful engagement with
the citizens and sectors they represent. However, there is currently no uniform
framework that institutionalizes constituent services, public consultations, and
accessibility mechanisms across all congressional offices.

       While many legislators maintain district, sectoral, or public assistance offices,
the quality, accessibility, and responsiveness of these services vary significantly.
This measure seeks to establish minimum standards for constituent engagement by
institutionalizing Constituent Services and Public Engagement Offices (CSPEOs)
that will serve as accessible channels for consultations, referrals, information
dissemination, and citizen feedback.
      The proposed measure is carefully designed to strengthen the representative
and consultative functions of Congress without duplicating the service delivery
responsibilities of executive agencies. It likewise promotes transparency,
accountability, and professionalism in constituent relations through regular
consultations, accessibility standards, constituency engagement reporting, and
compliance with data privacy requirements.

       By fostering stronger connections between elected representatives and the
people they serve, this bill seeks to promote a more participatory, responsive, and
citizen-centered legislative institution. It ensures that district representatives, party-
list representatives, and Senators alike maintain accessible and meaningful
mechanisms for public engagement consistent with the principles of democratic
governance and good public service.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. ± This Act shall be known as the ³Constituent
Engagement and Legislative Accessibility Act.´

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. ± It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to uphold the constitutional principles that sovereignty resides in the people, that
public office is a public trust, and that government authority emanates from the
citizenry. The State recognizes that meaningful citizen participation, accessibility to
elected representatives, and responsive governance are essential components of a
democratic society.
       Toward this end, the State shall strengthen mechanisms that facilitate
communication, consultation, and engagement between Members of Congress and
their constituents, promote transparency and accountability in legislative
representation, and ensure that citizens have accessible avenues through which their
concerns, recommendations, and aspirations may be communicated and considered
in the legislative process.

      SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. ± This Act shall apply to all Members of the
Senate and House of Representatives and their respective offices.

      Every Member of Congress shall establish and maintain a Constituent
Services and Public Engagement Office (CSPEO) or an equivalent mechanism
appropriate to the nature and scope of the constituency represented. For district
representatives, such office or mechanism shall be maintained within the
congressional district represented. For party-list representatives, such office or
mechanism shall be accessible to the sector or sectors represented. For Senators,
such office or mechanism shall be designed to provide accessible channels for
engagement with constituents nationwide through physical, digital, and other
appropriate means.

      SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. ² For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Constituent refers to any citizen, resident, sector, community, stakeholder,
      or group represented by a Member of Congress, whether territorial, sectoral,
      or national in scope;

      (b) Constituent Services and Public Engagement Office (CSPEO) refers to the
      office, unit, desk, digital platform, or combination thereof established by a
      Member of Congress to facilitate constituent engagement, referrals,
      consultations, information dissemination, and feedback mechanisms;

      (c) Constituent Service refers to the provision of assistance, facilitation,
      referral, information, or guidance intended to help constituents access
      government programs, services, and institutions;
      (d) Public Consultation refers to any dialogue, forum, town hall meeting,
      hearing, assembly, digital engagement, survey, or other mechanism intended
      to obtain the views, concerns, recommendations, and feedback of constituents
      on legislative, policy, or governance matters; and

      (e) Referral Assistance refers to the facilitation of communication and
      coordination between constituents and appropriate government agencies,
      local government units, government-owned and controlled corporations, and
      other relevant institutions.

      SEC. 5. Establishment of Constituent Services and Public Engagement
Offices. ± Every Member of Congress shall establish and maintain a Constituent
Services and Public Engagement Office (CSPEO) that shall serve as the primary
channel through which constituents may communicate concerns, seek information,
obtain referral assistance, participate in consultations, and engage with their elected
representatives.

       The CSPEO shall function as a facilitative and representative mechanism and
shall not assume or duplicate the service delivery functions of executive agencies,
local government units, or other government instrumentalities.

      SEC. 6. Functions. ± The Constituent Services and Public Engagement
Office (CSPEO) shall perform the following functions:

      (a) Receive, document, and monitor constituent concerns, complaints,
      requests, recommendations, inquiries, and policy proposals;

      (b) Facilitate referrals to appropriate government agencies, local government
      units, constitutional bodies, government-owned and controlled corporations,
      and other competent institutions;

      (c) Assist constituents in obtaining information regarding government
      programs, services, and processes;
      (d) Monitor, whenever practicable, the status of referrals and provide updates
      to concerned constituents;

      (e) Disseminate information regarding legislation, committee activities,
      public hearings, consultations, and other matters relevant to constituents;

      (f) Facilitate public consultations, town hall meetings, stakeholder dialogues,
      and citizen engagement activities;

      (g) Maintain records and documentation necessary for transparency,
      accountability, and reporting; and

      (h) Perform such other related functions consistent with the objectives of this
      Act.

       SEC. 7. Public Consultations, Citizen Participation, and Community
Engagement. ± Consistent with the representative and deliberative functions of
Congress, Members of Congress shall endeavor to maintain regular and meaningful
engagement with their constituents through consultations, dialogues, town hall
meetings, sectoral discussions, public forums, digital engagements, surveys, and
other appropriate participatory mechanisms.

      To the extent practicable, consultations shall be conducted through both
physical and digital means to maximize public participation and accessibility. Inputs,
recommendations, concerns, and proposals generated from consultations shall be
properly documented and may be considered in the formulation of legislation,
oversight activities, budget deliberations, and other official functions of Members of
Congress.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed as requiring a Member of Congress to
adopt any specific policy position arising from consultations conducted pursuant to
this Act.

     SEC. 8. Accessibility Standards. ± The CSPEO shall ensure that constituent
engagement mechanisms are accessible through multiple channels, including in-
person consultations, written correspondence, telephone communication, electronic
mail, online platforms, and other appropriate means.

       Appropriate accommodations shall be made to ensure accessibility for senior
citizens, persons with disabilities, indigenous peoples, geographically isolated and
disadvantaged communities, and other vulnerable sectors.

      SEC. 9. Transparency, Accountability and Constituency Engagement
Reporting. ±Every congressional office shall maintain records of constituent
concerns received, referrals facilitated, consultations conducted, and other
engagement activities undertaken pursuant to this Act.

      Not later than March 31 of each year, every congressional office shall prepare
a Constituency Engagement Report containing:

      (a) The number and categories of constituent concerns, requests, and inquiries
      received;
      (b) The number and nature of referrals facilitated;
      (c) The consultations, dialogues, and engagement activities conducted;
      (d) Common issues and concerns raised by constituents;
      (e) Measures undertaken to improve accessibility, responsiveness, and public
      participation; and
      (f) Other information that may demonstrate compliance with this Act.

      Such reports shall be made publicly accessible through official websites or
other appropriate platforms, subject to applicable laws on privacy and
confidentiality.

      SEC. 10. Data Privacy and Confidentiality. ± All information collected,
processed, stored, or transmitted pursuant to this Act shall be governed by the
provisions of Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the        a      a a

      Congressional offices shall adopt appropriate administrative, organizational,
physical, and technical safeguards to protect constituent information from
unauthorized access, disclosure, misuse, alteration, or destruction. Information
obtained through constituent engagement mechanisms shall be used solely for
legitimate official purposes and shall not be utilized for partisan political activities,
electioneering, commercial purposes, or any unauthorized undertaking.

       SEC. 11. National and Sectoral Constituency Engagement. ± Recognizing
the distinct nature of national and sectoral representation, Senators and party-list
representatives shall adopt engagement mechanisms appropriate to the
constituencies they represent. Such mechanisms may include regional consultations,
sectoral advisory councils, mobile constituency services, digital engagement
platforms, nationwide feedback systems, stakeholder assemblies, and other
accessible means of consultation and participation.

       The Senate and the House of Representatives may formulate supplementary
guidelines to ensure meaningful engagement with national and sectoral
constituencies.

        SEC. 12. Prohibited Act. ± To preserve the integrity, impartiality, and public
trust in constituent services, the following acts are hereby prohibited:

      (a) Using constituent service mechanisms, consultations, referrals, or
      assistance programs for partisan political activities, election campaigning,
      political endorsements, vote-buying, or any activity intended to provide undue
      political advantage to any candidate, political party, or organization;

      (b) Soliciting, demanding, receiving, or accepting money, gifts, favors,
      commissions, contributions, or any form of consideration in exchange for
      assistance, referrals, endorsements, or preferential treatment;

      (c) Discriminating against or denying assistance to any constituent on the
      basis of political affiliation, religion, ethnicity, gender, disability,
      socioeconomic status, or any other prohibited ground;

      (d) Knowingly providing false, misleading, or fraudulent information
      regarding government programs, benefits, services, or legislative actions;
      (e) Unauthorized disclosure, misuse, sale, transfer, or exploitation of
      confidential constituent information; and

      (f) Falsifying, concealing, destroying, or tampering with records, reports, or
      documents required under this Act.

      Any personnel found to have committed any prohibited act shall be subject to
appropriate administrative, civil, and criminal liability under existing laws, without
prejudice to the imposition of disciplinary measures by the House of
Representatives, the Senate, the Civil Service Commission, or other competent
authorities.

       SEC. 13. Oversight and Compliance. ± The Senate and the House of
Representatives shall each adopt internal rules, standards, and monitoring
mechanisms for the implementation of this Act consistent with their respective
constitutional powers and institutional autonomy.

       The Commission on Audit (COA) may review expenditures related to the
implementation of this Act, while the Civil Service Commission (CSC) and other
appropriate government agencies may exercise jurisdiction over administrative
violations involving covered personnel.

      SEC. 14. Appropriations. ± The amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the existing appropriations of the Senate and the
House of Representatives. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be
included in their respective annual budgets.

      SEC. 15. Separability Clause. ± If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 16. Repealing Clause ± All laws, executive orders, rules, regulations,
and other issuances inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.
      SEC. 17. Effectivity. ±This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
