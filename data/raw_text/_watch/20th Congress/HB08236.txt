13                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE
14
15   Scouting, or the discipline of scoutcraft, has long been recognized as one of the most
16   effective means of fostering the physical, moral, spiritual, and social development of
17   children and youth. Through structured programs grounded in service, leadership,
18   patriotism, environmental stewardship, and community engagement, Scouting
19   cultivates civic consciousness, discipline, integrity, and responsibility—values
20   essential to nation-building.
21
22   The Boy Scouts of the Philippines (BSP), originally established under Commonwealth
23   Act No. 111, as amended, has played a historic and enduring role in shaping
24   generations of Filipino youth. From disaster response and community service to
25   character formation and outdoor education, the organization has contributed
26   significantly to developing responsible and patriotic citizens.
27
28   However, the evolving social, educational, and institutional landscape calls for a
29   comprehensive review and restructuring of the legal framework governing the BSP.
30   The current charter, enacted in a different historical and administrative context, no
31   longer fully reflects contemporary governance standards, organizational best
32   practices, or the increasing complexities faced by youth development institutions. The
33   demands of transparency, accountability, program modernization, financial
34   sustainability, and alignment with national youth development policies require a
35   clearer and more responsive statutory structure.
36
37   The 1987 Constitution explicitly mandates the State to promote and protect the
38   physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being of the youth and to instill
39   in them patriotism and nationalism (Section 13, Article II). In furtherance of this
40   constitutional directive, this bill seeks to reorganize and strengthen the institutional
41   framework of the Boy Scouts of the Philippines to ensure that it remains relevant,
42   effective, and properly structured to fulfill its mission.
43
44   Specifically, the measure redefines the corporate structure, powers, functions, and
45   governance mechanisms of the BSP to align it with modern standards of nonprofit
46   governance and youth service delivery. It clarifies institutional roles, strengthens
47   oversight mechanisms, ensures fiscal accountability, and enhances programmatic
48   capacity to respond to contemporary challenges faced by Filipino youth—including
49   digital exposure, climate risks, community safety, and civic disengagement.
50

 1   Moreover, this bill recognizes the need for a formal and well-organized corporation
 2   that is insulated from ambiguity in legal status while maintaining its essential
 3   character as a youth-serving institution dedicated to public service. By providing a
 4   clearer statutory mandate, the measure ensures stability in leadership, continuity in
 5   operations, and enhanced collaboration with educational institutions, local
 6   government units, and other stakeholders.
 7
 8   Previous legislative efforts dating back to the 8th Congress have sought to modernize
 9   the legal framework of the BSP. This measure builds upon those efforts by proposing
10   a more concrete, comprehensive, and forward-looking institutional design. It ensures
11   that the Boy Scouts of the Philippines remains a vital partner of the State in molding
12   the character, discipline, and civic responsibility of future generations.
13
14   In strengthening Scouting as a national institution, this bill ultimately strengthens
15   the moral and civic foundation of the Republic. By investing in the organized
16   development of our youth, we invest in the long-term stability, leadership, and
17   integrity of our nation.
18
19   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

13                             AN ACT
14   INSTITUTING THE BOY SCOUTS OF THE PHILIPPINES CHARTER,
15   DEFINING ITS OBJECTIVES, POWER AND FUNCTIONS, REPEALING
16   FOR THE PURPOSE COMMONWEALTH ACT NO. 111, AS AMENDED
17
18   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representative of the Philippines in
19   congress assembled:
20
21   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known and cited as the “Boy Scouts of
22   the Philippines Charter”.
23
24   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - Recognizing the vital role of young people
25   (children and youth) under the ages 4 to 25 in nation-building, it is therefore the
26   policy of the State to ensure and protect their physical, moral, intellectual, emotional,
27   social and spiritual growth by promoting Scouting as an integral component of the
28   basic education system.
29
30   SECTION. 3. Recognition of the Boy Scouts of the Philippines. ” Pursuant to the
31   ideals of the World Organization of Scouting Movement (WOSM), the "Boy Scouts of
32   the Philippines", hereinafter referred to as the "BSP", is hereby recognized and known
33   as the body corporate as originally and duly created by virtue of Commonwealth Act
34   No. 111. The principal office of the BSP shall be located in Metropolitan Manila,
35   Philippines.
36
37   SECTION 4. Objectives. - The objectives of this Act are:
38       (a) To promote through organizations and cooperation with other agencies,
39           the ability of the young people and/or the Filipino youth to do useful things
40           for themselves and others;
41       (b) To train the young people and/or the Filipino youth in the attitudes, skills,
42           and the habits of responsible citizenship;
43       (c) To inculcate in the young people and/or the Filipino youth patriotism, civic
44           consciousness and responsibility, environmental protection awareness and
45           activities, courage, self-reliance, selflessness, discipline and kindred
46           virtues, as well as moral and spiritual values, using the method which are
47           in common use by Scouts.
48
49   SECTION 5. Boy Scouts of the Philippines. - The Boy Scouts of the Philippines (BSP)
50   hereinafter referred to shall have its principal office in Metropolitan Manila. They




                                                3
 1   may establish such other offices, agencies or branches anywhere in the Philippines as
 2   its operations would require and its National Executive Board would determine.
 3
 4   SECTION 6. Corporate Powers. - As a juridical entity, the BSP shall have perpetual
 5   succession and shall enjoy the following corporate powers:
 6         (a)    To sue and be sued;
 7         (b)    To acquire, hold and convey such real and personal properties, rights
 8                and choses in action as shall be deemed necessary and desirable for its
 9                purposes as hereinafter set forth;
10         (c)    To receive funds, real and personal properties by gift, devise and
11                donation, contribution or other means;
12         (d)    To adapt a seal and to alter and destroy the same at pleasure;
13         (e)    To have offices and conduct its business and affairs in the Metropolitan
14                Manila area and in the provinces, cities, municipalities and barangays
15                in the Philippines; and,
16         (f)    Generally, to exercise all the powers of a corporation and do all such acts
17                and things as may be necessary to carry into effect of the provisions of
18                this Act and promote the purposes of said organization.
19
20   The Boy Scouts of the Philippines (BSP) shall function as an independent, non-
21   governmental youth development organization organized under this Act and shall not
22   be deemed a government agency or instrumentality for purposes of administrative
23   control. However, in recognition of the public character of its mission and its long-
24   standing role in youth formation, civic education, and nation-building, the BSP shall
25   maintain institutional cooperation and coordination with relevant national
26   government agencies, local government units, and public educational institutions.
27
28   For purposes of implementing youth development initiatives and strengthening civic
29   education among Filipino youth, the BSP may enter into formal partnerships,
30   memoranda of agreement, or cooperative arrangements with appropriate
31   government agencies, including but not limited to the Department of Education
32   (DepEd), the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the
33   Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), the National Youth
34   Commission (NYC), and other agencies concerned with youth welfare, disaster
35   preparedness, environmental stewardship, and community development.
36
37   The Department of Education may recognize scouting programs as complementary
38   youth development activities within schools and may facilitate voluntary
39   participation of students in scouting activities consistent with existing education
40   policies and the principle of voluntary participation in extracurricular programs.
41
42   Local government units, through their respective sanggunians and youth
43   development offices, may support scouting activities and programs within their
44   jurisdictions through the provision of logistical assistance, facilities, training venues,
45   or other forms of cooperation consistent with applicable laws and local ordinances.
46
47   Nothing in this section shall be construed as placing the BSP under the administrative
48   supervision or control of any government agency; Provided, however, That
49   partnerships with government entities shall be governed by written agreements
50   approved by the National Executive Board of the BSP and implemented in accordance
51   with applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

 5
 6                                            Vision

 8   education committed to develop morally straight, disciplined, concerned, self-reliant

33   SECTION 12. National Executive Board. - The governing body of the said
34   corporation shall consist of not more than 33 members of the National Executive
35   Board, the members of which shall be Filipino citizens of good moral character. The
36   Board shall be composed of the following:
37         (a)    The National President of the Girl Scouts of the Philippines;
38         (b)    Regional Chairmen of the geographical regions of the Corporation to be
39                known as Scout Regions who shall be elected by the representatives of
40                all the chartered Provincial, City or conglomerate of cities and
41                municipalities to be known as Local Councils within the region during
42                its meeting called for this purpose;
43         (c)    One Senior/Rover Scout each from Luzon, Visayas and Mindanao areas,
44                to be elected during the conference of Scouts ages 13 to 25 known as the
45                Scout Youth Forum in their respective Philippine geographical areas;
46         (d)    Twelve (12) regular members to be elected by the members of the
47                National Council in its meeting called for this purpose; and,
48         (e)    Five (5) additional members from the private sector.
49
50         Thereafter, the National Executive Board as herein fully constituted shall elect
51   from among themselves the following officers of the corporation:

20   descriptive or designing marks, words and phrases, badges, uniforms and insignia for
21   the Boy Scouts of the Philippines in carrying out its program in accordance with the
22   purposes of this Act, and which shall be published in the Official Gazette or in any

24

27                registered Scouts or officials who have satisfactorily complied with the
28                international requirements and which BSP shall use in carrying out its
29                programs consistent with purpose of this Act. The BSP shall cause the
30                registration of said emblems, badges, insignias, uniforms and other
31                equipment in accordance with the provisions of Republic Act No. 8293,
32                otherwise known as the Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines, as
33                amended. The Intellectual Property Office (IPO) is hereby directed to
34                cause said registration free of charge.
35         (b)    The Securities and Exchange Commission, the IPO and the Department

37                registration of trade names and the filling of trademarks, commercial
38                marks and industrial models and designs making use of the emblems,
39                badges, insignias, uniforms and other equipment of the Boy Scouts of
40                the Philippines or their translation in any official language and dialect

29   SECTION 18. Penal Clause. - From and after the passage of this Act, it shall be
30   unlawful for any person within the jurisdiction of the Philippines to falsely and
31   fraudulently wear the Boy Scouts of the Philippines uniform, call himself as, or
32   represent himself to be a member of, or an agent for, the Boy Scouts of the
33   Philippines. It shall be unlawful for any person to manufacture, sell or distribute or
34   cause to be manufactured, sold or distributed fraudulently or without the official
35   knowledge and written approval of the National Executive Board of the Boy Scouts of
36   the Philippines badges, uniforms, insignia, or Scouting paraphernalia or the photos
37   or visuals of a Scout or Scouts in uniform, or the logo, seal or corporate name of the
38   Boy Scouts of the Philippines, in any print ad, radio or television commercial,
39   billboard, collateral material or any form of advertisement, including the internet; or
40   to use the name of the Boy Scouts of the Philippines in any illegal purpose or personal
41   gain. Any violation of the provisions of this section shall be punished by prison
42   correctional in its maximum period to prison mayor in its medium period and fine of
43   not less than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php200, 000.00) nor more than Five
44   Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php500,000.00). Provided, that in case of corporations,
45   partnership, associations, societies or companies, the manager, administrator or the
46   person in charge or the management or administration of the business shall be
47   criminally responsible for any such violation.
48
49          This shall be without prejudice to the applicable penalties for violations of the
50   intellectual property rights of the BSP under the Intellectual Code of the Philippines,
