                             EXPLANATORY NOTE


Systems loss charge has been defined as the component of the distribution
rate that represents the recovery of the cost of power due to technical and
non-technical losses. Technical losses are those inherent in the physical
delivery of electricity while non-technical losses refer to electricity lost due to
pilferage, meter reading errors, meter tampering, and losses which are not
related to the physical characteristics and function of the electric system.

According to Republic Act No. 9136, the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC)
is empowered to determine caps on systems loss charge based on load
density, sales mix, cost of service, delivery voltage, and other technical
considerations it may promulgate. The ERC is also mandated to establish
minimum efficiency performance standards for distribution utilities including
systems losses, interruption frequency rates, and collection efficiency.

While distribution utilities are allowed to pass on systems loss to consumers,
this entitlement did not lead to distribution systems improvement to achieve
efficiency. Eventually, as distribution systems remain the same, it is
inevitable for systems losses to rise, burdening consumers with additional
charges that do not redound to their benefit.

Hence, this bill is filed to remove systems loss as a component of electricity
bill and limit recovery costs that may be passed on to consumers. This bill
likewise requires distribution utilities to improve efficiency of electricity
distribution by modernizing facilities. Ultimately, this bill aims to alleviate
consumers plight of high electricity rates and improve electricity distribution
in the country. Thus, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                          AN ACT
   REMOVING SYSTEMS LOSS AS A SEPARATE COMPONENT OF
    ELECTRICITY BILLS, LIMITING RECOVERY OF COSTS AND
   PROMOTING ACCOUNTABILITY OF DISTRIBUTION UTILITIES,
AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136, OTHERWISE
  KNOWN AS THE “ELECTRIC POWER INDUSTRY REFORM ACT OF
                           2001”

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Systems Loss
Charge Abolition Act”.

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared policy of the
State to protect electricity consumers against unreasonable charges,
particularly those arising from theft, inefficiency, negligence, and other
causes within the control of distribution utilities.

       It is likewise the policy of the State to promote accountability,
development, and modernization of distribution utilities and ensure just
electricity rates and reliable power distribution. Towards this end, the State
shall require the adoption of efficient distribution systems to minimize
systems loss and ensure improved electricity infrastructure across the
country.

      SEC 3. Removing systems loss as a separate component of
electricity charges. – Section 25 of Republic Act No. 9136 is hereby amended
to read as follows:

             “SEC. 25. Retail Rate. – The retail rates charged by
      distribution utilities for the supply of electricity in their captive
      market shall be subject to regulation by the ERC based on the
      principle of full recovery of prudent and reasonable economic

        SEC. 5. Improvement of Distribution Infrastructure. – Distribution
utilities and electric cooperatives shall adopt and implement appropriate and
efficient programs to reduce system losses such as modernization of
distribution infrastructure, replacement of obsolete equipment, deployment
of new technologies, upgrading of metering systems, regular preventive
maintenance, improved network planning, and strengthened enforcement
against electricity theft and other illegal connections.

      The ERC shall establish systems loss targets and implement incentives
program for efficiency and impose penalties for inefficiency.

      SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the ERC, in consultation with the
Department of Energy, distribution utilities, consumer groups, and other
stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its
implementation.

       The rules shall provide for the determination of prudent and reasonable

       SEC. 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be
held unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions which are not
affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

       SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules, or regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
