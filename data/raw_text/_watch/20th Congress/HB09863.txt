15                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

16   The increasing prevalence of obesity, diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular diseases, and
17   other diet-related illnesses has become a significant public health concern in the
18   Philippines. These conditions impose substantial burdens on individuals, families, and the
19   healthcare system. One contributing factor is the lack of readily available nutritional
20   information at the point of purchase, which limits consumers' ability to make informed
21   dietary decisions.

22   In today's fast-paced environment, a growing number of Filipinos rely on meals prepared
23   by restaurants, fast-food chains, cafés, and other food establishments. However,
24   consumers are often unaware of the calorie content of the food and beverages they
25   consume. Without access to such information, it becomes difficult for individuals to
26   manage their dietary intake and make choices consistent with their health goals.

27   Providing calorie information on menus and menu boards empowers consumers to make
28   healthier choices, promotes transparency in the food service industry, and increases
29   public awareness of nutritional intake. Experiences in various jurisdictions have shown
30   that menu calorie labeling can positively influence consumer behavior and encourage
31   food establishments to offer healthier menu options. By making calorie information
32   readily accessible, consumers are better equipped to balance their food choices and adopt
33   healthier lifestyles.

34   This proposed measure seeks to require food establishments to display the calorie content
35   of standard food and beverage items in a clear, accurate, and accessible manner. The bill
36   aims to strengthen consumer rights by ensuring that essential nutritional information is
37   available before purchasing decisions are made, thereby enabling consumers to make
38   informed choices regarding their health and well-being.

39   The measure likewise recognizes the vital role of the Department of Health (DOH) in
40   promoting public health and preventing nutrition-related diseases through evidence-based
41   policies, nutrition education, and regulatory oversight. As the lead implementing agency,
42   the DOH shall establish standards and guidelines to ensure the accuracy, consistency, and
43   reliability of calorie information disclosed by food establishments.

44   The bill also acknowledges the important role of the Department of Trade and
45   Industry (DTI) in protecting consumer welfare and ensuring that consumers receive
46   accurate, transparent, and accessible information about the products and services they
47   purchase. Through its participation in the implementation of this measure, the DTI shall
48   help promote consumer awareness and support compliance with calorie disclosure
49   requirements as part of its mandate to uphold consumer rights.
50    Moreover, Local Government Units (LGUs) are essential partners in the effective
51    implementation and enforcement of this Act. As front-line regulatory bodies responsible
52    for business permitting, licensing, and local inspections, LGUs are strategically
53    positioned to monitor compliance by food establishments and assist in ensuring the
54    successful implementation of calorie disclosure requirements within their respective
55    jurisdictions.

56    Through the collaborative efforts of the DOH, DTI, LGUs, food establishments, and
57    consumers, this measure seeks to foster a culture of informed food choices, encourage
58    healthier eating habits, improve transparency in the food service industry, and contribute
59    to the prevention of obesity and other nutrition-related diseases among Filipinos.

60    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

114   AN ACT REQUIRING FOOD ESTABLISHMENTS TO DISPLAY THE CALORIE
115      CONTENT OF FOOD AND BEVERAGE ITEMS OFFERED FOR SALE,
116       PROMOTING INFORMED CONSUMER CHOICE, AND FOR OTHER
117                            PURPOSES

118   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in Congress assembled:

119   SECTION 1. Title.

120   This Act shall be known as the "Calorie Transparency and Consumer Information
121   Act."

122   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy.

123   It is the policy of the State to promote and protect the health of the people by ensuring
124   that consumers are provided with accurate nutritional information necessary to make
125   informed dietary choices. The State recognizes the importance of transparency in food
126   labeling as a means of addressing obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and other
127   nutrition-related health concerns.

128   SECTION 3. Definition of Terms.

129   For purposes of this Act:

130   (a) Food Establishment refers to any restaurant, fast-food outlet, café, bakery, food court
131   stall, catering service, or similar business offering prepared food or beverages for sale to
132   the public.

133   (b) Calorie Content refers to the amount of energy contained in a food or beverage item,
134   expressed in kilocalories (kcal).

135   (c) Menu Board refers to any display, printed or electronic, showing food and beverage
136   items available for purchase.

137   SECTION 4. Mandatory Calorie Disclosure.

138   All food establishments covered by this Act shall clearly and conspicuously display the
139   calorie content of each standard food and beverage item:

140   (a) On printed menus;
141   (b) On menu boards and digital ordering platforms;

142   (c) Adjacent to the name or price of each food or beverage item; and

143   (d) In a font size and format readily visible to consumers.

144   SECTION 5. Scope and Exemptions.

145   The provisions of this Act shall apply to food establishments with five (5) or more
146   branches nationwide or such threshold as may be determined by the implementing agency.

147   The following may be exempted:

148   (a) Temporary food stalls operating for less than thirty (30) days;

149   (b) Customized food orders prepared according to individual customer specifications; and

150   (c) Other categories as may be determined by the implementing rules and regulations.

151   SECTION 6. Duties of Food Establishments.

152   Food establishments shall:

153   (a) Determine and maintain reasonably accurate calorie information for standard menu
154   items;

155   (b) Make available additional nutritional information upon request; and

156   (c) Update calorie disclosures whenever substantial changes are made to menu items.

157   SECTION 7. Implementing Agency.

158   The Department of Health (DOH) shall be the lead implementing agency of this Act.
159   The DOH shall, in coordination with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI),
160   local government units (LGUs), and other concerned government agencies, ensure the
161   effective implementation, monitoring, and enforcement of the provisions of this Act.

162   The DTI shall assist in ensuring compliance by food establishments, promote consumer
163   awareness regarding calorie disclosure requirements, and exercise such powers and
164   functions as may be necessary under existing consumer protection laws.

165   SECTION 8. Penalties.

166   Any food establishment that fails to comply with the requirements of this Act shall be
167   subject to:

168   (a) First Offense – Written warning and directive to comply within thirty (30) days;

169   (b) Second Offense – Administrative fine of not less than Ten Thousand Pesos
170   (₱10,000.00) but not more than Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00);

171   (c) Third and Subsequent Offenses – Administrative fine of not less than Fifty Thousand
172   Pesos (₱50,000.00) but not more than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱200,000.00),
173   without prejudice to suspension of business permits as may be provided by law.

174   SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations.
175   Within one hundred twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department
176   of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI),
177   and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for
178   the effective implementation of this Act.

179   SECTION 10. Separability Clause.

180   If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining
181   provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

182   SECTION 11. Repealing Clause.

183   All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations inconsistent with this Act are
184   hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

185   SECTION 12. Effectivity.

186   This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or
187   in a newspaper of general circulation.
