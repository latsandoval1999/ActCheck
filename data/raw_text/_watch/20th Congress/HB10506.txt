                               AN ACT
PROHIBITING PRIVATE ELECTRIC UTILITIES FROM RECOVERING SYSTEM
LOSSES FROM CONSUMERS, LIMITING THE RECOVERABLE SYSTEM LOSSES OF
RURAL ELECTRIC COOPERATIVES TO FIVE PERCENT (5%), PROVIDING FOR THE
VERIFICATION AND USE OF SYSTEM-LOSS CHARGES, AMENDING FOR THE
PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7832, AS AMENDED, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Electricity is not a luxury but a basic necessity that powers our homes, schools,
hospitals, farms, and businesses. Every Filipino family depends on electricity for their
daily lives, yet many continue to struggle with high and rising power bills. Consumers
should only pay for the electricity they actually use and for costs that are fair,
reasonable, and necessary. They should not be made to shoulder expenses caused by
theft, poor maintenance, inef icient operations, or management failures that are
beyond their control.

Under existing laws, distribution utilities are allowed to recover certain system losses
through electricity rates, subject to rules set by the Energy Regulatory Commission
(ERC). While some technical losses are unavoidable in delivering electricity, many
losses result from illegal connections, electricity theft, defective equipment, billing
errors, and operational inef iciencies. These are problems that should be addressed
by the utility itself, not passed on to hardworking consumers who faithfully pay their
bills every month.

This bill seeks to put consumers irst by prohibiting private electric utilities from
charging consumers for any system loss. For rural electric cooperatives, which often

                                                                            1|Page
serve remote and dif icult-to-reach communities, the bill allows only veri ied and
unavoidable technical losses to be recovered, subject to a strict maximum cap of ive
percent (5%) and prior approval by the ERC. The measure also requires full
transparency by ensuring that any approved system-loss charge is clearly shown in
electricity bills and used only for projects that improve the distribution system and
reduce future losses. Non-technical and administrative losses, including electricity
theft, meter tampering, billing mistakes, collection inef iciencies, and poor
management, can never be charged to consumers.

This measure promotes fairness, accountability, and transparency in the power sector.
It encourages distribution utilities to improve their systems instead of passing the
cost of their inef iciencies to the public. More importantly, it protects Filipino families
from paying for electricity they never consumed and expenses they never caused. At
a time when every peso counts, this bill will help make electricity rates more just,
affordable, and responsive to the needs of the Filipino people.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Electricity System Loss
Reduction and Consumer Protection Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect electricity
consumers from charges arising from pilferage, meter tampering, billing errors,
administrative inef iciency, inadequate collection systems, and other losses that are
attributable to the negligence, inef iciency, or failure of a distribution utility to
maintain an ef icient and reliable distribution system.

The State likewise recognizes that unavoidable technical losses may arise from the
operation of an electricity distribution system. Such losses may be recovered only to
the extent authorized by this Act, subject to veri ication, ef iciency standards, due
process, and the protection of consumers from unreasonable electricity rates.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:


                                                                            3|Page
   (a) Distribution utility refers to any private electric utility or rural electric
       cooperative authorized to distribute electricity to end-users;

   (b) Private electric utility refers to a privately owned distribution utility
       authorized by law or franchise to engage in the distribution of electricity;

   (c) Rural electric cooperative refers to an electric cooperative organized and
       operating under applicable Philippine laws and regulations;

   (d) System loss refers to the difference between the kilowatt-hours purchased or
       generated and the kilowatt-hours sold by a distribution utility, expressed as a
       percentage of the kilowatt-hours purchased or generated;

   (e) Technical loss refers to the unavoidable loss of electrical energy inherent in
       the physical operation of conductors, transformers, and other distribution-
       system equipment, including veri ied technical metering losses;

   (f) Non-technical loss refers to energy lost through pilferage, theft, meter
       tampering, illegal connections, meter-reading errors, billing errors, and
       similar causes; and

   (g) Administrative loss refers to unbilled energy, energy used for the proper
       operation of the distribution system, and losses arising from administrative,
       billing, collection, or managerial inef iciency.

       The Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) shall issue the technical standards
       for the classi ication and measurement of system losses, consistent with this
       Act and the Philippine Distribution Code.

SECTION 4. Prohibition on Recovery of System Losses by Private Electric
Utilities. — A private electric utility shall not charge, recover, or pass on to its
consumers any system loss, whether classi ied as technical, non-technical, or
administrative loss.

No system-loss charge, adjustment, surcharge, or substantially similar charge shall be
included in the rates, bills, or other charges imposed by a private electric utility.

The prohibition under this section shall not prevent the ERC from requiring a private
electric utility to report, measure, and reduce its technical, non-technical, and
administrative losses for regulatory, monitoring, and enforcement purposes.

                                                                          4|Page
SECTION 5. Limitation on Recoverable System Losses of Rural Electric
Cooperatives. — A rural electric cooperative may recover from its consumers only
veri ied and unavoidable technical losses, subject to the following conditions:

   (a) The recoverable technical loss shall not exceed ive percent (5%) of the rural
       electric cooperative’s veri ied kilowatt-hour purchases or generation;

   (b) Non-technical and administrative losses shall not be recovered from
       consumers under any circumstance;

   (c) Technical losses arising from design defects, obsolete or inadequately
       maintained facilities, unauthorized expenditures, managerial inef iciency,
       project delays not excused by force majeure, or failure to comply with
       applicable technical standards shall not be recoverable;

   (d) The recoverable technical loss shall be determined by the ERC through a
       transparent and evidence-based process, after due notice and hearing; and

   (e) The ERC may prescribe a lower cap or phase out the recovery of technical
       losses entirely when warranted by the ef iciency, inancial condition, and
       operating characteristics of the rural electric cooperative, without exceeding
       the maximum cap established under this Act.

SECTION 6. Determination and Veri ication of System-Loss Charges. — The ERC
shall determine the allowable system-loss charge of each rural electric cooperative on
an annual basis, based on the cooperative’s veri ied system-loss data for the
immediately preceding twelve-month period.

The ERC shall independently verify the following:

   (a) The kilowatt-hours purchased or generated and sold;

   (b) The classi ication of losses as technical, non-technical, or administrative;

   (c) The necessity, prudence, and ef iciency of the expenditures relied upon by the
       rural electric cooperative;

   (d) The compliance of the cooperative’s facilities and operations with applicable
       technical and performance standards; and

                                                                            5|Page
   (e) The implementation of measures to reduce system losses.

       No system-loss charge shall be collected unless approved by the ERC. The ERC
       shall disallow any amount that is unsupported, excessive, duplicative,
       attributable to inef iciency, or otherwise not recoverable under this Act.

       The ERC shall establish procedures for the refund or crediting of amounts
       collected in excess of the approved system-loss charge, including the payment
       of applicable interest when warranted.

SECTION 7. Use of Authorized System-Loss Charges. — All system-loss charges
authorized under this Act shall be separately identi ied in the consumer’s bill and
shall be placed in a segregated account.

The charges shall be used exclusively for:

   (a) The repair, rehabilitation, upgrading, or replacement of distribution lines,
       transformers, meters, and related facilities;

   (b) The installation of technologies and equipment that reduce technical and
       non-technical losses;

   (c) Meter modernization, inspection, testing, and anti-pilferage measures; and

   (d) Other system-loss reduction projects expressly approved by the ERC.

        System-loss charges shall not be used for dividends, bonuses, entertainment
        expenses, unrelated capital projects, penalties, ines, interest arising from
        unexcused delays, or administrative expenses not directly connected with an
        approved system-loss reduction program.

        The ERC shall require each rural electric cooperative to submit an annual
        report on the collection and use of system-loss charges and shall make the
        report available to the public.

SECTION 8. Performance-Based Reduction and Phaseout. — The ERC shall
establish measurable system-loss reduction targets for every rural electric
cooperative authorized to recover technical losses.


                                                                         6|Page
The ERC shall periodically reduce the allowable cap when the cooperative fails to meet
its approved reduction targets, subject to notice and hearing. The ERC shall phase out
the recovery of technical losses when the cooperative’s system and inancial
conditions permit, consistent with the protection of consumers and the continued
provision of safe, adequate, reliable, and affordable electricity service.

SECTION 9. Consumer Protection and Transparency. — Every distribution utility
shall separately disclose in its electricity bill:

   (a) The amount of energy purchased or generated;

   (b) The amount of energy sold;

   (c) The technical, non-technical, and administrative losses reported;

   (d) The system-loss charge, if any, separately stated in pesos and as a percentage;
       and

   (e) The ERC order or authority supporting the collection of the charge.

       The ERC shall maintain and publish a current comparative report on the
       system-loss performance of all distribution utilities.

SECTION 10. Prohibited Acts and Sanctions. — A distribution utility, of icer,
employee, or agent that knowingly charges or recovers a system loss prohibited under
this Act, or collects an amount in excess of the amount approved by the ERC, shall be
subject to the administrative penalties, refund orders, and other sanctions authorized
under Republic Act No. 9136, otherwise known as the “Electric Power Industry
Reform Act of 2001,” and other applicable laws.

The imposition of administrative sanctions shall be without prejudice to civil,
criminal, or other administrative liability under existing laws.

SECTION 11. Amendatory Provision. — Section 10 of Republic Act No. 7832, as
amended, is hereby further amended, insofar as applicable, to read as follows:

     “SECTION 10. Rationalization of System Losses. — No private electric utility shall
     recover or pass on any system loss to its consumers.

     “A rural electric cooperative may recover only veri ied and unavoidable technical
     losses, subject to a maximum cap of ive percent (5%) and the conditions,
     veri ication requirements, reduction targets, and phaseout mechanism provided
     under the Electric System Loss Reduction and Consumer Protection Act.

     “Non-technical and administrative losses, including losses attributable to
     pilferage, theft, meter tampering, illegal connections, billing or meter-reading
     errors, collection inef iciency, managerial inef iciency, design defects, and failure
     to maintain or upgrade the distribution system, shall not be recovered from
     consumers.”

Section 43(f) of Republic Act No. 9136 is hereby amended accordingly. The ERC shall
exercise its authority to prescribe system-loss caps consistently with the absolute
prohibition applicable to private electric utilities and the ive-percent (5%) maximum
cap applicable to rural electric cooperatives under this Act.

SECTION 12. Exclusion of Authorized System-Loss Charges from the Value-
Added Tax Base. — The National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, is
hereby amended so that a system-loss charge separately identi ied in the bill of a rural
electric cooperative and approved by the ERC under this Act shall not form part of the
gross receipts or taxable base subject to value-added tax.

The Department of Finance and the Bureau of Internal Revenue shall issue the
necessary rules to implement this section, consistent with the National Internal
Revenue Code, as amended.

SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the ERC, in consultation with the National
Electri ication Administration, rural electric cooperatives, consumer groups, and
other affected stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules necessary for its
implementation.

The rules shall include uniform accounting, measurement, reporting, audit,
consumer-disclosure, refund, and system-loss reduction standards.

SECTION 14. Transitory Provision. — Within six (6) months from the effectivity of
this Act, the ERC shall review and revise all existing orders, rate-setting
methodologies, and approved system-loss caps to conform to this Act.

SECTION 15. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid

and effect.

SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 17. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
