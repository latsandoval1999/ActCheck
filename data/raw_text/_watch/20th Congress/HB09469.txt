PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8187 OR THE PATERNITY LEAVE ACT OF 1996

                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

In recent years, the traditional roles and responsibilities of fathers and mothers around
the world have shifted. Fathers are no longer seen solely as providers and
breadwinners of the families, but as vital partners in child-rearing, while mothers have
increased their participation in the workforce. In the Philippines, this shift has been
seen in households where fathers have assumed the primary parenting roles while
mothers migrate for work.1

Despite these changing dynamics and shifts in traditional gender roles, our legal
framework has not yet fully adopted. This can be seen in Republic Act No. 8187 or the
Paternity Leave Act of 1996, enacted 30 years ago, containing provisions that no
longer reflect modern situations. Under this law, married male employees are granted
seven days of paternity leave for the first four deliveries of the legitimate spouse with
whom he is cohabiting. This excludes fathers who are not married, but wish to support
their partners who have given birth, particularly those in common-law relationships or
long-term cohabitation. The law also limits paternity leaves to the first four deliveries,
unduly limiting support for larger families.

Further, the current number of days of paternity leave does not provide sufficient time
to meaningfully support the mother and newborn during the critical first month. The

 AN ACT EXPANDING PATERNITY LEAVE BENEFITS, AMENDING FOR THIS
PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8187 OR THE PATERNITY LEAVE ACT OF 1996

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Section 2 of Republic Act No. 8187 is hereby amended as follows:

      SECTION 2. Notwithstanding any law, rules and regulations to the
      contrary, every [married] male employee in the private and public
      sectors shall be entitled to a paternity leave of [seven (7)] FIFTEEN
      (15) days with full pay for [the first four (4) deliveries] EVERY
      INSTANCE OF DELIVERY BY [of] the legitimate spouse with whom
      he is cohabiting OR PARTNER WITH WHOM HE IS EXCLUSIVELY
      COHABITING WITHOUT THE BENEFIT OF MARRIAGE. The male
      employee applying for paternity leave shall notify his employer of the
      pregnancy of his legitimate spouse OR THE PARTNER WITH WHOM
      HE IS EXCLUSIVELY COHABITING WITHOUT THE BENEFIT OF
      MARRIAGE and the expected date of such delivery.

      For purposes of this Act, delivery shall include childbirth or any
      miscarriage.

SEC. 2. Repealing Clause. — Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation contrary to or
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed, modified, or amended
accordingly.
