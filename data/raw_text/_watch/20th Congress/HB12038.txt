                                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to require certain business establishments to install and maintain
Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) systems as a measure for crime prevention, public safety,
and the protection of persons and property. It sets clear coverage requirements for covered
business establishments, establishes standards for the operation and retention of CCTV
footage, and provides safeguards for its lawful use and disclosure consistent with the Data
Privacy Act of 2012.

        In August 2026, the Baguio City Police Office reported a sixteen and a half percent
(16.5%) increase in crime incidents from April to July 2026 compared with the same period
in 2025. They cited robbery and theft cases involving business establishments and instances
where the absence of witnesses or evidence made investigations more difficult, calling for a
stricter implementation of the city’s existing “No CCTV, no business permit” ordinance1.
While some local governments already require CCTV systems, the absence of a uniform
national standard results in varying requirements on coverage, operation, retention, and
access to recordings.

        The proposed measure requires covered establishments to install CCTV cameras in
entrances, exits, customer-accessible areas, cash-handling areas, parking areas, loading and
unloading areas, and other strategic locations. It also requires continuous recording and
retention of footage for at least thirty (30) days to preserve footage relevant to an ongoing
investigation, proceeding, or legal claim. Pursuant to Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise
known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”, the bill prohibits surveillance in areas where
individuals have a reasonable expectation of privacy and limits access, use, copying, and
disclosure of CCTV footage to lawful and legitimate purposes.

       CCTV systems cannot prevent every crime, but a properly installed and responsibly
managed system can provide an important record of events when it matters most. The
measure therefore seeks to establish a practical balance between public safety and the right
to privacy, giving businesses a clear responsibility to maintain appropriate security
measures while protecting the personal data captured through surveillance.

          In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                         AN ACT
REQUIRING CERTAIN BUSINESS ESTABLISHMENTS TO INSTALL AND
MAINTAIN CLOSED-CIRCUIT TELEVISION SYSTEMS FOR CRIME
PREVENTION AND PUBLIC SAFETY, AND PROVIDING PENALTIES
THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “CCTV Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby the policy of the State to maintain
peace and order, protect life, liberty, and property, and promote public safety and the
general welfare. The State recognizes Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) systems as a
security measure that may deter criminal activities, assist law enforcement
authorities in crime prevention and investigation, and facilitate the identification of
persons involved in unlawful acts, subject to the protection of the right to privacy. To
this end, the State shall require covered business establishments to install, maintain,
and operate CCTV systems in accordance with this Act.

       SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall apply to business establishments
operating in the Philippines that sell goods or provide services to the general public
and meet any of the following conditions:

         a. A business establishment that employs more than twenty (20)
       employees and occupies premises with a total floor area of at least fifty square
       meters (50 sq. m.); and

          b. A business establishment that employs twenty (20) employees or
       fewer, or occupies premises with a total floor area of less than fifty square
       meters (50 sq. m.) and has gross sales or gross receipts of at least Fifty
       thousand pesos (Php 50,000.00) per day.

      The coverage thresholds shall be determined per business establishment or
physical business location. The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe

the manner of determining employee count, floor area, gross sales or gross receipts,
and the coverage of branches, existing establishments, and newly established
businesses.

        SEC. 4. Installation of CCTV Cameras. – Covered business establishments shall
install and maintain CCTV cameras in good working condition at all entrances,
exits, customer-accessible areas, cash-handling areas, parking areas, loading and
unloading areas, and other strategic locations as may be prescribed by the
implementing rules and regulations.

       CCTV cameras shall operate and record continuously, twenty-four (24) hours
a day, seven (7) days a week. Recordings shall be retained for at least thirty (30)
days. Footage relevant to an ongoing investigation, proceeding, or legal claim shall
be preserved for as long as necessary or as required by law.

      Compliance with this Section shall be a requirement for the issuance or
renewal of the appropriate business permit or permit to operate, subject to the
compliance period under Section 12 of this Act.

       For purposes of this Act, covered business establishments include restaurants,
hospitals, malls, shopping centers, movie houses, theaters, supermarkets, groceries,
entertainment centers, office buildings, warehouses, cockpit arenas, and other
similar establishments.

        SEC. 5. Notice of Surveillance. – The fact that CCTV cameras have been
installed in a business establishment shall be made known to the general public
through a written notice prominently displayed at the entrance of the establishment.

      SEC. 6. Prohibited Surveillance. – The installation or operation of CCTV
cameras in restrooms, toilets, bathrooms, showers, changing rooms, and other areas
where individuals have a reasonable expectation of privacy is prohibited.

       SEC. 7. Confidentiality and Non-disclosure of Video Recordings. – The owner or
manager of a covered business establishment shall ensure the privacy and
confidentiality of all video feeds and recordings obtained under this Act. CCTV
footage shall be accessed, used, disclosed, retained, and disposed of only for lawful
and legitimate purposes, subject to Republic Act (RA) No. 10173, otherwise known
as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”, and its implementing rules and regulations.

       SEC. 8. Allowed Use and Disclosure. – CCTV footage may be accessed, used,
copied, or disclosed only for lawful and legitimate purposes, including the
following:

         a.​ By law enforcement authorities in connection with the investigation or
      prosecution of an offense punishable by law;

          b.​ In connection with an investigation, criminal or civil proceeding, or the
      establishment, exercise, or defense of a legal claim; or



                                          2
         c.​ By a person whose image is captured in the footage or whose person
      or property is involved in an incident recorded therein, for purposes of
      determining whether an offense was committed, identifying the persons
      involved, or establishing the circumstances of the incident.

       It shall be the responsibility of the owner and/or manager to ensure that the
conditions for the use, viewing, copying or disclosure of video feeds and recordings
are reasonably established before giving access to requesting parties. The extent of
video feeds and recordings to be used, viewed, copied, or disclosed shall be limited
to images pertaining to the above-mentioned instances.

       SEC. 9. Penalties. – Any covered business establishment that fails to comply
with the requirements of this Act or its implementing rules and regulations shall,
after due notice and opportunity to be heard, be subject to an administrative fine of
not less than Fifty thousand pesos (Php 50,000.00) but not more than Two hundred
thousand pesos (Php 200,000.00), depending on the gravity and frequency of the
violation.

       Repeated or continuing violations may, after due notice and opportunity to be
heard, constitute grounds for the suspension or revocation of the business permit or
permit to operate, in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

       The owner and/or manager of the covered business establishment shall be
held administratively liable for violations of this Act when the violation is
attributable to their direct participation, lack of supervision, or negligence.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed to limit or diminish any civil, criminal,
or administrative liability arising from the unlawful access, use, disclosure,
processing, or disposal of personal data obtained through CCTV systems under RA
No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”, and other applicable
laws.

      SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of the Interior
and Local Government (DILG) shall lead the implementation of this Act and, within
ninety (90) days from its effectivity, promulgate the necessary implementing rules
and regulations in consultation with the National Privacy Commission, the
Department of Information and Communications Technology, the Philippine
National Police, concerned local government units, and other relevant stakeholders.

       SEC. 11. Compliance Period. – Existing covered business establishments shall
comply with the requirements of this Act within one (1) year from the effectivity of
its implementing rules and regulations. Covered business establishments established
after the effectivity of this Act shall comply with its requirements before
commencing operations.
