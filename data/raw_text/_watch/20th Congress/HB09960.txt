                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to provide free tertiary agricultural education in State
Universities and Colleges (SUCs) to the dependent children of qualified indigent
farmers, along with educational support mechanisms to boost participation in
agricultural careers and strengthen the country’s food security.

       Agriculture employs millions of Filipinos yet remains plagued by an aging
workforce, youth disinterest, and deep rural poverty. The Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA), recorded a 27% poverty incidence among farmers in 2023 which
goes well above the national average. This reality limits the ability of many farming
households to send their children to college. Meanwhile, low enrollment in
agriculture-related programs has created a shortage of professionals vital to the
sector’s modernization.

       Although Republic Act No. 10931, otherwise known as the Universal Access
to Quality Tertiary Education Act, covers free tuition and other school fees in SUCs,
many children of indigent farmers continue to encounter financial constraints. These
constraints include but are not limited to transportation, books, instructional
materials, daily subsistence, and other educational expenses. As a result, many
farming families remain trapped in a cycle of poverty and limited economic
mobility.

       This measure seeks to empower farming households by providing targeted
educational assistance to the dependent children of qualified indigent farmers who
choose to pursue agriculture and related fields. The end goal is to aid harmer
households to produce agriculturists, veterinarians, and agribusiness professionals
that the country needs.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                         AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR FREE TERTIARY AGRICULTURAL EDUCATION AND
OTHER RELATED COURSES TO THE DEPENDENT CHILDREN OF
QUALIFIED INDIGENT FARMERS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Free Agri-Education for
Farmers’ Dependents Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote and protect the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and to
take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all. Recognizing the
pivotal role of agriculture in national development and food security, and the
socio-economic challenges faced by indigent farmers, the State shall prioritize the
empowerment of the agricultural sector through human capital development.
Towards this end, the State shall provide free tertiary agricultural education and
other related courses to the dependent children of qualified indigent farmers,
thereby ensuring a continuous supply of skilled and knowledgeable professionals
and practitioners in agriculture, promoting sustainable farming practices, and
enhancing the overall productivity and competitiveness of the Philippine
agricultural sector.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:​

           a. Dependent Child refers to a legitimate, illegitimate, legally adopted, or
       stepchild of a qualified indigent farmer, who is twenty-one (21) years of age
       or below, unmarried, and not gainfully employed, or if over twenty-one (21)
       years of age, is suffering from a disability that renders them incapable of
       self-support;​

          b. Free Tertiary Agricultural Education refers to the waiver of tuition fees,
       miscellaneous fees, and other mandatory fees, as may be determined by the

                                            1
       Commission on Higher Education (CHED), in State Universities and Colleges
       (SUCs) offering agricultural education and other related courses;​

           c.​ Indigent Farmer refers to a natural person engaged in farming,
       whether personally or through a farm worker, whose family income falls
       below the poverty threshold as determined by the Philippine Statistics
       Authority (PSA), and who is registered with the Department of Agriculture's
       Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA) or any equivalent
       official registry;​

           d.​ Qualified refers to a dependent child who meets the eligibility criteria
       set forth in this Act and its Implementing Rules and Regulations;​

          e.​ Related Courses refer to academic programs closely related to
       agriculture, such as, but not limited to, agricultural engineering, food
       technology, veterinary medicine, forestry, fisheries, environmental science
       with agricultural focus, and agribusiness management, as may be determined
       by the CHED, in consultation with the Department of Agriculture (DA); and​

           f.​ State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) refer to public higher education
       institutions established by law and are fully supported by the national
       government.

      SEC. 4. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all qualified dependent children of
indigent farmers who are enrolled or shall enroll in tertiary agricultural education
and other related courses in any SUC in the Philippines.

       SEC. 5. Entitlements. – A qualified dependent child of an indigent farmer shall
be entitled to the following benefits:

          a.​ Exemption from the payment of tuition fees, laboratory fees, library
       fees, athletic fees, computer fees, cultural fees, and all other miscellaneous
       and mandatory fees, as may be determined by CHED, throughout the
       duration of their tertiary agricultural education or related course in an SUC;
       and​

           b.​ Subject to the availability of funds and the guidelines to be prescribed
       in the Implementing Rules and Regulations, qualified beneficiaries may be
       granted a monthly stipend, book allowance, transportation allowance, and
       such other educational assistance as may be necessary to support the
       completion of their studies.

       SEC. 6. Qualifications of Beneficiaries. – To qualify for the benefits under this
Act, a dependent must:​

           1.​ Be a dependent child of an indigent farmer as defined in Section 3 of
       this Act;​



                                           2
         2.​ Be a Filipino citizen;​

         3.​ Have passed the entrance examination or admission requirements of
      the SUC where they intend to enroll or are currently enrolled;​

         4.​ Maintain the academic standards and good moral character as
      required by the SUC and the provisions of this Act; and​

         5.​ Not be a recipient of any other scholarship grant or financial assistance
      from any government agency or private institution that covers the same
      entitlements provided under this Act, unless the other grant is insufficient to
      cover all expenses and only the uncovered portion is claimed under this Act.​

      SEC. 7. Responsibilities of Beneficiaries. – Beneficiaries of this Act shall:​

          a.​ Maintain the academic performance required by the SUC for their
      course Failure to meet the academic requirements may lead to the forfeiture
      of the scholarship, subject to the policies of the SUC and the Implementing
      Rules and Regulations;​

         b.​ Complete their chosen course within the prescribed period; and​

         c.​ Upon graduation, render service to the agricultural sector in the
      Philippines for a period equivalent to the number of years they enjoyed the
      scholarship, in a manner and capacity to be determined by the DA in
      consultation with the CHED. This may include working in government
      agricultural agencies, engaging in farming, or contributing to agricultural
      research or extension services. The Implementing Rules and Regulations shall
      provide the specific guidelines for this service requirement.​

      SEC. 8. Implementing Agencies. – The CHED and DA shall be the primary
implementing agencies of this Act. They shall:​

         a.​ Formulate and issue the necessary Implementing Rules and
      Regulations (IRR) within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act;​

         b.​ Develop a comprehensive monitoring and evaluation system to ensure
      the effective implementation of this Act;​

        c.​ Coordinate with State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) to ensure the
      smooth admission, enrollment, and support of qualified beneficiaries; and​

         d.​ Establish a robust database of qualified indigent farmers and their
      dependent children, in coordination with relevant government agencies and
      local government units.​

       SEC. 9. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. The CHED and
DA shall jointly prepare the necessary budget proposals for this purpose.

       SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the CHED, in consultation with the DA, the Philippine
Association of State Universities and Colleges (PASUC), and other relevant
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
