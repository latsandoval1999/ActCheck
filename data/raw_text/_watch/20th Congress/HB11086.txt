                       AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF AGRICULTURAL
DEVELOPMENT WORKERS, STRENGTHENING THEIR WELFARE,
LABOR PROTECTION, AND PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT, AND
FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 18, Article II of the 1987 Constitution recognizes labor as a
primary social economic force and mandates the State to protect the rights of
workers and promote their welfare. Section 3, Article XIII further guarantees the
rights of all workers to just and humane conditions of work, security of tenure,
and a living wage, and promotes full employment and equality of employment
opportunities. Complementarily, Section 5, Article XIII directs the State to
provide support to agriculture through appropriate technology and research, and
adequate financial, production, marketing, and other support services. These
provisions underscore the State’s duty not only to strengthen the agricultural
sector but also to protect and develop the workforce that sustains it.

      Agricultural development workers, who include agriculturists, engineers,
technicians, extension workers, and support personnel, serve as the backbone of
agricultural productivity, extension services, and rural transformation. While
farmers and fisherfolk remain at the forefront of food production, agricultural
development workers play an equally critical role in sustaining and strengthening
the sector by providing the technical expertise, research support, extension
services, and institutional capacity necessary for modern, efficient, and climate-
resilient agriculture. They are indispensable in translating policies into practice,
supporting communities on the ground, and ensuring that agricultural programs
are effectively implemented across the country.

      Despite their indispensable role, agricultural development workers
continue to face structural challenges, including inadequate compensation,
limited career advancement opportunities, unsafe working conditions, and
inconsistent labor protection across institutions and local government units.

      The devolution of agricultural services under Republic Act No. 7160,
otherwise known as the “Local Government Code of 1991,” has resulted in
uneven capacity and disparities in the delivery of agricultural services across local
government units. This situation has been further shaped by the landmark ruling
in Mandanas v. Ochoa,1 which expanded the fiscal resources of local
governments by increasing their share in national taxes. While this development
enhances the capacity of LGUs to deliver devolved services, it likewise
underscores the need to strengthen institutional systems, standardize staffing, and
ensure adequate protection and professionalization of agricultural development
workers at the local level.

      While Republic Act No. 12215, otherwise known as the “Philippine
Agriculturists Act,” and other professional laws regulate licensure and
professional standards, there remains a significant policy gap in addressing the
employment conditions, welfare, labor rights, and occupational safety of
agricultural development workers across the public and private sectors.

       This proposed Magna Carta seeks to address these gaps by
institutionalizing a comprehensive framework that ensures just compensation,
wage protection, security of tenure, safe and humane working conditions, and
occupational safety and health protection, while strengthening the
professionalization of agricultural services, particularly at the local government
level.

       The measure further integrates labor standards, human resource
development mechanisms, and inter-agency coordination, recognizing that the
effectiveness of agricultural programs depends not only on policy and funding
but also on the competence, protection, and motivation of the workforce.

      Ultimately, this bill aims to strengthen the foundation of agricultural
development by investing in the people who drive it. By protecting and

                              ARTICLE I
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta of
Agricultural Development Workers.”

       Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to recognize agricultural development workers as vital partners in food
security, countryside development, agricultural modernization, and rural
transformation.

      The State shall promote their welfare by ensuring just compensation, wage
protection, security of tenure, safe and humane working conditions, occupational

                                                                                4
safety and health protection, professional development, and appropriate career
advancement.

      Section 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

      a. Agricultural Development Workers refer to persons engaged in
         agricultural development, extension, technical, research, regulatory,
         administrative, support, and related services in the Department of
         Agriculture (DA), its bureaus and attached agencies, local government
         units, state universities and colleges, government-owned and controlled
         corporations, non-government organizations, cooperatives, and private
         institutions.
             This shall include agriculturists, agricultural and biosystems
         engineers, fishery technologists, veterinarians, agricultural technicians,
         agricultural extension workers, agricultural employees, administrative
         and support personnel, and contractual, casual, job order, seasonal, or
         project-based personnel engaged in agricultural development services.
      b. Agriculturist refers to a person qualified and authorized to practice the
         agriculture profession in accordance with Republic Act No. 12215,
         otherwise known as the “Philippine Agriculturists Act,” and other
         applicable laws.
      c. Agricultural and Biosystems Engineer, Fishery Technologist, and
         Veterinarian refer to persons authorized to practice their respective
         professions under existing laws.
      d. Agricultural Technician refers to a trained or skilled worker certified by
         Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) or
         other competent authority who performs technical work related to
         agricultural production, processing, machinery operation, post-harvest
         handling, marketing, or related services.
      e. Agricultural Extension Worker refers to an agricultural development
         worker who provides technical assistance, training, field support, or
         extension services to farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives, and agricultural
         communities.
      f. Occupational Safety and Health (OSH) refers to policies, standards, and
         programs intended to prevent occupational injury, illness, and hazards
         in agricultural development work.

                                                                                 5
      Section 4. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all agricultural development
workers in the public and private sectors, regardless of employment status,
including permanent, temporary, casual, contractual, job order, seasonal, and
project-based workers engaged in agricultural development services.

                       ARTICLE II
      EMPLOYMENT, PROFESSIONALIZATION, AND CAREER
                     DEVELOPMENT

      Section 5. Recruitment and Qualifications. – The selection and
appointment of agricultural development workers shall be based on merit, fitness,
competence, integrity, and applicable civil service, labor, professional, and
technical qualifications.

      Positions requiring professional licensure shall be filled in accordance with
the applicable professional regulatory laws, including Republic Act No. 12215 for
agriculturists.

      Section 6. Employment Assistance. – The Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE), in coordination with the DA, Professional Regulation
Commission (PRC), TESDA, Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), and
academic institutions, shall promote employment opportunities for agricultural
development workers in the domestic and foreign labor markets.
      For this purpose, a National Employment Program for Agricultural
Development Workers shall be formulated to improve job matching, career
placement, skills upgrading, and employment facilitation.

       Section 7. Professionalization of Government Agricultural Services. –
The DA, in coordination with the Civil Service Commission (CSC), Department
of Budget and Management (DBM), Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), and recognized national associations of agricultural
development workers, shall review the existing functions, responsibilities,
position titles, qualifications, staffing patterns, and compensation of agricultural
development workers in government service.
       The review shall recommend appropriate reclassification, upgrading, or
standardization of positions to ensure just compensation, proper career
progression, and professionalized agricultural services.
                                                                                  6
       Section 8. Mandatory Positions of Agricultural Development Workers in
Local Government Units. – In addition to positions provided under existing laws,
local government units shall, subject to their needs, income classification, and
applicable civil service and budget rules, create and maintain appropriate
agricultural development positions, including:
       a. Provincial Agriculturist;
       b. Provincial Agricultural and Biosystems Engineer;
       c. Provincial Fishery Officer;
       d. Provincial Veterinarian, where applicable;
       e. City Agriculturist;
       f. City Agricultural and Biosystems Engineer;
       g. City Veterinarian, where applicable;
       h. Municipal Agriculturist; and,
       i. Other technical, extension, and support positions necessary for effective
          agricultural services.
       The DA, CSC, DBM, and DILG shall issue staffing standards for
agricultural development services in local government units.

       Section 9. Performance Evaluation and Merit Promotion. – The DA, in
consultation with the CSC, DOLE, local government units, and recognized
national associations of agricultural development workers, shall prepare a uniform
career and personnel development framework for agricultural development
workers.
       Such framework shall include merit promotion, performance evaluation,
training, scholarships, incentives, and awards systems.

       Section 10. Transfer or Geographic Reassignment. – No transfer or
geographic reassignment of an agricultural development worker shall be made
without prior written notice stating the reason therefor.
       Such transfer or reassignment shall be subject to existing civil service and
labor laws, rules on security of tenure, and the right of the worker to appeal before
the proper authority.

       Section 11. Security of Tenure. – Agricultural development workers shall
enjoy security of tenure in accordance with the Constitution, civil service laws,
labor laws, and applicable rules and regulations.
                                                                                   7
      No agricultural development worker shall be dismissed, suspended, or
separated from service except for just or authorized cause and after observance of
due process.

       Section 12. Assignment of Married Agricultural Development Workers.
– Whenever practicable, proper authorities shall take steps to allow married
agricultural development workers, both of whom are in government service, to be
employed or assigned in the same or adjacent city or municipality, subject to
existing laws and regulations.

      Section 13. Code of Conduct. – Agricultural development workers shall be
guided by a Code of Conduct to be formulated by the DA, in consultation with the
CSC, DOLE, PRC, and concerned stakeholders.
      For registered professionals, such Code shall be consistent with existing
professional codes of ethics and regulatory standards issued by the appropriate
professional regulatory boards.

                      ARTICLE III
   WAGE PROTECTION, HOURS OF WORK, AND LABOR RIGHTS

       Section 14. Wage Protection. – Agricultural development workers shall be
entitled to fair and just compensation.
       No agricultural development worker shall receive wages below the
applicable minimum wage or compensation lower than that prescribed under
existing salary standardization, civil service, labor, and wage laws.
       The DOLE, in coordination with the DA and DBM, shall periodically
review wage levels and compensation conditions of agricultural development
workers and recommend measures to address wage gaps, underpayment, and
inequities.

      Section 15. Normal Hours of Work. – The normal hours of work of
agricultural development workers shall not exceed eight (8) hours a day or forty
(40) hours a week, subject to existing civil service and labor laws.
      Hours of work shall include time during which the worker is required to be
on duty, at a prescribed workplace, in the field, or in another place required by
the employer or office.

                                                                                8
      Section 16. Overtime Work. – When exigencies of service require work
beyond normal working hours, agricultural development workers shall be entitled
to overtime pay, compensatory time-off, service credits, or other applicable
compensation in accordance with existing laws and rules.

       Section 17. Protection against Labor Exploitation. – Agricultural
development workers shall be protected against underpayment, delayed payment
of wages, excessive workload, unsafe working conditions, arbitrary
reassignment, unjust dismissal, and other unfair labor practices.
       The DOLE shall enforce labor standards for private sector workers, while
the CSC shall enforce applicable standards for government workers.

       Section 18. Personnel Required and Staffing Standards. – Government
offices, local government units, firms, cooperatives, organizations, and
establishments engaged in agricultural and fisheries development shall employ or
engage the necessary number of qualified agricultural development workers
appropriate to the nature and scope of their operations.
       Staff-to-clientele ratios and staffing standards shall be determined by the
DA, CSC, DBM, DOLE, and DILG.

                          ARTICLE IV
                OCCUPATIONAL SAFETY AND HEALTH

       Section 19. Occupational Safety and Health Standards. – Agricultural
development workers shall be entitled to safe and healthful working conditions.
       The DOLE, in coordination with the DA and concerned agencies, shall
formulate or update occupational safety and health standards specific to
agricultural development work, including field deployment, laboratory work,
exposure to pesticides and chemicals, handling of animals and biological
materials, operation of machinery, disaster response, and work in remote,
isolated, or conflict-affected areas.

      Section 20. Personal Protective Equipment and Safety Measures. –
Employers, offices, and institutions shall provide agricultural development
workers with appropriate personal protective equipment, safety devices, training,
and information necessary for the safe performance of their duties.

                                                                                9
      No agricultural development worker shall be required to perform
hazardous work without appropriate protective equipment and safety protocols.

       Section 21. Hazard Allowance. – Agricultural development workers
assigned to hazardous, difficult, isolated, distressed, strife-torn, calamity-
affected, or high-risk areas, or those exposed to chemicals, biological agents,
dangerous machinery, animal diseases, field hazards, laboratory risks, or other
occupational risks, shall be entitled to hazard allowance in accordance with
existing laws, rules, and regulations.

      Section 22. Medical Examination, Insurance, and Work-Related Injury
Protection. – Agricultural development workers shall be entitled to annual
medical examinations, appropriate immunization when warranted, and insurance
or compensation for work-related injury, illness, disability, or death in accordance
with existing laws.

                             ARTICLE V
                  RIGHTS, BENEFITS, AND INCENTIVES

       Section 23. Freedom from Interference or Coercion. – It shall be
unlawful for any person to interfere with, restrain, coerce, intimidate, or
discriminate against agricultural development workers in the exercise of their
rights, including the right to organize.

       Section 24. Non-Discrimination. – No agricultural development worker
shall be discriminated against by reason of sex, gender, age, civil status,
disability, religion, ethnicity, political belief, union affiliation, employment
status, or other analogous grounds.

       Section 25. Right to Join Organizations. – Agricultural development
workers shall have the right to form, join, or assist organizations, associations, or
unions for lawful purposes.
       Recognized organizations of agricultural development workers shall be
consulted in the formulation of policies, programs, standards, and reforms
affecting agricultural services and workers’ welfare.



                                                                                  10
      Section 26. Incentives and Rewards System. – The DA, in coordination
with the DBM, CSC, DOLE, local government units, and recognized workers’
organizations, shall establish an incentives and rewards system for agricultural
development workers.
      Such system may include honoraria, performance incentives, awards,
recognition, productivity incentives, and other benefits consistent with existing
laws and budget rules.

      Section 27. Other Compensation and Benefits. – Agricultural
development workers may be entitled, as applicable and subject to existing laws
and regulations, to the following:
      a. Representation and transportation allowances;
      b. Subsistence or field allowances;
      c. Clothing allowance;
      d. Housing or quarters when assigned in remote or field stations, or
         reasonable housing support when quarters are unavailable;
      e. Compensation for work-related injury, illness, disability, or death;
      f. Annual medical examination;
      g. On-call pay or equivalent compensation for emergency duty;
      h. Transportation, motorcycle, or vehicle loan programs, subject to
         availability of funds;
      i. Longevity or retention incentives; and,
      j. Other benefits authorized by law.

                           ARTICLE VI
                  HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT

       Section 28. Training and Continuing Professional Development. – The
DA, in coordination with the DOLE, PRC, Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, DILG, and
local government units, shall formulate and implement comprehensive training
and human resource development programs for agricultural development
workers.
       For registered professionals, such programs shall be aligned with the
Continuing Professional Development requirements and regulatory framework
under applicable professional laws, including Republic Act No. 12215 for
agriculturists.
                                                                              11
      Training conducted by the Agricultural Training Institute and other
accredited training providers may be considered for crediting under applicable
CPD rules, subject to approval by the Professional Regulation Commission and
the appropriate professional regulatory board.

      Section 29. Scholarship and Capacity-Building Program. – Agricultural
development workers and qualified students pursuing agriculture, agricultural
and biosystems engineering, fisheries, veterinary medicine, and related programs
shall have access to government scholarship, training, and capacity-building
programs, subject to existing laws and regulations.

      Section 30. Capability Assessment and Human Resource Development
Study. – The Department of Agriculture, in coordination with concerned
agencies, shall periodically conduct a capability assessment and human resource
development study covering:
      a. Staffing needs and personnel-to-clientele ratios;
      b. Adequacy of facilities, equipment, and supplies;
      c. Working conditions and occupational safety risks;
      d. Training and professional development needs;
      e. Career advancement and compensation concerns;
      f. Regional and local consultations on workers’ welfare;
      g. Standards for agricultural extension services; and
      h. Measures to retain and effectively utilize agricultural development
          workers for national development.
      The results shall be submitted to Congress every five (5) years, together
with policy and budget recommendations.

                      CHAPTER VII
    ADMINISTRATION, CONSULTATION, AND ENFORCEMENT

      Section 31. Agricultural Development Workers Consultative Council. –
There is hereby created an Agricultural Development Workers Consultative
Council to advise the Department of Agriculture on matters affecting the welfare,
professionalization, labor protection, occupational safety, and career
development of agricultural development workers.



                                                                              12
      The Council shall include representatives from the DA, DOLE, CSC,
DBM, DILG, PRC, local government units, and recognized national
organizations of agricultural development workers.

       Section 32. Monitoring and Compliance. – The DA shall serve as the lead
agency for the implementation of this Act.
       The DOLE shall enforce labor standards and occupational safety and health
requirements in the private sector. The CSC shall enforce applicable civil service
rules for government personnel. Local government units shall ensure compliance
within their respective jurisdictions.

       Section 33. Penalties. – Any person who willfully interferes with,
restrains, coerces, discriminates against, or violates the rights of agricultural
development workers under this Act shall be penalized in accordance with
applicable civil service, labor, administrative, and criminal laws.
       Where the offender is a public officer, administrative liability may include
suspension, dismissal, or disqualification from public office, as may be warranted
under existing laws.

      Section 34. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the
provisions of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
      Local government units may also allocate funds from local revenues for
the implementation of this Act, subject to existing budgeting and auditing rules.

       Section 35. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DA, in coordination with the DOLE,
CSC, DBM, DILG, PRC, TESDA, CHED, and recognized national organizations
of agricultural development workers, shall promulgate the rules and regulations
necessary to implement this Act.

      Section 36. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      Section 37. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances,
decrees, rules, and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of
this Act are deemed amended, modified or repealed accordingly.

       Section 38. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
