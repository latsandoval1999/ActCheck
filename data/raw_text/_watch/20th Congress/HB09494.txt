                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The growing demand for transparent, responsive, and technology-driven
governance has underscored the urgent need to modernize government systems
and eliminate outdated bureaucratic processes that burden citizens and businesses
alike. Despite the enactment of reforms on ease of doing business and anti-red
tape measures, many government transactions remain slow, repetitive, costly, and
dependent on obsolete documentary requirements. These inefficiencies not only
inconvenience the public but also create opportunities for corruption, discourage
investments, and undermine public trust in institutions. In an era where digital
transformation has become indispensable, there is a compelling necessity to
establish a more integrated, accessible, and citizen-centered system of
governance.

       The measure aims to simplify government procedures, reduce redundant
requirements, promote digitalization, and streamline citizen and business
transactions. Among its salient features are the removal of obsolete documentary
requirements such as the community tax certificate or cedula, the establishment
of interoperable and fully digital government systems, the promotion of online
applications and electronic payments, and the integration of government
databases and social service records.
         The bill likewise strengthens accountability and transparency in public
  service by encouraging process reengineering, reducing opportunities for graft
  and corruption, and improving the accessibility and inclusiveness of government
  services, particularly for ordinary citizens, small businesses, and beneficiaries of
  social welfare programs.

        Moreover, the proposed measure complements existing national initiatives
  on ease of doing business, electronic governance, and public sector
  modernization. By institutionalizing reforms that prioritize efficiency,
  transparency, and service delivery, the State can foster greater public confidence
  in government institutions while enhancing national competitiveness and
  economic productivity.

         Finally, we would like to recognize Rep. Joey Sarte Salceda of the 2nd
  District of Albay for filing a similar measure during the 19 th Congress.


        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

                              CHAPTER ONE
                           GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Clean and Efficient
Government Act”.


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is declared the policy of the State to promote
a high standard of ethics and efficiency in public service. Public offices, officials
and employees shall at all times be accountable to the people and shall discharge
their duties with utmost efficiency, responsibility, integrity, competence, and
loyalty.
SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act aims to:
      a) Modernize the delivery of local and national government services and
         remove redundant requirements and processes;
      b) Build the capacities of government offices to promote efficient service
         delivery;
      c) Initiate a Government Transformation Program (GTP) to modernize the
         most essential government services and processes;
      d) Improve ease of doing business by reducing business requirement
         processing time, bureaucratic red tape and discretion, and transaction
         costs to enterprises;
      e) Simplify government procedures and make them more inclusive and
         accessible;
      f) Allow fully remote processing of government transactions wherever
         allowed by law;
      g) Simplify the requirements for beneficiaries of social welfare programs;
      h) Improve the monitoring and evaluation of government modernization,
         anti-red tape measures, and process simplification programs;
      i) Improve the efficient and transparency of essential social programs
         such as healthcare;
      j) Promote the digitalization of government processes and transactions;
      k) Encourage compliance with government requirements; and
      l) Improve the standard and quality of life of citizens by enhancing public
         service delivery.

                           CHAPTER THREE
                   EASE OF BUSINESS REGISTRATION

SEC. 8. Single office for business registration. – There is hereby created, under
the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) an Enterprise Registration Authority
(ERA) which shall perform the following functions:
      a) Administer, in cooperation with the Department of Information and
         Communication Technology (DICT), the National Registry of
         Businesses;
      b) Create and administer a centralized online business registration
         platform that shall receive all business application requirements;
      c) Forward business application requirements received through its online
         registration facility and facilitate their processing with relevant
         government offices, including local government units;
      d) Set up, within the business registration platform, an electronic payment
         system for paying business registration charges and fees;
      e) Update registration applicants about the status of requirements
         forwarded with relevant government agencies;
      f) Within one day, issue tentative licenses to operate to applicants which
         have completed submitting all registration requirements pending final
         approval and do not require special permits and licenses to safeguard
         public safety, public order, public health, or public morals;
      g) Work with Negosyo Centers to set up on-site facilities where business
         registration through the online business registration platform can be
                                                                                 6
         conducted, for the benefit of applicants who, for whatever reason, are
         unable to access the online facility;
      h) Recommend the elimination of redundant processes in the business
         registration process; and
      i) Perform such other functions as may be necessary or as are inherent in
         role as the central business registration platform and agency of the
         government.


      As far as practicable, the online business registration platform which the
ERA is mandated to administer shall have a user interface that can be navigated
by the applicant without professional assistance.


      The funds required to create the office, hire personnel, and perform its
functions shall be sourced from the DTI’s existing funds for the current fiscal
year, after which, appropriations for the ERA shall be made in the General
Appropriations Act (GAA). The Authority shall be headed by a Director-General
which shall have the rank and privileges of an Assistant Secretary.


SEC. 9. Removal of redundant permits and licenses. – To effectively establish
a one-day, one-office business registration process, the Anti-Red Tape Authority
(ARTA) and the DTI shall conduct a thorough review and time-and-motion study
of the business registration process and recommend the removal of redundant
permits and licenses.


      The review and study shall be submitted to Congress within ninety (90)
days after the effectivity of this Act. The DTI and ARTA shall also propose to
the President executive action such as Executive Orders and Administrative



                                                                               7
Orders, whenever the removal of redundant permits and licenses do not require
Acts of Congress.


SEC. 10. National Registry of Businesses. – There shall be established, under
the administration of the ERA created under Section 5 of this Act, a National
Registry of Businesses, which shall be the centralized business registration
platform of the government. Within two (2) years upon the effectivity of this Act,
all business registration applications shall be processed and stored through the
Registry.


       Each duly completed business application shall have one unified record in
the Registry. The Record shall contain all of the completed applications
registration requirements, licenses and permits obtained, and other mandatory
disclosures and filings. The files and disclosures held by the ERA shall be subject
to the laws, rules, and regulations governing the privacy of information contained
in such documents.


       As it requires complete registration with all relevant government agencies,
the issuance of a Certification of Registration by the ERA, through the National
Registry of Business, shall be considered a valid proof of duly completed business
registration.

SEC. 12. Portability of Social Services. – It shall be the objective of national
government agencies to ensure that the receipt of national social services shall be
delinked from venue requirements. The Performance Management and Delivery
Office, created under Section 16 of this Act, shall formulate a comprehensive
report to Congress and the President identifying government services that require
the beneficiary to be physically present in a certain locality, and propose changes
in government regulations and practices to ensure that beneficiaries of social
services can access their social benefits conveniently in any locality in the
country.


      Once its recommendations are approved by the Cabinet, the Performance
Management and Delivery Office shall monitor the implementation of its
proposals, and perform functions and powers given to it under Section 16 of this
Act to ensure that identified government services are delinked from venue
requirements.

through the mechanisms created pursuant to this Section as an option for the
payment of all local government fees and charges. The DICT, DILG, and ARTA
shall monitor compliance with this paragraph.

SEC. 21. Congressional Oversight Committee on Government Modernization.
– There shall be created a Congressional Oversight Committee on Government
Modernization, which is a standing joint committee of Congress which shall be
composed of five (5) members and one co-chair from the Senate, and five (5)
members and one co-chair from the House of Representatives.


       The minority of both the Senate and the House shall be represented
proportionally in the Oversight Committee, and shall have at least one (1)
representative in each delegation.


       The Oversight Committee shall convene at least once every session to
oversee the implementation of this Act. The Oversight Committee shall receive a
copy of all reports mandated to be accomplished in this Act.


       The Oversight Committee shall adopt its own rules and procedures, in
accordance with the Rules of the Senate and the House of Representatives. The
Oversight Committee shall exercise subpoena powers requiring a nominee or
witness to testify at its hearing or for the taking of his deposition or to bring books,
documents, or other things under his control. The subpoena shall be signed, under
the seal of the Oversight Committee, by the co-chairs of the Committee. The

                                                                                     19
subpoena shall state the subject matter under inquiry and the nominee or witness
whose attendance is required, and in case of a subpoena duces tecum, shall

                      CHAPTER SIX
       POWERS TO REENGINEER GOVERNMENT PROCESSES

SEC. 22. Authority of the President to Cause the Reengineering of Government
Processes. – The President is hereby authorized to direct the Civil Service
Commission (CSC), the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the
National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) to craft guidelines and
general principles of a whole-of-government reengineering of the processes for
the processing of issuance of national and local permits, licenses, and
certifications issued by the Executive Branch and by Local Government Units,
except those which are explicitly required under law.


      For highly technical transactions specifically mandated by existing laws,
the President may direct the CSC and DBM to authorize the augmentation of
personnel performing tasks directly related to the processing and issuance of such
licenses, permits, and certifications, whenever the CSC and DBM find the
necessity for such augmentation.

SEC. 24. Power to Suspend or Remove Personnel in the Executive Branch. –
Consistent with Article VII, Sections 17 and 18 of the Constitution, the Revised
Administrative Code, other existing laws, and jurisprudence, the President shall
have the authority to suspend or remove, upon determination of a competent
body, any government official or employee performing acts contrary to the
preceding section.


SEC. 25. Power to Direct the Anti-Red Tape Authority to Recommend
Capacitation. – The Anti-Red Tape Authority (ARTA) is hereby given the
additional mandate of recommending structural and personnel augmentation,
and/or reorganization reforms whenever it is determined that the cause of delays
in the issuance of permits, licenses, and certifications by a relevant agency is a
systematic or structural issue. The Department of Budget and Management
(DBM) is mandated to study and comment on ARTA recommendations pursuant
to this Section and whenever deemed meritorious and as may be relevant and
appropriate to cause the inclusion of the relevant recommendations to the
immediately succeeding National Expenditure Plan.

                                                                               21
SEC. 26. Study and Report to Reduce Basic Business Registration Process. –
The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), the
ARTA and other relevant agencies are directed to formulate a joint study and
report to the Congress and the President on the elimination of redundant business
registration requirements, with the aim of reducing business registration to just
one (1) working day. The ARTA shall be the coordinator of efforts to complete
the joint study and report.


      The feasibility of a single business entities registration agency as the
receiving office and clearing house of registration requirements shall also be
included in the joint study and report.


      The joint study and report shall be submitted to Congress and the President
within ninety (90) days upon the effectivity of this Act.


SEC. 27. Power of the President to Repeal or Amend Rules and Regulations
which Preclude Agency of Process Digitalization. – Notwithstanding any law to
the contrary, the President shall have the power to repeal or amend any rule,
regulation, or issuance issued by any agency or instrumentality under the
Executive Branch, which prevents any agency or office from making their
processes compatible with the digital space or which makes compulsory the
physical appearance of the client to complete the processing and/or issuance of
any license, permit, or certification.


SEC. 28. Power to Direct the Commissioner of Internal Revenue and the
Commissioner of Customs. – Notwithstanding any law to the contrary, the

                                                                               22
President shall have the power to direct the Commissioner of Internal Revenue
and the Commissioner of Customs to issue such regulations and perform such
tasks as may be necessary to expedite the processing of tax filings and payments,
and to enhance taxpayer experience.


SEC. 29. Study and Report to Integrate the National Identification System with
Other Mandatory Registrations. – The National Economic and Development
Authority (NEDA) shall formulate a comprehensive study of all mandatory social
contributions, registrations, and other required forms of identification and
registration which may be subsumed into the Philippine Identification System
established under Republic Act No. 11055 or the Philippine Identification System
Act (PhilSys Act). The study shall include recommendations to remove redundant
registration and identification requirements.


       The initial findings of the study shall be submitted to Congress and the
President within ninety (90) days upon the effectivity of this Act. The NEDA
shall make subsequent reports to Congress and the President every thirty (30)
days thereafter, and the final report shall be submitted to Congress and the
President within one hundred eighty (180) days after the effectivity date of this
Act.


SEC. 30. Study and Report on the Digitization of Databases for Basic Social
Services. – The Department of Information and Communication Technology
(DICT)    shall formulate     a comprehensive study of information           and
communication technology-based solutions to minimize fraud and strengthen
data management and administration of mandatory social programs administered
by such agencies as the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation and the Social
Security System.

                                                                               23
      The DICT shall also seek the definitive opinion of the National Privacy
Commission (NPC) on the measures that can be taken to facilitate the sharing and
integration of databases among government agencies to facilitate the targeting
and distribution of social services.


      The initial findings of the study shall be submitted to Congress and the
President within ninety (90) days upon the effectivity of this Act. The DICT shall
make the subsequent reports to Congress and the President every thirty (30) days
thereafter, and the final report shall be submitted to Congress and the President
within one hundred eight (180) days after the effectivity date of this Act.


      The President may also request the DICT to prioritize the consideration of
specific social services in the conduct of the study.


                              CHAPTER SEVEN
                             OTHER PROVISIONS

SEC. 31. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be held
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not otherwise affected shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 32. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders or issuances or any part
thereof, which are inconsistent herewith, are hereby repealed or amended
accordingly.


SEC. 33. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
