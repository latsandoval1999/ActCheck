                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Barangay Tanods serve as our unsung heroes in the grassroots. They play a vital role
as frontliners in maintaining peace and order and ensuring public safety in our communities.
Despite their crucial role, they are still considered as volunteer with honoraria and allowances
rather than fixed monthly wages with benefits.

Article II, Section 5 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that the maintenance of peace
and order, the protection of life, liberty, and property, and the promotion of the general
welfare are essential for the enjoyment by all the people of the blessings of democracy. The
Barangay Tanods are the direct realization of this constitutional mandate at the grassroots
level.

        While the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) Memorandum
Circular No. 2024-086, otherwise known as the "Omnibus Policy on the Administration and
Strengthening Capacities of Barangay Tanods as Agents of Peace and Development," provides
a foundational framework for their duties, organization, and basic incentives, the reality is
that these volunteers remain undercompensated and highly vulnerable. Their current benefits
vary and are heavily dependent on the limited financial capacities of their respective
barangays, creating a glaring disparity in the welfare of these dedicated peacekeepers.

      This bill seeks to address the disparity through the creation of a Magna Carta for
Barangay Tanod. This measure will recognize their vital contributions and extend the
commensurate benefits, privileges, and protections they rightfully deserve at a national scale.

        Under the Magna Carta, Barangay Tanods will be assured that their services will be
met with tangible support. It is outlined that they will receive additional benefits such as
Hazard Pay of no less than One thousand pesos (P1,000.00) per month, comprehensive
insurance coverage through PhilHealth, SSS or GSIS, and Pag-IBIG, and a retirement gratuity
equivalent to one year of honoraria and allowances for those who have served at least fifteen
(15) years.
       To further professionalize their role, this bill also seeks to establish a National
Barangay Tanod Training Program to cover peacekeeping, first aid, disaster risk reduction,
and human rights. Lastly, it also seeks to create a Barangay Tanod Welfare Fund to provide
medical and hospitalization assistance, as well as death and disability benefits for their
families.

        The enactment of Magna Carta is not merely a matter of administrative policy, but a
profound moral imperative. By institutionalizing the dignity, safety, and livelihood of our
barangay frontliners, we ultimately strengthen the very foundation of our nation's peace,
security, and resilience.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

              AN ACT ESTABLISHING A MAGNA CARTA FOR
     BARANGAY TANODS, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS BARANGAY PUBLIC
   SAFETY OFFICERS (BPSO), BARANGAY POLICE OFFICERS (BPO), OR
 BARANGAY PEACEKEEPING AND SECURITY OFFICERS, PROVIDING FOR
     THEIR RIGHTS, BENEFITS, PROFESSIONALIZATION, WELFARE,
   MODERNIZATION, AND CAREER DEVELOPMENT, AND FOR OTHER
                            PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta for Barangay
Tanod."

        Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to recognize the vital
role of Barangay Tanods as frontliner in maintaining peace and order, promoting public safety,
and general welfare at the community level.

        To this end, the State shall ensure their protection, professionalization, welfare, and
career development, and provide adequate support for the effective discharge of their duties.

        Section 3. Definition. – For purposes of this Act, the term "Barangay Tanod" shall
refer to the members of the Barangay Tanod Brigades duly organized in every barangay
pursuant to the Local Government Code of 1991. They are civilian volunteers appointed to
assist barangay officials in maintaining peace and order, preventing and suppressing
criminality, enforcing laws and ordinances, and responding to emergencies.
        They are also known as Barangay Public Safety Officers (BPSO), Barangay
Peacekeeping and Security Officers (BPSO), Barangay Police Safety Officers (BPSO), or
Barangay Police Officers (BPO) in some localities, but for purposes of uniformity, they shall
collectively be referred to as "Barangay Tanod" under this Act.

       Section 4. Qualifications of Barangay Tanods. – A Barangay Tanod shall meet the
following qualifications:

       a) A citizen of the Philippines;
       b) A resident of the barangay concerned;
       c) can read and write;
       d) of good moral character;
       e) Physically and mentally fit.

       Retired government employees, police, or military personnel or officers may be
appointed as Barangay Tanod.

        Section 5. Rights and Privileges of Barangay Tanods. – All Barangay Tanods shall
enjoy the following rights and privileges:

       a. Legal Recognition as essential community peace and order personnel of the
Barangay;
       b. Protection from retaliation, harassment, or undue interference while in duty or in the
performance of official duties;
       c. Priority access to training, equipment, and welfare programs as provided in this Act/

        Section 6. Compensation and Benefits. – All Barangay Tanods shall be entitled to
minimum honoraria, transportation, and communication allowances, to be provided by their
respective barangays, in addition to the following incentives and benefits:

       a. Hazard Pay – Barangay Tanods shall be entitled to hazard allowance in the amount
          determined by the LGU concerned, which in no case shall be less than One thousand
          pesos (P 1,000.00) per month, subject to existing laws, rules and regulations;
       b. Insurance Coverage – Barangay Tanods shall be covered by the Philippine Health
          Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), Social Security System (SSS) or Government
          Service Insurance System (GSIS), and the Home Development Mutual Fund (Pag-
          IBIG Fund);
       c. Retirement Gratuity – Barangay Tanods who have served at least fifteen (15) years
          shall be entitled to a retirement gratuity equivalent to one year of honoraria and
          allowances.
       d. Death and Disability Benefits – In case of death or permanent disability in the line
          of duty, the family or dependents shall receive financial assistance from the
          Barangay Tanod Welfare Fund as provided in this Act.
       e. Civil Service Eligibility – Barangay Tanods who have completed at least five (5)
          years of continuous and satisfactory service, and who have completed the
          mandatory training and certification programs under this Act, shall be granted
           Barangay Official Eligibility (BOE) equivalent to Civil Service Subprofessional
           eligibility, pursuant to the rules of the Civil Service Commission (CSC).

       Section 7. Training and Professionalization. – A National Barangay Tanod Training
Program is hereby established under the supervision of the DILG, in coordination with the
Philippine National Police (PNP) and the Philippine Public Safety College. The training shall
cover peacekeeping, community policing, first aid, disaster risk reduction and management,
and human rights.

      Barangay Tanods shall undergo initial training within six (6) months of appointment,
and mandatory annual refresher courses thereafter.

      Section 8. Barangay Tanod Welfare Fund. – There is hereby created a Barangay
Tanod Welfare Fund under the DILG for the following purpose:

       (a) Medical and hospitalization assistance;
       (b) Scholarships for dependents;
       (c) Legal assistance in cases filed in relation to official duties; and
       (d) Emergency financial aid for injuries or deaths in the line of duty.

        The Fund shall be sourced from the General Appropriations Act, contributions from
local government units (LGUs), and grants, donations, or endowments.

       Section 9. Modernization and Equipment Support. – To ensure the modernization
of barangay peacekeeping, the following shall be provided:

       a) Standardized uniforms and identification cards;
       b) Radios, flashlights, and other basic operational equipment;
       c) Motorcycles, bicycles, or patrol vehicles for barangays with large geographic
           coverage; and
       d) Body cameras and digital tools for high-risk barangays.

The DILG shall prioritize disaster-prone areas and urban centers with higher crime incidence.

       Section 10. Legal Assistance. – All Barangay Tanods shall be entitled to free legal
representation and assistance in all administrative, civil, or criminal cases arising from the
lawful performance of their duties.

        Section 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DILG, in consultation with the DBM, the Department of Justice
(DOJ), the CSC, and the PNP, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this
Act, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of
this Act.
       Barangay Peace and Order Councils shall integrate the strengthening of the Barangay
Tanod in their annual plans.

       Section 12. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act under the budget of the
DILG. Local government units are encouraged to provide counterpart funds and support.

       Local government units are encouraged to provide counterpart funds and support.

       Section 13. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act be held invalid
or unconstitutional, the other parts or provisions that are not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

        Section 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, proclamations, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended,
or modified accordingly.

       Section 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in newspaper of general circulation.
