                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


        Equal access to opportunities is a fundamental component of social justice and
inclusive     development.     However,        for   many     Indigenous      Cultural
Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs), access to higher education,
employment, professional licensure, scholarships, and government assistance is often
constrained not only by geographic and socioeconomic disadvantages, but also by the
financial costs associated with securing official school documents required for such
opportunities.

       Official academic records, including transcripts of records, certificates,
diplomas, and other school-issued credentials, are essential documentary
requirements for educational advancement, employment, and participation in public
and private sector opportunities. Although these documents are administrative in
nature, the corresponding issuance and processing fees may impose an additional and
avoidable burden on Indigenous Peoples, particularly those from geographically
isolated, disadvantaged, and underserved communities.

       Consistent with the State’s constitutional mandate to promote social justice and
protect the rights and welfare of Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples
under Republic Act No. 8371, otherwise known as the “Indigenous Peoples’ Rights Act
of 1997” (IPRA), this measure seeks to remove financial barriers by exempting
Indigenous Peoples from the payment of fees in the procurement of official school
documents from all educational institutions, whether public or private.

        This exemption recognizes that access to essential academic credentials should
not be hindered by economic constraints, and affirms the State’s obligation to ensure
equal opportunity in education, employment, and public service participation. By
institutionalizing this policy, the State strengthens inclusive development and
enhances the capacity of Indigenous Peoples to fully participate in national social and
economic life.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
     EXEMPTING INDIGENOUS PEOPLES FROM FEES IN THE PROCUREMENT OF
     OFFICIAL SCHOOL DOCUMENTS FROM ALL EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS,
     AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

 1   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
 2   Congress assembled:
 3
 4        SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Indigenous
 5   Peoples School Documents Fee Exemption Act.”
 6
 7          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
 8   State to recognize, protect, and promote the rights and welfare of Indigenous Cultural
 9   Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) by ensuring that financial barriers do not
10   impede their access to educational, employment, and other lawful opportunities.
11
12          The State recognizes that official school documents are indispensable in
13   pursuing further education, securing employment, obtaining professional licenses,
14   applying for scholarships, and accessing government programs and services.
15   Accordingly, the State shall exempt Indigenous Peoples from the payment of fees for
16   the procurement of official school documents from all educational institutions, whether
17   public or private, in order to promote equitable access to opportunities and uphold the
18   principles of social justice and inclusive development.
19
20
21         SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:
22
23         (a) Educational Institution refers to any public or private institution duly
24         recognized or authorized by the Department of Education (DepEd), the
25         Commission on Higher Education (CHED), or the Technical Education and Skills
26         Development Authority (TESDA), offering basic, secondary, technical-
27         vocational, tertiary, or higher education.
 1
 2         (b) Fees refers to any charge, payment, assessment, or monetary
 3         consideration imposed by an Educational Institution for the processing,
 4         issuance, authentication, certification, reproduction, or release of Official School
 5         Documents.
 6
 7         (c) Graduate refers to an Indigenous Peoples individual who has completed a
 8         program of study in an Educational Institution and is entitled to request Official
 9         School Documents under this Act.
10
11         (d) Indigenous Peoples (IPs) shall refer to Indigenous Cultural
12         Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) as defined under Republic Act No.
13         8371, otherwise known as the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997 .
14
15         (e) Learner refers to any Indigenous Peoples student currently enrolled in an
16         Educational Institution, regardless of the level of education.
17
18         (f) Official School Documents refers to records, certifications, or other
19         academic documents officially issued by an Educational Institution, including
20         but not limited to transcripts of records, certificates of enrollment, certificates
21         of grades, certificates of graduation, diplomas, certificates of good moral
22         character, authenticated copies of academic records, and other documents of
23         similar nature required for educational, employment, professional, or lawful
24         purposes.
25
26         (g) Procurement refers to the request, processing, issuance, certification,
27         authentication, reproduction, or release of Official School Documents by an
28         Educational Institution.
29
30         (h) Qualified Beneficiary refers to any Indigenous Peoples learner or
31         graduate whose membership in an Indigenous Cultural Community/Indigenous
32         Peoples has been duly certified or otherwise verified by the National
33         Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) in accordance with existing laws,
34         rules, and regulations.
35

 4          SECTION 5. Coverage - This Act shall apply to all Indigenous Cultural
 5   Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) learners and graduates duly recognized
 6   or certified in accordance with Republic Act No. 8371, otherwise known as the
 7   Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997, who request or apply for the issuance,
 8   authentication, certification, reproduction, or release of Official School Documents
 9   from any Educational Institution in the Philippines, whether public or private, at any
10   level of education, including basic education, technical-vocational education, and
11   higher education.
12
13          SECTION 6. Fee Exemption - All Qualified Beneficiaries under this Act shall
14   be fully exempt from the payment of any and all fees, charges, costs, or monetary
15   considerations of whatever name or form, imposed or collected in connection with the
16   processing, issuance, authentication, certification, reproduction, verification, or
17   release of Official School Documents.
18
19          Such exemption shall include, but shall not be limited to, administrative fees,
20   processing fees, certification fees, documentary fees, system or platform charges,
21   convenience fees, and any similar or analogous charges that may be directly or
22   indirectly imposed by Educational Institutions.
23
24          No Educational Institution shall, directly or indirectly, require or collect any
25   payment from Qualified Beneficiaries for the procurement of Official School
26   Documents, whether such charges are imposed by the institution itself or by any office,
27   unit, contractor, or third-party service provider acting on its behalf.
28
29           Educational Institutions shall not refuse, delay, condition, or restrict the release
30   of Official School Documents on the basis of non-payment of any fee prohibited under
31   this Section.
32
33           SECTION 7. Verification of Indigenous Peoples Status - The status of a
34   Qualified Beneficiary as an Indigenous Cultural Community/Indigenous Peoples
35   (ICCs/IPs) member shall be verified through a certification, registry entry, or other
36   official documentation issued, recognized, or validated by the National Commission on
37   Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), pursuant to its mandate under Republic Act No. 8371,
38   otherwise known as the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997.
39
40   Educational Institutions shall strictly rely on the certification or verification issued or
41   recognized by the NCIP and shall not require additional documentary requirements
42   beyond those prescribed by the NCIP for purposes of confirming Indigenous Peoples
43   membership, except for standard identity verification through government-issued
44   identification cards or other official identification documents.
45
46   No Educational Institution shall impose additional certification, clearance,
47   endorsement, or validation requirements from any local government unit, school
 1   office, or other entity as a condition for the application or enjoyment of the exemption
 2   granted under this Act.
 3
 4          SECTION 8. Duties of Educational Institutions - All Educational
 5   Institutions shall:
 6
 7          (a) establish and maintain a standardized, accessible, and simplified procedure
 8          for the receipt, processing, and release of Official School Documents for
 9          Qualified Beneficiaries under this Act, which shall be made publicly available
10          through physical postings and official institutional websites, where applicable;
11
12          (b) process and release requests for Official School Documents within a fixed
13          period to be prescribed in the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), which
14          shall in no case exceed ten (10) working days from receipt of a complete
15          application, unless a longer period is justified by force majeure or other
16          exceptional circumstances as defined in the IRR;
17
18          (c) designate a specific office, unit, or duly authorized officer responsible for
19          the implementation and compliance with this Act, including the receipt,
20          tracking, and release of requests, and the maintenance of records of all
21          applications processed under this Act;
22
23          (d) ensure that all requests covered under this Act are given priority processing
24          and are not subjected to additional internal delays, queues, or requirements
25          not provided under this Act or its IRR; and
26
27          (e) strictly refrain from imposing, directly or indirectly, any substitute fees,
28          processing fees, administrative charges, convenience fees, or any other form
29          of payment in relation to the issuance, authentication, certification, or release
30          of Official School Documents covered under this Act.
31

44          with this Act, subject to guidelines to be jointly issued by the Department of

47          Department of Budget and Management (DBM); and
48

 8   coordinate, and oversee the enforcement of this Act:
 9
10         (a) The Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher Education
11         (CHED), and Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA)
12         shall exercise jurisdiction over all public and private educational institutions
13         under their respective mandates and shall ensure compliance with the

18         for the issuance, validation, and recognition of certification or documentation
19         establishing Indigenous Peoples status, and shall assist in the verification
20         standards necessary for the implementation of this Act;
21
22         (c) The Department of Education (DepEd), CHED, TESDA, and NCIP shall jointly

24         to ensure uniform implementation of this Act across all educational levels and
25         institutions;
26
27         (d) The Department of Education (DepEd) shall act as the lead coordinating
28         agency for basic education institutions, while CHED shall act as lead
29         coordinating agency for higher education institutions, and TESDA for technical-

32
33         (e) All implementing agencies shall coordinate with each other and may enter
34         into inter-agency agreements, subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations,
35         to ensure effective, efficient, and consistent implementation of this Act
36         nationwide.

38   SECTION 11. Prohibited Acts - It shall be unlawful for any Educational
39   Institution, or any of its officials, employees, agents, or representatives, to commit
40   any of the following acts:
41
42         (a) refuse, deny, or fail to issue, release, or provide Official School Documents
43         to a duly certified Qualified Beneficiary on the ground of non-payment of any
44         fee or charge prohibited under this Act;
45
46         (b) impose, demand, collect, or receive, directly or indirectly, any fee, charge,
47         cost, or consideration in violation of the provisions of this Act;
48
 1         (c) delay, withhold, suspend, or unreasonably defer the processing or release
 2         of Official School Documents beyond the period prescribed under this Act or its
 3         Implementing Rules and Regulations, without valid and documented
 4         justification as defined therein;
 5
 6         (d) impose additional documentary requirements, certifications, clearances,
 7         endorsements, or procedures not required or authorized under this Act or its
 8         Implementing Rules and Regulations;
 9
10         (e) condition the release of Official School Documents on the completion of any
11         internal requirement, clearance, or obligation unrelated to the purpose of the
12         request under this Act; and
13
14         (f) engage in any act, scheme, or practice intended to circumvent, frustrate, or
15         undermine the rights granted to Qualified Beneficiaries under this Act.
16
17   SECTION 12. Penalties - Any Educational Institution, including its responsible
18   officials, officers, employees, or agents, found to have violated any provision of this
19   Act shall be subject to administrative, civil, and/or criminal liabilities as may be
20   applicable under existing laws, without prejudice to the imposition of the following
21   sanctions:
22
23         (a) Administrative Sanctions - The appropriate implementing agency, after due
24         notice and hearing, may impose administrative fines, penalties, or other
25         monetary sanctions, the amount and manner of which shall be determined
26         under the Implementing Rules and Regulations, taking into account the gravity,
27         nature, duration, and extent of the violation;
28
29         (b) Continuing Non-Compliance Penalties - In cases of continuing or repeated
30         violations, the implementing agency may impose additional escalating
31         sanctions, including daily penalties or equivalent measures, until full compliance
32         is achieved;
33
34         (c) Suspension of Authority or Accreditation - In cases of serious, repeated, or
35         willful violations, the Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher
36         Education (CHED), or Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
37         (TESDA), as appropriate, may suspend the permit, recognition, accreditation,
38         or authority to operate of the Educational Institution concerned, subject to due
39         process;
40
41         (d) Revocation or Cancellation of Accreditation or Permit - In cases of gross,
42         flagrant, or malicious violation, the appropriate regulatory agency may revoke
43         or cancel the institution’s recognition or authority to operate, subject to
44         applicable laws and due process requirements;
45
46         (e) Liability of Responsible Officers - Officers, administrators, or personnel who
47         knowingly authorize, participate in, or allow the commission of prohibited acts
48         shall be subject to administrative sanctions, including suspension, dismissal, or
 1         disqualification from holding any position in any Educational Institution, without
 2         prejudice to other liabilities under existing laws;
 3
 4         (f) Other Remedies and Sanctions - The imposition of the foregoing penalties
 5         shall be without prejudice to any civil, criminal, or other administrative liability
 6         arising from existing laws, rules, and regulations.
