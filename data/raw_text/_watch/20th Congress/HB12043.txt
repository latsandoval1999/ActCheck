AN ACT
GRANTING MENTAL HEALTH WELLNESS LEAVE TO PRIVATE SECTOR
EMPLOYEES AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

EXPLANATORY NOTE

Mental health is an essential component of overall well-being and directly
affects productivity, workplace safety, and economic participation. In recent years, the
State has increasingly recognized the importance of promoting mental health in both
public and private institutions, as reflected in Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known
as the Mental Health Act.

Consistent with this policy, the Civil Service Commission (CSC) has approved
the grant of Mental Health Wellness Leave for government employees, recognizing
the need to institutionalize preventive mental health measures in the workplace.
However, no parallel statutory benefit exists for employees in the private sector,
resulting in unequal access to mental health support despite comparable workplace
pressures.

In Portugal, remote workers were granted the legal right to disconnect from
work when a communication law was brought into force in late 2021. In the United
Kingdom, employers have legal requirements to accommodate and safeguard the
mental health of workers. UK employers cannot discriminate against candidates or
employees with a mental health illness. The Dutch government enforces a
comprehensive sick leave policy, and the country has an advanced and progressive


===== [page 2] =====
attitude to mental health. Dutch citizens experiencing mental health problems are able
to take days off to aid their recovery.'

In the Philippines, while there are no existing laws about leaves for private
sector employees experiencing mental health illness symptoms, the Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE) has released Department Order No. 208, series of
2020, titled “Guidelines for the Implementation of Mental Health Workplace Policies
and programs for the Private Sector’, but it did not specify nor contain anything that
require private sector employers to accommodate or allow leaves dedicated for the
mental health of their employees.”

This bill seeks to address this gap by granting two (2) days of Mental Health
Wellness Leave per year to private sector employees. The proposed leave is modest
in duration, non-cumulative, and subject to reasonable employer regulation, thereby
balancing the welfare of employees with the operational needs of businesses.

By institutionalizing this benefit, the State affirms its commitment to protecting
mental health, reducing burnout, and promoting a healthier and more productive
workforce without imposing undue burden on employers.

AN ACT

GRANTING MENTAL HEALTH WELLNESS LEAVE TO PRIVATE SECTOR
EMPLOYEES AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Mental Health
Wellness Leave Act for Private Sector Employees.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — |t is the policy of the State to promote
the mental health and general well-being of workers as part of its duty to afford full
protection to labor. The State recognizes mental health as a vital factor in ensuring
safe, productive, and humane working conditions and seeks to institutionalize
preventive mental health measures in the workplace.

SECTION 3. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all employees in the private
sector, regardless of employment status, including regular, probationary, contractual,
project-based, and fixed-term employees, provided that the employee has rendered
at least six (6) months of service within the last twelve (12) months.

SECTION 4. Grant of Mental Health Wellness Leave. - Private sector
employees shall be entitled to two (2) days of Mental Health Wellness Leave per
calendar year, with pay.

The Mental Health Wellness Leave shall:

===== [page 4] =====
(a) Be separate and distinct from existing leave benefits under existing laws,
company policies, or collective bargaining agreements;

(b) Be non-cumulative and non-convertible to cash if unused; and

(c) Be availed of for purposes of mental rest, stress recovery, emotional well-
being, or psychological self-care.

SECTION 5. Availment and Notice. - The Mental Health Wellness Leave may
be availed of upon reasonable notice to the employer, subject to company rules and
regulations.

Employers shall not require medical certificates or disclosure of mental health
conditions as a prerequisite for availing of the leave, except in cases of abuse as may
be provided in the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).

SECTION 6. Non-discrimination and Non-retaliation. - No employer shall:

(a) Discriminate against an employee for availing of Mental Health Wellness
Leave; or

(b) Use the availment of such a leave as a ground for dismissal, demotion,
reduction of benefits, or any adverse employment action.

SECTION 7. Relation to Existing Benefits. - Nothing in this Act shall be
construed to diminish or impair benefits already enjoyed by employees under existing
laws, company policies, or collective bargaining agreements. Where existing benefits
are more favorable, the same shall prevail.

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE), in consultation with relevant stakeholders, shall
promulgate the Implementing Rules and regulations within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act.

SECTION 9. Penalties. — Any employer who willfully refuses to grant the
Mental Health Wellness Leave or violates any provision of this Act shall be subject to
administrative penalties as may be determined by DOLE, without prejudice to other
remedies available under existing labor laws.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. — lf any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue
in full force and effect.

SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, administrative
orders, rules, and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
