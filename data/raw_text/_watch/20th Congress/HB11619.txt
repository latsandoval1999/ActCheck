20                                              EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   There are moments when the promise of universal healthcare becomes most
23   meaningful.
24
25   It is when a Filipino can seek medical care without having to choose between treatment
26   and the family’s daily needs, when contributions to the health system translate into
27   substantial benefits that address not only the most serious illnesses but also the broader
28   healthcare needs, and when public health resources are managed with the highest
29   degree of efficiency that actually translates to concrete benefits that will bring smiles to
30   families.
31
32   Republic Act No. 11223, or the Universal Health Care Act, established the framework
33   for providing every Filipino with comprehensive and affordable healthcare. However,
34   aside from the debatable claim that healthcare under the law is truly “affordable,”, recent
35   developments have shown that the implementation of this framework still requires
36   clearer safeguards and stronger mechanisms. The Supreme Court's 2025 ruling on the
37   transfer of PhilHealth funds underscored the need to protect the Corporation's reserves
38   and ensure that excess funds are used consistently with the purposes expressly
39   provided under the UHC Act. Concerns raised during the 2026 budget deliberations
40   likewise highlighted questions surrounding the adequacy, release, and proper use of
41   government funds intended for PhilHealth and universal healthcare.
42
43   Hence, this proposed measure seeks to translate these reforms into concrete benefits
44   for Filipino patients and PhilHealth members. Excess PhilHealth reserves would be
45   directed toward expanding benefits and reducing premium contribution rates, while
46   future contribution rates would be based on updated actuarial assessments and
47   subjected to legislative review. For migrant workers, the bill provides a clearer and more
 1   equitable arrangement for premium contributions and protects them from having unpaid
 2   contributions become a barrier upon their return to the country.
 3
 4   This proposed measure likewise seeks to make every peso contributed to PhilHealth
 5   work harder by allowing cost-effective public, private, and public-private arrangements
 6   for benefit delivery, while strengthening the Special Health Fund so that PhilHealth
 7   reimbursements can be used to improve local health facilities.
 8
 9   Moreover, this measure strengthens the systems that support universal healthcare. A
10   national UHC Coordinating Council would bring together the agencies responsible for
11   health, finance, labor, social welfare, education, technology, and local governance;
12   independent external audits would supplement existing government oversight; and
13   specific penalties would apply to the misuse of funds intended for healthcare.
14
15   In practical terms, these reforms seek to ensure that the money contributed by Filipinos
16   and the resources appropriated for healthcare do not merely remain as funds on paper,
17   but are ultimately converted into better coverage, more responsive health services,
18   stronger local facilities, and greater financial protection for patients and their families.
19
20   With this, the promise of universal healthcare can become more tangible in the
21   everyday lives of Filipinos. Under the current system, ordinary Filipinos like Juan and
22   Maria may still find themselves forced to choose between paying a hospital bill and
23   keeping the savings they need for their children’s education, daily necessities, and their
24   family’s future, despite paying high contributions.
25
26   However, under this stronger healthcare system envisioned by this measure, Juan and
27   Maria can face that moment differently. Their PhilHealth contributions can be kept at a
28   level that is actuarially justified and corresponds to the realities of increasing costs of
29   living, while ensuring that those contributions translate into more substantial benefits
30   when illness strikes. Moreover, PhilHealth can now cover a greater share of their
31   hospital expenses, leaving them with a smaller balance to pay and reducing the need
32   to exhaust the savings they had set aside for their children’s education, a better home,
33   or simply a more secure and comfortable life.
34
35   For families like Juan and Maria’s, this is what universal healthcare should ultimately
36   feel like, that is, contributing fairly and receiving meaningful protection in return, knowing
37   that when illness comes, they can receive the care they need without having to sacrifice
38   the future they have worked so hard to build.
39
40   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
