                               EXPLANATORY NOTE
       Republic Act No. 10591, the Comprehensive Firearms and Ammunition
Regulation Act, governs who may own a firearm in this country. It says almost
nothing about what an owner must do with that firearm for the remaining hours of
the day. Section 9 of the Act disposes of the entire question of safekeeping in a single
sentence: that “a vault or a container secured by lock and key or other security
measures” is an acceptable requirement. It fixes no standard for that container,
imposes no continuing duty of custody, and attaches no consequence whatsoever to
an owner or an accountable custodian whose carelessness places a deadly weapon
within the reach of a child, a person in crisis, or a criminal. A locked drawer and a
bank-grade safe are, under present law, legally indistinguishable.

       That silence has a body count.

       On June 22, 2026, two minors aged fifteen and fourteen opened fire at San Jose
National High School in Tacloban City, killing three students and injuring twenty
others, fifteen of them by gunfire. The weapons were a Glock 17 pistol that was the
service firearm of a Philippine National Police officer related to one of the assailants,
and a .38-caliber revolver registered to a private security agency in Cebu City but
kept in the home of a former security guard.

        On August 18, 2026, a minor killed two students and wounded nine others at
the Ateneo de Zamboanga University Junior High School, livestreaming the attack;
the firearm was issued by the Bureau of Customs to his father. Between these events,
authorities intercepted a fourteen-year-old at Lut-od National High School in
Pinamungajan, Cebu, carrying a revolver said to belong to his grandfather.
       On September 18, 2026, a Grade 9 student opened fire at Banga National High
School in South Cotabato, killing and wounding fellow students before taking his
own life.

       These four incidents share a pattern that this Congress should find
intolerable. In no case was the firearm a loose or black-market weapon. Every one of
them was a firearm the State itself had licensed, registered, or issued — and in three
of the four, the firearm had been placed in the custody of a public officer or a
security professional. This is the gap the present measure closes, and it closes it at
the point where the evidence says the failure actually occurs.

       This measure addresses the narrower and more tractable problem to which
the last four tragedies point: the licensed, registered, and government-issued firearm
that was lawfully acquired and then left within reach of a child. That firearm is
already in the State’s records. Its custodian is already known. The only thing missing
is a duty and a consequence.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Safe Firearm Storage
and Anti-Firearm Negligence Act”.

SEC. 2. Declaration of State Policy. — The State recognizes that the maintenance of
peace and order, the protection of life, and the promotion of the general welfare are
essential for the enjoyment by all the people of the blessings of democracy. Towards
this end, the State affirms that firearm ownership is a highly regulated privilege that
carries the strict and non-delegable duty to prevent deadly weapons from falling
into the hands of children, unauthorized individuals, and persons in crisis. Through
these synchronized inter-agency measures, the State seeks to shield the youth,
preserve lives, and foster a culture of absolute responsibility and vigilance among all
firearm owners

additional devices and technologies as compliant, but shall not diminish, relax, or
dispense with any requirement prescribed by that Section, and shall not de ne,
expand, or modify the elements of any offense under this Act. No penal liability shall
attach by reason of non-compliance with a standard issued under this Section that is
not also a requirement prescribed by statute.

     SEC. 29. Annual Report; Program Review. — The PNP shall submit to the President
     and to Congress, within ninety (90) days from the end of every calendar year, a
     report on the implementation of this Act, including the number of certi cations led,
     inspections conducted, de ciencies cured, licenses suspended and revoked, petitions
     for Firearm Safety Protection Orders          led and granted,     rearms deposited and
     surrendered, prosecutions commenced and concluded under Sections 41 to 41-C of
     Republic Act No. 10591, as amended, and the data compiled under Section 42-A(e)
     thereof.

                Not later than ve (5) years from the effectivity of this Act, the Congressional
     Oversight Committee shall conduct a comprehensive review of its operation, with
     particular attention to the adequacy and proportionality of the penalties, the
     accessibility and cost of compliance, the operation of the Firearm Safety Protection
     Order, and the effect of the Act on        rearm-related deaths and injuries, and shall
     report its ndings and recommendations to both Houses.



     SEC. 30. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
     this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Philippine
     National Police, the Department of Health, the Department of Education, the
     Commission on Higher Education, the Department of the Interior and Local
     Government, the Department of Social Welfare and Development, and the Judiciary.
     Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be
     included in the annual General Appropriations Act, in addition to the Firearm Safety
     and Violence Prevention Fund established under Section 42-C of Republic Act
     No. 10591, as amended.

            Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the FEO-PNP shall
     publish minimum performance standards for secure storage devices, vehicle storage,
     training curricula, examinations, instructor accreditation, and compliance
     documentation.

     SEC. 32. Transitory Provisions. — New applicants shall comply upon publication of
     the minimum standards. Existing licensees shall acquire and use compliant secure
     storage within one hundred eighty (180) days from publication of the standards and
     shall complete the refresher course at the rst renewal after effectivity. No criminal
     penalty shall apply to conduct completed before effectivity, and no liability based on
     noncompliance with the new storage requirements shall attach before expiration of
     the applicable compliance period.

     SEC. 33. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act or its application to any
     person or circumstance is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder of this
     Act and the application of the provision to other persons or circumstances shall not
     be affected.

     SEC. 34. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, administrative
     issuances, rules, and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act are
     hereby repealed, amended, or modi ed accordingly.

     SEC. 35. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fteen (15) days after its publication
