EXPLANATORY NOTE

The shortage of qualified guidance counselors remains a serious challenge in Philippine
basic education. For many learners,school is more than a place for learning. It is where they spend
much of their formative years, build friendships, face challenges, and begin to understand
themselves and the world around them. Yet behind the classrooms and school activities are young
people who may be quietly struggling with anxiety, family problems, bullying, or other emotional
difficulties. For some, seeking help from a trusted adult in school may be the first step toward
getting the support they need.

Unfortunately, not every school has a qualified guidance counselor available to provide
that support. In August 2026, the Department of Education (DepEd) reported that fewer than 1,000
registered guidance counselors were serving more than 45,000 public schools nationwide.! In
CALABARZON, an October 2025 report by the Philippine Information Agency documented
approximately 50 licensed guidance counselors serving more than three million learners across
some 6,000 schools, with some counselors assigned to multiple schools due to the shortage of
qualified personnel.”

These figures represent more than a shortage of government personnel. They reflect the
difficulties faced by students who need professional guidance, parents who worry about their
children's well-being, and teachers who are often among the first to notice when a learner is
struggling. While teachers and school administrators do their best to provide assistance, they
cannot be expected to take the place of qualified mental health professionals.

Republic Act No. 12080, or the Basic Education Mental Health and Well-Being Promotion
Act, is an important step toward addressing this problem. It established a framework for school-
based mental health services, the deployment of qualified counseling personnel, and mechanisms
for counseling assistance and referrals. However, as the government continues to fill the necessary
positions, many schools must still contend with limited access to professional mental health
services.

The challenge, therefore, is not simply to create more positions or establish additional
programs, but to ensure that the needs of students who remain underserved are not lost in the
process. When referrals cannot be accommodated because qualified personnel are unavailable, or
when schools continue to experience prolonged counselor vacancies, these gaps must be properly
accounted for and reflected in government priorities.

This bill seeks to strengthen Republic Act No. 12080 by requiring persistent counselor
vacancies and documented unmet mental health service needs to be expressly considered in
personnel deployment and implementation planning. It further seeks to ensure that schools
experiencing prolonged shortages receive appropriate priority, particularly where the number of
affected learners and limited access to qualified professionals demonstrate a greater need for
assistance.

The proposed measure does not create another government office, program, or plantilla
position, nor does it impose additional professional counseling responsibilities on teachers. Rather,
it seeks to make existing mechanisms more responsive and accountable, particularly to students in
underserved and geographically isolated communities where professional assistance may be
difficult to access.

No Filipino student should feel that seeking help is pointless simply because there is no
guidance counselor available in their school. While addressing the nationwide shortage of qualified
professionals will take time, recognizing and responding to the needs of those who remain without
adequate support cannot wait. Every learner deserves to know that their well-being matters, and
that their struggles will not be overlooked.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnesfly sought.

AN ACT
STRENGTHENING ACCOUNTABILITY FOR UNMET SCHOOL-BASED MENTAL
HEALTH SERVICE NEEDS AND PERSISTENT SCHOOL COUNSELOR
VACANCIES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 12080,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “BASIC EDUCATION MENTAL HEALTH AND
WELL-BEING PROMOTION ACT”

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in (ongress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “School Mental Health Service
Continuity Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote and protect the mental health and well-being of learners and ensure equitable access to
appropriate mental health services in basic education.

Toward this end, the State shall strengthen the implementation of school-based mental
health services by ensuring that persistent staffing shortages and documented unmet service needs
are taken into account in personnel deployment and implementation planning, consistent with the
objectives of Republic Act No. 12080.

SECTION 3. Amendment to Section 13 of Republic Act No. 12080. — Section 13 of
Republic Act No. 12080 is hereby amended by adding the following paragraphs at the end thereof:

“Where referrals for professional mental health assistance remain unaccommodated due to
the unavailability of qualified personnel or appropriate services, the concerned Mental
Health and Well-Being Office shall identify and document such unmet service needs
through existing monitoring mechanisms, consistent with the referral, intervention, and
follow-up procedures provided under this Act and applicable regulations.

“In determining annual personne! deployment priorities, the Department of Education
(DepEd) shall consider the duration of vacancies in School Counselor and School
Counselor Associate positions, documented unmet mental health service needs, the number
of learners affected, and the availability and accessibility of qualified mental health
professionals. Particular consideration shall be given to schools and schools divisions in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas where persistent staffing shortages and
limited access to appropriate services have been identified.

“The DepEd shall ensure that these considerations are reflected in the preparation and
review of yearly implementation targets under this section. Schools and schools divisions
experiencing persistent staffing shortages and significant unmet service needs shall be
given appropriate priority in personnel deployment planning, subject to the availability of

SECTION 4. Implementation Through Existing Mechanisms. — This Act shall be
implemented through the Mental Health and Well-Being Offices established in Schools Division
Offices, school-based Care Centers, and existing personnel, facilities, and service delivery,
referral, and monitoring mechanisms provided for under Republic Act No. 12080 and other
applicable laws and regulations.

The DepEd shall utilize existing administrative records, personnel deployment plans, and
monitoring systems to the greatest extent practicable. No separate office, program, or reporting
system shall be established where appropriate mechanisms already exist.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize personnel who do not possess the
qualifications required by law to perform professional counseling or other regulated mental health
services, or to impose additional professional counseling responsibilities on teachers beyond those
authorized under existing laws.

SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, in coordination with the Department of Budget and Management
(DBM), the Civil Service Commission (CSC), the Department of Health (DOH), and other
concerned agencies, as may be necessary, shall issue the appropriate supplemental rules and
guidelines for its implementation.

Such guidelines shall prescribe the manner of identifying and consolidating unmet mental
health service needs through existing monitoring mechanisms, the criteria for determining
persistent staffing and service-access gaps, and the manner by which such findings shall be
considered in personnel deployment and annual implementation planning.

SECTION 6. Appropriations. — The implementation of this Act shall be charged against
the existing appropriations of the DepEd, subject to applicable budgeting, accounting, and auditing
laws, rules, and regulations. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 7. Separability Clause. — \f any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
and effect.

SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 9. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
