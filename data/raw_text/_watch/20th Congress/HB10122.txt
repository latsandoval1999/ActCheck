                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        On 30 June 2026, the Department of Education issued a memorandum reminding all
schools to strictly enforce the prohibition on the use of portable electronic devices, including but
not limited to mobile phones and similar gadgets, during class and instructional hours. The bill is
comprehensive as it covers not only students but also teaching personnel, reminding them that the
use of such devices is allowed only for legitimate educational, health and well-being, and emergency
purposes.

          The proposed bill aims to institutionalize the memorandum by establishing a uniform and
enforceable standard across schools. It reinforces the shared responsibility of all members of the
academic community in fostering a safe, focused, and effective learning environment. Additionally,
this bill guarantees that both students and teachers benefit from a conducive learning atmosphere
while upholding students’ right to a quality education.

                    Given the timeliness of the bill, its passage is highly sought after.

                            AN ACT
  TO REGULATE AND LIMIT USAGE OF MOBILE DEVICES IN PUBLIC AND
              PRIVATE EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS

       SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “School Screentime
Regulation Act of 2026.”

        SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the declared policy of the State to
promote a focused, safe, and conducive learning environment by institutionalizing the
prohibition on the use of portable electronic devices during class hours, subject only to
reasonable exceptions.

        SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall mean as follows:

           1. Mobile Device refers to any form of portable electronic devices, such as but
              not limited to smartphones, cellular phones, tablet computers, pocket personal
              computers, palmtops, smart watches, virtual reality (VR) headsets, smart
              glasses, wearable cameras, and other similar devices which can store, send,
              receive, and move data through the internet, bluetooth, or wireless connection.

           2. Electronic gadget refers to any portable electronic device that enables its users
              to make and receive calls, capture photos, record and stream videos, and play
              games, such as but not limited to laptops and other similar devices.

           3. Basic Education refers to kindergarten to high school, including senior high
              school students.

           4. Students refer to learners or any person currently enrolled in Kindergarten to
              Senior High School in basic education institutions.

           5. Digital Wellness Programs refer to school initiatives aimed at teaching learners
              responsible use of technology, self-regulation, and the impacts of screentime
              from mobile devices and electronic gadgets on students.

           6. Screentime refers to the cumulative duration of active exposure to screens of
              mobile devices or electronic gadgets, measured in minutes or hours per day or
              class period, excluding standby or sleep modes.

           7. School Premises refers to all areas within the physical boundaries of basic
              education institutions, including but not limited to classrooms, quadrangles,
              laboratories, swimming pools, playgrounds, libraries, canteens, and
              administrative offices.

           8. Class Hours refers to the official instructional time from the first bell to
              dismissal as defined by the Department of Education (DepEd) guidelines or
              school calendars.

       SECTION 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all public and private basic
education institutions offering any level of the basic education curriculum.

        SECTION 5. Prohibition on the Use of Mobile Devices and Electronic Gadgets
by Students. - All students in basic education institutions are prohibited from using mobile
devices and electronic gadgets within school premises during class hours except if done for the
purposes enumerated in Section 6. Students’ devices shall be kept in designated storage areas
during class hours. If it falls under the enumerated exceptions provided herein, students may
be allowed to use said devices.

       SECTION 6. Exceptions. – Use of mobile devices and electronic gadgets during
school hours within school premises is allowed when there is prior approval from the teacher
and only for the following purposes:

           a. Learning Exceptions – These include the use of mobile devices or electronic
              gadgets for classroom presentations or learning activities; or for students
              requiring reasonable adjustments to a learning program or curriculum due to
              disability or learning difficulty.
           b. Health and Well-Being Exceptions – These refer to instances when a student
              has health conditions that require the use of mobile devices or electronic
              gadgets, as certified by a licensed physician and noted by the school principal.

           c. Risk Management Exceptions – These cover instances of emergencies,
              including but not limited to natural or man-made disasters, or cases involving
              authorized field trips and school-related activities outside school premises.

         SECTION 7. Establishment of an Emergency Hotline Number. – To ensure and
facilitate immediate contact between parents and students in cases of emergencies, all basic
education institutions shall:

            a. Designate an emergency hotline number within school premises accessible to
               both students and parents, including both cellular and landline options;

            b. Develop clear protocols for school administrators and teachers to facilitate
               emergency communication, including proper handling of urgent calls from
               parents; and

            c. Ensure coordination with local emergency response units to establish direct
               communication channels, including both cellular and landline options, for
               faster response.

        SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DepEd, in
coordination with the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT)
and other public and private stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations with respect to:

            a. The imposition of sanctions on covered institutions in cases of non-
               compliance;

            b. The integration of Media and Information Literacy subjects in covered
               institutions; and

            c. The conduct of digital wellness programs, where students may be required to
               attend workshops or activities promoting healthy screen time habits, including
               outdoor learning sessions, physical activities, or art-based engagements.

        SECTION 9. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act shall be
declared invalid, unenforceable, or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which are not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

        SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, ordinances, administrative
orders, rules and regulations or any part thereof, which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed or amended accordingly.

        SECTION 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette and at least two (2) newspapers of national circulation.
