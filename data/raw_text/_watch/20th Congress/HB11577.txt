EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines is frequently affected by typhoons, floods, earthquakes,
volcanic eruptions, and other calamities that may damage homes, disrupt
livelihoods, reduce household income, and interrupt access to essential services.
During such emergencies, affected families may find it difficult to pay electricity,

water, telecommunications, and other utility charges while attending to their
basic needs and recovery.

Access to these services is essential to public health, safety,
communication, disaster response, and community recovery. Republic Act No.
10121, or the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 20 10,

recognizes the need to strengthen community resilience and provide coordinated
assistance during disasters.

This bill seeks to establish a temporary moratorium on utility payments
for residential customers in areas under a declared state of calamity. It prohibits
the enforcement of covered utility charges, disconnection solely for nonpayment,
and the imposition of penalties, surcharges, or interest arising from the
deferment during the moratorium period.

AN ACT

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Calamity Utility
Payment Moratorium Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to promote the general welfare, public safety, public health, and economic
security of the Filipino people during times of calamity and other emergencies.

The State recognizes the essential nature of electricity, water,
telecommunications, and other basic utility services, particularly during disaster
response and recovery. Accordingly, the State shall provide temporary relief to
affected households and ensure continued access to essential utility services

while balancing the legitimate interests and operational requirements of utility
providers.

SECTION 3. Scope. This Act shall apply to residential utility customers whose
principal residences are located in barangays, municipalities, cities, provinces,
or regions covered by a declared state of calamity.

Subject to the implementing rules and regulations, the benefits of this Act may
also be extended to qualified micro, small, and medium enterprises whose

===== [page 4] =====
operations are located in an affected area and have been materially disrupted by
the calamity.

This Act shall cover utility charges incurred during the period specified in Section
5, including charges for electricity, water supply and sewerage services,
telecommunications, including fixed-line, mobile, and broadband internet

services, and such other essential utility services as may be identified by the
appropriate government agencies.

SECTION 4. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act:

(a) “Calamity” refers to a serious disruption of the functioning of a
community or society caused by a natural or human-induced hazard,

resulting in widespread human, material, economic, or environmental
losses and impacts.

(b) “Utility service” refers to _ electricity, water, sewerage,
telecommunications, internet, or another essential service supplied to

a residential or qualified business customer by a public or private
utility provider.

(c) “Utility provider’ refers to any public or private entity authorized by law,
franchise, permit, certificate, or contract to provide a utility service.

(d) “Utility charge” refers to the amount billed for the consumption, use,
connection, reconnection, maintenance, or provision of a covered utility

service, including applicable taxes and lawful government-imposed
charges.

(e) “Covered customer” refers to a residential customer, or a qualified
MSME under Section 3, whose service address is located in an area
covered by a declared state of calamity.

(f) “Moratorium period” refers to the period beginning on the date of the
declaration of a state of calamity and ending one hundred twenty (120)

days thereafter, or upon the lifting of the declaration, whichever occurs
earlier.

SECTION 5. Moratorium on Utility Payments and Disconnection. There shall
be a moratorium on the collection and enforcement of utility charges incurred
by covered customers during the moratorium period.

During the moratorium period, utility providers shall not:


===== [page 5] =====
(a) demand immediate payment of covered utility charges;

(b) impose penalties, surcharges, or interest arising from the deferment of
payment;

(c) disconnect, suspend, or terminate a covered utility service solely on
account of nonpayment of charges covered by this Act; or

(d) commence or continue collection proceedings based solely on
nonpayment of charges covered by this Act.

The moratorium shall not prevent a covered customer from voluntarily paying
all or part of the utility charges due during the moratorium period.

The moratorium shall not relieve a customer from paying utility charges incurred
before or after the moratorium period, except as otherwise provided by law or by
a more favorable agreement with the utility provider.

SECTION 6. Installment Payment of Deferred Utility Charges. Utility charges
covered by the moratorium shall remain payable after the expiration of the

moratorium period.

The deferred charges shall be paid in not less than six (6) equal monthly
installments, without penalties, surcharges, or interest arising from the
deferment. The first installment shall become due not earlier than thirty (30)

days after the expiration of the moratorium period.

SECTION 8. Duties of Utility Providers. All utility providers shall:

(a) separately identify deferred charges in the customer’s bill;

(b) provide clear information regarding the moratorium and installment
arrangements;

(c) refrain from imposing penalties, interest, or other charges arising solely
from the deferment;

(d) establish accessible payment and complaint channels; and

(e) comply with the rules and directives issued by the appropriate
regulatory agencies.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Energy, the Metropolitan
Waterworks and Sewerage System, the Local Water Utilities Administration, the
Department of Information and Communications Technology, the National
Telecommunications Commission, the Energy Regulatory Commission, and
other concerned agencies shall, in consultation with utility providers, local
government units, and consumer representatives, promulgate the necessary
rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
rules and regulations or parts thereof which are in conflict or inconsistent with
the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. In the event that any provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional, the validity of the other provisions shall not be

affected by such declaration.

SECTION 10. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
