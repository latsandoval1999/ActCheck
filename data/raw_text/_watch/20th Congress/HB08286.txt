                        AN ACT
STRENGTHENING PUBLIC HEALTH THROUGH THE MANDATORY
REFORMULATION OF HIGH-SODIUM PREPACKAGED FOOD PRODUCTS
TO REDUCE SODIUM CONSUMPTION AND PREVENT NON-
COMMUNICABLE DISEASES

                                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

High sodium intake is strongly linked to noncommunicable diseases (NCDs), such as
hypertension, kidney disease, heart disease, and stroke. According to the Philippine
Statistics Authority (PSA), heart disease remains the leading cause of death in the
country, accounting for more than 60,000 deaths annually, or 19.8% of total registered
deaths in 2024.¹ This persistent trend reflects the growing burden of cardiovascular and
related conditions among Filipinos.

The economic impact is equally alarming. In 2023 alone, the Philippine government
spent PHP 17.3 billion through PhilHealth for 3.6 million hemodialysis claims. ² In
2024, the annual package coverage for hemodialysis increased to PhP990,600 per
patient, ³ making it one of the most expensive single treatment packages under the
National Health Insurance Program. These figures underscore the immense fiscal strain
caused by preventable chronic diseases and highlight the urgent need for upstream,
population-wide preventive interventions.

Excessive sodium intake is a major contributor to hypertension and chronic kidney
disease, both of which significantly drive up long-term healthcare costs. Studies show
that Filipinos consume an average of 4,113 milligrams (mg) of sodium per day,
equivalent to 10.4 grams of salt—more than double the World Health Organization’s
(WHO) recommended daily limit of 2,000 mg.⁴ This excessive intake is one of the key
drivers of the country’s growing NCD burden. The WHO has consistently identified
sodium reduction as one of the most cost-effective “best buy” interventions to prevent
NCDs and premature deaths. ⁵

This bill seeks to reduce the burden of NCDs and strengthen public health through the
mandatory reformulation of high-sodium prepackaged food products, including sauces
and condiments that significantly contribute to sodium intake in Filipino diets. It
introduces maximum sodium limits for identified high-impact food categories and
adopts a Stepwise Approach to reformulation. This phased strategy allows consumers
to gradually adjust to taste changes while providing the food industry with sufficient
time for research, development, and product adaptation.

The measure mandates the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Food
and Drug Administration (FDA), the Department of Science and Technology – Food
and Nutrition Research Institute (DOST-FNRI), and other relevant agencies, to develop
sodium reduction targets, establish a national sodium data system, and implement public
education initiatives. Regular monitoring of dietary sodium intake and sodium content
in prepackaged food will ensure evidence-based policy adjustments and measurable
progress.

This proposed legislation is aligned with the World Health Organization’s global call
for government-led mandatory sodium reduction policies. It supports WHO’s global
sodium benchmarks and reflects the best international practices in public health
regulation. The WHO and the United Nations Development Programme have
emphasized that investing in NCD prevention yields substantial economic returns by
reducing healthcare expenditures, productivity losses, and premature mortality. ⁶

By enacting this measure, the State affirms its constitutional duty to protect and promote
the right to health of all Filipinos. Sodium reformulation is not merely a regulatory
intervention—it is a strategic public health investment that reduces preventable deaths,
lowers dialysis dependence, eases the burden on PhilHealth, and safeguards the long-
term sustainability of the country’s healthcare system.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                     ARTICLE I
                                 GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION. 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Iwas Alat, Iwas Sakit Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and promote the right
to health of all Filipinos and instill health consciousness among them. The State shall safeguard
consumers from excessive dietary sodium intake, a major contributor to non-communicable
diseases such as hypertension, cardiovascular diseases, stroke, and kidney diseases, by
improving food environment through phased reformulation of prepackaged foods and
complementary public education efforts. To this end, the State shall require the reformulation
of prepackaged food products to gradually reduce sodium levels.

SEC. 3. Objectives. — This Act seeks to:
  a) Reduce population-wide sodium intake by requiring the reformulation of high-sodium
      prepackaged food products, including condiments and sauces, through a Stepwise
      Approach;
  b) Establish phased and science-based sodium reduction targets for high-impact food
      categories along with regular and timely monitoring of their sodium levels and
      consumption;
  c) Promote public awareness on the health risks of excessive sodium intake and encourage
      healthier food choices; and
  d) Foster collaboration among government, industry, and civil society to ensure effective
      and sustainable sodium reduction efforts.
  e)
SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. — For the purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
defined as follows:
    a. Baseline Data for Population Dietary Sodium Intake refers to the initial set of data
       collected and maintained by the Department of Health (DOH), in collaboration with the
       Department of Science and Technology–Food and Nutrition Research Institute (DOST-
       FNRI), to assess sodium consumption patterns among Filipinos. This data informs the
       establishment and periodic adjustment of sodium reformulation targets and public
       health strategies;

   b. Dietary sodium intake refers to the total amount of sodium consumed from food and
      beverages, including naturally occurring sodium, added sodium in processed foods,
      discretionary salt use, and sauces and condiments. Typically measured in milligrams
      (mg) per day, it is a key factor in assessing the risk of hypertension and other excessive
      sodium intake-related diseases and is influenced by eating habits, food processing
      methods, and seasoning use;

   c. Food refers to any substance or product, whether processed, partially processed, or
      unprocessed that is intended for human consumption. It includes drinks, chewing gum,
      water, condiments, sauces, and other substances that were used as an ingredient or a
      component in the manufacture, preparation, or treatment of food, such as oils and fats,
      whether sold alone or incorporated in processed food and/or prepackaged food;

   d. Food Business Operators refer to a person engaged in the food business and is
      responsible for ensuring that the requirements under this Act and existing laws are met
      by the food business under one’s control;

   e. High-impact food categories refer to prepackaged food products identified based on
      national dietary assessments and monitoring data as major contributors to population
      sodium intake. These categories may include processed meats, instant noodles, sauces,
      condiments, and other widely consumed high-sodium products;

   f. Maximum sodium limits refer to the highest allowable sodium content for prepackaged
      food products within specific food categories, as determined by the Department of
      Health (DOH). These limits are designed to be progressively reduced over time in
      alignment with national public health objectives;

   g. Micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) refer to food businesses as defined in
      Republic Act No. 9501 or the “Magna Carta for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises
      (MSMEs)”;

   h. National Sodium Content Database refers to a centralized repository established and
      maintained by the DOH, in collaboration with the Food and Drug Administration
      (FDA) and the DOST-FNRI, to track and monitor sodium levels in prepackaged food
      available in the Philippine market. This database supports regulatory compliance,
      policy adjustments, and public health monitoring;

   i. National Sodium Reduction Goals refer to the measurable public health objectives
      established under the National Sodium Reduction Plan pursuant to SEC. 15 of this Act,
      which set progressive targets for reducing population-wide dietary sodium intake over
      time. These goals shall be based on data from the Sodium Data System, aligned with
       internationally recognized public health benchmarks, and regularly updated to guide
       the setting of sodium reformulation targets and related policy measures;

   j. Prepackaged food refers to processed food prepared in advance and placed in a
      container, labelled and ready for sale or distribution, or for catering purposes;

   k. Processed food refers to food that has been subjected to some degree of processing
      (e.g., milling, drying, concentrating, canning, etc.) which partially or completely
      changes the physicochemical and/or sensory characteristics of the raw material;

   l. Sodium refers to a chemical element that exists naturally in food, associated with other
      molecular residues or atoms as an ionic bond, forming chemical salts. It is of great
      importance since it helps maintain the water balance of any organism, constituting its
      most common compound, sodium chloride or what is usually called table salt. Likewise,
      other sodium salts of industrial origin include monosodium glutamate (flavor
      enhancer); sodium benzoate, sodium acetate, sodium bicarbonate (acidity regulators
      and preservatives); sodium hydroxide, sodium carbonate (acidity regulator); sodium
      erythorbate (antioxidant), among others. One gram of “table salt” contains 400 mg of
      sodium (alternatively, 1 gram of sodium is equivalent to 2.5 grams of table salt);

   m. Sodium content refers to the total amount of sodium present in a food product, whether
      naturally occurring or added during processing. In prepackaged food, sodium content
      is typically indicated on the nutrition facts label, expressed in mg per serving or per 100
      grams, to help consumers make informed dietary choices;

   n. Sodium reduction refers to the process of lowering dietary sodium intake by
      reformulating food products, modifying eating habits, and implementing regulatory
      measures to align with public health recommendations;

   o. Sodium reformulation refers to the process of reducing the sodium content of
      prepackaged food;

   p. Sodium reformulation targets refer to benchmarks for reducing sodium levels in
      prepackaged food products, established based on findings from the Baseline Data for
      Population Dietary Sodium Intake and the National Sodium Content Database. These
      targets include the identification of high-impact food categories and setting of
      maximum sodium limits. These targets shall be regularly reviewed and adjusted,
      ensuring a phased and evidence-based approach to reformulation efforts.

                                        ARTICLE II
                                        COVERAGE

SEC. 5. Scope and Application. — This Act shall apply to all food business operators involved
in the production, manufacture, import, distribution, supply, advertisement, or marketing of
prepackaged food within the territory of the Philippines.

SEC. 6. Exemptions for Therapeutic Food Products. — Prepackaged food products for
special therapeutic dietary use that are specifically formulated to address particular
physiological conditions or intended as partial replacements for meals shall be exempt from
the maximum sodium limits prescribed under this Act.
                           ARTICLE III
            GENERAL FRAMEWORK ON SODIUM REFORMULATION

SEC. 7. Adoption of a Stepwise Approach in Reformulation. – The State shall adopt a
stepwise approach to sodium reformulation. The stepwise approach involves a gradual
reduction of sodium in food products, implemented in phases, to allow consumers to adjust to
the taste changes and to give the food industry time to adapt through research and development.
The approach shall be designed to reduce overall population sodium intake while maintaining
product palatability and consumer acceptance.

SEC. 8. Baseline Data for Population Dietary Sodium Intake. – Within one (1) year from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in collaboration with DOST-FNRI, shall establish the
Baseline Data for Population Dietary Sodium Intake, to assess sodium consumption patterns
among Filipinos. This may include age- and sex-disaggregated data on sodium consumption
patterns, key dietary sources of sodium, and regional variations in intake levels.

For this purpose, the DOST-FNRI shall conduct, or commission as necessary, 24-hour urinary
sodium collection as part of the National Nutrition Survey (NNS), in addition to dietary recall
and other validated methods of measurement.

The results of such studies, together with other relevant data on sodium consumption, shall be
directly integrated into the Sodium Data System created under this Act to inform the
identification of high-impact food categories, the establishment of sodium reformulation
targets, and the evaluation of progress in reducing population-wide sodium intake.

SEC. 9. Mandatory Sodium Reformulation of Prepackaged Foods and Grace Period
through a Stepwise Approach. —

      a. Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH shall establish the
         Baseline Data for Population Dietary Sodium Intake pursuant to SEC. 8 of this Act.

      b. After six (6) months from the completion of Baseline Data for Population Dietary
         Sodium Intake, the DOH, through an appropriate issuance, shall identify high-impact
         food categories and prescribe maximum sodium limits for each category in
         accordance with SEC. 10 of this Act.

      c. A grace period for compliance shall be set by the DOH, which shall not exceed one
         (1) year from the issuance of prescribed maximum sodium limits for each identified
         food category.

      d. After the grace period for compliance set in SEC. 9(c), no person or food business
         operator shall manufacture, import, distribute, or sell any prepackaged food item
         within the identified high-impact food categories if its final sodium content exceeds
         the prescribed maximum sodium limit. Compliance with the prescribed limits shall
         be a condition for the issuance or renewal of its Certificate of Product Registration
         (CPR) by the FDA.
      e. The DOH may expand the mandatory reformulation requirements to additional food
         categories, and shall progressively reduce maximum sodium limits to achieve
         national sodium reduction goals.

SEC. 10. Sodium Reformulation Targets. —

           a. High-Impact Food Categories — The DOH, in coordination with the FDA,
              DOST-FNRI, and other relevant stakeholders, shall identify high-impact food
              categories that contribute significantly to sodium intake. Identification shall be
              based on:
                   i. Data recorded in the Baseline Data for Population Dietary Sodium
                      Intake and the National Sodium Content Database;
                  ii. International best practices and guidelines issued by the World Health
                      Organization (WHO) and other public health bodies; and
                 iii. Consumer dietary patterns, ensuring a smooth transition to low-sodium
                      options.

           b. Maximum Sodium Limits — Pursuant to SEC. 9 of this Act, the DOH shall
              establish maximum sodium limits for each identified food category, ensuring
              that these limits align with national sodium reduction goals. The determination
              of these limits shall be guided by national sodium reduction goals and data from
              the Baseline Data for Population Dietary Sodium Intake and the National
              Sodium Content Database, ensuring that policy decisions are supported by
              robust and up-to-date evidence. These limits shall be progressively reduced over
              time, considering public health goals, feasibility, and industry adaptation
              capacity, and consistent with the Philippine Nutrient Profile Model (PNPM)
              issued by the DOH, and other authoritative public health standards, such as the
              WHO Sodium Benchmarks.

SEC. 11. Voluntary Sodium Reformulation. —

       a. After the grace period set in SEC. 9(c) of this Act, food business operators are
          encouraged to voluntarily reduce the sodium content of their prepackaged food
          products for food categories not yet covered by mandatory reformulation.

       b. The DOH and the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) shall provide incentives
          to support early voluntary compliance, which may include, but shall not be limited
          to:
                   i. Inclusion of sodium limits in procurement criteria; Certifications or
                      awards recognizing industry compliance;
                  ii. Priority product registration under the Food Safety Act; and/or
                 iii. Capacity-building programs for sodium reformulation strategies.

SEC. 12. Marketing and Labeling Restrictions. — Compliance with the mandatory sodium
limits established under this Act shall not be used as a basis for marketing or promotional
claims. No food manufacturer, distributor, or importer shall label, advertise, or promote their
products with statements such as “low sodium,” “reduced sodium,” or similar terms if such
claims merely reflect compliance with the minimum standards required by this Act, unless the
product likewise meets the specific nutrient content claim conditions established under the
Codex Alimentarius, or Food Code, and adopted by the FDA.
For voluntary reductions beyond the mandatory limits, nutrient content claims relating to
sodium may be permitted only if they are consistent with Republic Act No. 10611, otherwise
known as the “Food Safety Act of 2013,” and other relevant food labeling and consumer
protection laws, issuances, and regulations. Such claims shall, at all times, be truthful, not
misleading, and reflective of a meaningful reduction in sodium content, as determined by the
DOH and FDA.


                                       ARTICLE IV
                                    IMPLEMENTATION

SEC. 13. Lead Agency. – The DOH is tasked with ensuring the implementation of the
provisions of this Act and shall have the power to issue other policies, rules, regulations, and
standards for the implementation of this Act. In line with this, the DOH shall review, expand
the coverage of food categories, and progressively tighten sodium limits every three (3) years,
or earlier, if supported by updated evidence from the Sodium Data System in accordance with
Section 16 herein or other credible scientific sources.

SEC. 14. Inter-Agency Committee (IAC) on Sodium Reformulation. — To assist in the
effective setting, review, and updating of sodium reformulation targets, there is hereby created
an Inter-Agency Committee (IAC) on Sodium Reformulation composed of representatives
from the DOH, FDA, DOST-FNRI, Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of
Education (DepEd), Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), and the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG).

The IAC shall provide technical advice, ensure cross-sectoral coordination, and recommend
revisions to sodium limits every three (3) years. To ensure transparency, the IAC may, when
necessary, conduct separate public consultations with industry stakeholders for transparency
and data gathering, provided that industry stakeholders shall not be given membership or voting
rights.

To safeguard the integrity and independence of the Committee, all members of the IAC shall
adhere to the conflict of interest provisions under Section 24 of this Act.

SEC. 15. National Sodium Reduction Plan. — The DOH shall establish and update, every
three (3) years, a National Sodium Reduction Plan, which shall set National Sodium Reduction
Goals, and the timeline and strategies to be implemented to ensure measurable progress toward
achieving such goals. The long-term National Sodium Reduction Goal under this Act shall be
to reduce average population sodium intake to no more than 2,000 milligrams (mg) per day,
consistent with the World Health Organization’s recommended daily intake.

SEC. 16. Sodium Data System. — The DOH shall integrate the Baseline Data for Population
Dietary Sodium Intake into a comprehensive data system to inform reformulation targets and
monitor progress in sodium reduction efforts, comprised of:

     a. Data for Population Dietary Sodium Intake — The DOH, in collaboration with the
        DOST-FNRI, shall collect and maintain data on population dietary sodium intake to
        evaluate sodium consumption trends. Data may be sourced from The National
        Nutrition Survey (NNS), conducted by the DOST-FNRI every three (3) years; 24-hour
        urinary sodium excretion studies which shall be included in the NNS conducted by the
        DOST-FNRI in accordance with Section 8 of this Act; Household food expenditure
        and procurement data; Industry and market surveys tracking sodium levels in
        commonly consumed high-impact food products; and/or any other method necessary
        to ensure a comprehensive sodium intake assessment.

     b. National Sodium Content Database — The DOH, in collaboration with the FDA and
        the DOST-FNRI, shall develop and maintain a National Sodium Content Database to
        systematically track and monitor the sodium content of prepackaged food available in
        the Philippine market. The database shall compile and regularly update sodium content
        information for all commercially available prepackaged food, including sodium
        content data from nutrition labels submitted by food business operators to the FDA as
        part of product registration, as well as other existing data from relevant national
        agencies; support regulatory enforcement by identifying non-compliant products and
        monitoring adherence to sodium reformulation targets; and serve as a resource for
        public health initiatives, research, and consumer awareness campaigns to promote
        informed food choices.

     c. Data Sharing and Coordination — The DOH, FDA, DOST-FNRI, and the DEPDev
        shall establish clear data-sharing protocols to facilitate the timely access, analysis, and
        publication of sodium intake and content data. Such protocols shall address legal
        clearance processes and mandate that all collaborating agencies provide data to the
        DOH and to each other in a timely and transparent manner.

The DOH shall ensure accessibility of sodium content data to stakeholders, including
policymakers, health professionals, researchers, and consumers, through appropriate public
dissemination platforms, while maintaining data protection standards in accordance with
Republic Act No. 10173 or the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and its Implementing Rules and
Regulations.

The DOH shall update the Data for Population Dietary Sodium Intake every three (3) years and
the National Sodium Content Database annually to ensure ongoing policy effectiveness and
accurate monitoring of both dietary sodium intake among Filipinos and sodium content in
prepackaged food.

                                 ARTICLE V
                         ENFORCEMENT AND MONITORING

SEC. 17. Incentives for Low-Sodium Alternatives. — To promote voluntary reformulation,
foster industry innovation, and increase the availability of low-sodium alternatives in the
Philippine market in advance of mandatory requirements, the DTI, in coordination with the
DOH, FDA, Department of Agriculture (DA), and other relevant government agencies and
stakeholders, shall develop and implement an incentive program that will encourage the
development, production, and promotion of low-sodium food products and alternatives, such
as iodized reduced-sodium salts and other lower-sodium salt substitutes, as well as
reformulated lower-sodium versions of commonly consumed prepackaged food.

Incentives may include financial grants, non-financial incentives such as certifications and
awards, capacity-building programs through the DTI’s Food Processing Division and Food
Innovation Hub, and consumer education campaigns conducted in partnership with the DOH,
among others.

SEC. 18. MSME Support and Compliance. — The DTI, in coordination with the DOH, FDA,
DA, local government units (LGUs), and other relevant government agencies and stakeholders,
shall develop and implement programs to assist micro, small, and medium-sized (MSMEs)
food business operators of prepackaged food in complying with the provisions of this Act.

This support shall include, but shall not be limited to, extended grace periods, targeted capacity-
building programs, incentives for voluntary compliance, development of resource materials,
technical assistance for research and development, and any other necessary support to help
MSMEs meet sodium reformulation requirements while maintaining product safety, quality,
and affordability.

SEC. 19. Social and Behavior Change Communication (SBCC). — The DOH, in
collaboration with relevant government agencies, LGUs, and stakeholders, shall develop and
implement SBCC strategies to promote healthier dietary habits, address cultural preferences
for high-sodium foods, and reduce health risks associated with excessive sodium intake.

The DOH, in coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd) and other relevant
government agencies, shall incorporate sodium reduction education into school curricula and
existing health and nutrition programs, including Dietary Supplementation Program (DSP) and
School-Based Feeding Program (SBFP), and prenatal health screenings.

Using baseline survey data and the National Sodium Content Database, the DOH shall tailor
SBCC strategies to specific dietary patterns and sodium consumption levels. Public awareness
initiatives shall highlight successful reformulation efforts and sodium intake reductions to
encourage continued support, promote the availability of affordable low-sodium alternatives,
and emphasize the long-term health and financial savings for Filipino families.

All SBCC initiatives under this Act shall be designed to remain consistent with the objectives
of Republic Act No. 8172, otherwise known as the ASIN Law, to ensure that sodium reduction
policies are implemented without undermining iodine sufficiency and the prevention of iodine
deficiency disorders.

SEC. 20. Certificate of Product Registration and Marketing Restrictions. — The FDA
shall not issue a Certificate of Product Registration (CPR) or renew an existing CPR to any
food manufacturer, distributor, and importer whose prepackaged food product does not comply
with Section 9 of this Act.

SEC. 21. Inspection Powers and Record-Keeping. — The FDA, through its authorized
agents, shall have the power to inspect the premises and records of food manufacturers to
determine compliance with this Act. The FDA shall issue inspection procedures and guidelines
on record-keeping.

SEC. 22. Enforcement for Traditional Markets and Food Service Establishments. —
LGUs, through an appropriate issuance and in coordination with the FDA and DTI, shall
establish a mechanism to enforce the provisions of this Act on prepackaged food produced and
marketed in traditional markets and food service establishments within their respective
jurisdictions and shall impose penalties for violations thereof.
SEC. 23. Consumer Complaints. — The FDA may commence an investigation motu proprio,
upon petition, or upon letter-complaint from any consumer for violations of this Act. Upon
finding by the FDA of prima facie violation of any provisions of this Act, it may motu proprio
or upon a verified complaint, commence formal administrative action against the food business
operator or any person who appears responsible.

The procedure in the commencement and processing of consumer complaints mentioned herein
shall follow the existing rules and regulations of the FDA.

SEC. 24. Conflict of Interest. — To uphold the fundamental objective of this Act in advancing
public health, the development of related policies and the enforcement and monitoring of this
Act shall promote multi-sectoral coordination, while ensuring the integrity and independence
of regulatory processes against potential conflicts of interest.

The DOH and FDA shall establish and enforce clear guidelines for managing conflicts of
interest in all stakeholder engagements. Such guidelines shall include, at the minimum:

   i.   Requiring declarations of conflict of interest from all persons or entities that take part,
        directly or indirectly, in setting and implementing policies under this Act;

 ii.    Prohibiting participation in the IAC and in policymaking processes by any person or
        entity with a conflict of interest, except in the context of public consultations conducted
        for transparency and accountability;

 iii.   Requiring all members of the IAC, DOH, and FDA to disclose any instance of
        interaction with the food and beverage industry, and making such disclosures publicly
        accessible;

 iv.    Prohibiting the inclusion, as members of the IAC or any implementing body under this
        Act, of any person or entity upholding or accommodating the interests of the food and
        beverage industry;

  v.    Prohibiting members of the IAC, DOH, FDA, or any implementing agency from
        receiving, directly or indirectly, any contribution, compensation, sponsorship, or benefit
        from the food and beverage industry;

 vi.    Requiring the Secretariat to document and report any attempt at interference by the food
        and beverage industry in the implementation of this Act, with non-compliance
        constituting grounds for removal of said member and other applicable sanctions under
        existing laws, rules, and regulations.

SEC. 25. Multi-Sectoral Collaboration and Civil Society Participation for Enforcement,
Monitoring, and Surveillance. —

   a)   The State recognizes the critical role of civil society organizations, academic
        institutions, and the private sector in promoting public health and supporting the
        objectives of this Act. The DOH, FDA, and DOST-FNRI may engage civil society
        organizations, academia, and other stakeholders to provide technical assistance in the
        implementation and monitoring of the provisions of this Act, provided that such
       organizations are not affiliated with or related to the food and beverage industry. The
       DOH shall ensure that all stakeholder engagements are conducted transparently and
       limited to regulatory purposes. In addition, all multi-sectoral engagements, including
       those involving civil society, the private sector, and academic institutions, shall adhere
       to the conflict of interest guidelines set forth in Section 24 of this Act. The DOH shall
       develop and implement programs to encourage the participation of civil society and
       grassroots organizations in the monitoring and enforcement of this Act. These programs
       shall ensure balanced representation and safeguard public health interests by
       prioritizing transparency and accountability in policy implementation.

  a.   Any citizen, civil society organization, non-governmental organization, or grassroots
       organization is hereby authorized to file an appropriate civil, criminal, or administrative
       action before the proper courts or administrative body against any public officer or
       entity who willfully or grossly neglects the performance of a responsibility specifically
       enjoined by this Act and its implementing rules and regulations. The same authorization
       applies to violations of this Act by any member of the food and beverage industry,
       including their representatives or employees.

The court shall exempt such actions from the payment of filing fees and shall exempt the
plaintiff from the filing of a bond for the issuance of preliminary injunctions. Should the
plaintiff prevail, the court shall award reasonable attorney’s fees, moral damages, and litigation
costs as appropriate.

The remedies provided under this Section shall be cumulative and shall not preclude any other
person from filing a similar action under applicable laws.


                                   ARTICLE VI
                          PROHIBITED ACTS AND PENALTIES

SEC. 26. Prohibited Acts. — It shall be unlawful for any person to:

   a) Produce, manufacture, import to the Philippines, distribute, sell, supply, advertise or
      market any prepackaged food falling under the identified high-impact food categories
      that exceeds the prescribed maximum sodium limits in accordance with Section 9 of
      this Act;
   b) Use “low sodium,” “reduced sodium,” or similar claims when such statements merely
      reflect compliance with mandatory limits, or when the claims do not meet the specific
      conditions established under the Codex Alimentarius as adopted by the FDA, or when
      voluntary claims are false, misleading, or inconsistent with relevant food labeling and
      consumer protection laws, issuances, and regulations in accordance with Section 12 of
      this Act;
   c) Refuse access to pertinent records or entry of inspection officers of the FDA and LGUs

SEC. 28. Sale, Distribution or Transfer in Good Faith. — No person shall be subject to the
penalties under Section 27 of this Act for having sold, offered for sale, or transferred
prepackaged food that are not compliant with the provisions of Section 9 of this Act, if such
sale, distribution or transfer was made in good faith. A sale, distribution, or transfer is presumed
to be made in good faith if the seller or distributor has obtained, in writing, a guaranty from the
person or food business operator from whom the prepackaged food were obtained, that said
prepackaged food comply with the requirements under Section 9 of this Act.

SEC. 29. Other Corrective Actions. — In addition to the fines and penalties in Section 27,
the following corrective actions shall be imposed by the appropriate authorities to the
offenders:
    a) The disapproval of an application, suspension, revocation or cancellation of an existing
       CPR or any authorization; and/or
    b) Seizure and condemnation, destruction, recall, and/or appropriate disposition of non-
       compliant food products by the FDA as provided under their mandate.

SEC. 30. Liability of Juridical and Other Persons. —

                                       ARTICLE VII
                                    FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 31. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOH, through the Health Promotion Bureau (HPB), together with
other relevant government agencies and stakeholders shall develop the IRR for this Act. Non-
issuance of the IRR shall not prevent the implementation of this Act upon its effectivity.

SEC. 32. Monitoring and Evaluation. — The DOH shall report annually, or more frequently
as necessary, to the President and the Congressional Committees on Health, Agriculture and
Food, and Trade and Industry on the population dietary sodium intake, the sodium content of
prepackaged food products, and progress in meeting national sodium reduction targets. The
FDA shall likewise report annually, or more frequently as needed, to the same bodies on
industry compliance. These reports shall include recommendations for policy adjustments,
regulatory enhancements, and program improvements to strengthen sodium reduction efforts
and protect public health nationwide.

SEC. 33. Appropriations. — The initial amount necessary for the implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriation of all concerned agencies. Thereafter, the
DOH, FDA, DOST-FNRI, and other concerned agencies shall be provided with adequate
funding for the performance of their functions under this Act, including post-market
surveillance, inspection, data management, compliance monitoring, and enforcement of
sodium reformulation targets, under the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).

SEC. 34. Separability Clause. — If any part or provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

SEC. 35. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, and rules and regulations
contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended
accordingly.

SEC. 36. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in any newspaper of general circulation.
