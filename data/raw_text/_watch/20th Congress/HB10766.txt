                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The strength of any democracy rests upon an informed and discerning
electorate. Each election cycle, millions of young Filipinos become eligible voters,
and that is why it is urgent and imperative to prepare them for this life-changing
civic participation.
        The rise of digital platforms has transformed the way Filipinos receive
political information. Like a double-edged sword, technology has widened access to
public discourse but has also exposed citizens, particularly the youth, to
misinformation, disinformation, historical distortion, and political propaganda
disseminated through social media and other online platforms. As stated in a
research published in 20201, the Philippines has been in a “post-truth era,” wherein
public opinion is often shaped less by verified facts and more by emotionally
charged narratives, viral content, and algorithm-driven information consumption2.
This development poses serious risks to democratic participation and electoral
integrity.

        This phenomenon in the Philippines demonstrates the growing influence of
digital misinformation in shaping political behavior. Numerous studies and reports3
identified the country as highly vulnerable to organized online disinformation
campaigns, including the use of troll networks, fake accounts, and coordinated
propaganda to manipulate public opinion and electoral discourse. More recently,
researchers documented the continuing proliferation of fake and coordinated online
activities surrounding the Philippine elections, emphasizing the need to strengthen
citizens’ capacity to critically assess political information.

        Despite the importance of civic participation, voter education among young
Filipinos remains largely limited to election-period campaigns or shallow
discussions in classrooms. Many first-time voters enter the electoral process without
a clear understanding of democratic institutions, electoral systems, governance,
media literacy, or the responsibilities accompanying the right to suffrage.

         This measure seeks to institutionalize voter education in the Junior High
School and Senior High School curricula to cultivate politically aware, media-
literate, and critically thinking Filipino citizens. By integrating age-appropriate
lessons on democratic participation, electoral processes, responsible citizenship, fact-
checking, digital literacy, and critical evaluation of political information, students
will be better equipped to distinguish truth from propaganda and make informed
electoral choices.

        More importantly, this bill recognizes that voting is not merely a
constitutional right but a solemn civic responsibility. Schools must help prepare
future voters not only to participate in elections, but to exercise discernment, resist
manipulation, and uphold democratic values.

        Sa panahong mabilis ang pagkalat ng mga propaganda, tahasang pagsisinungaling

Kabataan na maging mapanuri, maalam, kritikal, at responsableng mamamayan ng ating

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

AN ACT INTEGRATING VOTER EDUCATION IN THE JUNIOR AND
            SENIOR HIGH SCHOOL CURRICULA

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Compulsory
Voter Education Act.”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to enlighten citizens on their right to vote and to inculcate in them the duty to
preserve the integrity of the electoral process through the educational system.

        SEC. 3. Definition of Voter Education. –As used in this Act, "Voter
Education" refers to the dissemination of information, materials, and
programs designed to inform citizens about the specifics and mechanics of
the voting process. It includes information on the right of suffrage, its
importance, and the proper exercise thereof, as well as information on who is
eligible to vote; where and how to register; how electors may verify their
inclusion in the voter list; the type of elections being held; where, when, and
how to vote; who the candidates are; and how to file complaints.

       For purposes of this Act, Voter Education also means the full
understanding of the electoral process and of students' responsibilities as
future voters. It includes, among others, the functions, qualifications, and
responsibilities of elective officials; the various elective positions in local and
national government; the powers and duties attached to each position; and
the importance of evaluating candidates based on their qualifications,
platforms, and public service record.

        Voter Education shall also include media literacy, developed to build
critical thinking among Junior and Senior High School students, enabling
them to distinguish whether online material constitutes propaganda,
misinformation, or reliable information.
         SEC. 4. Integration of Voter Education in the Junior and Senior High
School Curricula. – Voter Education shall be integrated by the Department of
Education (DepEd) in the junior and senior high school curricula, effective at the
start of the school year immediately following one (1) year from the effectivity of this
Act. The one (1) year transition period by conducting seminars and trainings on the
electoral system and process. COMELEC shall certify that the teacher, after
attending such seminars and trainings, is qualified to teach topics relating to the
electoral system and process.

     SEC. 5. Voter Education Syllabus. – This section outlines the core
components of the Voter Education Syllabus, presenting the following:

        Voter education. – The main syllabus shall include, but not limited to,
the following thematic areas:

1. Suffrage – This area introduces to students the right to vote accorded by
the Constitution, and other rights enabling citizens to participate in the
conduct of public affairs;

2. Electoral system and process – This area deals with the general principles
underlying the present electoral system, and legislation regulating all the
phases of election namely, pre-election, election proper, and post-election. It
discusses the proper conduct of elections including registration, casting of
ballots, and counting of votes;

3. Elective offices— This area concentrates on the various elective positions in
local and national government, including but not limited to positions at the
barangay, municipal, city, provincial, and national levels. It covers the
qualifications, terms of office, and duties attached to each position, as well as
the powers, functions, and accountability mechanisms governing elected
officials. It also emphasizes the importance of choosing candidates based on
their qualifications, platforms, track record, and demonstrated commitment
to public service, rather than on popularity, patronage, or personal ties.

4.Educated voting – This area focuses on the attitude and behavior a voter must
adopt to ensure fair, free, and honest elections. It emphasizes the importance
of participating in elections in a manner that shall stimulate and strengthen
democracy.

5. Media Literacy- This area develops the critical thinking skills of students to
enable them to responsibly navigate election-related information across print,
broadcast, and online platforms. It covers the identification of fake news,
disinformation, and propaganda; the verification of sources and claims
through fact-checking; an understanding of how algorithms and social media
shape the spread of political content; and the responsible use of social media
in discussing candidates, platforms, and electoral issues.

6. Anti-Political Dynasty Provision of the 1987 Constitution - This area discusses
Article II, Section 26 of the 1987 Constitution, which provides that "the State
shall guarantee equal access to opportunities for public service, and prohibit
political dynasties as may be defined by law." It examines the history and
intent behind this provision, which include the framers' discussion, minutes,
and deliberations. This area must explain the distinction between self-
executory and non-self-executory constitutional provisions, and why the anti-
political dynasty clause requires enabling legislation from Congress to be
implemented. Finally, it discusses the role of Congress in enacting an anti-
political dynasty law, the legislative history of related bills filed over the
years, and the reasons such measures have yet to be passed into law.

       SEC. 6. Implementing Agency. – The Secretary of Education is hereby
authorized and directed to adopt a system to implement and carry out the
provisions of this Act. The Secretary of Education shall, within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, to promulgate the rules and regulations, including
those of a disciplinary nature, to carry out and enforce the provisions of this Act.

       SEC. 7. Educational Materials. – It shall be obligatory on all schools
offering secondary education to keep in their libraries an adequate number of
copies of the textbooks, primers, and leaflets on voter education to be issued by
the DepEd. The DepEd shall determine the adequacy of the number of books,
depending upon the enrolment of the school.

        SEC. 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such declaration
shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, and rules and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or amended
accordingly.

        SEC. 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
