                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE


        Investment is a key driver of economic growth, employment generation, and technological
development. Strategic investments—particularly those involving infrastructure, advanced
industries, and sustainable technologies—can produce significant economic spillovers and
contribute to long-term national development.

        Despite ongoing reforms, investors in the Philippines continue to face regulatory and
administrative barriers that delay the implementation of major projects. Large-scale investments
often require permits and approvals from multiple national government agencies and local
government units, resulting in fragmented procedures and prolonged processing timelines. At the
same time, foreign investment inflows remain modest compared with regional peers. Data from the
Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) show that the Philippines recorded approximately USD 8.93
billion in foreign direct investment (FDI) inflows in 2024, highlighting the importance of
strengthening the country’s investment climate and improving regulatory efficiency to attract
greater investment.

       International experience demonstrates that countries competing for strategic investments
have adopted streamlined regulatory systems to accelerate project approvals. Neighboring
economies in Southeast Asia have implemented centralized investment facilitation mechanisms
and single-window approval systems to reduce administrative burdens and expedite investment
processes.

        Recognizing these challenges, the Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028 underscores
the need to improve the country’s investment facilitation framework and strengthen coordination
among government agencies involved in investment approvals. Facilitating the timely
implementation of strategic investments will support infrastructure development, promote
industrial diversification, generate high-value employment, and enhance the country’s global
competitiveness.

        This measure therefore seeks to establish Green Lanes for Strategic Investments, supported
by a One-Stop Action Center for Strategic Investments (OSAC-SI) under the Department of Trade
and Industry – Board of Investments. The proposed mechanism will streamline the processing of
permits, licenses, certifications, and authorizations across national government agencies and local
government units by establishing dedicated green lanes and improving inter-agency coordination.

        By institutionalizing an efficient and integrated investment facilitation system, this bill
aims to accelerate the implementation of priority investment projects, strengthen investor
confidence, and position the Philippines as a more competitive destination for both domestic and
foreign investments.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

        AN ACT ESTABLISHING GREEN LANES FOR STRATEGIC INVESTMENTS

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

1            SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Green Lanes System for
2    Strategic Investments Act.”

 3           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to: It is the
 4   policy of the State to promote and facilitate investments in the country, particularly those that are
 5   aligned with the Philippine Development Plan or any similar national development plan and can be
 6   characterized by significant capital or investment to the country, consequential economic impact,
 7   positive impact on the environment, significant contribution to the country's balance of payments,
 8   and improvement in the country's infrastructure capabilities. To achieve this, it is necessary to
 9   establish a One-Stop Action Center for Strategic Investments to streamline and expedite the
10   issuance of permits, licenses, certifications, or authorizations.

11          SEC. 3. Coverage. This Act covers all national government agencies (NGAs) and their
12   regional and provincial offices, government-owned or -controlled corporations (GOCCs), and
13   other government instrumentalities, as well as local government units (LGUs), involved in the
14   issuance of permits, licenses, certifications or authorizations (collectively referred to herein as
15   "permit/s and/or license/s") covering Strategic Investments specified under Section 2 hereof.
16
17            SEC. 4. Strategic Investments. Strategic Investments are those which are aligned with the
18   Philippine Development Plan or any similar national development plan; and can be characterized
19   by the significant capital or investment to the country; consequential economic impact; positive
20   impact on the environment; significant contribution to the country's balance of payments; with
21   complex technical processes and engineering designs; and will bring about improvement in the
22   country's infrastructure capabilities.
23            For purposes of this Act, Strategic Investments shall include, but shall not be limited to,
24   the following:
25
26               a) Highly Desirable Projects. Investment Projects recommended by the Fiscal
27                  Incentives Review Board to the President for modification of incentives or the

 2                 strategies for creating high-value jobs, building new industries to diversify
 3                 economic activities, and attracting significant foreign and domestic capital or
 4                 investment, and the fiscal requirements of the activity or project, as provided under
 5                 Section 301 of RA No. 11534 or the ''Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for
 6                 Enterprises (CREATE) Act. "
 7              b) Foreign Direct Investments. Foreign Direct Investments are those resulting from
 8                 the implementation of the Foreign Invesünent Promotion and Marketing Plan, as

13                 the SIPP formulated by the Department of Trade and Industry-Board of
14                 Investments (DTI-BOI) and approved by the President in accordance with Section
15                 300 of RA No. 11534. These projects cover registrations under the DTI-BOI or
16                 other Investment Promotion Agencies.

18           SEC. 5. Single Point of Entry. Within six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act, the
19   DTIBOI shall establish a One-Stop-Action-Center for Strategic Investments (OSAC-SI), which
20   shall serve as the single point of entry for all projects qualified as Strategic Investments.
21
22            The OSAC-SI shall address investor concerns starting with identifying and designating an
23   investment as Strategic Investment under this Act and endorse the same to concerned NGAs, LGUs,
24   and/or quasi-judicial bodies for processing of permits and licenses, and monitoring and reporting
25   of actions taken thereon. The OSAC-SI shall include aftercare or post-investment assistance as part
26   of its services.
27
28           Within three (3) months from the issuance of this Act, the DTI-BOI shall produce and
29   regularly update, an investor manual or guidebook or its equivalent, containing the list of
30   government requirements for the establishment of Strategic Investments per sector, as well as the
31   concerned NGAs, LGUs or quasi-judicial bodies issuing the relevant permits and licenses.
32
33          The DTI-BOI will be supported by additional manpower, including the designation of
34   Account Officers for Strategic Investments, as well as the equipment necessary to operationalize
35   the OSACSI, in coordination with the Department of Budget and Management.
36
37           SEC. 6. Green Lanes for Strategic Investments. The concerned NGAs, including their
38   regional and provincial offices when applicable, LGUs, and quasi-judicial bodies shall establish or
39   designate, whichever is applicable, a Green Lane within their offices in charge of expediting and
40   streamlining the processes and requirements for the issuance of permits and licenses of Strategic
41   Investments endorsed by the OSAC-SI. The said Green Lane shall be integrated and shall not be
42   duplicative of the existing one- stop shops or one-stop action centers established under existing
43   laws such as, RA No. 11534, RA No. 11234 or the "Energy Virtual One-Stop Shop Act," and RA
44   No. 11032.

 1          For this purpose, the above agencies or offices shall designate an account officer for the
 2   Green Lane, who is responsible for coordinating with the OSAC-SI, relevant government offices
 3   or agencies in streamlining and simplifying business permitting and licensing processes, and
 4   recommending modifications or amendments to existing procedures that are outdated, redundant,
 5   and adds undue regulatory burden and cost to the transacting public, in coordination with the Anti-
 6   Red Tape Authority (ARTA).

 8           SEC. 7. Action on the Application. NGAs and LGUs shall act on the applications for the
 9   issuance of a permit or license within the prescribed processing time provided in the concerned
10   agency or LGUs Citizen's Charter (original period), which shall not be longer than three (3)
11   working days in the case of simple transactions, seven (7) working days in the case of complex
12   transactions, and twenty (20) working days for highly technical transactions from the date the
13   complete application was received. The said prescribed maximum processing period may be
14   extended only once for the same number of days, provided the same is indicated in the Citizen's
15   Charter (extension period), pursuant to Rule VII, Section 3(b) of the Implementing Rules and
16   Regulations of RA No. 11032. In all cases, denial of such application shall be made in writing
17   within the same period prescribed.
18
19          Consistent with Section 10 of RA No. 9485, as amended, the complete application for the
20   issuance of a permit or license shall be deemed approved in case of failure of the NGA or LGU to
21   act, whether to approve or disapprove, on said application. An application shall be considered
22   "complete" when all the documentary requirements indicated in the respective Citizen's Charters
23   are submitted and the required fees and charges are duly paid.
24
25            Imposition of additional requirements other than those listed in the Citizen's Charter and
26   imposition of additional costs not reflected in the Citizen's Charter shall be punished in accordance
27   with Sections 21 and 22 of RA No. 9485, as amended. However, with respect to applications for
28   registration and tax incentives under RA No. 11534, the same shall not be deemed approved in case
29   of failure of the NGA or LGU to act on said applications.
30
31           SEC. 8. Issuance of Permits and Licenses. After the lapse of the original or extension
32   period under Section 7 of this Act, the NGA or LGU shall issue the requested permit or license,
33   otherwise, the DTI-BOI shall endorse the case to the ARTA. Upon receipt of the endorsement,
34   together with the presentation of the acknowledgment receipt and/or official receipt of the payment
35   of the necessary license or permit fees, and other transaction costs, and upon due investigation and
36   verification that the applicant has indeed fully submitted all necessary documents and paid all the
37   required fees, the ARTA shall issue a declaration of completeness and order the concerned office
38   or agency to issue the appurtenant approval, extension, and/or renewal of the license, clearance,
39   permit, certification, or authorization which is deemed approved pursuant to Section 10 of RA No.
40   9485, as amended.
41
42           The above mechanism shall likewise apply to issuance of a license or permit by quasi-
43   judicial bodies consistent with Rule VIII Section I (b) of the Implementing Rules and Regulations
44   of RA No. 9485, as amended.

 1           SEC. 9. Simultaneous Processing of Applications. NGAs and LGUs that receive
 2   applications for permits and licenses shall process the same with the presumption that the relevant
 3   documents from other agencies have already been issued consistent with Rule VIl Section 3(c) of
 4   the Implementing Rules and Regulations of RA No. 9485, as amended. The applicant shall execute
 5   an affidavit of undertaking, using the template prescribed by the DTI-BOI, that it has secured the
 6   relevant documents from specific NGAs or LGUs and/or that it shall submit the complete
 7   documentary requirements within thirty (30) working days.
 8
 9           For this purpose, the concerned NGAs and/or LGUs may coordinate with other relevant
10   offices or agencies to check or verify the status of applications for related permits or licenses to
11   avoid delay in their own evaluation of applications pending with them. The NGA or LGU
12   concerned shall then issue the corresponding permits or licenses, with an annotation that it shall be
13   subject to the completion of requirements covered by the undertaking.
14
15           SEC. 10. Monitoring. Each concerned NGA, LGU and/or quasi-judicial body shall submit
16   to the DTI-BOI monthly updates regarding the status of applications received and acted upon
17   involving Strategic Investments covered under this Act.
18
19           SEC. 11. Electronic Submission. The concerned NGA or LGU shall enable the electronic
20   submission of application for, and issuance of, license, clearance, permit, certification or
21   authorization, including payment and issuance of receipts, whenever applicable.
22
23           All remaining cities and municipalities that have yet to comply with Section I I (c) of RA
24   No. 9485, as amended, shall facilitate the computerization of their respective business permit and
25   licensing systems. To this end, the Department of Information and Communications Technology
26   (DICT) shall make available to LGUs the software for the computerization of the business permit
27   and licensing system. The DICT, DTI, and Department of the Interior and Local Government
28   (DILG), shall provide technical assistance in the planning and implementation of a computerized
29   or software-enabled business permitting and licensing system of LGUs.
30
31           SEC. 12. Technical Working Group. A Technical Working Group (TWG) is hereby
32   created as an oversight body that shall ensure the implementation of this Act. The TWG shall be
33   headed by the DTI-BOI with the representatives from the following agencies as members: (i) DTI;
34   (ii) DILG; (iii) Department of Finance (DOF); (iv) National Economic and Development Authority
35   (NEDA); and (v) ARTA.
36
37           The TWG shall act on complaints and take appropriate actions, consistent with their
38   respective mandates, against third-party business consultants engaged by foreign and local
39   investors who act in violation of RA No. 9485, as amended, or prejudice the interest of the project
40   proponents and/or the government.
41
42          The DTI OSAC-SI Account Officers and the action officers of NGAs, LGUs, GOCCs and
43   other government instrumentalities, shall directly coordinate with each other to ensure the
44   successful implementation of this Act.
