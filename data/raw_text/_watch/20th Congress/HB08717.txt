SPACES ACT”

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Republic Act No. 11313, otherwise known as the “Safe Spaces Act” or “Bawal
Bastos Law,” was enacted in 2019 to prevent and penalize gender-based sexual
harassment in streets, public spaces, workplaces, educational institutions, and online
platforms. The law marked a significant step in protecting individuals, particularly
women and marginalized groups, from harassment and abuse in both physical and
digital environments.

         Despite this progress, evolving technologies and the increasing prevalence of
online interactions have exposed gaps in the current law. New platforms, including
artificial intelligence and digital communication tools, have created additional avenues
for harassment, intimidation, and exploitation. Reports from law enforcement and
government agencies also indicate that gender-based sexual harassment continues
to occur in workplaces, schools, and online spaces, often going underreported due to
fear of retaliation, stigma, or victim-blaming1.

       Data from the Philippine National Police (PNP) recorded hundreds of violations
of the Safe Spaces Act, while reports from the Department of Education (DepEd) and
other agencies reveal continuing incidents of sexual harassment and abuse in

        This measure seeks to strengthen the Safe Spaces Act by expanding the
definitions of prohibited acts, including the recognition of grooming as a form of
predatory conduct, and by addressing gender-based online harassment committed
through emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence. It likewise imposes stiffer
penalties for gender-based sexual harassment in workplaces and educational or
training institutions, where power imbalances may place victims in particularly
vulnerable situations. The measure also promotes greater accountability for public
officials and government employees who engage in or promote gender-based
harassment.

       It must be noted that the Philippines has reaffirmed its commitment to
eliminating violence and harassment in the world of work through the ratification of
International Labour Organization (ILO) Convention No. 1904, which recognizes every
worker’s right to a workplace free from violence and harassment. This measure
complements these commitments by ensuring that existing national laws remain
responsive to emerging threats and evolving social realities.

      By strengthening penalties, expanding protections, and reinforcing
accountability mechanisms, this proposed measure seeks to ensure that the Safe
Spaces Act continues to effectively safeguard dignity, equality, and safety in
workplaces, educational institutions, public spaces, and digital environments.

       In the 20th Congress, this bill adopts the version of Representatives Sarah Jane
Elago, Antonio Tinio, Renee Louise Co, Arlene Bag-ao, Jose Manuel Diokno, Percival
Cendaña and Dadah Kiram Ismula. Further, similar bills have been filed by
Representatives JC Abalos, Ching Bernos, Ann Matibag, Benjamin Agarao, and
Crispin Diego Remulla.

       The immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
