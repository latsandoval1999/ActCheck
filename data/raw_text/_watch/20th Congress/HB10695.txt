                           AN ACT
PROMOTING THE INSTALLATION AND UTILIZATION OF SELF-GENERATING
 FACILITY ZERO-EXPORT SOLAR SYSTEMS AND MICRO-SOLAR SYSTEMS
  FOR OWN USE, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “ELECTRIC POWER INDUSTRY REFORM ACT
                           OF 2001”


                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The increasing cost of electricity, coupled with rapid advancements in solar and
energy storage technologies, underscores a need for a clear policy that will promote the
adoption of own-use distributed energy systems. Self-Generating Facility Zero-Export Solar
Systems (SGF ZESS), Micro-Solar Systems (MSS), and Battery Energy Storage Systems
(BESS) enable consumers to generate and/or store electricity for their own consumption,
thereby enhancing energy security, improving consumer resilience, reducing electricity costs,
and supporting the country's transition to a cleaner and more sustainable energy future.

        For consumers, these technologies help reduce electricity costs, improve resilience
during power interruptions, and lessen dependence on the grid. The wider adoption of these
technologies can also help reduce pressure on the distribution system, contributing to a more
reliable, resilient, and efficient power sector.

       At present, however, Republic Act 9136 or the Electric Power Industry Reform Act of
2001 (EPIRA), does not expressly distinguish own-use distributed energy systems from
commercial power generation. This regulatory gap has created uncertainty regarding the legal
treatment of end-users who install and operate such systems solely for their own
consumption, resulting in the imposition of regulatory and administrative requirements
intended for generation companies. This creates an unnecessary burden that discourages the
adoption of these technologies.

                                                       1
        This draft bill seeks to establish a clear policy that governs own-use energy systems.
It clarifies that end-users who install and operate SGF ZESS, MSS, BESS, and similar
technologies solely for their own consumption shall not be considered generation companies
or be subject to regulatory requirements applicable to commercial power generators. It
likewise removes unnecessary administrative barriers, strengthens consumer protection,
promotes uniform safety standards, and enhances inter-agency coordination to facilitate the
responsible deployment of these systems.

        Additionally, this draft measure empowers consumers to generate and store their own
electricity, enabling them to better manage electricity costs, improve resilience against power
interruptions, and contribute to a more reliable, secure, and sustainable electric power system.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

        Sec. 1. Short Title – This Act shall be known as the “Sariling Kuryente Act.”

        Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote safe, reliable, affordable and widespread adoption of own-use distributed energy
system, including Self-Generating Facility Zero Export Solar System (SGF ZESS),
Micro-Solar Systems (MSS) and Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS), as a means of
empowering end-users to generate and store electricity for their own consumptions to attain
consumer energy independence, improve energy efficiency and grid resilience, reduce
electricity costs, reduction of transmission losses, create private investment in distributed
energy resources and contribute to the country's energy security and clean energy transition.

       Sec. 3. Scope and Applicability – This Circular shall govern the utilization of
Self-Generating Facility Zero-Export Solar System (SGF ZESS) and Micro-Solar System
(MSS), as defined herein, and other similar legitimate technologies and systems intended
solely for own-use consumption and do not export electricity to the distribution system,
regardless of connection type.

Notwithstanding the definition of SGFs under the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR)
of the Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001 (EPIRA), this Act shall likewise cover

systems installed and utilized by households, clinics, hospitals, and other medical facilities.
The application thereto shall solely be for purposes of implementing this Act and shall not be

and its IRR.

        Sec. 4. Definition of Terms – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
defined as follows:
    a.​ “Battery Energy Storage System” or “BESS” refers to an energy storage system
        composed of one or more batteries, together with associated power conversion
        systems, controls, protection devices, and other components, capable of storing
        electrical energy and subsequently supplying such energy for own-use, including in
        connection with an SGF ZESS or MSS;
    b.​ “Distribution Utility” or “DU” shall have the same meaning as provided under
        Republic Act No. 9136;
    c.​ “End-user” refers to any person or entity supplied with electricity for its own use;
    d.​ “Micro-Solar System” or “MSS” refers to a consumer-grade solar generation system
        intended solely for own use, consisting of: (1) photovoltaic modules with an
        aggregate direct-current nameplate capacity not exceeding one thousand watts (1,000
        W) or one kilowatt (1 kW); (2) a factory-set, grid-tied micro-inverter with a maximum
        alternating-current output not exceeding eight hundred watts (800 W) or zero point
        eight kilowatt (0.8 kW), equipped with automatic anti-islanding disconnection; and
        (3) a standard connection designed and installed in accordance with the Philippine
        Electrical Code and applicable product and safety standards. The Department of
        Energy, in consultation with the Energy Regulatory Commission and the Department
        of Trade and Industry, may update the foregoing technical limits and specifications
        through the implementing rules and regulations when warranted by technological
        developments and safety considerations. An MSS may be equipped with BESS;
    e.​ “Non-Compensated Export” refers to electricity inadvertently or unlawfully exported
        to a distribution system that does not entitle the end-user to any billing credit, offset,
        payment, or other monetary compensation;
    f.​ “Own use” refers to electricity generated and consumed within the end-user’s
        premises and not intended for sale or compensated export to a distribution system;
        and
    g.​ “Self-Generating Facility Zero-Export Solar System” or “SGF ZESS” refers to a
        solar photovoltaic installation of any capacity intended solely for own use and not for
        the export of electricity to a distribution system. An SGF ZESS may be either
        connected or isolated, so long as it is equipped with functional control or isolation
        mechanisms that prevent the export of electricity to the distribution system. ZESS
        may be integrated with a BESS.

        Sec. 8. Responsibilities of End-Users – An end-user shall: (a) ensure that the design,
installation, operation, and maintenance of an SGF ZESS or MSS comply with this Act, the
Philippine Electrical Code, applicable safety and quality standards, and all other relevant
laws, rules, and regulations; (b) secure and maintain all permits, clearances, certifications,
and protective devices required by law; (c) continuously maintain functional and effective
control or isolation mechanisms that prevents the export of electricity to the distribution
system; and (d) bear the operational and financial risks arising from the underperformance,
downtime, or failure of the system.

Any electricity exported to a distribution system in violation of this Act shall be treated as
Non-Compensated Export and shall not entitle the end-user to any billing credit, offset,
payment, or monetary compensation, unless the end-user is duly enrolled in the Net-Metering
Program and has executed the required net-metering agreement with the host DU.


                                              7
Failure to maintain the prescribed anti-export, anti-islanding, or protective mechanisms shall
constitute a violation of this Act, without prejudice to the authority of the DU, in accordance
with uniform ERC rules, to require reasonable and proportionate corrective measures and
additional protection schemes, or to take immediate action when necessary to address an
imminent threat to public safety or distribution-system reliability.

       Sec. 9. Protection Against Unreasonable Restrictions – No homeowners’
association, condominium corporation, subdivision developer, property manager, building
administrator, or similar entity shall prohibit the installation of an SGF ZESS or MSS on
property owned or exclusively controlled by an end-user. Reasonable, objective, and
non-discriminatory requirements may be imposed only for structural safety, public safety, or
the protection of common property. Provided, however, that such requirements shall not
prohibit the installation or unreasonably increase its cost.

        The DOE, in coordination with the Department of Human Settlements and Urban
Development (DHSUD), or other concerned agencies, shall develop and publish model rules
that such entities may adopt.

​       Sec. 10. Cybersecurity, Data Protection, and Digital Security Requirements – To
safeguard the reliability, integrity, and resilience of the electric power system and protect
consumers from cybersecurity risks, all SGF ZESS, MSS, BESS, and other own-use
distributed energy resources covered by this Act shall, where applicable, comply with the
following requirements:

   (A)​Equipment capable of digital communication, remote monitoring, remote control, or
       internet connectivity shall comply with the requirement prescribed or adopted by the
       Department of Information and Communications technology (DICT), as well as
       incorporate reasonable and appropriate cybersecurity measures to protect against
       unauthorized access, malicious software, cyber intrusion, data manipulation, and other
       cybersecurity threats that may compromise the safe and reliable operation of the
       system;
   (B)​Smart inverters, battery management systems, energy management systems,
       monitoring platforms, communication gateways, and other digital control devices
       shall comply with the cybersecurity requirements prescribed or adopted by the DICT,
       including the capable of receiving authenticated security patches, firmware updates,
       and software upgrades necessary to address identified cybersecurity vulnerabilities
       throughout their supported operational life;
   (C)​Manufacturers, importers, distributors, installers, and service providers shall ensure
       that equipment placed into the Philippine market complies with applicable
       cybersecurity, interoperability, communication, and product security requirements
       prescribed or adopted under applicable rules and regulations of the DICT pursuant to
       this Act;
   (D)​End-users, installers, and service providers shall implement reasonable measures
       prescribed or adopted by the DICT, including measures to secure user credentials,
       communication interfaces, wireless connections, remote access functions, and
       administrative controls to prevent unauthorized operation or manipulation of the
       covered systems;
   (E)​Customer energy consumption data, operational information, and other electronically
       generated information collected through the operation of covered systems shall be
       processed, stored, transmitted, and disclosed only in accordance with Republic Act

                                              8
       No. 10173, otherwise known as the "Data Privacy Act of 2012," and other applicable
       laws, rules, and regulations. Such information shall not be disclosed to unauthorized
       persons except as required by law or with the consent of the data subject, as may be
       applicable. The National Privacy Commission (NPC) shall exercise its mandate over
       matters involving the processing and protection of personal data; and
   (F)​The DOE, in coordination with the ERC, the DICT, the Department of Trade and
       Industry (DTI), the NPC, and other concerned government agencies, shall prescribe,
       through the implementing rules and regulations, the minimum energy sector
       requirements for cybersecurity, communication, interoperability, and data protection
       applicable to covered systems, consistent with cybersecurity and digital security
       standards and guidelines issued by the DICT and other applicable standard and
       regulations, taking into consideration internationally recognized standards and
       evolving technological developments.

Compliance with this section shall not be construed as requiring prior approval from the
distribution utility before the installation or operation of an own-use system, except where
otherwise required under existing laws or regulations governing systems capable of exporting
electricity to the distribution network.

         Sec. 12. Financing and Incentive Mechanisms – The ERC, in coordination with the
other concerned agencies, shall develop appropriate financing, benefit-sharing, and incentive
mechanisms to encourage the installation, expansion, and continued use of the subject
facilities, including their safe and reliable synchronization with the distribution system,
without prejudice to the viability of distribution utilities and the security, reliability, and
integrity of the power network.

For this purpose, expenses incurred by distribution utilities in implementing ERC-approved
programs for the promotion, integration, and synchronization of the subject facilities may be
included in their capital expenditure plans, operating expenses, or other appropriate
regulatory filings.

        Sec. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOE, in consultation with the ERC and other concerned
government agencies, distribution utilities, consumer groups, industry participants, and other
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe the appropriate mechanisms for
monitoring, verifying, and ensuring compliance with this Act, including the applicable

       Sec. 14. Transitory Provisions – This Act and its implementing rules and regulations
shall apply prospectively, including to applications pending upon their effectivity, except
when the ERC determines that such application would be infeasible or unjust, in which case
the prior procedure may govern. Nothing in this Act shall prejudice rights vested in SGF
ZESS or MSS installed, operated, or approved before its effectivity.

For an MSS registered through the prescribed notification process and served by an existing
analog meter, the net consumption recorded by such meter shall be honored as the basis for
billing for a transitional period not exceeding twelve (12) months from notification. Within
such period, the DU shall replace the meter with a meter suitable for MSS and compliant with
applicable ERC regulations. The allocation and recovery of meter-replacement costs shall be
governed by applicable ERC rules.

       Sec. 15. Separability Clause – If any provision of this Act is declared

and effect.

        Sec. 16. Repealing Clause – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

        Sec. 17. Effectivity – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
