                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The role of fathers in Filipino families continues to evolve beyond the traditional
concept of being solely providers for the household. Today, fathers play an
increasingly active role in caregiving, child development, and providing emotional and
physical support to their spouses and children, particularly during the critical period
following childbirth.

Republic Act No. 8187, otherwise known as the “Paternity Leave Act of 1993,”
recognized the importance of allowing fathers to support their spouses and bond with
their newborn children by granting paternity leave benefits. However, the current
period provided under the law remains limited and may no longer be sufficient to
address the realities and responsibilities of modern parenthood.

The early days following childbirth require shared responsibility between parents.
Providing fathers with additional time to care for their spouses, assist in childcare, and
adjust to their new family responsibilities promotes stronger family relationships,
improves the well-being of mothers and children, and encourages a more equitable
distribution of caregiving duties within the household.

Therefore, this bill seeks to expand the existing paternity leave benefits by increasing
the number of leave days granted to fathers and allowing greater support during
childbirth, miscarriage, and other pregnancy-related circumstances. By strengthening
paternity leave protections, this bill recognizes the essential role of fathers in nurturing
their families and promoting a healthier work-life balance.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT PROVIDING FOR EXPANDED PATERNITY LEAVE BENEFITS,
AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT 8187, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “PATERNITY
                      LEAVE ACT OF 1996”

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in the
Congress assembled:


SEC. 1. The title of Republic Act 8187, otherwise known as The Paternity Leave Act
of 1996 is hereby amended to read as follows:
      AN ACT GRANTING PATERNITY LEAVE OF [SEVEN (7) DAYS]
      TWENTY (20) WORKING DAYS WITH FULL PAY TO [MARRIED]
      MALE EMPLOYEES IN THE PRIVATE AND PUBLIC SECTORS FOR
      [THE FIRST FOUR (4) DELIVERIES] IN EVERY INSTANCE OF
      DELIVERY OF THE LEGITIMATE SPOUSE WITH WHOM HE IS
      COHABITING OR THE WOMAN WITH WHOM HE IS EXCLUSIVELY
      COHABITING WITHOUT THE BENEFIT OF MARRIAGE AND FOR
      OTHER PURPOSES
SEC. 2. Section 2 of the same law is hereby further amended to read as follows:
      Section 2. PATERNITY LEAVE – Notwithstanding any law, rules and
      regulations to the contrary, every [married] male employee,
      REGARDLESS OF EMPLOYMENT OR CIVIL STATUS, MARRIED OR
      IN A COMMON-LAW RELATIONSHIP, in the private and public sectors
      shall be entitled to a paternity leave of [seven (7)] TWENTY (20)
      WORKING days with full pay for [the first four (4) deliveries] IN
      EVERY INSTANCE OF DELIVERY of the legitimate spouse with whom
      he is cohabiting OR THE WOMAN WITH WHOM HE IS EXCLUSIVELY
      COHABITING WITHOUT THE BENEFIT OF MARRIAGE WITH AN
      OPTION TO EXTEND FOR AN ADDITIONAL TEN (10) DAYS
      WITHOUT PAY. The male employee applying for paternity leave shall
      notify his employer of the pregnancy of his legitimate spouse OR THE
       WOMAN WITH WHOM HE IS EXCLUSIVELY COHABITING
       WITHOUT THE BENEFIT OR MARRIAGE and the expected date of
       such delivery. For purposes of this Act, delivery shall include childbirth,
       [or any] miscarriage, OR EMERGENCY TERMINATION OF
       PREGNANCY.
SEC. 3. Section 3 of Republic Act No. 8187 is further amended to read as follows:
       Section 3. Definition of Term. – For purpose of this Act, Paternity Leave
       refers to the benefits granted to a [married] male employee,
       REGARDLESS OF EMPLOYMENT OR CIVIL STATUS, MARRIED OR
       IN A COMMON-LAW RELATIONSHIP, allowing him not to report for
       work for [seven (7) days] TWENTY (20) WORKING DAYS but
       continues to earn the compensation therefor, on the condition that his
       spouse OR THE WOMAN WITH WHOM HE IS EXCLUSIVELY
       COHABITING WITHOUT THE BENEFIT OF MARRIAGE has delivered
       a child, or suffered a miscarriage OR EMERGENCY TERMINATION OF
       PREGNANCY, for purposes of enabling him to effectively lend support
       to his wife in her period of recovery and/or nursing of the newly-born
       child.
SEC. 4. Non-Diminution of Benefits. – Nothing herein shall be construed to reduce,
diminish, or replace any benefits more favorable to the employee under existing laws,
collective bargaining agreements, or company policies.
SEC. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within thirty (30) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), in
coordination with the Civil Service Commission (CSC) and the Department of Health
(DOH), shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to implement the
provisions of this Act.
SEC. 6. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional,
the remainder shall not be affected and shall continue to be in full force and effect.
SEC. 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.
SEC. 8. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
