                                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

        With the widespread use and growing number of social media users across all
ages, more Filipinos are active online, paving the way for the gambling industry to
penetrate and expand its presence on digital gaming platforms right before the
fingertips of Filipino online users. With gross gaming revenues reaching
P396,138,802,763.57 for Fiscal Year 2025, the Philippine gambling industry is seeing
record-breaking growth.1 Although this growth can help boost the economy, it poses
a serious risk to those who engage in gambling online down to their households.

      According to the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation (PAGCOR),
the number of Filipino electronic gaming players reached 32.117 million in 20252,
representing an almost 200 percent increase from the 8.2 million recorded players in
2024. The growing accessibility of online gambling platforms has been accompanied
by an increase in advertisements, sponsorships, marketing, and other forms of
consumer communication, which have become more widespread and targeted.

       The increasing participation in online gambling has raised concerns regarding
its impact on gamblers’ mental health, physical well-being, socio-economic stability,
and even to their duties and responsibilities in their households. The growing visibility
and accessibility of gambling-related advertisements and services have contributed to
issues such as the participation of minors and the underage, excessive, compulsive,
and irresponsible gambling behavior, financial distress, and other gambling-related
harms affecting minors and vulnerable sectors of society.

       Recognizing these growing concerns, the bill seeks to regulate gambling
advertisements by prohibiting promotional activities such as celebrity endorsements,
sponsorships, and other forms of gambling-related consumer communications. It also
requires authorized gambling advertisements to include responsible gambling
advisories and imposes appropriate administrative and criminal penalties for violations
to ensure effective implementation. Similar to the provisions of Republic Act No. 9211,
or the Tobacco Regulation Act of 2003, this measure aims to create a more responsible
gaming environment by protecting minors and vulnerable individuals, promoting
informed consumer decisions, and safeguarding public interest.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill, which is a
counterpart measure of Senate Bill 2347, is earnestly sought.

 1          SECTION. 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as “Gambling Advertising
 2   Prohibition Act”.
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State
 5   to protect life, liberty, and property, and promote the general welfare, which are
 6   essential for the enjoyment by all the people of the blessings of democracy. Towards
 7   this end, in recognition of the risks posed by unrestricted, misleading, and
 8   irresponsible consumer communications, the State shall prohibit advertisements,
 9   celebrity endorsements, and sponsorships relating to gambling activities, gambling
10   operators, gambling establishments, and gambling platforms; regulate gambling-
11   related promotions; protect vulnerable persons from undue exposure to gambling-
12   related consumer communications; and promote responsible gambling.
13
14          SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. As used in this Act:
15
16          (a) Advertisement shall refer to any visual and/or audible message
17   disseminated to the public about or on a particular product, service, activity, operator,
18   establishment, or platform that promotes and gives publicity by words, designs,
19   images or any other means through mass media, print and outdoor advertising, mobile
20   applications, digital platforms, social media platforms, e-commerce platforms,
21   streaming services, electronic messaging services, or any other analogous means of
22   communication accessible to the general public. For the purpose of this Act,
23   advertisement shall be understood as gambling advertisement.
24
25          (b) Age Assurance Measures shall refer to appropriate processes undertaken to
26   verify whether a person has attained the minimum age prescribed by law to access,

 6   public attention, enjoys substantial public recognition, or gives the public a legitimate
 7   interest in his or her doings, affairs and character. The term includes anyone who has

39

42
43                  (iv) Promotional contests, giveaways, raffles, free bets, sign-up bonuses,

45          promotional schemes intended to encourage gambling patronage or
46          participation in any gambling activity; and

15          SEC. 4. Responsible Gambling Advisory. PAGCOR shall develop, prescribe,
16   and periodically update standardized responsible gambling advisories and warning
17   messages for use in consumer communications authorized under this Act. Such
18   advisories and warning messages shall be prominently displayed or communicated by
19   all gambling operators, gambling establishments, and gambling platforms in the
20   manner and form prescribed by PAGCOR.
21
22           SEC. 5. Prohibition on Gambling-Related Advertisements. Upon the
23   expiration of the transition period provided under Section 13 of this Act, no person
24   shall disseminate, publish, broadcast, display, or otherwise make available any
25   advertisements relating to any gambling activity, gambling operator, gambling
26   establishment, or gambling platform: Provided, That this prohibition shall not apply
27   to: (i) advertisements displayed solely within the premises of a gambling
28   establishment; and (ii) advertisements appearing on the official websites or official
29   mobile application of the gambling operators, gambling establishments or gambling
30   platforms: Provided, further, That advertisements appearing on the official websites
31   or official, mobile applications of the gambling operators or gambling platforms shall
32   be restricted to persons twenty-one (21) years of age or older through reasonable age
33   assurance measures.
34
35          SEC. 6. Prohibition on Celebrity Endorsements. Upon the expiration of
36   the transition period provided under Section 13 of this Act, no celebrity shall endorse,
37   recommend, promote, or publicly associate his or her name, image, voice, likeness,
38   or other identifying attributes with any gambling activity, gambling operator, gambling
39   establishment, or gambling platform, whether directly or indirectly, whether for
40   financial or non-financial consideration or otherwise.
41
42          SEC. 7. Restriction on Promotions. Upon the expiration of the transition
43   period provided under Section 13 of this Act, the following restrictions shall apply to
44   any promotions relating to gambling activities, gambling operators, gambling
45   establishments, or gambling platforms:
46
47         (a) Promotions shall not be directed to persons below twenty-one (21) years of
48   age. No person below twenty-one (21) years of age, or who appears to be below such

 1   age, shall be allowed to participate in any gambling promotion. Participants shall be
 2   required to provide proof of their age;
 3
 4          (b) Consumer communications pertaining to promotions shall comply with the
 5   applicable requisites and limitations provided under this Act: Provided, That the age
 6   requirement for participation in any promotion must be clearly marked on the program
 7   materials distributed to consumers;
 8
 9          (c) All stalls, kiosks, booths, and other promotional installations relating to
10   gambling activities, gambling operators, gambling establishments, or gambling
11   platforms shall be located only within facilities accessible exclusively to persons

13
14          (d) Consumer communications relating to promotional offers, programs or
15   events shall be sent only to persons twenty-one (21) years of age or older who have
16   given prior express consent to receive such information and shall include a responsible
17   gambling advisory in English or Filipino;
18
19          (e) No placement shall be made by or on behalf of any gambling operator,
20   gambling establishment, or gambling platform of any gambling advertisement, logo,
21   trade name, trademark, or other identifying mark as a prop or as part of the content
22   of any television program, motion picture, audiovisual work, video game, livestream,
23   or other entertainment content made available to the general public.
24
25          (f) No promotional merchandise such as, but not limited to, toys, school
26   supplies, personal apparel, backpacks, writing implements, umbrellas, and other
27   personal accessories may be distributed, sold, or offered, directly or indirectly, with
28   the name, logo or other indicia of a gambling operator, gambling establishment, or
29   gambling platform displayed so as to be visible to others when worn or used;
30
31          (g) No name, logo, or other identifying mark of a gambling operator, gambling
32   establishment, or gambling platform shall appear on promotional merchandise or
33   element of a brand-related marketing activity that is marketed to or likely to appeal
34   to persons below twenty-one (21) years of age. The gambling operator, gambling
35   establishment, or gambling platform concerned shall take reasonable measures to
36   prevent unauthorized use of their respective brand names, logos, or other identifying
37   marks on products that are directed toward persons below - twenty-one years (21) of
38   age; and
39
40         (h) No gambling advertisement or name, logo, or other identifying mark of a
41   gambling operator, gambling establishment, or gambling platform shall be placed on
42   shopping bags or other packaging materials for general retail distribution.

44          SEC. 8. Prohibition on Sponsorship. Upon the expiration of the transition.
45   period provided under Section 13 of this Act, gambling operators, gambling
46   establishments, and gambling platforms shall be prohibited from sponsoring any
47   sports, concert, cultural or art event, as well as individual and team athletes, artists
48   or performers where such sponsorship requires or involves the advertisement or

 7          SEC. 9. Administrative Enforcement. Without prejudice to the filing of
 8   appropriate criminal, civil, or administrative action and the imposition of penalties
 9   provided for under Section 10 of this Act, PAGCOR is hereby authorized, after due
10   notice and summary hearing, to perform the following:
11
12         (a) Issue cease-and-desist orders directing any person to immediately cease
13   the dissemination, publication, broadcast, display, or other prohibited consumer
14   communication in violation of this Act or its implementing rules and regulations;
15
16          (b) Issue an ex parte takedown order directing the disabling of access to,
17   removal, withdrawal, or discontinuance of any advertisement, endorsement,
18   sponsorship, promotion, or other consumer communication found to be in violation of
19   this Act, where immediate action is necessary to prevent the continued dissemination
20   of prohibited consumer communications; and
21
22          (c) Exercise such other powers as may be necessary to effectively implement
23   the provisions of this Act.
24
25          SEC. 10. Penalties. Any person who violates any provision of this Act, or the
26   rules and regulations issued pursuant thereto, shall be subject to the following
27   penalties:
28
29          (a) For the first offense, a fine of not more than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos
30   (P200,000.00) or imprisonment of not more than one (1) year, or both, at the
31   discretion of the court;
32
33          (b) For the second offense, a fine of not more than Three Hundred Thousand
34   Pesos (P300,000.00) or imprisonment of not more than two (2) years, or both, at the
35   discretion of the court;
36
37          (c). For the third offense, in addition to a fine of not more than Five Hundred
38   Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00) or imprisonment of not more than three (3) years, or
39   both, at the discretion of the court, the appropriate business permits, licenses, or
40   authority to operate issued by the PAGCOR or other government agencies concerned
41   shall be revoked or cancelled:
42
43          If the offender is a juridical person, the penalty shall be imposed upon the
44   responsible officers, as the case may be, who participated in, or by their gross
45   negligence, allowed the commission of the violation. If the offender is an alien, he or
46   she shall, in addition to the penalties herein prescribed, be deported without further
47   proceedings after serving the penalties prescribed.

 1          SEC. 11. Responsible Gambling Awareness and Education Campaign.
 2   Subject to the provisions of this Act, the PAGCOR, in coordination with other
 3   government agencies concerned, shall undertake a continuing responsible gambling
 4   awareness and education campaign on the risk and harmful effects of excessive,
 5   compulsive or irresponsible gambling. Such campaign shall include information on
 6   responsible gambling practices, and available interventions, counseling, treatment,
 7   and support services for individuals and families affected by gambling-related
 8   concerns. As part of the whole-of-society approach for responsible gambling, the
 9   private sector shall also be encouraged to undertake or support awareness and
10   education campaigns on responsible gambling.
11
12         SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days
13   from the effectivity of this Act, the PAGCOR, in coordination with other government
14   agencies concerned and in consultation with other relevant stakeholders, shall
15   promulgate the rules and regulations to effectively implement the provisions of this
16   Act.
17
18           SEC. 13 Transitory Provision. Gambling operators, gambling
19   establishments, gambling platforms, advertisers, broadcasters, publishers, online
20   platforms, sponsors, advertising agencies, and all persons concerned shall have a
21   transition period of one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act within which to phase
22   out gambling advertisements, promotions, sponsorships, endorsements, and other
23   prohibited consumer communications. During the transition period, no new advertising
24   or sponsorship agreement or arrangement shall be entered into except those
25   necessary to wind down existing contractual obligations, subject to such rules as may
26   be prescribed by the implementing agency.
27
28         SEC. 14. Separability Clause. If any provision or part hereof is declared
29   unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall
30   remain in full force and effect.
31
32          SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. All laws, acts, decrees, executive orders,
33   issuances, and rules and regulations or parts thereof which are contrary to and
34   inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.
35
36          SEC. 16. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
37   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
