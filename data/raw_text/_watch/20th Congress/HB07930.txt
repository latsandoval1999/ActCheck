                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Learners, parents, and educators often encounter problems in schools such as abuse,
bullying, unfair treatment, or mismanagement, but have no independent and safe
place to raise their concerns. Existing grievance mechanisms are usually internal to
education agencies or institutions, which can discourage complaints and delay justice.

This bill creates the Education Ombuds Of ice, an independent and accessible body
that will receive, investigate, and help resolve education-related complaints in basic,
higher, and technical-vocational education, in both public and private institutions. It
provides a fair, con idential, and child-sensitive process, while coordinating with
proper authorities and respecting the constitutional role of the Of ice of the
Ombudsman.

By establishing a dedicated watchdog for the education sector, this measure promotes
accountability, transparency, and trust in the education system. It empowers learners
and educators to speak up without fear and helps prevent recurring problems
through informed policy and institutional reforms.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                             AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE EDUCATION OMBUDS OFFICE AS AN INDEPENDENT
SECTORAL GRIEVANCE MECHANISM FOR BASIC, HIGHER, AND TECHNICAL-
VOCATIONAL EDUCATION, PROVIDING FOR ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS, AND
FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Education Ombuds Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect the rights
and welfare of learners, educators, and families by promoting accountability,
transparency, fairness, and responsiveness in the delivery of education services in
both public and private institutions.

To this end, the State shall establish an independent and accessible mechanism to
receive, evaluate, resolve, and monitor education-related grievances, and to
recommend reforms to prevent recurring violations and systemic failures.

SECTION 3. Creation of the Education Ombuds Of ice. — There is hereby created
an Education Ombuds Of ice (EOO) as an independent sectoral of ice, separate from
and not subject to the direction, control, or supervision of the Department of
Education (DepEd), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and the Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) in the performance of its
functions.

SECTION 4. Coverage. — This Act shall cover grievances arising from education
services involving:

   (a) Public basic education schools and DepEd of ices;

   (b) State universities and colleges and other public higher education
       institutions;

   (c) Public technical-vocational institutions; and

   (d) Private schools and private higher education/technical-vocational
       institutions, to the extent that grievances involve compliance with applicable
       education laws, student/teacher rights, accreditation/permit obligations, or
       use of public funds/subsidies.

SECTION 5. Powers and Functions. — The EOO shall have the following powers and
functions:

   (a) Complaint intake and assessment. Receive complaints iled in any form or
       manner by learners, parents, teachers, and education personnel, and evaluate
       jurisdiction and suf iciency.

   (b) Investigation and fact- inding. Conduct fact- inding investigations, require
       submission of documents, and call conferences for clari ication, subject to due
       process and child-protection safeguards.

   (c) Mediation and early resolution. Offer voluntary mediation/conciliation and
       other alternative dispute resolution mechanisms, when appropriate and not
       contrary to the best interest of the child or public policy.

   (d) Issuance of indings and recommendations. Issue written indings
       and recommend corrective actions, restitution, policy changes, and
       administrative measures to the proper authorities.

   (e) Referral. Refer cases:

             Ombudsman, as warranted; consistent with the Ombudsman’s

   (h) Annual reporting. Submit an annual report to the President and Congress on

SECTION 6. Limitations; Non-Interference with Constitutional Ombudsman. —

   (a) The EOO shall not:

      1. Exercise criminal prosecutorial powers; or

      2. Impose administrative penalties on public of icers or employees in a

         mandate and the disciplinary systems under existing laws.

   (b) For complaints against public of icials/employees, the EOO shall
       prioritize referral and coordination with the proper disciplining authority
       and/or the Of ice of the Ombudsman.

SECTION 7. Subpoena and Access Powers. —

   (a) The Education Ombuds may, in the exercise of fact- inding
       functions, require the production of records and documents relevant to a
       complaint and may request attendance of persons in conferences and
       clari icatory proceedings. Summon persons, issue subpoenas, and compel the
       production of documents necessary for investigation;

   (b) Access to school premises shall be coordinated with school heads and subject
       to safeguarding rules, con identiality, and child protection protocols.

   (c) The IRR shall de ine the scope of compulsory process consistent with
       constitutional rights and existing regulatory powers.

SECTION 8. Due Process Standards. — The EOO shall adopt procedures that ensure:

   (a) notice and opportunity to be heard;
   (b) impartial evaluation;
   (c) reasoned written dispositions; and
   (d) timelines for action.

SECTION 9. Con identiality; Anti-Retaliation. —

   (a) Complaints and records shall be treated with con identiality, subject to lawful
       exceptions and requirements of disclosure in proceedings.

   (b) Retaliation against complainants, whistleblowers, or witnesses—such as
       harassment, discrimination, adverse school action, employment reprisal, or
       threats—shall be grounds for administrative action by the proper authority
       and may be referred for criminal prosecution when warranted.

SECTION 10. Organization; Appointment; Term. —

   (a) Education Ombuds. The EOO shall be headed by an Education Ombuds, who
       shall be:

      2. A member of the Philippine Bar or a recognized expert in education

      4. Not a candidate for any elective of ice in the immediately preceding

   (b) Appointment. The Education Ombuds shall be appointed by the President

       representatives from:

       1. The Commission on Human Rights (CHR);
       2. The Civil Service Commission (CSC);
       3. A nationally recognized association of education institutions (public and
          private);
       4. An education reform civil society organization; and
       5. The parent’s and learner’s sectors.

   (c) Term. The Education Ombuds shall serve a single, non-renewable term of
       seven (7) years. For clarity, the term shall be a ixed term from appointment
       (not an “unexpired term” system), consistent with how ixed terms are treated
       for constitutionally independent of ices.

   (d) Deputies and regional of ices. The EOO may have Deputies for Basic, Higher,
       and Technical-Vocational Education and may establish regional of ices, subject
       to staf ing standards and appropriations.

SECTION 11. Independence Safeguards. —

   (a) The EOO shall be independent in the discharge of its functions and shall not be
       subjected to control or supervision that undermines its impartiality.

   (b) Any arrangement that effectively places the EOO’s key of icials under
       disciplinary control of of ices whose acts are subject to EOO review shall be
       avoided, consistent with the Supreme Court’s cautions on executive
       interference with independent accountability bodies.

SECTION 12. Coordination with Existing Grievance Mechanisms. — The EOO shall
coordinate with existing school-level, division-level, institutional, and agency
grievance mechanisms. The IRR shall provide rules on:

   (a) exhaustion of internal remedies when appropriate;
   (b) exceptions for urgent cases (violence, severe bullying, sexual harassment,
       threats to safety, irreparable harm); and
   (c) avoidance of forum-shopping and duplicative proceedings.

SECTION 13. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against available appropriations.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the EOO, in consultation with DepEd, CHED, TESDA, the
CHR, the CSC, stakeholder organizations, and relevant child protection bodies, shall
promulgate the IRR.

SECTION 15. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act are
hereby repealed or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 17. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
