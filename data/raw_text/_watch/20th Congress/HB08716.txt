      STRENGTHENING LEGAL PROTECTIONS AGAINST RAPE AND OTHER
      FORMS OF SEXUAL ABUSE AND EXPLOITATION, AMENDING FOR THE
    PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11648, REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8353, OTHERWISE
       KNOWN AS “THE ANTI-RAPE LAW OF 1997,” AND ACT NO. 3815, AS
       AMENDED, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS “THE REVISED PENAL CODE”

                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

      By amending Republic Act No. 8353, otherwise known as the “Anti-Rape Law
of 1997”, Republic Act No. 11648 strengthened legal protections against rape and
sexual abuse, including raising the age of sexual consent from 12 to 16 years old.
However, despite these legislative measures, rape and sexual exploitation, particularly
against women and children, remain serious and persistent concerns.

       Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) recorded 11,479 cases of
rape involving girls below 16 years old in 2023, yet no case was filed under Republic
Act No. 11648 during the same period1. Reports from child protection organizations
further estimate that millions of children in the Philippines experience sexual abuse
each year, with the majority of victims between 10 and 18 years old2. These alarming
figures highlight the continued vulnerability of children and the urgent need to
strengthen legal safeguards and enforcement mechanisms.

       Moreover, sexual violence is often framed primarily as a crime against women,
leaving the experiences of male rape victims largely hidden and underreported.
Although current laws recognize that any person, regardless of gender, may be a
victim of rape, disparities in legal definitions and penalties create additional barriers
for male victims. Under existing law, rape by sexual assault only carries a penalty of
prison mayor, which is significantly lower compared to rape by sexual intercourse,
which has the penalty of reclusion perpetua. This disparity underscores the need to

        While reports from the Philippine National Police (PNP) indicate a decline in
rape cases in recent years4, incidents of sexual violence remain significantly
underreported due to fear, stigma, and barriers to accessing justice. Moreover,
evolving methods of exploitation, including grooming and abuse facilitated through
digital platforms, create new risks that require clearer legal definitions and stronger
accountability.

       This bill seeks to strengthen the legal framework against rape and sexual
exploitation by refining and expanding the definition of rape to emphasize the absence
of consent, recognizing grooming as a form of predatory conduct that may lead to
sexual abuse, and strengthening aggravating circumstances when the crime is
committed by persons in positions of authority or trust. The measure also introduces
stronger procedural safeguards, including mandatory reporting obligations, the
prohibition of amicable settlements and affidavits of desistance in rape cases, and
enhanced protections for the privacy and confidentiality of victims.

      By strengthening definitions, penalties, and accountability mechanisms, this
measure aims to reinforce the State’s commitment to protect individuals, particularly
women and children, from sexual violence and exploitation and to uphold their
fundamental rights to dignity, safety, and justice.

       In the 20th Congress, this bill adopts the version of Representative Sarah Jane
Elago. A similar bill has been filed by Representatives Renee Louise Co, Antonio Tinio,
and Iris Marie Demesa Montes.

        The immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 4          SEC. 5. Persons mandated to report rape cases and other sexual offenses. –
 5
 6          A. It shall be the duty of the following persons to report all incidents of rape and
 7   other sexual offenses to the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD),
 8   the Department of Education (DepEd), the Philippine National Police (PNP) or any law
 9   enforcement agency, or the concerned Local Government Units (LGU) within forty
10   eight (48) hours from knowledge thereof:
11
12          1. Any healthcare provider, including the attending physician, nurse, clinician,
13             barangay health worker, or any person who has caused the examination or
14             treatment of the victim appearing to have suffered from rape or other sexual
15             offenses;
16          2. Teacher, guidance counselor, principal, school head, or any person whose
17             work involves the education, training, and care of children;
18          3. Any social worker who has reason to believe that rape or other & offenses
19             have been committed or has been duly informed thereof by the victim; or
20          4. Any national or local public officer.
21
22         B. The persons mandated to report cases of rape or other sexual offenses shall
23   have, as far as applicable, the following responsibilities:
24
25          1. Properly document any of the victim's physical, emotional, or psychological
26             injuries;
27          2. Properly record observation on the victim's condition and circumstances
28             during the examination or visit;
29          3. Safeguard the records and make them immediately available to the victim
30             upon request; and
31          4. Immediately report the suspected commission of rape to the proper
32             authorities.
33
34          C. Failure to report possible rape or sexual offenses under this act shall be
35   punishable by a fine of not more than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) without
36   prejudice to any administrative liability, if applicable.
37
38          D. No criminal, civil, or administrative action may be filed against any person
39   who is performing the responsibilities mandated under this article.
