                        AN ACT
RECOGNIZING UNPAID CARE AND DOMESTIC WORK AS AN
ECONOMIC CONTRIBUTION TO NATIONAL DEVELOPMENT,
INTEGRATING   IT   INTO   NATIONAL   STATISTICS AND
POLICYMAKING, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Despite the significant contribution of unpaid care and domestic work to the
well-being of Filipino families, communities, and the national economy, such labor
remains largely invisible, undervalued, and unsupported in public policy and
economic planning. Across the Philippines, women and girls continue to bear the
disproportionate burden of caregiving and household responsibilities, often at the
expense of their education, employment opportunities, economic participation,
health, and personal development.

       Unpaid care work includes essential activities such as childcare, eldercare,
caring for persons with disabilities and sick household members, cooking, cleaning,
laundry, household management, and other domestic responsibilities necessary for
daily life and social reproduction. While indispensable to the functioning of society
and the economy, these contributions are not adequately measured in conventional
economic systems and are frequently excluded from national statistics, development
planning, and public investment priorities.

                                                                                      1
        Thus, this proposed measure seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive
 national framework for recognizing, reducing, redistributing, and supporting unpaid
 care and domestic work through the establishment of a coherent care economy policy
 system. The bill mandates the integration of unpaid care work into national statistics
 through regular time-use surveys and the development of a Satellite Account for
 Unpaid Work. It also strengthens evidence-based policymaking by ensuring that
 care-related data are incorporated into development planning, budgeting, labor
 policies, and social protection programs.

        The measure further establishes the Care Economy Policy and Planning
 Office (CEPPO) under the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development
 (DEPDev) to serve as the central coordinating body for care economy policies and
 programs. It likewise promotes whole-of-government and whole-of-society
 approaches through inter-agency coordination, local government participation,
 public awareness campaigns, capacity-building initiatives, and partnerships with
 civil society, labor groups, academia, and the private sector.

       By recognizing care work as essential social and economic labor, this measure
 aims to promote gender equality, strengthen family and community resilience,
 expand women’s economic participation, improve human development outcomes,
 and build a more inclusive, equitable, and sustainable economy.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Care Economy Support
and Recognition Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - Consistent with the provisions of the 1987
Constitution, the State’s policy of recognizing the role of women in nation-building,
the principles embodied in the Magna Carta of Women, and the State’s commitments



                                                                                   1
under international agreements including the Convention on the Elimination of All
Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW) and the Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs), particularly Goal 5.4 on recognizing and valuing
unpaid care and domestic work, the State hereby affirms that unpaid care and
domestic work constitute essential social and economic contributions that sustain
households, communities, and the national economy.


Towards this end, the State shall:


   a. Recognize unpaid care and domestic work, disproportionately performed by
      women and girls, as valuable contributions to social reproduction, human
      development, economic productivity, and national well-being;
   b. Promote gender equality, women’s economic empowerment, and inclusive
      development by addressing the unequal distribution of unpaid care and
      domestic responsibilities;
   c. Adopt measures to reduce and redistribute unpaid care work through
      accessible, affordable, and quality public services, social protection programs,
      care infrastructure, and family-friendly policies;
   d. Integrate the measurement and valuation of unpaid care and domestic work
      into national statistical systems, development planning, public investment
      programming, and gender-responsive budgeting frameworks;
   e. Strengthen evidence-based policymaking through the conduct of time-use
      surveys, development of satellite accounts, and generation of sex-
      disaggregated and care-sensitive data;




                                                                                     2
   f. Ensure that national and local development policies, programs, and budgets
      take into account the care economy and its impact on labor force participation,
      poverty reduction, education, health, and social welfare;
   g. Promote the equal sharing of domestic and caregiving responsibilities among
      all genders and foster cultural transformation toward shared care
      responsibilities within households and communities; and
   h. Support the development of a sustainable and resilient care economy that
      advances social justice, decent work, human dignity, and inclusive economic
      growth.


SEC. 3. Definition of Terms -


   a. Care Burden refers to the disproportionate allocation of unpaid care and
      domestic responsibilities borne by individuals or groups, particularly women
      and girls, which may limit participation in education, employment, public life,
      and leisure.
   b. Care Economy refers to the system of paid and unpaid labor, services,
      relationships, infrastructure, and institutions involved in the care of persons
      and households necessary for social reproduction, human well-being, and
      economic productivity, including childcare, eldercare, disability support,
      healthcare support, domestic work, and household maintenance.
   c. Community Care Work refers to unpaid or underpaid caregiving and support
      activities performed within communities or volunteer settings for collective
      welfare, including community health work, neighborhood caregiving, disaster
      response support, and informal social assistance.




                                                                                    3
   d. Paid Care Work refers to remunerated labor involving caregiving or domestic
       support services performed in households, communities, institutions, or care
       facilities, including work performed by domestic workers, childcare workers,
       healthcare aides, personal caregivers, and similar occupations.
   e. Time-Use Survey refers to a statistical tool or data collection methodology
       used to measure how individuals allocate time across various activities,
       including paid work, unpaid care and domestic work, personal care, leisure,
       and community participation.
   f. Unpaid Care and Domestic Work refers to non-remunerated activities
       performed within households or communities for the maintenance of persons
       and the home, including cooking, cleaning, laundry, caregiving, household
       management, fetching water or fuel, and similar tasks necessary for daily
       living and well-being.
   g. Satellite Account for Unpaid Work refers to a supplementary statistical
       framework that measures and estimates the economic value and contribution
       of unpaid care and domestic work separately from, but complementary to, the
       Philippine System of National Accounts.

lead implementing and coordinating agency for the effective implementation of this

SEC. 5. Creation of the Care Economy Policy and Planning Office (CEPPO). –
There is hereby created, under the Department of Economy, Planning, and
Development (DEPDev), the Care Economy Policy and Planning Office (CEPPO),
which shall serve as the primary policy, planning, coordination, and monitoring body
of the government on matters relating to the care economy and unpaid care and
domestic work.


The CEPPO shall serve as the central coordinating mechanism for the integration of
care economy principles, policies, and programs into national and local development
planning, budgeting, labor and employment strategies, social protection systems,
public service delivery, and gender-responsive governance.


The CEPPO shall have the following powers and functions:


   a. Formulate, recommend, and oversee the implementation of a national
      framework and strategic action plan for the recognition, reduction,
      redistribution, and representation of unpaid care and domestic work;




                                                                                  6
b. Coordinate with national government agencies, local government units,
   government-owned and controlled corporations, civil society organizations,
   academic institutions, labor groups, development partners, and the private
   sector in the implementation of this Act;
c. Ensure the integration of care economy considerations into national
   development plans, public investment programs, poverty reduction strategies,
   labor force participation     policies,     and gender-responsive budgeting
   frameworks;
d. Develop policy guidelines, standards, and monitoring mechanisms for the
   implementation of care-sensitive and gender-responsive programs and
   services across government agencies and local government units;
e. Provide technical assistance and capacity-building support to government
   agencies and local government units in mainstreaming care economy policies,
   programs, and data systems;
f. Monitor and evaluate the implementation of this Act and submit an annual
   State of the Care Economy Report to Congress and the Office of the President
   containing policy assessments, accomplishments, challenges, care-related
   indicators, and recommendations;
g. Conduct or commission policy studies, research, and impact assessments on
   unpaid care work, care infrastructure, labor participation, social protection,
   gender equality, and related socioeconomic concerns;
h. Advocate for investments in care infrastructure, family-friendly policies,
   public care services, and community-based care systems that reduce the
   disproportionate burden of unpaid care work; and
i. Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out the objectives
   and purposes of this Act.


                                                                                7
The CEPPO shall coordinate closely with the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA)
on matters relating to time-use surveys, satellite accounts, statistical methodologies,
and care-related data systems, and with the Philippine Commission on Women
(PCW) on gender mainstreaming, advocacy, and public education initiatives.


Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, DEPDev shall organize and
operationalize the CEPPO and determine its organizational structure, staffing
pattern, and administrative requirements, subject to existing civil service, budgeting,
and compensation laws, rules, and regulations.


SEC. 6. Mandate to Integrate Unpaid Work in National Statistics. - The
Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) shall:


   a. Develop and institutionalize Time-Use Surveys to be conducted at least once
      every three (3) years to capture data on unpaid care and domestic work
      disaggregated by sex, age, location, and socioeconomic status;
   b. Develop a Satellite Account for Unpaid Work to estimate its monetary value
      and contribution to the economy;
   c. Ensure that these data are integrated into the Philippine System of National
      Accounts (PSNA) and are published regularly.


SEC. 7. Policy Integration and Use. - All government agencies, especially the
DEPDev, Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), and
Department of Budget and Management (DBM), shall:




                                                                                     8
   a. Incorporate the value and impact of unpaid care and domestic work in social
       protection policies, economic planning, and development programs;
   b. Prioritize care-sensitive budgeting and the provision of public services and
       infrastructure (e.g., daycare centers, elderly care services) to reduce and
       redistribute unpaid care burdens;
   c. Use the data to support targeted gender-responsive interventions in education,
       labor, and welfare programs.

with the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), through

the Department of Education (DepEd), the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), and other relevant agencies, shall develop and implement a comprehensive
national program on capacity building, public awareness, and social norm
transformation relating to the care economy and unpaid care and domestic work.


The program shall aim to strengthen public understanding of the social and economic
value of unpaid care and domestic work, promote shared caregiving responsibilities,
and build institutional capacities for the effective implementation of this Act.


For this purpose, the concerned agencies shall:


   a. Conduct sustained nationwide information, education, and communication
       campaigns on the value of unpaid care and domestic work and its impact on

   local government units in the collection, analysis, and utilization of care-
   related and time-use data for local governance and development planning;
e. Encourage the integration of care economy concepts, gender equality

   into educational curricula, professional development programs, leadership

   unpaid care work, care infrastructure, family-friendly policies, and

   advocacy, public education, and policy dialogue initiatives relating to the care

SEC. 9. Targeted Support Measures for Caregivers and Care Workers. – The
State shall institutionalize targeted support measures to strengthen the welfare,
capacity, and well-being of unpaid caregivers and workers in the care economy,
recognizing their essential role in sustaining households, communities, and social
development.


For this purpose, and subject to existing laws, rules, regulations, and the availability
of funds, the following measures shall be implemented:


   a. Support for Unpaid Caregivers. – Unpaid caregivers, including family
      members and household members performing unpaid care and domestic work,
      shall be provided access to the following support measures:


          1. Priority access to community care services, including childcare,
             eldercare, disability support services, and other publicly provided or
             community-based care programs;


                                                                                     11
     2. Caregiver training and capacity-building programs to enhance
        caregiving skills, financial literacy, psychosocial resilience, and access
        to available government services;
     3. Respite care services, including temporary substitute care arrangements
        to allow caregivers periods of rest, recovery, or personal time;
     4. Inclusion in applicable social protection programs, including
        conditional and unconditional cash assistance, health insurance
        coverage, and other forms of social safety nets under existing
        government programs;
     5. Mental health and psychosocial support services, including counseling,
        community-based support groups, and referral systems within public
        health and social welfare programs; and
     6. Livelihood support and flexible work facilitation, including access to
        skills training, employment facilitation services, and promotion of
        flexible or hybrid work arrangements where applicable.


b. Support for Paid Care Workers. – Workers engaged in paid care work,
  including but not limited to domestic workers, childcare workers, caregivers,
  health aides, and other similar occupations, shall be entitled to the following
  support measures:


     1. Skills   development,    training,   and   certification   programs,    in
        coordination with the Technical Education and Skills Development
        Authority (TESDA) and other relevant agencies, to enhance
        professionalization and career progression in the care sector;




                                                                               12
         2. Occupational safety and health protections, including enforcement of
            workplace safety standards, protection from abuse and exploitation, and
            access to grievance and reporting mechanisms;
         3. Access to social protection systems, including health insurance, social
            security coverage, and other applicable benefits under existing laws;
         4. Career development and professionalization pathways, including
            recognition of competencies, opportunities for advancement, and
            access to continuing education and training; and
         5. Fair and decent working conditions, including protection of labor
            rights, just compensation consistent with applicable labor laws,
            reasonable working hours, rest periods, and protection against
            discrimination and unfair labor practices.


SEC. 10. Local Government Unit Implementation and Compliance. – Local
government units shall:


All local government units (LGUs) shall integrate care economy and unpaid care
and domestic work considerations into their respective local development plans,
local gender and development (GAD) plans and budgets, local investment programs,
and other relevant local policies and programs.


In furtherance thereof, LGUs shall:


   a. Utilize available time-use data, sex-disaggregated statistics, and care-related
      indicators in local planning, policymaking, and program development;




                                                                                    13
   b. Develop and implement programs, services, and infrastructure that recognize,
      reduce, and redistribute unpaid care and domestic work, particularly among
      women and marginalized sectors, including but not limited to childcare
      services, community care facilities, eldercare support, water and sanitation
      systems, public transportation access, and family support programs;
   c. Establish mechanisms to support caregivers, especially solo parents, persons
      with disabilities, older persons, low-income households, and informal workers
      with caregiving responsibilities;
   d. Allocate appropriate funding for care-responsive and gender-responsive
      programs consistent with existing Gender and Development (GAD) budgeting
      policies and other applicable laws, rules, and regulations; and
   e. Submit periodic reports on the implementation of care economy initiatives
      and programs to the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG)
      and the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev),
      through the Care Economy Policy and Planning Office (CEPPO).


The DILG, in coordination with DEPDev, the Philippine Commission on Women
(PCW), the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and other relevant
agencies, shall formulate and issue guidelines for the implementation, monitoring,
and evaluation of LGU compliance with this Act.


Compliance with the provisions of this Act shall be incorporated, whenever
practicable, into existing local governance performance assessment systems,
including gender and development monitoring and local development planning
evaluations.




                                                                                14
The DILG and DEPDev shall provide technical assistance, capacity-building
support, and policy guidance to LGUs to ensure the effective implementation of this
Act.


SEC. 11. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting Framework. – The Department
of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), through the Care Economy
Policy and Planning Office (CEPPO), shall establish and administer a
comprehensive     monitoring    and   evaluation   framework     for   the   effective
implementation of this Act.


The framework shall assess the progress, effectiveness, and impact of national and
local government policies, programs, services, and investments relating to the care
economy and unpaid care and domestic work.


For this purpose, CEPPO, in coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority
(PSA), the Philippine Commission on Women (PCW), the Department of Budget
and Management (DBM), the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG), and other relevant agencies, shall:


   a. Develop national indicators and benchmarks for measuring unpaid care and
       domestic work, care burdens, access to care services and infrastructure, labor
       force participation impacts, gender equality outcomes, and other care
       economy-related concerns;
   b. Monitor the integration of care economy considerations into national and local
       development plans, investment programs, gender and development (GAD)
       plans and budgets, and social protection policies;


                                                                                   15
   c. Evaluate the effectiveness of government interventions intended to recognize,
      reduce, and redistribute unpaid care and domestic work;
   d. Maintain a centralized database and information system on care economy
      indicators, time-use data, and related statistics, subject to existing data privacy
      and statistical laws, rules, and regulations;
   e. Conduct periodic policy reviews and impact assessments to identify gaps,
      implementation challenges, and emerging priorities relating to the care
      economy; and
   f. Recommend legislative, policy, administrative, and budgetary measures to
      strengthen care-responsive governance and development planning.


All national government agencies and local government units covered by this Act
shall cooperate with and provide relevant information, reports, and assistance to
CEPPO for monitoring and evaluation purposes, subject to applicable laws and
regulations.


DEPDev, through CEPPO, shall submit to Congress and the Office of the President
an annual State of the Care Economy Report not later than June 30 of each year,
containing:


   a. Updates on the implementation of this Act;
   b. Key national and local care economy indicators;
   c. Findings from time-use surveys and related statistical studies;
   d. Assessment of government programs, expenditures, and policy outcomes
      relating to unpaid care and domestic work;
   e. Status of LGU compliance and implementation; and


                                                                                      16
   f. Recommendations for policy reforms and future government action.


The annual report shall be made publicly accessible through appropriate government
platforms, subject to applicable laws and regulations on public disclosure and data
privacy.


SEC. 12. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act, including the establishment and operationalization of the Care Economy
Policy and Planning Office (CEPPO) under the Department of Economy, Planning,
and Development (DEPDev), and the implementation of priority programs under
this Act, shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DEPDev and such
other concerned agencies as may be determined by the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM).


Thereafter, the funding requirements for the continued implementation of this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA), and may be
sourced from the budgets of implementing agencies, including, where applicable,
their Gender and Development (GAD) budgets, program funds, and other available
appropriations authorized under existing laws, rules, and regulations.


Local government units shall likewise be authorized to allocate funds for the
implementation of this Act from their respective internal revenue allotment (IRA) or
National Tax Allotment (NTA), local development funds, and GAD budgets, subject
to applicable budgeting guidelines.




                                                                                 17
The Department of Budget and Management (DBM), in consultation with DEPDev
through CEPPO and other concerned agencies, shall issue necessary budget
guidelines to ensure the effective and coordinated allocation and utilization of funds
for the implementation of this Act.


SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The DEPDev shall, in
coordination with the PSA, DILG, PCW and the DBM promulgate the rules and
regulations to implement the provisions of this Act within one hundred twenty (120)
days after its effectivity.


SEC. 14. Separability Clause. - If any part or provision of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.


SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders, issuances, decrees, rules
and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are deemed
amended, modified or repealed accordingly.


SEC. 16. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
