 AN ACT PROHIBITING THE SALE AND MARKETING OF UNHEALTHY FOODS
  SUCH AS SUGAR-SWEETENED BEVERAGES AND JUNK FOODS WITHIN
  ONE HUNDRED (100) METERS OF PUBLIC AND PRIVATE SCHOOLS, AND
                  PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       According to the Expanded National Nutrition Survey of 2019, 9.1% of children
aged 5-10 and 9.8% of children aged 10-19 are overweight. The number of overweight
adolescents has tripled within 15 years from 2004 to 2019. The Department of Health
recognizes that there is a growing problem of overweight and obesity among Filipino
children. Part of the problem lies in their access to unhealthy foods.

       Department of Education Order No. 13, Series of 2017 prohibits the sale of
unhealthy foods within school canteens. However, the order does not control food sold
outside of schools. School children are able to easily access unhealthy foods right
outside their school premises, which negates whatever regulation happens within the
schools. These food outlets are already covered by police power, which is exercised
by the legislative branch of government. As such, laws and ordinances are necessary
in order to regulate foods sold in these settings.

      This bill seeks to restrict access of children to unhealthy foods and help prevent
overweight and obesity by prohibiting the sale and marketing of unhealthy foods within
one hundred meters of school premises.

   Considering the rationale stated above, passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known and cited as the “Anti-Unhealthy
Foods in Schools Act.”

SEC. 2. Scope – This Act shall cover all public and private preparatory, elementary,
and junior and senior high schools in the Philippines.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms shall mean:

(a) “Premises” includes inside the school, and outside of the school within 100 meters of its
perimeter;

(b) “Perimeter” means the fence or boundary of the school;

(c) “Preparatory school” includes day care, nursery, kindergarten, and other equivalent
school levels;

(d) “Unhealthy food” refers to foods low in nutritional content and high in calories,
salt, and fats; and

(e) “Sugary Drinks” refer to any beverage with added sugar or other sweeteners (high
fructose corn syrup, sucrose, fruit juice concentrates, and more).

SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. – It shall be unlawful to sell, promote, market, or advertise
unhealthy foods or junk foods and sugary drinks within one hundred (100) meters of
school premises in all public and private preparatory, elementary, and junior and
senior high schools. For the
purpose of this Act, the Department of Education shall identify through its Implementing
Rules and Regulations which foods and beverages are to be prohibited.

A grace period of ninety (90) days after the Implementing Rules and Regulations of this
Act have been issued shall be given to all food establishments selling food near school
premises.

SEC. 5. Penalties. - All violations of the previous section shall be punishable as
      follows:

       First offense – Fine of Php 1,000.00
       Second offense – Fine of Php2,000.00
       Third offense – Fine of Php 3,000.00
       Fourth and succeeding offenses – Fine of Php 4,000.00 and grounds for
       closure or abeyance as public nuisance in accordance with the Civil Code of the
       Philippines.

SEC. 6. Appropriation. – The amount necessary for the implementation of the
provisions of this Act shall be taken from the current year’s budget of the Department
of Education. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of the Act shall be incorporated in the annual General Appropriations
Act.

SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of
Education shall promulgate the rules and regulations implementing the provisions of
this Act within ninety
(90) days from its effectivity.

SEC. 8. Separability Clause. – If for any reason, any part or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall
remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 10. Effectivity – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of national circulation.
