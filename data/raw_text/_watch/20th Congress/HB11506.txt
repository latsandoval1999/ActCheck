EXPLANATORY NOTE

Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that
“The State shall protect and promote the right to health of the people and
instill health consciousness among them.” Central to this mandate is the
guarantee of patient safety and the delivery of high-quality healthcare services
in all medical facilities across the country.

Despite advancements in medical technology, Healthcare-Associated
Infections (HAIs) remain a persistent and life-threatening risk to patients. A
critical vulnerability in infection control is the improper cleaning,
decontamination, and sterilization of reusable medical devices and hospital
workspaces. The emergence of highly resilient pathogens, such as prions, and
the increasing complexity of surgical instruments necessitate a rigorous,
scientifically backed approach to infection control that leaves no room for
error.

Currently, there is a need to professionalize and standardize stcrile
processing as a distinct, highly regulated discipline within the Philippine
healthcare system. The Central Sterile Services Department (CSSD) is the
beating heart of hospital infection control, yet standardizing its operations to
meet rigorous international benchmarks requires dedicated legislative
backing.

This bill seeks to elevate sterilization sciences by requiring all health
care facilities, whether government or private, hospital or office-based clinic,
to secure a specialized “Sterile Permit” prior to operation.

===== [page 2] =====
To achieve this, the proposed measure:

1. Establishes Strict Regulatory Standards: Mandates health care
facilities to submit detailed operational plans for decontamination,
disinfection, and sterilization based on international best practices,
including the Spaulding Classification System.

2. Empowers the Department of Health: Designates the Health
Facilities and Services Regulatory Bureau (HFSRB) as the licensing
agency tasked with inspecting facilities, issuing permits, monitoring
HAI rates, and enforcing compliance.

3. Professionalizes the Workspace and Workforce: Requires dedicated
architectural spaces for decontamination and _ sterile storage,
specialized equipment like medical washers, strict use of Personal
Protective Equipment (PPE), and concrete protocols for process failures
and occupational exposure.

4. Protects Healthcare Workers: Integrates occupational satety
measures and transmission-based precautions to shield the personnel
handling bioburden and hazardous materials daily.

By treating sterile processing not merely as a hospital support service,
but as a critical, highly specialized component of patient survival, we can
drastically reduce preventable infections, protect our frontline healthcare
workers, and align Philippine medical facilities with global health standards.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

REP. PHI CONRAD “DOK BONG” M. ACOP

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Sterile Permit
Act of the Philippines.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State shall promote, foster, nurture,
and protect the right to health of the people by issuing policies and standards
intended for use by healthcare members and for use by health care facilities
to help ensure that reusable medical instruments are sterilized and
decontaminated for patient use, work spaces have been properly disinfected
and single use devices have been disposed of properly.

c) Sterilization - Sterilization describes a process that destroys or

Spaulding Classification - The selection of a disinfectant must be
based, in part, upon the intended use of the device and the degree of
disinfection required for that device. The Spaulding Classification
System divides patient care items into three categories, each based on
the degree of risk of infection when the items are used in patient
care. the Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the
Association for the Advancement of Medical Instrumentation (AAMI) use
the Spaulding Classification System in their guidelines and
standards. The three Spaulding categories are:

e Critical items — Instruments or objects introduced directly into
the bloodstream or into other sterile areas of the body; examples
include surgical instruments, cardiac catheters and implants,
these items must be sterilized before use (disinfection is
inadequate).

e Semi-critical items-— Although these items come in contact with
intact mucous membranes, they do not ordinarily penetrate body
surfaces. Examples include non-invasive flexible fiberoptic
endoscopes, endotracheal tubes and anesthesia breathing
circuits. Semi- critical items should be subjected to a high level
disinfection process that can be expected to destroy all microbial
organisms, but not necessarily microbial spores.

e Non-critical items —- These items usually come into direct
contact with the patient's unbroken skin. Examples include
crutches and countertops. These items require thorough
cleaning, and in some _ cases, low-level disinfection to
intermediate-level disinfection.

Sterile — Free from viable microorganisms.

SEC 3. Sterile Permits. — No hospital, government or private, health care
facility, office-based health care facility or any establishment providing
medical or diagnostic services shall be allowed to operate unless plans have
been approved and a sterile permit is issued by the licensing agency as defined
in this Act.

SEC 4. Licensing Agency. — For purposes of setting standards in sterile
processing, the Health Facilities and Services Regulatory Bureau and the
Regulation Licensing and Enforcement Division within the regional offices, in
addition to its present duties shall act as the licensing agency. The Secretary
of Health shall reorganize this Bureau to include a staff of hospital infection
prevention and control practitioners, hospital administrators, sanitary
engineers, at least two (2) prominent citizens from the private sector who have
distinguished themselves in their profession or fields of central services and
sterile management and such personnel as may be necessary to carry out the
purposes of this Act without necessarily increasing the present personnel
strength of this Bureau.

hospitals in the Philippines, government or private, with a view to
determine their fitness to operate considering their respective Central

b) To prescribe guidelines for processing of medical devices according to
the Spaulding classification for the intended level of use;

SEC. 6. Filing of Application for Sterile Permit. — Application for a stcrile
permit shall be submitted to the Office of the Director, Bureau of Medical
Services, in a form prescribed by the latter and accompanied by a
decontamination plan proposed to be implemented. The application shall
state the name of the healthcare facility, ownership, location and type of
facility.

SEC. 7. Minimum Standards for Issuance of Sterile Permit. — To qualify
for a sterile permit, the hospital must submit a detailed plan for the cleaning,
disinfection, and sterilization of its medical devices and workspaces.

The facility must include designated rooms or areas for decontamination,
disinfection, decasing or breakout, sterile processing, and sterile storage, in
addition to an operational medical washer. Proper labeling must be
implemented in all required areas, and standard operating procedures must
be established for managing loaned items.

Furthermore, the overall plan must specify personnel qualifications, personal
protective equipment (PPE) requirements, work practice controls, and
transmission-based precautions. It must also outline concrete protocols for
addressing process failures, occupational exposures, and outbreaks involving
prions or pyrogens.

SEC 8. Inspection. - A sterile permit shall be issued by the licensing agency
only after a representative of the licensing agency has conducted an ocular
inspection and certified that the applicant has satisfactorily complied with
requisites prescribed in this Act. The sterile permit shall be renewed every
year upon payment of the prescribed fees.

SEC 9. Revocation of License. The licensing agency may suspend or revoke
a license already issued for any of the following grounds:

a) Repeated violation by the licensee of any provision of this Act or of any
other existing law;

b) Repeated violation of rules and regulations prescribed in the
implementation of this Act; or

c) Repeated failure to make necessary corrections or adjustments required
by the licensing agency in the improvement of facilities and services.

SEC. 10. Hearing - Any person, association, corporation, or any other private
entity who has been refused a sterile permit or whose permit has been
suspended or revoked shall be entitled to an administrative hearing to be
conducted by the Secretary of Health and his two undersecretaries to
determine the justifiability of such denial, suspension or revocation of the
permit: Provided, That such party may resort to the courts, as in other cases
provided by law.

SEC 11. Separate Permits Required.- Separate permits shall be required
for health care facilities or branches thereof maintained in separate premises,
even though they are operated under the same management: Provided,
However, That separate permits shall not be required for separate buildings
in the same compound: Provided, further, That permits of buildings within the
same compound shall also be secured from the licensing agency to determine
compliance with standards and requirements herein authorized.

===== [page 10] =====
SEC. 12. License not transferable. - The permit shall not be
transferable. The licensing agency shall be notified of any change in
ownership, change of name of the health care facility, and transfer of location
and in the latter case, an application for a new permit should be submitted.

SEC. 13. Rules and Regulations. - The Health Facilities and Services
Regulatory Bureau acting as a licensing agency and subject to the approval
of the Secretary of Health, shall promulgate rules and regulations to
implement the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 14. Fees. - Each applicant for a sterile permit shall pay the amount of
five hundred pesos as permit fee. A registration fee of five hundred pesos and
an annual license fee of one thousand pesos shall likewise be collected for
each hospital and for each approved license: Provided, that a government
hospital shall be exempt from the payment of such fees. The amount herein
collected shall be officially receipted by the licensing agency and shall
constitute as a revolving fund for the use of the licensing agency.

SEC. 15. Penalties. — Any person, partnership, association, or corporation
who establishes, operates, conducts, manages or maintains a hospital or
health care facility within the meaning of this Act without first obtaining a
permit as provided for in this Act or violates any provision hereof shall be
guilty of a misdemeanor, and upon conviction thereof shall be liable to a fine
of not more than five hundred pesos for the first offense and not more than
one thousand pesos for each subsequent offense, and each day that the
hospital shall operate after the first conviction shall be considered a
subsequent offense.

SEC 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after cach
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
