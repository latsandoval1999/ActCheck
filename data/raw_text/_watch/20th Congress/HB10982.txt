                                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE


Gambling in the Philippines was once a physically circumscribed activity, confined to licensed
casinos, cockpits, and lottery outlets whose physical barriers naturally kept children and other
vulnerable persons at bay. Mobile technology has since dissolved those barriers: every
smartphone is now a potential twenty-four-hour casino.
According to the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation (PAGCOR), the industry’s
gross gaming revenues reached approximately ₱396.14 billion in 2025, driven chiefly by
electronic and online gaming, which grew by roughly 30 percent to ₱201.12 billion and, for the
first time, overtook land-based casinos to account for 50.77 percent of total industry revenue.1
PAGCOR further estimates that approximately ten million Filipinos are active users of licensed
online platforms — a figure that has expanded several-fold within two years, and that does not
account for users of unlicensed offshore platforms operating beyond the reach of Philippine
regulation.
This is not merely an economic phenomenon but a matter of public health. The World Health
Organization, in its 2024 fact sheet on gambling, characterized gambling disorder as a health-
harming addictive behavior fueled by the industry’s commercialization and digitization, and
called for banning gambling advertising, promotion, and sponsorship as a necessary first step
toward reducing harm.2 The Lancet Public Health Commission on Gambling likewise found, in
2024, that an estimated 450 million people worldwide experience gambling-related harm, with
about 80 million meeting the clinical threshold for gambling disorder, and identified online
accessibility and sophisticated marketing, including targeted digital advertising and sports
sponsorships, as principal drivers, with harm falling disproportionately on adolescents and
lower-income communities. 3
This measure adopts a risk-proportionate structure. It prohibits outright the advertising,
promotion, endorsement, and sponsorship of online gambling, whose defining characteristics
such as continuous availability, high event frequency, the absence of any physical barrier to
entry, and algorithmically personalized solicitation are precisely the characteristics the evidence
associates most strongly with gambling-related harm. It does not prohibit, but instead regulates,

the charity sweepstakes and lottery draws conducted by the Philippine Charity Sweepstakes

The distinction rests on the structure of the product, not on the identity of its operator. It
therefore applies with equal force to the State’s own gambling operations and to those of private
licensees, and it does not exempt any operator from the prohibition merely because the operator
is a government instrumentality.
Because operators of online platforms are frequently affiliated with land-based establishments
sharing a common brand, Section 9 addresses the promotion of an online platform through the
advertising of a related non-online activity or of other goods and services. Republic Act No.
9211 confronted the same problem in the tobacco context, and the provision follows that
precedent. Without such a provision, a prohibition confined to online advertising would be
defeated within weeks of its effectivity.
Regulatory measures already undertaken have demonstrated both their worth and their limits.
PAGCOR has ordered the removal of gambling billboards and other out-of-home advertising,
entered into a memorandum of understanding with the Ad Standards Council subjecting
gambling advertisements to pre-screening, restricted advertising during primetime broadcast
hours, and, in June 2026, directed its licensees to replace gaming advertisements with materials
promoting the newly launched twenty-four-hour National Problem Gambling Helpline. 4 These
are regulatory issuances and voluntary industry undertakings. They bind licensees but not the
advertising agencies, media entities, digital platforms, affiliate networks, and endorsers through
whom the bulk of gambling marketing now flows; they may be revised or withdrawn by the
very agency whose remittances depend upon the activity being advertised; and they carry no
penal consequence. What is required is a statute.
This is not the first instance in which the State has confronted a legal product whose promotion
poses a disproportionate risk to public welfare. Republic Act No. 9211, the Tobacco Regulation
Act of 2003, kept tobacco lawful for adults while imposing stringent restrictions on its
advertising, promotion, and sponsorship. That was a legislative judgment consistent with the
constitutional mandates that the State protect and promote the right to health of the people
(Article II, Section 15) and promote and protect the physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and
social well-being of the youth (Article II, Section 13), and with the right of children to special
protection from all conditions prejudicial to their development (Article XV, Section 3[2]).
The measure has been calibrated to withstand constitutional scrutiny on four fronts.
First, it proceeds from clear legislative rather than merely administrative authority. In
Pharmaceutical and Health Care Association of the Philippines v. Duque III, the Supreme Court
invalidated an administratively imposed absolute advertising ban because the enabling statute
authorized regulation, not prohibition, expressly leaving to Congress the question of a legislated
prohibition.5
Second, it regulates commercial speech only. Section 6 expressly excludes news reporting and
editorial comment, academic and scientific discourse, artistic and literary works, government
and regulatory communications, responsible gambling information, and communications not

made for consideration, so that the Act reaches the marketing of gambling and not discussion
of it.
Third, it preserves functioning channels for lawful commercial communication. Section 7
permits informational communications on an operator’s own website or application behind age
and identity verification, and Chapter III preserves regulated advertising for non-online
activities. The restriction is therefore no more extensive than necessary to serve the State’s
interest, and consumers retain the means to distinguish licensed platforms from the unlicensed
operators against whom this Act is also directed.
Fourth, enforcement against online resources proceeds upon judicial order. In Disini, Jr. v.
Secretary of Justice, the Supreme Court struck down Section 19 of the Cybercrime Prevention
Act of 2012 because it permitted the Department of Justice to restrict access to computer data
upon a mere prima facie finding, without judicial determination.6 Section 16 accordingly vests
the power to order the disabling of an online resource in designated cybercrime courts, subject
to expedited timelines, a proportionality requirement, mandatory notice, and a summary remedy
to lift.
The bill further addresses what a prohibition alone cannot. Chapter V requires genuine age and
identity verification while prohibiting the retention of raw biometric data and any secondary use
of verification data, consistent with the Data Privacy Act of 2012. Chapter VII provides for
treatment, rehabilitation, and referral, in recognition that gambling disorder is a diagnosable
condition and that a State which derives revenue from gambling owes care to those whom it
harms.
Regulating the marketing of gambling is ultimately a matter of safeguarding the future of the
Filipino people, and most especially the youth. A sustainable and dignified national prosperity
must rest on the values of labor, education, and thrift, consonant with the constitutional policy
of promoting social justice in all phases of national development, rather than on the exploitative
pull of chance dressed up as opportunity.
In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                             AN ACT
   PROHIBITING THE ADVERTISING, PROMOTION, ENDORSEMENT, AND
 SPONSORSHIP OF ONLINE GAMBLING, REGULATING THE ADVERTISING,
 PROMOTION, ENDORSEMENT, AND SPONSORSHIP OF OTHER GAMBLING
    ACTIVITIES, STRENGTHENING AGE AND IDENTITY VERIFICATION
 REQUIREMENTS FOR ONLINE GAMBLING, ESTABLISHING JUDICIAL AND
  ADMINISTRATIVE ENFORCEMENT MECHANISMS, PROVIDING FOR THE
TREATMENT AND REHABILITATION OF PERSONS AFFECTED BY GAMBLING
          DISORDER, AND PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR


Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:


                                           CHAPTER I
                                   GENERAL PROVISIONS


SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Online Gambling Advertising
Prohibition Act of 2026”.
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect the public, particularly
children, young persons, and persons vulnerable to gambling-related harm, from the harmful
effects of gambling and from the pervasive promotion, advertising, and normalization of
gambling activities.
The State recognizes that gambling disorder is a diagnosable health condition, and that the rapid
expansion of digital platforms, social media, online advertising, influencer marketing, affiliate
marketing, and algorithmic advertising has substantially increased the accessibility and
visibility of gambling content, particularly among children and young persons.
The State further recognizes that gambling products differ materially in the risk of harm they
present, and that this risk is a function of the structure of the product rather than of the identity
of its operator. Gambling conducted remotely through a personal device is continuously
available, of high event frequency, unconstrained by any physical barrier, and susceptible to
algorithmic personalization, and its promotion shall accordingly be prohibited. The advertising
of gambling not conducted remotely shall be regulated in a manner proportionate to the risk it
presents. This Act applies with equal force to gambling conducted by government
instrumentalities and by private licensees.
Accordingly, the State shall adopt a comprehensive and technology-responsive regulatory
framework that shall:
(a) Protect children and young persons from exposure to gambling advertising, promotion, and
marketing;
(b) Prohibit the commercial promotion of online gambling through mass media, digital
platforms, social media, outdoor advertising, influencer marketing, and other communication
channels;
(c) Regulate the advertising, promotion, endorsement, and sponsorship of gambling activities
not conducted remotely, through mandatory advisories, content standards, and placement
restrictions;
(d) Prevent the use of shared brands, marks, and trade dress to promote online gambling through
the advertising of other activities or products;
(e) Require effective age and identity verification before an individual may establish or access
an online gambling account, while protecting the personal data collected for that purpose;
(f) Establish clear obligations for online platforms, Internet Service Providers (ISPs),
advertising-technology companies, influencers, affiliates, and other intermediaries;
(g) Strengthen the authority of government agencies to detect, investigate, remove, disable
access to, and penalize unlawful gambling advertisements, subject to judicial determination and
the requirements of due process;
(h) Ensure the availability of treatment, rehabilitation, and support services for persons affected
by gambling-related harm; and
(i) Ensure that the regulation of gambling advertising is implemented consistently with the
Constitution, particularly the freedoms of speech, expression, and of the press, by distinguishing
commercial gambling promotion from legitimate news reporting, academic discussion, artistic
expression, public-interest discourse, and government regulatory communications.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:
(a) Advertisement refers to any communication, material, representation, or message made
through any medium for the purpose of promoting, marketing, publicizing, endorsing, or
encouraging the purchase, use, participation in, or engagement with a product, service, activity,
or undertaking;
(b) Advertising technology or ad-tech company refers to any natural or juridical person that
develops, operates, or provides digital systems, platforms, software, or services designed to
facilitate, automate, manage, target, deliver, measure, or optimize online advertising content,
including advertising exchanges, demand-side and supply-side platforms, and programmatic
advertising networks;
(c) Affiliate marketing refers to a marketing arrangement whereby an individual or entity
receives compensation, commission, referral fees, or other consideration for directing users to a
gambling operator, gambling platform, or gambling-related service;
(d) App store refers to a digital distribution platform through which mobile or desktop
applications are offered for download or installation by users in the Philippines;
(e) Biometric or liveness verification refers to a technological process designed to establish
that the person undergoing identity verification is a live, present individual and is not merely a
photograph, recording, artificial or synthetic representation, or other spoofed identity;
(f) Child refers to any person below eighteen (18) years of age;
(g) Consideration refers to anything of value, whether monetary or non-monetary, including
compensation, commission, discounts, promotional equity, goods, services, access, or other
benefit;
(h) Gambling refers to any game, scheme, or arrangement in which a person stakes or risks
money or anything of value upon an outcome determined wholly or partly by chance, in the
expectation of receiving money or anything of value, and includes wagering and betting,
whether conducted physically or through electronic means. The term shall not include (1) sales
promotion activities duly permitted under Republic Act No. 7394 and the rules of the
Department of Trade and Industry; (2) games of skill or amusement in which no prize
convertible into money or its equivalent is awarded; and (3) electronic sports and social or
simulated games in which no stake of monetary value is placed and no prize convertible into
money or its equivalent may be won: Provided, That a free-to-play, demonstration, or simulated
version of an online gambling product offered for the purpose of inducing participation in
gambling shall be treated as online gambling for purposes of Chapter II;
(i) Gambling operator refers to any person or entity conducting, operating, or offering
gambling activities, whether or not duly authorized by the appropriate government authority,
and whether a private person or a government instrumentality;
(j) Influencer or content creator refers to any individual who, through a social media account,
website, streaming service, or other digital platform, creates or distributes content to an audience
and receives compensation, consideration, or other benefit for promoting a product, service, or
activity;
(k) Internet service provider or ISP refers to any person that provides to subscribers in the
Philippines the means to access the internet, including mobile network operators;
(l) Minimum gambling age refers to the minimum age prescribed by law or by the regulations
of the competent regulator for participation in the gambling activity concerned, and in no case
lower than eighteen (18) years. For online gambling, the minimum gambling age is twenty-one
(21) years;
(m) Non-online gambling activity refers to any gambling that is not online gambling, including
(1) gambling conducted within the physical premises of a licensed gaming establishment or
venue, including play at electronic gaming terminals and electronic bingo; (2) charity
sweepstakes, lotteries, and draw-based games conducted by the Philippine Charity Sweepstakes
Office (PCSO) under Republic Act No. 1169, as amended; (3) horse races conducted under
Republic Act Nos. 6631, 6632, and 7978 and Presidential Decree No. 420, including wagering
at authorized off-track betting stations; and (4) cockfighting conducted in a licensed cockpit
under Presidential Decree No. 449 and duly authorized by the local government unit concerned;
(n) Online gambling refers to gambling in which the customer places, transmits, or settles the
wager remotely through a computer, mobile device, or other electronic device under the
customer’s own control, by means of the internet, a mobile application, or any other electronic
communications system, regardless of the location of the operator, its servers, or its equipment,
where the gambling is directed at or accessible to persons in the Philippines. The term does not
include play conducted at a gaming terminal, or with the assistance of personnel, within the
physical premises of a licensed gaming establishment or venue;
(o) Online gambling advertisement refers to any advertisement, communication,
representation, or promotional material made for consideration or in the course of trade or
business that promotes, markets, endorses, solicits participation in, or encourages online
gambling;
(p) Online gambling promotion refers to any act done for consideration or in the course of
trade or business that is intended to attract users to, encourage participation in, or increase
engagement with online gambling, whether or not accompanied by a direct invitation to place a
wager;
(q) Online platform refers to a website, social media service, mobile application, marketplace,
content-sharing platform, streaming service, or other digital service through which users may
create, upload, access, share, disseminate, or receive content;
(r) Own licensed platform refers to the official website, native mobile application, own media,
or other digital property operated and controlled by a licensed online gambling operator and
accessible only to registered account holders who have completed the verification required
under Section 18;
(s) Public figure refers to any celebrity, actor, recording or performing artist, professional or
amateur athlete, media personality, social media influencer, digital creator, or other person with
a substantial public following;
(t) Responsible gambling information refers to factual information regarding the risks of
gambling, available treatment and support services, self-exclusion mechanisms, age restrictions,
and other information required by law or regulation, which shall not constitute an advertisement
when disseminated in accordance with this Act;
(u) Self-excluded person refers to a person who has requested exclusion from gambling
activities under the self-exclusion mechanism established under Section 20;
(v) Shared mark refers to a trademark, brand name, logo, trade dress, slogan, color scheme, or
other distinctive identifying element used by or on behalf of an online gambling operator or
platform, whether or not the same element is also used in connection with a non-online gambling
activity or with any other product or service; and
(w) Sponsorship refers to any arrangement under which a person provides consideration to an
individual, team, league, organization, event, program, or facility in exchange for the public
association of a gambling trademark, brand name, logo, or product therewith.


                                         CHAPTER II
          PROHIBITION ON THE ADVERTISING OF ONLINE GAMBLING


SEC. 4. Prohibition on Online Gambling Advertising. — Except as provided in Sections 6
and 7, online gambling advertisements, promotions, endorsements, and sponsorships intended
for public dissemination are hereby prohibited. No person shall, for consideration or in the
course of trade or business, advertise, promote, market, endorse, or sponsor:
(a) Any online gambling activity, operator, platform, or service;
(b) Any online gambling website, application, or account;
(c) Any bonus, reward, rebate, free bet, deposit match, jackpot, odds, promotional code, referral
code, or similar incentive intended to induce online gambling;
(d) Any product or service where the principal purpose or reasonably foreseeable effect of the
communication is to encourage participation in online gambling; or
(e) Any online gambling activity through the use of public figures.
SEC. 5. Prohibited Media, Channels, and Venues. — The prohibition under Section 4 shall
apply, without limitation, to:
(a) Television and radio;
(b) Newspapers, magazines, and other print media;
(c) Billboards, wallscapes, transit advertisements, and other outdoor advertising;
(d) Social media platforms;
(e) Video-sharing and streaming platforms;
(f) Websites and online publications;
(g) Mobile applications, in-application advertising, and push notifications;
(h) Search-engine advertising and paid search placement;
(i) Programmatic and targeted advertising;
(j) Influencer and creator marketing;
(k) Affiliate marketing;
(l) Electronic mail, short messaging service, and other direct digital marketing;
(m) Sponsorships of sports teams, leagues, events, concerts, festivals, and entertainment
programs;
(n) Advertising through livestreams, podcasts, and digital broadcasts; and
(o) Any other medium or technology used to publicly disseminate commercial online gambling
promotion.
SEC. 6. Excluded Communications. — Nothing in this Act shall be construed to prohibit,
restrict, or penalize:
(a) News reporting, editorial comment, commentary, documentary treatment, or other
journalistic coverage of gambling, gambling operators, or gambling regulation;
(b) Academic, scientific, medical, statistical, or research publication or discussion;
(c) Artistic, literary, cinematic, or dramatic works in which gambling is depicted, provided that
the depiction is not made for consideration furnished by or on behalf of a gambling operator;
(d) Communications by the Government, PAGCOR, or any regulatory agency in the
performance of their functions, including the publication of a registry of licensed gambling
operators and their authorized platforms;
(e) Responsible gambling information, and materials promoting the national problem gambling
helpline, self-exclusion, or treatment and support services;
(f) Transactional and account-related communications addressed to a verified account holder,
including notices on account status, transactions, security, self-exclusion, and responsible
gambling, provided that such communications contain no gambling promotion;
(g) Communications between gambling operators and their suppliers, licensors, regulators,
professional advisers, or investors, and disclosures required by law or by the rules of a securities
exchange; and
(h) Any communication not made for consideration and not made in the course of trade or
business, unless made by or on behalf of a gambling operator or pursuant to an affiliate
marketing arrangement.
SEC. 7. Permitted Communications: Own Licensed Platform of Online Operators. — By
way of exception, a licensed online gambling operator may display informative and non-
predatory communications concerning its own licensed platforms and games, subject to the
following cumulative conditions:
(a) No online gambling advertisement or promotion shall be displayed, and no game shall be
made accessible, until the user has completed registration and the age and identity verification
required under Section 18. Prior to the completion of verification, their own licensed platform
may display only the operator’s name and license details, information required by law,
responsible gambling information, and the registration and verification interface;
(b) The communication is not disseminated, redirected, syndicated, or otherwise made available
to any person other than a registered and verified account holder, and is not indexed for public
search;
(c) No communication under this Section is directed to a self-excluded person; and
(d) Every communication under this Section complies with the content standards prescribed
under Section 13 and carries the responsible gaming advisory prescribed under Section 14.
Nothing in this Section shall authorize a licensed online gambling operator to procure, sponsor,
or pay for the dissemination of any communication through a medium or channel prohibited
under Section 5.
SEC. 8. Endorsements and Sponsorships Relating to Online Gambling. — It shall be
unlawful for any public figure to endorse, recommend, promote, or publicly associate their
name, image, voice, likeness, or other identifying attributes with any online gambling activity,
brand, operator, or platform, where such association is made (1) pursuant to an endorsement,
promotional, sponsorship, affiliate, or similar arrangement, (2) for consideration, whether
monetary or non-monetary, and (3) with the intent or the reasonably foreseeable effect of
promoting online gambling. It shall likewise be unlawful for any person to procure, commission,
compensate, or otherwise facilitate an endorsement prohibited under this Section.
Online gambling operators, brand affiliates, and intermediaries are prohibited from sponsoring,
directly or indirectly, any cultural, athletic, entertainment, or community event, activity, team,
league, or facility. It shall be unlawful to enter into or renew any contract for the naming rights
of any facility using an online gambling trademark, brand name, or logo; to display such marks
on sports jerseys, athletic apparel, team uniforms, event banners, or event venues; to distribute,
sell, or offer branded merchandise bearing such marks; or to sponsor or integrate such brand
names within movies, television programs, online videos, livestreams, podcasts, or digital
broadcasts.
Any person who receives sponsorship consideration in violation of this Section shall remove or
cause the removal of the offending mark within thirty (30) days from the effectivity of this Act
or from receipt of notice from the enforcing agency, whichever is later, and shall thereafter be
solidarily liable with the sponsor for its continued display.
This Section shall not apply to the incidental appearance of a public figure in a news report,
documentary, artistic work, or other communication excluded under Section 6, nor to the mere
presence of a public figure in a licensed gambling establishment.
Nothing in this Act shall be construed as authorizing the promotion of owned media through
advertising and other means prohibited under this Act.
SEC. 9. Use of Shared Marks; Cross-Promotion. — Any advertisement, promotion,
endorsement, or sponsorship of a non-online gambling activity that employs a shared mark shall
be treated as an online gambling advertisement and shall be prohibited under Section 4, unless
it complies with all of the following:
(a) It does not display, reference, or embed a uniform resource locator, domain name, mobile
application name, download instruction, machine-readable code, or account registration prompt
relating to an online gambling platform;
(b) It does not depict, describe, or allude to remote or device-based play;
(c) The mark employed is one used in the ordinary course of the non-online gambling activity
concerned, and is not a mark, trade dress, or slogan distinctive to an online gambling platform;
and
(d) It complies with Chapter III.
Any advertisement, promotion, endorsement, or sponsorship of goods or services other than
gambling that employs a shared mark shall likewise be treated as an online gambling
advertisement and shall be prohibited under Section 4, unless it complies with paragraphs (a)
and (b) of this Section and the mark employed is one used in the ordinary course of the goods
or services concerned and is not a mark, trade dress, or slogan distinctive to an online gambling
platform. Such a communication shall not be required to comply with Chapter III.
No person shall use the advertising of any product or service to promote, or to direct any person
to, an online gambling platform.


                                        CHAPTER III
  REGULATION OF THE ADVERTISING OF OTHER GAMBLING ACTIVITIES


SEC. 10. Permitted Advertising of Non-Online Gambling. — The advertising, promotion,
marketing, endorsement, and sponsorship of a non-online gambling activity by a person duly
authorized to conduct it are permitted, subject to Section 9 and to Sections 11 to 14 and to such
further rules as the competent regulator may prescribe. Nothing in this Chapter shall be
construed to authorize any communication prohibited under Chapter II.
SEC. 11. Placement Restrictions. — No communication permitted under Section 10 shall be:
(a) Directed at, or have content likely to appeal to, persons below the minimum gambling age
for the activity concerned;
(b) Placed in any program, publication, platform, event, or medium whose audience is composed
predominantly of persons below the minimum gambling age;
(c) Placed, in the case of outdoor advertising, within one hundred (100) meters from any point
of the perimeter of an elementary or secondary school, day care centre, playground, or other
facility principally frequented by children;
(d) Delivered through programmatic or behaviorally targeted placement, influencer or creator
marketing, affiliate marketing, or direct digital marketing by electronic mail, short messaging
service, or similar means; or
(e) Directed to a self-excluded person.
SEC. 12. Endorsements and Sponsorships of Non-Online Gambling. — A public figure may
endorse a non-online gambling activity subject to Sections 11 and 13, provided that the
endorsement is not made by a person whose audience is composed predominantly of persons
below the minimum gambling age.
Sponsorship in connection with a non-online gambling activity is permitted subject to Sections
11 and 13, except that no person shall:
(a) Sponsor any event, team, activity, or facility primarily attended by or directed at students, or
bearing the name of an educational institution;
(b) Enter into or renew any contract for the naming rights of a facility of an educational
institution; or
(c) Distribute, sell, or offer branded merchandise designed or likely to appeal to persons below
the minimum gambling age.
SEC. 13. Content Standards. — Every communication permitted under Section 7 or Section
10 shall:
(a) State clearly that persons below the minimum gambling age for the activity concerned are
prohibited from participating in it;
(b) Not imply that a player’s skill can influence the outcome of a game of chance;
(c) Not give the impression that gambling is a means of earning income, of achieving financial
security, or of resolving financial, personal, or family difficulty;
(d) Not include misleading statements about odds, returns, or prizes, including any implication
of guaranteed winnings;
(e) Not associate gambling with financial success, social or personal prestige, sexual
attractiveness, or achievement;
(f) Not employ imagery, characters, animation, music, or memes likely to appeal to persons
below the minimum gambling age; and
(g) Carry the responsible gaming advisory prescribed under Section 14.
SEC. 14. Responsible Gaming Advisory and Warning. — Every communication permitted
under Section 7 or Section 10 shall carry a standardized responsible gaming advisory and
financial risk warning. The advisory shall be clearly legible and shall occupy not less than thirty
percent (30%) of the total visual area of a communication under Section 7, and not less than
twenty percent (20%) of the total visual area of a communication under Section 10. It shall be
presented in both English and Filipino in bold capital letters, and shall state that gambling
involves severe financial risk and the hazard of addiction, the minimum gambling age for the
activity concerned, an exhortation to play responsibly, and the national problem gambling
helpline.
The current contact details of the national problem gambling helpline designated by PAGCOR
shall be prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations and may be updated without need
of amendment of this Act. Audio and audio-visual communications shall carry the same
advisory, read in a clear, audible, and unhurried manner at the conclusion of the communication.
PAGCOR, in coordination with the PCSO, the Philippine Racing Commission, and the Games
and Amusements Board, shall prescribe the form and format of the advisory, including a short
form for outlet signage and merchandise, and may require its translation into such other
Philippine languages as it may determine.


                                         CHAPTER IV
                        INTERMEDIARIES AND ENFORCEMENT


SEC. 15. Duties of Online Platforms, Intermediaries, and Advertising Technology
Companies. — Every online platform, ad-tech company, advertising agency, media entity, ISP,
and app store operating in or directing services to the Philippines shall:
(a) Maintain and enforce a category-level prohibition on the serving, placement, or delivery of
online gambling advertisements to users located in the Philippines;
(b) Refuse to accept, process, or serve any advertising order that would violate Section 4, 5, 9,
or 11(d);
(c) Establish an accessible mechanism through which any person may report a prohibited
gambling advertisement, and act upon such report within seventy-two (72) hours;
(d) Comply with any order issued under Section 16 within twenty-four (24) hours from service;
(e) Publish a semiannual report on its enforcement of gambling advertising policies in the
Philippines; and
(f) Submit an annual compliance certification to PAGCOR in such form as the implementing
rules and regulations may prescribe.
No person shall be held civilly or criminally liable for any act done in good faith and in
compliance with this Section or with an order issued under Section 16.
SEC. 16. Judicial Disabling of Unlawful Online Gambling Advertising Resources. —
Access to an online resource used to disseminate advertisements in violation of Chapter II may
be disabled only upon order of a court, in accordance with the following:
(a) Application. — PAGCOR, the Department of Justice through its Office of Cybercrime, the
Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), or the Cybercrime
Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC) may file a verified application for a Gambling
Advertising Disabling Order with any designated cybercrime court of the Regional Trial Court,
identifying the online domain, subdomain, uniform resource locator, internet protocol address,
mobile application, or account concerned. The application shall be raffled and acted upon within
twenty-four (24) hours from filing;
(b) Issuance. — The court may issue the order ex parte upon a finding of probable cause that
the resource is being used in violation of this Act. The order shall identify the resource with
reasonable particularity, specify the least restrictive technical means adequate to give it effect,
and be effective upon service;
(c) Extreme urgency. — Where the application shows that grave and irreparable harm would
result before the matter can be acted upon, the Executive Judge may issue an interim disabling
order effective for seventy-two (72) hours, within which period the application shall be raffled
and resolved;
(d) Compliance. — ISPs, hosting providers, online platforms, search engines, and app stores
shall execute the blocking, disabling, removal, or de-indexing directed by the order within
twenty-four (24) hours from service and in accordance with its terms. The National
Telecommunications Commission (NTC) shall transmit the order to the ISPs concerned and
monitor compliance;
(e) Proportionality. — No order shall direct the blocking of an internet protocol address or of
an entire platform where a narrower technical measure would be adequate, and no order shall
have the effect of disabling access to lawful content that can reasonably be segregated from the
offending material;
(f) Notice and remedy. — The applicant shall serve a copy of the order upon the operator or
registrant of the affected resource, at the contact details on record, within twenty-four (24) hours
from issuance, or by publication on the website of PAGCOR where such details cannot be
ascertained. Any aggrieved person may file a verified motion to lift or modify the order, which
shall be heard summarily and resolved within five (5) days from filing;
(g) Duration. — The order shall be effective for thirty (30) days and may be extended by the
court, upon motion and for good cause, for successive periods of thirty (30) days, or made
permanent after hearing upon a finding that the resource is operated by an unlicensed gambling
operator or has been used persistently in violation of this Act; and
(h) Restoration. — Where an order is lifted, modified, or reversed, access shall be restored
within twenty-four (24) hours, without prejudice to any action for damages arising from bad
faith or gross negligence on the part of the applicant agency.
SEC. 17. Enforcement Agencies; Cease-and-Desist Authority. — This Act shall be enforced
as follows:
(a) PAGCOR, as to persons under its licensing or regulatory authority, and as to its own
gambling operations;
(b) The Department of Trade and Industry, as to advertisers, advertising agencies, media entities,
and other persons not under the licensing authority of PAGCOR, pursuant to its mandate under
Republic Act No. 7394;
(c) The PCSO, the Philippine Racing Commission, the Games and Amusements Board, and the
sanggunian concerned, as to Chapter III and the activities and entities within their respective
jurisdictions; and
(d) The Movie and Television Review and Classification Board, as to the carriage of gambling
advertisements in broadcast and linear streaming content within its jurisdiction.
Any agency named in this Section may, after notice and an opportunity to be heard, issue a
cease-and-desist order directing the discontinuance of any communication in violation of this
Act. Non-compliance shall constitute an independent ground for the imposition of
administrative sanctions under Section 21.


                                          CHAPTER V
          AGE AND IDENTITY VERIFICATION AND DATA PROTECTION


SEC. 18. Mandatory Age and Identity Verification. — No gambling operator shall permit a
person to create, maintain, or access an online gambling account unless the operator has first
verified that the person is at least twenty-one (21) years of age and is otherwise eligible to
participate in gambling. Verification shall include:
(a) Presentation of a government-issued identification document or of a government-approved
digital identity credential;
(b) Verification of the person’s date of birth;
(c) Verification against the Philippine Identification System or such other government or
authorized identity database as may be permitted by law, subject to Republic Act No. 11055 and
its implementing rules and regulations;
(d) Biometric or liveness verification; and
(e) Such other identity-authentication measures as PAGCOR may prescribe in consultation with
the DICT and the National Privacy Commission (NPC).
The operator shall verify, before each session, that the account is being accessed by the
registered and verified account holder, in such manner as the implementing rules and regulations
may prescribe. Where biometric verification cannot reasonably be undergone, the operator shall
provide a secure alternative verification procedure, approved by PAGCOR, that affords an
equivalent level of assurance and includes safeguards against fraud and identity theft;
compliance with such alternative procedure shall satisfy the requirements of Section 7(a).
This Section shall likewise apply to any account-based wagering on a non-online gambling
activity, in which case the applicable minimum gambling age shall govern.
SEC. 19. Data Protection Safeguards. — All personal data collected for purposes of Section
18 shall be processed in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173, and subject to the following:
(a) Raw biometric images, recordings, and samples shall not be retained. Verification shall be
performed on a match-and-discard basis, and any raw biometric data shall be permanently
deleted immediately upon completion of verification;
(b) Where a biometric template is retained for authentication, it shall be retained only in
encrypted, non-reversible form, solely for the purpose of authenticating the account holder, and
shall be deleted within thirty (30) days from the closure of the account;
(c) Personal data collected under Section 18 shall not be used, disclosed, shared, sold, or
otherwise processed for marketing, profiling, credit assessment, behavioral targeting, or any
purpose other than verification, authentication, self-exclusion, regulatory reporting, and
compliance with law;
(d) Every gambling operator shall conduct and maintain a privacy impact assessment, register
its data processing system with the NPC, and designate a data protection officer;
(e) Personal data collected under Section 18 shall not be transferred outside the Philippines
except upon a demonstration to the NPC of an adequate level of protection; and
(f) Any personal data breach shall be reported to the NPC and to the affected data subjects in
accordance with Republic Act No. 10173, and to PAGCOR within seventy-two (72) hours from
knowledge thereof.
Violation of this Section shall be punishable under Republic Act No. 10173, without prejudice
to the administrative sanctions under Section 21 of this Act.
SEC. 20. Player Protection and Self-Exclusion. — PAGCOR shall establish and maintain a
national self-exclusion registry applicable to all licensed gambling operators, to the PCSO, and
to persons authorized to conduct account-based wagering on horse races. No gambling operator
shall permit a self-excluded person to create or access a gambling account, or direct to a self-
excluded person any communication permitted under Section 7 or Section 10. PAGCOR shall
prescribe rules on deposit and loss limits, session limits, cooling-off periods, and the prohibition
of credit-based funding of gambling accounts.
                                         CHAPTER VI
                              PENALTIES AND LIABILITIES


SEC. 21. Administrative Sanctions on Gambling Operators and Other Persons. — After
notice and hearing, the enforcing agency shall impose upon any gambling operator or other
person that violates Section 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 18, 19, or 20 of this Act the following
administrative sanctions:
(a) First offense — a fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00) nor
more than Two Million Pesos (₱2,000,000.00), or three (3) times the gross value of the
advertising, promotional, or sponsorship placement concerned, whichever is higher;
(b) Second offense — a fine of not less than Two Million Pesos (₱2,000,000.00) nor more than
Five Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00), or three (3) times the gross value of the placement
concerned, whichever is higher, and, where the offender holds a licence issued by the enforcing
agency, suspension of the licence for a period not exceeding ninety (90) days; and
(c) Third and subsequent offenses — a fine of not less than Five Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00)
nor more than Ten Million Pesos (₱10,000,000.00), or five (5) times the gross value of the
placement concerned, whichever is higher, and, where the offender holds a licence issued by the
enforcing agency, suspension of the licence for not less than ninety (90) days nor more than one
(1) year, or revocation of the licence.
In determining the sanction to be imposed, the enforcing agency shall consider the gravity,
duration, and reach of the violation; whether it was willful; whether it targeted or was likely to
reach persons below the minimum gambling age or self-excluded persons; whether it was
promptly and voluntarily remedied; and the compliance history of the person concerned. A
violation that is technical and inadvertent in character and promptly remedied shall be dealt with
by written warning or by the minimum fine. Decisions under this Section shall be appealable to
the Court of Appeals in accordance with Rule 43 of the Rules of Court.
SEC. 22. Criminal Liability. — Any person who willfully violates Section 4, 5, 7, 8, or 9 of
this Act shall, upon conviction, suffer :
(a) First offense — imprisonment of not more than one (1) year, or a fine of not more than Two
Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱200,000.00), or both;
(b) Second offense — imprisonment of not less than one (1) year nor more than two (2) years,
or a fine of not more than Three Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱300,000.00), or both; and
(c) Third and subsequent offenses — imprisonment of not less than two (2) years nor more than
three (3) years, or a fine of not more than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00), or both,
and the revocation of any business permit or licence.
A third or subsequent willful violation of Section 11, 12, 13, or 14 within a period of three (3)
years shall be punishable under paragraph (a) of this Section. Criminal actions under this Act
shall be filed with the Regional Trial Court, and criminal liability shall be without prejudice to
the administrative sanctions under Section 21.
SEC. 23. Liability of Public Figures and Endorsers. — Any public figure who violates
Section 8 or Section 12 of this Act shall be liable for:
(a) First offense — a fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00) or three times (3x)
the gross value of the endorsement, promotional, or sponsorship arrangement, whichever is
higher, and disgorgement of all consideration received thereunder; and
(b) Second and subsequent offenses — a fine of Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00)
or three times (3x) the gross contract value, whichever is higher, and disgorgement of all
consideration received thereunder, and disqualification from entering into any gambling-related
endorsement, promotional, or sponsorship arrangement for a period of not less than three (3)
years nor more than five (5) years.
No liability shall attach under this Section unless the person knew, or in the exercise of ordinary
diligence should have known, that the subject of the arrangement was a gambling activity,
gambling operator, or gambling platform.
SEC. 24. Liability of Media Entities, Online Platforms, and Intermediaries. — Any media
entity, advertising agency, online platform, ad-tech company, app store, or ISP that violates
Section 15 of this Act, or that fails without lawful cause to comply within the prescribed period
with an order issued under Section 16, shall be subject to:
(a) An administrative fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) for every day of continued
non-compliance; and
(b) For repeated or willful non-compliance, the suspension or revocation of their secondary
licenses, franchise authorities, or business permits to operate in the Philippines.
No liability shall attach under this Section where non-compliance was due to circumstances
beyond the control of the person concerned, or where that person acted in good faith and with
reasonable diligence.
SEC. 25. Liability of Corporate Officers and Foreign Nationals. — Where the offender is a
juridical person, criminal liability under Section 22 shall attach to the president, chief executive
officer, directors, trustees, or other officers who knowingly and actively participated in or
authorized the violation, or who, having the duty and the actual ability to prevent it, deliberately
failed to do so. Mere membership in the board of directors, without such participation,
authorization, or deliberate failure, shall not give rise to criminal liability.
If the offender is a foreign national, the offender shall, after service of sentence and payment of
any fine imposed, be deported without need of further proceedings.
SEC. 26. Non-Liability of Players. — No criminal or administrative liability shall attach under
this Act to any person by reason only of participating in gambling or of accessing or receiving
a gambling advertisement. In no case shall a child be penalized under this Act.


                                         CHAPTER VII
                AWARENESS, TREATMENT, AND REHABILITATION


SEC. 27. Responsible Gambling Awareness and Education Campaign. — PAGCOR, in
coordination with the PCSO and other relevant government agencies, shall undertake a
continuing responsible gambling awareness and education campaign on the risks and harmful
effects of excessive, compulsive, or irresponsible gambling, including information on
responsible gambling practices and on available interventions, counseling, treatment, and
support services for individuals and families affected by gambling-related harm. The campaign
shall be conducted in coordination with the Department of Education and the Commission on
Higher Education for purposes of age-appropriate, school-based information, which shall be
integrated into the basic education curriculum and teacher development modules within one (1)
school year from the effectivity of this Act, and shall promote the national problem gambling
helpline. The private sector shall likewise be encouraged to undertake or support such
campaigns.
SEC. 28. Treatment, Rehabilitation, and Referral. — PAGCOR, in coordination with the
Department of Health (DOH), the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), and
the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), shall:
(a) Maintain and operate a national problem gambling helpline available at all times and free of
charge from any Philippine network;
(b) Accredit treatment and rehabilitation facilities and train service providers for the
management of gambling disorder;
(c) Establish a referral pathway from the helpline to accredited facilities and to mental health
services under Republic Act No. 11036; and
(d) In coordination with PhilHealth, develop benefit packages for the treatment of gambling
disorder.


                                       CHAPTER VIII
                                    FINAL PROVISIONS


SEC. 29. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the agencies concerned and against the
corporate operating budget of PAGCOR. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
SEC. 30. Annual Report and Periodic Review. — PAGCOR shall submit to the President and
to Congress an annual report on the implementation of this Act, including enforcement statistics,
orders applied for and issued under Section 16, helpline and treatment utilization, and available
indicators of gambling-related harm and of the size of the unlicensed market. Congress shall
review this Act three (3) years from its effectivity and every three (3) years thereafter.
SEC. 31. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, PAGCOR and the Department of Trade and Industry shall jointly
promulgate the implementing rules and regulations, in consultation with the PCSO, the
Philippine Racing Commission, the Games and Amusements Board, the DILG, DICT, DOJ,
DOH, NTC, NPC, and other agencies concerned, and with relevant stakeholders. Failure to
promulgate the implementing rules and regulations within the period prescribed shall not
suspend the effectivity or enforceability of this Act.
SEC. 32. Transitory Provision. — This Act shall apply immediately upon its effectivity to all
gambling advertisements, promotions, sponsorships, and endorsements, except that obligations
arising under agreements existing on the date of effectivity may be performed for a period of
not more than one (1) year from effectivity, solely to the extent necessary to wind down such
obligations.
During the transition period, no new advertising or sponsorship agreement or arrangement shall
be entered into, renewed, or extended except those necessary to wind down existing contractual
obligations, subject to such rules as may be prescribed by the enforcing agencies. No
consideration already paid under such an agreement shall be recoverable by reason only of this
Act.


SEC. 33. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions shall continue to be in full force and effect.
SEC. 34. Savings and Repealing Clause. — Republic Act No. 1169, as amended; Republic
Act Nos. 6631, 6632, and 7978; Presidential Decree Nos. 420 and 449; and the authority of local
government units over cockpits under Republic Act No. 7160 shall remain in force, subject to
the standards prescribed in Chapter III of this Act. Presidential Decree No. 1869, as amended
by Republic Act No. 9487, and all other laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.
SEC. 35. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
