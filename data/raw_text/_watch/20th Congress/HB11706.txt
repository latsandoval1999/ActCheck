EXPLANATORY NOTE

Under Republic Act No. 9262 (Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act)
and Republic Act No. 9710 (Magna Carta of Women), Barangay Violence Against Women and
their Children (VAWC) Desks serve as a frontline response and primary refuge where women
and children experiencing abuse can safely report incidents, obtain protection, and access vital
support services. While there are over 40,000 active desks nationwide,’ significant operational
gaps still persist such as responders facing severe resource limitations, lack of standardized

training, low compensation, and no security of tenure.

The proposed measure addresses these critical issues by institutionalizing and
professionalizing barangay-level VAWC response nationwide. It requires every barangay to
establish a fully equipped VAWC Desk and designate at least two (2) qualified VAWC Desk
Officers. It also establishes facility standards, mandates a minimum monthly honorarium
alongside key benefits for non-plantilla desk officers, and identifies funding sources for its
implementation. Essential safeguards are introduced to protect VAWC Desk officers from
harassment, threats, or retaliation while performing their duties, alongside strict accountability

measures for non-compliant local officials.

By empowering frontline personnel and securing institutional support, this bill ensures

every Barangay VAWC Desk continues to serve as a secure and accessible sanctuary for justice.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A MAGNA CARTA FOR BARANGAY VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN
AND THEIR CHILDREN (VAWC) DESKS AND OFFICERS, INSTITUTIONALIZING THEIR

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress

ARTICLE I. GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Magna Carta of Barangay
Violence Against Women and Their Children (VAWC) Desks and Officers.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

(a) Protect and promote the rights, dignity, safety, and welfare of women and children
against all forms of violence, abuse, exploitation, and discrimination;

(b) Ensure that every barangay maintains a permanent, functional, adequately resourced,
and accessible frontline mechanism for the prevention of and response to violence
against women and their children;

(c) Recognize Barangay VAWC Desk Officers as essential frontline service providers whose
functions require competence, continuity, protection, accountability, and adequate
compensation;

(d) Institutionalize national standards of operation, training, documentation, and case

handling while respecting local autonomy under Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise

known as the "Local Government Code of 1991";
(e) Strengthen the implementation of Republic Act No. 9262 and Republic Act No. 9710

through professionalized and sustainable barangay-level mechanisms; and

===== [page 3] =====
(f) Guarantee immediate, survivor-centered, trauma-informed, gender-responsive,

confidential, and accessible services for victim-su rvivors.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

(a) "Barangay VAWC Desk" refers to a facility established in every barangay to
receive, respond to, document, assess, manage, and refer cases of violence
against women and their children in a gender-responsive and

survivor-centered manner;
(b) "Barangay VAWC Desk Officer" refers to the barangay personnel or

functionary designated or appointed by the Punong Barangay to manage and
operate the Barangay VAWC Desk and perform duties under this Act;

(c) "Victim-survivor" refers to a woman or child who has experienced any act of
violence, abuse, exploitation, or gender-based violence as defined under
Republic Act No. 9262, Republic Act No. 9710, and other related laws;

(d) "Survivor-centered approach" refers to an approach that prioritizes the rights,

needs, safety, dignity, choices, and confidentiality of victim-survivors; and
(e) "Trauma-informed care" refers to services and interventions that recognize

the psychological, emotional, and social effects of trauma and seek to avoid

re-traumatization.

SEC. 4. Guiding Principles. — The implementation of this Act shall be guided by the
following principles:
(a) Survivor-centered and trauma-informed response,
(b) Confidentiality and data privacy;
(c) Accessibility, inclusivity, and non-discrimination,

(d) Gender equality and women's empowerment;
(e) Local autonomy with nationally mandated minimum standards; and

(f) Accountability, professionalism, and transparency.

ARTICLE Il. BARANGAY VAWC DESKS

SEC. 5.. Mandatory Establishment of Barangay VAWC Desks. - All barangays
nationwide shall establish and maintain a Barangay Violence Against Women and Their

Children (VAWC) Desk, which shall serve as the primary community-level facility for receiving,

documenting, responding to, and referring VAWC cases.

The Barangay VAWC Desk shall be located within the barangay hall or in another secure,
accessible, and private facility designated by the barangay.

Every Barangay VAWC Desk shall ensure immediate, confidential, gender-responsive,

and accessible assistance to victim-survivors.

===== [page 4] =====
SEC. 6. Minimum Standards for Barangay VAWC Desks. - Every Barangay VAWC
Desk shall, at a minimum, be equipped with:
(a) A private and secure interview or consultation space;

(b) Standardized intake, documentation, and reporting tools;
(c) Secure and confidential record-keeping systems compliant with applicable data

privacy laws;
(d) Updated referral directories for law enforcement agencies, hospitals, social
welfare offices, prosecutors, legal aid providers, shelters, and other support

SeETVICeS;
(e) Basic communication facilities necessary for coordination and emergency

response; and
(f) Information materials on VAWC laws, victim rights, referral mechanisms, and

available support services.

ARTICLE III. BARANGAY VAWC DESK OFFICERS

SEC. 7. Institutionalization of Barangay VAWC Desk Officers. - Each barangay shall
designate at least two (2) Barangay VAWC Desk Officers who shall be responsible for the
management and operation of the Barangay VAWC Desk.

Barangays with larger populations or higher incidence of VAWC cases may designate

additional officers as necessary.

SEC. 8. Status and Nature of Appointment. —
(a) A Barangay VAWC Desk Officer may be appointed or designated as:
1. A barangay employee occupying a plantilla position; or
2. A barangay functionary receiving honoraria.

(b) The appointment or designation shall be made through a barangay ordinance,
resolution, or written order, consistent with Republic Act No. 7160.

(c) Preference shall be given to qualified women barangay officials, employees, or
trained community members’ with demonstrated commitment _ to
gender-responsive public service.

(d) No Barangay VAWC Desk Officer shall be removed except for just cause and after

due process.

SEC. 9. Qualifications. — No person shall be appointed or designated as a Barangay
VAWC Desk Officer unless he or she:
(a) Isa Filipino citizen and resident of the barangay;
(b) Is at least twenty-one (21) years of age;
(c) Has completed at least secondary education;
(d) Has completed accredited training on gender sensitivity, VAWC laws,

trauma-informed care, survivor-centered response, and case handling conducted

===== [page 5] =====
or recognized by the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD),
the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the Philippine
Commission on Women (PCW), or comparable agencies;

(e) Possesses_ integrity, discretion, trustworthiness, and commitment to

gender-responsive public service; and
(f) Has no conviction for any crime involving moral turpitude, violence, abuse, or

sexual offenses.

SEC. 10. Functions and Duties of Barangay VAWC Desk Officers. — Barangay VAWC
Desk Officers shall:
(a) Receive, assess, document, and manage VAWC complaints and reports;
(b) Assist victim-survivors in securing Barangay Protection Orders pursuant to

Republic Act No. 9262;
(c) Facilitate referrals to appropriate law enforcement, judicial, health, legal, and

social welfare agencies and support organizations;

(d) Maintain secure, accurate, and confidential case records;

(e) Conduct barangay-level information, advocacy, education, and prevention
activities on VAWC and gender sensitivity;

(f) Assist in the preparation and implementation of barangay gender-responsive
plans and programs related to VAWC prevention and response;

(g) Coordinate with government agencies, non-government organizations, civil
society groups, and community support networks;

(h) Submit reports required under this Act and other applicable laws; and

(i) Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out this Act and

related laws.

ARTICLE IV. NATIONAL STANDARDS AND THE
BARANGAY VAW DESK HANDBOOK

SEC. 11. Adoption of the Barangay VAW Desk Handbook. - The Barangay Violence

Against Women (VAW) Desk Handbook jointly issued by the Philippine Commission on Women
and the Department of the Interior and Local Government is hereby formally adopted as the
official national operational and procedural standard for all Barangay VAWC Desks and Officers.

SEC. 12. Mandatory Compliance. — All Barangay VAWC Desk Officers and barangay
officials shall strictly comply with the standards, protocols, and procedures provided in the
Barangay VAW Desk Handbook, particularly on:

(a) Case intake, assessment, documentation, and reporting;

(b) Issuance and enforcement of Barangay Protection Orders;

(c) Survivor-centered, trauma-informed, and gender-responsive handling of cases;

(d) Confidentiality and data privacy;
(e) Referral and coordination mechanisms; and

===== [page 6] =====
(f) Monitoring and reporting procedures.

SEC. 13. Legal Effect and Updating of the Handbook. — The Barangay VAW Desk
Handbook shall have suppletory application to this Act and its Implementing Rules and

Regulations.

The Philippine Commission on Women, in coordination with the Department of the
Interior and Local Government, the Department of Social Welfare and Development, the
Department of Health, the Philippine National Police, and other concerned agencies, shall
periodically review and update the Handbook to reflect changes in laws, jurisprudence, policies,

and best practices, without diminishing the rights and standards provided under this Act.

ARTICLE V. COMPENSATION, BENEFITS, AND FUNDING

SEC. 14. Right to Just Compensation. —- Barangay VAWC Desk Officers shall be entitled
to just, fair, and reasonable compensation in recognition of the nature, sensitivity, and

importance of their duties as frontline service providers.

SEC. 15. Salaries and Honoraria. —

(a) A Barangay VAWC Desk Officer occupying a plantilla position shall receive
compensation in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

(b) A Barangay VAWC Desk Officer not occupying a plantilla position shall receive a
monthly honorarium of not less than Eight Thousand Pesos (P8,000.00), subject to
adjustment by barangay ordinance and availability of funds.

(c) The honorarium and compensation under this Act shall form part of the

barangay'’s annual budget and personal services allocation, subject to existing

budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules.
(d) Barangays may provide higher compensation, subject to availability of funds and

compliance with applicable laws and regulations.

SEC. 16. Allowance and Benefits. - Barangay VAWC Desk Officers may be granted the

following, subject to barangay ordinance and budgetary rules:

(a) Transportation allowance;

(b) Communication allowance;

(c) Training, educational, and capability-building support;

(d) Subsistence allowance during official activities;

(e) Insurance coverage and social protection benefits under applicable laws and

programs; and
(f) Other lawful allowances and benefits.

SEC. 17. Funding Sources — Funds necessary for the implementation of this Act,

including compensation, benefits, training, and operational expenses, shall be charged against:

===== [page 7] =====
(a) The barangay Gender and Development (GAD) Fund pursuant to Republic Act

No. 9710;
(b) Barangay appropriations for personal services and maintenance and other

operating expenses under Republic Act No. 7160; and

(c) Other lawful sources.

The Sangguniang Barangay shall annually appropriate sufficient funds in the barangay
budget to support the implementation of this Act.

ARTICLE VI. PROTECTION, ACCOUNTABILITY, AND MONITORING

SEC. 18. Protection from Harassment, Threats, and Retaliation. —- Barangay VAWC Desk
Officers shall be protected from harassment, intimidation, coercion, threats, retaliation, or undue

influence arising from the lawful performance of their duties under this Act.

SEC. 19. Audit Compliance and Accountability. — All expenditures, disbursements, and
payments under this Act shall comply with existing Commission on Audit (COA) rules and

regulations, including requirements on legality, necessity, reasonableness, documentation, and

budget limitations.

Disbursements found to be without legal basis or in violation of auditing and budgeting

rules shall be subject to audit disallowance, refund, and other administrative, civil, or criminal

liabilities under existing laws.

SEC. 20. Monitoring and Oversight. - The Department of the Interior and Local
Government, in coordination with the Philippine Commission on Women and the Department of
Social Welfare and Development, shall monitor compliance with this Act and develop

standardized monitoring, reporting, and evaluation mechanisms.

Barangays shall submit quarterly reports on VAWC cases handled, services provided,

referrals made, and related activities to the appropriate City or Municipal offices of the DILG
and DSWD.

SEC. 21. Penalties for Non-Compliance. — Any barangay official or responsible officer

who fails to comply with the provisions of this Act shall be subject to administrative, civil, or

criminal liability under applicable laws.

The following acts shall constitute violations of this Act:
(a) Failure to establish or maintain a functional Barangay VAWC Desk;

(b) Failure to designate qualified Barangay VAWC Desk Officers;

(c) Misuse, diversion, or non-allocation of funds intended for the implementation of

this Act;

===== [page 8] =====
(d) Breach of confidentiality and unauthorized disclosure of information related to
victim-survivors; and
(e) Neglect, refusal, or unreasonable delay in acting upon VAWC complaints or

requests for assistance.

ARTICLE VII. FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local Government, in coordination
with the Philippine Commission on Women, the Department of Social Welfare and
Development, the Department of Health, the Philippine National Police, and other concerned
agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective

implementation of this Act.

SEC. 23. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and
effect.

SEC. 24. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, issuances, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 25. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
