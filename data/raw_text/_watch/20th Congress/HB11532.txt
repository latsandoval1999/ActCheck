EXPLANATORY NOTE

Republic Act No. 9514, also known as the Fire Code of the Philippines
of 2008, stands as a cornerstone of the nation's commitment to public safety,
economic development, and the professionalization of the fire service. Since
its enactment, the Code has provided a comprehensive framework for fire
prevention, suppression, and the enforcement of safety standards across the
country. However, with the rapid pace of urbanization, came the emergence
of new economic and industrial zones, technological advancements, and the
increasing complexity of fire hazards that have exposed regulatory gaps and
operational challenges within the jurisdiction of the Bureau of Fire Protection
(BFP) that must be inevitably addressed.

This proposed measure seeks to update and strengthen the Fire Code
to ensure its continued relevance and effectiveness in safeguarding lives,
property, and critical infrastructure. The bill expands the applicability of the
law to encompass all persons, buildings, facilities, and structures-including
those situated within economic zones, information technology parks, tourism
zones, and other special entities, as well as vehicles, ships, petroleum
installations, airports, and other high-risk areas. By closing existing
regulatory gaps, the measure ensures that all sectors, particularly those with
heightened fire risks, are subject to uniform and robust fire safety standards.

Recognizing the need for adequate fiscal resources to fulfill its
mandate, the bill likewise modernizes the funding mechanisms of the BFP by
introducing new sources of income, including fees for the issuance of
clearances assessed based on floor area and hazard level. These mechanisms
are intended to support the continuous upgrading of the BFP's manpower,

===== [page 2] =====
infrastructure, and equipment, enabling it to respond effectively to the
evolving landscape of fire risks that BFP are currently facing.

Given the wider scope that this measure seeks to achieve, this measure
will invariably address the concerns on regulatory gaps in jurisdiction as well
as to give the BFP sufficient autonomy to carry out its mandate
independently and effectively.

AN ACT
AMENDING SECTIONS 4 AND 12 OF THE REPUBLIC ACT NO.
9514, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "FIRE CODE OF THE
PHILIPPINES
OF 2008”

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Section 4 of Republic Act No. 9514 is hereby
amended to read as follows:

"SECTION 4. Applicability of the Code. The provisions
of the Fire Code shall apply to all persons and all private and
public buildings, facilities or structures erected or constructed
before and after its effectivity including those that are classified
as economic zone, agro-industrial economic, free ports and
special economic zone, Information Technology (IT) parks and
centers, tourism economic zones, and other similar entities.

SECTION 2. Section 12 of Republic Act No. 9514 is hereby
amended to read as follows:

"SEC. 12. Appropriation and Sources of Income. -

a. To support the manpower, infrastructure and equipment
needs of the fire service of the BFP, such amount as may be
necessary to attain the objectives of the Fire Code shall be
appropriated and included in the annual appropriation of the

===== [page 4] =====
BFP.

b. To partially provide for the funding of the fire service, the
following taxes and fees which shall accrue to the general fund of
the National Government, are hereby imposed:

(1) Fees to be charged. for the issuance of certificates, permits
and licenses as provided for in Section 7(a) hereof;

(2) One-tenth of one per centum (0.1%) of the verified
estimated value of buildings or structures to be erected, from the
owner thereof, one half to be paid prior to the issuance of the
building permit, and the balance, after final inspection and prior
to the issuance of the occupancy permit;

(3) One-hundredth of one per centum (0.10%) of the assessed
- value of buildings or structures annually payable upon payment
of the real estate tax, except on structures used as single family
dwellings;

(4) Two per centum (2%) of all premiums, excluding
reinsurance premiums for the sale of fire, earthquake and
explosion hazard insurance collected by companies, persons or
agents licensed to sell such insurances in the Philippines:

(5) | Two per centum (2%) of gross sales of companies, persons
or agents selling firefighting equipment, appliances or devices,
including hazard detection and warning systems; and

(6) Two per centum (2%) of the service fees received from fire,
earthquake, and explosion hazard reinsurance surveys and post
loss service of insurance adjustment companies doing business in
the Philippines directly through agents.

SECTION 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty
(60) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of the Interior and Local
Government shall issue the rules and regulations for its effective
implementation.

===== [page 5] =====
SECTION 4. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is
declared constitutional or invalid, the other provision not affected thereby
shail remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 5. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and
regulation, or parts therefor, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 6. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen

(15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of
general circulation.
