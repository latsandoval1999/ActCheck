                              AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY, PROVIDING FOR ITS
ADMINISTRATION, PRESCRIBING REGISTRATION DUTIES AND PENALTIES, AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Sexual offenses cause deep and lasting harm to victims and threaten public safety,
especially for women and children. While offenders can be punished after conviction,
the government must also take stronger steps to prevent repeat offenses and improve
coordination among law enforcement agencies. One important tool is a reliable
system that helps authorities monitor convicted offenders and share information
when necessary to protect the public, while still respecting constitutional rights and
privacy laws.

Current law already recognizes the need for such a system in cases involving child
sexual exploitation. Republic Act No. 11930 created a registry for individuals
convicted of online sexual abuse or exploitation of children and related offenses. The
law requires authorities to maintain and update records, coordinate with law
enforcement agencies, and share information when necessary to prevent danger to
the public, while following the safeguards of the Data Privacy Act. However, the
existing registry focuses mainly on offenses involving children, leaving gaps when it
comes to other serious sexual offenses and the need for consistent nationwide
monitoring.

This bill addresses that gap by establishing a National Sex Offender Registry under
the Department of Justice. The proposed system will help law enforcement agencies

across the country identify and monitor convicted offenders, share information
responsibly, and provide limited public access that promotes safety without exposing
victims or encouraging misuse. By strengthening coordination while protecting
privacy and the dignity of victims, this measure aims to improve public safety and
prevent further harm.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Sex Offender Registry Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect the public,
especially women and children, from sexual abuse and exploitation, and to strengthen
law enforcement’s capacity to prevent, investigate, and prosecute sexual offenses,
while respecting the constitutional rights to due process and privacy and the
con identiality of victims.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) “Registry” or “National Sex Offender Registry (NSOR)” refers to a
       centralized, secure national database of Registrable Sex Offenders maintained
       for law enforcement coordination, risk management, and limited public
       information, as provided in this Act.

   (b) “Registrable Sex Offender” refers to any person who, by inal judgment of a
       Philippine court, is convicted of any of the following:


                                                                            3|Page
      a. Rape and related offenses under the Revised Penal Code, as amended;
      b. Acts of lasciviousness and other felony offenses against chastity under the
         Revised Penal Code, as amended;
      c. Sexual violence under Republic Act No. 9262 (Anti-VAWC Act), including
         Section 5(g), as amended;
      d. Such other offenses as may be expressly included by law where the
         gravamen involves sexual abuse, sexual exploitation, or sexual violence.

          For purposes of this Act, “Registrable Sex Offender” includes a person
          convicted by inal judgment in a foreign jurisdiction of an offense
          substantially similar to those enumerated above, subject to veri ication and
          the procedures in Section 8.

   (c) “Law Enforcement Agency (LEA)” refers to agencies authorized by law to
       prevent, investigate, or prosecute crimes, including the PNP, NBI, and other
       agencies as may be designated in the IRR.

   (d) “Public Website” refers to the limited-access online portal for public
       information under Section 11 of this Act.

SECTION 4. Establishment of the National Sex Offender Registry (NSOR). Lead
and Support Agencies. — The NSOR is hereby established.

The Department of Justice (DOJ) shall be the lead implementing agency and system
owner. The DOJ shall coordinate with the Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), the Philippine National Police (PNP), the National Bureau of
Investigation (NBI), the Department of Information and Communications Technology
(DICT), and local government units (LGUs) for the effective implementation of this
Act.

SECTION 5. Coordination With Existing Registries; Harmonization. — The NSOR
shall be harmonized with existing offender registries created by law, including the
OSAEC and CSAEM offenders registry under Republic Act No. 11930.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to repeal, diminish, or amend the duties,
powers, and restrictions on disclosure provided under Republic Act No. 11930 and its
implementing rules.



                                                                          4|Page
SECTION 6. Contents of the Registry; Required Information. — The NSOR shall
contain, at a minimum, the following information for each Registrable Sex Offender:

   (a) Full name and known aliases;
   (b) Date and place of birth;
   (c) Recent photograph;
   (d) Current and permanent residential addresses and other contact information,
       as may be provided in the IRR;
   (e) Employment and school information, if applicable;
   (f) Fingerprints and other biometric identifiers as may be authorized by law and
       the IRR;
   (g) Complete criminal history relevant to registrable offenses, including the court,
       docket number, date of conviction, and penalty imposed; and
   (h) Such other information as may be necessary for accurate identification and
       risk management, subject to the Data Privacy Act and this Act.

SECTION 7. Duty to Register; When and Where to Register. — A Registrable Sex
Offender shall register as follows:

   (a) If sentenced to imprisonment: prior to release from con inement, the offender
       shall accomplish initial registration through the facility and the appropriate
       LEA, in coordination with the DOJ.

   (b) If sentenced to a non-custodial penalty or otherwise not con ined: the offender
       shall register within ifteen (15) days from inality of judgment.

   (c) The offender shall register in the city or municipality of residence. The
       offender shall likewise report any place of work or school, if applicable, in the
       manner provided by the IRR.

SECTION 8. Foreign Convictions; Veri ication and Due Process. — A person
convicted by inal judgment in a foreign jurisdiction of an offense substantially similar
to a registrable offense shall be included in the NSOR only upon:

   (a) submission or acquisition of authenticated or otherwise veri iable records of
       conviction; and

   (b) a determination by the DOJ, after notice and opportunity to be heard, that the
       foreign offense is substantially similar to a registrable offense and that the
       identity of the offender is established.

                                                                            5|Page
       The IRR shall provide the procedure, timelines, and remedies, including
       administrative appeal within the DOJ, without prejudice to judicial remedies
       under existing laws.

SECTION 9. Updating; Change of Address/Status; Periodic In-Person
Veri ication. — Registrable Sex Offenders shall:

   (a) update any change in residence, employment, or school within ten (10) days
       from such change; and

   (b) appear in person at least once every year for veri ication of identity and
       updating of records, in the manner provided by the IRR.

SECTION 10. Duration of Registration. — Unless otherwise provided by subsequent
law, registration shall be for life.

Provided, That the IRR shall prescribe reasonable classi ication and reporting
frequencies based on objective factors, consistent with this Act, without shortening
the lifetime inclusion mandated herein.

SECTION 11. Public Website; Scope of Publicly Disclosable Information. — The
DOJ shall maintain a Public Website that may disclose only the following information:

   (a) Name of the offender;
   (b) Recent photograph;
   (c) City/municipality of residence (not exact address);
   (d) The registrable offense/s of conviction and date of conviction; and
   (e) Such other limited information as may be authorized by the IRR, consistent
       with public safety and the Data Privacy Act.

       The Public Website shall not disclose the identity of victims, exact residential
       addresses, personal contact details, or any information that would endanger
       victims, witnesses, or the offender’s minor children or household members.

       LGUs shall not establish separate public registries inconsistent with this Act.
       LGUs may, however, maintain local access portals integrated with the NSOR for
       LEA use, subject to DOJ standards.



                                                                           6|Page
SECTION 12. Access for Law Enforcement; Data Sharing; Security. — Full access
to the NSOR shall be limited to authorized LEAs and other authorized government
agencies as may be provided by the IRR, subject to strict authentication, logging, and
data security standards.

Information sharing with foreign jurisdictions shall be allowed only through of icial
channels and subject to veri ication and safeguards under the IRR and applicable
laws.

SECTION 13. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. —

   (a) Failure to Register or Update. — Any Registrable Sex Offender who knowingly
       and willfully fails to register, verify, or update required information under
       Sections 7 to 9 shall suffer the penalty of imprisonment of one (1) year to ive
       (5) years and a ine of Ten thousand pesos (Php10,000.00) to Fifty thousand
       pesos (Php50,000.00), at the discretion of the court.

   (b) Misuse or Unauthorized Disclosure. — Any person who, without authority,
       accesses, discloses, leaks, sells, or uses NSOR information beyond the purposes
       authorized by this Act and the IRR shall suffer the penalty of imprisonment of
       two (2) years to six (6) years and a ine of One hundred thousand pesos
       (Php100,000.00) to Five hundred thousand pesos (Php500,000.00), without
       prejudice to liability under other laws.

   (c) False Information. — Any Registrable Sex Offender who knowingly provides
       false material information to the NSOR shall suffer the penalty of
       imprisonment of one (1) year to six (6) years and a ine of Fifty thousand pesos
       (Php50,000.00) to Two hundred thousand pesos (Php200,000.00).

SECTION 14. Public Information and Education. — The DOJ, in coordination with
the DILG, PNP, DICT, DepEd, and LGUs, shall conduct continuing public information
and education campaigns on the existence, purpose, and proper use of the Public
Website, including warnings against harassment, vigilantism, and misuse of registry
information.

SECTION 15. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
DOJ. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days

SECTION 17. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or

SECTION 18. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with

SECTION 19. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
