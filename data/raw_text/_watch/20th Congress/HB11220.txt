                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Quezon City has already earned international recognition as the heart of
Philippine cinema and television, and it is time national law caught up with what
the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO),
the industry, and generations of Filipino filmmakers have long known to be true.

       This bill declares Quezon City as the “Film and Television Arts Capital of the
Philippines” in recognition of its status as home to the country’s largest film and
television production companies and institutions. It directs the Department of
Tourism (DOT), in consultation with the Quezon City government, to incorporate
this official designation into its national tourism programs, and further directs the
DOT to develop, together with the Quezon City government and local film and
television institutions, tourism events and destinations that showcase the country’s
film and television history and heritage.

       Quezon City is already the undisputed center of Philippine broadcast and
film production. It hosts an estimated sixty to sixty-five percent (60-65%) of the
country’s television transmission facilities and forty percent (40%) of its
post-production capacity, anchored by the headquarters of GMA Network in
Diliman, the ABS-CBN Complex nearby, and the Araneta City studios in Cubao that
host forty-five percent (45%) of Metro Manila’s major concert and live event
productions.1 This industry weight has already drawn international recognition: in
October 2025, Quezon City became one of the first cities in Southeast Asia to be
designated a UNESCO Creative City of Film, a title tracing back through the golden
era of Sampaguita Pictures and the legacies of Lino Brocka, Dolphy, Nora Aunor,
and Fernando Poe Jr.2

       The Quezon City government has already moved to institutionalize this
identity at the local level. In May 2025, the city established the Quezon City Screen
Commission through a City Council ordinance, tasked with harmonizing
film-related policies, streamlining permits through a dedicated Film Permits Office,
and coordinating training and industry programs down to the barangay level.3
Building on its UNESCO designation, the Commission is now developing the QC
Screen Academy, the Global Producers Exchange, and the Southeast Asia Film
Summit to further cement the city’s role as a regional hub for film production and
worker welfare. What remains missing is the corresponding recognition and support
at the national level: without a law declaring Quezon City’s status and directing
national tourism agencies to build around it, these local gains remain vulnerable to
being under-promoted nationally and unsupported by the country’s tourism
infrastructure.

       The scale of what is at stake is considerable. Quezon City’s media and
creative production sector is estimated at forty-two and a half (42.5) billion and
growing at six point two percent (6.2%) annually, generating thousands of jobs in
production, post-production, and animation, with the city’s universities producing
roughly fifteen thousand (15,000) communication and film graduates every year.4 A
formal national declaration, implemented with a DOT-led tourism program built
around the city’s film and television heritage, would not only affirm an identity
Quezon City has already earned locally and internationally, but would also open
new avenues for tourism revenue, structured industry promotion, and pride of place
for the workers, artists, and institutions that have sustained the country’s film and
television industry for generations.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
DECLARING QUEZON CITY AS THE “FILM AND TELEVISION ARTS
CAPITAL OF THE PHILIPPINES”

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Quezon City Film and
Televisions Arts Capital Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
conserve, promote, and develop the country’s historical, cultural, and artistic
heritage, including its film and television arts and audiovisual heritage. Towards
this end, the State shall recognize and promote Quezon City as a principal center of
Philippine film and television arts, culture, heritage, education, tourism, and
creative enterprise. The State shall encourage the development of film and television
tourism, the preservation and appreciation of significant audiovisual heritage, the
promotion of Filipino creative talent, and the establishment of programs that
strengthen the country’s creative economy and cultural identity.

       SEC. 3. Film and Television Arts Capital of the Philippines. – Quezon City is
hereby declared as the “Film and Television Arts Capital of the Philippines”, in
recognition of its significant historical and continuing contribution to the
development of Philippine film and television arts, its concentration of film and
television institutions and creative professionals, and its role as a center for
audiovisual production, education, exhibition, and cultural activity.

      The declaration shall be understood in furtherance of the City’s existing
programs and initiatives for the development of its screen industry and shall
complement, and not diminish, the role of other cities and localities in the
development of Philippine film and television arts.

      SEC. 4. Incorporation of the Official Designation in Tourism Programs. – The
Department of Tourism (DOT), in consultation with the Quezon City government,

shall incorporate in its program the official designation and promotion of Quezon
City as the Film and Television Arts Capital of the Philippines.

       SEC. 5. Development of Film and Television Industry Tourism Promotion Programs
in Quezon City. – The DOT shall, in cooperation with the Quezon City government
and the film and television institutions in Quezon City, develop tourism events and
destinations that would highlight our country's film and television history and
heritage.

        SEC. 6. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 8. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
