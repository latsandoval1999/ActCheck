                             AN ACT
AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7309, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS “AN ACT
CREATING A BOARD OF CLAIMS UNDER THE DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE FOR
VICTIMS OF UNJUST IMPRISONMENT OR DETENTION AND VICTIMS OF VIOLENT
CRIMES,” BY PROVIDING ENHANCED COMPENSATION FOR PERSONS
WRONGFULLY CONVICTED AND IMPRISONED, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The State recognizes that no person should suffer imprisonment for a crime they did
not commit. The Republic Act No. 7309 created the Department of Justice Board of
Claims to provide compensation to victims of unjust imprisonment and violent crimes
through a simple and affordable administrative process. However, recent rulings of
the Supreme Court of the Philippines, including Mohammad v. Of ice of the
Secretary, clari ied that the law must be applied strictly according to its wording.
Because of this, some individuals who were wrongfully convicted and imprisoned but
later cleared by inal court action may not clearly fall within the law’s existing
coverage.

This bill seeks to address that gap by expressly recognizing a new category of
claimants: persons who were wrongfully convicted and imprisoned and were later
fully cleared or exonerated by inal judicial action. It also establishes clear safeguards
to ensure that compensation is granted only to those who can show, through strong
evidence, that they did not commit the crime and did not cause their own wrongful
conviction. In addition, the measure updates the compensation amounts under the
law, which have remained largely unchanged since 1992 and no longer re lect the

serious personal, economic, and social harm caused by years of wrongful
imprisonment.

Finally, the bill improves access to justice by giving quali ied claimants a clearer and
fairer period for iling claims and by ensuring that compensation corresponds only to
the period of imprisonment caused by the wrongful conviction. By strengthening the
existing system under Republic Act No. 7309 while keeping the administrative
process simple and accessible, the proposed “Wrongful Conviction Compensation
Act” af irms the State’s commitment to fairness, accountability, and justice for those
who have suffered the grave injustice of wrongful imprisonment.

                             AN ACT
AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7309, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS “AN ACT
CREATING A BOARD OF CLAIMS UNDER THE DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE FOR
VICTIMS OF UNJUST IMPRISONMENT OR DETENTION AND VICTIMS OF VIOLENT

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Wrongful Conviction
Compensation Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to provide a swift,
fair, and accessible compensation mechanism for persons who were wrongfully
convicted and imprisoned, in recognition of the severe and lifelong harm caused by
the loss of liberty, stigma, and economic displacement, without prejudice to their right
to pursue other remedies under existing laws.

SECTION 3. Amendment to Section 3 of Republic Act No. 7309 (Who may File
Claims). — Section 3 of Republic Act No. 7309 is hereby amended to include an
additional paragraph to read as follows:


                                                                            3|Page
     “(e) Any person who was convicted by inal judgment and imprisoned, and
     who was subsequently released or exonerated by virtue of a judgment of
     acquittal on appeal, or by a inal judgment declaring that the conviction is
     void or erroneous, or by other inal judicial action establishing that the
     claimant did not commit the crime for which he or she was convicted.”

For purposes of this paragraph, the claimant must establish by clear and convincing
evidence that:

   (1) the claimant was convicted and served all or part of a sentence of
       imprisonment;

   (2) the claimant did not commit the crime for which he or she was convicted; and

   (3) the claimant did not, by his or her own voluntary conduct, knowingly and
       intentionally cause or bring about the conviction.

SECTION 4. Amendment to Section 4 of Republic Act No. 7309 (Award Ceiling)
for Wrongful Conviction Claims. — A new subsection is inserted in Section 4 to read
as follows:

     “For claims under Section 3(e), the Board may grant compensation not
     exceeding either:

     (a) One hundred thousand pesos (₱100,000.00) for each year of
         imprisonment, and a proportionate amount for a fraction thereof; or

     (b) Twice the claimant’s annual gross income in the year immediately
         preceding incarceration, whichever is higher: Provided, That the Board
         may also award reimbursement for reasonable and necessary expenses
         directly related to the injury, including medical or psychological treatment,
         and documented loss of wage or loss of support, subject to documentary
         requirements.”**

SECTION 5. Filing Period (Amending Section 5 of RA 7309). — Section 5 of RA
7309 is amended to add:

     “For claims under Section 3(e), the claim shall be iled within two (2) years
     from the date of release from imprisonment, or from inality of the


                                                                          4|Page
     judgment or inal judicial action establishing exoneration, whichever is
     later.”

     This clari ies accrual and prevents forfeiture where release precedes inal
     exoneration.

SECTION 6. Exclusions. — A new section is inserted providing that:

   (a) A claimant shall not be eligible under Section 3(e) if, during the period of
       imprisonment for the mistaken conviction, the claimant was also serving a
       sentence for another conviction that is not reversed or set aside, and the
       imprisonment periods were fully concurrent;

   (b) Partial concurrency shall not bar compensation, but the Board shall compute
       compensation only for the portion of imprisonment attributable to the
       reversed conviction.

SECTION 7. Procedure; Nature of Proceedings. — Claims under this Act shall be
 iled with the DOJ Board of Claims, which shall conduct an independent
administrative hearing and resolve claims under an expeditious and inexpensive
procedure, consistent with RA 7309.

SECTION 8. Funding; Victim Compensation Fund. — The amounts necessary to
implement the enhanced compensation for wrongful conviction claims shall be
sourced from the Victim Compensation Fund and other funding mechanisms
provided under RA 7309, and such additional appropriations as may be provided in
the General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from
effectivity, the DOJ, through the Board of Claims, shall promulgate the necessary rules
and regulations to implement this Act.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed or modi ied accordingly.
