                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The National Anthem is more than a song performed at the opening of government
programs, school ceremonies, and other public gatherings. It is one of the symbols through
which the Filipino people express their shared history, identity, and continuing
commitment to the nation. For this reason, its words and manner of rendition are matters
properly governed by law.

       The 1987 Constitution recognizes the authority of Congress to adopt, by law, a
national anthem that reflects and symbolizes the ideals, history, and traditions of the
Filipino people. Section 2, Article XVI, of the 1987 Constitution provides that:“The
Congress may, by law, adopt a new name for the country, a national anthem, or a national
seal, which shall all be truly reflective and symbolic of the ideals, history, and traditions of
the people. Such law shall take effect only upon its ratification by the people in a national
referendum.”

        Pursuant to this constitutional authority, Congress enacted Republic Act No. 8491,
otherwise known as the “Flag and Heraldic Code of the Philippines.” The law in its Section
2 declares that reverence and respect shall at all times be accorded to the national flag, the
anthem, and other national symbols: “Reverence and respect shall at all times be accorded
the flag, the anthem, and other national symbols which embody the national ideals and
traditions and which express the principles of sovereignty and national solidarity. [x x x]”

        Section 36 of Republic Act No. 8491 provides: “The National Anthem shall always
be sung in the national language within or without the country. The following shall be the
lyrics of the National Anthem:

       Section 36 of Republic Act No. 8491 has served as the statutory reference for the
Filipino lyrics of Lupang Hinirang for more than two decades. Its importance extends
beyond the wording printed in the law.

       The present measure seeks to make a limited amendment to Section 36 by adopting
the forms “’Di” and “’Pag” in the corresponding portions of the lyrics to make it more
accurate since “’Di” came from the root word “Hindi” and “’Pag” came from the root word
“Kapag.” The proposed amendment does not introduce a new national anthem, change its
melody, or alter its message. It likewise leaves untouched the provisions of Republic Act
No. 8491 governing the manner in which the National Anthem is rendered and the respect
accorded to it during public gatherings.

        The measure is intended to provide a clear and uniform statutory text for the words
that Filipinos are required to sing as the National Anthem. The proposed amendment aims
at precision in the statutory text while preserving the National Anthem that generations of
Filipinos have known. It does not diminish the historical character of Lupang Hinirang.
Rather, it affirms that its words, as prescribed by law, should be stated with the same care
and consistency expected of any other national symbol established by statute.
       At a time when national identity is expressed not only within our borders but also
by Filipinos living, studying, working, and representing the country abroad, maintaining a
clear and uniform statutory formulation of the National Anthem is a modest but meaningful
exercise of legislative responsibility.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

        SEC. 2. Separability Clause. - If, for any reason, any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or contrary to law, the other parts or provisions hereof which are not affected
thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

        SEC. 3. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, rules,
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

        SEC. 4. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in any newspaper of general circulation.
