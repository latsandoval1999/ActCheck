16                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE
17

18   The rapid growth of digital communication has significantly transformed the way
19   Filipinos engage in public discourse, exchange information, and interact with one
20   another. Social media platforms have become powerful tools for communication,
21   commerce, and civic participation. However, these same platforms have increasingly
22   been misused to commit acts that violate the rights of others, including cyberbullying,
23   harassment, impersonation, identity theft, fraud, and the dissemination of defamatory
24   or malicious content.

25   The ease with which individuals can create multiple or anonymous online identities has
26   contributed to the proliferation of such unlawful activities. Perpetrators frequently hide
27   behind fictitious or unverified accounts to evade accountability for acts that cause
28   serious harm to individuals, institutions, and the public at large.

29   Data from the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT)
30   underscores the magnitude of cyber-related offenses in the country. According to the
31   “Briefer on Monitoring of Cyber-Related Crimes 2023–2024,” the Philippines recorded
32   87,595 cybercrime incidents from March 2013 to October 2025, reflecting the growing
33   scale of cyber-enabled criminal activities nationwide. The same report identifies online
34   libel, illegal access, computer-related identity theft, computer-related fraud, data
35   interference, and system interference among the most frequently reported cyber
36   offenses, many of which are facilitated through the misuse of social media platforms and
37   anonymous digital identities.

38   DICT monitoring further reveals the increasing vulnerability of digital platforms to
39   abuse and cyber threats. In 2023 alone, 1,834 cyber incidents were recorded,
40   representing a 62.44 percent increase from the 1,129 incidents reported in 2022,
41   highlighting the rapid escalation of cyber threats affecting individuals, organizations,
42   and government institutions.
43   Recognizing the increasing risks associated with anonymous digital communication, the
44   Philippine government has already taken steps to strengthen accountability in online
45   and telecommunications systems. Through Republic Act No. 11934, Congress required
46   all mobile subscribers to register their SIM cards using verified personal identification
47   in order to prevent scams, fraud, and other crimes facilitated through anonymous
48   communication channels.

49   The policy rationale behind the SIM Card Registration Act demonstrates the State’s
50   commitment to ensuring that digital communication tools are not misused for criminal
51   activities while still protecting legitimate use and personal privacy. Just as anonymous
52   SIM cards had been exploited for scams, phishing schemes, and harassment,
53   anonymous social media accounts have likewise become instruments for committing
54   cybercrimes, spreading misinformation, and violating the rights of others.

55   Given that mobile numbers are now registered identities under the SIM Card
56   Registration Act, social media platforms—which often rely on mobile numbers for
57   account creation and verification—should likewise adopt stronger identity
58   authentication mechanisms to ensure accountability in digital spaces.

59   At the same time, many countries have begun implementing identity verification
60   requirements for social media platforms. Jurisdictions such as China, Vietnam,
61   Malaysia, and South Korea have adopted systems requiring identity authentication for
62   users of major online platforms in order to deter cybercrime, protect minors, and
63   improve accountability for digital activities. Other countries, including Thailand,
64   Australia, and the United Kingdom, are also implementing or studying similar identity
65   verification measures in response to increasing online harms.

66   In the Philippines, the government has already established a secure national
67   identification framework through the Philippine Identification System (PhilSys)
68   pursuant to Republic Act No. 11055. PhilSys provides a reliable and efficient mechanism
69   for verifying the identity of individuals while maintaining safeguards for personal data
70   and privacy.

71   This proposed measure seeks to build upon these existing policies by requiring social
72   media accounts to undergo identity verification through PhilSys authentication. While
73   individuals may maintain multiple social media accounts, each account must be linked
74   to a verified identity in order to discourage the proliferation of anonymous or fraudulent
75   accounts frequently used to commit cybercrimes and violate the rights of others.

76   This measure does not seek to curtail freedom of expression. Rather, it ensures that the
77   exercise of such freedom in digital spaces is accompanied by responsibility and
78   accountability. Moreover, the bill incorporates safeguards to ensure that personal
79   information collected for identity verification purposes is protected in accordance with
80   Republic Act No. 10173.

81   By strengthening identity verification mechanisms in social media platforms and
82   aligning with the policy framework established under the SIM Card Registration Act,
83   this bill aims to foster a safer and more responsible digital environment while protecting
84   citizens from cyber-enabled crimes and abuses.
85    In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

141      AN ACT REQUIRING SOCIAL MEDIA ACCOUNTS TO BE VERIFIED
142     THROUGH THE PHILIPPINE IDENTIFICATION SYSTEM (PHILSYS),
143   PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

144   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
145   assembled:

146   SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Verified Social Media
147   Account Act.”

148   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
149   promote accountability, transparency, and responsibility in digital communication
150   platforms. The State recognizes the growing influence of social media on public
151   discourse, public safety, and national security. To prevent online fraud, impersonation,
152   cybercrime, and misinformation, the State shall ensure that social media accounts are
153   linked to verified identities through the national identification system while protecting
154   personal data and privacy.

155   SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act:

156      a. Social Media Platform – Any internet-based platform that allows users to
157         create profiles, publish content, communicate, and interact with other users.
158      b. Verified Account – A social media account that has undergone identity
159         verification through the national identification system.
160      c. User – Any natural person who creates or maintains a social media account.
161      d. PhilSys Identification – The unique identification credentials issued under
162         the national identification system administered by the Philippine Statistics
163         Authority.

164   SECTION 4. Mandatory Social Media Identity Verification. All social media
165   accounts operating within the Philippines shall undergo identity verification through the
166   Philippine Identification System (PhilSys). Individuals may maintain multiple social
167   media accounts; however, each account must be linked to a verified identity through the
168   PhilSys authentication system.

169   SECTION 5. PhilSys-Based Verification Requirements. Social media platforms
170   operating in the Philippines shall conform to the following:

171      a. Require users to verify their identity through the Philippine Identification System
172         (PhilSys);
173      b. Verification shall include authentication using the PhilSys Number (PSN) or
174         other authorized PhilSys credentials; and
175      c. Ensure that no social media account is activated unless the user’s identity has
176         been authenticated through the PhilSys system.

177   SECTION 6. Duties of Social Media Platforms. Social media platforms operating
178   in the Philippines shall:

179      a. Implement secure systems to authenticate users through the PhilSys verification
180         system;
181      b. Prevent and detect fraudulent or impersonation accounts;
182      c. Ensure secure handling and encryption of identity verification data; and
183      d. Comply with lawful requests from authorized government agencies as provided
184         by law.

185   SECTION 7. Implementing Agencies. The implementation of this Act shall be
186   jointly overseen by:

187      a. the Department of Information and Communications Technology;
188      b. the National Privacy Commission; and
189      c. the Philippine Statistics Authority.

190   These agencies shall coordinate to establish secure systems enabling social media
191   platforms to verify users through PhilSys.

192   SECTION 8. Penalties.

193      a. For Individuals: Any person who intentionally uses false identities, falsified
194         credentials, or attempts to bypass PhilSys verification shall be subject to:
195            i.  A fine of ₱10,000 to ₱100,000;
196           ii.  Suspension or termination of the account; or
197          iii.  Both, at the discretion of the appropriate authority.
198      b. For Social Media Platforms: Social media platforms that fail to implement
199         the required PhilSys verification mechanisms shall be subject to administrative
200         fines and possible restrictions on operations within the Philippines.

201   SECTION 9. Data Privacy Protection. All personal information collected under this
202   Act shall be protected in accordance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012. Such
203   information shall only be used for identity verification purposes and shall not be
204   disclosed without lawful authority.
205   SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days
206   from the effectivity of this Act, the implementing agencies shall promulgate the
207   necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

208   SECTION 11. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
209   unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

210   SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
211   regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

212   SECTION 13. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
213   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
