                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The practice of law in the Philippines is reserved only for licensed lawyers because it
directly affects people’s rights, property, and freedom. This rule exists to protect the
public from unquali ied or dishonest individuals who may give wrong advice or
mishandle legal matters. The Supreme Court has emphasized that only those who are
properly trained, tested, and regulated should be allowed to provide legal services.

However, many people are still being misled by individuals or groups who pretend to
be lawyers. Some offer legal advice, prepare documents, or even handle cases,
sometimes through social media or online platforms, despite having no authority to
do so. This can result in wasted money, invalid ilings, missed deadlines, and serious
harm to a person’s legal rights. It also creates problems for courts and government
agencies that must deal with improper or unauthorized submissions.

This bill seeks to stop these harmful practices by clearly de ining and penalizing the
unauthorized practice of law. It covers acts such as pretending to be a lawyer, giving
legal advice without a license, preparing or iling legal documents for others, and
appearing in legal proceedings without authority. It also punishes those who use fake
lawyer credentials or knowingly support and pro it from these illegal activities, while
still allowing properly supervised law students to assist under existing rules.

By setting clear rules and stronger penalties, this measure aims to protect the public
from fraud, ensure that only quali ied professionals provide legal services, and uphold

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Anti-Unauthorized
Practice of Law Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect the public,
the courts, and the integrity of the legal profession by penalizing the unauthorized
practice of law, the holding out as a lawyer, and related fraudulent acts, consistent
with the Supreme Court’s constitutional authority to regulate admission to the
practice of law and the ethical conduct of those authorized to practice.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) “Member of the Philippine Bar” refers to a person admitted by the Supreme
       Court to the practice of law and whose name appears in the Roll of Attorneys.

   (b) “Good standing” refers to eligibility to practice law as a member of the
       Philippine Bar under the Code of Professional Responsibility and
       Accountability and applicable Supreme Court rules.




                                                                            3|Page
   (c) “Practice of law” includes, when done for or on behalf of another and for
       compensation or otherwise, any act requiring the application of legal
       knowledge, training, or skill, such as:

      (1) giving legal advice or legal opinion;
      (2) preparing, drafting, completing, or causing the preparation of pleadings,
          motions, petitions, af idavits, complaints, answers, briefs, contracts, or
          other legal instruments intended to secure legal rights or remedies;
      (3) representing, appearing for, or negotiating on behalf of another before any
          court, tribunal, quasi-judicial body, or government agency in a manner that
          affects the legal rights or obligations of such person; and
      (4) holding oneself out to the public as authorized to practice law.

   (d) “Holding out as a lawyer” means representing oneself, directly or indirectly,
       as a member of the Philippine Bar or as authorized to perform acts constituting
       the practice of law, including the use of the titles “Attorney,” “Attorney-at-law,”
       “Atty.,” “Abogado/Abogada,” or their equivalents, in any medium, where such
       representation is false.

SECTION 4. Acts Constituting Unauthorized Practice of Law. — The following acts,
when committed by a person who is not a member of the Philippine Bar in good
standing, constitute unauthorized practice of law:

   (a) Holding out as a lawyer;

   (b) Appearing as counsel or representative, or entering an appearance, before any
       court, tribunal, quasi-judicial body, or government agency, except when
       expressly allowed by law or Supreme Court rules;

   (c) Signing, iling, or submitting any pleading, motion, petition, entry of
       appearance, manifestation, position paper, or similar paper on behalf of
       another in any court, tribunal, quasi-judicial body, or government agency,
       except when expressly allowed by law or Supreme Court rules;

   (d) For compensation or otherwise, giving legal advice or legal opinion to another,
       whether in person or through any platform including social media, websites,
       messaging applications, or other electronic means;

   (e) Advertising, soliciting, or offering legal services to the public despite not being
       a member of the Philippine Bar in good standing, including claims that one can
       “facilitate” or “process” court petitions or remedies, such as annulment, nullity
                                                                              4|Page
         of marriage, or declaration of presumptive death, when such claims amount to
         the practice of law or holding out as a lawyer.

SECTION 5. Prima Facie Evidence; Fake or Tampered Identi ication. —
Possession, use, or presentation of a fake, counterfeit, altered, or tampered
identi ication card or document purporting to be issued by the Supreme Court or the
Integrated Bar of the Philippines for members of the Philippine Bar shall be prima
facie evidence of holding out as a lawyer and unauthorized practice of law.

SECTION 6. Aiding or Abetting Unauthorized Practice of Law. — Any person who
knowingly aids, abets, employs, engages, or pro its from the unauthorized practice of
law, including by permitting the use of the person’s name, of ice, letterhead, or law
of ice to enable a non-lawyer to perform acts constituting the practice of law, shall be
liable under this Act, without prejudice to administrative, civil, or other criminal
liability. If the offender is a lawyer, such act shall be without prejudice to disciplinary
action under applicable Supreme Court rules.

SECTION 7. Penalties. —

   (a)     For violations of Section 4(a) and Section 4(e) (holding
           out/advertising/solicitation): prision correccional in its medium to
           maximum period, or a ine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos
           (Php100,000.00) but not more than One Million Pesos (Php1,000,000.00),
           or both.

   (b)     For violations of Section 4(b), 4(c), or 4(d) (appearance, signing/ iling
           pleadings, legal advice/opinion): prision mayor in its minimum to medium
           period, or a ine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos
           (Php100,000.00) but not more than Five Million Pesos (Php5,000,000.00),
           or both.

   (c)     Aggravated circumstances. — The maximum penalty under the applicable
           subsection shall be imposed when any of the following is present:

           (1) use of fake or tampered identi ication under Section 5;
           (2) the offender is a repeat offender;
           (3) the act is committed through fraud, deceit, or as part of a syndicate;
           (4) the victim is a minor, senior citizen, or a person under disability; or
           (5) the act results in substantial prejudice, including loss of money or
               dismissal/impairment of legal rights.

SECTION 8. Unauthorized Notarial Acts by Non-Lawyers. — Any person who, not
being a lawyer in good standing and not having a valid notarial commission granted
in accordance with the Rules on Notarial Practice promulgated by the Supreme Court,
performs any act of notarization or holds oneself out as a notary public shall suffer
prision correccional in its medium to maximum period, or a ine of not less than One
Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php100,000.00) but not more than Five Million Pesos
(Php5,000,000.00), or both.

SECTION 9. Accessory Penalty; Public Of icers. — When the offender is a public
of icer or employee, or when the offense is committed by taking advantage of public
position, the penalty shall include perpetual disquali ication from public of ice.

SECTION 10. Disbarred or Stricken-Off Lawyers. — The penalties under this Act
shall likewise apply to persons who commit acts punished herein after they have been
disbarred or after their names are stricken off the Roll of Attorneys by order of the
Supreme Court.

SECTION 11. Non-Exclusivity Clause. — Prosecution under this Act shall not bar
prosecution under other laws, nor shall it bar civil or administrative actions arising
from the same acts.

SECTION 12. Implementing Clause; Coordination. — The Department of Justice, in
coordination with the Supreme Court, the Integrated Bar of the Philippines, and
relevant agencies, shall formulate guidelines for referral, investigation, and
prosecution of violations of this Act, consistent with judicial independence and
existing Supreme Court rules.

SECTION 13. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, other provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 15. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
