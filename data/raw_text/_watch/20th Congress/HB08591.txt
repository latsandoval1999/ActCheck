                               AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING BASIC FINANCIAL LITERACY EDUCATION IN THE K TO 12
BASIC EDUCATION CURRICULUM, INCLUDING ITS OFFERING AS A CORE SUBJECT
IN JUNIOR AND SENIOR HIGH SCHOOL, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Financial literacy is an essential life skill that helps Filipino families manage their
money wisely and build a more secure future. However, many young Filipinos enter
adulthood without learning basic financial skills such as budgeting, saving,
responsible borrowing, insurance protection, and the safe use of digital financial
services. Because of this, many become vulnerable to excessive debt, financial scams,
and poor financial decisions that can affect their long-term well-being.

The government has already recognized the importance of financial education. The
Youth Entrepreneurship Act (Republic Act No. 10679) promotes youth
entrepreneurship and financial literacy programs in all levels of education and directs
the Department of Education (DepEd) to support the K to 12 curriculum with
programs on entrepreneurship and financial literacy. In addition, Republic Act No.
10922, which declares Economic and Financial Literacy Week, requires all public and
private elementary and secondary schools to conduct activities that raise awareness
and expand knowledge about financial management and economic literacy.

While these laws encourage financial education programs and activities, there is still
a need to strengthen formal and structured financial literacy instruction in the K
to 12 Basic Education Program. Students should not only participate in occasional

activities but also receive organized lessons that are age-appropriate, consistent, and
measurable.

The K to 12 education reform aims to produce responsible and productive citizens
equipped with practical skills for lifelong learning, employment, and
entrepreneurship. The Supreme Court has also recognized this goal when it upheld
the constitutionality of the K to 12 program, emphasizing that Senior High School is
designed to prepare graduates for employment, entrepreneurship, and skills
development.

This bill therefore seeks to strengthen financial education in schools by:

   1. Introducing Basic Financial Literacy as a core subject in Junior High
      School and Senior High School, while teaching basic financial concepts in the
      elementary level;

   2. Directing the Department of Education, in coordination with the Bangko
      Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), and
      the Insurance Commission, to develop learning standards, teaching materials,
      and teacher training programs; and

   3. Establishing monitoring and evaluation systems, including baseline
      studies, to measure improvements in students’ financial knowledge and skills.

By doing so, the bill supports the State’s goal of providing a functional education
system that prepares students with the knowledge and skills they need for
responsible and productive lives. It also strengthens existing laws that promote
financial literacy and economic education among the youth.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Financial Education and
Literacy Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote financial capability as an essential life skill and a pillar of inclusive growth
and financial inclusion. Consistent with the State policy of establishing a functional
basic education system responsive to the needs of learners and society, the State shall
institutionalize age-appropriate, evidence-based, and developmentally sequenced
financial literacy education within the K to 12 Basic Education Program.

In pursuit of this policy, the State shall:

   (a) Institutionalize basic financial literacy education in basic education through
       curriculum standards, learning resources, teacher training, and assessment;

   (b) Promote responsible financial decision-making, consumer awareness, and
       digital financial safety among learners;


                                                                              3 | Page
   (c) Support national objectives on financial inclusion and consumer protection;
       and

   (d) Encourage whole-of-community participation in financial education, including
       parents and guardians.

SECTION 3 Scope; Integration in the K to 12 Curriculum. —

   (a) Coverage. This Act shall apply to all public and private elementary and
       secondary schools offering basic education.

   (b) Junior High School and Senior High School. The Department of Education
       (DepEd) shall institutionalize Basic Financial Literacy as a core subject in
       Junior High School and Senior High School: Provided, That DepEd may phase
       implementation by grade level and shall ensure alignment with existing
       learning areas, workload standards, and graduation requirements under the K
       to 12 program.

   (c) Elementary Level Integration. At the elementary level, DepEd shall integrate
       foundational financial literacy concepts in appropriate learning areas, with
       emphasis on values and habits for financial responsibility and good
       citizenship.

   (d) Harmonization with Existing Laws. The implementation of this Act shall be
       harmonized with existing laws and programs on entrepreneurship and
       financial literacy and on nationwide financial literacy activities.

SECTION 4. Curriculum Standards and Learning Competencies. —

   (a) Curriculum Development. DepEd, in coordination with the Bangko Sentral
       ng Pilipinas (BSP), Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), and
       Insurance Commission (IC), and in consultation with relevant stakeholders,
       shall develop the curriculum standards, learning competencies, and
       assessment framework for Basic Financial Literacy.

   (b) Core Content. The curriculum shall be age-appropriate and may include,
       among others:

      1. Money and value; income and goal-setting;

                                                                          4 | Page
      2. Budgeting, saving, and basic financial planning;
      3. Credit, debt, interest, and responsible borrowing;
      4. Banking and other regulated financial institutions, including cooperatives
         and e-money issuers, as applicable;
      5. Insurance and risk management;
      6. Consumer rights, fraud prevention, scams, and redress mechanisms;
      7. Digital financial services, privacy, and cybersecurity hygiene;
      8. Basic taxation concepts relevant to individuals; and
      9. Basic investing concepts, including risk and diversification, subject to age-
         appropriate safeguards.

   (c) Language. Instructional delivery and learning materials shall use English and
       Filipino, and may be translated into local languages where appropriate under
       DepEd policy.

SECTION 5. Learning Resources; Library and School-Based Support. —

   (a) Learning Resources. DepEd shall ensure the development, procurement,
       and distribution of adequate, age-appropriate, and accessible learning
       resources, including printed, digital, and teacher-support materials.

   (b) Library Resources. Public schools shall maintain a readily accessible
       collection of financial literacy learning resources within school libraries or
       learning resource centers. Private schools are encouraged to do the same.

   (c) Parent and Community Information. DepEd, in coordination with the BSP,
       SEC, and IC, shall develop and disseminate parent- and community-facing
       information materials to reinforce learning outcomes and promote safe
       financial behaviors.

SECTION 6. Teacher Qualifications and Training. —

   (a) Competency Standards. DepEd, in consultation with the BSP, SEC, and IC,
       shall issue guidelines on the minimum competencies for teachers handling
       Basic Financial Literacy.

   (b) Capacity Building. DepEd shall implement continuous training programs,
       including training-of-trainers, coaching, and communities of practice, with
       technical assistance from the BSP, SEC, and IC.


                                                                             5 | Page
SECTION 7. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learner Assessment. — DepEd shall
establish monitoring and evaluation mechanisms to ensure effective implementation,
including:

   (a) Classroom-level monitoring of curriculum delivery;
   (b) Learner assessment aligned with competencies, which may include
       performance tasks and practical applications; and
   (c) Periodic baseline and follow-through surveys or studies on learner financial
       literacy to measure progress and inform policy improvements.

SECTION 8. Inter-Agency Coordination. — The DepEd shall be the lead
implementing agency. The BSP, SEC, and IC shall serve as technical partners for
curriculum standards, learning resources, and teacher capacity building, consistent
with their respective mandates.

SECTION 9. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DepEd.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, DepEd, in coordination with the BSP, SEC, and IC,
and after consultation with stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules
and regulations.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
