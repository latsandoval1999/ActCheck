                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State affirms its constitutional duty to uphold the best interests of the
child, particularly for children in conflict with the law (CICL), who require
protection, rehabilitation, and meaningful reintegration into society. Under Republic
Act No. 9344, Bahay Pag-asa facilities were established as child-caring institutions
that provide temporary residential care and intervention services. These facilities are
central to the country’s juvenile justice and welfare system and are intended to
operationalize a child-centered and restorative approach to justice.

       Over the years, the Philippines has seen significant progress in reducing
juvenile delinquency. Data from the Juvenile Justice and Welfare Council indicate
that cases involving CICL declined from 26,850 in 2017 to 4,383 in 2024, reflecting
the effectiveness of diversion and rehabilitation programs. However, recent trends
suggest potential increases in cases, highlighting the need to strengthen existing
mechanisms and prevent regression. Despite these gains, persistent gaps in Bahay


                                                                                       1
Pag-asa operations continue to undermine the delivery of holistic and rights-based
care.

      Among the most critical gaps is the absence of a standardized, enforceable
framework on child-sensitive visitation and reintegration practices. While family
contact is widely recognized as essential to a child’s emotional well-being and
successful reintegration, current practices across facilities remain inconsistent and,
in some cases, unregulated. Visitation is often conducted without proper risk
assessment, structured supervision, or meaningful child participation. Inadequate
documentation and lack of post-visit psychosocial support further weaken the
system. These deficiencies expose children to risks of retraumatization, coercion,
manipulation, and continued exposure to harmful environments—factors that may
ultimately undermine rehabilitation and increase the likelihood of recidivism.

       Compounding these issues are infrastructure and service delivery challenges
faced by Bahay Pag-asa facilities nationwide. With approximately 118 operational
centers, many experience overcrowding, limited resources, and insufficient facilities
for safe and child-friendly visitation. Moreover, the shortage of trained personnel to
manage visitation and reintegration processes constrains the ability of institutions to
implement child-sensitive interventions effectively.

       International standards, including the United Nations Convention on the
Rights of the Child, emphasize that children deprived of liberty have the right to
maintain contact with their families in a manner consistent with their well-being and
best interests. Such contact must be facilitated within a safe, structured, and child-
sensitive environment, with appropriate safeguards and professional oversight. The
absence of a national framework places the implementation of these standards at risk
of inconsistency across local government units.

       This proposed measure seeks to address these gaps by institutionalizing a
Child-Centered Reintegration and Visitation Standards Framework in all Bahay Pag-
asa facilities. It mandates the development of individualized visitation and
reintegration plans, grounded in social case studies and psychological assessments,
and subject to regular review. It establishes risk-based visitor screening and
accreditation, ensuring that only individuals who do not pose harm to the child are
granted access. The measure also defines levels of visitation—including supervised,
therapeutic, and virtual modalities—tailored to the needs and circumstances of each
child.



                                                                                     2
        Crucially, the bill ensures that children are recognized as active rights-holders
 by requiring their participation and assent in visitation decisions, while safeguarding
 them from forced or distressing interactions. It mandates the provision of safe, child-
 friendly visitation spaces, as well as strict prohibitions against abuse, coercion, and
 other harmful conduct during visits. To strengthen accountability, the measure
 requires comprehensive documentation, monitoring, and post-visitation assessment,
 enabling timely intervention and continuous improvement of care.

        Furthermore, the bill integrates visitation within a broader child-centered
 reintegration strategy, recognizing that meaningful family engagement, when
 properly managed, can significantly improve emotional recovery, strengthen support
 systems, and reduce reoffending. By embedding visitation within a structured and
 trauma-informed framework, the State ensures that family contact becomes a tool
 for healing rather than a source of harm.

       Ultimately, this measure reinforces the principle that children in conflict with
 the law are not merely subjects of legal processes but individuals in need of care,
 guidance, and opportunity. By establishing clear, enforceable standards for visitation
 and reintegration, the State strengthens its commitment to restorative justice and the
 long-term well-being of every child.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
 sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Bahay Pag-asa
Child-Centered Reintegration and Visitation Standards Act.”


      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to:




                                                                                1
      (a) Ensure that Bahay Pag-asa prioritizes care, restoration, and reintegration
      over punishment;
      (b) Uphold the best interests, dignity, and developmental needs of the child at
      all times;
      (c) Ensure that all interventions are child-centered, trauma-informed,
      culturally sensitive, and rights-based;
      (d) Promote safe, regular, and meaningful family engagement as essential to
      rehabilitation;
      (e) Guarantee privacy, respect, and emotional safety in all forms of visitation
      and communication;
      (f) Provide holistic and integrated services, including education, health care,
      psychosocial support, and legal assistance;
      (g) Enforce a zero-tolerance policy against all forms of violence, abuse,
      exploitation, and neglect; and
      (h) Facilitate the timely reintegration of children into their families and
      communities.


      SECTION 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all Bahay Pag-asa facilities
established pursuant to Republic Act No. 9344, as amended, including those
operated by local government units (LGUs) and accredited private entities.


      SECTION 4. Establishment of a National Child-Centered Reintegration
Framework. — There is hereby established a National Child-Centered
Reintegration Framework (NCCRF) to govern visitation, care standards, and
reintegration processes in all Bahay Pag-asa facilities.




                                                                                   2
      The Juvenile Justice and Welfare Council (JJWC), in coordination with the
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) and Commission on
Human Rights (CHR), shall develop, implement, and monitor the NCCRF.


      SECTION 5. Minimum Standards for Child-Centered Care and
Reintegration – All Bahay Pag-asa facilities shall adopt and implement a unified
system of minimum standards to ensure that care, visitation, and reintegration
services are delivered in an integrated, child-centered, and rehabilitative manner.
Such standards shall be anchored on the best interests of the child, restorative justice,
and trauma-informed approaches, and shall take into consideration the child’s age,
developmental stage, and individual circumstances.


      Care provided under this Act shall not be limited to custodial supervision but
shall encompass holistic interventions that promote the child’s physical, emotional,
psychological, social, and educational well-being, including the preparation for
successful reintegration into the family and community, with mechanisms to ensure
continuity of support.


      SECTION 6. Visitation Rights and Standards. – Every child under the care
of a Bahay Pag-asa facility shall have the right to maintain regular, meaningful, and
humane contact with his or her family, guardian, and legal counsel, as part of the
child’s rehabilitation and reintegration.


      Visitation shall be allowed at least once a month, and, whenever feasible and
consistent with the best interests of the child, on a more frequent basis, including




                                                                                       3
weekly visits. All visitation shall be conducted in private, safe, and child-friendly
spaces that uphold the dignity and confidentiality of the child.


      Facilities shall provide for alternative modes of communication, including
virtual or remote visitation, when in-person contact is not practicable. Visitation
schedules shall be reasonably flexible and responsive to the needs and circumstances
of the child, subject to the recommendations of the Multi-Disciplinary Team.


      SECTION 7. Child Participation and Autonomy – In all matters affecting
the child, due regard shall be given to the child’s right to participation, consistent
with the child’s evolving capacities. The informed consent or assent of the child shall
be obtained, as appropriate, in relation to visitation and reintegration processes. The
child shall have the right to express his or her views freely and to refuse, defer, or
request reasonable modifications to visitation arrangements.


      No visitation shall be imposed against the will of the child, except when
recommended by qualified professionals and determined to be clinically necessary
and in the best interests of the child, with adequate safeguards to ensure the child’s
protection and well-being.


      SECTION 8. Facility Environment and Conditions. — All Bahay Pag-asa
facilities shall maintain a home-like, nurturing, and non-punitive environment
conducive to the child’s rehabilitation and development. The use of detention-like
conditions, structures, or practices is hereby prohibited. Facilities shall be designed
and maintained to promote safety, dignity, and a sense of normalcy, and shall
provide adequate and appropriate spaces for education, recreation, play, counseling,


                                                                                     4
and family interaction. The overall environment shall reflect a rights-based and
rehabilitative approach to child care.


      SECTION 9. Multi-Disciplinary Care and Case Management. – Each
Bahay Pag-asa facility shall establish a Multi-Disciplinary Team (MDT) composed
of qualified professionals, including, but not limited to, social workers,
psychologists, educators, health personnel, and legal practitioners, who shall be
responsible for the assessment, planning, implementation, and monitoring of each
child’s case.


      An individualized care and reintegration plan shall be prepared within seven
(7) days from the child’s admission, setting forth appropriate interventions,
objectives, and timelines. Such a plan shall be reviewed and updated at least once
every month through case conferences, or as may be necessary, to ensure
responsiveness to the child’s progress and emerging needs.


      SECTION 10. Safety and Protection Standards. — Bahay Pag-asa facilities
shall uphold the highest standards of child protection and shall adopt a policy of zero
tolerance against all forms of abuse, violence, exploitation, discrimination, and
neglect. Appropriate mechanisms for the prompt reporting, documentation, and
response to any incident shall be established and strictly enforced. All personnel
shall be required to undergo training on child safeguarding and shall comply with
established protocols, including mandatory reporting obligations. Preventive and
responsive measures shall be instituted to ensure that all children are protected from
harm at all times.




                                                                                     5
       SECTION 11. Integrated Services, Diversion Support and Reintegration
Programs. – Bahay Pag-asa facilities shall ensure that CICL are provided with
access to integrated and coordinated services, including education, health care,
mental health and psychosocial support, life skills training, and legal assistance,
consistent with diversion and intervention programs under Republic Act No. 9344.
Case management shall be conducted without undue delay, in coordination with the
courts, local government units, and other stakeholders. Reintegration programs shall
include family-based interventions, restorative justice processes, community
involvement, and aftercare services to support the child’s transition back into society
and to prevent recidivism.


       SECTION 12. Documentation, Monitoring and Aftercare Support. – All
interventions, including visitation, case management, and rehabilitation activities,
shall be properly documented in accordance with applicable laws on confidentiality
and data privacy. Bahay Pag-asa facilities shall establish monitoring and evaluation
systems to ensure compliance with standards and to assess the effectiveness of
programs.


       Post-visitation debriefing and psychosocial support shall be provided, when
necessary, to assist the child in processing experiences and emotions. Information
derived from documentation and monitoring shall be used to inform policy, improve
practice, and update individualized intervention plans, ensuring that all measures
remain aligned with the best interests of the child and the objectives of restorative
justice.




                                                                                     6
      SECTION 13. Grievance, Complaints and Reporting Mechanism. – All
Bahay Pag-asa facilities shall establish a confidential, accessible, and child-friendly
grievance and reporting mechanism that allows children in conflict with the law
(CICL), their families, guardians, and facility personnel to report complaints,
concerns, or incidents of abuse, neglect, or misconduct without fear or intimidation.
The system shall be designed to be age-appropriate, understandable to children, and
capable of receiving complaints through multiple channels, including verbal,
written, and electronic means, with assistance provided to CICLs when necessary.


      All complaints shall be acted upon promptly, fairly, and in accordance with
due process, with strict observance of confidentiality and data privacy laws. The
mechanism shall ensure full protection against retaliation or reprisal, whether direct
or indirect, against complainants or witnesses, and shall be supported by designated
child protection focal persons responsible for assisting in the reporting and
resolution process.


      SECTION 14. Oversight and Compliance – The Juvenile Justice and
Welfare Council (JJWC), pursuant to Republic Act No. 9344, shall conduct regular
inspections, audits, and compliance monitoring of all Bahay Pag-asa facilities,
including unannounced visits to ensure full compliance with child protection and
rehabilitation standards.


      SECTION 15. Appropriations – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
concerned agencies. Thereafter, funding shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.


                                                                                     7
      SECTION 16. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts shall constitute
violations of this Act:


      (a). Denial, unjustified restriction, or arbitrary limitation of a child’s right to
      regular and meaningful visitation;
      (b). Failure to provide safe, private, and child-friendly visitation
      environments;
      (c). Imposition of punitive, detention-like, or non-child-sensitive conditions
      within the facility;
      (d). Failure to establish or maintain a Multi-Disciplinary Team (MDT) and
      individualized care plans;
      (e) Neglect in providing access to education, health care, psychosocial
      support, or legal assistance;
       (f) Failure to implement safeguarding measures or respond to incidents of
      abuse, violence, or neglect;
       (g) Obstruction of monitoring, inspection, or audit functions of oversight
      agencies; and
       (h) Falsification or non-maintenance of required records and
      documentation.


      SECTION 17. Administrative Sanctions. – Any official, employee, or
personnel of a Bahay Pag-asa facility, whether under a local government unit (LGU)
or accredited private entity, who violates any provision of this Act or its
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) shall, after due notice and hearing, be
subject to appropriate administrative sanctions, including but not limited to


                                                                                            8
reprimand, suspension, or dismissal from service, without prejudice to the filing of
civil or criminal actions under existing laws.


      SECTION 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the JJWC, in coordination with the DSWD,
shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.


      SECTION 19. Separability Clause. - If any provision, or part hereof, is held
invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provision not otherwise
affected shall remain valid and subsisting.


      SECTION 20. Repealing Clause. - Any law, presidential decree or issuance,
executive order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation contrary
to, or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed, modified, or
amended accordingly.


      SECTION 21. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days
following its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of
general circulation.




      Approved,
