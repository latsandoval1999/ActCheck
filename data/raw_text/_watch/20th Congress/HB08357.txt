                              AN ACT
AMENDING SECTION 5 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9367, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
“BIOFUELS ACT OF 2006”

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE:

The Philippines is heavily dependent on imported petroleum to power transportation, agriculture,
industry, and household consumption. As a net importer of oil, the country is highly vulnerable to
disruptions in global energy supply. Developments in international oil markets—particularly
geopolitical conflicts in major oil-producing regions—are quickly transmitted to domestic fuel prices
and, consequently, to the cost of goods and services throughout the economy.

      Recent hostilities in the Middle East involving Iran, Israel, and the United States have
significantly disrupted global energy markets. The conflict has resulted in attacks on energy
infrastructure and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical maritime chokepoint through which
roughly one-fifth of the world’s crude oil shipments normally pass. Any disruption in this route
constrains global oil supply and drives up international crude oil prices.

      For oil-importing countries such as the Philippines, rising crude oil prices translate directly into
higher domestic fuel costs. Crude oil is the primary feedstock used to produce refined fuels such as
gasoline, diesel, and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG). When global crude prices rise, the cost of
producing and importing these fuels increases accordingly. This results in higher pump prices and
increased transportation, logistics, and production costs, which ultimately push up the prices of food,
transport services, electricity, and other basic goods.

      Higher fuel prices disproportionately affect ordinary Filipino households. Diesel powers the
trucks that transport food and essential commodities, as well as buses and jeepneys used by millions of
commuters. LPG is widely used as cooking fuel in homes and small businesses. When fuel prices rise,
transportation fares increase, food prices climb due to higher logistics costs, and household budgets
become further strained.
      This measure seeks to cushion the impact of global energy disruptions by introducing greater
flexibility in the implementation of the Biofuels Act of 2006 (Republic Act No. 9367). While the law
promotes the use of locally sourced biofuels to enhance energy security and reduce dependence on
imported fossil fuels, the mandatory blending requirements may, in certain circumstances, contribute
to higher fuel prices when biofuel components become more expensive than pure petroleum fuels.

      The proposed amendment authorizes the Department of Energy (DOE), upon recommendation
of the National Biofuels Board (NBB), to temporarily suspend the mandatory biofuel blending
requirements when compliance would cause the price of blended gasoline or diesel to exceed the price
of pure fuels by at least five percent (5%).

      By allowing temporary flexibility during periods of global supply disruption, the measure helps
prevent unnecessary increases in fuel prices while preserving the long-term objectives of energy
security and sustainable development.

     In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
