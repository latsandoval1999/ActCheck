DEVELOPMENT IN BASIC EDUCATION, INSTITUTIONALIZING FOR THE

POSITIVE RELATIONSHIPS, AND PROVIDING SUPPORT FOR TEACHERS
AND SCHOOL PERSONNEL

EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution recognizes education as an essential instrument in the
development of the Filipino learner and directs the State to promote and protect
the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels. It likewise recognizes
the vital role of the youth in nation-building and the responsibility of the State to
foster the development of the Filipino child as a responsible, compassionate, and
productive member of society.

Education, however, is not limited to the transmission of academic
knowledge. A child who can read, compute, analyze, and memorize but cannot
recognize the feelings of others, manage conflict, respect differences, listen with
understanding, or respond appropriately to another person's needs remains
incompletely prepared for life in a family, a school, a workplace, and a
democratic society.

Empathy is therefore not merely a desirable personal trait. It is a
learnable and developable capacity that supports respectfu! relationships,
responsible decision-making, peaceful conflict resolution, inclusion, and a
positive schoo} climate. It helps children understand experiences different from
their own and respond to others with appropriate concern while maintaining
respect for boundaries, dignity, and individual differences.

The urgency of developing these capacities has become even more
evident amid growing concerns over the mental health and emotional well-being
of Filipino children and adolescents. No less than Education Secretary Sonny
Angara publicly called attention to what he described as a “mental! health
epidemic” among the Filipino youth and urged the education sector to
understand its roots and take positive steps to address it. He emphasized the
need for educators to look beyond academic performance, recognize the

===== [page 2] =====
personal circumstances and struggles of learners, and strengthen meaningful
connections between teachers and students.!

This concern comes at a time when schools are increasingly confronted
with issues involving learner isolation, emotional distress, interpersonal conflict,
bullying, harmful online influences, and_ difficulties in forming healthy
relationships. DepEd has likewise emphasized the importance of closer attention
to learners' well-being and is strengthening efforts involving parents, Child
Protection Committees, guidance personnel, and other school and community
partners.

The present situation demonstrates that academic achievement alone
cannot fully address the needs of today's learners. A comprehensive education
must also help children understand and manage their emotions, recognize the
emotions and experiences of others, communicate respectfully, resolve
disagreements constructively, establish healthy relationships, seek help when
needed, and respond appropriately to the needs of people around them.

Empathy education therefore has an important preventive and
developmental role. It can help create school environments in which learners
feel seen, heard, respected, and connected, while giving them practical skills for
dealing with differences, conflict, emotional challenges, and relationships. These
competencies are particularly important during childhood and adolescence, when
social relationships, identity, emotional development, and exposure to new
influences are rapidly evolving.

This measure does not seek to replace mental health services,
professional psychological interventions, guidance and counseling, or other
specialized support. Rather, it complements these services by strengthening the
social and emotional foundations that can contribute to learner well-being and
positive school environments. Where learners require professional assessment,
treatment, psychological first aid, or other specialized intervention, existing
referral mechanisms and qualified professionals shall continue to perform those
functions.

The Philippine education system already recognizes the importance of
these competencies. The Department of Education expressly includes socio-
emotional and values development among the developmental domains, including
the ability to relate well with others, appreciate cultural diversity, and
demonstrate concern for oneself and others.

Existing learning materials likewise address the recognition and
expression of one’s emotions, understanding the emotions of others,
appreciation of differences, and interaction with others as members of the school

framework further incorporates social and emotional learning, experiential
learning, role-playing, collaborative learning, conflict resolution, healthy
relationships, and other approaches that develop interpersonal and character
competencies.

These existing initiatives demonstrate that the foundations for empathy
education are already present in the Philippine basic education system. The
policy challenge, therefore, is not whether empathy belongs in education, but
how its development can be made more coherent, continuous, developmentally
appropriate, adequately supported by teachers, and meaningfully connected to
the everyday experiences of learners.

At present, however, empathy-related learning may be encountered
through different learning areas, values formation activities, guidance programs,
anti-bullying interventions, mental health initiatives, and other school-based
activities. Without a common national framework establishing age-appropriate
competencies, progression, teacher preparation, learning resources, safeguards,
and mechanisms for monitoring implementation, the development of these skills
may vary considerably among schools and learners.

This measure seeks to address that gap.

The proposed Empathy Education and Social-Emotional Development Act
seeks to establish a national framework through which empathy and social-
emotional development may be systematically strengthened within the existing
basic education curriculum and school structures.

The bill provides for an Empathy Learning Period, which may be delivered
through an appropriate combination of existing learning areas and school
programs, including Homeroom Guidance, Good Manners and Right Conduct,
Values Education, and other relevant activities, subject to standards prescribed
by the Department of Education. This approach preserves the objective of
regular and sustained empathy education while allowing schools sufficient
flexibility to implement it within the existing curriculum.

The proposed framework shall progressively develop learners’ capacities in
recognizing and understanding emotions, perspective-taking, respectful
communication, appreciation of differences, responsible responses to the needs
of others, conflict resolution, cooperation, help-seeking, and_ positive
relationships. These competencies shall be taught through age-appropriate,
experiential, reflective, and participatory methods rather than through rote
instruction alone.

The measure shall likewise recognize the importance of strengthening the
relationship between learners, teachers, families, and the wider school
community. Teachers and school personnel shall be supported in recognizing
changes in learner behavior, maintaining supportive classroom environments,
encouraging respectful communication, and referring learners to appropriate
guidance and mental health services when necessary. This approach is
consistent with the recent call of DepEd leadership for teachers to become more
attentive to learners’ circumstances and regularly check on their well-being.

===== [page 4] =====
Equally important, the measure recognizes that teachers cannot be
expected to develop these competencies among learners without appropriate
preparation and institutional support. It therefore provides for teacher and
school personnel development, learning resources, and coordination with teacher
education institutions to strengthen the capacity of educators to facilitate
empathy and social-emotional learning.

The proposed framework shail complement, rather than duplicate, existing
government programs addressing learner mental health, child protection,
bullying prevention, values education, and guidance services. It shall strengthen
the preventive and developmental dimensions of these efforts while preserving
the distinct roles of guidance counselors, psychologists, social workers, health
professionals, and other qualified practitioners.

Ultimately, the measure seeks to strengthen an important dimension of
Philippine education: the formation of learners who are not only knowledgeable
and capable, but also able to understand others, respect human dignity, manage
differences constructively, recognize when help is needed, seek appropriate
support, and contribute positively to their families, schools, communities, and
country.

At a time when the Department of Education itself has raised an urgent
warning about the mental health challenges confronting Filipino youth, the
education system must look beyond academic performance and strengthen the
conditions that allow young people to feel connected, valued, understood, and
supported. The development of empathy and social-emotional competencies is
one important part of that broader response.

The measure therefore seeks to institutionalize empathy not as an
abstract value, but as a practical educational competency that learners can
develop and apply in their everyday lives—helping them understand themselves,
understand others, navigate differences, build healthy relationships, respond to
challenges constructively, and seek help when they need it.

For these reasons, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly

sought.

AN ACT
STRENGTHENING EMPATHY EDUCATION AND SOCIAL-EMOTIONAL
DEVELOPMENT IN BASIC EDUCATION, INSTITUTIONALIZING FOR THE
PURPOSE SCHOOL-BASED PROGRAMS FOR COMPASSION, RESPECT AND
POSITIVE RELATIONSHIPS, AND PROVIDING SUPPORT FOR TEACHERS
AND SCHOOL PERSONNEL

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shail be known as the “Empathy
Education and Social-Emotional Development Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right of every learner to quality, inclusive, learner-centered,
developmentally appropriate, and holistic basic education.

The State recognizes that education extends beyond the acquisition of
academic knowledge and includes the development of competencies necessary
for learners to understand themselves and others, establish respectful
relationships, manage differences constructively, make responsible decisions,
and participate meaningfully in family, school, community, and national life.

For this purpose, the State shall strengthen and institutionalize efforts to
promote empathy and social-emotional competencies in existing basic education
policies, curricula, values education, guidance programs, mental health
initiatives, child protection measures, and anti-bullying programs through a
coherent, continuous, age-appropriate, evidence-based, and developmentally
appropriate national framework that ensures holistic student development,
fosters emotional resilience, and guides educational institutions in cultivating a
safe, supportive, and inclusive learning environment that ultimately empowers
the youth to become compassionate and productive citizens who actively
contribute to national development.

SEC. 3. Objectives. — This Act shall have the following objectives:

(a) Strengthen the development of empathy and _ social-emotional
competencies among learners in basic education;

===== [page 6] =====
(b) Establish a coherent and age-progressive national framework for empathy
education and social-emotional development;

(c) Integrate empathy education into existing curriculum and _ school
structures, including, where appropriate, Homeroom Guidance, Good Manners
and Right Conduct, Values Education, and other relevant learning areas and
programs;

(d) Provide learners with regular opportunities to develop skills in recognizing
and understanding emotions, perspective-taking, respectful communication,
cooperation, conflict resolution, appreciation of differences, and positive
relationships;

(e) Strengthen the capacity of teachers, school heads, guidance personnel,
and other school personnel to facilitate empathy and social-emotional learning;

(f) Promote a safe, inclusive, respectful, and supportive school climate;

(g) Establish appropriate safeguards to protect learners’ dignity, privacy,
mental health, and well-being; and

(h) Develop appropriate mechanisms for formative assessment, research,
monitoring, and evaluation of empathy and social-emotional learning.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

(a) Basic Education refers to the education provided under Republic Act No.
10533, otherwise known as the “Enhanced Basic Education Act of 2013,” and
such subsequent laws governing basic education;

(b) Empathy refers to the capacity of a person to recognize, understand, and
appropriately respond to the emotions, perspectives, experiences, needs, and
circumstances of another person, while respecting the person's dignity,
autonomy, and boundaries;

(c) Empathy Education refers to structured, age-appropriate learning
experiences designed to develop learners' capacity for empathy through
instruction, reflection, experiential activities, interaction, cooperation, and other
appropriate pedagogical approaches;

(d) Social-Emotional Development refers to the development of learners’
abilities to recognize and manage emotions, understand themselves and others,
establish and maintain healthy relationships, make responsible decisions,
communicate respectfully, and respond appropriately to social situations;

(e) Social-Emotional Learning refers to the process through which learners
acquire and apply knowledge, attitudes, and skills necessary for effective
emotional regulation, interpersonal relationships, responsible decision-making,
and constructive participation in social environments;

(f) Empathy Learning Period refers to a regular and developmentally
appropriate period within the existing basic education program devoted to the

===== [page 7] =====
structured development of empathy and social-emotional competencies, which
may be integrated with existing learning areas and school programs in
accordance with the standards prescribed under this Act; and

(g) Learner refers to any person enrolled in a basic education program
covered by this Act.

SEC. 5. National Empathy Education and Social-Emotional Development
Framework. — The Department of Education (DepEd), in coordination with
relevant government agencies, education stakeholders, child development
experts, mental health professionals, teacher education institutions, parents,
learners, and other appropriate stakeholders, shall establish a National Empathy
Education and Social-Emotional Development Framework.

The Framework shall provide national direction and standards for the
progressive development of empathy and social-emotional competencies among
learners and shall:

(a) Establish age- and developmentally appropriate competencies and
learning outcomes;

(b) Provide a progression of competencies across key stages and grade
levels;

(c) Identify appropriate pedagogical approaches and learning experiences;
(d) Provide standards for teacher and school personnel preparation;
(e) Establish appropriate safeguards for learner welfare, privacy, and dignity;

(f) Provide guidance for integration with existing curriculum and school
programs;

(g) Establish mechanisms for monitoring, evaluation, research, and
continuous improvement; and

(h) Provide for appropriate coordination with existing child protection, anti-
bullying, mental health, guidance, and values education policies.

The Framework shall be periodically reviewed and updated based on
research, evaluation results, and emerging needs of learners.

SEC. 6. Integration into the Basic Education Curriculum. — Empathy
education and social-emotional development shall be integrated into the basic
education curriculum in a manner consistent with Republic Act No. 10533 and
other applicable laws and policies.

The DepEd shall ensure that empathy and social-emotional competencies are
developed progressively across appropriate grade levels and learning areas.

===== [page 8] =====
Nothing in this Act shall be construed as requiring the creation of an entirely
separate and independent academic subject where the objectives of this Act can
be effectively achieved through existing curriculum structures.

The integration shall, where appropriate, include Homeroom Guidance, Good
Manners and Right Conduct, Values Education, relevant learning areas, co-
curricular activities, and other existing school-based programs.

SEC. 7. Empathy Learning Period. — The DepEd shall establish a regular
Empathy Learning Period for learners in basic education.

The Empathy Learning Period shall be conducted at least once every school
week, for a minimum of forty-five (45) minutes or its equivalent period, subject
to appropriate integration with existing learning areas and school programs.

The DepEd may prescribe alternative delivery arrangements when necessary
to accommodate different grade levels, school contexts, instructional schedules,
and existing curriculum structures, provided that learners are provided regular,
sustained, and developmentally appropriate opportunities to develop empathy
and social-emotionai competencies.

The Empathy Learning Period shall not be used as a substitute for
professional psychological, psychiatric, medical, or other clinical services.

SEC. 8. Learning Domains. — The Empathy Learning Period and related
learning experiences shall address, as appropriate to the _ learner's
developmental stage, the following domains:

(a) Self-awareness and recognition of emotions;

(b) Understanding and interpreting the emotions and perspectives of others;

(c) Respectful communication and active listening;

(d) Appreciation of individual, cultural, social, and other differences;

(e) Cooperation, kindness, compassion, and responsible responses to others;

(f) Conflict resolution and peaceful problem-solving;

(g) Development and maintenance of healthy and respectful relationships;

(h) Responsible decision-making and consideration of the consequences of
one's actions on others;

(i) Respect for personal dignity, autonomy, consent, and boundaries; and

(j) Other competencies identified by the DepEd through the National Empathy
Education and Social-Emotional Development Framework.

===== [page 9] =====
SEC. 9. Age-Appropriate Learning Standards. — The DepEd shall establish
age-appropriate learning standards and competencies for empathy and social-
emotional development.

Such standards shall take into account:

(a) The cognitive, emotional, social, and developmental characteristics of
learners;

(b) Differences in learning needs and abilities;

(c) Cultural, linguistic, and community contexts;

(d) The principles of inclusion and non-discrimination; and

(e) Relevant Philippine research and internationally recognized evidence-
based practices, insofar as these are appropriate to the Philippine educational

context.

The standards shall progressively increase in complexity as learners advance
through the different stages of basic education.

SEC. 10. Pedagogical Approaches. — Empathy education shall be delivered
through learner-centered, developmentally § appropriate, experiential,
participatory, and culturally responsive approaches.

Appropriate methods may include:

(a) Guided discussions and reflection;

(b) Storytelling and literature;

(c) Role-playing and perspective-taking exercises;

(d) Cooperative and collaborative activities;

(e) Conflict-resolution exercises;

(f) Classroom and school-based projects;

(g) Age-appropriate community engagement;

(h) Peer interaction and structured dialogue; and

(i) Other evidence-based approaches identified by the DepEd.

No pedagogical approach shall require a learner to disclose personal,
traumatic, family, medical, psychological, or other sensitive experiences as a

condition for participation.

SEC. i1. Learner Assessment. — Assessment of empathy and _ social-
emotional learning shall primarily be formative and developmental.

===== [page 10] =====
The DepEd shall establish appropriate assessment tools for determining
whether learners are acquiring the knowledge, skills, and competencies
identified under the National Framework.

Assessment shall not be used to determine the inherent moral worth,
character, personality, family circumstances, psychological condition, or overall
worth of a learner.

No learner shail receive a failing grade solely on the basis of a teacher's
subjective assessment that the learner is insufficiently empathetic.

Assessment results shall be used primarily to guide instruction, identify
learning needs, improve interventions, and strengthen the school learning
environment.

SEC. 12. Teacher and School Personnel Development. — The DepEd shail
provide continuing professional development for teachers, school heads,
guidance personnel, and other appropriate school personnel on empathy
education and social-emotional learning.

Training and capacity-building shall include, as appropriate:

(a) Principles and developmental foundations of empathy;

(b) Social-emotional learning strategies;

(c) Facilitation of reflective and experiential learning;

(d) Conflict resolution and positive classroom management;

(e) Inclusive and culturally responsive practices;

(f) Identification of learners who may require additional support;

(g) Child protection and safeguarding;

(h) Appropriate referral mechanisms; and

(i) Protection of learner privacy and confidentiality.

The DepEd shall ensure that such capacity-building is integrated, to the
extent practicable, into existing professional development systems and shall
avoid unnecessary duplication of training requirements.

SEC. 13. Pre-Service Teacher Education. — The DepEd, in coordination with
the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Professional Regulation
Commission (PRC), teacher education institutions, and other appropriate
stakeholders, shall promote the inclusion of appropriate competencies on
empathy education, social-emotional learning, positive classroom relationships,

safeguarding, and referral mechanisms in relevant pre-service teacher education
and teacher preparation programs.

===== [page 11] =====
SEC. 14. School-Based Empathy and Positive School Climate. — Every public
and private basic education institution shall, consistent with existing laws and
regulations, promote a school environment characterized by respect, inclusion,
safety, cooperation, and constructive conflict resolution.

Schools shali, as appropriate, integrate empathy and _ social-emotional
development into existing:

(a) School improvement planning;

(b) Child protection programs;

(c) Anti-bullying programs;

(d) Guidance and counseling activities;

(e) Mental health promotion programs;

(f) Values formation activities; and

(g) Other relevant school-based programs.

Nothing in this Section shall be construed as requiring schools to establish
separate administrative offices or programs when existing structures can
perform the functions contemplated under this Act.

SEC. 15. Relationship with Existing Laws and Programs. — The
implementation of this Act shall be harmonized with Republic Act No. 10533,
Republic Act No. 10627, Republic Act No. 11036, and other applicable laws,
rules, and regulations concerning basic education, child protection, anti-bullying,

mental health, guidance and counseling, and values education.

This Act shall supplement and strengthen existing programs and shall not
diminish any protection, service, or right already provided under existing law.

SEC. 16. Safeguarding and Referral Mechanisms. — The DepEd shail ensure
that empathy education is implemented consistently with existing child
protection and safeguarding policies.

Schools shail utilize existing referral mechanisms for learners who may
require:

(a) Guidance and counseling;
(b) Psychological services;
(c) Mental health services;
(d) Medical services;

(e) Child protection intervention;

===== [page 12] =====
(f)} Social welfare assistance; or

(g) Other specialized services.

Teachers and school personnel shall not be required to diagnose mental
health conditions or provide clinical treatment by reason of their participation in
empathy education.

Where appropriate, referrals shall be made in accordance with existing laws,
policies, professional standards, confidentiality requirements, and the best
interests of the child.

SEC. 17. Family and Community Participation. — The DepEd shall encourage
appropriate participation of parents, guardians, families, and communities in
supporting empathy and social-emotional development.

Such participation may include:

(a) Parent and caregiver orientation;

(b) Family learning activities;

(c) School-community activities promoting respectful relationships;

(d) Information and educational materials for parents and caregivers; and

(e) Other appropriate activities consistent with the objectives of this Act.

Participation shali be voluntary where the activity is not otherwise required
by law and shall not require disclosure of sensitive family or personai
circumstances.

SEC. 18. Learning Resources. — The DepEd shall develop, adopt, or make
available age-appropriate, culturally responsive, accessible, and evidence-based

learning resources for empathy education and social-emotional development.

Where appropriate, learning resources shall be made available in Filipino,
English, and relevant local Janguages.

The DepEd shall prioritize the development and use of resources that are
accessible to learners with disabilities and responsive to diverse learning needs.

Existing learning resources shall be reviewed and, where appropriate,
enhanced rather than unnecessarily duplicated.

SEC. 19. Pilot and Phased Implementation. — The implementation of this Act
shall initially be undertaken through a pilot and phased approach as determined
by the DepEd.

The pilot implementation shall cover a representative selection of schools
reflecting differences in geographic location, schoo! size, socioeconomic
conditions, language, and other relevant circumstances.

===== [page 13] =====
The DepEd shall assess, among others:

(a) Learner participation and engagement;

(b) Teacher readiness and workload;

(c) Availability and suitability of learning resources;

(d) Integration with existing curriculum and programs;

(e) School-level implementation requirements;

(f) Learner and teacher feedback;

(g) Safeguarding and referral concerns;

(h) Observable changes in relevant social-emotional competencies; and
(i) Resource requirements and implementation costs.

The results of the pilot shall guide the refinement and _ nationwide
implementation of the program.

SEC. 20. Research, Monitoring, and Evaluation. — The DepEd shall establish
a continuing system of research, monitoring, and evaluation to determine the
effectiveness, implementation quality, and resource requirements of empathy
education and social-emotional development.

The evaluation shall, where practicable, examine:

(a) Learner development and social-emotional competencies;

(b) School climate and interpersonal relationships;

(c) Incidents and responses involving bullying, conflict, or other harmful
behavior;

(d) Learner attendance, participation, and engagement;
(e) Teacher preparedness and workload;
(f) Effectiveness of teaching approaches and learning resources;

(g) Equity of implementation among different groups of learners and schools;
and

(h}) Cost and resource requirements.

Evaluation shall distinguish between increased reporting of incidents and
increased incidence of harmful behavior. An increase in reporting shall not, by
itself, be treated as evidence of program failure and may indicate improved
awareness of rights, trust in reporting mechanisms, or access to support.

SEC. 21. Annual Report to Congress. — The DepEd shall submit to Congress,
through the appropriate committees of the Senate and the House of
Representatives, an annual report on the implementation of this Act.

The report shall include:

(a) The number and proportion of schools implementing the program;

(b) Coverage of learners and teachers;

(c) Status of teacher and school personne! preparation;

(d) Availability and utilization of learning resources;

(e) Results of monitoring and evaluation;

(f) Major implementation challenges and corrective measures;

(g) Relevant learner and school climate indicators;

(h) Budgetary requirements and utilization;

(i) Results of pilot or phased implementation, where applicable; and

(j) Recommendations for legislative, administrative, or budgetary action.

The report shail contain sufficient information to enable Congress to assess
whether appropriated resources are producing the intended educational
outcomes.

SEC. 22. Data Privacy and Confidentiality. — All personal information and
sensitive personal information collected, generated, or processed in connection
with the implementation of this Act shall be handled in accordance with Republic

Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and other
applicable laws and regulations.

No learner shall be identified in public reports on the basis of psychological,
behavioral, family, or other sensitive information unless expressly authorized by
law and necessary for the protection of the learner or another legitimate

purpose.

Aggregated and anonymized information shall be used whenever individual-
level information is not necessary.

SEC. 23. Non-Discrimination and Learner Dignity. — The implementation of
this Act shall uphold the dignity, equality, privacy, and rights of every learner.

No learner shall be discriminated against, stigmatized, punished, or denied
educational opportunities on the basis of perceived deficiencies in empathy,
emotional expression, social behavior, disability, neurodevelopmental
characteristics, socioeconomic circumstances, family background, culture,
language, or other protected or relevant personal circumstances.

===== [page 15] =====
The DepEd shall ensure that implementation recognizes legitimate differences
in temperament, communication style, developmental needs, disability, and
cultural expression.

SEC. 24. Implementing Agencies and Inter-Agency Coordination. — The
DepEd shall be the primary implementing agency of this Act.

The DepEd shall coordinate, as appropriate, with the following:

(a) The CHED, particularly with respect to teacher education and pre-service
preparation;

(b) The PRC, consistent with its mandate over professional regulation;

(c) The Department of Health (DOH), particularly on mental health
promotion, referral, and appropriate technical guidance;

(d) The Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), particularly
on child and family welfare and social protection referral mechanisms;

(e) Local government units, consistent with their respective mandates;

(f) Teacher education institutions and professional organizations;

(g) Child protection and mental health experts;

(h) Parents, learners, and civil society organizations; and

(i) Other government agencies and stakeholders as may be necessary.

Such coordination shall, to the extent practicable, utilize existing inter-agency
mechanisms and shall not result in unnecessary duplication of government
functions.

SEC. 25. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the _ initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the DepEd and other concerned agencies, subject to applicable budgeting,

accounting, auditing, and procurement laws, rules, and regulations.

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation
of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

The DepEd shall prioritize the use of existing curriculum, training, guidance,
child protection, mental health, and learning resource structures to minimize
additional administrative and fiscal burdens.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed as requiring the creation of permanent
positions, offices, or organizational units without the availability of corresponding
appropriations and compliance with applicable civil service and budgeting laws.

===== [page 16] =====
SEC. 26. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty
(180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, in consultation with the
CHED, DOH, DSWD, PRC, local government units, teacher education institutions,
education and child development experts, parents, learners, and other relevant
stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the
effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 27. Transitory Provision. — Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the DepEd shall conduct an inventory of existing programs, learning
competencies, teaching resources, training programs, guidance activities, child
protection mechanisms, mental health initiatives, and other related programs
concerning empathy and social-emotional development.

The inventory shall identify:

(a) Existing programs and activities that may be integrated into the
implementation of this Act;

(b) Areas of duplication that may be consolidated;

(c) Existing gaps in curriculum, teacher preparation, learning resources, and
referral systems;

(d) Resource and staffing requirements; and

(e) Existing indicators and data systems that may be utilized for monitoring
and evaluation.

The DepEd shall use the results of the inventory in designing the pilot and
phased implementation and shall avoid unnecessary creation of parallel
programs or administrative mechanisms.

SEC. 28. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional! or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SEC. 29. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 30. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved.
