                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       A worker deciding what to learn next should be able to find out which skills
are in demand, where to acquire them, and how to earn a credential that employers
recognize. A learner who completes a technical course should be able to build on
it toward a higher qualification. As Chairperson of the Committee on Higher and
Technical Education, I file this measure to make those pathways clearer and more
accessible to Filipinos throughout their working lives.

      The bill converts the Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA) into the Cabinet-level Department of Skills Innovation and
Workforce Development. Building on TESDA’s training and certification
systems, the Department will use labor market information from a National Skills
Observatory to guide standards, scholarships, and training investments toward
occupations and skills that employers and regional economies need. Its
performance will be assessed by employment, wage progression, productivity,
entrepreneurship, and career mobility.
       Through enterprise-based training and apprenticeships, learners can
acquire skills in the workplace. Recognition of prior learning will give
experienced workers a way to have their competencies assessed and certified.
Stackable microcredentials and credit transfer will help learners build toward
higher qualifications, while the Digital Skills Passport will allow them to carry
verified credentials and decide whether employers may access their records.

       The measure separates training delivery, provider regulation, scholarship
administration, and assessment and certification to protect the integrity of
credentials. It strengthens trainer and workplace mentor development and
provides for a national innovation center and regional centers to develop and test
new training approaches and make specialized facilities available to local
providers and enterprises. The Skills Innovation Fund will support learners,
workplace and community training, and workers who need to acquire new skills
as jobs change.

      The bill also provides for a review of TESDA Technology Institutions and
other government training functions to address duplication and identify where
public provision remains essential. Any transfer, conversion, or phaseout of a
TESDA institution must protect learners and personnel, preserve records, and
maintain access for disadvantaged learners. The phased transition safeguards the
continuity of scholarships, training programs, and certification, and the measure
provides for annual public reporting and independent review.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

                                   TITLE I
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Department of Skills
Innovation and Workforce Development (DSID) Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
establish a future-ready, innovation-driven, market-responsive, user-centered,
inclusive, and lifelong skills development system that enables Filipinos to
acquire, update, certify, and apply skills for decent work, higher incomes,
productivity,    entrepreneurship,     career     advancement,      and     national
competitiveness.

       The State recognizes the skills of the Filipino people as a strategic national
asset central to employment, industry transformation, innovation, regional
development, social mobility, and economic growth. It shall therefore shift from
a supply-driven training model to a market-driven and user-centered system in
which programs, qualifications, and financing respond to the demonstrated needs
of learners, workers, industries, employers, and local economies. Its success shall
be measured primarily by employment, income, productivity, entrepreneurship,
and career outcomes, and not merely by enrollment, graduation, or facility
utilization.

       The State shall prepare Filipinos for emerging technologies, evolving
forms of work, and changing domestic and global skills requirements, including
the transition to a digital, innovative, green, and climate-resilient economy. Skills
development shall be integrated with the national innovation agenda to enable
workers, enterprises, and industries to adopt, adapt, develop, and diffuse new
technologies, products, processes, and business models.

       The Department created under this Act shall govern and coordinate the
national skills and workforce development system by forecasting demand, setting
standards and direction, assuring quality, financing skills development,
strengthening trainers and workplace mentors, mobilizing industry, supporting
regional and local development, rationalizing public investments, and enabling
Filipinos to learn and relearn throughout life.

       This Act shall harmonize the national skills development system with
Republic Act Nos. 12313, 12063, 11293, 10968, 10647, 12124, 11962, 11927,
and 7796, as amended, and other relevant laws.

                                     TITLE II
               CREATION, CONVERSION, AND MANDATE



SEC. 4. Creation of the Department. There is hereby created the Department of
Skills Innovation and Workforce Development, hereinafter referred to as the
Department, which shall be the primary policy, planning, coordination, standards-
setting, regulatory, financing, quality assurance, and workforce intelligence
agency of the State for skills development, technical education, lifelong learning,
enterprise-based    education     and      training,     apprenticeships,   assessment,
certification, regional skills innovation, and workforce development.

      The Department shall be a Cabinet-level line agency headed by a Secretary.


SEC. 5. Conversion of TESDA. – The Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority created under Republic Act No. 7796 is hereby converted
into the Department of Skills Innovation and Workforce Development.


      The Department shall inherit the powers, functions, personnel,
appropriations, records, assets, liabilities, scholarship programs, training
standards, training regulations, assessment and certification systems, EBET
functions, provider registration systems, and regional structures of TESDA,
subject to the reorganization, rationalization, and transition provisions of this Act.


      The conversion of TESDA into the Department shall be understood as an
expansion of national responsibility rather than a mere change in name. The
Department shall preserve the gains already made by TESDA while placing them
within a broader national framework for skills intelligence, technical education,
workforce     development,      lifelong     learning,     enterprise-based    training,

qualifications, entrepreneurship and livelihood training, assessment, certification,
training finance, regional skills innovation, industry partnership, and workforce
transition.


SEC. 6. Mandate and Powers of the Department. The Department shall ensure
that Filipinos acquire the right skills, at the right time, in the right place, and for
the right opportunities.

       The Department shall have the following powers and functions:

              National Skills and Workforce Development Plan, as the overall
              framework for skills development, employment, entrepreneurship,
              and innovation-led growth across the lifelong learning continuum.
              Said Plan shall be aligned with the Philippine Development Plan and
              shall identify priority occupations, regional skills needs, emerging
              sectors, projected workforce gaps, and financing requirements;
          b. Establish a National Skills Intelligence System, and align with
              respective   regional    skills   intelligence    systems,    to   track

              wages, migration, and technological disruption, to guide training
              design, fund and scholarship allocation, qualifications development,
              and workforce matching. Said system shall operate through the
              National Skills Observatory, which shall serve as the country's
              permanent center for workforce intelligence and shall be capable of
              identifying sudden shortages in critical sectors and anticipating the
              impact of artificial intelligence and automation before workers are
              displaced;

SEC. 7. Harmonization with Other Agencies and Existing Laws. The
Department shall exercise its mandate in coordination with the relevant agencies
and in a manner that does not diminish:

         a. DepEd’s mandate over basic education;

         b. CHED’s mandate over higher education;

         c. DOLE’s mandate over labor standards, labor relations, employment
             protection, occupational safety and health, and workers’ rights;

         d. DTI’s mandate over trade, investment, enterprise development,
             consumer protection, and industry promotion;

         e. DMW’s mandate over overseas employment and migrant worker
             protection;

         f. PRC’s mandate over professional licensure;

         g. PSA’s mandate over official statistics; and

         h. LGUs’ mandate over devolved local development and community-
             based services.

      The Department shall lead skills planning, workforce development,
technical education, skills forecasting through the National Skills Intelligence
System, official skills-based occupational certification, training quality, skills
financing, EBET, lifelong learning pathways for skills, and regional and
provincial workforce development. Skills-related labor market information,

occupational information, employment forecasting, career guidance intelligence,

SEC. 8. Coordination with DepEd and CHED. – The Department shall maintain
strong and permanent coordination with DepEd and CHED to make education
and skills development one connected journey, enabling learners to move from
school to training, from training to work, from work back to education, and from
one career stage to another.

        With DepEd, the Department shall collaborate on career guidance, work
immersion, technical-vocational pathways from junior high school onwards,
foundational digital skills, entrepreneurship, alternative learning pathways, and
transition assistance for out-of-school youth; and

        With CHED, the Department shall collaborate on laddered learning, credit
transfer, applied degree programs, recognition of prior learning, work-integrated
learning, microcredential crediting, industry-linked programs, and continuing
education.

SEC. 9. Secretary, Department Officials, and Staffing. – The Department shall
be headed by a Secretary appointed by the President. The Secretary shall have the
rank, salary, emoluments, privileges, and protocol of a Department Secretary.


      The Secretary shall be assisted by Undersecretaries, Assistant Secretaries,
Directors, Regional Directors, and such other officials as may be necessary and
as may be provided in the approved staffing pattern.

      The Department may include functional clusters for:

         a. Skills Intelligence, Policies, Plans, and Futures;

         b. Standards, Qualifications, and Quality Assurance;

         c. Lifelong Learning, Credit Transfer, and Career Transitions;

         d. Enterprise-Based Education and Training, Apprenticeships, and
              Industry Partnerships;

         e. Regional Workforce Development, Community-Based Upskilling
              and Reskilling, and Skills Innovation;

         f. Digital Skills Systems and Innovation;

         g. Administrative, Finance, and Corporate Services; and

                                                                              17
         h. Legal and Legislative Services.

SEC. 10. National Skills Innovation and Workforce Development Advisory
Council. – There is hereby created a National Skills Innovation and Workforce
Development Advisory Council, chaired by the Secretary, which shall serve as
the principal advisory and coordination body for national skills policy, workforce
development priorities, sector skills strategies, lifelong learning alignment, EBET
implementation, and inter-agency coordination.

      The Council shall include representatives from DOLE, DTI, DEPDev,
DepEd, CHED, PRC, DMW, DOST, DICT, DILG, DBM, employers, labor
organizations, industry associations, Sector Skills Councils, TVET providers,
local governments, private training institutions, schools and higher education
institutions, youth groups, persons with disabilities, development partners, and
other representatives from special sectors or marginalized communities who shall
be appointed by the President. The Council shall be advisory and strategic in
nature. Its role is to provide grounded advice, bring labor-market realities into
policy design, and ensure that the Department’s programs are informed by the
needs of workers, enterprises, and communities. The Department shall retain
clear accountability for implementation.

      A Regional and Provincial Skills Innovation and Workforce Development
Advisory Council shall be established with the same composition as the National
Skills Innovation and Workforce Development Advisory Council, which shall
serve as the principal advisory and coordination body for regional and provincial
skills policy, workforce development priorities, sector skills strategies, lifelong
learning alignment, EBET implementation, regional and provincial workforce
planning, and inter-agency coordination.

      The Department shall ensure that such offices are adequately staffed,

      SEC. 17. Regional Skills Innovation Centers. – The Department shall

      SEC.     18.   Devolution,    Detachment,      Transfer,    Amalgamation,

                                    TITLE VI
    SKILLS PROVISION, TRAINING ENTITIES, AND FINANCING




SEC. 19. National Typology of Skills Provision. – The Department shall clarify
delivery roles and governance mechanisms among LGUs, Community Learning
Centers, public and private technical vocational institutions (TVIs), enterprises,
SUCs, LUCs, polytechnics, sector agencies, and Regional Skills Innovation
Centers.

      The Department shall develop the typology of institutions based on
capacity, quality, scope, and other relevant criteria that will define institutions’
horizontal and vertical classifications, including their expansion towards a
Polytechnic, and shall further define delivery roles and governance mechanisms.
Local government units shall be empowered and supported to operate
Community Skills Hubs, and private providers, schools, colleges, and universities
shall be encouraged to innovate and to offer applied technical programs and
industry-aligned microcredentials within strong quality standards.

SEC. 20. Rationalization of Government Training Entities. – Within one year
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department, in coordination with DBM,
DEPDev, COA, CSC, DOLE, DTI, DepEd, CHED, DILG, DMW, DA, DOST,
DICT, LGUs, and other relevant agencies, shall conduct a whole-of-government
inventory of public training institutions, training centers, scholarship programs,
livelihood training programs, enterprise training programs, certification systems,

                                                                                  28
and workforce development initiatives, including SkillSync and other programs
designed for progressive upskilling.




      Based on the inventory, the Department shall submit to the President and
Congress a Training Functions Rationalization Plan identifying which programs,
funds, institutions, or functions shall be transferred, attached, coordinated,
merged, devolved, discontinued, retained by their mother agencies, or aligned
through standards, qualifications, data sharing, and quality assurance.

      Government training entities shall not be automatically absorbed by the
Department. Sector-specific, constitutional, judicial, diplomatic, public safety,
civil service, maritime, agricultural, or professional training entities may remain
with their respective agencies where justified by law, specialization,
independence,                  or                  public                 interest.


SEC. 21. Sectoral Skills Council. – The Department shall establish Sectoral
Skills Councils for priority sectors. The Sectoral Skills Councils shall provide
formal advice on skills standards, priority occupations, EBET, apprenticeships,
certification requirements, microcredentials, equipment needs, and sector
workforce plans.

      The Department may enter into Sectoral Skills Compacts with industry
bodies, employer organizations, labor organizations, training providers, and
relevant agencies to address skills shortages, emerging skills, productivity gaps,
and transition needs. The Department may provide for a compensation structure
for participants in the Sectoral Skills Council, subject to reasonable market rates
and audit procedures.


                                                                                29
      The Sectoral Skills Councils may, upon accreditation or delegation by the
National Skills Certification Authority, be authorized to conduct or supervise
sector-based assessment and certification, develop assessment tools, recommend
or issue sector-recognized credentials, accredit industry assessors or workplace
assessment arrangements, and support recognition of prior learning. Such
authority shall be subject to national standards, conflict-of-interest safeguards,
audit, data reporting, accessibility requirements, quality assurance mechanisms,
and the continuing oversight of the National Skills Certification Authority.

      Each Sectoral Skills Council per industry shall have a dedicated secretariat
for purposes of coordination, research, policy and program implementation, and
additional tasking as may be required to achieve its purpose, subject to applicable
civil service laws and issuances.

SEC. 22. Skills Innovation Fund. – There is hereby established a Skills
Innovation Fund to finance learner support, enterprise-based training support and
certification, local and community skills programs, recognition of prior learning,
future skills and emerging industry initiatives, and workforce transition support.


      The Fund shall receive stable, recurring funding under the annual General
Appropriations Act, supplemented by the sources under Section 30. The
Department shall issue transparent standards governing the criteria and processes
for allocating Fund resources across regions, sectors, and beneficiary categories.

      The Fund shall include dedicated financing windows for:

         a. Learner access and mobility, including scholarships and training
             allowances;

         b. Enterprise-based training and apprenticeship support;

                                                                                30
         c. Lifelong learning and Community Learning Centers;

         d. LGU Learning Cities and Skills Compacts;

         e. Future skills, innovation, and emerging industries;

         f. Trainer, workplace mentor, and assessor development and
             certification;

         g. Recognition of prior learning, microcredentials, and credit transfer;

         h. National and Regional Skills Innovation Centers, including
             advanced equipment and facility upgrades;

         i. MSME EBET support; and

         j. Workforce transition and career mobility.

      The Department shall issue rules to prevent duplication, leakage,
patronage, and supply-driven allocation of public training funds. Allocation of
the Fund shall be transparent, evidence-based, and outcome-oriented, measuring
whether training leads to employment, wage improvement, enterprise
development, productivity, career mobility, or recognized qualifications.
Outcome-based financing shall be adjusted for learner profile, regional context,
disability, poverty, remoteness, conflict exposure, and labor market conditions to
prevent exclusion of disadvantaged learners and workers, and shall avoid a
narrow system that rewards only easy-to-place trainees and neglects
disadvantaged communities.

      The Department shall submit to Congress an annual report on the Fund's
allocation standards, disbursements per financing window, and outcome-based
performance indicators, as part of the accountability and reporting requirements
under Section 25.

                         TITLE VII
          LEARNER, WORKER, AND TRAINEE PROTECTION


SEC. 24. Rights of Learners, Workers, and EBET Trainees. – Every learner,
worker, and EBET trainee participating in a program under this Act shall have
the right to:

          a. Accurate information on program cost, duration, requirements,
                certification   outcomes,    employment      outcomes,     provider
                performance, available subsidies, and pathways for further learning;
          b. Transparent fees and protection from hidden charges;
          c. Accessible grievance and appeals mechanisms;
          d. Protection from misleading employment, wage, migration, or
                certification claims;
          e. Reasonable accommodation and accessibility for persons with
                disabilities;
          f. Safe spaces and protection from harassment, abuse, discrimination,
                and exploitation;
          g. Occupational safety and health protection in workplace learning;

                                                                                 32
         h. Written training plans for EBET, apprenticeship, learnership, and
            dual training;
         i. Portable and secure records including ownership of and lifelong
            access to the learner's Skills Passport;
         j. Data privacy and informed consent, including the right to withhold
            or grant employer access to Skills Passport records;
         k. Refund, transfer, or teach-out arrangements when programs close or
            are discontinued; and
         l. Recognition of prior learning appeals, where applicable.

      The foregoing list shall be without prejudice to the rights of trainees as
provided by Republic Act No. 12063, or the Enterprise-Based Education and
Training (EBET) Framework Act.
