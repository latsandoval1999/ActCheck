  AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE RIGHT OF CONSUMERS TO INSPECT GOODS
 BEFORE PAYMENT IN CASH-ON-DELIVERY AND OTHER ONLINE-PURCHASE
TRANSACTIONS, REGULATING THE REMITTANCE AND REFUND OF DISPUTED
   PAYMENTS BY COURIERS AND DIGITAL PLATFORMS, REQUIRING THE
POSTING OF A CONSUMER PROTECTION BOND, PROHIBITING UNSOLICITED
  DELIVERIES, PENALIZING FRAUDULENT OR ABUSIVE CLAIMS, AND FOR
                         OTHER PURPOSES

                                               Explanatory Note

        The promise of electronic commerce in the Philippines rests on a simple act of trust: a
consumer orders goods online and expects to receive what was promised. For the millions of
Filipinos who transact through cash-on-delivery (COD), which is still the most widely used
payment arrangement in Philippine online retail, precisely because it spares the buyer the risk of
paying in advance, that trust is extended at the doorstep, in cash, before the parcel is ever
opened. It is at this exact point in the transaction that fraud has taken root.

        Recent data leaves no doubt about the scale of the problem. According to the H1 2026
fraud-trends report of credit-reporting agency TransUnion, logistics recorded the highest
suspected digital fraud rate of any industry in the country in 2025, at 8.5 percent, which is higher
than insurance, retail, and financial services, and a sharp rise from 6 percent the year before. The
Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center logged 10,004 cybercrime complaints in
2024, more than triple the 3,317 recorded in 2023; of these, 3,534 were classified as consumer
fraud, including the non-delivery of purchased goods and other deceptive online transactions,
with reported losses approaching ₱198 million (a figure that almost certainly understates actual
victimization).

       The schemes are by now familiar to ordinary buyers. A consumer is made to pay cash on
delivery for a parcel that turns out to contain an item materially different from the one ordered,
or a defective or counterfeit substitute, the deception discovered only after the rider has left and
the money has passed into the courier’s hands. In its most brazen form, in the practice known as
“brushing”, unsolicited parcels are shipped to persons who never ordered anything at all, often
using personal information harvested without consent, with payment demanded on delivery. In
each variation, the harm crystallizes at the doorstep, and in each, the consumer has already parted
with cash before being allowed to verify what was received.

        The existing legal framework, though substantial, does not reach this precise moment.
The Internet Transactions Act of 2023 (Republic Act No. 11967) built a robust regulatory
architecture for e-commerce under the Department of Trade and Industry, but by its own terms it
does not cover consumer-to-consumer transactions, which are the very channel through which a
large share of social-commerce parcel scams are conducted. The Anti-Financial Account
Scamming Act (Republic Act No. 12010) supplies strong protection where a financial account is
compromised, criminalizing money muling and social-engineering schemes and providing for
the temporary holding of disputed funds, but cash handed to a delivery rider does not pass
through a regulated financial account, and so the COD payment falls outside its protective reach.
And while the Civil Code has long granted the buyer the right to examine goods before
acceptance, that right is expressly defeasible, applying only “unless otherwise agreed upon,”
which is exactly what allows platform terms of service and routine courier practice to extinguish
it and why riders are able to insist on payment before a box may be opened.

         This measure is designed to close that gap. It does not duplicate existing penal statutes;
estafa, cyber-fraud, and financial-account scamming remain governed by the Revised Penal
Code, the Cybercrime Prevention Act, and the Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act. What this
bill does instead is repair the transaction itself. It establishes, as a mandatory and non-waivable
right, that every consumer in a COD transaction may inspect the parcel to verify conformity with
the order before payment is made and acceptance is completed. It allows the consumer who finds
non-conformity to refuse payment and acceptance at no cost. It requires couriers and platforms
that operate COD to maintain a consumer protection bond from which disputed payments are
promptly refunded to the consumer, so that honest sellers and hand-to-mouth delivery riders
continue to be paid without delay while the consumer is made whole from the bond pending
resolution, adapting to the cash-on-delivery chain the same protective logic the Anti-Financial
Account Scamming Act already applies to financial accounts. To keep these safeguards from
being abused, it entitles sellers and delivery personnel to contest a consumer’s claim and
penalizes those who file fraudulent or bad-faith disputes. It prohibits the shipment of unsolicited
goods and the misuse of personal data to generate them. And it designates a single point of
complaint and inter-agency coordination, so that a victimized consumer is not left to navigate the
Department of Trade and Industry, the cybercrime authorities, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas,
and the National Privacy Commission alone.

        Critically, these protections are made to apply to consumer-to-consumer and
social-commerce transactions that the Internet Transactions Act does not reach, so that the
doorstep is protected regardless of the channel through which the sale was made.

        The measure proceeds from a straightforward principle of fairness: no Filipino should be
required to pay for a parcel before being allowed to open it. The Constitution commands the
State to protect consumers from trade malpractices. This bill answers that command at the one
point in the digital marketplace where Filipinos remain most exposed.

       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE RIGHT OF CONSUMERS TO INSPECT GOODS
 BEFORE PAYMENT IN CASH-ON-DELIVERY AND OTHER ONLINE-PURCHASE
TRANSACTIONS, REGULATING THE REMITTANCE AND REFUND OF DISPUTED
   PAYMENTS BY COURIERS AND DIGITAL PLATFORMS, REQUIRING THE
POSTING OF A CONSUMER PROTECTION BOND, PROHIBITING UNSOLICITED
  DELIVERIES, PENALIZING FRAUDULENT OR ABUSIVE CLAIMS, AND FOR
                         OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Cash-on-Delivery Consumer
Protection Act.”

     SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State shall protect consumers from trade
malpractices, consistent with Article XVI, Section 9 of the Constitution, and shall ensure that the
growth of electronic commerce is matched by safeguards that protect Filipinos at every stage of
an online transaction, including the point of delivery and payment. Toward this end, the State
recognizes that consumers who pay in cash upon delivery are entitled to verify, before payment
and acceptance, that the goods received conform to those ordered; that no person should be
compelled to pay for goods that were not ordered or that materially differ from what was
ordered; and that couriers, logistics service providers, and digital platforms that facilitate
cash-on-delivery transactions share responsibility for the integrity of those transactions. This Act
shall be liberally construed in favor of the consumer and shall supplement, and not diminish, the
protections available under other applicable laws.

     SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

               (a) Cash-on-Delivery (COD) Transaction refers to any sale of goods, whether
               business-to-consumer or consumer-to-consumer, concluded through any digital
               platform, online channel, social media service, or other electronic means, in
               which the consumer pays the purchase price in cash or its equivalent to the courier
               or logistics service provider at the time of delivery.

               (b) Consumer refers to a natural person who purchases, or to whom is delivered,
               goods through a cash-on-delivery or online-purchase transaction for personal,
               family, or household use.

               (c) Courier or Logistics Service Provider refers to any person or entity engaged in
               the business of delivering parcels or goods to consumers, including the collection
               of cash-on-delivery payments on behalf of a seller or consignor.

               (d) Digital Platform refers to any website, mobile application, electronic
               marketplace, social media service, or online system through which goods are
               offered, ordered, or sold, consistent with the meaning under Republic Act No.
               11967 or the “Internet Transactions Act of 2023” where applicable.

               (e) Online Merchant or Seller refers to any person or entity that offers or sells
               goods to consumers through a digital platform or online channel, whether or not
               registered as a business.

               (f) Right to Inspect refers to the right of the consumer to open and examine a
               parcel and verify the conformity of its contents with the order placed before
               payment is made and before acceptance is completed.
               (g) Unsolicited Delivery, or “Brushing,” refers to the delivery, or attempted
               delivery, of goods to a person who did not order them, including deliveries
               generated through the unauthorized use of another person’s personal information.

               (h) Disputed COD Payment refers to a cash-on-delivery payment that the
               consumer contests on the ground that the goods were not delivered, were not
               ordered, materially differ from those ordered, or are defective or counterfeit.

     SEC. 4. Right to Inspect Before Payment. — In every cash-on-delivery transaction, the
consumer shall have the right, before payment and before acceptance, to open and inspect the
parcel for the sole purpose of verifying that its contents conform to the order placed, including
the identity, quantity, and apparent condition of the goods and the correspondence of the parcel to
the relevant order or transaction reference. This right shall be mandatory and non-waivable. Any
stipulation, term of service, platform policy, or agreement that waives, limits, or conditions this
right, or that requires payment before inspection, shall be void for being contrary to law and
public policy. Notwithstanding Article 1584 of the Civil Code, the right granted under this
Section may not be defeated by agreement.

     The inspection shall be conducted in a reasonable manner and within a reasonable time at
the point of delivery. The courier or logistics service provider, through its delivery personnel,
shall afford the consumer a genuine opportunity to exercise this right and shall not demand
payment as a precondition to inspection. For goods whose nature requires that the integrity of
tamper-evident or hygienic packaging be preserved, or whose inspection is otherwise regulated
by law, inspection under this Section shall be limited to verifying the packaging, labeling, and
correspondence of the parcel to the order, without prejudice to the consumer’s other remedies
under this Act and other laws.

     SEC. 5. Refusal and No-Cost Rejection. — A consumer who, upon inspection under
Section 4 of this Act, finds that the goods were not ordered, materially differ from those ordered,
or are defective or counterfeit, may refuse to accept the parcel and refuse payment. In such case,
no payment shall be due from the consumer, and the consumer shall incur no delivery, return, or
handling charge. The parcel shall be returned to the seller or consignor through the courier or
logistics service provider, and the cost of such return shall be borne by the seller, the consignor,
or the digital platform, and not by the consumer.
     SEC. 6. Mandatory Remittance Hold Period, Hold-Back, and Refund of Disputed
Payments. — In every cash-on-delivery transaction, the courier, logistics service provider, or
digital platform that collects or processes the payment shall withhold the remittance of the
cash-on-delivery proceeds to the seller or consignor until after the lapse of seven (7) days from
the date of delivery, within which period the consumer may dispute the transaction on any
ground specified in Section 3(h) of this Act. No cash-on-delivery proceeds shall be released to
the seller or consignor before the lapse of this period.

     Where a consumer disputes the transaction within the said seven (7)-day period, the courier,
logistics service provider, or digital platform shall continue to hold back the disputed amount,
and shall not remit it to the seller or consignor, pending resolution of the dispute, and shall act
upon the dispute within a period not exceeding fifteen (15) days from its filing. Where the
dispute is found to be substantiated, the disputed amount shall be refunded to the consumer;
where it is not, the amount may be released to the seller or consignor.

     Any courier, logistics service provider, or digital platform that remits the cash-on-delivery
proceeds to the seller or consignor before the lapse of the period required under this Section, or
that otherwise releases a disputed amount in violation of this Section, shall be primarily and
directly liable to refund the disputed amount to the consumer, without prejudice to its right to
recover the same from the seller or consignor.

     Every digital platform and every courier or logistics service provider that facilitates
cash-on-delivery transactions shall establish an accessible and cost-free mechanism through
which consumers may file and pursue disputes under this Section. This mechanism shall be in
addition to, and consistent with, the dispute-resolution requirements under Republic Act No.
11967 and its implementing rules and regulations.

     SEC. 7. Consumer Protection Bond. — Every courier, logistics service provider, or digital
platform that facilitates or collects payment for cash-on-delivery transactions shall post and
maintain a consumer protection bond, in the form of a surety bond, cash bond, escrow,
irrevocable letter of credit, or other equivalent security acceptable to the Department of Trade
and Industry, in an amount to be determined by the Department in proportion to the volume and
value of the cash-on-delivery transactions the entity handles.
     The consumer protection bond shall answer for the prompt refund of disputed
cash-on-delivery payments to consumers under Sections 5 and 6 of this Act. Where a dispute is
substantiated, or where the disputed amount can no longer be recovered through the hold-back
under Section 6, the refund due to the consumer shall be paid out of the bond without awaiting
recovery from the seller or consignor, so that the consumer is made whole without delay while
honest sellers and delivery personnel continue to be paid in the ordinary course. This mechanism
adapts to the cash-on-delivery chain the same protective logic that Republic Act No. 12010, or
the “Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act”, applies to funds held in financial accounts.

     An entity that draws upon its bond shall replenish it to the required amount within the
period fixed by the Department, and shall retain its right to recover from the seller, consignor, or
other person ultimately liable any amount paid to a consumer from the bond. No person shall
operate, offer, or facilitate cash-on-delivery transactions without a subsisting consumer
protection bond as required under this Section. The Department of Trade and Industry shall
prescribe the amount, form, conditions, replenishment, and administration of the bond in the
implementing rules and regulations, and may adopt graduated or pooled bonding arrangements
for micro and small enterprises so that this requirement does not unduly restrict legitimate
commerce.

     SEC. 8. Right to Contest Claims; Liability for Fraudulent or Abusive Disputes. — The
seller, the consignor, and the delivery personnel shall have the right to contest a consumer’s
refusal, rejection, or dispute under Sections 5 and 6 of this Act and to submit evidence in support
thereof through the dispute mechanism under Section 6 and the complaint system under Section
11 of this Act. The courier, logistics service provider, or digital platform shall provide a fair and
accessible means for such contest and shall resolve it within the period provided under Section 6.

     No consumer shall file a false, fraudulent, or bad-faith dispute, or refuse payment on a claim
known to be untrue, whether by falsely asserting that the goods were not delivered, were not
ordered, materially differ from those ordered, or are defective or counterfeit, or by tampering
with, substituting, damaging, or retaining goods and thereafter claiming non-conformity, for the
purpose of evading payment or obtaining an unwarranted refund.

     A consumer who files a fraudulent or bad-faith dispute shall be liable to pay the amount
properly due, to return the goods or their value, and to indemnify the seller, consignor, courier,
logistics service provider, or digital platform for the damages and costs occasioned by the
dispute, without prejudice to liability under the Revised Penal Code and other applicable laws. In
addition, the Department of Trade and Industry may, after due notice and hearing, impose upon a
consumer found to have engaged in a fraudulent or bad-faith dispute, or in a pattern thereof, an
administrative fine of not less than Five thousand pesos (₱5,000.00) and not more than One
hundred thousand pesos (₱100,000.00). The protections afforded to consumers under this Act
shall not be construed to shield fraudulent or abusive claims, and this Act shall be applied so as
to protect both the consumer and the honest seller and delivery personnel.

     SEC. 9. Prohibition on Unsolicited Deliveries. — It shall be unlawful to ship, deliver, or
attempt to deliver goods to any person who did not order them, or to demand payment for such
goods. A consumer who receives an unsolicited delivery shall be under no obligation to pay for
or to return the goods, and may retain or dispose of them at the consumer’s option without any
obligation to the sender. The use of another person’s personal information, without consent, to
generate, address, or facilitate an unsolicited delivery, or to post fictitious reviews or
transactions, shall be dealt with in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 or the “Data Privacy
Act of 2012”, in addition to the sanctions provided under this Act.

     SEC. 10. Obligations of Couriers, Logistics Service Providers, and Digital Platforms.
— Couriers, logistics service providers, and digital platforms that facilitate cash-on-delivery
transactions shall —

       (a) afford every consumer the right to inspect under Section 4 of this Act, and adopt
       operating procedures and train delivery personnel accordingly to give effect to that right;

       (b) clearly disclose to consumers, before the conclusion of a transaction, the terms
       applicable to cash-on-delivery payment, inspection, refusal, and refund;

       (c) maintain records of cash-on-delivery transactions, remittances, and disputes sufficient
       to permit verification and the resolution of disputes, subject to RA 10173; and

       (d) cooperate with the Department of Trade and Industry and other competent authorities
       in the investigation and resolution of complaints under this Act.
     Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize the shifting to delivery riders or
personnel of any loss arising from a consumer’s refusal, dispute, or non-payment that is properly
the responsibility of the seller, the consignor, or the digital platform.

     SEC. 11. Single Point of Complaint and Inter-Agency Coordination. — The Department
of Trade and Industry, through the Electronic Commerce (E-Commerce) Bureau created under
the Internet Transactions Act, shall serve as the lead agency for receiving and acting upon
consumer complaints arising from cash-on-delivery and online-purchase parcel fraud under this
Act, and shall maintain an accessible mechanism for that purpose. The E-Commerce Bureau
shall coordinate with, and as appropriate refer matters to, the Department of Information and
Communications Technology and the Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center; the
Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, where a financial account is involved; the National Privacy
Commission, where personal data is misused; and the Philippine National Police
Anti-Cybercrime Group and the National Bureau of Investigation Cybercrime Division, where
the acts complained of constitute criminal offenses. The agencies concerned shall adopt a referral
and coordination protocol to ensure that a consumer needs to file only a single complaint.

     SEC. 12. Penalties and Administrative Sanctions. — Without prejudice to liability under
the Revised Penal Code, Republic Act No. 10175 or the “Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012”,
Republic Act No. 12010 or the “Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act”, Republic Act No. 8484
or the “Access Devices Regulation Act of 1998”, and other applicable laws for the underlying
fraud —

        (a) any courier, logistics service provider, or digital platform that denies or obstructs the
        right to inspect under Section 4, that demands payment as a precondition to inspection,
        that fails to hold back or refund a disputed payment as required under Section 6, that fails
        to post or maintain the consumer protection bond required under Section 7, or that
        otherwise violates its obligations under Section 10, shall be subject to an administrative
        fine of not less than Five thousand pesos (₱5,000.00) and not more than One million
        pesos (₱1,000,000.00) for each offense, to be imposed by the Department of Trade and
        Industry, in addition to such other administrative sanctions as it may impose under
        Republic Act No. 11967 and Republic Act No. 7394 or the “Consumer Act of the
        Philippines”;
          (b) any person who ships or delivers unsolicited goods and demands payment therefor in
          violation of Section 9 shall be subject to the same range of administrative fines, without
          prejudice to criminal and civil liability; and

          (c) where the offender is a juridical entity, the responsible officers, directors, or agents
          who participated in, authorized, or permitted the violation shall be held liable.

     The Department of Trade and Industry may also direct the restitution or refund of amounts
unlawfully collected from consumers, and may avail itself of the compliance, takedown, and
blacklisting powers under Republic Act No. 11967 against persistent violators.

     SEC. 13. Relationship to Existing Laws. — This Act shall supplement and shall not
repeal, amend, or diminish the protections available under the Civil Code, Republic Act No.
7394, Republic Act No. 8792, Republic Act No. 11967, Republic Act No. 12010, Republic Act
No. 10173, Republic Act No. 10175, Republic Act No. 8484, and Republic Act No. 11934. The
protections under this Act shall apply to cash-on-delivery and online-purchase transactions
regardless of       whether they      are covered by Republic Act No. 11967, including
consumer-to-consumer transactions. In case of doubt, the provisions of this Act shall be
construed in favor of the consumer.

     SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Trade and Industry, in coordination with the
Department of Information and Communications Technology, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas,
the National Privacy Commission, and the National Telecommunications Commission, and after
consultation with consumer groups, courier and logistics service providers, and digital platforms,
shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary to implement this Act.

     SEC. 15. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department of Trade and Industry.
Thereafter, the amount needed for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

     SEC. 16. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.
     SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

     SEC. 18. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
