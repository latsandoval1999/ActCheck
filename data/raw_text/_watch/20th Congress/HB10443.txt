                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Electricity is a basic necessity, yet its high cost continues to burden
Filipino families and businesses. The government must prioritize making
electricity more affordable.

       During his fifth State of the Nation Address on July 27, 2026, President
Ferdinand R. Marcos, Jr. called for the immediate amendment of the Electric
Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) and the prohibition of charging system
losses to consumers, including the VAT imposed thereon.

       System loss refers to the difference between the electricity delivered to a
distribution system and the electricity ultimately delivered to consumers.
These losses may be technical, arising from the physical delivery of electricity,
or non-technical, resulting from theft, pilferage, meter tampering, and other
unlawful activities.

       Under existing law, distribution utilities (DUs) may recover allowable
system losses from consumers through a system loss charge. In effect,
consumers pay for electricity they did not use, including losses caused by
inefficiencies and illegal activities.

      For a MERALCO consumer using 200 kWh per month, the system loss
charge averaged ₱148.95 monthly from August 2025 to July 2026, or about
₱1,787.38 annually, exclusive of VAT. The charge also increased from ₱0.64
per kWh in January 2025 to ₱0.88 in July 2026, a 37.6% increase.




                                                                                    1
      This bill seeks to provide immediate relief by prohibiting DUs from
imposing or recovering system loss costs from end-users, whether technical or
non-technical. Any system loss costs illegally collected shall be promptly
refunded to consumers. It likewise exempts from VAT the electricity
corresponding to system losses.

       The measure also strengthens accountability. The Energy Regulatory
Commission (ERC) shall strictly enforce the prohibition and continue
monitoring the system loss performance of DUs. Meanwhile, the National
Electrification Administration (NEA) shall strengthen its institutional,
financial, and technical support to electric cooperatives in reducing system
losses.

       DUs may not use the prohibition on system loss recovery as justification
for reducing service quality, delaying necessary maintenance or investments,
or refusing service to qualified consumers. Violations shall be subject to
administrative fines and, when warranted, suspension or revocation of
authority to operate.

        Consumers should pay only for the electricity they actually use. This
measure seeks to make electricity bills fairer, provide immediate financial
relief to Filipino families, and strengthen accountability in the power sector.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
     REMOVING THE BURDEN OF SYSTEM LOSS COSTS FROM CONSUMER
     ELECTRICITY   BILLS,    PROHIBITING  FOR   THE   PURPOSE
     DISTRIBUTION UTILITIES FROM IMPOSING SYSTEM LOSS CHARGES
     ON CONSUMERS AND REMOVING VALUE ADDED TAX ON THE SALE
     OF ELECTRICITY THAT BECOMES SYSTEM LOSS, AND FOR OTHER
     PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
           Congress assembled:


 1             SECTION 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “System Loss Charge
 2      Abolition Act.”

 3             SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby the declared policy of the
 4      State to ensure the affordability of electricity for consumers. Towards this end, the
 5      State shall incentivize distribution utilities to operate with greater efficiency and
 6      prohibit the imposition of electricity charges that unduly burden the public.
