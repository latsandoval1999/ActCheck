                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Mobility is fundamental to the exercise of many constitutional rights and the
availment of a number of economic and social opportunities. Every day, millions of
Filipinos rely on public transportation to access employment, education, healthcare,
and other essential services.
        Yet for many commuters, what should be a routine journey has become a daily
struggle marked by overcrowding, long waiting times, unreliable services, unsafe
infrastructure, and rising transportation costs.
       The country's public transportation system remains inadequate to meet the
growing demands of an expanding urban population1. While public utility vehicles
continue to serve as the primary mode of transport for the majority of Filipinos, years
of underinvestment, fragmented planning, and inadequate transport infrastructure
have resulted in persistent congestion and declining service quality2.
         Studies and commuter surveys consistently show that traffic congestion,
delays, and overcrowding impose significant economic, physical, and emotional costs
on commuters, adversely affecting productivity, work-life balance, and overall quality
of life.3

         The Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028 itself recognizes the inadequacy
of the country's public transportation system and the need to improve mobility by
expanding efficient, accessible, and sustainable transport networks 4. These
challenges are further aggravated by transport systems that have historically
prioritized private motor vehicles over public transportation, pedestrians, cyclists, and
other active modes of mobility5.
       Although various laws govern transportation, road safety, accessibility, and
environmental protection, there remains no single measure that establishes commuter
welfare as a primary consideration in transport planning, infrastructure development,
and public service delivery.
        This bill seeks to fill this legislative gap by enacting the Magna Carta for
Commuters. It institutionalizes a comprehensive Bill of Rights for Commuters,
guarantees access to adequate and affordable public transportation, promotes safe
and accessible mobility infrastructure, including suitable facilities for persons with
disabilities, prioritizes pedestrians, cyclists, and public transport users in the allocation
of public road space, strengthens road safety measures, ensures access to timely
transportation information, provides compensation during service disruptions, and
guarantees meaningful public participation in transportation policymaking.
      The bill also establishes National and Local Offices of Commuter Affairs to
promote commuter welfare, coordinate mobility policies, assist the riding public, and
ensure accountability. It further requires comprehensive Public Transportation
Services and Infrastructure Plans aligned with the mobility needs of Filipinos.
        By placing commuters at the center of transportation governance, this measure
seeks to transform mobility from a daily burden into a reliable public service that
promotes economic productivity, social inclusion, environmental sustainability, and
human dignity. It affirms that transportation is not merely about moving vehicles, but
about enabling people to participate in society safely and efficiently and fully exercise
their rights.

the 20th Congress. Similar measures have been filed by Representatives Claudine
Diana D. Bautista-Lim, Brian Raymund S. Yamsuan, Eduardo Villanueva, Ferdinand

 4       SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Magna Carta for
 5   Commuters"

 7           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Recognizing that mobility is a fundamental
 8   human need, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to guarantee and protect the
 9   right of every person to travel safely, conveniently, reliably, affordably, and with dignity.
10   The State recognizes that mobility is indispensable to the enjoyment of fundamental
11   freedoms and access to work opportunities and basic services, such as health and
12   education.
13
14           The State shall afford protection to commuters, and assure mobility of persons
15   of all ages, abilities, genders, and economic statuses. The State shall protect the
16   welfare of commuters at all times, especially in decisions related to the provision of
17   mobility infrastructure and transportation services.

24   provisions of this Act, and to monitor and ensure compliance of the country with

26   Commuter Affairs under the Department of Transportation (DOTr), which shall have
27   the following functions:
28

34             compromised;

36             education;

38          f) Ensure adequate representation of commuters in any public consultation

 2                                         CHAPTER III
 3                                    RIGHTS OF COMMUTERS
 4
 5         SEC. 6. Right to Adequate Public Transportation Services. - The State shall
 6   ensure that adequate public transportation services are available to meet the needs
 7   of commuters. For purposes of this Act, public transportation services shall be
 8   considered adequate when:
 9
10          a) Public transportation service vehicles are:
11             i. Sufficient in quantity and frequency, to ensure that commuter waiting
12                time at stops and terminals does not exceed ten (10) minutes even
13                during peak demand periods;
14            ii. Accorded priority in the use of road space and traffic signal operations,
15                to ensure that commuters are able to traverse fifteen (15) kilometers
16                within one (1) hour, including all scheduled stops; and
17           iii. Designed to accommodate all persons, including those with disabilities
18                or special needs.
19
20          b) Public transportation service terminals and stops are:
21             i. Available within five hundred (500) meters from any residence or activity
22                center;
23            ii. Connected to another public transportation service terminal within three
24                hundred (300) meters to promote seamless connectivity of travel; and
25           iii. Properly equipped, adequately sized, well-maintained, and suitably
26                designed to promote commuter safety and comfort and accommodate
27                all persons, including persons with disabilities or special needs.
28
29         SEC. 7. Right to Affordable Public Transportation Services. – The State shall
30   ensure that public transportation services are affordable to the general public.
31
32          Vulnerable road users shall continue to benefit from concessional or discounted
33   fares, as provided for by law or relevant regulations.
34
35          The State shall promote the use of public transportation services by providing
36   financial and non-financial incentives to ensure that it is more economical for
37   commuters to use public transportation services more frequently than to use private
38   motorized transport.
39
40          SEC. 8. Right to Alternative Public Transportation Services. - When public
41   transportation services are deficient, the State shall provide alternatives to ensure the
42   mobility of the people, including the use of public resources, such as government-
43   owned vehicles, and through State charter, service contracting, lease of private
44   vehicles, or other similar arrangements.
45
46         SEC. 9. Right to Road Safety. - The State shall pursue the elimination of road
47   crashes and adopt measures to prevent their occurrence. The DOTr, through the
48   National Office, shall implement a comprehensive road safety program and shall
49   implement the activities under the five pillars of the Philippine Road Safety Action Plan,


                                                 4
 1   namely: Road Safety Management, Safer Roads, Safer Vehicles, Safer Road Users,
 2   and Post-Crash Response.
 3
 4          The State shall also promote safe and efficient driving behavior by public
 5   transportation drivers through mandatory training programs and more stringent
 6   licensing. The National Office, in coordination with the Land Transportation
 7   Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB), is hereby mandated to monitor the
 8   location and speed of public transportation vehicles, and the status of their public
 9   transportation drivers in real-time, by requiring the installation of electronic vehicle
10   tracking devices and CCTVs. The State shall repeal all laws, rules, and regulations
11   which incentivize public transportation drivers in relation to ridership or fare revenue,
12   to eliminate dangerous on-street competition and behavior among public transport
13   drivers.
14
15            SEC. 10. Right to Proper Mobility Infrastructure. - The State shall provide proper
16   mobility infrastructure to promote the welfare of commuters. In all renovations of
17   existing infrastructure or construction of new infrastructure, the improvement of travel
18   time impact on public transport service users, cyclists, and pedestrians shall be
19   prioritized.
20
21          Road infrastructure shall be designed and engineered to ensure the safety and
22   access of non-motorized commuters, as well as the disadvantaged or vulnerable
23   groups. Every road where a speed limit of over 20 kilometers per hour (kph) and under
24   60 kph is imposed shall feature an unobstructed walking path at least 2.5 meters wide
25   in each direction. Every road where a speed limit of 60 kph or more is imposed shall
26   feature dedicated bike lanes wide enough to accommodate at least two bikes.
27
28          The State shall develop adequate sidewalks and bicycle lane networks that
29   encourage active transport and provide safe and direct access to priority destinations,
30   such as housing, schools, business centers, and public transportation terminals. Such
31   walking and cycling infrastructure shall be included in the Comprehensive Land Use
32   Plan of every city and urbanized municipality incorporated in the design and
33   implementation of road transport projects at national and local government levels. The
34   Local Office of a city or urbanized municipality shall ensure that each barangay
35   includes:
36
37          a) Safe, well-protected, and unobstructed community-wide footpaths,
38             sidewalks, and bicycle lanes, compliant with universal design guidelines, to
39             enable travel around the city or urbanized municipality entirely by foot,
40             bicycle, or other forms of micro mobility;
41          b) Networks of greenways to promote healthy commuting and encourage
42             active transport;
43          c) Proper lighting on all roads and footpaths to allow safe travel even after
44             dark;
45          d) Tactile paving on sidewalks along high-volume transport corridors for the
46             benefit of blind pedestrians;
47          e) Bicycle racks in every public transportation terminal or stop and activity
48             center that can accommodate at least ten percent (10%) of its anticipated
49             users; and


                                                 5
 1          f) Safe intersections and pedestrian crossings, equipped with raised
 2             pedestrian platforms, highly visible advance warning signages, adequate
 3             street lighting, mid-crossing refuges, traffic calming, and curb outstands to
 4             reduce road pavement crossing width.
 5
 6          The State shall require all government offices and buildings to be equipped with
 7   shower facilities for commuter use. The DOTr, through the National Office, and the
 8   Local Offices shall develop and support measures to incentivize the private sector to
 9   include shower facilities in their respective activity centers.
10
11          SEC. 11. Right to a Fair Share of Public Road Space. - The State shall ensure
12   that public road space is efficiently used. Modes of transportation that move more
13   people, such as public transportation services, active transport, and micro mobility,
14   shall have priority over the use of road space.
15
16          Public road space and footpaths shall not be appropriated or acquired for
17   parking, commerce, or other activities which restrict public use of, or access to, public
18   roads.
19
20          SEC. 12. Right to Breathe Clean Air During Travel. - In line with the provisions
21   of Republic Act No. 8749, otherwise known as the Philippine Clean Air Act of 1999,
22   the State shall take measures to ensure that commuters are able to travel using public
23   transportation services or active transport without compromising their health, by:
24
25          a) Strengthening enforcement measures for private motorized transportation
26             and public transportation vehicles to comply with the emission standards set
27             by the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR);
28          b) Measuring air quality along the major transport corridors in all cities and
29             providing this information to the public in real time; and
30          c) Activating emergency procedures to reduce public exposure to emissions
31             when air quality exceeds safe levels.
32
33          SEC. 13. Right to Information for Efficient and Convenient Travel. - The State
34   shall provide timely and adequate information to commuters to enable them to develop
35   safe, convenient, and efficient travel plans, including selection of transportation modes
36   and routes, by:
37
38          a) Requiring all public transport services to prominently display route maps,
39             fares, schedules, and real time vehicle location and availability, in all public
40             transportation service terminals and other strategic locations;
41          b) Establishing technical standards and regulations for the display of street
42             maps at strategic locations in each neighborhood;
43          c) Ensuring that all public transportation terminals, ports, and airports have a
44             twenty-four (24)-hour information assistance counter to serve commuter
45             needs; and
46          d) Ensuring the collection, processing, and availability to the public of data on
47             mobility, including geo-referenced data on road crashes, air pollution levels,
48             public transportation service travel times.

 1          SEC. 14. Right to Compensation for Public Transportation Service
 2   Breakdowns. – The State shall ensure that passengers are compensated in case of
 3   service breakdowns in public transportation services. During public transportation
 4   service breakdowns, transport operators shall:
 5
 6         a) Give passengers a full refund of the fare for the trip affected by the
 7            breakdown;
 8         b) Assist the affected passengers in reaching their final destination;
 9         c) Inform passengers how they will be redirected to their final destination; and
10         d) Provide food, water, ventilation, and access to waiting areas and bathrooms
11            if the passengers are stranded for more than one (1) hour.
12
13         SEC. 15. Right to Participation in Decision-Making Processes Involving Mobility
14   and Public Transportation Services. – The State shall consult commuters and
15   commuter rights groups, and consider their welfare and interest in all decision-making
16   processes involving mobility and public transportation services by:
17
18         a) Giving commuters, including civil society organizations, advance notice
19            which shall contain all relevant information on any proposed policy,
20            regulation, project, or service affecting mobility at the local or national level;
21         b) Allowing commuters to comment and make suggestions on the proposed
22            policy, regulation, project, or service, through official traditional and social
23            media channels and other similar channels;
24         c) Responding to comments and suggestions from commuters within fifteen
25            (15) working days from receipt; and
26         d) Setting public consultation meetings at a time and place that will maximize
27            participation from commuters.
