                             EXPLANATORY NOTE


The Philippines consistently ranks among the most climate-vulnerable countries
in the world. As an archipelagic nation situated along the Pacific Typhoon Belt,
the country bears the brunt of increasingly severe tropical cyclones, prolonged
droughts, rising sea levels, and catastrophic flooding. While structural
investments in climate adaptation and disaster risk management are critical,
long-term resilience requires a fundamental shift in mindset—a transformation
that must begin in the classroom.

Section 16, Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates that the State
shall "protect and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful
ecology in accord with the rhythm and harmony of nature." Furthermore,
Paragraph 2, Section 3, Article XIV dictates that educational institutions shall
inculcate patriotism and nationalism, foster love of humanity, and teach the
rights and duties of citizenship.

While existing subjects in the K to 12 basic education curriculum incorporate
environmental concepts in general science, climate change education remains
fragmented, abstract, and unevenly integrated. Youth today are not merely
passive victims of environmental degradation; they are pivotal agents of
community resilience, innovation, and climate action.

To prepare Filipino youth for the reality of climate disruption, climate literacy
must be institutionalized as a distinct, age-appropriate, and practical subject
across elementary and secondary levels. True climate literacy extends beyond
fundamental climate science—it encompasses ecological ethics, community
disaster preparedness, local ecosystem conservation, renewable energy, and
climate justice.
This proposed measure seeks to mandate the Department of Education (DepEd),
in coordination with the Climate Change Commission (CCC) and the Department
of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), to institutionalize a
comprehensive Climate Literacy Subject in the basic education curriculum.

In light of the urgent need to build a climate-resilient citizenry from the ground
up, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING CLIMATE LITERACY IN THE BASIC
EDUCATION CURRICULUM, MANDATING TEACHER TRAINING THEREFOR,
             AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Climate Literacy
in Basic Education Act."

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology. The State
recognizes the crucial role of youth in nation-building and acknowledges
education as a primary instrument to foster environmental stewardship, climate
resilience, and sustainable development. Towards this end, the State shall
institutionalize comprehensive, age-appropriate, and localized climate literacy as
an integral component of basic education.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   1. Basic Education - refers to education intended to meet basic learning
      needs, encompassing Kindergarten, Elementary, Junior High School, and
      Senior High School.

   2. Climate Literacy - refers to an understanding of the Earth’s climate system,
      the impacts of human activities and climate change on ecological and socio-
      economic systems, and the skills and knowledge required to adopt
      mitigation, adaptation, and resilient practices.
       SEC. 4. Institutionalization of Climate Literacy. — Climate Literacy shall be
institutionalized as a mandatory subject in all public and private elementary and
high schools nationwide.

         (a) Elementary Level: The subject shall emphasize basic ecological
             concepts, environmental responsibility, waste management, and
             practical community preparedness.

         (b) Secondary Level (Junior & Senior High School): The curriculum shall
             cover climate science, climate adaptation and mitigation strategies,
             climate policy and law, renewable energy, disaster risk reduction,
             and community-led climate action projects.

       SEC. 5. Curriculum Development. — The Department of Education (DepEd),
through its Bureau of Curriculum Development, shall formulate the standardized
Climate Literacy subject curriculum within one hundred eighty (180) days from
the effectivity of this Act. In developing the curriculum, DepEd shall consult with:

   1. The Climate Change Commission (CCC);

   2. The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR);

   3. The Department of Science and Technology – PAGASA (DOST-PAGASA);

   4. Civil society organizations, youth-led environmental groups, and climate
      scientists.

      The curriculum shall be context-sensitive, localized, and tailored to address
the regional and ecological vulnerabilities of specific provinces and indigenous
communities.

      SEC. 6. Capacity Building and Teacher Training. — The DepEd, through
the National Educator Academy of the Philippines (NEAP), in partnership with the
CCC and accredited Higher Education Institutions (HEIs), shall establish and
implement continuous pre-service and in-service training programs for teachers
handling the Climate Literacy subject.

      SEC. 7. Instructional Materials Development. — DepEd shall ensure the
development, publication, and distribution of contextualized learning modules,
textbooks, and digital learning tools. The materials shall be rendered in Filipino,
English, and regional languages as appropriate under existing mother tongue-
based education policies.
      SEC. 8. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Department of Education. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its
continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA).

       SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Education, in consultation
with the Climate Change Commission, DENR, and relevant stakeholders, shall
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation
of this Act.

      SEC. 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees, rules, and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
