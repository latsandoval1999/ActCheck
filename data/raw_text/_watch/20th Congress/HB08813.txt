                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

This bill aims to address the continuing severe housing shortage in the Philippines by
allowing the implementation and establishment of Accessory Dwelling Units or ADUs
in residential lots nationwide, simplify permitting procedures, and provide incentives to
homeowners and builders to encourage their development.

According to the Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development
(DHSUD), as of 2025, the national housing backlog has exceeded 6.5 million housing
units and continues to grow each year due to rapid urbanization, population growth,
and rising land prices in urban centers, which means that these Filipino families lack
access to adequate, safe, and affordable housing. Without a proper and long-term
solution, the housing backlog may grow significantly up to 22 million by 2040.

It is said that one of the main reasons for the housing backlog is because of how
expensive lands are in urban areas, the priority developers tend to focus on middle to
high-income housing instead of affordable ones, the cost of materials, the slow
process of approving permits and land titles, among others. Therefore, new and
innovative housing solutions must be explored to complement existing government
programs.

Under this Act, a possible solution is the implementation of the Accessory Dwelling
Unit (ADU) system, also commonly known as “granny flats”, “backyard homes”, or
“second dwellings”. These are small, self-contained residential units built within the
same lot as an existing primary dwelling.

ADU policies have been successfully implemented in countries such as Australia, the
United States, Canada, and New Zealand, where they have proven to be an effective
tool in increasing housing supply quickly and efficiently without requiring large tracts
of new land or massive government expenditure. In the United States alone, ADUs
now make up 20% of all housing permits in California from 2016 to 2023.

By allowing homeowners to construct small secondary housing units within their
residential properties, ADUs provide multiple benefits, such as additional affordable
housing units within existing communities, supplemental income for homeowners
through rental opportunities, housing options for students, workers, and young
families, multi-generational housing arrangements for Filipino families, and more
efficient use of existing urban land and infrustructure.

Under this Act, the following shall be the main goals:

   a) Allow the construction of up to two (2) Accessory Dwelling Units per residential
      lot;
   b) Establish a streamlined and time-bound permit system to reduce bureaucratic
      delays;
   c) Provide tax incentives and financing support to homeowners and builders;
   d) Promote modular and prefabricated housing technologies to accelerate
      construction; and
   e) Encourage local government to integrate ADUs into local housing strategies.

By unlocking the potential of residential lots across the country, this measure could
generate hundreds of thousands of new housing units within a few years, significantly
helping address the country’s housing backlog.

More importantly, this measure empowers Filipino homeowners to become active
partners in solving the housing crisis while generating additional economic
opportunities for families and communities.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

AN ACT ALLOWING ACCESSORY DWELLING UNITS (ADUs) AND MICRO-HOMES
IN RESIDENTIAL LOTS NATIONWIDE, SIMPLIFYING PERMITS, PROVIDING
INCENTIVES TO HOMEOWNERS AND BUILDERS, PROMOTING MODULAR
HOUSING TECHNOLOGIES, AND ACCELERATING AFFORDABLE HOUSING
SUPPLY, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREOF

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Philippine Accessory Dwelling
Unit and Micro-Housing Act"

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby the policy of the State to undertake a
continuing program of urban land reform and housing which will make available at
affordable cost, decent housing, and basic services to underprivileged and homeless
citizens.

Furthermore, the State shall promote innovative housing solutions that aims to address
the national housing backlog estimated at over six (6) million units, encourage small-scale
housing construction of homeowners, promote affordable rental housing for workers and
young families, support multi-generational Filipino households, encourage industrialized
and modular housing technologies, and maximize efficient use of urban residential land.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

   a) Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) refers to a self-contained residential unit located in
      the same residential lot as a primary dwelling, with its own sleeping area, kitchen,
      bathroom, and independent entrance.
SEC. 4. Types of ADUs. – The following are the types of ADUs that shall be implemented
under this Act:

   a) Detached ADU – small house built within the lot;
   b) Attached ADU – extension of the main dwelling unit;
   c) Garage Conversion ADU – garage of the main dwelling unit that shall be converted
      into a small, independent living space;
   d) Roof Deck ADU – small, independent living space that is built on top of an existing
      building or main dwelling unit that shall be converted into a livable unit;
   e) Modular or Prefabricated ADU – small, independent home that is built off-site in a
      factory and then transported to a property for installation.

SEC. 5. Nationwide Allowance of ADUs. – ADUs shall be allowed in all residential
zones in the Philippines, wherein each residential lot may build up to two (2) ADUs,
Provided, That local government units cannot prohibit its implementation.

SEC. 6. Authorized Size Limits of ADUs. – All ADUs shall have the minimum size of 18
square meters and the maximum size of 75 square meters with a maximum height of up
to two (2) floors.

Furthermore, ADUs may be built on lots with a minimum size of 120 square meters.

SEC. 7. Automatic Permit Approval. – In pursuant to Republic Act 9485, as amended
by Republic Act No. 11032 otherwise known as the Anti-Red Tape Law, local government
units must approve ADU permits within sixty (60) days from submission, otherwise, the
permit shall be deemed automatically approved.

All ADU permits shall be processed through a national digital permit platform integrated
with DHSUD, LGUs, and the Housing and Land Regulatory Board (HLURB) zoning data.

SEC. 8. Pre-Approved ADU Design. – The Department of Human Settlements and
Urban Development (DHSUD) shall create a national catalogue for the following ADU
models, which shall receive immediate permit approval nationwide:

   a) Pre-approved ADU Designs;
   b) Modular ADU Systems; and
   c) Contained ADU Systems.

SEC. 9. No Additional Parking Requirement. – In pursuant to global housing best
practices, ADUs are not required to have additional parking spaces, particularly in urban
areas near public transportation routes.

SEC. 10. Incentives for Homeowners. – Homeowners who build ADUs are entitled to
the following incentives:
   a) Real property tax Incentive of 50% for every five (5) years, Provided, That the ADU
      is exclusively built for purposes of affordable housing, student housing, rental
      housing for workers, and/or additional dwelling for a family member;
   b) Income Tax Exemption for annual rental income from ADUs who earn up to
      P300,000.00.

SEC. 11. Incentives for Builders and Manufacturers. – Companies manufacturing
ADU systems shall be entitled to the following incentives:

   a) 5-year income tax holiday;
   b) VAT exemption for ADU units priced below P3M;
   c) Duty-free importation of machinery for modular housing plants; and
   d) BOI strategic investment priority classification.

SEC. 12. ADU Financing Programs. – Government financial institutions, such as Pag-
Ibig fund, Landbank, and the Development Bank of the Philippines shall create special
ADU loan programs with the following features:

   a) Up to P3 million loanable amount;
   b) Up to 25-year repayment;
   c) Low interest housing loan.

SEC. 13. Partnerships with the Private Sector. – The DHSUD may enter into
agreements with the private sector for purposes of financing the program.

SEC. 14. Rental Housing Support. – Homeowners renting ADUs to teachers, nurses,
police officers, government employees, and students shall receive additional incentives
of property tax rebates and government-backed rental insurance.

SEC. 15. Responsibilities of Local Government Units. – Local government units shall
have the following responsibilities in the implementation of this Act, Provided, That LGUs
shall not impose excessive fees on ADU constructions:

   a) Integrate ADUs into zoning ordinances;
   b) Establish ADU information desks; and
   c) Streamline permit processes.

SEC. 16. Implementing Agency. – DHSUD shall serve as the primary implementing
agency for this Act.

SEC. 17. Appropriations. – The Secretary of DHSUD shall include the funding
necessary for this program under the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DHSUD shall, in consultation
with the DILG, LGUs, BOI, and the Pag-Ibig Fund, shall promulgate not later than one
twenty (120) days upon the effectivity of this Act, the rules and regulations for its effective
implementation: Provided, that the failure to promulgate the rules and regulations shall
not prevent the implementation of this Act upon its effectivity.

SEC. 19. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.

SEC. 20. Repealing Clause. - All orders, issuances, rules and regulations or parts
thereof that are contrary to, or inconsistent with, the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, modified or amended accordingly. This Act is consistent with and shall not
supplant the other streamlining initiatives of the government covered by existing laws and
previously issued executive orders.

SEC. 21. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
