                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

For decades, our approach to stray animals has been trapped in a broken, costly cycle.
Local governments constantly round up and euthanize street animals, yet our streets are
never cleared, rabies remains a threat, and the stray population never drops.

This policy fails because of the "vacuum effect." Removing strays simply clears space for
new, unsterilized animals to move in and breed. Taxpayers are essentially funding a
costly, endless cycle that does not reduce the stray population. This bill breaks that cycle
by institutionalizing the Trap-Neuter-Vaccinate-Return (TNVR) as the primary national
policy for stray management.

This Act aims to replace culling with management. By trapping, fixing, and vaccinating
street animals before returning them to their territory, we create a stable, rabies-immune
population that naturally shrinks over time without a single drop of blood spilled.

In addition, this Act also shields Registered Community Caregivers who feed and care for
street animals at their own expense. Instead of facing harassment, fines, or unfair legal
liabilities under local ordinances, these citizens will now receive statutory protection that
validates their role in community safety.

We can no longer afford to mistake cruelty for control. It is time to replace an expensive,
broken cycle of violence with compassion.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN      ACT      INSTITUTIONALIZING     THE       HUMANE
TRAP-NEUTER-VACCINATE-RETURN     (TNVR)  STRATEGY       FOR
COMMUNITY ANIMALS, RECOGNIZING THE STATUS OF REGISTERED
VOLUNTARY CAREGIVERS, AND AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8485
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Humane Stray Management
and TNVR Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State shall adopt humane, non-lethal, and
scientifically proven methods for stray animal population control. The
Trap-Neuter-Vaccinate-Return (TNVR) paradigm is hereby recognized as the primary
national strategy to manage community animals, protect public health, and safeguard
citizens who care for these animals.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. —

       (a) Trap-Neuter-Vaccinate-Return (TNVR) – The process where free-roaming
       animals are humanely captured, surgically sterilized by a licensed veterinarian,
       vaccinated against rabies, marked via ear-tipping, and released back to their
       original location.

       (b) Community Animal – A free-roaming dog or cat with no single owner that is
       supported or tolerated by residents of a locality.

       (c) Registered Community Caregiver – Any individual or group registered with
       the local government unit (LGU) who voluntarily provides sustenance and care to
       community animals.

       (d) Ear-tipping – The surgical removal of a small portion of a stray animal's left
       ear during sterilization, serving as its permanent visual identification marker.

SECTION 4. Institutionalization of TNVR. — All cities and municipalities shall
establish a localized TNVR Program through their respective Veterinary Offices,
allocating annual funds for sterilization, rabies vaccines, and humane trapping equipment.
Pound operations shall prioritize unsterilized community animals for entry into the
TNVR program over euthanasia.

SECTION 5. Protected Status and Caregiver Protections. —
       (a) Ear-tipped community animals are legally protected. LGUs are prohibited
       from impounding or euthanizing them unless they are confirmed rabid, terminally
       ill, or pose an immediate physical danger to public safety.

       (b) Registered community caregivers shall not be legally classified as "owners" or
       "keepers" under local licensing caps or fees, and they possess the right to feed
       community animals sanitarily in designated zones. They are exempt from civil
       liability for isolated incidents involving these animals, provided they act in good
       faith.

SECTION 6. Amending Clause. — Section 6 of Republic Act No. 8485, as amended, is
further amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 6. It shall be unlawful for any person to torture any animal... or to kill
      any animal... except in the following cases:

                                       xxx xxx xxx

      (5) WHEN THE KILLING IS DONE FOR ANIMAL POPULATION
      CONTROL; PROVIDED, THAT IN NO CASE SHALL CULLING OR
      EUTHANASIA BE APPLIED TO COMMUNITY ANIMALS THAT HAVE
      UNDERGONE THE TRAP-NEUTER-VACCINATE-RETURN (TNVR)
      PROGRAM AND BEAR THE PRESCRIBED EAR-TIP MARKER,
      UNLESS   DETERMINED    NECESSARY   BY   A   LICENSED
      VETERINARIAN FOR REASONS OF PUBLIC SAFETY OR SEVERE
      MEDICAL AFFLICTION.

      LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS SHALL PRIORITIZE TNVR IN
      EXECUTING STRATIFIED POPULATION CONTROL ACTIONS
      WITHIN THEIR JURISDICTIONS."

SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from
effectivity, the Bureau of Animal Industry (BAI), in coordination with the DILG, DOH,
and accredited animal welfare organizations, shall issue the guidelines for this Act.

SECTION 8. Appropriations, Separability, and Repealing Clauses. — Funding shall be
sourced from current LGU and Department of Agriculture appropriations, and integrated
into subsequent annual budgets. If any part of this Act is held invalid, remaining
provisions stand. All laws or ordinances inconsistent herewith are modified accordingly.

SECTION 9. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
