                             AN ACT
 PROVIDING FOR THE LIFETIME VALIDITY OF PERSONS WITH DISABILITY
     IDENTIFICATION CARDS ISSUED TO PERSONS WITH PERMANENT
 DISABILITY, FURTHER AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO.
    7277, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “MAGNA CARTA FOR DISABLED
                      PERSONS”, AS AMENDED

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

A government identification card ought to make it easier for a citizen to access their rights, not
require them to repeatedly prove that they deserve them. For persons with permanent
disabilities, the current requirement to periodically renew a Persons with Disability (PWD)
Identification Card can turn a necessary government service into an unnecessary and repetitive
burden. A person whose blindness, deafness, loss of a limb, or other disability has been
medically established as permanent should not have to repeatedly secure certifications, undergo
administrative procedures, travel to government offices, and spend time and money simply to
prove once again what is already known to be permanent.

For many persons with disabilities, every renewal may mean additional transportation
expenses, physical exertion, time away from work or livelihood, dependence on companions,
and navigating government facilities and procedures that may themselves present accessibility
barriers. A process intended to facilitate access to benefits and privileges can, in practice,
become another barrier to accessing them. This is why the law must distinguish between
disabilities that are permanent and those that are temporary. The appropriate policy, therefore,
is not to subject everyone to the same renewal requirement, but to establish a system
proportionate to the nature of the disability.

This bill seeks to establish that distinction between permanent and temporary disabilities. It
grants lifetime validity to PWD Identification Cards issued to persons with permanent
disabilities, upon certification by the appropriate Municipal or City Health Office, while
retaining a two-year validity period and medical reassessment requirement for persons with
temporary disabilities. These PWD Identification Cards shall conform to the universal design
prescribed by the National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA) and adopted by local
government units, and shall be issued free of charge.

At its core, this measure advances a simple principle: government should not make persons
with permanent disabilities repeatedly prove what the government has already certified as
permanent. Instead, public systems should adapt to the realities of persons with disabilities by
removing unnecessary administrative barriers while maintaining reasonable safeguards against
fraud, misuse, and inaccurate records. These proposed reforms also affirm the Philippines’
obligations under the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities
(CRPD), particularly its commitment to eliminate barriers that prevent persons with disabilities
from exercising their rights and participating fully and equally in society. Accessibility must
extend beyond physical spaces to include identification systems, government procedures, and
administrative requirements that directly affect the daily lives of persons with disabilities.

By establishing lifetime validity for PWD Identification Cards of persons with permanent
disabilities, maintaining appropriate reassessment for temporary disabilities, requiring
universal design, and ensuring that the cards are provided free of charge, this bill seeks to make
the PWD identification system more accessible, equitable, efficient, and humane. The proposed
legislation will reduce unnecessary paperwork, repeated medical certification, transportation
costs, and time spent on renewal procedures, while allowing local government units to focus
their administrative and medical resources on cases that genuinely require assessment or
reassessment. Most importantly, this measure recognizes that government systems should
respond to the realities of persons with disabilities.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Lifetime Validity of Persons with
Disability Cards for Persons with Permanent Disability Act”.

SECTION 2. Section 32 of Republic Act No. 7277, otherwise known as the “Magna Carta
for Disabled Persons”, as amended, is hereby further amended to read as follows:

       “SEC. 32. Persons with [disability] DISABILITIES shall be entitled to the
       following:

       xxx

       The above-mentioned privileges are available only to persons with [disability]
       PERMANENT OR TEMPORARY DISABILITIES who are Filipino citizens,
       REFUGEES, OR STATELESS PERSONS upon submission of [any of the
       following as proof of his/her entitlement thereto]:

          [(I)] An identification card issued by the [city or municipal mayor or the
          barangay captain] PERSONS WITH DISABILITY AFFAIRS OFFICE
          (PDAO) OR A DESIGNATED FOCAL PERSON PERFORMING
          PDAO FUNCTIONS, DULY SIGNED BY THE CITY OR MUNICIPAL
          MAYOR of the place where the persons with [disability] DISABILITIES
          reside[s], AS PROOF OF THEIR ENTITLEMENT THERETO.

          [(II) The passport of the persons with disability concerned; or

          (III) Transportation discount fare Identification Card (ID) issued by the
          National Council for the Welfare of Disabled Persons (NCWDP).

      THE IDENTIFICATION CARDS ISSUED TO PERSONS WITH
      DISABILITIES SHALL CONFORM TO THE UNIVERSAL DESIGN
      PRESCRIBED BY THE NATIONAL COUNCIL ON DISABILITY
      AFFAIRS (NCDA) AND ADOPTED BY THE LOCAL GOVERNMENT
      UNITS.

      THE IDs ISSUED TO PERSONS WITH PERMANENT DISABILITIES
      SHALL BE VALID DURING THE LIFETIME OF THE HOLDER, AND
      MAY BE RENEWED IN CASE OF LOSS OR DAMAGE: PROVIDED,
      THAT SUCH PERMANENT DISABILITY IS CERTIFIED BY THE
      MUNICIPAL OR CITY HEALTH OFFICE WHERE THE PERSON WITH
      DISABILITY RESIDES; PROVIDED FURTHER, THAT THE ISSUING
      OFFICES SHALL IMPLEMENT MEASURES TO ENSURE THAT THE
      LIFETIME VALIDITY OF THE IDENTIFICATION CARDS IS NOT
      ABUSED, ESPECIALLY WHEN THE HOLDER OF THE ID DIES.

      CONVERSELY, THE IDs FOR PERSONS WITH TEMPORARY
      DISABILITIES SHALL BE VALID FOR A PERIOD OF TWO (2) YEARS,
      AND MAY BE RENEWED ONLY UPON COMPLETION OF
      REASSESSMENT CONDUCTED BY AN APPROPRIATE MEDICAL
      PRACTITIONER.

      THE IDs, AS PROVIDED IN THE PRECEDING PARAGRAPH SHALL
      BE FREE OF CHARGE, INCLUDING REISSUANCE DUE TO LOSS OR
      DAMAGE.”

SECTION 4. Community-Based Monitoring System. – The National Council on
Disability Affairs (NCDA) and the Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD) shall, in coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority, the Department
of Health (DOH), the Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG), the
Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), and the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), strengthen a unified and reliable database or
registry of persons with disabilities, including a Community-Based Monitoring System.

SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred eighty (180) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the NCDA and the DSWD shall, in coordination with the
DILG, the DOH, and other relevant government agencies and stakeholders, issue the necessary
rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 6. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, parts or provisions of this Act which are not affected shall continue
to be in full force and effect.

SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
Implementing Rules and Regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

SECTION 8. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
