                                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Philippine education has long relied on a tri-focalized structure, with the
Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) exercising distinct
statutory mandates. While specialization has its virtues, without sustained coordination,
it can also lead to fragmentation.

       This concern lies at the heart of the findings of the Second Congressional
Commission on Education (EDCOM II). In its Final Report Turning Point: A Decade of
Necessary Reforms1, EDCOM II described a systemic “proficiency collapse.” Only 30.5% of
Grade 3 students meet proficiency standards. This low baseline declines sharply to 0.40%
by Grade 12.2 The Philippine Statistics Authority likewise reported that while 93.1% of
Filipinos aged 10 to 64 possess basic literacy, only 70.8% are functionally literate3,
revealing a substantial gap between the ability to read words and the ability to understand
and use information.

        This documented learning crisis cannot be solved through isolated reforms within
individual agencies. Early learning affects later achievement; teacher preparation affects
classroom instruction; curriculum must connect with further education and training;
qualifications must translate across learning pathways; and education itself must
ultimately respond to the skills Filipinos need to participate meaningfully in society and
the economy.

       EDCOM II found that while tri-focalization brought greater focus to the respective
education subsectors, it did not produce corresponding improvements in learning
outcomes.4 Research found no statistically significant improvement in education

the absence of a permanent center of coordination with sufficient authority and continuity
to make policies, plans, data, investments, and reforms pull in the same direction.

       The Executive Branch has already recognized this need through the creation of the
Education and Workforce Development Group under Administrative Order No. 36 (s.
2025). This bill seeks to institutionalize that whole-of-government approach through the
creation of the National Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE) under the Office of
the President, so that education reform does not depend on temporary administrative
arrangements or begin anew with every change in leadership.

       Anchored on the National Education and Workforce Development Plan 2026–
2035, the NCCE shall serve as the primary mechanism for strategic coordination, policy
alignment, convergence planning, and monitoring of education and workforce
development reforms. It shall promote coherent policies across the education continuum,
strengthen human resource and labor market planning, facilitate seamless learner
pathways and lifelong learning, improve education-to-employment linkages, coordinate
complementary programs, and advance secure and interoperable education and
workforce data.

       This measure does not create another bureaucratic layer. It favors the use of
existing mechanisms, provides for the rationalization of overlapping bodies, and succeeds
to the coordinating functions of the existing Education and Workforce Development
Group. The answer to fragmented government is not necessarily more government, but
government that works together.

      Education is a continuum—from the first word a child learns to read to the skills a
young person carries into work and adult life. Government should govern it with the
same continuity. Our education system must learn to work as one system.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate enactment of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
      CREATING THE NATIONAL COORDINATING COUNCIL FOR EDUCATION,
     DEFINING ITS MANDATE, POWERS AND FUNCTIONS, INSTITUTIONALIZING
          COORDINATED EDUCATION AND WORKFORCE DEVELOPMENT
             GOVERNANCE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

            Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1         Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Coordinating
 2   Council for Education (NCCE) Act.”
 3
 4          Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and to take
 6   appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all.
 7
 8          Consistent with the constitutional mandate to establish, maintain, and support a
 9   complete, adequate, and integrated system of education relevant to the needs of the
10   people and society, the State shall promote a coherent, learner-centered, inclusive,
11   equitable, evidence-based, and outcome-oriented education and workforce development
12   system.
13
14           Toward this end, the State shall establish a permanent mechanism for strong,
15   effective, and sustained coordination among agencies responsible for education, training,
16   skills development, employment, and national development to ensure continuity of
17   reforms, coherence of policies and public investments, seamless learner pathways, and
18   accountability for education and workforce outcomes across administrations.
19
20           Section 3. Creation of the National Coordinating Council for Education. – There
21   is hereby created under the Office of the President a high-level inter-agency body to be
22   known as the National Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE), hereinafter referred
23   to as the Council.

32   respective jurisdictions, of programs, projects, and activities aligned with the NEWDP.

12         Section 6. National Education and Workforce Development Plan. – The NEWDP
13   2026–2035, as adopted by Congress as the ten-year policy framework of the National
14   Government, shall serve as the initial strategic framework for the coordinated
15   implementation of education and workforce development policies under this Act.
16
17         The Council shall promote the effective and coordinated implementation of the
18   NEWDP and establish an appropriate framework for monitoring its outcomes, targets,
19   responsibilities, and resource requirements.
20
21           The Council shall periodically review the implementation and continuing
22   relevance of the NEWDP and may recommend such adjustments as may be necessary in
23   light of emerging education, workforce, demographic, technological, social, and economic
24   conditions.
25
26          Prior to the expiration of the existing NEWDP, the Council shall coordinate the
27   preparation of a successor NEWDP. The preparation and substantial revision of the
28   NEWDP shall include appropriate consultation with relevant government agencies,
29   educational institutions, industry, labor, local government units, learners, educators, civil
30   society, and other stakeholders.
31
32           Section 7. Education and Workforce Data Governance and Interoperability. – The
33   Council shall coordinate, through the appropriate agencies, the development and
34   implementation of an interoperable education and workforce data architecture capable of
35   supporting longitudinal analysis of learner participation, progression, completion,
36   qualifications, training, and employment outcomes.
37
38          Interoperability among existing government information systems shall be
39   preferred over the creation of new or duplicative centralized databases.
40
41          The collection, processing, sharing, retention, and disclosure of personal data
42   pursuant to this section shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as
43   the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” its implementing rules and regulations, and other
44   applicable laws and issuances.

 8          Section 8. Technical and Inter-agency Mechanisms. – The Council may establish
 9   such technical working groups, committees, advisory bodies, or other mechanisms as may
10   be necessary to carry out its mandate.
11
12          To the extent practicable, the Council shall utilize existing inter-agency
13   mechanisms and avoid the creation of bodies whose functions substantially duplicate
14   those of existing government entities.

16          The Council may call upon the assistance of departments, agencies, government
17   instrumentalities, educational institutions, local government units, industry, labor, civil
18   society, research institutions, professional organizations, and other relevant stakeholders
19   as may be necessary in the performance of its functions.
20
21           Section 9. Rationalization and Convergence of Inter-Agency Bodies. – The Council
22   shall, in coordination with DBM and other concerned agencies, review existing inter-
23   agency councils, committees, task forces, and similar mechanisms relating to education
24   and workforce development for purposes of identifying duplicative, overlapping,
25   obsolete, or substantially similar functions and improving policy coherence and
26   administrative efficiency.
27
28          The Council may recommend to the President, the Committee on Optimizing the
29   Executive Branch (COEB) established pursuant to Republic Act No. 12231, otherwise
30   known as the “Government Optimization Act,” or such other competent authority, as
31   appropriate, the retention, consolidation, restructuring, deactivation, abolition, or other
32   rationalization of such mechanisms in accordance with law.
33
34          Nothing in this Act shall authorize the Council, by itself, to abolish, merge,
35   consolidate, transfer, deactivate, reorganize, or otherwise modify the statutory mandate
36   of any government agency, office, council, committee, or body.
37
38          Section 10. Council Secretariat. – There shall be a Council Secretariat under the
39   Office of the President, which shall serve as the central technical, analytical, monitoring,
40   and program management arm of the Council.
41

 3         Section 11. Transition from the Education and Workforce Development Group. –

19           Upon the organization of the Council, Administrative Order No. 36, series of 2025,

23          Section 12. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
24   of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Office of the
25   President and such other funds as may be legally available for the coordinating functions
26   of the existing Education and Workforce Development Group, subject to applicable
27   budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.
28
29          Thereafter, the amounts necessary for the continued implementation of this Act,
30   including the authorized staffing and operational requirements of the Council Secretariat,
31   shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
32
33           Section 13. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is held
34   unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in
35   full force and effect.
36
37          Section 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
38   administrative orders, rules and regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with

41          Section 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
