                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to mandate all Public Telecommunications Entities (PTEs) and
Internet Service Providers (ISPs) operating in the Philippines to institute an
automatic refund or bill adjustment mechanism. This is in favor of subscribers who
have experienced service outages or interruptions for an aggregate period of
twenty-four (24) hours or more within a billing month.

        The Philippines faces a persistent and well-documented crisis in internet
reliability. In 2024, Opensignal ranked the country among the lowest-performing
nations in the world on this metric1. A separate Opensignal study likewise placed
the Philippines alongside India, Colombia, and Mexico as one of the lowest-scoring
countries globally for broadband reliability2. This is particularly damaging given
that more than one and a half (1.5) million Filipinos work remotely and the Business
Process Outsourcing (BPO) sector, which employs 1.82 million and contributes to
roughly nine percent (9%) of the country’s GDP, depends entirely on stable Internet
connectivity3. Aside from this, ordinary consumers feel this too through dropped
calls during online meetings, failed digital payments, and sudden data loss.

       Under existing regulations, subscribers bear the burden of seeking
compensation for service interruptions. This bill eliminates that transfer of burden to
the consumers by mandating for refunds to be automatic and provider-initiated.

       Reliable internet connectivity is no longer a luxury but an essential
infrastructure in a competitive economy. Filipinos must not be forced to pay for a
service they did not duly receive.

                           AN ACT
MANDATING PUBLIC TELECOMMUNICATIONS ENTITIES AND INTERNET
SERVICE PROVIDERS TO INSTITUTE AN AUTOMATIC REFUND
MECHANISM FOR SERVICE OUTAGES AND DISRUPTIONS, PROVIDING
PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Automatic Refund for
Internet and Telecommunications Services Outage and Disruptions Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the vital role of
information and communications technology in nation building. It shall implement
measures to regulate internet and telecommunications services offered by public
telecommunications entities (PTEs) and internet service providers (ISPs), to ensure
reliable, accessible, and affordable connectivity, responsive and effective customer
service, and the protection and promotion of the rights and welfare of the
consuming public.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

          a. Fortuitous event refers to either an “act of God” or natural occurrences,
      such as floods, typhoons, storms, earthquakes and the like; or an “act of
      man”, namely riots, strikes, wars, governmental prohibitions, robbery, theft,
      sabotage, cyberattacks, deliberate destruction, or accidental damage to
      telecommunications facilities by third parties and the like;

          b. Internet refers to the biggest global computer network providing a
      wide variety of information and communication facilities, consisting of
      interconnected networks using standardized communication protocols;

         c. Internet access refers to the process of connecting to the internet using
      personal computers, laptops, or mobile devices by users;



                                           1
          d.​ Internet-Service Provider (ISP) refers to a public telecommunications
      entity (PTE) or value-added service (VAS) provider duly authorized by or
      registered with the National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) that
      provides users or other entities with data connection allowing internet access
      through physical transport infrastructure, and such access is necessary for
      internet users to reach content materials online; and

          e.​ Public telecommunications entity (PTE) refers to any person, firm,
      partnership or corporation, government or private, engaged in the provision
      of telecommunications services to the public for a fee.

      SEC. 4. Coverage. – All PTEs, including ISPs in the Philippines, shall be
covered under this Act.

       SEC. 5. Network Maintenance. – In the conduct of their necessary network
maintenance to sustain or update their services, whether minor or major, the
services provided by PTEs and ISPs may not be available. Thus, the planned repairs
and improvements or the scheduled network maintenance carried out from time to
time by PTEs and ISPs, which should be notified at least forty-eight (48) hours in
advance and which shall not exceed a cumulative duration of forty-eight (48) hours
in advance and which shall not exceed a cumulative duration of forty-eight (48)
hours in a month, shall not be deemed as service outage. Likewise, the unscheduled
maintenance or the emergency network maintenance where a repair is needed to an
internet equipment or facility that suffered from unexpected breakdown or change
in condition, shall not be considered as service outage.

       SEC. 6. Automatic Refunds for Internet and Telecommunications Service Outages and
Interruptions. – PTEs, including ISP, shall, on a pro-rated basis, provide a refund credit
to a customer, or adjust a customer’s bill, who was adversely affected by an internet
service outage or interruption for an aggregate period of twenty-four (24) hours or
more, within a month, except when the reasons for such outage is due to network
maintenance, a fortuitous event, or acts of third party or subscriber.

      The concerned PTEs and ISPs shall apply an automatic bill adjustment, without
need for consumer to initiate request, after a standard process but shall not preclude the
consumer from filing a complaint to the appropriate administrative or quasi-judicial
agency for disputes regarding refund or bill adjustment amount.

       The refund credit granted under the circumstances mentioned supra, shall
likewise be granted to customers subscribing to a service on a pre-paid basis.

       SEC. 7. Penalties. – Any PTE or ISP who fails to comply with any of the
provisions of this Act, the NTC may, motu proprio or upon complaint of any interested
party, in the appropriate administrative or quasi-judicial process, and in accordance
with due process, shall impose the following penalties:




                                          2
         a.​ A fine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos (P50,000.00) but not more than
       Two hundred thousand pesos (P200,000.00) for each count of violation; and

           b.​ For repeated violations, aside from fine, revocation or cancellation of the
       license, registration or franchise of the PTEs or ISPs, whichever applies,
       including the waiving of any pre-termination fees of affected subscribers and
       timely disbursement of any remaining credits from excessive downtime.

​       SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days after the
effectivity of this Act, the NTC shall, in coordination with the Department of
Information and Communications Technology, and other concerned agencies,
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary to ensure the effective implementation
of this Act.

       SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
