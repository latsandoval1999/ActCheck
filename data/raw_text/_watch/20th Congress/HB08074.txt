                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This measure seeks to enact the Charter of the Philippine Cancer Center to
institutionalize its authority, structure, and functions as a national specialty hospital
and research institution. It aims to establish a clear governance framework, define
its staffing pattern, ensure sustainable financing, and mandate the PCC to lead the
country’s cancer care, research, and innovation agenda.

       Section 15, Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State
to protect and promote the right to health of the people. Consistent with this,
Republic Act No. 11215 or the National Integrated Cancer Control Act (NICCA)
recognizes cancer as a national health priority and provides for the establishment of
the Philippine Cancer Center (PCC) as the national center of excellence for cancer
care, research, and capacity development.


                                                                                         1
         Despite this, the full operationalization of the PCC remains constrained by
 gaps in governance, infrastructure, and resources. The Center currently shares its site
 with the Lung Center of the Philippines, and much of its hospital and research
 facilities are still under construction. The absence of a clear governance and
 coordination framework among the PCC, the Department of Health – Cancer Control
 Division, and other relevant agencies has resulted in fragmented programs and
 overlapping mandates. Moreover, the lack of a defined organizational structure,
 delineated mandates, approved bed capacity, and staffing pattern hampers the PCC’s
 ability to fully implement its functions and deliver advanced cancer and precision
 oncology services.

        Through the passage of this Act, the State not only fulfills its constitutional
 mandate to protect and promote the right to health and advance universal health care,
 but also reaffirms its commitment to the 70th World Health Assembly Resolution
 on Cancer Prevention and Control in the Context of an Integrated Approach, which
 recognizes cancer as a critical health and development priority and calls for
 comprehensive national strategies on prevention, early detection, diagnosis,
 treatment, and palliative care.

        Ultimately, the enactment of this Charter will enable the Philippines to
 establish a sustainable, integrated, and world-class national cancer program
 anchored on innovation, research, and equitable access to quality care towards the
 realization of a cancer-free Philippine society.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Charter of the
Philippine Cancer Center."


   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right to health of the people and to instill health
consciousness among them, in accordance with Section 15, Article II of the 1987


                                                                                   1
Constitution. In accordance with Republic Act No. 11215 otherwise known as the
National Integrated Cancer Control Act (NICCA), the State likewise recognizes
cancer as a national health priority and the need to adopt an integrated,
comprehensive, and equitable approach to cancer prevention, detection, diagnosis,
treatment, rehabilitation, palliative, and survivorship care. Towards this end, the
State shall strengthen the Philippine Cancer Center through this Act by restructuring
its organization, elaborating its mandates, roles, and functions, and providing it with
adequate powers, authority, and resources to effectively carry out its mission
towards a cancer-free Philippines.


   SECTION 3. The Role of the Philippine Cancer Center (PCC) as a National
Specialty Center and a Government Health Agency. – Cognizant of the vital roles
and responsibilities of PCC in the implementation of NICCA, the following crucial
information are hereby supplemented and complemented:


   A. The PCC shall be a government-owned and -controlled corporation (GOCC)
      under the Department of Health (DOH) as an attached agency and headed by
      Executive Director IV.
   B. The PCC’s current location and delineated property (inclusive of the allocated
      space for parking slots, treatment plant, and other facilities that ensure the
      building’s proper functioning) within the Lung Center of the Philippines
      (LCP) compound covering a total lot area of 8,345 square meters is hereby
      transferred from LCP to PCC as its own property. The Usufruct Agreements
      with Control No. 2020-1027-180 dated October 27, 2020 and 2023-0824-305
      dated August 24, 2023 between the PCC and LCP are hereby rescinded.
      Furthermore, the Usufruct Agreement between the Philippine Blood Center


                                                                                      2
   (PBC) and LCP is hereby rescinded, thereby transferring the whole property
   to PCC until PBC shall have its own property under the law.
C. The PCC shall not only refer to a national specialty center for complex and
   rare cancers but also to a government health agency in order to perform its
   overarching mandates, roles, and functions as stated herein.
D. The PCC shall perform oversight and accountability mechanisms and shall
   provide capacity development and technical assistance to all cancer treatment
   facilities, both public and private, on (1) resource-stratified services along the
   cancer care continuum; (2) standard and quality of care; (3) cancer data
   management; (4) research and technological innovations; (5) networking and
   linkages; and (6) financial risk protection, among others.
E. Pursuant to Sec. 41 of the General Provisions of Republic Act No. 7180 (An
   Act Appropriating Funds for the Operation of the Government of the Republic
   of the Philippines from January One to December Thirty-One, Nineteen
   Hundred and Ninety-Two and for Other Purposes), Executive Order No. 641
   Authorizing the Establishment and Administration of Provident Funds in the
   Government, and the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) Budget
   Circular No. 2008-3 Rules, Regulations and Procedure on the Establishment
   and Administration of Provident Funds in the Philippine Government, the
   PCC shall establish and administer Provident Fund for the general welfare of
   its employees. Furthermore, the PCC shall formulate and implement its own
   Occupational Safety and Health Policy and Program pursuant to Department
   Order No. 2016-0264 or Guidelines for the Institutionalization of
   Occupational Safety and Health in the Department of Health.

      SECTION 4. Overall Mandates of the PCC. – In congruence with NICCA,
the mandates of PCC are further elaborated in order to fulfill the letter and spirit of
the law.


   A. The PCC shall be the centerpiece, cornerstone, and hub of the National
      Integrated Cancer Control Program of the DOH, especially in comprehensive
      cancer care continuum, research and innovation, and training and education.
      The PCC shall develop a comprehensive National Cancer Control Plan
      (NCCP) periodically.
   B. The Executive Director IV of PCC shall be a permanent ex-officio member
      and the Vice-Chair of the National Integrated Cancer Control Council
      (NICCC).
   C. The overall management of existing Cancer and Supportive-Palliative
      Medicines Access Program (CSPMAP) and Cancer Assistance Fund (CAF)
      shall be transferred to PCC from Disease Prevention and Control Bureau –
      Cancer Control Division (DPCB-CCD).
   D. The PCC shall enter into strategic collaborations and partnerships with
      various local and international stakeholders as well as development partners
      in order to fulfill its overarching mandates.
   E. PCC shall be the clearinghouse of all cancer-focused and –related researches,
      clinical practice guidelines, consensus guidelines, clinical care pathways, et
      cetera, and shall develop and publish biennially a compendium of evidence.
   F. The PCC shall be a permanent recipient of at least 10% of the total funding
      for research allocated to DOH through the annual General Appropriations Act.
      The said fund shall go directly to the budget line item of PCC. The PCC shall
      be responsible for its prudent and judicious expenditure based on the National


                                                                                     4
      Cancer Research Agenda including Cancer-Focused and -Related Policy and
      Systems Research.
   G. The PCC shall set up a Trust Fund for research and innovation including
      cancer drug discovery and development including repurposing, emergency
      procurement of medicines, among others. Cash and cheque donations from
      philanthropists, corporations and other individuals or entities and the proceeds
      from fund-raising activities shall be deposited straight away into the Trust
      Fund of PCC. A Trust Fund Board shall be created and shall be chaired by the
      Executive Director IV.
   H. Managed Entry Agreements (MEAs), either financial or outcome-based type,
      shall be forged between the DOH (e.g., PCC, PhilHealth, etc.) and
      pharmaceutical companies (manufacturers, distributors, etc.) to provide
      access to innovative and new medicines (post-regulatory approval, usually
      post-Phase III or in Phase IV) and other health technologies with or without
      positive recommendations from the Health Technology Assessment Council
      (HTAC).
   I. In line with prioritizing cancer prevention, the PCC shall lead and collaborate
      with all stakeholders in committing to and achieving a tobacco-free generation
      (TFG) or nicotine-free generation (NFG) which serves as a ‘preventative
      vaccine’ against tobacco and nicotine addiction and ultimately prevents
      smoking-associated cancers.


      SECTION 5. Strategic Roles and Functions of PCC. – Cognizant of the
purposes and objectives of PCC in the NICCA, the PCC shall perform the following
strategic roles by establishing the following Centers with corresponding personnel
complement in order to fulfill the shared vision of Cancer-Free Philippines


                                                                                    5
(Philippines Free from the Burden of Cancer) through concerted and unified efforts
with various stakeholders:


   A. Center for Complex and Rare Cancers (CCRC) – CCRC is a level 3 (at least
      100-bed capacity), PhilHealth-accredited smart national specialty center
      (NSC) dedicated to complex and rare cancers, which shall provide the highest
      level of expertise in clinical services, teaching and training, research and
      innovation, and the country’s apex or end referral facility in cancer care.
      Within five years of its full operation, the hospital shall be Joint Commission
      International (JCI)-accredited for Hospital Program (Gold Seal of Approval:
      best practices in quality of care and patient safety) and JCI-certified for Cancer
      Center. A Deputy Executive Director IV shall head the CCRC.


   B. Center for Cancer Data, Research, and Digital Oncology (CCDRDO) – The
      CCDRDO shall be responsible for the governance, management, and
      operationalization of the (a) National Cancer Registry and Monitoring System
      which shall be named as the Philippine Cancer Registry (both population- and
      hospital-based cancer registry, etc.); and (b) National Cancer Data Repository
      System vis-à-vis Philippine Health Insurance Corporation’s (PhilHealth)
      National Health Data Repository System). It shall be the “source of single
      truth” of all cancer data nationwide to ensure veracity, reliability and
      coherence of cancer information across various domains. The Philippine
      Cancer Registry Annual Report shall be published and widely disseminated
      to all stakeholders.




                                                                                       6
        The CCDRDO shall serve as a think tank on data-driven and evidence-
  based cancer plans, policies, programs, standards, and regulations as well as
  center of excellence for research and innovation wherein intra- and extra-
  mural programs and innovative technologies to prevent, diagnose, treat and
  predict cancer shall be developed and managed. The CCDRDO shall be a
  Cancer-Focused and -Related Health Policy and Systems Research Institution
  and shall be part of the Philippine Network for Health Policy and Systems
  Research. The CCDRDO shall also be an integral part of the national
  pharmacovigilance reporting system particularly on all pharmaceutical
  products related to cancer. The CCDRDO shall establish the Cancer Drug
  Discovery and Development Division (CDDDD).


        The CCDRDO shall develop, adopt, integrate, and standardize the use
  of digital health technologies to improve cancer patient’s journeys and
  treatment outcomes. A Deputy Executive Director IV shall head the
  CCDRDO.


C. Center for Cancer Care Network, Training and Development (CCCNTD) –
  The CCCNTD is a hub-and-spoke-model cancer care delivery system catering
  to the general population across the life course and along the entire cancer
  care continuum. CCCNTD shall provide oversight and accountability
  mechanisms over the Cancer Care Networks (CCN), which consist of
  multisectoral multistakeholders with designated Comprehensive Cancer
  Center/s (either Advanced or Basic) within the region, province, city and
  municipality as the “hub” and the rest of the CCN components as “spokes”.




                                                                              7
CCCNTD shall cater to the general population, that is, healthy, at-risk, cancer
patients, persons living with cancer, and cancer survivors.


      The CCCNTD shall establish the National Cancer Grid (NCG). NCG
is a large network of cancer treatment facilities (both private and government),
research organizations, professional societies, charitable institutions, and
patient groups within the CCN wherein the participating institutions or
organizations share freely their cancer data [following the principles of
findability, accessibility, interoperability, and reusability (FAIR)], expertise,
resources, and best practices to improve the quality of care for cancer patients.
The NCG shall be in charge of standardizing the quality of multidisciplinary
cancer patient care through Clinical Practice Guidelines (CPGs) and Clinical
Standards/Metrics of Quality Assurance, among others. Clinical Trial
Network and E-Learning Portal shall be created and managed by the NCG.


      CCCNTD shall be the center of excellence for training and education
wherein (a) the fundamental and highest level of education in cancer-focused
and -related fields, including researchers, policymakers, medical and allied
health professionals; and (b) training in cancer-focused and -related fields
including cancer science and policy, among others, shall be available and
accessible.


      The Cancer Control Division (CCD) of the Disease Prevention and
Control Bureau (DPCB) shall be transferred to CCCNTD and shall perform
its roles and responsibilities accordingly. A Deputy Executive Director IV
shall head the CCCNTD.


                                                                                8
   D. Center for Blood Disease and Transfusion (CBDT) – The CBDT shall cater
      to patients with blood disorders and hematologic malignancies in addition to
      blood donation and transfusion. CBDT shall occupy the existing five-story
      building of the Philippine Blood Center. A Deputy Executive Director IV
      shall head the CBDT.


            Furthermore, the PCC and its four (4) Centers are expected to perform
      the following inherent functions: (A) Core and Strategic Functions, namely:
      (1) policies, standards, and guidelines, (2) capacity building and technical
      assistance, (3) stakeholder engagement and strategic partnerships, (4) research
      and innovation, and (5) program management; and (B) Support Functions,
      namely: (1) human resource management and organizational development, (2)
      infrastructure, equipment, and information and communications technology
      management,     (3) procurement and supply chain management,                (4)
      performance management and accountability, and (5) budget planning and
      execution.


   SECTION 6. Duties and Responsibilities of the Office of the Executive
Director IV. – The Office of the Executive Director IV shall:


   A. Lead, direct, and oversee the full implementation of this Act in conjunction
      with other pertinent provisions of NICCA;
   B. Determine and propose the organizational structure and staffing pattern of
      PCC as a GOCC subject to the evaluation and approval of the Governance




                                                                                    9
      Commission for GOCCs (GCG), Department of Budget and Management
      (DBM) and in accordance with civil service laws, rules and regulations;
   C. Ensure the development, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of
      policies, standards, regulations, protocols, guidelines, and other issuances
      related to PCC’s various strategic roles and functions as identified herein;
   D. Delegate specific tasks to the respective Deputy Executive Directors IV; and
   E. Perform other duties and responsibilities as may be assigned or delegated by
      the Secretary of Health, by the NICCC, or by the Board of Trustees of the
      Specialty Hospitals.


   SECTION 7. Exemption of Cancer and Supportive-Palliative Medicines from
the Philippine National Formulary (PNF). – Pursuant to 1) Section 3 of RA No.
11223 (Universal Health Care Act), which guarantees all Filipinos equitable access
to quality and affordable health care goods and services while protecting them
against financial risk, and 2) Section 2 of the RA No. 11215 (NICCA), which seeks
to make cancer prevention, screening, diagnosis, treatment, and care more equitable,
affordable, and accessible—particularly for the underprivileged, poor, and
marginalized—the inclusion of cancer and supportive-palliative medicines in the
PNF and the Drug Price Reference Index (DPRI) shall no longer be required for
purposes of government procurement of such medicines.


   To ensure efficiency and affordability, the PCC shall undertake bulk or pooled
procurement of these medicines every three (3) years for all designated access sites
within the Cancer Care Network (CCN).




                                                                                     10
   Accordingly, Executive Order No. 49, s. 1993, entitled “Directing the Mandatory
Use of the Philippine National Formulary Volume I as the Basis for Procurement of
Drug Products by the Government,” and all other related or inconsistent issuances,
are hereby amended or modified as necessary to conform with this provision.


   SECTION 8. Patient Navigators as Allied Health Care Workers. – Recognizing
the indispensable value of patient navigation in the journey of patients along the
cancer care continuum, patient navigators are hereby considered as allied health care
workers and shall be a prerequisite in the licensing and operation of hospital-based
cancer treatment facilities.


   SECTION 9. Data Encoders or Cancer Registrars as Allied Health Care
Workers. – Recognizing the vital role of data management in the institutionalization
of hospital cancer registry, data encoders are hereby considered as allied health care
workers and shall be a prerequisite in the licensing and operation of hospital-based
cancer treatment facilities.


   SECTION 10. Institutionalization of Multidisciplinary Patient Care. –
Functional multidisciplinary patient care or tumor board and/or molecular tumor
board shall be a prerequisite in the licensing of hospital-based cancer treatment
facilities. PhilHealth shall ensure the inclusion of such in its benefit packages for
cancer.


   SECTION 11. Cancer Rehabilitation and Surveillance, Supportive and
Survivorship Care. – The PCC shall lead in the development of implementing




                                                                                   11
guidelines on cancer rehabilitation and surveillance, supportive and survivorship
care. PhilHealth shall ensure the inclusion of such in its benefit packages for cancer.


   SECTION 12. National Palliative Care and Pain Management Program. – The
overall management of the National Palliative Care and Pain Management Program
shall be transferred from the DPCB-CCD to PCC. PhilHealth shall ensure the
inclusion of such in its benefit packages for cancer.


   SECTION 13. Cancer Control Policy and Program in All Settings. – The PCC
shall closely coordinate and collaborate with the following national government
agencies, among others, to implement the cancer control policy and program in the
respective settings:


   A. Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) and Civil Service
      Commission (CSC) for the workplace;
   B. Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) for the
      communities; and
   C. Department of Education (DepEd) and Commission on Higher Education
      (CHED) for schools and academic institutions.


      SECTION 14. Carcinogenic Substances Notification. – The Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) and PCC shall lead in the development and annual
publication of the master list of carcinogenic substances from food, drugs, cosmetics
and household/urban hazardous products, among others. The FDA shall require all
products to include any carcinogenic ingredients or substances in their full ingredient

      SECTION 16. Tax Exemptions and Privileges. – All donations,
contributions, endowments, equipment, devices and supplies to be imported by
PCC’s authorized entities or persons, for the actual use and benefit of the PCC, shall
be exempt from income and gift taxes and the same shall be further deductible in
full for the purpose of determining the maximum deductible amount under Section
30 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.


      The PCC shall be exempt from the payment of taxes, charges and fees
imposed by the government or any political subdivision or instrumentality thereof
with respect to purchase of medicines, equipment, medical devices and supplies
made by, or for, the PCC.


      SECTION 17. Sanctions. – Non-compliance with any of the provisions of
this Act or its implementing rules and regulations by any officer, employee, or entity
of the PCC, or any concerned government agency, shall constitute neglect of duty or
such other administrative offense as may be warranted under existing laws, rules,
and regulations, and shall be dealt with accordingly pursuant to Civil Service laws,
the Revised Administrative Code, and other pertinent issuances.




                                                                                   14
      SECTION 18. Annual Report. – The PCC shall render an annual report on
the implementation of this Act to the House Committee on Health and the Senate
Committee on Health and Demography.


      SECTION 19. Appropriations. – The amount needed for the PCC and its four
(4) Centers towards the implementation of their roles, functions, and mandates as
stipulated in this Act shall be established and shall be reflected in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA) beginning Fiscal Year 2026. Likewise, the budget line
items for the Cancer Supportive-Palliative Medicines Access Program (CSPMAP)
and the Cancer Assistance Fund (CAF) are hereby transferred from the Disease
Prevention and Control Bureau (DPCB) to the PCC.


      SECTION 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). - Within thirty
(30) days from the effectivity of this Act, the PCC, in consultation with the DOH
and other appropriate government agencies, shall formulate and prepare the IRR to
implement the provisions of this Act.


      SECTION 21 Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining part or provisions not otherwise
affected shall remain in force.


      SECTION 22. Repealing Clause. – Any law, decree, ordinance, or
administrative circular not consistent with any provision of this Act is hereby
amended, repealed, or modified accordingly.
