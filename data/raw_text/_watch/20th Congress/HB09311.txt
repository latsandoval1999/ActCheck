                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to streamline the registration and licensing processes for
Minahang Bayan areas and small-scale mining operations. It promotes responsible
mineral development, strengthens government regulation, and encourages the
formalization of small-scale mining activities. It amends Republic Act (RA) No. 7076,
otherwise known as the “People’s Small-Scale Mining Act of 1991”.

        The Philippines is home to vast mineral resources that remain critical to
overall national development and industrial growth. The mining sector continues to
hold significant economic potential and contributes to exports, local revenues, and
rural economic activity, especially in provinces rich with minerals. The Philippine
Statistics Authority states that the total monetary value of the country’s Class A
gold, copper, nickel, and chromite reserves reached approximately Four hundred
and eighty-one billion (P481.45 Billion) in 20241. This is equivalent to 1.69% of the
country’s gross domestic product for the same year.

       Small-scale mining has long formed part of the local economy and identity of
mining communities in the Cordillera Administrative Region (CAR), specifically in
the Province of Benguet. It remains a major source of livelihood in seven (7) of
Benguet’s thirteen (13) municipalities and continues to support thousands of
households dependent on mining for subsistence. The Cordillera region likewise
remains one of the country’s most active areas for Minahang Bayan operations. As of
2022, eleven (11) Minahang Bayan sites had already been established in the region,
including the four (4) in Benguet, namely the Loacan Itogon Pocket Miners
Association, Bagtangan-Goldstar Small Scale Miners Association, Benguet
Federation of Small Scale Miners Association Inc., and the Federation of Taneg
Indigenous Communities Livelihood Association2. The continuing declaration of
Minahang Bayan areas in Benguet reflects the government’s recognition of the need

       Despite the enactment of RA No. 7076, many small-scale miners continue to
struggle in securing the permits and approvals necessary to operate legally. The
process for declaring Minahang Bayan areas and issuing small-scale mining contracts
remains slow and costly, particularly for those with limited technical and financial
resources.

      This measure establishes a more efficient framework for the declaration of
Minahang Bayan areas by prescribing definite processing periods, standardizing
documentary requirements, and clarifying the responsibilities of government
agencies involved in the issuance of permits. It likewise strengthens safeguards for
environmental protection, occupational safety, indigenous people’s rights, and
compliance with mining laws and regulations3

       The bill also supports small-scale miners through technical assistance,
training programs, and access to lawful and regulated mining opportunities under
government supervision. Institutionalizing a more responsive and transparent
regulatory framework strengthens not only small-scale mining operations, but also
improves environmental stewardship and the protection of mining communities.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

       SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The DENR Secretary shall, in
consultation with the DILG, DOLE, DTI and small-scale mining stakeholders,
promulgate the rules and regulations to effectively implement the provisions within
ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act. Priority shall be given to such rules
and regulations of the small-scale miners.

      SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, issuances, rules, and regulations
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

       SEC. 17. Separability Clause. - If any of the sections or provisions of this Act is
held invalid, all other provisions not affected thereby shall remain valid.

      SEC. 18. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
