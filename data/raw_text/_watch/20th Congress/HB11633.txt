                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This measure seeks to institutionalize the training, recognition, and
engagement of Indigenous Community Health Workers (ICHWs) who are
members of the communities they serve. They will serve as a vital link
between Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples
(ICCs/IPs) communities and the formal health system by promoting
health education, disease prevention, sanitation, nutrition, maternal and
child health, immunization, basic health monitoring, first aid within the
limits of their training, referral, and assistance in accessing appropriate
health facilities.

      The measure likewise recognizes the value of traditional health
practices of ICCs/IPs while ensuring that their use and promotion remain
consistent with applicable laws, public health standards, and regulations
governing traditional and herbal medicines. Community herbal gardens
may also be supported as appropriate, subject to relevant health,
environmental, biodiversity, and traditional knowledge protection
requirements.

      The selection of ICHWs shall respect the customs, traditions, and
customary decision-making processes of the concerned ICC/IP
community, while their training and recognition shall be subject to
standards established by the Department of Health. The measure also
provides for continuing training, health insurance and hospitalization
assistance, educational assistance, legal assistance, and appropriate
honoraria and other incentives, subject to existing laws and the
availability of funds.

       The proposed program shall complement, and not diminish,
existing programs for Barangay Health Workers under Republic Act No.
7883. It shall likewise be implemented in coordination with the National
Commission on Indigenous Peoples, local government units, and other
concerned government agencies.

     Through this measure, the State can strengthen community-based
primary health care, improve access to essential health services, and
promote a more responsive and culturally appropriate healthcare system
for Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is
earnestly sought.

                           “AN ACT
       STRENGTHENING THE HEALTHCARE SYSTEM IN
      CULTURAL   AND    INDIGENOUS   COMMUNITIES  BY
      INSTITUTIONALIZING THE TRAINING AND ENGAGEMENT
      OF INDIGENOUS COMMUNITY HEALTH WORKERS AND
      FOR OTHER PURPOSES”

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Tribal
Health Workers Act.”

       SECTION 2. Statement of Policy and Objectives. - It is the
policy of the State to protect and promote the right to health of all
Filipinos and to adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to
health development which shall endeavor to make essential goods,
health, and other social services available to all people at affordable cost,
particularly those residing in geographically isolated and disadvantaged
areas and Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples
(ICCs/IPs).

      The State recognizes the rights of ICCs/IPs to participate in
decisions affecting their communities and to maintain, develop, and
strengthen their traditional health practices consistent with existing
laws, public health standards, and the protection of human health.

       Toward this end, the State shall institutionalize a program for the
training, recognition, and engagement of Indigenous Community Health
Workers who shall complement existing health personnel and facilitate
access to essential health services in underserved indigenous
communities.

       SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the
following words and phrases shall be defined as:

      (a) Barangay Health Worker – refers to a person who has
          undergone health care training programs in any accredited
          government or non-government organization and who
          voluntarily renders primary health care services in the
          community after having been accredited to function as such by
          the local health board in accordance with the guidelines
          promulgated by the Department of Health (DOH), as provided
          under Republic Act No. 7883.

      (b) Indigenous    Cultural   Communities/Indigenous    Peoples
          (ICCs/IPs) -refer to indigenous cultural communities or
          indigenous peoples as defined under Republic Act No. 8371,
          otherwise known as the “Indigenous Peoples’ Rights Act of
          1997,”.

      (c) Indigenous Community Health Worker (ICHW) – refers to a
          qualified member of an ICC/IP community who has undergone
          appropriate health care training provided or recognized by the
          DOH or other authorized government or non-government
          organizations, has been selected or endorsed through an
          appropriate community process, and is recognized or
          accredited to perform community-based health functions
          under this Act.

      (d) Traditional Health Practices – refer to health-related knowledge,
          practices, medicines, and healing traditions traditionally used
          by ICCs/IPs, subject to applicable laws and regulations and
          appropriate public health and safety standards.

      SECTION 4. Indigenous Community Health Workers. – ICC/IP
communities which are geographically isolated, difficult to access,
underserved by existing health facilities or personnel, or otherwise
determined by the DOH to have inadequate access to essential health
services shall be eligible to establish and maintain Indigenous
Community Health Worker positions.

       In determining the number of ICHWs required in a community, the
DOH shall consider the number of families and population, distance and
travel time to the nearest health facility, terrain and accessibility,
availability of existing health personnel and facilities, and other relevant
local conditions.

      The DOH shall establish appropriate standards for determining the
number of ICHWs for each qualified community, taking into account the
need to ensure adequate and accessible community-based health
services.

     SECTION 5. Selection and Training of Indigenous Community
Health Workers. - The ICHW shall be selected or endorsed through the
customary traditions and decision-making processes of the concerned
ICC/IP community, subject to the minimum qualifications and training
standards prescribed by the DOH in consultation with the National
Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP).

      The selected or endorsed ICHW shall undergo the training and
competency requirements prescribed by the DOH before being recognized
or accredited to perform the functions provided under this Act.

      The concerned local health board, in coordination with the NCIP
and the DOH, shall recognize qualified ICHWs in accordance with
applicable rules and regulations. The DOH shall register recognized
ICHWs in the national registry.

      The selection and training of ICHWs shall take into consideration
the language, customs, traditions, health practices, and particular
health needs of the community they serve.

      SECTION 6. Responsibilities of Indigenous Community Health
Workers. - The ICHW shall be responsible for providing community-
based health services to members of the concerned ICC/IP community
within the limits of his or her training and recognition under this Act.

      The ICHW shall perform the following functions:

      (a) Promote health education, disease prevention, sanitation,
          nutrition, maternal and child health, immunization, and other
          public health programs;

      (b) Assist in basic health monitoring and community health
          surveillance as authorized by the DOH;

      (c) Provide basic first aid and other appropriate interventions
          within the limits of his or her training;

      (d) Assist sick or injured persons in obtaining appropriate medical
          care and, when necessary, facilitate their referral and transport
          to the nearest appropriate health center, clinic, hospital, or
          other health facility;

      (e) Assist in emergency response and disaster-related health
          activities within the limits of his or her training;

      (f) Serve as a link between the ICC/IP community and government
          health facilities and personnel; and

      (g) Perform such other community-based health functions as may
          be authorized by the DOH.

      Nothing in this Act shall authorize an ICHW to practice medicine,
nursing, pharmacy, midwifery, or any other regulated health profession
without the license or authority required by law.
       The State shall recognize the value of traditional health practices
of ICCs/IPs. Where appropriate and legally permissible, the ICHW may
assist in the preservation and promotion of traditional health knowledge
and practices, subject to applicable laws, public health standards, and
regulations governing traditional and herbal medicines.

      Community herbal gardens may be established with the assistance
of the barangay, municipal, city, or provincial government, and
appropriate government agencies, subject to applicable health,
environmental, biodiversity, and traditional knowledge protection laws
and regulations.

      SECTION 7. Benefits of Indigenous Community Health
Workers. - Similar to Barangay Health Workers who are provided with
benefits under Republic Act No. 7883, recognized and active ICHWs shall
be granted the following incentives and benefits, subject to applicable
laws, rules and regulations, and the availability of appropriations:

      (a) Health Insurance and Hospitalization Assistance - The ICHW
          shall be provided health insurance coverage and applicable
          government health benefits in accordance with existing laws
          and regulations.

         An ICHW who suffers illness or injury arising from or directly
         connected with the performance of his or her duties shall be
         entitled to appropriate medical and hospitalization assistance
         through programs established by the DOH and other concerned
         government agencies, subject to applicable rules and the
         availability of funds.

      (b) Educational Benefits - Active and qualified ICHWs shall be
          eligible to avail of scholarship, tuition assistance, and other
          educational assistance programs of the national government,
          local government units, and public educational institutions,
          subject to existing eligibility requirements and availability of
          funds.

      (c) Continuing Training - The DOH shall be responsible for
          providing continuing training and competency development for
          ICHWs on primary health care, disease prevention, health
          promotion,    emergency      response,   community   health
          management, and other relevant health concerns, including
          appropriate information on traditional and herbal medicines
          consistent with applicable laws and regulations.

      (d) Free Legal Services - The Public Attorney’s Office or other
          appropriate government legal assistance agencies shall provide
          free legal advice and assistance to an ICHW when the need
          arises from or is directly connected with the lawful exercise of
         his or her duties and responsibilities as an ICHW, subject to
         applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

      (e) Honoraria and Other Incentives - Recognized and active ICHWs
          may be granted honoraria, transportation assistance,
          communication allowance, and such other reasonable
          incentives as may be authorized by law or regulations, subject
          to the availability of funds and existing budgeting, accounting,
          and auditing rules.

      SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The
Department of Health (DOH), the Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), the Department of Environment and Natural
Resources (DENR), and the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples
(NCIP), in coordination with the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
(PhilHealth), the Department of Education (DepEd), concerned local
government units, and representatives of ICCs/IPs, shall be responsible
for the formulation of the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of
this Act which shall be promulgated within one hundred twenty (120)
days from the effectivity of this Act

       The IRR shall provide for, among others, the identification of
qualified communities, selection and recognition of ICHWs, minimum
qualifications and training requirements, scope of functions, benefits and
incentives, coordination with Barangay Health Workers and other health
personnel, monitoring and evaluation mechanisms, and mechanisms for
the protection of traditional health knowledge and practices.

       SECTION 9. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder, or any provision not
affected thereby, shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 10. Non-Impairment Clause. - Nothing in this Act shall
be construed to diminish, impair, or repeal rights, benefits, privileges, or
protections recognized, granted, or made available to ICCs/IPs, Barangay
Health Workers, disadvantaged, marginalized, or specific sectors under
the Constitution and existing laws.

      SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive
orders, proclamations, rules and regulations, and other issuances, or
parts thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 12. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act and shall be sourced from such appropriations and
other funding sources as may be authorized by law.
        Concerned local government units may provide additional funding,
facilities, equipment, transportation, and other forms of assistance for
the implementation of the Indigenous Community Health Worker
Program, subject to existing budgeting, accounting, auditing, and local
government laws, rules, and regulations.

     SECTION 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2)
newspapers of general circulation.
