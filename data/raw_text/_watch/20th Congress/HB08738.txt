                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill underscores the importance of integrating voter education into the junior and
senior high school curricula to prepare the youth for their role in shaping the nation’s
democratic future. By providing students with the knowledge, skills, and values necessary to
participate responsibly in elections, the State seeks to instill a culture of informed citizenship,
accountability, and civic responsibility. Through voter education, students will gain a clear
understanding of the electoral system, the mechanics of voting, and the rights and duties of
citizens, thereby enabling them to make reasoned and conscientious decisions at the ballot box.

        The measure envisions a structured and comprehensive approach to voter education. It
requires the Department of Education (DepEd), in coordination with the Commission on
Elections (COMELEC), to integrate voter education into the junior and senior high school
curricula, ensuring that teachers are properly trained and certified to deliver such instruction.
The curriculum will cover key topics such as suffrage, the electoral system and processes, the
principles of educated voting, and supplementary studies on current political, social, and
economic issues that influence the conduct of elections. By doing so, the program aims to equip
students with the practical knowledge and critical thinking skills needed to uphold the integrity
of the electoral process and strengthen democratic governance in the country.

        Moreover, the bill mandates that all schools maintain adequate educational materials
on voter education, including textbooks, primers, and leaflets, to ensure students have access
to reliable information. The State recognizes that early exposure to voter education not only
empowers the youth to participate meaningfully in elections but also cultivates a generation of
civic-minded and principled voters who can discern wisely, resist misinformation, and promote
transparency and accountability in public office.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

       AN ACT INTEGRATING VOTER EDUCATION IN THE JUNIOR AND SENIOR
                         HIGH SCHOOL CURRICULA

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

1            SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Compulsory Voter Education
2    Act of 2026.”

3            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to enlighten
4    citizens on their right to vote and to inculcate in them the duty to preserve the integrity of the
5    electoral process through the educational system.

 6           SEC. 3. Definition of Voter Education. – As used in this Act, “Voter education’ means
 7   dissemination of information, materials and programs designed to inform citizens about the
 8   specifics and mechanics of the voting process. It includes providing information on the right to
 9   suffrage, its importance, and the proper exercise thereof, as well as information on who is eligible
10   to vote; where and how to register; how electors can check the voter lists to ensure they have been
11   duly included; what type of elections are being held; where, when and how to vote; who the
12   candidates are; and how to file complaints.

13           SEC. 4. Integration of Voter Education in the Junior and Senior High School Curricula.
14   – Voter Education shall be integrated by the Department of Education (DepEd) in the junior and
15   senior high school curricula, effective at the start of the school year immediately following one (1)
16   year from the effectivity of this Act. The one (1) year transition period by conducting seminars
17   and trainings on the electoral system and process. COMELEC shall certify that the teacher, after
18   attending such seminars and trainings, is qualified to teach topics relating to the electoral system
19   and process.

20          SEC. 5. Voter Education Syllabus. – This section outlines the core components of the
21   Voter Education Syllabus, presenting the following:

22               A. Voter education. – The main syllabus shall include, but not limited to, the following
23                  thematic areas:


                                                                                                        1
 1                           1. Suffrage. – This area introduces to students the right to vote accorded
 2                              by the Constitution, and other rights enabling citizens to participate in
 3                              the conduct of public affairs;
 4                           2. Electoral system and process. – This area deals with the general
 5                              principles underlying the present electoral system, and legislation
 6                              regulating all the phases of election namely, pre-election, election
 7                              proper, and post-election. It discusses the proper conduct of elections
 8                              including registration, casting of ballots, and counting of votes;
 9                           3. Educated voting. – This area focuses on the attitude and behavior a
10                              voter must adopt to ensure fair, free, and honest elections. It emphasizes
11                              the importance of participating in elections in a manner that shall
12                              stimulate and strengthen democracy.
13
14               B. Supplementary topics. – This curriculum should have additional topics and studies
15                  on the political system of the country, the contextual background of elections,
16                  current political, social, and economic issues affecting the elections, and the
17                  possible solutions to address them.
18               C. Other subject matters. – Other areas of voter education may be included in the
19                  syllabus.

20           SEC. 6. Implementing Agency. – The Secretary of Education is hereby authorized and
21   directed to adopt a system to implement and carry out the provisions of this Act. The Secretary of
22   Education shall, within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act, to promulgate the rules and
23   regulations, including those of a disciplinary nature, to carry out and enforce the provisions of this
24   Act.

25            SEC. 7. Educational Materials. – It shall be obligatory on all schools offering secondary
26   education to keep in their libraries an adequate number of copies of the textbooks, primers, and
27   leaflets on voter education to be issued by the DepEd. The DepEd shall determine the adequacy of
28   the number of books, depending upon the enrolment of the school.

29           SEC. 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
30   unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in full force and
31   effect.

32           SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, and rules and regulations
33   inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

34           SEC. 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
35   publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
