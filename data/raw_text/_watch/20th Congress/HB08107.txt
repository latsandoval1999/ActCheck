                              AN ACT
EXPRESSLY EXEMPTING QUALIFIED CLIENTS OF THE LEGAL AID OFFICES OF
THE INTEGRATED BAR OF THE PHILIPPINES FROM PAYMENT OF DOCKET AND
OTHER LEGAL FEES IN COURTS AND QUASI-JUDICIAL BODIES, PROVIDING FOR
COORDINATION WITH THE SUPREME COURT AND THE UNIFIED LEGAL AID
SERVICE, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution guarantees that no Filipino should be denied access to the courts
simply because they are poor. To carry out this promise, the Supreme Court has issued
rules that allow indigent litigants, especially those assisted by legal aid lawyers, to be
exempt from paying court fees. These rules are meant to ensure that people with valid
cases can seek justice without being blocked by costs they cannot afford.

Despite these rules, many poor litigants assisted by the Integrated Bar of the
Philippines (IBP) still face real problems. Some courts are unfamiliar with the
exemption, while others require documents that are costly or dif icult to obtain.
Unlike clients of the Public Attorney’s Of ice, who have a clear law granting fee
exemptions, IBP legal aid clients often rely only on court rules, which are not always
applied consistently, especially in quasi-judicial bodies and administrative
proceedings.

This bill solves that problem by clearly stating in law that quali ied IBP legal aid clients
are exempt from paying docket and other lawful legal fees. It builds on existing
Supreme Court standards to make the process uniform, fair, and protected against
abuse, while keeping safeguards such as the right to question false claims of indigency.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “IBP Legal Aid Fee
Exemption Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to ensure free access
to the courts and quasi-judicial bodies and that adequate legal assistance shall
not be denied by reason of poverty, and to remove inancial barriers that prevent
indigent litigants from pursuing meritorious claims or defenses.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   (a) IBP Legal Aid Of ice refers to the legal aid of ices in local chapters of the
       Integrated Bar of the Philippines and/or legal aid mechanisms recognized by
       the IBP in accordance with Supreme Court issuances.

   (b) Quali ied IBP Legal Aid Client refers to an indigent and deserving person
       who is accepted as a client by an IBP Legal Aid Of ice after compliance with

       the means and merit tests and other eligibility requirements under
       applicable Supreme Court rules and IBP legal aid rules, as may be updated.

   (c) Legal Fees include docket, iling, sheriff’s, mediation-related, and other lawful
       fees incidental to instituting or defending an action or proceeding, whether as
       an original action or on appeal, in courts and, to the extent allowed by the rules
       of the forum, in quasi-judicial bodies.

SECTION 4. Statutory Exemption from Legal Fees for Quali ied IBP Legal Aid
Clients. — A Quali ied IBP Legal Aid Client shall be exempt from payment of
docket and other legal fees incidental to instituting or defending an action or
proceeding in:

   (a) All courts; and
   (b) Quasi-judicial bodies, to the extent permitted by their governing statutes and
       rules.

SECTION 5. Proof of Indigency; Certi ication; Contest. —

   (a) Suf icient proof. A Certi ication of Indigency issued by a covered lawyer in
       accordance with the Supreme Court’s Uni ied Legal Aid Service (ULAS) rules,
       or its successor rules, shall be suf icient for the court to issue the authority of
       exemption from payment of docket and other lawful fees under Rule 3, Section
       21 of the Rules of Court.

   (b) Right to contest. The adverse party may contest the grant of the authority of
       exemption before judgment, consistent with ULAS and Rule 3, Section 21
       practice.

   (c) Lien on favorable judgment. The amount of legal fees from which the
       indigent was exempted shall be a lien on any favorable judgment, unless the
       court otherwise provides, consistent with existing rules.

SECTION 6. Coordination with the Supreme Court and the Integrated Bar of the
Philippines. — Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the IBP shall
coordinate with the Supreme Court, through the Of ice of the Court Administrator and
such of ices as may be designated, to ensure harmonized implementation consistent
with Supreme Court rules on legal aid, exemption from legal fees, and court
procedure.

SECTION 7. Non-Diminution of Existing Exemptions; PAO Clients. — Nothing in
this Act shall be construed to diminish exemptions already granted by law or Supreme
Court rule, including the statutory exemption of Public Attorney’s Of ice (PAO) clients
from docket and other fees.

SECTION 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 10. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
