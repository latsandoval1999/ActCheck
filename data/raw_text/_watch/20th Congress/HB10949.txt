    AN ACT ESTABLISHING A GRADUATED, AGE-CALIBRATED

                          EXPLANATORY NOTE




When Republic Act No. 9344, or the "Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of
2006," was enacted, it represented a deliberate and well-intentioned shift
toward a child-centered, restorative approach to juvenile justice, raising
the minimum age of criminal responsibility (MACR) from nine (9) to fifteen
(15) years old and placing children in conflict with the law (CICL) at the
center of intervention, diversion, and rehabilitation rather than
punishment. That policy shift was, and remains, consistent with the
State's obligations under the United Nations Convention on the Rights of
the Child and with the constitutional mandate to protect and promote the
rights of children to assistance, proper care, and special protection from
all forms of neglect, abuse, cruelty, exploitation, and other conditions
prejudicial to their development.


Nearly two decades later, the law as currently calibrated did not anticipate
and has struggled to respond to a disturbing pattern of petty and more
recently heinous crimes committed by children who, because of the
blanket exemption enjoyed by those fifteen (15) years old and below, are
shielded from criminal liability regardless of the gravity of the act or the
clarity of intent behind it. This gap has become impossible to ignore in
light of a string of recent, well-documented incidents involving children in
conflict with the law:




                                    Page 1
On June 16, 2026, a 14-year-old Grade 8 student armed with a kitchen
knife entered a Grade 5 classroom at a private school in General Trias City,
Cavite, and stabbed seven (7) young pupils, two of whom required surgery.
On June 22, 2026, two students aged 14 and 15 opened fire inside San
Jose National High School in Tacloban City, killing three (3) fellow students
and wounding twenty (20) others in what has been described as one of the
deadliest school-related attacks in the country's recent history.
On June 23, 2026, a 17-year-old student stabbed a fellow 17-year-old
student outside Julio Ledesma National High School in San Carlos City,
Negros Occidental, arising from a personal grudge, leaving the victim
seriously wounded.
On July 31, 2026, a 14-year-old student fatally stabbed his 13-year-old
classmate inside Las Piñas National High School over a trivial dispute
involving ten pesos (Php10.00) in change, prompting the school to suspend
face-to-face classes and review how a bladed weapon evaded metal
detectors and security personnel.
Most recently, on August 18, 2026, a 14-year-old Grade 9 student brought
firearms onto the campus of the Ateneo de Zamboanga University in
Zamboanga City, shot and killed a fellow student, wounded several others,
and then turned the gun on himself — an attack that was livestreamed on
social media and that has been described as the country's second fatal
school shooting in less than two months.


These incidents, taken together with the increasing frequency of law
enforcement interceptions of children carrying firearms, knives, and other
bladed weapons on their way to school, reveal that a rigid, single-threshold
exemption no longer reflects the reality on the ground. A significant
number of children between the ages of nine (9) and seventeen (17) are
demonstrably capable of forming intent, understanding the wrongfulness
of grave acts, and, in the most extreme cases, planning and executing
them; circumstances that the pre-2006 regime under the Revised Penal
Code was designed to address through the concept of "discernment" and
the privileged mitigating circumstance of minority, without abandoning
the child's constitutional and statutory protections.
This bill does not seek to undo the restorative and rehabilitative
philosophy that undergirds Republic Act No. 9344. Diversion, restorative
justice, confidentiality of records, the prohibition against detention of
children in regular jails alongside adults, the automatic suspension of
sentence, access to Bahay Pag-Asa and comprehensive intervention
programs, and the child's right to counsel and to be heard remain fully
intact under this measure.
What this bill proposes is a more calibrated, three-tiered framework
restoring the pre-Republic Act No. 9344 age brackets under Article 12 of
the Revised Penal Code under which: (i) children below nine (9) years of
age remain absolutely exempt from criminal liability and are referred to
intervention programs; (ii) children nine (9) years old up to below fifteen


                                    Page 2
(15) years old are conditionally liable, subject to a threshold inquiry into
whether they acted with discernment; and (iii) children fifteen (15) years
old up to below eighteen (18) years old are subject to the full weight of
criminal responsibility, tempered by the privileged mitigating
circumstance of minority and continued access to suspended sentence
and rehabilitative disposition.


This measure seeks to strike the balance the public is demanding: one that
neither treats a nine-year-old as a hardened offender nor allows a heinous,
premeditated act by a sixteen-year-old to go entirely unanswered by the
criminal justice system, while preserving every child-sensitive and
restorative safeguard that Republic Act No. 9344 has built over the past
two decades. In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly
requested.
The passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

  THE LAW AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTIONS 6 AND 7 OF
  REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9344, AS AMENDED BY REPUBLIC ACT NO.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:


     SECTION 1. This Act shall be known as the "Graduated Juvenile
Accountability Act."

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to
protect and promote the rights of children, to provide for a system of
juvenile justice and welfare that is child-sensitive, restorative, and
consistent with the country's obligations under the United Nations
Convention on the Rights of the Child, the United Nations Standard
Minimum Rules for the Administration of Juvenile Justice (the "Beijing
Rules"), and the United Nations Guidelines for the Prevention of Juvenile
Delinquency (the "Riyadh Guidelines"). At the same time, the State
recognizes its equally compelling duty to maintain peace and order, protect
the public — including other children — from serious harm, and ensure
that accountability is graduated according to a child's demonstrated
capacity for discernment and moral responsibility, rather than fixed at a
single, undifferentiated age threshold. Towards this end, the State shall
adopt a graduated, age-calibrated framework of liability for children in
conflict with the law (CICL), without diminishing the restorative,
diversionary, and rehabilitative safeguards already guaranteed under
Republic Act No. 9344, as amended.

     SECTION 3. Section 6 of Republic Act No. 9344, as amended by
Republic Act No. 10630, is hereby further amended to read as follows:
"SEC. 6. Minimum Age of Criminal Responsibility. — In lieu of the age
brackets previously provided, the following graduated, age-calibrated
framework of liability shall apply to a child in conflict with the law:


                                   Page 4
      (a) Absolute Exemption. — A child nine (9) years of age or under at
the time of the commission of the offense shall be absolutely exempt from
criminal liability. Such child shall instead undergo the appropriate
intervention program under Section 20 of this Act, and shall in all cases
remain in the custody and care of his or her parents, guardian, or the
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), as the case may
be.

       (b) Conditional Liability Based on Discernment. — A child above
nine (9) years of age but below fifteen (15) years of age at the time of the
commission of the offense shall be exempt from criminal liability, unless
it is shown that he or she acted with discernment, in accordance with the
procedure laid down in Section 7 of this Act, in which case such child shall
be subjected to the appropriate proceedings in accordance with this Act,
including, where qualified, diversion under Sections 23 to 32 hereof.

      (c) Full Criminal Responsibility Subject to Privileged Mitigating
Circumstance. — A child fifteen (15) years of age or over but below
eighteen (18) years of age at the time of the commission of the offense shall
be subject to the full force and effect of the appropriate criminal
proceedings and, where warranted, to conviction:

       Provided, That such child shall in every case be credited with the
privileged mitigating circumstance of minority under Article 13, paragraph
2, and Article 68 of the Revised Penal Code, or the equivalent provision of
the special penal law violated, where applicable:

      Provided, further, That the child shall remain entitled to diversion
under this Act where qualified, and to automatic suspension of sentence
under Section 38 of this Act where diversion is inapplicable or
unsuccessful. The determination of the child's age shall be based on the
child's birth certificate, baptismal certificate, or any other pertinent
document. In the absence of these documents, the child's age may be
based on information from the child, testimony of other persons, physical
appearance, and other relevant evidence, in accordance with Section 7 of
this Act. In case of doubt as to the age of the child, it shall be resolved in
his or her favor. Nothing in this Section shall be construed to authorize
the imposition of the death penalty or life imprisonment, or reclusion
perpetua without the benefit of parole, upon any child in conflict with the
law, consistent with Section 5 of the Convention on the Rights of the Child
and Section 21 of Republic Act No. 9344."

      SECTION 4. Section 7 of Republic Act No. 9344 is hereby amended
to read as follows: "SEC. 7. Determination of Age and Discernment. — The
age of a child may be determined in the manner set forth in the preceding
Section. Where a child covered by Section 6(b) of this Act is alleged to have
acted with discernment, the determination of discernment shall
differentiate a discerning child from a non-discerning one, and shall take
into consideration all relevant circumstances, such as the appreciation of
the consequences of the wrongful act, the context of the child's
environment, upbringing, and the degree of criminal sophistication
involved in the planning and execution of the offense, based on the



                                     Page 5
assessment of a social worker duly appointed as the child's guardian ad
litem or, in the absence of the latter, a social worker of the Department of
Social Welfare and Development. The child in conflict with the law shall
enjoy the presumption of minority and shall enjoy all the rights of a child
in conflict with the law until proven guilty, and shall be treated with
respect and dignity throughout the proceedings, consistent with Sections
5 and 22 of this Act."

      SECTION 5. Continued Application of Diversion and Restorative
Justice. — Nothing in this Act shall be construed to repeal, diminish, or
otherwise affect the system of diversion, mediation, family conferencing,
and other restorative justice processes under Sections 23 to 32 of Republic
Act No. 9344, which shall remain available, as a matter of right where the
imposable penalty does not exceed the threshold set therein, to children
in conflict with the law falling under Section 6(b) and 6(c) of this Act, as
amended.

      SECTION 6. Continued Application of Other Safeguards. — Except
as expressly amended by this Act, all other provisions of Republic Act No.
9344, as amended by Republic Act No. 10630, shall remain in full force
and effect, including but not limited to: (a) the prohibition against the
detention of children in conflict with the law in jails or detention cells
together with adults, and the corresponding mandate that such children
be committed to a youth detention home or Bahay Pag-asa under Sections
20 and 61; (b) the automatic suspension of sentence in favor of a qualified
child in conflict with the law under Section 38; (c) the confidentiality of
records and proceedings involving children in conflict with the law under
Section 43; (d) the prohibition against labeling and shaming under Section
60; (e) the right to counsel, to be heard, and to be assisted by a social
worker at every stage of the proceedings under Section 5; (f) the
comprehensive local juvenile intervention and diversion programs required
of local government units under Republic Act No. 10630; and (g) the
establishment and maintenance of Bahay Pag-asa facilities and the
regional juvenile justice and welfare councils. This Act shall be interpreted
and applied in a manner that harmonizes accountability with the child-
sensitive and restorative processes already guaranteed under existing law.

      SECTION 7. Strengthened Intervention Programs. — In view of the
expanded population of children who may now be subject to conditional
or full liability under Section 6(b) and 6(c) of this Act, the Department of
Social Welfare and Development, in coordination with the Juvenile Justice
and Welfare Council (JJWC), the Department of Education, and concerned
local government units, shall strengthen and adequately fund community-
based intervention programs, mental health and psychosocial services,
and Bahay Pag-asa facilities, to ensure that graduated liability under this
Act is matched by graduated, evidence-based, and rehabilitative
intervention, and not by mere incarceration.

      SECTION 8. Review and Reporting. — The Juvenile Justice and
Welfare Council shall submit an annual report to Congress on the
implementation of this Act, including but not limited to the number of
children processed under each of the three (3) age brackets under Section


                                    Page 6
6, as amended, the outcomes of discernment inquiries, diversion rates,
and recidivism data, to inform future legislative review of the minimum
age of criminal responsibility.

     SECTION 9. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to carry out
the strengthened intervention programs, Bahay Pag-asa facilities, and
capacity-building activities required under this Act shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act, and augmented, as necessary,
from the Local Government Support Fund, the Special Local Government
Support Fund, and other funds available to local government units for
juvenile intervention programs under Republic Act No. 10630.

       SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Juvenile Justice and Welfare
Council, in coordination with the Department of Social Welfare and
Development, the Department of Justice, the Department of the Interior
and Local Government, the Philippine National Police, and the Department
of Education, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.

      SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If, for any reason, any
provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other
provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

     SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. — Section 6 of Republic Act No.
9344, as amended by Republic Act No. 10630, and all other laws, decrees,
executive orders, administrative orders, rules, and regulations, or parts
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly. All other provisions of
Republic Act No. 9344, as amended, not inconsistent herewith, shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
