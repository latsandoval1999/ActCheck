13                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE
14
15   The Constitution directs the State to promote social justice and to protect senior
16   citizens and persons with disability through measures that recognize their particular
17   needs and circumstances. Congress has given concrete expression to this policy
18   through the mandatory discounts and privileges provided under Republic Act No.
19   7432, as amended, for senior citizens, and Republic Act No. 7277, as amended, for
20   persons with disability.
21
22   When Republic Act No. 7432 was enacted in 1992, Congress required private
23   establishments to grant the statutory senior citizen discount but allowed them to claim
24   the cost of the discount as a tax credit. The Supreme Court subsequently confirmed
25   that the credit contemplated by the original law was not a mere deduction from gross
26   income. In Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Central Luzon Drug Corporation,
27   G.R. No. 159647, April 15, 2005, the Court held that a tax credit operates after the tax
28   is computed and reduces the taxpayer's liability directly, while a tax deduction merely
29   reduces the income upon which the tax is computed.
30
31   The policy changed with Republic Act No. 9257 and was retained under Republic Act
32   No. 9994. Instead of a full tax credit, establishments granting senior citizen discounts
33   were allowed to claim the discount only as a deduction from gross income. Republic
34   Act No. 10754 adopted the same tax-deduction treatment for the statutory discounts
35   granted to persons with disability. The difference is substantial. A peso of tax credit
36   reduces tax liability by a peso; a peso of deduction produces tax relief only to the extent
37   of the applicable tax rate. The unrecovered portion of the statutory discount is
38   therefore borne by the private establishment.
39
40   The Supreme Court has sustained the validity of the tax-deduction scheme as an
41   exercise of the State's police power. Carlos Superdrug Corporation v. Department of
42   Social Welfare and Development, G.R. No. 166494, June 29, 2007; Manila Memorial
43   Park, Inc. v. Secretary of the Department of Social Welfare and Development, G.R. No.
44   175356, December 3, 2013. This measure does not proceed from the premise that the
45   present arrangement is unconstitutional. Rather, it makes a deliberate legislative
46   judgment that what the State may validly require private establishments to bear need
47   not remain the most equitable or sustainable way of financing a public social benefit.



                                                   1
 1   Senior citizen and PWD discounts are benefits created by law for a public purpose.
 2   Private establishments are indispensable partners in delivering those benefits at the
 3   point of sale, but the ultimate fiscal responsibility for a government-mandated social
 4   benefit should rest with the Government. Restoring the full tax credit treatment does
 5   not reduce, condition, or diminish the discount received by the beneficiary. It changes
 6   only the manner by which the cost of that benefit is allocated after the transaction has
 7   been completed.
 8
 9   The measure is intentionally limited to the mandatory discounts granted under the
10   senior citizen and PWD laws. It does not automatically alter the tax treatment of
11   student fare discounts, athlete incentives, solo parent privileges, or other statutory
12   benefits. Any extension of the same policy to other social discounts should be
13   separately considered by Congress together with the corresponding fiscal impact. The
14   bill also addresses the practical difficulties that attended the earlier tax-credit regime.
15   The credit is limited to the statutory discount actually and finally borne by the
16   claimant. Where the underlying transaction is VAT-exempt, the credit is computed on
17   the VAT-exclusive selling price and does not include VAT that was never imposed or
18   collected. Any rebate, reimbursement, subsidy, cost-sharing payment, or similar
19   recovery received or receivable with respect to the same discount is deducted from the
20   amount creditable. The same discount may not be claimed twice as a deduction, credit,
21   refund, or other tax incentive.
22
23   Recognizing that the value of a tax credit depends upon the establishment's capacity
24   to use it, this measure also provides a meaningful mechanism for unutilized credits.
25   The credit may be applied against national internal revenue taxes for which the
26   establishment is directly liable, excluding withholding taxes. Micro and small
27   taxpayers may elect a cash refund of any verified unutilized balance after the taxable
28   year in which the credit arose. Medium and large taxpayers may carry the credit
29   forward and, if it remains unutilized after the prescribed period, may likewise seek a
30   refund. This ensures that the promise of full reimbursement does not benefit only
31   establishments with large and immediate tax liabilities.
32
33   A full tax credit necessarily entails a greater fiscal cost to the National Government
34   than a deduction. For this reason, the bill couples reimbursement with clear
35   substantiation, anti-duplication safeguards, electronic reporting for larger taxpayers,
36   simplified compliance for smaller establishments, and annual reporting by the
37   Department of Finance and the Bureau of Internal Revenue to Congress on the amount
38   of discounts granted, credits claimed, credits utilized, refunds paid, and the resulting
39   revenue impact. Fiscal responsibility requires both fair allocation of the cost and
40   transparent accounting of that cost.
41
42   This measure therefore restores the central policy of the original Republic Act No.
43   7432 while adapting its implementation to the present tax system: senior citizens and
44   persons with disability continue to receive the full benefits guaranteed by law; private
45   establishments continue to deliver those benefits; and the National Government
46   assumes, through a verifiable tax-credit and refund mechanism, the cost of the
47   statutory discounts actually and finally borne by the establishments.

28   SECTION 1. Short Title. —
29      This Act shall be known as the “Social Benefits Fiscal Responsibility Act.”
30
31   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State
32   to preserve and fully protect the mandatory discounts and privileges granted by law to
33   senior citizens and persons with disability, while ensuring a fair, transparent, and
34   sustainable allocation of the fiscal cost of such benefits.
35
36       The State recognizes that private establishments required by law to grant such
37   discounts perform a public function in the implementation of social legislation. As a
38   matter of fiscal policy, the National Government shall assume the cost of the statutory
39   discounts actually and finally borne by such establishments through a full tax credit,
40   subject to reasonable safeguards against duplicate claims, overstatement, and fraud.
41
42        The State shall further ensure that the tax-credit mechanism operates equitably
43   for establishments of different sizes by allowing the utilization, carryover, and, where
44   authorized under this Act, refund of verified unutilized credits, with simplified
45   compliance for micro and small taxpayers and appropriate reporting and verification
46   requirements for medium and large taxpayers.

 8   SECTION 7. Minimum Substantiation, Anti-Duplication, and Reporting
 9   Requirements. — A claim for tax credit under this Act shall be supported by records
10   sufficient to establish the qualified transaction, the statutory discount granted, and the
11   amount actually and finally borne by the claimant. At a minimum, the records shall
12   contain or permit verification of the following:
13
14        a) the registered invoice or other document required under the National
15           Internal Revenue Code, including its number and date;
16        b) the selling price or charge before application of the statutory discount and,
17           where applicable, the VAT-exclusive selling price;
18        c) the amount of the statutory discount granted and the net amount paid by the
19           beneficiary;
20        d) the identification number or other unique reference from a valid proof of
21           entitlement authorized under the applicable senior citizen or PWD law,
22           subject to Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act
23           of 2012,” and other applicable privacy laws;
24        e) the amount of any rebate, reimbursement, subsidy, cost-sharing payment, or
25           similar recovery received or receivable in respect of the same discount; and
26        f) such other information as is reasonably necessary to prevent duplicate or
27           fraudulent claims, provided that no requirement shall diminish the
28           substantive tax credit granted by this Act.
29
30       Medium and large taxpayers, as classified under Section 21(B) of the National
31   Internal Revenue Code, shall electronically report qualified discount transactions in
32   such periodic schedule as may be prescribed by the Bureau of Internal Revenue. Micro
33   and small taxpayers shall be subject to simplified substantiation and reporting
34   procedures consistent with the Ease of Paying Taxes Act and the policy of reducing
35   compliance costs for smaller taxpayers.
36
37       No two or more persons or entities may claim tax credits for the same portion of a
38   statutory discount. Any fraudulent, fictitious, duplicated, or materially overstated
39   claim shall be subject to assessment, interest, penalties, and criminal liability under
40   the National Internal Revenue Code and other applicable laws.

21   SECTION 9. Non-Diminution of Benefits. — Nothing in this Act shall be
22   construed to reduce, diminish, impair, condition, or otherwise modify any discount,
23   exemption, privilege, or benefit presently granted by law to senior citizens or persons
24   with disability. This Act concerns solely the fiscal treatment and reimbursement of the
25   statutory discounts granted by private establishments under the laws expressly
26   amended herein.
