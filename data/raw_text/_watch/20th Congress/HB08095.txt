                           AN ACT
MANDATING THE ESTABLISHMENT OF COMMUNITY READING CENTERS
IN EVERY BARANGAY, STRENGTHENING COMMUNITY-BASED LITERACY
 SUPPORT, PROVIDING INCENTIVES FOR LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS
     AND PRIVATE STAKEHOLDERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II)
identified a severe foundational learning crisis in the Philippines, particularly in
early-grade literacy. A significant number of Filipino learners in Kindergarten to
Grade 3 are unable to read at grade level, undermining long-term educational
outcomes, workforce readiness, and national development.

       EDCOMII emphasized that schools alone cannot resolve the literacy crisis.
It called for stronger community-based interventions, expanded local
government unit (LGU) participation, and whole-of-society approaches to
foundational learning. Barangays, as the most basic political units, are uniquely
positioned to provide accessible and localized reading support that complements
schooling.

      This measure seeks to institutionalize Community Reading Centers in
every barangay as non-formal, supplementary learning hubs focused on
foundational literacy. At the community level, improved reading skills are
expected to lead to better school attendance, reduced grade repetition, and lower
dropout rates, particularly among learners from disadvantaged households.
Community Reading Centers likewise enable barangays to take an active role in
human capital development by promoting volunteerism and partnerships with
schools, civil society, and local businesses.

      By adopting a results-oriented, incentive-based approach, this measure
affirms that literacy development is a shared responsibility of government,
communities, and the private sector. Community Reading Centers are
envisioned not merely as physical spaces, but as collaborative platforms that
enhance local governance, improve service delivery, and support inclusive and
sustainable community development, fully aligned with the Department of
Education (DepEd) standards and existing literacy programs.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

 Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Community
Reading Centers and Foundational Literacy Support Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to prioritize foundational literacy as a cornerstone of quality education. The State
shall promote community-based, inclusive, and collaborative approaches to
improve reading proficiency, strengthen the role of LGUs in education support
among young learners and non-readers, strengthen the role of local government
units in education support, ensure coordination with existing education
programs, and encourage private sector and civil society participation in
advancing literacy.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

   1. Community Reading Center (CRC) - refers to a barangay-based, non-
      formal learning facility that provides structured reading support,
      remediation, and literacy activities for Kindergarten to Grade 3 learners
      and other identified non-readers;
  2. Foundational Literacy – refers to the ability to read with understanding
     and fluency appropriate to early grade levels;

  3. Reading Facilitator – refers to a trained individual who conducts reading
     sessions and literacy interventions in a Community Reading Center;

  4. Private Supporting Establishments - refer to private entities, including
     corporations, partnerships, cooperatives, and sole proprietorships, that
     provide financial, material, or technical assistance to Community Reading
     Centers.

SECTION 4. Phased Establishment of Community Reading Centers.

  1. Community Reading Centers shall be established nationwide, subject to
     phased and prioritized implementation.

  2. Priority shall be given to barangays with high prevalence of non-readers,
     low literacy proficiency, geographically isolated, and disadvantaged areas,
     and other criteria to be determined by DepEd and DILG.

  3. Community Reading Centers may be housed in existing facilities such as
     barangay halls, day care centers, public or private schools after class
     hours, libraries, or other suitable community spaces. No new building
     shall be required unless existing facilities are unavailable.

SECTION 5. Functions of Community Reading Centers. – Community
Reading Centers shall:

  1. Provide reading remediation and enrichment programs aligned with
     DepEd-approved literacy frameworks, including existing programs such as
     Early Language, Literacy and Numeracy (ELLN) and similar initiatives;

  2. Support learners in Kindergarten to Grade 3 and other identified
     struggling or non-readers;

  3. Conduct or support regular reading assessments in coordination with
     partner schools;

  4. Engage parents, guardians, and community volunteers in literacy
     activities;

  5. Serve as venues for reading camps, storytelling sessions, and community-
     based literacy programs.
SECTION 6. Reading Facilitators and Capacity Building.

  1. Reading activities shall be conducted by trained Reading Facilitators, who
     may include Alternative Learning System (ALS) teachers, public school
     teachers on voluntary assignment, barangay child development workers,
     or accredited volunteers.

  2. The DepEd, in coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local
     Government (DILG) and the Technical Education and Skills Development
     Authority (TESDA), shall develop and implement training modules and
     capacity-building programs for Reading Facilitators.

SECTION 7. Integration with Public and Private Schools.

  1. Community Reading Centers shall complement and support existing
     public and private school literacy programs and shall not replace formal
     instruction.

  2. Schools are encouraged to partner with Community Reading Centers for
     learner referral, reading diagnostics, remediation, and progress
     monitoring.

  3. Learner progress data generated by Community Reading Centers shall be
     shared with schools, subject to existing data privacy laws.



SECTION 8. Roles of Local Government Units.
  1. Barangays shall serve as primary implementers of Community Reading
     Centers.

  2. Cities and municipalities shall provide technical, administrative, and
     logistical support to barangays, including access to training, learning
     materials, and coordination with schools and national agencies.


SECTION 9. Incentives for Barangays.

  1. Barangays with operational and compliant Community Reading Centers
     demonstrating measurable improvements in reading outcomes shall be
     eligible for incentives, including but not limited to:

     a. Additional points or recognition under the Seal of Good Local
        Governance (SGLG) or other similar performance-based assessment
        mechanism;
      b. Priority access to literacy-related grants, training programs, and
         educational assistance from national government agencies;
      c. Public recognition through awards or certification programs
         administered by the DILG and DepEd.

   2. Incentives shall be results-oriented and         shall   consider   learner
      participation and demonstrated reading gains.

SECTION 10. Incentives for Private Supporting Establishments.

   1. Private Supporting Establishments that donate books, learning materials,
      equipment, facilities, or financial support to accredited Community
      Reading Centers shall be entitled to tax incentives in accordance with
      existing provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended.

   2. The Department of Finance (DOF) and the Bureau of Internal Revenue
      (BIR) shall promulgate the necessary rules to implement the tax incentives
      provided herein, ensuring fiscal responsibility and transparency.

SECTION 11. Funding.
   1. Funding for Community Reading Centers shall be sourced from the
      Special Education Fund (SEF), national government appropriations
      subject to the General Appropriations Act, and grants or donations from
      private and civil society partners.

   2. Barangays shall not be required to fund Community Reading Centers
      solely from their regular funds.

SECTION 12. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Congressional Oversight.
   1. The DepEd, in coordination with the DILG, shall establish a monitoring
      and evaluation system focused on literacy outcomes, learner
      participation, and program effectiveness.

   2. A consolidated report on the implementation and the outcomes of this
      Act shall be submitted to Congress every three (3) years.

SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, in coordination with DILG, DOF,
TESDA, and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary
implementing rules and regulations.
SECTION 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not otherwise affected
shall remain in full force and effect.
SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, orders, rules, and regulations, other
issuances, or any part thereof, inconsistent with any provision of this Act, are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
