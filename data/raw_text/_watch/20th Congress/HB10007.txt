                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

This bill seeks to ensure that farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives, and agricultural enterprises are not only
provided with agricultural and fisheries machinery, but are also given access to the necessary repair
manuals, service information, diagnostic tools, and basic technical guidance needed to properly
operate, maintain, and repair such equipment. At present, the lack of accessible manuals and repair
information often forces farmers to hire the services of mechanics even for basic troubleshooting or
minor repairs, resulting in additional costs and delays in farm operations. With the availability of
proper manuals, repair guides, and technical orientation, farmers and fisherfolk will be better equipped
to maintain their machinery, reduce unnecessary expenses, and prevent avoidable downtime. In this
regard, the role of the Department of Agriculture should not be limited to the distribution of machinery
and equipment, but must also include education, training, and capacity-building to ensure that
beneficiaries can fully and effectively use the machinery provided to them.

Farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives, and agricultural enterprises rely on tractors, harvesters, irrigation
equipment, dryers, cold storage units, sensors, and other machinery to sustain production, reduce
losses, and support food security.

According to the Philippine Institute for Development Studies and International Rice Research
Institute (PIDS–IRRI), agricultural mechanization must be evaluated not only in terms of production,
but also in relation to employment, income distribution, and the public and private support needed for
an effective mechanization strategy. The study likewise recognized that mechanization policy should
be responsive to the needs of rural communities and small farmers.

A study by Elepaño et al. shows that the level of agricultural mechanization in the Philippines remains
relatively low, with many farm operations still performed manually. They also emphasized that small
and medium-scale farmers may benefit from machinery pooling, custom hiring services, and
cooperative arrangements that allow them to use farm machines without individually owning costly
equipment.

According to a study by Severino, cooperatives can help promote mechanization among smallholding
farms through information dissemination, education, training, and shared machinery services.
However, the study also identified financial barriers, preference for manual labor, lack of appropriate
technology, and weaknesses in cooperative operations as continuing challenges to mechanization
adoption.

While Republic Act No. 10601, or the Agricultural and Fisheries Mechanization Law, already
promotes mechanization through standards, testing, registration, after-sales service, warranty
requirements, repair service centers, and related support programs, access to machinery alone is not
sufficient. Farmers and fisherfolk must also be able to keep such machinery operational. When basic
repairs are delayed because manuals, diagnostic tools, spare parts, firmware support, or service
information are unavailable, agricultural users face higher costs, avoidable downtime, and possible
crop or post-harvest losses.

This bill complements Republic Act No. 10601 by addressing a specific post-acquisition concern: the
right of equipment owners and independent repair providers to lawfully diagnose, maintain, and repair
agricultural and fisheries machinery. It requires fair and reasonable access to repair manuals,
diagnostic tools, wiring diagrams, calibration procedures, error codes, service bulletins, firmware tools
necessary for lawful repair, and spare parts.

At the same time, the bill protects safety, intellectual property, data privacy, equipment integrity, and
lawful restrictions against tampering with emissions, security, or safety systems.

By improving access to repair information, tools, and spare parts, this measure seeks to reduce repair
costs, extend the useful life of agricultural and fisheries machinery, minimize production losses, and
strengthen rural repair enterprises that support farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives, and agricultural
communities.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT PROMOTING THE RIGHT OF FARMERS, FISHERFOLK, COOPERATIVES,
 AND INDEPENDENT REPAIR PROVIDERS TO REPAIR FARM MACHINERY AND
AGRICULTURAL EQUIPMENT, REQUIRING ACCESS TO REPAIR INFORMATION,
          TOOLS, AND SPARE PARTS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Farm Machinery Right-to-Repair and
Spare Parts Access Act".

SEC. 2. Declaration of State Policy— It is the policy of the State to promote food security, rural
productivity, fair competition, consumer welfare, and sustainable use of agricultural equipment by
ensuring reasonable access to repair information and parts.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:

   a. Agricultural equipment refers to machinery, implements, tools, embedded software,
      sensors, and devices used in farming, fisheries, livestock, post-harvest handling, irrigation,
      and related agricultural operations.

   b. Repair provider refers to any owner, cooperative, technician, service center,
      microenterprise, or other person engaged by an owner to diagnose, maintain, or repair
      agricultural equipment.

   c. Manufacturer includes any producer, importer, distributor, authorized dealer, or brand
      owner placing covered equipment in the Philippine market.

   d.    Critical agricultural or fisheries period refers to any period involving planting, harvesting,
        irrigation, livestock care, fisheries operations, post-harvest handling, drying, milling, cold
        storage, transport, or other time-sensitive agricultural or fisheries activity where delay in
        repair may result in loss of income, spoilage, production delay, or damage to crops,
        livestock, fishery products, or equipment.


   e. Repair information refers to manuals, wiring diagrams, diagnostic codes, service bulletins,
      calibration procedures, parts catalogues, software information, and other information
      reasonably necessary to diagnose, maintain, or repair covered agricultural equipment.
    f. Repair tools refers to physical tools, diagnostic devices, software tools, firmware access,
       calibration equipment, or other instruments reasonably necessary to perform lawful
       diagnosis, maintenance, or repair.


SEC. 4. Right to Practical and Timely Repair Access. — Farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives, agricultural
enterprises, and independent repair providers shall have the right to obtain the information, tools, software
support, and spare parts reasonably necessary to diagnose, maintain, and repair covered agricultural and
fisheries equipment.

Manufacturers, importers, distributors, authorized dealers, and brand owners shall make available,
under fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory terms, repair manuals, wiring diagrams, diagnostic
tools, calibration procedures, error codes, service bulletins, firmware tools necessary for lawful
repair, and genuine or compatible spare parts.

Access under this section shall be made available in a manner that is practical for rural users,
including through printed copies, electronic access, authorized online portals, regional service
channels, or accredited repair providers, as may be provided in the implementing rules and
regulations.

Manufacturers may restrict access only when strictly necessary to protect safety, cybersecurity,
emissions compliance, intellectual property, or trade secrets not required for ordinary diagnosis,
maintenance, or repair.

Warranty coverage shall not be denied solely because the owner sought repair from an independent
repair provider or used a compatible non-original part, unless the manufacturer clearly proves that
the damage was directly caused by such repair or part.

SEC. 5. Priority Repair Access During Critical Agricultural and Fisheries Operations. — The
implementing rules and regulations shall provide priority procedures for repair access requests
involving equipment used during critical agricultural or fisheries periods.

Priority action may be given to requests involving tractors, harvesters, irrigation pumps, dryers,
milling equipment, cold storage units, fisheries equipment, livestock care equipment, post-harvest
machinery, and other equipment where delay in repair may result in crop loss, spoilage,
interruption of livelihood, or substantial economic loss to farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives, or
agricultural enterprises.

For this purpose, manufacturers, dealers, and covered entities may be required to provide
reasonable response periods, spare parts availability information, referral to qualified repair
providers, or other practical assistance necessary to prevent avoidable downtime.


SEC. 6. Implementing Agency. - The Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA), and the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPO), shall issue guidelines,
maintain a registry of covered manufacturers, and support training for rural repair providers.

The DA, in coordination with TESDA and other concerned agencies, shall support training,
certification, and capacity-building programs for rural technicians, cooperatives, repair
microenterprises, and agricultural service providers to improve local repair capability and reduce
dependence on distant service centers.
The DA may also maintain a registry of covered manufacturers, importers, distributors, authorized
dealers, repair providers, and available service channels, subject to the guidelines to be issued
under this Act.

SEC. 7. Repair Assistance and Grievance Mechanism. - The lead implementing agency shall
establish an accessible repair assistance and complaint mechanism for farmers, fisherfolk,
cooperatives, and independent repair providers who are denied reasonable access to repair
information, tools, software support, or spare parts.

The mechanism shall allow complaints to be filed through regional or provincial offices, online
platforms, or other simple and low-cost means. Priority action shall be given to complaints
involving equipment needed for planting, harvesting, irrigation, post-harvest handling, cold
storage, or other time-sensitive agricultural operations.

Covered manufacturers and dealers shall keep records of requests for repair information, diagnostic
access, warranty claims, and spare parts availability, subject to safeguards on privacy,
cybersecurity, lawful confidentiality, and trade secrets.

SEC. 8. Penalties and Remedies. - A manufacturer or dealer that unjustifiably refuses access,
misrepresents warranty rights, or engages in discriminatory pricing shall be subject to
administrative fines of not less than One hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00) but not more than
One million pesos (P1,000,000.00) for each violation, without prejudice to other remedies under
existing laws.

SEC. 9. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be
charged against the current appropriations of the concerned agencies. Thereafter, such sums as
may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA).

SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of
this Act, the lead implementing agency, in coordination with concerned agencies and stakeholders,
shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 11. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid,
the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations, and other
issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

SEC. 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
