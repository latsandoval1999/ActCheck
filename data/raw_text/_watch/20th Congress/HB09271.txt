                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The rapid acceleration of the digital revolution and the lessons learned from
the Covid-19 pandemic have highlighted the immediate need to equip every Filipino
with essential Digital Literacy and Future Skills. Digital literacy, defined as the broad
set of competencies necessary to use digital media, computers, and information
technologies effectively and safely, is no longer a luxury but a fundamental
requirement for social inclusion and economic competitiveness. Given the
continuous rise globally in time spent online and on social media, international efforts
are being accelerated to the development of these digital skill set, coupled with the
ability for careful discernment.

      This legislative measure is, therefore, an important step in preparing the
country for the demands of the global digital economy.

        This Bill, the “Digital Literacy Act of 2025,” aims to institutionalize digital
literacy and future skills development to improve the competencies of Filipinos to
integrate into, and operate within, a rapidly evolving, technology-driven society. It
ensures that no Filipino child misses out on their basic right to education, which
increasingly relies on digital technology, while simultaneously upskilling the adult
workforce and keeping them relevant for the future of work and their ability to access
goods and services digitally.

        This Bill draws inspiration from significant legislative efforts in both houses of
Congress, including House Bill No. 166 (National Digital Skills and AI Readiness Act
of 2025) and House Bill No. 4814 (Samar ICT Education and Digital Literacy Act),
both filed during the 20th Congress, as well as Senate Bill No. 709 (Social Media
Awareness in Schools and Universities Act of 2022) from the previous Congress.
The core objective is to ensure that all Filipinos are equipped with the digital literacy
and future skills necessary to participate fully, responsibly, and ethically in the global
digital economy and information society.

     Building upon these proposals, this Act adopts and refines the following key
measures:

       a) Including digital literacy and future skills in the curriculum, expanded to
include technical-vocational education.

       b) Integrating basic digital literacy in the National Service Training Program
(NSTP) (Note: The term “Social media awareness” is replaced with the b r o a d e r
term “digital literacy and future skills” in this   measure).

       c) Establishing Digital Literacy and Future Skills Centers that utilize existing
public places, such as libraries, malls, universities and colleges, and cultural
centers.

       To further achieve the aforementioned objective, the following new measures
are also proposed:

      a) Launching a mobile training program (Digital Literacy on Wheels) to reach
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

       b) Creating an Inter-Agency Task Force on Digital Literacy and Future Skills.

       c) Declaring December 2nd of every year as “Digital Literacy Day” (Note: This
date aligns with the globally observed World Computer Literacy Day and s i m i l a r
national initiatives, such as the Singapore Digital Literacy Day).

        By strengthening digital competency and ensuring access to future skills
training for all, this Bill promotes a more resilient, capable, and united Filipino
citizenry. Digital empowerment strengthens the Filipino family and the nation by
boosting communication, creating economic opportunities, and helping everyone
thrive in the modern world.

      In light of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this Bill is respectfully
requested.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Digital Literacy Act
of 2025.”

        SEC 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the vital role of
information and communication technology (ICT), education, and communication in
nation-building, social progress, and human development. It is the declared policy of
the State to ensure that all Filipinos are equipped with the digital literacy and future
skills necessary to participate fully, responsibly, and ethically in the global digital
economy and information society. The State shall prioritize inculcating the virtues of
discernment, critical thinking, responsible digital citizenship, and ethical use of digital
platforms.

       SEC 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

        a) Digital Literacy refers to the ability to access, manage, understand,
integrate, communicate, evaluate, and create information safely and appropriately
using digital technology. It encompasses competences related to computer literacy,
ICT literacy, information literacy, media literacy, and digital citizenship.

       b) Future Skills refer to advanced digital and technical competencies critical
for the Fourth Industrial Revolution, including, but not limited to, digital coding, digital
marketing, data science, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and cybersecurity.



                                              4
       SEC 4. Inclusion of Digital Literacy and Future Skills in the Curriculum. –
The Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), in coordination with the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT) and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA), shall formulate, develop, and integrate a comprehensive and progressive
curriculum on Digital Literacy and Future Skills in the basic, higher, and technical-
vocational education curricula. This curriculum shall be periodically reviewed and
updated to include emerging Future Skills.

       SEC 5. Basic Digital Literacy in the National Service Training Program
(NSTP). – Basic digital literacy shall be made an integral and mandatory component
of the National Service Training Program (NSTP), specifically in the Literacy Training
Service (LTS) and the Civil Welfare Training Service (CWTS) components.

        Prior to deployment, NSTP students shall be equipped with the skills to
effectively teach basic digital literacy and future skills to communities.

       SEC 6. Access to Digital Learning Resources. – The DepEd. CHED, and
TESDA shall provide or facilitate access to necessary software, hardware, and
internet connectivity to all public school students and teachers across the country.
This shall include, but not limited to, the provision of modern digital learning devices
and sufficient internet access to support the effective integration of the Digital
Literacy and Future Skills curriculum.

       SEC 7. Establishment of Digital Literacy and Future Skills Development
Center. – The TESDA, in collaboration with the DICT, Department of the Interior and
Local Government (DILG), and other relevant stakeholders, shall establish a Digital
Literacy and Future Skills Development Center in every region of the Philippines,
aimed at providing free seminars, trainings, and certification programs on Digital
Literacy and Future Skills. These centers shall be established in easily accessible
public facilities, such as libraries, malls, universities and colleges, cultural centers, or
equivalent existing centers.

       To promote inclusivity and lifelong digital upskilling, the centers shall offer both
on-site and accessible online learning options catering to all Filipinos, particularly
underserved groups such as Overseas Filipino Workers, senior citizens, persons
with disabilities, and out-of-school youth.

       SEC 8. Digital Literacy on Wheels. – The DICT and TESDA, in coordination
with local government units, shall jointly establish a “Digital Literacy on Wheels”
program utilizing mobile training facilities. This program shall complement the Digital
Literacy and Future Skills Development Centers by reaching geographically isolated
and disadvantaged areas to provide localized, hands-on training in digital literacy to
underserved communities.

      SEC 9. Creation of Inter-Agency Task Force on Digital Literacy and
Future Skills. – An Inter-Agency Task Force on Digital Literacy and Future Skills
(IATF-DLFS) is hereby created to ensure the effective and coordinated
implementation of this Act. The IATF-DLFS shall be chaired by the Secretary of the

DICT, with the Secretaries of the DepEd and DILG, and the Chairpersons of CHED
and TESDA as members. The Task Force shall be responsible for policy formulation,
program monitoring, and securing necessary funding.

      SEC 10. Digital Literacy Day. – To further promote Digital Literacy and
Future Skills Development, the second of December of every year is hereby
declared as “Digital Literacy Day” in the Philippines.

        SEC 11. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provisions
of this Act shall be included in the General Appropriations Act for the year following
its enactment into law and thereafter.

       SEC 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the IATF-DLFS shall promulgate the necessary rules
and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

       SEC 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part hereof is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provision or part not otherwise
affected shall remain valid and subsisting.

       SEC 14. Repealing Clause. – Any law, presidential decree or issuance,
executive order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule, or regulation contrary
to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or
amended accordingly.

        SEC 15.         Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
