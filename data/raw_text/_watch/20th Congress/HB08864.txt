    AN ACT REGULATING THE REGISTRATION, LICENSING, AND
 UTILIZATION OF ELECTRIC TWO-WHEELED AND THREE-WHEELED
    VEHICLES ON MAJOR THOROUGHFARES AND EXPRESSWAYS,
 AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 4136, REPUBLIC
    ACT NO. 11697, AND REPUBLIC ACT NO. 2000, AND FOR OTHER
                           PURPOSES


                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE
The Philippine transportation landscape is currently confronting an administrative
bottleneck regarding the classification and road integration of two-wheeled and
three-wheeled electric vehicles (EVs). While Republic Act No. 11697, or the Electric
Vehicle Industry Development Act (EVIDA), laid a vital foundation for promoting
electric mobility, the absence of granular, speed-and-power-specific statutory
guidelines has resulted in regulatory friction across national highways and local
intersections alike.

Our current traffic enforcement relies heavily on Republic Act No. 4136, a 1964
statute severely underequipped to handle modern micro-mobility devices. This
statutory silence has forced the Land Transportation Office (LTO), the Metropolitan
Manila Development Authority (MMDA), and various Local Government Units
(LGUs) to issue a patchwork of administrative orders and ordinances. Consequently,
we see conflicting enforcement where electric kick-scooters and high-performance
electric motorcycles are often caught in the same regulatory dragnet, leading to
abrupt blanket bans on major thoroughfares. Furthermore, the implementation of
Republic Act No. 2000 (Limited Access Highway Act) continues to rely on internal
combustion engine (ICE) displacement metrics—specifically the 400cc minimum
threshold established by older departmental issuances. This archaic standard
effectively bars technologically advanced, highway-capable electric vehicles from
our expressways, penalizing their electric propulsion rather than evaluating their
actual performance capabilities.

This bill seeks to resolve these conflicts by establishing a unified, tiered regulatory
framework based strictly on vehicle speed, continuous rated power output, and
intended road utilization. It proposes to categorize these vehicles into three distinct
classes:

First, it defines Micro Light Electric Vehicles (Micro-LEVs), which include
low-speed pedal-assist bicycles weighing 50 kilograms or less and capped at 25
kilometers per hour. Recognizing their nature as active personal mobility devices, the
bill formally exempts them from motor vehicle registration and driver's licensing.
However, to protect operators and the motoring public, their use is strictly confined
to local roads, barangay roads, and designated active transport lanes, explicitly
prohibiting them from major thoroughfares and expressways.

Second, the measure addresses the "middle ground" of commuter EVs by defining
Standard Light Electric Vehicles (Standard LEVs)—those exceeding 25 km/h but
falling below the 11 kW (for two-wheels) or 15 kW (for three-wheels) threshold. The
bill removes existing ambiguities by mandating LTO registration and requiring
operators to hold a valid driver's license. In exchange for accountability, licensed
Standard LEV operators are guaranteed access to the outermost right lane of national
roads and major thoroughfares. To prevent the arbitrary disruption of the transport
network, LGUs and the MMDA are required to obtain DOTr approval and designate
alternate routes before imposing any bans on these compliant vehicles.

Finally, the bill introduces the Highway-Capable Electric Vehicle (HCEV)
classification. By establishing a statutory equivalency—recognizing an 11 kW
two-wheeled HCEV as the performance equivalent of a 400cc ICE motorcycle, and a
15 kW three-wheeled HCEV as the equivalent of a 600cc motorcycle —the
legislation modernizes expressway access. Toll operators and traffic authorities will
be prohibited from denying access to duly registered HCEVs that meet these
continuous rated power thresholds.

By amending Republic Act Nos. 4136, 11697, and 2000, this Act harmonizes our
road utilization laws with the realities of modern electric mobility. It ensures
pedestrian safety on local streets, mandates accountability for commuter vehicles,
and prevents administrative discrimination against high-performance EVs on our
national expressways.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Electric Vehicle Registration
and Road Utilization Act."


Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall ensure the safe and orderly
integration of electric vehicles into the national transportation network through clear,
unified statutory guidelines tiered according to vehicle speed, power output, and
intended road use. This Act shall be implemented in a manner consistent with, and in
furtherance of, the policy of promoting electric vehicle adoption under Republic Act
No. 11697 (Electric Vehicle Industry Development Act or EVIDA), balancing road
safety with the necessity of inclusive mobility.
Section 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
hereby defined as follows:


       (a) Micro Light Electric Vehicle (Micro-LEV) — an electric two-wheeled
       vehicle or pedal-assist bicycle weighing fifty kilograms (50 kg) or below, with
       a maximum design speed not exceeding twenty-five kilometers per hour (25
       km/h).


       (b) Standard Light Electric Vehicle (Standard LEV) — an electric
       two-wheeled or three-wheeled vehicle capable of speeds exceeding 25 km/h
       and designed for local or arterial commuting, with continuous rated power
       below eleven kilowatts (11 kW) for two-wheeled configurations and below
       fifteen kilowatts (15 kW) for three-wheeled configurations.


       (c) Highway-Capable Electric Vehicle (HCEV) — an electric two-wheeled or
       three-wheeled motor vehicle (without sidecar) with continuous rated power of
       at least eleven kilowatts (11 kW) for two-wheeled, or at least fifteen kilowatts
       (15 kW) for three-wheeled, capable of safely sustaining at least one hundred
       kilometers per hour (100 km/h) on a level road.


       (d) Major Thoroughfare — any national primary road, radial road,
       circumferential road, or highway connecting municipalities and cities with
       high vehicular volume, as designated by the DPWH.


       (e) Continuous Rated Power — the maximum power an electric motor can
       sustain continuously for at least thirty (30) minutes without exceeding
       manufacturer-rated thermal limits. This shall be established through
       DTI-accredited testing, internationally recognized type-approval certificates,
       or manufacturer technical declarations recognized by the Land Transportation
       Office (LTO).


Section 4. Micro-LEVs: Exemption and Road Restrictions – Micro-LEVs are active
personal mobility devices. They are exempt from LTO motor vehicle registration and




​
driver's licensing requirements. They are limited to local roads, barangay roads, and
designated active transport lanes, and are strictly prohibited from traversing major
thoroughfares and expressways.


An operator of a Micro-LEV caught on a prohibited road shall be cited and fined
under   Section    8(a).   Identification   shall be established through     a valid
government-issued ID. To facilitate enforcement despite the lack of a license plate,
traffic authorities are authorized to immediately impound the Micro-LEV if the
operator refuses citation or fails to present competent proof of identity.


The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) shall mandate a point-of-sale recording
system for Micro-LEVs to retain baseline ownership data.


Section 5. Standard LEVs: Registration, Licensing, and Road Access —


        (a) Registration. — Standard LEVs used on any public road are subject to
        mandatory LTO registration. This provision expressly amends Section 9(b) of
        Republic Act No. 11697: the registration exemption for private-use LEVs
        under EVIDA is retained only for Micro-LEVs and for Standard LEVs used
        exclusively off the public road network.


        (b) Licensing. — Operators must hold a valid driver's license with the
        appropriate restriction code and wear a motorcycle helmet compliant with
        Republic Act No. 10054.


        (c) Road Access. — Registered and licensed Standard LEV operators may use
        national roads and major thoroughfares strictly along the outermost right lane
        or any designated micro-mobility lane.


        (d) Local Government and MMDA Harmonization. — To prevent arbitrary
        disruptions to the national transport network, the DOTr shall issue National
        EV Integration Standards. Any Local Government Unit (LGU), the MMDA,
        or other traffic authority seeking to enact an ordinance or regulation banning




​
       or restricting compliant Standard LEVs from a major thoroughfare must first
       formally consult with the DOTr.


Such local restrictions shall only be valid if they conform to the National EV
Integration Standards, are justified by a localized road safety necessity, and designate
an accessible, contiguous alternate route for LEV users. Existing blanket bans failing
these conditions are deemed suspended pending harmonization with DOTr standards.


Section 6. HCEVs: Power Equivalency and Expressway Access — For all purposes
of expressway access under Republic Act No. 2000 and its implementing orders:


       (a) A two-wheeled HCEV with continuous rated power of at least 11 kW is the
       statutory equivalent of a two-wheeled ICE motorcycle with displacement of at
       least 400cc.


       (b) A three-wheeled HCEV with continuous rated power of at least 15 kW is
       the statutory equivalent of a three-wheeled ICE motorcycle with displacement
       of at least 600cc.


A duly registered HCEV meeting the applicable threshold may use all limited-access
expressways on the same terms as the equivalent ICE motorcycle class. No toll
operator, agency, or traffic authority may deny expressway access to a registered
HCEV solely on account of its electric propulsion.


Section 7. Registration and Visual Identification. — Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the LTO shall:


       (a) establish distinct registration classifications for Standard LEVs and
       HCEVs;


       (b) issue color-coded plates or decals immediately distinguishing Standard
       LEVs (restricted to the outer lane) from HCEVs (expressway-cleared); and
       (c) publish guidelines for accepting manufacturer power certifications as proof
       of continuous rated power.




​
Section 8. Penalties —


       (a) Micro-LEV on a prohibited road: fine of One Thousand Pesos (PhP 1,000)
       per violation. Failure to establish identity shall result in the immediate
       impoundment of the unit.


       (b) Unregistered Standard LEV or HCEV on a public road, or operation
       without a valid license: fines and impoundment under Republic Act No. 4136.


       (c) Standard LEV operating outside the outermost right lane on a major
       thoroughfare: fine of Two Thousand Pesos (PhP 2,000).


       (d) Sub-threshold electric vehicle on a limited-access expressway: fine of Ten
       Thousand Pesos (PhP 10,000) and immediate impoundment.


       (e) Toll operator denying expressway access to a registered HCEV in violation
       of Section 6: fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP 50,000) per violation,
       adjudicated by the Toll Regulatory Board (TRB). TRB decisions are
       appealable to the DOTr and thereafter to the Court of Appeals on questions of
       law.


Section 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations — The DOTr, in coordination with
the LTO, TRB, MMDA, DPWH, and DTI, shall issue the Implementing Rules and
Regulations, including the National EV Integration Standards required under Section
5(d), within one hundred twenty (120) days from effectivity. Existing regulations not
inconsistent with this Act remain in force on a transitory basis pending the issuance of
the IRR.

Section 10. Separability Clause — If any provision of this act is held invalid, the
other provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

Section 11. Repealing Clause — All laws, executive orders, issuances, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this act are repealed or
modified accordingly.

Section 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after THE
COMPLETION OF its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of
general circulation.
