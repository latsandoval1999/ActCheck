                        AN ACT
 CREATING THE PHILIPPINE REGISTRY OF OVERSEAS FILIPINO
                    WORKERS (OFWs)


                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 18, Article II of the 1987 Constitution mandates the State to protect
labor and promote the welfare of workers, including Overseas Filipino Workers
(OFWs) whose remittances significantly contribute to national development and
sustain millions of Filipino families. Despite their vital role in the economy,
OFWs continue to face illegal recruitment, contract substitution, inadequate pre-
departure preparation, fragmented government monitoring, and limited access to
timely welfare assistance. There remains no single, centralized, and
comprehensive database that consolidates accurate and up-to-date information on
OFWs and overseas employment opportunities.

       This bill seeks to establish the Philippine Registry of OFWs, a centralized,
secure, and integrated national database covering land-based and sea-based
workers, as well as individuals intending to work abroad. The Registry shall
contain verified information on workers, accredited recruitment agencies,
licensed employers, and approved job orders to ensure transparency and prevent
fraudulent recruitment practices. It shall serve as an official platform for
monitoring deployment, tracking employment status, and facilitating immediate
government response in cases of emergency, distress, or repatriation. The
measure designates the Department of Migrant Workers as the lead implementing
  agency, in coordination with relevant government offices, to establish, manage,
  and safeguard the Registry in compliance with data privacy and cybersecurity
  standards.

         By institutionalizing a unified information system, the bill strengthens
  regulatory oversight, enhances inter-agency coordination, improves delivery of
  welfare and reintegration services, and reinforces the State’s constitutional duty
  to protect Filipino migrant workers throughout the entire migration cycle.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “The OFW Registry
Act.”
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – As used in this Act:


   (a) Data Sharing – refers to the disclosure or transfer to a third party of
        personal data under the control or custody of a personal information
        controller (PIC) or personal information processor (PIP).
   (b) Data Sharing Agreement – refers to a contract, joint issuance, or similar
        document that contains the terms and conditions of a data sharing
        agreement between two or more parties.
(c) Financial Literacy – refers to the ability to understand and effectively use
   various financial skills, including personal financial management,
   budgeting, and investing.
(d) First-time OFW – refer to a Filipino who is seeking employment abroad
   for the first time.
(e) Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) – refers to a Filipino who is to be
   engaged, or has been engaged in remunerated activity in a country of which
   he or she is not an immigrant, citizen, or permanent resident or is not
   awaiting naturalization, recognition, or admission, whether land-based or
   sea-based regardless of status, excluding a Filipino engaged under a
   government-recognized exchange visitor program for cultural and
   educational purposes. For purposes of this provision, a person to be
   engaged or is engaged in remunerated activity covers a person, whether
   land-based or sea-based, who has been contracted for overseas
   employment and is already at the point of hire ready for deployment. The
   term “OFW” is synonymous to “Migrant Worker”.
(f) OFW Registry – refers to a comprehensive database maintained by the
   government that records and tracks information on all registered OFWs,
   both land-based and sea-based, including their employment details,
   personal information, educational background, training, and other relevant
   data necessary to enhance government protection.
(g) OFW Scorecard – refers to a tool used to evaluate and monitor the
   readiness and suitability of an individual for overseas employment. The
   scorecard includes various metrics and indicators and other relevant
   criteria that determine the overall competency and work readiness of an
   OFW.
(h) Physical Examination – A medical evaluation conducted by licensed
   healthcare professionals to assess the overall physical health and fitness of

                                                                               2
      an individual for overseas employment. This examination ensures that
      OFWs are physically         capable of performing their duties          and
      responsibilities in a foreign country.
   (i) Psychological Assessment – A standardized evaluation conducted by
      licensed professionals to assess the mental health, emotional stability, and
      psychological readiness of an individual for overseas employment. This
      assessment aims to ensure that OFWs are mentally and emotionally
      prepared for the challenges of working abroad.
   (j) Returning OFWs (or balik-manggagawa) – refers to an overseas Filipino
      worker who has served or is serving his/her employment contract and is
      returning to the same employer in the same jobsite or returning to the same
      employer in a new job site and who has an existing record in the DMW
      database of deployed workers.
   (k) Work Readiness – The state of being fully prepared and equipped with the
      necessary competence, knowledge, physical fitness, psychological
      stability and mental health to undertake employment in a foreign country.
SEC. 3. Objectives. – The objectives of this Act are as follows:
   (a) To empower Filipinos who are seeking employment abroad to be fully
      prepared and equipped with the necessary skills, knowledge, physical
      fitness, psychological stability and mental health to undertake and sustain
      employment in a foreign country;
   (b) To facilitate the matching of skills or competencies and qualifications of
      all Filipinos seeking employment abroad with available job opportunities;
   (c) To eliminate illegal recruitment activities and protect potential overseas
      workers from exploitation, abuse and fraud;
   (d) To effectively monitor Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) to ensure their
      welfare and protection while working abroad; and



                                                                                 3
   (e) To enable the DMW and other agencies of the government to effectively
      and efficiently provide the appropriate interventions to enhance the
      competencies or skills and work readiness of OFWs and all those intending
      to work abroad.


SEC. 4. Establishment of the OFW Registry. – The DMW shall establish the
OFW Registry, which shall be made accessible to all OFWs, as well as those
intending to work abroad.


      This registry shall include a comprehensive scorecard to assess the
competencies and work readiness of applicants for overseas employment. The
DMW shall develop the scorecard that will measure various aspects, including
qualifications, competencies or skills, financial literacy, psychological stability,
mental health, and physical fitness.


      For easier access, the DMW may segregate the OFWs based on sector,
whether land-based or sea-based, gender, country of destination, nature of work,
nature of OFW whether first-time or returning OFWs (balik manggagawa), and
other reasonable criteria.


SEC. 5. Contents of the Registry. – All OFWs shall provide the following details
or information to the registry, when applicable:


   (a) Full name;
   (b) Date of birth;
   (c) Address;
   (d) Contact details;



                                                                                   4
   (e) National ID number, including Overseas Workers Welfare Administration
      (OWWA), Social Security System (SSS), Bureau of Internal Revenue Tax
      Identification Number (BIR TIN), PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG MID
      numbers;
   (f) Educational attainment;
   (g) Qualifications, relevant trainings or seminars attended, if any;
   (h) Transcripts/diplomas, if any;
   (i) Work history;
   (j) Psychological Assessment Results;
   (k) Physical Examination Results;
   (l) Financial Literacy Assessment Results; and
  (m) Any other pertinent information deemed necessary by the DMW and other
      relevant government agencies, such as the Bureau of Immigration.


SEC. 6. Mandatory Registration. – All OFWs, including returning OFWs and
those who intend to work abroad, must register with the OFW Registry.


      Only those registered and belong to the OFW Green List shall be allowed
to work abroad.


      The DMW shall provide the mechanism for easy registration and
validation of information provided by the registrant, including uploading of
pertinent documents.


SEC. 7 Minimum Period from Registration. – For first-time OFWs, a minimum
of ninety (90) days from registration shall be required in order to be eligible for
deployment.



                                                                                  5
      For other types of OFWs, the DMW shall determine the length of time
from registration – from zero (0) to ninety (90) days – before the OFW may be
legally deployed.


SEC. 8. OFW Green List. – In addition to the required minimum period, the
DMW shall determine who are eligible for deployment based on the scorecard it
has developed, ensuring that only individuals who meet the established criteria
and demonstrate work readiness through the scorecard evaluation will be allowed
to join the pool of Filipinos eligible for deployment abroad.


      All those eligible for deployment abroad shall be considered part of
DMW’s OFW Green List.


SEC. 9. Recruitment within the OFW Registry. – Unless exempted by the
DMW, recruitment of OFWs – whether land-based or sea-based – shall only be
withing the OFW Registry. In this regard, all verified job orders or crew orders
shall be immediately posted by the DMW in the OFW Registry, which shall be
made accessible to all qualified pool of applicants.


SEC. 10. Illegal Recruitment. – Any recruitment activities outside the OFW
Registry, or those exempted by DMW, shall be considered illegal recruitment.


SEC. 11. Protection of Privacy and Protection Against Unlawful Disclosure of
Information. – The DMW, in coordination with the DICT, shall ensure the
integrity and privacy of the OFWs, as well as those intending to work abroad,
who are in the OFW Registry.




                                                                               6
      No person may disclose, collect, record, convey, disseminate, publish, or
use any information pertaining to registered individuals with the OFW Registry,
give access thereto or give copies thereof to third parties or entities, except when
the registered person has given his or her consent, specific to the purpose prior to
the processing.


      Information disclosed shall not be used except for the specific purpose for
which it was authorized and shall not be divulged by any person to any third party
other than the person so authorized.


      The DMW must ensure that information in the OFW Registry is used only
in accordance with its intended purpose as set forth in this Act.


      Any information obtained as a result of unlawful disclosure under this Act
shall be inadmissible in any judicial, quasi-judicial or administrative proceedings.


SEC. 12. Data Sharing Involving Government Agencies. – Data sharing
agreements involving the information contained in the OFW Registry between
government agencies must strictly adhere to the data privacy principles
delineated in Republic Act No. 10173, also known as the “Data Privacy Act of
2012,” along with its Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), and all
issuances by the National Privacy Commission (NPC).


SEC. 13. Regular Updating of the Registry. – OFWs are required to regularly
update their information in the registry. All updates shall be subject to validation
by the DMW to ensure the accuracy of the data and the current status of the
OFWs.



                                                                                   7
SEC. 14. Blacklisting. – The DMW may blacklist OFWs or those intending to
work abroad for violation of their overseas contract, or on other legal grounds.


SEC. 15. Role of Government Agencies. – The DMW shall be the lead agency
responsible for implementing this Act. Accordingly, the DMW shall:


   (a) Develop and maintain the system infrastructure for the OFW registry;
   (b) Develop the scorecard system that will assess the competencies or skills
      and work readiness of the OFWs or those intending to work abroad;
   (c) Implement a systematic approach for the continuous and regular updating
      of the registry;
   (d) Validate the information provided by the OFWs or those intending to work
      abroad;
   (e) Adequately inform upon registration for the OFW Registry on how their
      data will be used or processed, and how they can access their registered
      information and record history;
   (f) Post verified job orders or crew orders in the OFW Registry which shall
      be made accessible to all qualified candidates;
   (g) Transmit the information of interested qualified applicants to the
      concerned recruitment agency or manning agency;
   (h) Enforce blacklisting as provided in the immediately preceding section;
   (i) Grant access to specific individuals or groups on specific data in the OFW
      Registry as may be necessary in the performance of their mandates or
      functions; and
   (j) Undertake appropriate measures to ensure that the objectives of this Act
      are realized.




                                                                                   8
       The DICT shall provide technical assistance to the DMW in developing
and operating the OFW Registry.


       The Bureau of Immigration (BI), Overseas Workers Welfare Affairs
(OWWA), and the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) shall also provide
technical support and assistance to the DMW as may be necessary.


SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DMW, in coordination
with the DICT, BI, OWWA, and DFA, and upon consultation with relevant
stakeholders, including associations of recruitment agencies, manning agencies,
OFW groups, and advocates, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations
to implement the provisions of this Act within ninety (90) days after its
effectivity.


SEC. 17. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the
departments and agencies concerned. Thereafter, the amount necessary for the
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.


SEC. 18. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act or any part hereof
be declared unconstitutional or invalid, the same shall not affect the validity of
the other provisions of Act.


SEC. 19. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations or
parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

SEC. 20. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effects fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
