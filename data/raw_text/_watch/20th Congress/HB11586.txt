EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution recognizes the family as the foundation of the nation and
directs the State to protect and strengthen it. Consistent with this policy, Republic
Act No. 10354, otherwise known as the “Responsible Parenthood and Reproductive
Health Act of 2012,” already requires applicants for a marriage license to receive
instruction on responsible parenthood, family planning, breastfeeding, and infant
nutrition as a condition for the issuance of a Certificate of Compliance.

However, while Section 15 of Republic Act No. 10354 provides an important
foundation for pre-marriage education, it does not prescribe a comprehensive and
standardized curriculum on child development, child behavior, positive parenting,
positive discipline, responsive caregiving, and child protection.

Since the enactment of Republic Act No. 10354, Republic Act No. 11148, or
the “Kalusugan at Nutrisyon ng Mag-Nanay Act,” has further recognized the
importance of positive parenting, responsive caregiving, parent- or caregiver-child
interaction, and early childhood development. Philippine policy has therefore
evolved, but the pre-marriage education requirements under Section 15 have not
been correspondingly expanded.

This measure aims to enrich, not replace, Section 15 of Republic Aet No.
10354 by weaving these vital competencies into the current pre-marriage orientation
and counseling program. It introduces a unified, evidence-based Pre-Marriage
Responsible Parenthood and Family Preparation Course that explores child
development, behavior, emotional regulation, positive discipline, parent-child
communication, child protection, and responsible parenthood.

The measure is educational and preventive. It does not create a parental-fitness
test or authorize the government to determine an individual's moral, psychological,
religious, economic, or parental suitability for marriage. Completion of the Course,
rather than a determination of fitness, shall constitute compliance.

By reinforcing an established legal framework and harmonizing it with
evolving Philippine policies on positive parenting and child development, this
measure aspires to equip future spouses for the challenges of family life and
champion the protection, well-being, and flourishing of Filipino children.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

KAJRINA REIKO CHUA-TAI

AN ACT
STRENGTHENING THE PRE-MARRIAGE RESPONSIBLE PARENTHOOD
PROGRAM, AMENDING FOR THIS PURPOSE SECTION 15 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO.
10354, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “RESPONSIBLE PARENTHOOD AND
REPRODUCTIVE HEALTH ACT OF 2012,” AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Pre-Marriage Child

Development and Positive Parenting Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and strengthen the

family and promote the welfare, rights, safety, and healthy development of children.

While Section 15 of Republic Act No. 10354 already requires marriage-license applicants
to receive instruction on responsible parenthood, family planning, breastfeeding, and infant
nutrition, the existing framework does not prescribe a comprehensive and standardized curriculum
on child development, child behavior, positive parenting, positive discipline, responsive

caregiving, and child protection.

Republic Act No. 11148, or the Kalusugan at Nutrisyon ng Mag-Nanay Act, subsequently
recognized positive parenting, responsive caregiving, and parent or caregiver-child interaction as

important components of government programs for maternal and child health and development.
