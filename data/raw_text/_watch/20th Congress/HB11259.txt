  AN ACT PROMOTING THE WIDESPREAD USE OF SOLAR POWER AMONG
   FILIPINOS, PROVIDING INCENTIVES FOR SOLAR ENERGY ADOPTION,
       ESTABLISHING THE SOLAR POWER ACCESS PROGRAM, AND
                  APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The Philippines, as an archipelago situated in the tropical Pacific, is among the
most solar-rich countries in the world, receiving an average solar irradiance of
approximately 4.5 to 5.5 kilowatt-hours per square meter per day. Despite this
immense natural endowment, solar energy remains significantly underutilized, with
millions of Filipinos — particularly those in rural, remote, and island communities —
still lacking access to reliable and affordable electricity.

       According to the Department of Energy, the Philippines' total installed solar
capacity stands at approximately 3,600 megawatts, a fraction of the country's
enormous solar potential estimated at over 150,000 megawatts. The Philippine
Development Plan and the National Renewable Energy Program have set ambitious
targets for renewable energy, yet the pace of solar adoption — especially at the
household and community levels — remains far too slow to meet these goals.

       The high upfront cost of solar power systems remains the single greatest barrier
to adoption for ordinary Filipino families. A basic residential rooftop solar installation
costs between Php 150,000 to Php 400,000 — far beyond the reach of most
households, particularly the estimated 22 million Filipinos living below the poverty line.
Compounding this is the lack of accessible financing options, insufficient consumer
protection, and the absence of a comprehensive national framework targeting solar
adoption among ordinary citizens.
       Meanwhile, the country continues to suffer from high electricity rates, among
the highest in Southeast Asia, which disproportionately burden low-income families
and undermine the competitiveness of Filipino businesses. Frequent power outages,
dependence on imported fossil fuels, and the devastating impacts of climate change
— to which the Philippines is acutely vulnerable as one of the world's most disaster-
prone nations — underscore the urgency of accelerating the transition to solar energy.

       The enactment of Republic Act No. 9513 or the Renewable Energy Act of 2008
and subsequent Net Metering Rules were significant milestones. However, these laws
have largely benefited middle- and upper-income consumers and businesses, leaving
behind the very Filipinos who stand to gain the most from cheaper, cleaner electricity.
There is presently no dedicated law that comprehensively addresses the barriers to
solar adoption for low-income households, geographically isolated communities, and
small enterprises.

      In response to this urgent need, this bill — the Solar Power Access and
Adoption Act of the Philippines or the SolarPH Act — seeks to establish a
comprehensive legal and institutional framework for the widespread adoption of solar
energy among all Filipinos. Specifically, the bill aims to:

      First, establish the Solar Power Access Program (SolarPH Program) — a
      government-led initiative that will provide subsidized and affordable solar power
      systems to low-income households, fund community solar installations in public
      schools and health centers, and ensure the full electrification of geographically
      isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs) through off-grid solar solutions;

      Second, provide a robust package of fiscal incentives — including a 25%
      income tax credit, VAT exemptions, customs duty exemptions, and low-interest
      concessional loans — to make solar power genuinely affordable for ordinary
      Filipino households and small businesses;

      Third, strengthen and expand the Net Metering Program to enable more Filipino
      solar prosumers to sell excess electricity back to the grid, transforming
      households from passive consumers into active participants in the energy
      market;

      Fourth, mandate local government units to play an active role in promoting solar
      energy adoption within their jurisdictions, streamlining permitting processes,
      and funding community solar projects;

      Fifth, promote the development of a local solar industry through domestic
      manufacturing incentives, a TESDA certification program for solar technicians,
      and government-funded research and development; and
      Sixth, establish strong consumer protection mechanisms, including mandatory
      product standards, warranty requirements, and a licensing system for solar
      installers, to safeguard Filipino consumers.

        The Philippines cannot afford to delay its solar transition. Every year without
comprehensive solar policy is a year of continued dependence on expensive imported
oil, continued exposure to fuel price volatility, continued carbon emissions, and
continued energy poverty for millions of our countrymen. The sun shines generously
on every Philippine island — this bill ensures that its light and warmth reach every
Filipino home.

      In view of the foregoing, the swift passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

                                     ARTICLE I
                                 GENERAL PROVISIONS

      Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Solar Power Access and
Adoption Act of the Philippines" or the "SolarPH Act."

       Section 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

       (a) Promote the development and utilization of solar energy as a clean, renewable, and
       sustainable source of electricity for all Filipinos, particularly for low-income
       households, rural communities, and underserved areas;

       (b) Reduce the country's dependence on fossil fuels and lower greenhouse gas
       emissions in line with the Philippines' commitments under the Paris Agreement and its
       Nationally Determined Contributions;

       (c) Ensure energy security, affordability, and access to electricity across all regions of
       the archipelago, including islands and geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas
       (GIDAs);


       (d) Create sustainable livelihood opportunities through the growth of the local solar
       energy industry; and

       (e) Encourage public and private sector participation in the adoption and expansion of
       solar power technology.
        Section 3. Scope and Coverage. — This Act shall apply to all residential, commercial,
industrial, and government users of electricity throughout the Philippines, with special
emphasis on programs for low-income households and remote communities.

        Section 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
mean:

        (a) "Solar Energy" refers to radiant light and heat from the sun that is harnessed using
        photovoltaic (PV) panels, solar thermal collectors, or other solar energy technologies;
        (b) "Solar Power System" means the complete set of equipment used to convert solar
        energy into usable electricity, including solar panels, inverters, charge controllers,
        batteries, mounting systems, and related components;
        (c) "Net Metering" refers to the arrangement whereby a qualified end-user who
        generates electricity from an eligible on-site renewable energy (RE) facility and
        operates in parallel with the distribution grid is allowed to deliver excess electricity to
        the distribution grid and be credited therefor;
        (d) "Qualified End-User" refers to any electricity end-user who installs an eligible on-
        site RE facility for self-generation;
        (e) "Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs)" refers to communities
        not connected to the main power grid, located in remote or far-flung areas, islands, and
        mountainous regions;
        (f) "SolarPH Program" refers to the Solar Power Access Program established under this
        Act; and
        (g) "Department" refers to the Department of Energy (DOE).

                                   ARTICLE II
                          SOLAR POWER ACCESS PROGRAM

       Section 5. Establishment of the SolarPH Program. — There is hereby established
the Solar Power Access Program, hereinafter referred to as the "SolarPH Program," under the
administration of the Department of Energy, in coordination with the Department of Finance
(DOF), the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), the Department of
Agriculture (DA), the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), and local
government units (LGUs).

        Section 6. Objectives of the SolarPH Program. — The SolarPH Program shall aim
to:

        (a) Provide affordable or subsidized solar power systems to low-income households
        identified under the Listahanan or the National Household Targeting System for
        Poverty Reduction;
        (b) Establish community solar installations in barangays, public schools, rural health
        centers, and government offices;
        (c) Electrify all GIDAs through off-grid solar solutions within a period of ten (10) years
        from the effectivity of this Act;
        (d) Train and capacitate local technicians, cooperatives, and community organizations
        in solar system installation, maintenance, and operation; and
        (e) Develop a National Solar Power Roadmap in coordination with relevant agencies.

        Section 7. Priority Beneficiaries. — The SolarPH Program shall prioritize:
       (a) Households classified as poor or near-poor under the Listahanan;
       (b) Communities in GIDAs, indigenous peoples communities, and island
       municipalities;
       (c) Public elementary and secondary schools without reliable electricity access;
       (d) Rural health centers and barangay health stations; and
       (e) Disaster-prone areas and municipalities declared under a state of calamity.

        Section 8. Minimum Solar Installation Targets. — Within five (5) years from the
effectivity of this Act, the government shall ensure that:

       (a) At least fifty percent (50%) of all public school buildings and government buildings
       are equipped with solar power systems;
       (b) All barangay halls and health centers in GIDAs are powered by solar energy; and
       (c) A cumulative solar capacity of at least five thousand (5,000) megawatts is installed
       nationwide, with at least twenty percent (20%) allocated to residential installations of
       low-income households.

                                  ARTICLE III
                       INCENTIVES AND FINANCIAL SUPPORT

       Section 9. Financial Incentives for Households and Businesses. — To encourage the
adoption of solar power among Filipinos, the following incentives are hereby granted:

       (a) Tax Credit. — Individual taxpayers and businesses that install solar power systems
       shall be entitled to an income tax credit equivalent to twenty-five percent (25%) of the
       total cost of the solar power system, including installation, for a period of five (5) years
       from the date of installation;
       (b) Value-Added Tax (VAT) Exemption. — The sale, importation, and installation of
       solar power systems and components, including solar panels, inverters, batteries,
       mounting hardware, and monitoring systems, shall be exempt from the value-added tax;
       (c) Customs Duty Exemption. — The importation of solar power equipment,
       components, and accessories not locally manufactured shall be exempt from customs
       duties; and
       (d) Local Tax Exemption. — LGUs are hereby authorized and encouraged to exempt
       solar power installations from real property tax assessments and local business taxes
       for a period of ten (10) years.

       Section 10. Concessional Loans and Financing Schemes. — The Department of
Finance, in coordination with the Land Bank of the Philippines, the Development Bank of the
Philippines, and participating private financial institutions, shall:

       (a) Offer low-interest loans at a maximum rate of three percent (3%) per annum for the
       purchase and installation of solar power systems for residential, commercial, and
       agricultural use;
       (b) Provide loan guarantees for qualified borrowers who cannot meet standard collateral
       requirements; and
       (c) Develop solar financing programs accessible through micro-finance institutions,
       cooperatives, and rural banks.
      Section 11. Net Metering Enhancement. — The Energy Regulatory Commission
(ERC) shall, within six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act, issue rules and regulations
to:

       (a) Expand the existing Net Metering Program to allow prosumers with solar
       installations of up to one hundred kilowatts (100 kW) to participate;
       (b) Mandate distribution utilities to process net metering applications within thirty (30)
       calendar days;
       (c) Establish a fair and transparent pricing mechanism for excess electricity fed back
       into the grid; and
       (d) Prohibit unreasonable fees, charges, and technical barriers to net metering
       participation.

       Section 12. Solar Home Ownership Program. — The Social Housing Finance
Corporation (SHFC) and the National Housing Authority (NHA) shall incorporate solar power
systems as a standard feature of all new socialized housing projects funded or subsidized by
the government.

                           ARTICLE IV
          LOCAL GOVERNMENT SUPPORT AND COMMUNITY SOLAR

       Section 13. Role of Local Government Units. — LGUs shall:

       (a) Develop local solar energy plans consistent with the National Solar Power
       Roadmap;
       (b) Establish expedited permitting processes for solar power installations, with a
       maximum processing period of fifteen (15) working days for residential applications;
       (c) Designate Solar Energy Officers within their respective offices to coordinate and
       oversee local solar programs;
       (d) Allocate a portion of their local development funds for community solar projects;
       and
       (e) Enforce compliance with building codes that require solar-readiness in new
       construction projects.

        Section 14. Community Solar Projects. — The Department shall promote community
solar projects whereby multiple households or enterprises share the output of a single solar
installation. Guidelines for community solar arrangements, including cost-sharing, ownership
structures, and electricity credit allocation, shall be issued within one (1) year from the
effectivity of this Act.

        Section 15. Barangay Solar Electrification Fund. — A Barangay Solar
Electrification Fund is hereby established under the management of the DOE, which shall
provide grants to barangays with zero or partial electricity access to fund community solar
installations. Eligible barangays shall be determined based on criteria established by the DOE.

                                 ARTICLE V
                     LOCAL SOLAR INDUSTRY DEVELOPMENT
       Section 16. Promotion of Local Manufacturing. — The Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI), in coordination with the DOE, shall formulate a Philippine Solar
Manufacturing Roadmap to:

       (a) Develop local capacity for the manufacture of solar panels, inverters, batteries, and
       other solar components;
       (b) Provide incentives to domestic manufacturers under the Philippine Innovation Act
       and related laws; and
       (c) Establish quality standards and certification requirements for solar products sold in
       the Philippines.

       Section 17. Solar Technician Training and Certification. — The Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) shall:

       (a) Develop and implement a national competency standard for solar photovoltaic
       installation and maintenance;
       (b) Accredit training providers for solar technician courses; and
       (c) Issue National Certificates to qualified solar technicians under the Philippine TVET
       Qualifications Framework.

       Section 18. Solar Energy Research and Development. — The Department of Science
and Technology (DOST) shall allocate funds for research and development on solar energy
technologies suited to Philippine conditions, including:

       (a) High-efficiency solar cells adapted for tropical climates;
       (b) Energy storage solutions for off-grid solar applications; and
       (c) Agri-solar systems integrating solar power generation with agricultural production.

                               ARTICLE VI
                   CONSUMER PROTECTION AND STANDARDS

        Section 19. Solar Product Standards. — The Bureau of Philippine Standards (BPS)
under the DTI shall issue mandatory Philippine National Standards (PNS) for solar power
products sold in the country. No solar product that does not meet the PNS shall be sold,
distributed, or installed in the Philippines.

       Section 20. Consumer Rights. — Consumers who purchase and install solar power
systems shall be entitled to:

       (a) A minimum performance warranty of twenty-five (25) years on solar panels and
       five (5) years on inverters and other components;
       (b) Transparent disclosure of all costs, financing terms, and projected energy savings
       prior to purchase;
       (c) Access to a fair dispute resolution mechanism through the ERC and the DTI; and
       (d) Protection from unfair trade practices by solar system suppliers and installers.
        Section 21. Solar Installer Licensing. — No person or entity shall engage in the
business of installing solar power systems without a valid license from the Professional
Regulation Commission (PRC) or a valid accreditation from TESDA, as applicable. The PRC
and TESDA shall issue the implementing rules for this provision within one (1) year from the
effectivity of this Act.

                                ARTICLE VII
                    ADMINISTRATION AND IMPLEMENTATION

        Section 22. Lead Agency. — The Department of Energy shall be the lead agency for
the implementation of this Act. The DOE Secretary shall chair an inter-agency Solar Power
Coordinating Council (SPCC) composed of representatives from the DOF, DSWD, DTI,
DOST, TESDA, NHA, SHFC, ERC, and representatives from the private sector and civil
society.

        Section 23. National Solar Power Roadmap. — Within one (1) year from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOE shall prepare and publish the National Solar Power Roadmap,
which shall include:

       (a) Current baseline data on solar energy adoption and capacity;
       (b) Short-term (five-year) and long-term (twenty-year) solar energy targets;
       (c) Strategies for achieving universal solar access in GIDAs; and
       (d) Annual monitoring and evaluation indicators.

        Section 24. Annual Report. — The DOE shall submit an annual report to Congress on
the status of solar power adoption, program implementation, fund utilization, and progress
toward targets set under this Act. The report shall be publicly available on the DOE website.

        Section 25. Monitoring and Evaluation. — The DOE, in coordination with the
National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA), shall conduct a comprehensive mid-
term and final evaluation of this Act's implementation within the first five (5) and ten (10)
years, respectively, from its effectivity.

                                       ARTICLE VIII
                                        PENALTIES

         Section 26. Penalties. — Any person or entity that violates any provision of this Act
or its implementing rules and regulations shall be subject to the following penalties:

       (a) For individuals: A fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php 50,000.00) but
       not more than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 500,000.00), or imprisonment of not
       less than six (6) months but not more than six (6) years, or both, at the discretion of the
       court;
       (b) For corporations and partnerships: A fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand
       Pesos (Php 500,000.00) but not more than Five Million Pesos (Php 5,000,000.00), and
       the cancellation of their business permits and licenses; and
       (c) For government officials or employees who willfully obstruct the implementation
       of this Act: Administrative sanctions in accordance with Civil Service laws, without
       prejudice to criminal prosecution.
                                        ARTICLE IX
                                         FUNDING

       Section 27. Appropriations. — The sum of Ten Billion Pesos (Php
10,000,000,000.00) is hereby appropriated from the General Appropriations Act for the initial
implementation of this Act. Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       Section 28. Solar Power Trust Fund. — A Solar Power Trust Fund is hereby created
under the management of the DOE, to be funded by:

       (a) Annual appropriations under the General Appropriations Act;
       (b) Grants, donations, and contributions from foreign governments, international
       organizations, and the private sector;
       (c) Climate finance and green bonds; and
       (d) Such other sources as may be authorized by law.

                                      ARTICLE X
                                   FINAL PROVISIONS

        Section 29. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The DOE, in coordination with
the relevant agencies, shall issue the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of this Act
within ninety (90) days from its effectivity.

        Section 30. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be
valid and operational.

       Section 31. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations,
and administrative regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

       Section 32. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.

       Approved.
