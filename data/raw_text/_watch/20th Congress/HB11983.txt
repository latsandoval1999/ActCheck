                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Solo parents bear the responsibility of raising and providing for their
children and dependents, often with limited financial resources. Rising costs of
food, healthcare, education, housing, and other basic necessities further
aggravate their economic difficulties, making timely and adequate government
assistance essential.

      While Republic Act No. 11861, or the Expanded Solo Parents Welfare Act,
provides various benefits and privileges, there remains a need to institutionalize
a dedicated financial assistance program that directly addresses the immediate
and essential needs of low-income solo parents.

      This measure seeks to institutionalize the Financial Assistance for Solo
Parents Program (FASPP) of the Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD), providing assistance for food, medical expenses, education, livelihood,
cash relief, and funeral expenses, as well as rice assistance through local
government units. It likewise establishes clear eligibility and assessment
procedures, safeguards against misuse of public funds, and mechanisms for
monitoring and accountability.

      Through this measure, the State seeks to strengthen social protection for
solo parents, complement existing benefits under the law, and ensure that they
and their dependents receive timely and responsive assistance.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                              “AN ACT
            INSTITUTIONALIZING THE FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE
            FOR SOLO PARENTS PROGRAM (FASPP) OF THE
            DEPARTMENT     OF   SOCIAL   WELFARE    AND
            DEVELOPMENT (DSWD), PROVIDING FOR ITS
            IMPLEMENTATION, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
            THEREFOR”

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Solo Parents
Economic Support Act.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of
the State to recognize and protect solo parents and their dependents as a
vulnerable sector and to promote their welfare by ensuring access to timely,
adequate, and responsive social protection, financial assistance, and essential
goods and services necessary for food security, health, education, livelihood, and
other basic needs.

       It is further declared the policy of the State to institutionalize a
coordinated, accessible, and sustainable system of financial assistance for
qualified solo parents through the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD), in partnership with local government units (LGUs) and
other relevant government agencies and service providers, particularly for solo
parents whose limited incomes make them vulnerable to poverty, economic
shocks, inflation, illness, and other circumstances that threaten the well-being
and continued support of their dependents.
      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

      1. Financial Assistance for Solo Parents Program (FASPP) - refers to the
         program implemented by the DSWD providing various forms of
         financial and other assistance to qualified solo parents under this Act;

      2. Solo Parent - refers to an individual who qualifies as a solo parent under
         Republic Act No. 11861, otherwise known as the "Expanded Solo
         Parents Welfare Act," and its implementing rules and regulations;

      3. Beneficiary – refers to a solo parent whose gross monthly income from
         all sources does not exceed Fourteen Thousand Pesos (PhP 14,000.00),
         computed in accordance with the guidelines prescribed by the DSWD,
         and who is qualified to receive assistance under this Act, subject to
         assessment and submission of the required documentary evidence;

      4. Dependent - refers to a child or dependent qualified under Republic Act
         No. 11861 and its implementing rules and regulations;

      5. Certificate of Eligibility (CE) - refers to a document issued by the DSWD
         certifying that a solo parent is eligible to avail of assistance under the
         FASPP;

      6. General Intake Sheet (GIS) - refers to the form used by DSWD social
         workers to record relevant information on the solo parent and the
         dependents;

      7. Social Case Study Report (SCSR) - refers to the document prepared by
         a professional social worker describing the socio-economic condition,
         assessment, and recommended interventions for the solo parent;

      8. Service Providers - refers to entities providing goods and services
         covered by the FASPP, including but not limited to hospitals, clinics,
         educational institutions, pharmacies, and other relevant providers; and

      9. Guarantee Letter (GL) - refers to a document issued by the DSWD
         guaranteeing payment to service providers on behalf of the solo parent.

      SECTION 4. Coverage and Modalities in Providing Assistance. -
Financial and other assistance shall be provided through the DSWD and in
partnership with LGUs, subject to eligibility, assessment of need, and availability
of funds, under the following modalities.

      1. Implementation through the DSWD. - Qualified solo parents who need
financial assistance for goods and services may avail of the following:
a. Food Assistance - refers to outright cash assistance or other
   forms of assistance provided to meet the food and nutritional
   requirements of the solo parent and the dependents;

b. Medical Assistance - refers to outright cash assistance or a
   Guarantee Letter provided to cover hospitalization expenses and
   professional fees, cost of medicines, and other medical treatment
   or procedures, such as common laboratory tests and diagnostic
   imaging for any illness or medical condition, including
   postpartum complications. This assistance may also cover
   necessary medical aftercare directly related to the treatment or
   condition;

c. Educational Assistance - refers to outright cash assistance or a
   Guarantee Letter provided to cover school-related expenses of the
   solo parent and/or dependents, such as tuition and
   miscellaneous fees, purchase of school supplies, uniforms,
   books, payment of transportation expenses, and other incidental
   costs necessary for the continued attendance and completion of
   primary, secondary, or tertiary education, including technical-
   vocational education or skills training programs, subject to
   assessment by the DSWD social worker;

d. Livelihood Assistance - refers to outright cash assistance or a
   support package granted to a qualified solo parent for the
   purpose of initiating, sustaining, or expanding a small-scale
   livelihood or income-generating activity. This assistance may
   cover the procurement of necessary tools, raw materials, training
   costs, or other inputs directly related to the livelihood
   undertaking, as determined by the assessment and
   recommendation of the DSWD social worker;

e. Cash Relief Assistance - refers to outright cash assistance
   provided to qualified beneficiaries for essential expenses arising
   from financial difficulty or circumstances affecting their capacity
   to provide for their dependents, including but not limited to the
   effects of high inflation, difficulty in paying house rental,
   electricity and water expenses, and other urgent household
   necessities, as determined by the assessment of the DSWD social
   worker; and

f. Funeral Assistance - refers to outright cash assistance or a
   Guarantee Letter provided to help defray funeral and burial
   expenses arising from the death of the solo parent or any
   qualified dependent. In case of the death of the solo parent, the
   assistance may be claimed by the deceased's qualified dependent
               or an immediate family member or authorized representative who
               incurred or assumed responsibility for the funeral and burial
               expenses, subject to assessment by the DSWD social worker.

      Cash assistance may be granted outright in amounts determined by the
DSWD based on the assessed needs of the beneficiary, not exceeding Fifteen
Thousand Pesos (PhP 15,000.00) per approved assistance transaction.
Assistance exceeding Fifteen Thousand Pesos (PhP 15,000.00) may be provided
through a Guarantee Letter, direct payment to the service provider, or other
appropriate non-cash assistance modality, subject to applicable approval
requirements.

       No application shall be divided into multiple transactions for the purpose
of circumventing the applicable approval limits.

       The foregoing monetary limits shall apply to each approved assistance
transaction and shall not be construed to prevent a qualified beneficiary from
receiving other forms of assistance under this Act, subject to separate
assessment and applicable requirements.

      The implementation of this Act shall be complementary to, and shall be
harmonized with, Republic Act No. 11861, otherwise known as the "Expanded
Solo Parents Welfare Act." The benefits and privileges granted under Republic
Act No. 11861 shall remain governed by the eligibility requirements, conditions,
and limitations provided therein. The receipt of assistance under this Act shall
not, by itself, affect the entitlement of a qualified solo parent to other benefits
and privileges under Republic Act No. 11861, subject to the restrictions on the
receipt of other government cash assistance or subsidies expressly provided
under Section 15(a) thereof.

       The distribution of assistance shall be without prejudice to the adoption
of digital payment solutions once the necessary infrastructure is in place.

       2. Implementation through Local Government Units (LGUs). - The financial
assistance that shall be provided to qualified beneficiaries through the LGUs
shall be called rice assistance. For this purpose, the DSWD shall enter into a
Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) with the concerned LGUs to provide the
details of the transfer of funds, implementation arrangements, monitoring, and
requirements for liquidation, in accordance with existing budgeting, accounting,
and auditing rules and regulations.

      The amount of rice assistance that may be provided to qualified
beneficiaries shall be equivalent to one-half (1/2) of the cost of twenty-five (25)
kilograms of rice, based on the prevailing suggested retail price (SRP) determined
by the Department of Agriculture (DA) for the applicable rice variety at the time
of computation. The amount of each fund transfer to an LGU shall not exceed
Thirty Million Pesos (PhP 30,000,000.00).

       The financial assistance under this Act may complement any support
provided by the LGUs, other government agencies, and non-government
organizations. A beneficiary who has received rice assistance through the LGU
may still qualify to receive other financial assistance from the DSWD, subject to
the requirements under this Act and the professional assessment of the DSWD
social worker.

       The DSWD shall establish appropriate coordination and monitoring
mechanisms to ensure the proper utilization of funds transferred to LGUs and
prevent duplication of assistance for the same purpose, without unduly
restricting a beneficiary's access to other forms of assistance under this Act.

       SECTION 5. Eligibility and Documentary Requirements. - To qualify
for assistance under this Act, an applicant must:

      1. Be a qualified solo parent under Republic Act No. 11861;

      2. Have a gross monthly income not exceeding Fourteen Thousand Pesos
         (PhP 14,000.00); and

      3. Be assessed by a DSWD social worker as having a financial or other
         legitimate need warranting assistance under this Act.

      4. Comply with the applicable eligibility requirements and restrictions
         under Republic Act No. 11861, particularly with respect to the receipt
         of other government cash assistance or subsidies.

       For purposes of assessment and evaluation, the solo parent shall submit
the following:

            a. Solo Parent Identification Card (SPIC); Provided, That in the
               absence of an SPIC, the DSWD and/or the LGU shall assist the
               solo parent in securing the same, and the absence thereof shall
               not, by itself, be a ground for denying or unduly delaying urgent
               assistance, subject to verification of the applicant's eligibility;
               and

            b. Proof of residency, such as a barangay certificate, utility bill,
               Voter's Identification Card, or other equivalent documents
               acceptable to the DSWD.

      The DSWD may require additional supporting documents when
reasonably necessary to establish the applicant's eligibility and the nature of the
assistance requested. The documentary requirements shall be applied in a
manner that facilitates access to assistance and avoids unnecessary
administrative burdens on qualified solo parents.

      SECTION 6. Assessment and Approval of Assistance. - The DSWD shall
establish a uniform assessment and approval procedure for applications under
the FASPP.

      A DSWD social worker shall assess the applicant's eligibility, financial
circumstances, and the nature and extent of the assistance required. The
assessment may be supported by a General Intake Sheet, Social Case Study
Report, or other appropriate assessment instruments prescribed by the DSWD.
The DSWD shall issue a Certificate of Eligibility to qualified beneficiaries and
determine the appropriate form and amount of assistance based on the
applicant's assessed needs, subject to the limits and approval requirements
under this Act.

       The DSWD shall endeavor to process applications promptly, particularly
those involving medical emergencies, urgent subsistence needs, and other crisis
situations.

      The DSWD may adopt simplified procedures for urgent applications,
subject to subsequent verification and compliance with applicable budgeting,
accounting, and auditing rules and regulations.

      The DSWD shall establish appropriate coordination and information-
sharing mechanisms with concerned government agencies and local government
units to verify the assistance received by applicants and beneficiaries, prevent
duplication of cash assistance or subsidies where prohibited by law, and ensure
the proper implementation of this Act in relation to Republic Act No. 11861,
subject to applicable data privacy and confidentiality laws.

      SECTION 7. Penalties. - The penalty of imprisonment of not less than one
(1) month but not more than one (1) year, or a fine of not less than Ten Thousand
Pesos (PhP 10,000.00) but not more than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (PhP
100,000.00), or both imprisonment and fine, at the discretion of the court, shall
be imposed upon any person who knowingly commits any of the following acts:

      1. Submits falsified documents or knowingly makes material
         misrepresentations in order to qualify for or obtain assistance under
         this Act;

      2. Knowingly causes or aids in the grant of financial assistance to persons
         who are not qualified beneficiaries under this Act;
      3. Diverts, withholds, or misappropriates funds or assistance intended for
         qualified beneficiaries;

      4. Knowingly authorizes or causes the disbursement of assistance in
         violation of the eligibility requirements or monetary limits prescribed
         under this Act; or

      5. Connives with another person to commit any of the foregoing acts.

       A public officer or employee who commits or aids in the commission of any
of the foregoing acts shall, upon conviction, suffer the penalties provided herein,
without prejudice to administrative liability, disqualification from public office,
and other penalties under applicable laws.

       Nothing in this Act shall be construed to impose criminal liability upon a
social worker or public officer solely for an honest error in assessment or a good-
faith exercise of professional judgment in the performance of official duties,
without prejudice to liability arising from fraud, malice, gross negligence,
manifest partiality, or violations of other applicable laws.

      SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within six (6)
months from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of the DSWD, in
consultation with relevant government agencies, LGUs, service providers, and
other stakeholders, shall formulate and promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations to effectively implement the provisions of this Act.

       The implementing rules and regulations shall include, among others, the
eligibility verification process, standards for assessment, documentary
requirements, approval procedures, forms of assistance, fund transfer and
liquidation arrangements with LGUs, monitoring mechanisms, and measures to
ensure transparency and accountability.

     SECTION 9. Monitoring and Reporting. - The DSWD shall establish a
monitoring and evaluation system for the implementation of the FASPP.

      The DSWD shall submit the annual report to Congress not later than
March 31 of each year, covering the implementation of the FASPP during the
preceding calendar year.:

      1.   The number and geographical distribution of beneficiaries;

      2.   The amount of funds appropriated, released, and utilized;

      3.   The number of beneficiaries who received assistance under each
           modality;
      4.   The status of fund transfers and liquidation by participating LGUs;
           and

      5.   Such other information as may be necessary to evaluate the
           effectiveness and impact of the program.

      The report shall be prepared in a manner consistent with applicable data
privacy and confidentiality laws and regulations.

      SECTION 10. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year's
appropriations of the DSWD. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

       The DSWD shall include in its annual budget proposal the funding
requirements necessary to ensure the effective and continuing implementation
of the FASPP.

      SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders,
proclamations, rules, regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

        SECTION 12. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

       SECTION 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
following its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of
general circulation.

      Approved.
