                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The role of a father in the immediate postpartum period is crucial for the well-being of
both the mother and the newborn. Postpartum period1 refers to the period after delivery when
the maternal physiological and anatomical changes return to non-pregnant state, which can last
up to 6 weeks to 6 months. During this period, the presence of the father is not just a passive
supporting role, but an active intervention for the recovery of the mother and the care of the
newborn.

       While there are legislative reforms that rightfully expanded maternity leave to at least
105 days, the paternity leave remains at seven (7) days. It is interesting to note that when
Republic Act No. 8187 was passed in 1996, childrearing was viewed as a primary maternal
duty. At present, our understanding of the family dynamic has shifted toward a model of active
co-parenting, where fathers are expected to be direct caregivers from day one.

        The current 7-day leave is disproportionate to the actual responsibilities of a modern
father. As such, this bill seeks to expand the scope of paternity leave in two ways: (1) by
removing the qualifying circumstance of being married, and (2) by extending the period of the
leave from the seven (7) days to fifteen (15) days.

        Through this bill, we are not just adapting with the evolving societal norms; we are
moving towards the path of excellent childrearing and maternal care practices in the country.
This also ensures that the burden of recovery and the joy of early parenthood are shared, which
fosters a stronger foundation for Filipino families.

    AN ACT EXPANDING THE SCOPE OF AND PROVIDING FOR EXPANDED
     PATERNITY LEAVE BENEFITS, AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8187,
        OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “PATERNITY LEAVE OF 1996”

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      Section 1. Section 2 of the Republic Act No. 8187 is hereby amended to read as follows:

              Section 2. Notwithstanding any law, rules and regulations to the contrary, every
      [married] male employee in the private and public sectors, REGARDLESS OF
      EMPLOYMENT OR CIVIL STATUS, shall be entitled to a paternity leave of [seven
      (7) days] FIFTEEN (15) WORKING DAYS with full pay for the first four (4)
      deliveries of the spouse OR PARTNER with whom he is EXCLUSIVELY cohabiting.
      The male employee applying for paternity leave shall notify his employer of the
      pregnancy of his legitimate spouse OR HIS PARTNER and the expected date of such
      delivery.

           For purposes of this Act, delivery shall include childbirth, [or any] miscarriage,
      OR EMERGENCY TERMINATION OF PREGNANCY.

      Section 2. Section 3 of Republic Act No. 8187 is further amended to read as follows:

              Section 3. Definition of Term. – For purpose of this Act, Paternity Leave refers
      to the benefits granted to a [married] male employee, regardless of EMPLOYMENT
      OR CIVIL STATUS, allowing him not to report for work for [seven (7) days]
      FIFTEEN (15) WORKING DAYS but continues to earn the compensation therefor,
       on the condition that his spouse OR HIS PARTNER has delivered a child [or], suffered
       a miscarriage, OR EMERGENCY TERMINATION OF PREGNANCY for
       purposes of enabling him to effectively lend support to his wife OR HIS PARTNER,
       in her period of recovery and/or in the nursing of the newly-born child.

       Section 3. Separability Clause – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in full force
and effect.

        Section 4. Repealing Clause – All laws, executive orders, decrees, rules and
regulations, or any part thereof inconsistent with the provision of this Act are deemed repealed
or modified accordingly.

        Section 5. Effectivity – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
