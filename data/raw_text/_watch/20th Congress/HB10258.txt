    AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTIONS 6, 43, 45 AND 71 OF REPUBLIC
              ACT NO. 9136, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Republic Act No. 9136, otherwise known as the Electric Power Industry
Reform Act of 2001 (EPIRA), restructured the electric power industry by dividing it
into four (4) sectors—generation, transmission, distribution, and supply—and by
adopting a regulatory framework intended to promote competition, market
development, consumer protection, and the penalization of market power abuse. 1

        EPIRA likewise declared that the generation sector, while a “business affected
with public interest,” “shall not be considered a public utility operation,” and that
entities engaged in power generation and supply of electricity “shall not be required
to secure a national franchise.”2 The Supreme Court has acknowledged that this
legislative classification produces concrete legal consequences, including the
removal of power generation from the ambit of local franchise taxes that may be
imposed only on businesses “enjoying a franchise.”3 At the same time, jurisprudence
addressing constitutional challenges to EPIRA’s design recognizes that EPIRA
continues to treat the generation and supply sectors as businesses affected with
public interest and subjects industry participants to rules against market power
abuse, cartelization, and other anti-competitive or discriminatory behavior.4

        While EPIRA contains safeguards against harmful monopoly and market
power abuse, including ERC authority to monitor and take measures against
cartelization and other anti-competitive conduct, continued sector experience has
underscored the need for stronger structural safeguards, transparency requirements,

       Further, EPIRA’s design places policy formulation and rulemaking within the
Department of Energy (DOE) while the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC)
enforces the implementing framework within its delegated regulatory and quasi-
judicial powers; administrative issuances must remain germane to EPIRA’s
objectives and cannot contradict the statute.

Accordingly, this bill seeks to: (1) strengthen the legal framework governing the
generation sector by treating generation as a public utility operation while
implementing a workable authorization regime, and by enhancing transparency and
enforcement against market power abuse; (2) strengthen safeguards against
structural self-dealing and cross-ownership between generation and distribution,
and prohibit prohibited affiliate supply arrangements subject to due process and a
clear transition; (3) reinforce ERC powers and disclosure requirements in relation to
pass-through items and consumer protection, including clearer accountability for
system losses; and (4) provide transitory provisions to ensure uninterrupted service
and orderly compliance.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.
