                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill seeks to mandate the establishment of public crematoriums in all cities
and municipalities across the Philippines. It amends Section 17 of Republic Act (RA) No.
7160, otherwise known as the “Local Government Code of 1991”, to include public
crematoriums among the basic facilities and services that local government units must
provide. This provides for a dignified, affordable and accessible alternative to
traditional burial services while ensuring free cremation services for indigent families.

       Public cemeteries throughout the country are currently experiencing congestion,
while the rising cost of funeral and burial services places financial burden on many
Filipino families. Although cremation has emerged as a practical and space-efficient
alternative, its use remains largely dependent on private facilities whose services are
often beyond the means of low-income households. The absence of
government-operated and maintained crematoriums limits affordable options and
compels many families to incur debts during periods of bereavement.

       This measure strengthens the capacity of cities and municipalities to deliver
essential funeral services, improve land use management, and promote public health
and social welfare.

        To safeguard public health and environmental integrity, all public crematoriums
shall comply with the requirements of Presidential Decree No. 856, otherwise known as
the “Code on Sanitation of the Philippines”, and RA No. 8749, otherwise known as the
“Philippine Clean Air Act of 1999”.

        This bill offers a sustainable solution to urban congestion and alleviates the
financial distress of bereaved Filipino families. It ensures that even in death, all citizens
are afforded dignity without placing an economic burden on those they leave behind.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                          AN ACT
MANDATING THE ESTABLISHMENT OF PUBLIC CREMATORIUMS IN CITIES
AND MUNICIPALITIES, PROVIDING FREE CREMATION SERVICES TO
INDIGENT FAMILIES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTION 17 OF
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7160, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “LOCAL
GOVERNMENT CODE OF 1991”

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Public Crematorium Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby the declared policy of the State to
promote social justice and protect the welfare of the people by ensuring access to
affordable, dignified, and environmentally sound cremation services. Toward this end,
the State shall support local government units (LGUs) in the establishment and
operation of public crematoriums and provide free cremation services to indigent
families.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

          a. Crematorium refers to a designated place duly authorized by law to
       cremate dead persons; and

          b. Indigent refers to a person who has no visible means of support or whose
       income falls below the minimum wage in the region, as determined by the
       Regional Tripartite Wage Board; or whose income falls within the minimum
       wage but has two (2) other persons dependent upon him or her for support.

       SEC. 4. Establishment of Public Crematoriums. – Section 17 (b)(2)(x) of Republic No.
7160, otherwise known as the “Local Government Code of 1991”, as amended, is hereby
amended to read as follows:

       SEC. 5. Regulatory Compliance. – The design, construction, technical requirements,
sanitary certification, operation, and maintenance of public crematoriums, as well as the
handling of dead bodies, shall be in compliance with Presidential Decree No. 856,
otherwise known as the “Code on Sanitation of the Philippines”, and Republic Act (RA)
No. 8749, otherwise known as the “Philippine Clean Air Act of 1999”, and their respective
implementing rules and regulations.

       SEC. 6. Anti-Pollution Measures. – The Department of Environment and Natural
Resources (DENR), in coordination with the Department of Health (DOH), shall issue
the necessary guidelines and practices promoting anti-pollution measures such as, but
not limited to, installation of filter systems and similar pollution control devices,
designation of pollution officers, neutralization of pollutants and toxic wastes, reduction
of particulate matter, more efficient use of fuel and energy, adequate controls on
emission levels, among others.

         SEC. 7. Coverage. – Free cremation services under this Act shall be available to
indigent families, as certified by the city or municipal social welfare and development
office, in accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations.

       SEC. 8. Provision of Free Cremation Services for Indigents. – Notwithstanding any
provision of law, decedents belonging to indigent families, as determined by the city or
municipal social welfare and development office, shall be entitled to free cremation
services in public crematoriums established under this Act. The local city or municipal
health officer shall duly record and maintain a registry of cremations performed in
public crematoriums.

       SEC. 9. Appropriations – The establishment, operation, maintenance, and
improvement of public crematoriums shall be funded from the local funds of the
concerned LGUs, including their respective shares from national taxes and other
available local revenues, in accordance with Section 17(g) of RA No. 7160, as amended.

        SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Interior and Local Government, in coordination

shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of
this Act.

       SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All provisions of laws and rules inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

       SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If for any reason, any section or provision of this
Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other sections or provisions not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following its
publication in a newspaper of general circulation.
