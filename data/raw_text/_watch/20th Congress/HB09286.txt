                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

    Section 24, Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution provides that the State
recognizes the vital role of communication and information in nation-building.
Information, after all, is power. Mere recognition of its power, however, is not enough.
One can only witness the true power of information when it has been used and applied
effectively in nation-building. Instilling discipline, for instance, requires information.
A disciplined citizenry presupposes an informed society. One cannot be expected to
obey laws, rules, or code of behavior without knowing and understanding what these
laws, rules, and code of behavior are. Clearly, the role of communication and
information is vital in nation-building.

     The government is the agency or instrumentality through which the will of the State
is formulated, expressed and realized.1 One of the important tasks of the government
is to act for the State as parens patriae, or guardian of the rights of the people.2 As
guardian of the rights of the people, it is the duty of the government to see to it that the
citizens are well-informed of their rights, duties and responsibilities. It is only when
people are well-informed of these rights, duties, and responsibilities that they realize
the importance of their role and purpose in society.

     Mass media, on the other hand, is the primary means of communication to reach
the vast majority of the general public. The vast reach and influence of media can be
seen especially during elections. Political advertisements have been an effective tool in
conveying a candidate’s message and in gaining votes and public support. Television,
for instance, is considered the most cost effective medium of dissemination.3 Even a
slight increase in television exposure can significantly boost a candidate's popularity,
name recall and electability.4 If mass media can be used as means to get votes, the more
it can be used to inform the public of their rights, their duties, and their responsibilities.
Above all, mass media has the duty to inform people.

     Considering all these, it is vital that we have a law mandating mass media, in
particular radio and television broadcasting companies to allocate airtime for public
service announcements and infomercials on laws, social welfare, public safety,
government processes and other matters of public and national concern to increase
awareness and inform the public of their rights, duties and responsibilities.

    In truth, radio and television broadcasting companies, which are given franchises,
do not own the airwaves and frequencies through which they transmit broadcast signals
and images. They are merely given the temporary privilege of using them. Since a
franchise is a mere privilege, the exercise of the privilege may reasonably be burdened
with the performance by the grantee of some form of public service.5 By allocating
public service announcements and government infomercials, TV and radio networks
are performing some form of public service for the Filipino people. It is time
government and mass media work together for the betterment of this country.

    In view of the foregoing, immediate passage of this bill is earnestly requested.

                          AN ACT
REQUIRING BROADCAST MEDIA TO ALLOCATE ADEQUATE FREE
AIRTIME FOR PUBLIC SERVICE ANNOUNCEMENTS ON VITAL PUBLIC
ISSUES AND CONCERNS

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

    SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Public Service
Announcement Act.”
    SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the vital role of
communication and information in nation-building, and the influence, breadth,
and accessibility of broadcast media to reach the population on important public
issues. Towards this end, the State shall adopt measures to implement an
integrated approach to enable the government to reach the people and keep
them well-informed and cognizant of the issues and concerns affecting them.
    SEC. 3. Coverage.–This Act shall apply to all radio and television
broadcasting stations.
    For purposes of this Act, radio and television broadcasting station refers to
radio and television networks engaged in the business of distributing audio and
video content to a dispersed audience via any audio, mass, digital or online
communication media.
    SEC.    4.   Public    Service    Announcement.–       A   public   service
announcement (PSA) is a non-commercial broadcast advertisement for the
purpose of educating the public to generate awareness and disseminate
information on matters of public interest.
    SEC. 5. Allocation for Public Service Announcements. – To fulfill the
obligation of broadcast media to render public service under their franchises, all
broadcast media are required to allocate adequate media time for public service
announcements, free of charge, an accumulated maximum of two (2) minutes
within each hour of broadcast to enable the government to effectively reach out
to the people, educate and increase their awareness, and disseminate
information on matters of public concern. The time allocation does not refer to
the length of the PSA, which is subject to prevailing industry standards.
    The time allocation requirement may be suspended during special programs
or coverage of natural calamities, disasters and emergencies and events of
national significance where the insertion of non-program content is impractical
to perform during the duration of said special program or coverage.
    SEC. 6. Production of Public Service Announcements.– The Philippine
Information Agency shall produce public service announcements designed to
promote and inform the public on the latest issues, announcements, warnings,
plans and programs of the government to be aired by the broadcast media. All
national government agencies are encouraged to develop their own PSA, if
warranted, and submit the same to the Philippine Information Agency (PIA) for
consideration and facilitation of its broadcast.
    The public service announcement under this Act shall be non-commercial,
non-denominational and non-political.
    Public announcements involving public figures running for elective posts in
the elections shall not be allowed and shall be subject to existing election laws
and COMELEC promulgations on media use.
    SEC. 7. Availment by Government Agencies. – All government
departments, bureaus, offices, agencies or instrumentalities, including
government-owned and controlled corporations, except local government units,
are enjoined to utilize, cooperate and avail of the benefits under this Act.
    SEC. 8. Penalty. – The failure of any covered broadcasting station to
provide the required public service time under this Act shall be a ground for the
suspension, revocation, or non-renewal of license to operate television and
radio networks.
    SEC. 9. Monitoring. – The Philippine Information Agency, in coordination
with the National Telecommunications Commission shall monitor the
compliance of covered broadcast media with the provisions of this Act.
    They shall submit a quarterly report to the Senate of the Philippines and
House of Representatives on the compliance of broadcast media and the
implementation of this Act.
    SEC. 10.    Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement the
provisions of this Act shall be charged to the current year’s appropriations of
the Philippine Information agency and the National Telecommunications
Commission. Thereafter, such sum as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.
    SEC. 11.    Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the approval of this Act, the Philippine Information Agency and the
National Telecommunications Commission, in coordination with the Kapisanan
ng mga Brodkaster ng Pilipinas, promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.     The implementing
rules and regulations shall include the determination of time standards for public
service announcements of government agencies, offices or instrumentalities.
    SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees, rules and regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with any provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.
    SEC. 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.


    Approved
