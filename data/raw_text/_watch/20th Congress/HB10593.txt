                               AN ACT
AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10591, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
“COMPREHENSIVE FIREARMS AND AMMUNITION REGULATION ACT,” BY
REQUIRING THE SAFE STORAGE OF FIREARMS AND AMMUNITION, PREVENTING
UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS BY MINORS, IMPOSING LIABILITY FOR CHILD FIREARM
ACCESS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Recent incidents involving the access of minors to irearms demonstrate the need for
more speci ic and enforceable safe-storage requirements. Republic Act No. 10591
presently provides for the licensing and regulation of irearms, requires applicants to
pass a gun safety seminar, and authorizes the suspension, cancellation, or revocation
of a irearm license for, among other grounds, the loss of a irearm through negligence.

However, the law does not expressly establish a detailed statutory duty requiring
 irearm owners and custodians to secure irearms and ammunition against access by
minors. Nor does it provide a speci ic offense for knowingly or negligently permitting
a minor to obtain unauthorized access to a irearm.

The State has a legitimate interest in preventing irearm-related injury and death.
Firearm ownership and possession are statutory privileges subject to the conditions
imposed by law, and not constitutional property rights.

This measure therefore proposes mandatory safe-storage requirements, a child-
 irearm-access offense, administrative sanctions, reporting duties, and a rebuttable
presumption of negligence in appropriate civil and criminal proceedings. The

proposed law preserves due process by requiring proof of knowledge or negligence
and a causal connection between the storage violation and the resulting harm.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                               AN ACT
AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10591, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Child Firearm Access
Prevention and Safe Storage Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to:

   (a) Protect children and other vulnerable persons from unauthorized access to
        irearms and ammunition;

   (b) Promote responsible irearm ownership and possession;

   (c) Require irearm owners, licensees, and custodians to exercise the highest
       degree of care reasonably necessary in the storage, handling, and disposition
       of irearms;

   (d) Prevent the loss, theft, unauthorized use, and accidental discharge of irearms;
       and

                                                                           3|Page
   (e) Impose proportionate civil, criminal, and administrative consequences for
       violations of the safe-storage requirements under this Act.

SECTION 3. Amendment to Section 3 of Republic Act No. 10591. —

Section 3 of Republic Act No. 10591 is hereby amended by adding the following
de initions:

     (aa) Child or minor refers to a person below eighteen (18) years of age, or a
     person over eighteen (18) years of age who is unable to fully protect himself or
     herself from abuse, neglect, or exploitation because of a physical or mental
     disability.

     (bb) Firearm custodian refers to any person who has actual or constructive
     possession, control, or custody of a irearm, whether as owner, licensee, household
     member, employer, security of icer, or authorized representative of a juridical
     entity.

     (cc) Safe-storage device refers to a locked irearm safe, locked container, trigger
     lock, cable lock, chamber lock, or other security device approved or prescribed by
     the Firearms and Explosives Of ice of the Philippine National Police.

     (dd) Unauthorized access refers to the acquisition, handling, possession, or use
     of a irearm or ammunition by a person who is not legally authorized to possess or
     use the same, including a minor.

SECTION 4. Insertion of a New Section 24-A in Republic Act No. 10591. —

A new Section 24-A is hereby inserted after Section 24 of Republic Act No. 10591, to
read as follows:

     “SEC. 24-A. Mandatory Safe Storage of Firearms and Ammunition.

     “Every irearm owner, licensee, or irearm custodian shall exercise due diligence
     and shall comply with the following requirements:

     “(a) When not in immediate lawful use, the irearm shall be stored in an
     unloaded condition inside a safe-storage device;


                                                                           4|Page
     “(b) Ammunition shall be stored in a locked container or compartment separate
     from the irearm whenever practicable;

     “(c) Keys, combinations, access cards, passwords, and biometric access
     credentials for the safe-storage device shall be kept inaccessible to minors and
     unauthorized persons;

     “(d) The irearm shall not be left unattended in a place or manner that permits
     a minor or unauthorized person to obtain access to it;

     “(e) A irearm stored in a vehicle shall be secured in a locked container that is
     not visible from outside the vehicle and shall not be left in the vehicle for an
     unreasonable period;

     “(f) The irearm owner or custodian shall take reasonable measures to prevent
     household members, visitors, domestic workers, and other persons from
     obtaining unauthorized access to the irearm; and

     “(g) Any loss, theft, or suspected unauthorized access involving a irearm shall
     be reported to the nearest police station or appropriate unit of the Philippine
     National Police within twenty-four (24) hours from discovery.

     “The Chief of the Philippine National Police, through the Firearms and
     Explosives Of ice, shall issue rules prescribing minimum technical standards for
     safe-storage devices, taking into account the type of irearm, the place of storage,
     and the circumstances of possession.

     “Nothing in this section shall be construed to prohibit the temporary deposit of
     a registered irearm with the Firearms and Explosives Of ice or a Police Regional
     Of ice for safekeeping, consistent with Section 24 of this Act.”

SECTION 5. Insertion of a New Section 24-B in Republic Act No. 10591. —

A new Section 24-B is hereby inserted after Section 24-A of Republic Act No. 10591,
to read as follows:

     “SEC. 24-B. Child Firearm Access Prevention.




                                                                            5|Page
     “Any irearm owner, licensee, or irearm custodian who knowingly or
     negligently permits a minor to obtain unauthorized access to a irearm or
     ammunition shall be liable under this Act.

     “For purposes of this section, a person shall be deemed to have knowingly or
     negligently permitted unauthorized access when:

     “(a) The irearm or ammunition was not stored in accordance with Section 24-
     A of this Act;

     “(b) The person knew, or by the exercise of reasonable diligence should have
     known, that a minor could obtain access to the irearm or ammunition; and

     “(c) The minor obtained access because of the person’s failure to adopt
     reasonable security measures.

     “The prosecution shall prove the accused’s knowledge or negligence and the
     causal connection between the violation and the minor’s access or resulting act.

     “Compliance with the storage requirements under Section 24-A shall not be a
     defense when the irearm was intentionally or knowingly made available to the
     minor.”

SECTION 6. Insertion of a New Section 24-C in Republic Act No. 10591. —

A new Section 24-C is hereby inserted after Section 24-B of Republic Act No. 10591,
to read as follows:

     “SEC. 24-C. Penalties for Child Firearm Access.

     “Any person who violates Section 24-B of this Act shall suffer the following
     penalties:

     “(a) If the minor merely obtains unauthorized access to the irearm or
     ammunition, imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but not more than
     two (2) years, and a ine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos (₱50,000.00) but
     not more than Two hundred thousand pesos (₱200,000.00);

     “(b) If the irearm is discharged but no person suffers physical injury or death,
     imprisonment of not less than two (2) years but not more than six (6) years, and

                                                                         6|Page
     a ine of not less than Two hundred thousand pesos (₱200,000.00) but not more
     than Five hundred thousand pesos (₱500,000.00);

     “(c) If the unauthorized access results in serious physical injuries, imprisonment
     of not less than six (6) years but not more than twelve (12) years, and a ine of
     not less than Five hundred thousand pesos (₱500,000.00) but not more than
     One million pesos (₱1,000,000.00); and

     “(d) If the unauthorized access results in the death of any person, imprisonment
     of not less than twelve (12) years but not more than twenty (20) years, and a
      ine of not less than One million pesos (₱1,000,000.00) but not more than Two
     million pesos (₱2,000,000.00).

     “The penalties under this section shall be without prejudice to prosecution
     under the Revised Penal Code or any other applicable law, when the elements of
     the other offense are present.

     “If the offender is a parent, legal guardian, or person exercising substitute
     parental authority over the minor, the court may consider the relationship as an
     aggravating circumstance, unless the relationship is an element of the offense
     charged.

     “If the offender is a juridical entity, the responsible of icer, director, trustee,
     partner, or irearm custodian who authorized, tolerated, or failed to prevent the
     violation shall be liable, without prejudice to the civil liability of the juridical
     entity.”

SECTION 7. Rebuttable Presumption of Negligence. —

A new Section 24-D is hereby inserted after Section 24-C of Republic Act No. 10591,
to read as follows:

     “SEC. 24-D. Rebuttable Presumption in Civil and Criminal Proceedings.

     “Failure to comply with the mandatory safe-storage requirements under Section
     24-A shall constitute prima facie evidence of negligence in a civil action for
     damages when a minor obtains unauthorized access to the irearm.

     “In a criminal prosecution under Section 24-C, failure to comply with Section 24-
     A shall create a rebuttable presumption of negligence only when:

                                                                             7|Page
     “(a) The minor obtained unauthorized access to the irearm;

     “(b) The irearm was discharged or used to commit an unlawful act; and

     “(c) The resulting injury, death, or unlawful act was a reasonably foreseeable
     consequence of the failure to comply with the safe-storage requirements.

     “The presumption may be rebutted by proof that the irearm was secured in
     accordance with law, that the access resulted from an unforeseeable and
     unlawful act despite reasonable precautions, or that the violation did not legally
     cause the injury, death, or unlawful act.

     “Nothing in this section shall relieve the prosecution of its burden to prove all
     the elements of the offense beyond reasonable doubt.”

SECTION 8. Amendment to Section 39 of Republic Act No. 10591. —

Section 39 of Republic Act No. 10591 is hereby amended by adding the following
paragraphs:

     “(k) Violation of the mandatory safe-storage requirements under Section 24-A of
     this Act;

     “(l) Failure to report the loss, theft, or suspected unauthorized access of a irearm
     within the period prescribed under Section 24-A of this Act;

     “(m) Allowing a minor or unauthorized person to obtain access to a irearm or
     ammunition through negligence; and

     “(n) Failure to surrender or deposit a irearm that is subject to a lawful order of
     suspension, cancellation, revocation, con iscation, or safekeeping.”

Upon the iling of a criminal complaint supported by probable cause for a violation of
Section 24-B or Section 24-C of this Act, the Chief of the Philippine National Police or
the authorized representative may impose a preventive suspension of the irearm
license, subject to notice and opportunity to be heard under the implementing rules.

The suspension, cancellation, or revocation of a irearm license shall be without
prejudice to the criminal and civil liability of the irearm owner, licensee, or custodian.

                                                                              8|Page
SECTION 9. Administrative Sanctions. —

In addition to the criminal penalties under this Act, the following administrative
sanctions may be imposed after notice and hearing:

   (a) For a irst violation of Section 24-A without resulting injury or death, a ine of
       not less than Ten thousand pesos (₱10,000.00) but not more than Fifty
       thousand pesos (₱50,000.00), and suspension of the irearm license for not
       more than six (6) months;

   (b) For a second violation, suspension of the irearm license for one (1) year and
       con iscation of the irearm, subject to applicable proceedings;

   (c) For a violation resulting in serious physical injuries or death, revocation of the
        irearm license and forfeiture of the irearm, without prejudice to criminal
       prosecution; and

   (d) For repeated violations or failure to comply with an order of suspension,
       cancellation, revocation, or safekeeping, disquali ication from applying for a
        irearm license for a period of ive (5) years, or perpetual disquali ication when
       authorized by law and the applicable rules.

The administrative proceedings under this section shall observe due process and shall
not prevent the iling of a separate civil or criminal action.

SECTION 10. Safe-Storage Training and Certi ication. —

The gun safety seminar required for the issuance or renewal of a license to own and
possess a irearm shall include:

   (a) Safe storage of irearms and ammunition;

   (b) Prevention of child and unauthorized access;

   (c) Recognition of warning signs involving suicidal or violent behavior;

   (d) Reporting of lost, stolen, or unlawfully accessed irearms;

   (e) Legal consequences of negligent irearm storage; and


                                                                             9|Page
   (f) Emergency response and coordination with law enforcement authorities.

The Firearms and Explosives Of ice shall require the irearm owner or licensee to
certify compliance with the safe-storage requirements upon renewal of the license.
Knowingly submitting a false certi ication shall constitute a ground for the revocation
of the license and prosecution under applicable law.

SECTION 11. Civil Liability. —

The civil liability arising from a violation of this Act shall be governed by the Civil Code
and other applicable laws.

A irearm owner, licensee, or custodian who, through negligence or violation of this
Act, allows a minor to obtain access to a irearm shall be liable for the damages caused
by the resulting act when the violation is the proximate cause of the injury, death, or
damage.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to limit the liability of parents or guardians
under the Civil Code, including the applicable provisions on parental responsibility
and damages caused by minor children living in their company.

A person or entity engaged in an activity involving irearms shall exercise a degree of
care commensurate with the dangerous nature of the instrumentality. The Supreme
Court has held that persons or entities engaged in businesses involving inherently
dangerous instrumentalities, including irearms, may be held directly and primarily
liable for failure to exercise the required exceptional precautions.

SECTION 12. Duties of Firearm Dealers and Gun Clubs. —

Authorized dealers, importers, manufacturers, gunsmiths, and gun clubs shall:

   (a) Provide every purchaser with written safe-storage instructions;

   (b) Require the purchaser to acknowledge receipt of the instructions;

   (c) Make available, at reasonable cost, compliant safe-storage devices;

   (d) Maintain records of safe-storage instruction and acknowledgment; and



                                                                              10 | P a g e
   (e) Immediately report to the Philippine National Police any attempted purchase
       or transfer made by a person who is legally disquali ied from possessing a
        irearm.

No irearm or ammunition shall be sold, delivered, or transferred to a minor, except
where expressly authorized by law and under the direct supervision of a quali ied and
licensed person in a lawful training or sporting activity.

SECTION 13. Firearm Incidents Involving Minors. — A school, hospital, local
government unit, law enforcement agency, or social welfare of icer who receives
information that a minor has obtained or may obtain access to a irearm shall
immediately coordinate with the appropriate law enforcement and child-protection
authorities.

Good-faith reporting under this section shall not give rise to civil, criminal, or
administrative liability, except in cases of gross negligence, malice, or bad faith.

The identity of the minor and the reporting person shall be protected in accordance
with applicable privacy and child-protection laws.

SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Philippine National Police, through the
Firearms and Explosives Of ice, in coordination with the Department of the Interior
and Local Government, Department of Social Welfare and Development, Department
of Education, and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the implementing rules
and regulations.

The implementing rules shall include:

   (a) Technical standards for safe-storage devices;

   (b) Procedures for inspection based on lawful processes and applicable
       constitutional protections;

   (c) Procedures for voluntary deposit and safekeeping;

   (d) Administrative due-process requirements;

   (e) Reporting and recordkeeping procedures; and

   (f) Public education and awareness programs.

No implementing rule shall authorize warrantless inspection of a private residence or
require an applicant to waive constitutional rights as a condition for obtaining a
 irearm license.

SECTION 15. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,

hereby repealed, amended, or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 17. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
