                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The right to mobility is integral to the exercise of other fundamental rights, as it ensures
access to food, healthcare, education, livelihood, and participation in community life. When
transportation systems are unsafe, inefficient, or unaffordable, this right is effectively denied,
undermining social equity, public health, and national development.

        Recent data show that the Philippines is confronting serious setbacks in commuter
safety. Road crash fatalities surged by 35% in 2024, rising from 2,030 in 2023 to 2,747 in 2024,
according to the Highway Patrol Group. 1 The annual number of road crash deaths has reached
roughly 11,000 persons nationwide. The death rate from traffic collisions is now about 11 per
100,000 persons which is far from the target of 4 per 100,000. 2

       On mobility efficiency, the EDSA Busway in Metro Manila provided service to over 63
million commuters in 2024, and in January 2025 alone served more than 5.5 million passengers,
averaging some 177,000 daily riders. 3 While laudable, the scale of demand underscores how
much more is needed to shift more commuters to safe, high-capacity public transport. 4

        Air quality has also declined. In 2024, the country’s average PM₂.₅ (fine particulate
matter) level rose to 14.8 µg/m³, up from 13.5 µg/m³ in 2023—a nearly 10% worsening. 5 In
Metro Manila, air pollution sometimes reaches “very unhealthy” and “acutely unhealthy”
levels, especially during festive periods: for example, during New Year celebrations, PM₁₀
reached up to 390 µg/m³ (more than double guideline limits), and PM₂.₅ reached 186 µg/m³.6

      AN ACT PROVIDING FOR A MAGNA CARTA FOR DIGNIFIED COMMUTING,
           CREATING THE NATIONAL OFFICE OF COMMUTER AFFAIRS,
         APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

1            SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “The Dignity in Commuting
2    Act.”

3            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to recognize
4    that mobility is a fundamental human need and to guarantee and protect the right of every person
5    to travel safely, conveniently, reliably, affordably, and with dignity. The State acknowledges that
6    mobility is essential for individuals to exercise their fundamental freedoms and to access work
7    opportunities and basic services, including health care and education.

 8          In pursuit of this policy, the State shall ensure the protection of all commuters and promote
 9   the mobility of people of all ages, physical abilities, genders, and economic backgrounds. The
10   welfare of commuters shall be safeguarded at all times, particularly in decisions concerning the
11   planning, development, and provision of mobility infrastructure and transportation services.

12           SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

13           (a) Active Transport shall refer to non-motorized means of travel, including walking or
14               using a bicycle;
15           (b) Activity Center shall refer to an area in a city or municipality where people gather
16               regularly for work, worship, education, social events, trade, exercise, leisure, or other
17               similar activities;
18           (c) Advance Notice shall refer to the provision of early information regarding any
19               consultation with stakeholders at least ten (10) working days prior to such consultation;
20           (d) Commuter shall refer to a person who needs to travel from one place to another, often
21               using a combination of transportation modes;
22           (e) Greenway shall refer to a road, street, or path with ample greenery and shade where
23               private motorized transport is not allowed, that also serves as a recreational space or a


                                                                                                        1
 1              public park in order to welcome and encourage environmentally sustainable
 2              transportation modes, especially walking and cycling;
 3          (f) High-Volume Transport Corridor shall refer to walkways, paths, roads, bridges, streets,
 4              or mass transportation terminals where the flow of people is at least 2,000 persons per
 5              hour per direction during peak travel periods;
 6          (g) Local Office shall refer to the Local Office of Commuter Affairs in each city or
 7              urbanized municipality;
 8          (h) Micro Mobility shall refer to travel using electronic or non-motorized vehicles
 9              weighing not more than 100 kilograms;
10          (i) Mobility shall refer to the ability of a person to travel from one place to another to
11              conduct daily and extraordinary activities and business;
12          (j) Mobility Infrastructure shall refer to physical facilities, such as foot paths, bicycle
13              lanes, roads, public utility vehicle (PUV) loading/unloading bays, PUV terminals, and
14              public transport systems, that support and enhance a person’s mobility;
15          (k) National Office shall refer to the National Office of Commuter Affairs;
16          (l) Passenger shall refer to persons who avail public transportation services;
17          (m) Private Motorized Transport shall refer to travel using motorized vehicles weighing
18              more than 100 kilograms;
19          (n) Public Transport shall refer to travel using public transportation services;
20          (o) Public Transportation Service Drivers shall refer to persons who operate public
21              transportation vehicles;
22          (p) Public Transportation Services shall refer to public sector and franchised private sector
23              services that transport people over land or water, including jeepneys, PUVs, buses,
24              minibuses, railways, ferries, taxis, and tricycles;
25          (q) Public Transportation Service Terminals shall refer to areas where passengers (i) board
26              and disembark public transportation vehicles, or (ii) purchase tickets for public
27              transportation services;
28          (r) Public Transportation Service Vehicles shall refer to the vehicles used to provide public
29              transportation services to the public;
30          (s) Transport Operators shall refer to persons or entities who provide public transportation
31              services to the public;
32          (t) Urbanized Municipality shall refer to a municipality in the Philippines where the
33              aggregate area zoned as residential, commercial, or industrial exceeds forty percent
34              (40%) of the total land area of the municipality; and
35          (u) Vulnerable Road Users shall refer to students, senior citizens, and persons with
36              disabilities.

37           SEC.4. National Office of Commuter Affairs. – To administer and implement the
38   provisions of this Act, and to monitor and ensure compliance of the country with international
39   standards set for mobility, there is hereby created the National Office of Commuter Affairs under
40   the Department of Transportation (DOTr), which shall have the following functions:

41                  (a) Coordinate with the respective government agencies dealing with road, rail, air,
42                      and maritime transportation policy to ensure the safe, efficient, and comfortable
43                      travel of commuters;
44                  (b) Operate help desks for commuters;

1                   (c) Assist commuters whenever the safety and efficiency of travel is compromised;
2                   (d) Undertake massive information dissemination campaigns on commuter
3                       education;
4                   (e) Ensure legal assistance for commuters, when necessary; and
5                   (f) Ensure adequate representation of commuters in any public consultation that
6                       will impact their welfare and interest.
7                       The National Office shall be under the direct supervision of an Assistant
8                       Secretary.

 9           SEC.5. Local Office of Commuter Affairs. – To administer and implement this Act in the
10   local level, each city and urbanized municipality shall establish its own Local Office of Commuter
11   Affairs, which shall be under the direct supervision of the city or municipal administrator.

12           SEC.6. Right to Adequate Public Transportation Services. – The State shall ensure that
13   adequate public transportation services are available to meet the needs of commuters. For purposes
14   of this Act, public transportation services shall be considered adequate when:

15          (a) Public Transportation service vehicles are:
16                 a. Sufficient in quantity and frequency, to ensure that commuter waiting time at
17                      stops and terminals does not exceed ten (10) minutes even during peak demand
18                      periods;
19                 b. Accorded priority in the use of road space and traffic signal operations, to
20                      ensure that commuters are able to traverse fifteen (15) kilometers within one
21                      (1) hour, including all scheduled stops; and
22                 c. Designed to accommodate all persons, including those with disabilities or
23                      special needs;
24          (b) Public transportation service terminals and stops are:
25                 a. Available within five hundred (500) meters from any residence or activity
26                      center;
27                 b. Connected to another public transportation service terminal within three
28                      hundred (300) meters to promote seamless connectivity of travel; and
29                 c. Properly equipped, adequately sized, well maintained, and designed to promote
30                      commuter safely and comfort and accommodate all persons, including persons
31                      with disabilities or special needs.

32           SEC.7. Right to Affordable Public Transportation Services. – The State shall ensure that
33   public transportation services are affordable to the general public.

34                   Vulnerable road users shall continue to benefit rom concessional or discounted
35          fares, as provided for by law.

36                  The State shall promote the use of public transportation services by providing
37          financial and non-financial incentives to ensure that it is more economical for commuters
38          to use public transportation services more frequently than to use private motorized
39          transport.

1            SEC.8. Right to Alternative Public Transportation Services. – When public transportation
2    services are deficient, the State shall provide alternatives to ensure the mobility of the people,
3    including the use of public resources, such as government-owned vehicles, and through State
4    charter or lease of private vehicles.

5            SEC.9. Right to Road Safety. – The State shall ensure that road crashes are eliminated. The
6    DOTr, through the National Office, shall implement a comprehensive road safety program and
7    shall implement the activities under the five pillars of the Philippine Road Safety Action Plan,
8    namely: Road Safety Management, Safer Roads, Safer Vehicles, Safer Road Users, and Post-Crash
9    Response.

10           The State shall also promote safe and efficient driving behavior by public transportation
11   drivers through mandatory training programs and more stringent licensing. The National Office, in
12   coordination with the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB), is hereby
13   mandated to monitor the location and speed of public transportation vehicles, and the status of their
14   public transportation drivers in real time, by requiring the installation of electronic vehicle tracking
15   devices and CCTVs. The State shall repeal all laws, rules, and regulations which incentivize public
16   transportation drivers in relation to ridership or fare revenue, to eliminate dangerous on-street
17   competition and behavior among public transport drivers.

18           SEC.10. Right to Proper Mobility Infrastructure. – The State shall provide proper
19   mobility infrastructure to promote the welfare of commuters. In all renovations of existing
20   infrastructure or construction of new infrastructure, the improvement of travel time impact on
21   public transport service users, cyclists, and pedestrians shall be prioritized.

22            Road infrastructure shall be designed and engineered to ensure the safety and access of
23   non-motorized commuters, as well as the disadvantaged or vulnerable groups. Every road where a
24   speed limit of over 20 kilometers per hour (kph) and under 60 kph is imposed shall feature an
25   unobstructed walking path at least 2.5 meters wide in each direction. Every road where a speed
26   limit of over 60 kph is imposed shall feature dedicated bike lanes wide enough to accommodate at
27   least two bikes.

28           The State shall develop adequate sidewalks and bicycle lane networks that encourage active
29   transport and provide safe and direct access to priority destinations, such as housing, schools,
30   business centers, and public transportation terminals. Such walking and cycling infrastructure shall
31   be included in the Comprehensive Land Use Plan of every city and urbanized municipality
32   incorporated in the design and implementation of road transport projects at national and local
33   government levels. The Local Office of a city or urbanized municipality shall ensure that each
34   barangay includes:

35                   (a) Safe, well-protected, and unobstructed community-wide foot paths, sidewalks,
36                       and bicycle lanes, compliant with universal design guidelines, to enable travel
37                       around the city or urbanized municipality entirely by foot, bicycle, or other
38                       forms of micro mobility;
39                   (b) Networks of greenways to promote healthy commuting and encourage active
40                       transport;
41                   (c) Proper lighting on all roads and footpaths to allow safe travel even after dark;

                                                                                                           4
 1                   (d) Tactile paving on sidewalks along high-volume transport corridors for the
 2                       benefit of blind pedestrians;
 3                   (e) Bicycle racks in every public transportation terminal or stop and activity center
 4                       that can accommodate at least ten (10%) of its anticipated users; and
 5                   (f) Safe intersections and pedestrian crossings, equipped with raised pedestrian
 6                       platforms, highly visible advance warning signages, adequate street lighting,
 7                       mid-crossing refuges, traffic calming, and curb outstands to reduce road
 8                       pavement crossing width.

10                       The State shall require all government offices and buildings to be equipped with
11                       shower facilities for commuter used. The DOTr, through the National Office,
12                       and the Local Offices shall develop and support measures to incentivize the
13                       private sector to include shower facilities in their respective activity centers.

14           SEC.11.Right to a Fair Share of Public Road Space. – The State shall ensure that public
15   road space is efficiently used. Modes of transportation that move more people, such as public
16   transportation services, active transport, and micro mobility, shall have priority over the use of road
17   space.

18         Public road space and foot paths shall not be appropriated or acquired for parking,
19   commerce, or other activities which restrict public use of, or access to public roads.

20            SEC.12.Right to Breathe Clean Air During Travel. – In line with the provisions of
21   Republic Act No. 8749, otherwise known as the Philippine Clean Air Act of 1999, the State shall
22   take measures to ensure that commuters are able to travel using public transportation services or
23   active transport without compromising their health, by:

24           (a) Strengthening enforcement measures for private motorized transportation and public
25               transportation vehicles to comply wit the emission standards set by the Department of
26               Environment and Natural Resources and/or the LTFRB, whichever affords greather
27               protection for clean air;
28           (b) Measuring air quality along the major transport corridors in all cities and provide this
29               information to the public in real time; and
30           (c) Activating emergency procedures to reduce public exposure to emissions when air
31               quality exceeds safe levels.

32           SEC.13.Right to Information for Efficient and Convenient Travel. – The State shall
33   provide timely and adequate information to commuters to enable them to develop safe, convenient,
34   and efficient travel plans, including selection of transportation modes and routes, by:

35           (a) Requiring all public transport services to prominently display route maps, fares,
36               schedules, and real time vehicle location and availability, in all transportation service
37               terminals and other strategic locations;
38           (b) Establishing technical standards and regulations for the display of street maps at
39               strategic locations in each neighborhood;
40           (c) Ensuring that all public transportation terminals, ports, and airports have a twenty-four
41               (24) information assistance counter to serve commuter needs; and

3               service travel times.

4            SEC.14. Right to Compensation for Public Transportation Service Breakdowns. – The
5    State shall ensure that passengers are compensated in case of service breakdowns in public
6    transportation services. During public transportation service breakdowns, transport operators shall:

 7          (a) Give passengers a full refund of the fare for the trip affected by the breakdown;
 8          (b) Assist the affected passengers in reaching their final destination;
 9          (c) Inform passengers how they will be redirected to their final destination; and if the
10              passengers are stranded for more than (1) hour.

11           SEC.15. Right To Participation in Decision-Making Processes Involving Mobility and
12   Public Transportation Services. – The State shall consult commuters and consider their welfare
13   and interest in all decision-making processes involving mobility and public transportation services
14   by;

15          (a) Giving commuters, including civil society organizations, advance notice which shall
16              contain all relevant information on any proposed policy, regulation, project, or service
17              affecting mobility at the local or national level;
18          (b) Allowing commuters to comment and make suggestions on the proposed policy,
19              regulation, project, or service, through official traditional and social media channels
20              and other similar channels;
21          (c) Responding to comments and suggestions from commuters within twenty (20) working
22              days from receipt; and
23          (d) Setting public consultation meetings at a time and place that will maximize
24              participation from commuters.

25           SEC.16. Public Transportation Services and Infrastructure Plan. - Within one- hundred
26   eighty (180) days from the effectivity of the rules and regulations implementing this Act, in
27   consultation with relevant government agencies, the National Office and Local Offices shall
28   publish a Public Transportation Services and Infrastructure Plan, which should satisfy the evolving
29   mobility requirements at the national and local levels, respectively. To ensure that these
30   mobility requirements are kept up to date, the National and Local Offices shall publish an updated
31   Public Transportation Services and Infrastructure Plan on or before December of each year. The
32   Public Transportation Services and Infrastructure Plan shall ensure that all the rights mentioned in
33   Section V of this Act shall be enforced.

34         SEC.17. Implementing Agencies. – In the implementation of the provisions of this Act,
35   the DOTr shall lead and coordinate the implementation activities of the Department of Public
36   Works and Highways, Department of the Interior and Local Government, Metropolitan Manila
37   Development Authority, and concerned local government units.
