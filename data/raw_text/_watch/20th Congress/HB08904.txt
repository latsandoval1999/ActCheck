                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Section 18, Article II of the 1987 Constitution mandates that the State shall
maintain peace and order, the protection of life, liberty, and property, and the
promotion of the general welfare as essential for the enjoyment by all the people
of the blessings of democracy. To fulfill this, the State must ensure that its
primary law enforcement agency, the Philippine National Police (PNP), is staffed
by experienced and capable personnel.

      This bill seeks to increase the compulsory retirement age of all officers and
non-officers of the PNP from fifty-six (56) years old to fifty-seven (57) years old.

      In 2023, the enactment of Republic Act No. 11939 amended the retirement
laws of the Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP), officially setting the
mandatory retirement age at fifty-seven (57) years. This shift was intended to
provide military leadership with a longer horizon to implement strategic reforms
and modernization initiatives. Given that the PNP and AFP are twin pillars of
national security, often working in close coordination for counter-insurgency and
  internal security operations, it is imperative to harmonize their policy
  frameworks. Maintaining disparate retirement ages creates unnecessary
  administrative gaps and can affect the morale and "concordant relations" between
  these two vital instrumentalities.

         Beyond inter-agency alignment, this measure serves a critical internal
  function: the preservation of institutional memory. By permitting veteran
  personnel to stay in service for an additional year, the PNP retains a wealth of
  expertise in field operations, criminal investigation, and community relations that
  would otherwise be lost. In an era where law enforcement faces increasingly
  complex and technology-driven crimes, the mentorship provided by seasoned
  officers is indispensable for the development of the younger force.

        A similar bill was filed during the 19th Congress.

        In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      Section 1. – Section 39 of Republic Act No. 6975, otherwise known as the
“Department of the Interior and Local Government Act of 1990,” as amended, is
further amended to read as follows:


            “Section 39. Compulsory Retirement. – Compulsory retirement, for
      office and non-officer, shall be upon the attainment of age [fifty six (56)]
      FIFTY-SEVEN (57): Provided, That, in case of any officer with the rank
      of chief superintendent, director or deputy director general, the
      Commission may allow his retention in the service for an unextendible
      period of one (1) year.”


      Section 2. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, ordinances,
administrative circulars, or other issuances not consistent with any provision of
this Act are hereby amended, repealed, or modified accordingly.


      Section 3. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
