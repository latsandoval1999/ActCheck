                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines is among the most disaster-prone countries in the world.
Typhoons, earthquakes, floods, storm surges, volcanic eruptions, landslides, public
health emergencies, cyber incidents, armed conflict, and other crises regularly test
the capacity of government to communicate timely, accurate, and life-saving
information to the public.

      In many emergencies, the first systems to fail or become unreliable are
precisely the systems people now depend on most: commercial cellular networks,
internet connectivity, cable systems, and television infrastructure. Towers may be
damaged. Fiber lines may be cut. Power may be unavailable. Networks may be
congested. Remote and geographically isolated communities may be left without
reliable access to official information when they need it most.

      Radio remains one of the most resilient, accessible, and practical means of
public communication during disasters. It can reach communities with limited
connectivity, can operate with modest receiving equipment, and can continue
functioning even when other communications systems are impaired, provided that
government broadcast facilities and public radio assets are hardened, powered,
connected, and properly integrated into emergency response systems.

      This bill strengthens the Presidential Broadcast Service – Bureau of Broadcast
Services (PBS-BBS), including the Radyo Pilipinas network, as the Government Last-
Mile Emergency Communications Lifeline. It designates its essential facilities as
Critical Emergency Communications Infrastructure for purposes of this Act and
establishes a National Emergency Broadcast Resilience Program to ensure that
      government broadcast facilities and public radio assets can continue
operating before, during, and after disasters, emergencies, crises, and widespread
communications disruptions.

      Legislation is necessary because this measure is not merely an internal
administrative adjustment. It establishes a statutory policy, creates an inter-agency
coordination framework, provides a basis for recurring appropriations,
institutionalizes modernization and resilience planning, protects authorized
emergency broadcast operations, and ensures congressional oversight. These
objectives require continuity across administrations and coordinated implementation
among the Presidential Communications Office, PBS-BBS, National Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Council, Office of Civil Defense, Department of
Information and Communications Technology, National Telecommunications
Commission, Department of Energy, Department of the Interior and Local
Government, local government units, and other relevant agencies.

      The bill is designed to be technically sound, fiscally disciplined, and
implementable. It does not diminish the authority of existing agencies, including the
spectrum management and regulatory powers of the National Telecommunications
Commission. It preserves freedom of speech, expression, and of the press. It protects
privacy and sensitive information. It allows flexible and technology-neutral
implementation, including the use of leased, managed, shared, satellite, radio,
digital, power, and other appropriate systems. It requires lifecycle asset
management, maintenance, post-incident reviews, corrective action planning, and
congressional review.

      At its core, the bill treats emergency public communication as a life-saving
public service. In a disaster, information is not merely an announcement; it can be
the difference between evacuation and exposure, rescue and isolation, calm and
panic, survival and loss. A resilient government emergency broadcast system is
therefore an essential part of national preparedness, public safety, and continuity of
government communications.

      Immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Government Last-Mile
Emergency Communications Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect the life, safety,
security, and welfare of the people by ensuring the continuous availability of reliable
public information and emergency communications before, during, and after
disasters, emergencies, crises, and widespread communications disruptions.

      The State recognizes that, in an archipelagic country frequently exposed to
typhoons, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, flooding, storm surges, tsunamis,
landslides, armed conflict, cyber incidents, public health emergencies, and other
hazards, commercial cellular, internet, cable, satellite, and television systems may
become unavailable, congested, damaged, or unreliable. In such circumstances,
terrestrial radio remains a vital, accessible, and resilient means of reaching remote,
geographically isolated, disaster-stricken, and underserved communities.

               (a) declaration of a state of calamity, state of national emergency, public health

          SEC. 15. Post-Incident Review and Corrective Action. – Following major
          emergency activations, signi cant communications disruptions, major drills, or
          events revealing operational vulnerabilities, PBS-BBS shall conduct a post-incident
          review in coordination with the NDRRMC, OCD, DICT, NTC, concerned LGUs, and
          other relevant agencies, as appropriate.

                  The post-incident review shall identify operational gaps, equipment or facility
          failures, communications bottlenecks, coordination issues, public information
          challenges, cybersecurity concerns, personnel safety issues, and other matters
          affecting the performance of PBS-BBS as the Government Last-Mile Emergency
          Communications Lifeline.

                 PBS-BBS shall prepare an after-action report and corrective action plan based
          on the post-incident review. The ndings and recommendations, subject to lawful
          restrictions involving security, privacy, operational sensitivity, or protected
          information, shall inform updates to the National Emergency Broadcast Resilience
          Plan, continuity of operations plans, training programs, procurement priorities, and
          annual reports required under this Act.
