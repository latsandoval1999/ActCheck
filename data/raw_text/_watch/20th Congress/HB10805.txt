                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) are the backbone of the Philippine economy.
They are the sari-sari stores in our communities, family-owned restaurants and shops, farmers and
producers, online sellers, small manufacturers, and countless businesses that provide jobs and
livelihood to Filipino families.

        According to the Department of Trade and Industry, MSMEs account for 99.63% of registered
business establishments in the Philippines. Yet despite their importance, many continue to face
difficulties in accessing affordable financing, technology, markets, government opportunities, and
reliable business support.

        The Magna Carta for MSMEs, or Republic Act No. 6977, as amended by Republic Act No.
9501, was enacted to address these challenges. However, the business environment has changed
significantly. Digitalization, e-commerce, new financing models, and global markets now play a
much greater role in determining whether a small business can survive and grow.

      This measure therefore seeks to modernize the Magna Carta for MSMEs and make
government support more responsive to the realities of today's Filipino entrepreneurs.

       First, the bill adopts a harmonized MSME classification system based on employment size
and annual sales turnover, removing asset size as a determining factor. This will provide a clearer and
more consistent basis for identifying MSMEs and delivering government assistance.

       Second, it establishes a National MSME Information System to consolidate government data
on MSMEs. Better data will allow government to better understand the needs of entrepreneurs and
design programs that reach the right businesses.

        Third, the bill promotes digital transformation and connectivity through a National Digital
Skills Program and an MSME Digital Adoption Fund. Digitalization is no longer a luxury for small
businesses. In 2025, the Philippine digital economy reached ₱2.74 trillion, or 9.8% of GDP,
demonstrating the growing importance of digital tools and markets to our economy.
        Fourth, the bill strengthens access to finance by empowering the Small Business Corporation
to develop innovative financing mechanisms, including risk-based and movable asset-based lending,
and to expand credit guarantees for startups and underserved MSMEs.

        Fifth, the bill gives MSMEs greater access to the government procurement market by
requiring procuring entities to set aside at least twenty percent (20%) of their procurement of goods
and services for local MSMEs, while providing for simplified bidding procedures and prompt
payment.

        Finally, the bill establishes an Expanded Global Market Access Program for MSMEs to help
Filipino businesses move beyond local markets and compete internationally. The Program will
provide export-readiness training, market intelligence, assistance in obtaining international
certifications, participation in trade fairs and business missions, access to digital marketplaces, and
linkages with foreign buyers and global value chains.

        The objective is simple: when a Filipino takes the risk of starting a business, government
should help make that business capable of surviving, growing, creating jobs, and competing—not
only in the Philippines, but in the global marketplace.

       By modernizing the Magna Carta for MSMEs, we invest in the entrepreneurs who keep our
communities and our economy moving. We give Filipino MSMEs a fairer chance to access capital,
technology, government opportunities, and international markets—and ultimately, a better chance to
grow from small businesses into stronger and more competitive enterprises.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     SECTION. 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "MSME Development
Modernization Act of 2026."

STATISTICAL VALIDITY. ANONYMIZED AND AGGREGATED DATA FROM THIS
SYSTEM SHALL BE MADE PUBLICLY AVAILABLE TO SUPPORT POLICY
RESEARCH AND MARKET ANALYSIS.

       SEC. 5. A new section, to be designated as Section 16, is hereby inserted after
Section 15 of the same Act, as amended, with the succeeding sections renumbered
Accordingly:
"SEC. 16. MANDATORY MSME PARTICIPATION IN GOVERNMENT PROCUREMENT. - TO
OPTIMIZE THE DOMESTIC MARKET AND SUPPORT LOCAL ENTERPRISES, ALL
PROCURING ENTITIES OF THE NATIONAL GOVERNMENT, ITS DEPARTMENTS,
BUREAUS, OFFICES, AND AGENCIES, INCLUDING STATE UNIVERSITIES AND
COLLEGES      (SUCS), GOVERNMENT-      OWNED    AND/OR   CONTROLLED
CORPORATIONS (GOCCS), AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS (LGUS), SHALL
ENSURE THE PARTICIPATION OF LOCAL MSMES IN GOVERNMENT
PROCUREMENT.



(A) MANDATORY SET-ASIDE. - ALL PROCURING ENTITIES SHALL SET ASIDE AT
LEAST TWENTY PERCENT (20%) OF THEIR TOTAL PROCUREMENT VALUE FOR
GOODS AND SERVICES FOR THE YEAR TO BE EXCLUSIVELY SOURCED FROM
LOCAL MSMES. THE GOVERNMENT PROCUREMENT POLICY BOARD (GPPB)
SHALL, WITHIN SIX (6) MONTHS FROM THE EFFECTIVITY OF THIS ACT, ISSUE
THE NECESSARY RULES AND REGULATIONS TO IMPLEMENT THIS PROVISION.

(B) SIMPLIFIED BIDDING PROCESS. - THE GPPB SHALL CREATE A SIMPLIFIED
BIDDING PROCESS WITH REDUCED DOCUMENTARY REQUIREMENTS FOR
PROCUREMENT PROJECTS BELOW A THRESHOLD TO BE DETERMINED BY THE
BOARD, WHICH ARE RESERVED FOR MSMES.



(C) PROMPT PAYMENT. - ALL PROCURING ENTITIES SHALL PAY THEIR MSME
SUPPLIERS FOR DELIVERED GOODS AND RENDERED SERVICES WITHIN A
MAXIMUM PERIOD OF THIRTY (30) CALENDAR DAYS FROM THE SUBMISSION OF
COMPLETE DOCUMENTS. ANY UNJUSTIFIABLE DELAY IN PAYMENT SHALL BE
SUBJECT TO PENALTIES AS PRESCRIBED BY THE GPPB."



       SEC. 6. New sections, to be designated as Section 17 and Section 18, are hereby inserted
with the succeeding sections renumbered accordingly:



"SEC. 17. MSME DIGITAL TRANSFORMATION AND CONNECTIVITY. - THE STATE
RECOGNIZES THE CRITICAL ROLE OF DIGITALIZATION IN ENHANCING MSME
COMPETITIVENESS. TO THIS END, THE FOLLOWING SHALL BE PURSUED:

 (A) NATIONAL DIGITAL SKILLS PROGRAM FOR MSMES. - THE DEPARTMENT OF
INFORMATION AND COMMUNICATIONS TECHNOLOGY (DICT), IN
COORDINATION WITH THE DTI AND THE TECHNICAL EDUCATION AND SKILLS
DEVELOPMENT AUTHORITY (TESDA), SHALL ESTABLISH AND IMPLEMENT A
CONTINUOUS NATIONAL DIGITAL SKILLS PROGRAM. THIS PROGRAM SHALL
PROVIDE ACCESSIBLE AND AFFORDABLE TRAINING FOR MSME OWNERS AND
THEIR EMPLOYEES ON CRITICAL DIGITAL COMPETENCIES, INCLUDING E-
COMMERCE, DIGITAL MARKETING, CYBERSECURITY, AND THE USE OF
BUSINESS PRODUCTIVITY TOOLS.



(B) DIGITAL ADOPTION FUND. - THERE IS HEREBY CREATED AN MSME DIGITAL
ADOPTION FUND, TO BE ADMINISTERED BY THE SMALL BUSINESS CORPORATION,
WHICH SHALL PROVIDE GRANTS AND SOFT LOANS FOR MSMES TO ACQUIRE OR
SUBSCRIBE TO ESSENTIAL DIGITAL SOLUTIONS, INCLUDING BUT NOT LIMITED
TO ENTERPRISE RESOURCE PLANNING (ERP) SYSTEMS, CUSTOMER
RELATIONSHIP MANAGEMENT (CRM) SOFTWARE, AND OTHER PRODUCTIVITY-
ENHANCING                                              TECHNOLOGIES.



(C) CONNECTIVITY FOR UNDERSERVED AREAS. - THE DICT, IN PARTNERSHIP
WITH THE PRIVATE SECTOR, SHALL PRIORITIZE THE ESTABLISHMENT OF
AFFORDABLE AND RELIABLE HIGH-SPEED INTERNET CONNECTIVITY,
INCLUDING SATELLITE AND OTHER ALTERNATIVE TECHNOLOGIES, IN RURAL
AND GEOGRAPHICALLY ISOLATED AREAS TO ENSURE THAT ALL MSMES CAN
PARTICIPATE IN THE DIGITAL ECONOMY."



"SEC. 18. GLOBAL MARKET ACCESS PROGRAM FOR MSMES. – TO ENABLE
PHILIPPINE MSMES TO PARTICIPATE IN GLOBAL VALUE CHAINS AND EXPAND
THEIR EXPORT OPPORTUNITIES, THERE IS HEREBY ESTABLISHED A GLOBAL
MARKET ACCESS PROGRAM FOR MSMES, TO BE JOINTLY IMPLEMENTED BY
THE DTI, THROUGH THE EXPORT MARKETING BUREAU (EMB) AND THE BUREAU
OF SMALL AND MEDIUM ENTERPRISE DEVELOPMENT (BSMED), IN
COORDINATION WITH THE DEPARTMENT OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS (DFA), THE
DICT, THE PHILIPPINE TRADE AND INVESTMENT CENTERS (PTICS), THE SMALL
BUSINESS CORPORATION, AND OTHER RELEVANT GOVERNMENT AGENCIES AND
PRIVATE SECTOR ORGANIZATIONS.

THE PROGRAM SHALL INCLUDE, BUT SHALL NOT BE LIMITED TO, THE
FOLLOWING:

(A) DEVELOPMENT AND IMPLEMENTATION OF EXPORT READINESS PROGRAMS,
INCLUDING TRAINING ON EXPORT PROCEDURES, INTERNATIONAL TRADE
REGULATIONS, CUSTOMS DOCUMENTATION, PRODUCT STANDARDS AND
CERTIFICATIONS, INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY PROTECTION, PACKAGING,
LOGISTICS, CROSS-BORDER TAXATION, AND MARKET INTELLIGENCE;

(B) PROVISION OF TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE TO MSMES IN OBTAINING
INTERNATIONALLY RECOGNIZED QUALITY CERTIFICATIONS, SUSTAINABILITY
CERTIFICATIONS, FOOD SAFETY CERTIFICATIONS, HALAL CERTIFICATION, AND
OTHER PRODUCT STANDARDS REQUIRED FOR ACCESS TO FOREIGN MARKETS;

(C) ASSISTANCE IN PROMOTING PHILIPPINE MSMES THROUGH INTERNATIONAL
TRADE FAIRS, BUSINESS MATCHING ACTIVITIES, TRADE MISSIONS, EXPORT
EXHIBITIONS, AND DIGITAL MARKETPLACES, INCLUDING CROSS-BORDER
ELECTRONIC COMMERCE PLATFORMS;

(D) ESTABLISHMENT OF AN MSME EXPORT ASSISTANCE DESK WITHIN DTI TO
PROVIDE    ADVISORY   SERVICES  ON    EXPORT   FINANCING,   MARKET
OPPORTUNITIES, TARIFF AND NON-TARIFF MEASURES, RULES OF ORIGIN, FREE
TRADE AGREEMENTS, AND APPLICABLE TRADE REMEDIES;

(E) DEVELOPMENT OF DIGITAL EXPORT PLATFORMS AND ELECTRONIC TRADE
FACILITATION SYSTEMS THAT WILL ENABLE MSMES TO MARKET AND SELL
THEIR PRODUCTS AND SERVICES DIRECTLY TO INTERNATIONAL BUYERS;

F) FACILITATION OF ACCESS TO EXPORT FINANCING, EXPORT CREDIT
INSURANCE, AND GUARANTEE PROGRAMS THROUGH THE SMALL BUSINESS
CORPORATION, GOVERNMENT FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS, AND ACCREDITED
FINANCING INSTITUTIONS;

(G) PROMOTION OF PARTICIPATION OF WOMEN-OWNED, YOUTH-LED,
INDIGENOUS PEOPLES', RURAL, AGRICULTURAL, AND INNOVATION-DRIVEN
MSMES IN INTERNATIONAL TRADE AND EXPORT DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMS;
(H) ESTABLISHMENT OF STRATEGIC PARTNERSHIPS WITH FOREIGN CHAMBERS
OF    COMMERCE,    INTERNATIONAL     DEVELOPMENT     ORGANIZATIONS,
MULTINATIONAL ENTERPRISES, AND GLOBAL E-COMMERCE PLATFORMS TO
EXPAND BUSINESS OPPORTUNITIES FOR PHILIPPINE MSMES; AND

(I) FORMULATION OF A NATIONAL MSME EXPORT DEVELOPMENT ROADMAP,
WHICH SHALL IDENTIFY PRIORITY INDUSTRIES, EMERGING EXPORT MARKETS,
AND    STRATEGIC  INTERVENTIONS    NECESSARY   TO   INCREASE  THE
PARTICIPATION OF MSMES IN INTERNATIONAL TRADE.

THE DTI SHALL SUBMIT TO CONGRESS, NOT LATER THAN MARCH 31 OF EVERY
YEAR, A REPORT ON THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THIS PROGRAM, INCLUDING THE
NUMBER OF MSMES ASSISTED, EXPORT SALES GENERATED, EMPLOYMENT
CREATED, MARKETS ACCESSED, AND RECOMMENDATIONS FOR FURTHER
STRENGTHENING MSME INTERNATIONALIZATION."


        SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the Department of Trade and Industry, in consultation with the MSMED Council and other
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
        SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations, and
other issuances inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
        SEC. 10. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
