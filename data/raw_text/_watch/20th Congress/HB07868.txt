                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The importance of quality education cannot be overstated. Article XIV, Section
1 of the 1987 Constitution recognizes this, mandating the State to "protect and
promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels" and to “take
appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all." Central to this mission is
ensuring the availability and accessibility of quality educational materials, particularly
textbooks, which remain a primary learning resource. The Global Education Evidence
Advisory Panel (GEEAP 2023) notes that textbooks, coupled with appropriate-level
instruction, are among the most cost-effective educational interventions.
       However, recent reports continue to document the persistent challenges in
textbook availability for our public schools, with regional gaps in access and
timeliness.1 These findings reinforce the need for a more decentralized, locally
responsive publishing ecosystem to ensure that learners in all regions can obtain
quality textbooks when needed.
       In this context, procurement delays have been a longstanding concern. The
Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II) previously observed
that, despite available funds, only a small number of textbooks were procured for
Grades 1–10 since 2012. After the shift to the K to 12 curriculum, successful
procurement was limited to Grades 5 and 6.
       Notably, meaningful progress has since been made. In 2024, DepEd delivered

over 87 million textbooks and procured more than 100 new titles, demonstrating that
administrative bottlenecks can be addressed through focused reforms. The Early
Procurement Activities (EPA) also significantly shortened acquisition timelines. In
2024, DepEd delivered over 87 million textbooks and procured more than 100 new
titles, a sharp improvement from the limited procurement in the past decade.
         But despite this progress, institutional weaknesses remain. A study by the
Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS), cited by EDCOM II, found that
textbook production remains heavily concentrated in Luzon, with limited publishing
capacity in the Visayas and Mindanao.2 Without addressing this imbalance, supply
chain vulnerabilities and regional inequities will continue.
         To address these issues and other challenges impeding the growth of the book
industry in the Philippines, this measure proposes to amend Republic Act No. 8047,
to substantially empower the NBDB as a primary driver of a sustainable, high-quality
publishing ecosystem in the Philippines. Under the proposed National Textbook and
Learning Resources Plan, the Department of Education (DepEd) and the NBDB are
to work jointly in preparing specific policies, guidelines, and measures on the provision
of public school textbooks and learning resources. This strengthened partnership
would help ensure learning materials meet both industry standards and educational
competencies, while aligning with regional needs.
         Furthermore, the bill mandates that the NBDB Governing Board examine and
implement strategies to future-proof textbook materials. In addition to redefining the
term “book,” policies should be developed to incorporate innovative technologies,
global trends, and sustainable industry practices. This would ensure that Philippine
textbooks and other locally produced books remain effective, relevant, and
competitive in a fast-changing educational landscape.
         Finally, involving key agency representatives, such as those from the
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and the Department of Science and
Technology (DOST), in the Governing Board will better equip the NBDB to formulate
programs and strategies that align with evolving industry needs and stakeholder
interests. By clearly defining the roles of relevant government offices, the state can
maximize its expertise, with each agency contributing to a publishing ecosystem that
is sustainable and equitable.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

        d. JOINT FORMULATION OF THE NATIONAL TEXTBOOK AND
           LEARNING RESOURCE PLAN WITH THE BOARD.

          THE PRINTING OF PUBLIC ELEMENTARY AND SECONDARY
          SCHOOL TEXTBOOKS SHALL BE DONE ON A REGIONAL OR
          PROVINCIAL LEVEL, WHENEVER ECONOMICALLY FEASIBLE.

          THE DEPED SHALL, WITHIN A PERIOD OF NOT MORE THAN
          THREE (3) YEARS FROM THE EFFECTIVITY OF THIS ACT, PHASE
          OUT ITS ELEMENTARY AND SECONDARY TEXTBOOK
          PUBLICATION AND DISTRIBUTION FUNCTIONS AND SHALL
          SUPPORT THE PHASING IN OF PRIVATE SECTOR PUBLISHERS TO
          ASSUME THESE FUNCTIONS HERE. FOR ITS PART, THE BOARD
          SHALL MONITOR AND CONDUCT AN ANNUAL EVALUATION OF
          THE PROGRESS OF THE SHIFT OF FUNCTIONS FROM THE DEPED
          TO THE PRIVATE SECTOR.”

SECTION 9. A new Section 10-A is hereby inserted to read as follows:
     “SECTION 10-A. THE NATIONAL TEXTBOOK AND LEARNING
     RESOURCES PLAN - THE BOARD AND THE DEPARTMENT OF
     EDUCATION SHALL JOINTLY FORMULATE AND IMPLEMENT THE
     NATIONAL TEXTBOOK AND LEARNING RESOURCES PLAN (TEXTBOOK
     PLAN), WHICH SHALL INCLUDE SPECIFIC POLICIES, GUIDELINES, AND
     MEASURES ON THE PROVISION OF PUBLIC SCHOOL TEXTBOOKS AND
     LEARNING RESOURCES.

     THE FORMULATION OF THE PLAN SHALL INCLUDE:

        a. FORECASTING, PLANNING, AND PREPARATION OF BUDGET FOR
           THE PROCUREMENT OF PUBLIC TEXTBOOKS;


                                 12
      b. IDENTIFICATION OF AREAS WHERE THERE ARE GAPS AS TO THE
         NEEDS OF THE CURRICULUM AND AVAILABLE TEXTBOOKS, AND
         ADDRESSING THOSE GAPS BY BUILDING EXPERTISE THROUGH
         CAPACITY BUILDING AND IDENTIFICATION OF LOCAL EXPERTS,
         AND PLANNING FOR THEIR ACQUISITION;
      c. ESTABLISHING INSTITUTIONAL MECHANISMS FOR THE REGULAR

         EVALUATION OF THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THE TEXTBOOK

      d. INSTITUTIONALIZATION OF CONSULTATIVE MECHANISMS

   NEED ARISES, REVISED TO BE RESPONSIVE TO THE NEEDS OF THE
   LEARNERS, REGULARLY EVERY FIVE YEARS.

   ADMINISTRATIVE CIRCULAR WHICH SHALL PROVIDE THE GUIDELINES
   FOR THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THE NATIONAL TEXTBOOK AND
   LEARNING RESOURCE PLAN.”

other parts or sections of this Act.
