                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 1 of Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that
the goals of the national economy are a more equitable distribution of
opportunities, income, and wealth, increased productivity, and an improved
quality of life for all, especially the underprivileged. Complementing this
mandate, Section 5 of Article XIII directs the State to support farmers,
farmworkers, cooperatives, and independent farmers' organizations through
appropriate technology, research, financing, and other support services.
Likewise, Sections 9 and 16 of Article II mandate the State to promote social
justice, sustainable development, and environmental protection.
       Consistent with these constitutional principles, this measure seeks to
establish the Post-Harvest Facility Development Incentives Program to
strengthen community-based post-harvest systems by providing financial
assistance, fiscal incentives, technical support, and institutional capacity-building
for the establishment, modernization, and operation of post-harvest facilities
nationwide. By expanding access to modern drying, storage, milling, processing,
packaging, and cold chain facilities, the proposed measure aims to reduce post-
harvest losses, improve product quality, increase farmers' incomes, strengthen
agricultural value chains, and enhance national food security.

      Although the Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization Act of 1997
(Republic Act No. 8435) and the Agricultural and Fisheries Mechanization
(AFMech) Law (Republic Act No. 10601) established the foundation for
agricultural modernization and mechanization, many smallholder farmers,
cooperatives, and rural enterprises continue to face limited access to modern post-
harvest facilities due to financial and infrastructure constraints. The Philippine
Development Plan 2023–2028 likewise identifies agricultural transformation,
improved logistics, post-harvest infrastructure, and strengthened agricultural
value chains as essential strategies for achieving inclusive and sustainable
economic growth.1

       Supporting these policy directions, the Food and Agriculture Organization
of the United Nations (FAO) recognizes that investments in post-harvest
technologies and infrastructure are essential in reducing food losses and
improving food security.2 Similarly, the Philippine Center for Postharvest
Development and Mechanization (PhilMech) has consistently promoted modern
drying, storage, processing, and mechanization technologies to improve product
quality and farmer incomes,3 while the World Bank emphasizes that
strengthening post-harvest systems and agricultural logistics enhances market
access, rural livelihoods, and the competitiveness of the agricultural sector.4

       Recognizing these opportunities, this proposed legislation institutionalizes
a comprehensive incentives program that provides matching grants, concessional
financing, equipment subsidies, technical assistance, and capacity-building for
community-based post-harvest facilities. By strengthening coordination among
government agencies, local government units, cooperatives, financial institutions,
academic institutions, and the private sector, this measure seeks to reduce post-
harvest losses, increase farmers' productivity and income, generate rural
employment, strengthen food security, and improve the global competitiveness
of Philippine agricultural products.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Post-Harvest
Facility Development Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to promote sustainable agricultural development by strengthening post-
harvest systems that reduce agricultural losses, improve food security, increase
farmers' productivity and income, enhance agricultural value chains, and improve
the competitiveness of Philippine agricultural and fishery products in both
domestic and international markets.
      Towards this end, the State shall encourage investments in modern,
climate-resilient, energy-efficient, and community-based post-harvest facilities
through financial assistance, fiscal and non-fiscal incentives, technical support,
research and innovation, institutional capacity-building, and public-private
partnerships, particularly for small farmers, fisherfolk, agricultural cooperatives,
agrarian reform beneficiaries, Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous
Peoples (ICCs/IPs), local government units, and qualified rural enterprises.

      SEC. 3. Objectives. — This Act shall pursue the following objectives:

      (a)   Reduce post-harvest losses through the establishment                and
      modernization of post-harvest facilities;

      (b) Increase farmers' income by improving product quality, minimizing
      spoilage, and promoting value addition;

      (c) Expand access to modern drying, storage, milling, processing,
      packaging, cold chain, and transport facilities in agricultural communities;

      (d) Strengthen agricultural cooperatives, farmers' organizations, and rural
      enterprises as local agribusiness hubs;

      (e) Promote food safety, quality assurance, and compliance with national
      and international standards;

      (f) Encourage climate-resilient, energy-efficient, and environmentally
      sustainable post-harvest technologies;

      (g) Generate employment opportunities and stimulate rural economic
      development;

      (h) Improve market access and strengthen agricultural value chains; and

                                                                                  2
      (i) Promote innovation, digitalization, and technology adoption in post-
      harvest operations.

      SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Post-Harvest Facility refers to any structure, building, equipment,
      machinery, technology, vehicle, or system used for drying, cleaning,
      grading, sorting, packing, cooling, storing, transporting, milling,
      processing, preserving, or otherwise handling agricultural and fishery
      products after harvest.

      (b) Community-Based Post-Harvest Facility refers to a post-harvest facility
      owned, managed, or operated by agricultural cooperatives, farmers'
      organizations, fisherfolk associations, agrarian reform beneficiary
      organizations, Indigenous Peoples' organizations, local government units,
      or qualified rural enterprises.

      (c) Beneficiary Organization refers to any qualified agricultural
      cooperative, farmers' organization, fisherfolk association, agrarian reform
      beneficiary organization, Indigenous Peoples' organization, local
      government unit, state university or college, or qualified micro, small, and
      medium enterprise engaged in agricultural production or processing.

      (d) Matching Grant refers to financial assistance provided by the
      government requiring counterpart funding from the beneficiary in
      accordance with guidelines issued by the Department of Agriculture.

      SEC. 5. National Post-Harvest Facility Development Framework. — The
Department of Agriculture (DA), through the Philippine Center for Postharvest
Development and Mechanization (PhilMech), shall formulate a National Post-
Harvest Facility Development Framework to guide the planning, establishment,

                                                                                3
modernization, operation, maintenance, financing, and sustainable management
of post-harvest facilities throughout the country in coordination with the
following agencies and institutions:

      (a) Cooperative Development Authority (CDA);

      (b) Department of Trade and Industry (DTI);

      (c) Department of Science and Technology (DOST);

      (d) Department of Agrarian Reform (DAR);

      (e) Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);

      (f) National Irrigation Administration (NIA);

      (g) Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA);

      (h) State Universities and Colleges (SUCs);

      (i) Local Government Units (LGUs); and

      (j) Government Financial Institutions (GFIs).

      The Framework shall be reviewed and updated every six (6) years, or as
may be necessary, to ensure its responsiveness to emerging technologies, climate
risks, market conditions, and national agricultural development priorities.

      SEC. 6. Establishment of the Post-Harvest Facility Development
Program. — There is hereby established the Post-Harvest Facility Development
Program (PHFDP) under the Department of Agriculture to implement the
National Framework and provide financial, technical, institutional, and capacity-
building assistance for the establishment, rehabilitation, modernization,


                                                                               4
expansion, operation, and maintenance of community-based post-harvest
facilities nationwide.

      SEC. 7. Program Components. — The Program shall include:

      (a) establishment, rehabilitation, and modernization of post-harvest
      facilities;

      (b) grants, subsidies, concessional financing, and matching grants;

      (c) technical, engineering, and feasibility assistance;

      (d) skills development and facility management training;

      (e) digitalization, automation, and smart agriculture technologies;

      (f) food safety, quality assurance, and product certification;

      (g) agribusiness development and market linkage;

      (h) renewable energy integration in post-harvest operations;

      (i) research, innovation, and technology commercialization; and

      (j) monitoring, evaluation, and impact assessment.

      SEC. 8. Qualified Beneficiaries. — Priority shall be given to:

      (a) registered agricultural cooperatives;

      (b) farmers' and fisherfolk organizations;

      (c) agrarian reform beneficiary organizations;

      (d) local government units;

      (e) Indigenous Peoples' organizations;
                                                                            5
      (f) State Universities and Colleges engaged in agricultural extension and
      research;

      (g) qualified micro, small, and medium enterprises engaged in agricultural
      processing; and

      (h) social enterprises operating within agricultural value chains.

      Priority shall likewise be accorded to underserved municipalities,
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs), climate-vulnerable
communities, and major agricultural production areas.

      SEC. 9. Eligible Projects. — Assistance may be granted for the
establishment, acquisition, rehabilitation, modernization, or expansion of:

      (a) mechanical and solar dryers;

      (b) rice, corn, coconut, coffee, cacao, feed, and other processing mills;

      (c) warehouses, grain silos, and storage facilities;

      (d) cold storage and refrigerated facilities;

      (e) packing houses;

      (f) grading, sorting, and packaging centers;

      (g) food processing facilities;

      (h) refrigerated transport equipment;

      (i) renewable energy systems supporting post-harvest operations;

      (j) digital inventory management and traceability systems; and


                                                                                  6
      (k) such other post-harvest technologies as may be approved by the
      Department of Agriculture.

      SEC. 10. Incentives. — Qualified beneficiaries may receive one or more
of the following, subject to existing laws:

      (a) matching grants covering up to seventy percent (70%) of project
      costs;

      (b) concessional loans through government financial institutions;

      (c) equipment subsidies;

      (d) technical and engineering assistance;

      (e) entrepreneurship, cooperative management, and facility operation
      training;

      (f) digital technology support;

      (g) market promotion and business development assistance; and

      (h) such fiscal and non-fiscal incentives as may be available under
      existing laws and regulations.

      SEC. 11. Post-Harvest Facility Development Fund. — There is hereby
created a Post-Harvest Facility Development Fund to finance grants, subsidies,
technical assistance, research, innovation, technology transfer, and capacity-
building programs under this Act. The Fund shall be administered by the
Department of Agriculture and shall be sourced from:

      (a) appropriations under the General Appropriations Act;

      (b) official development assistance;

                                                                             7
      (c) grants, donations, and contributions;

      (d) income generated from Program activities; and

      (e) other lawful sources.

      SEC. 12. Capacity Building and Technical Assistance. — The
Department of Agriculture, PhilMech, CDA, DTI, DOST, TESDA, State
Universities and Colleges, and other concerned agencies shall jointly conduct
training programs covering post-harvest management, facility operation and
maintenance, agribusiness development, cooperative governance, digital
technologies, food safety, climate-resilient agriculture, entrepreneurship, and
financial literacy.

      SEC. 13. National Post-Harvest Facility Development Council. — There
is hereby created the National Post-Harvest Facility Development Council,
chaired by the Secretary of Agriculture, with the Executive Director of PhilMech
serving as Vice Chairperson. The Council shall be composed of representatives
from CDA, DTI, DOST, DAR, DILG, TESDA, government financial institutions,
farmers' organizations, agricultural cooperatives, MSMEs, State Universities and
Colleges, and the private sector.

      The Council shall formulate policies, coordinate implementation, monitor
program performance, recommend policy improvements, and submit an annual
report to Congress.

      SEC. 14. Monitoring and Evaluation. — The Department of Agriculture
shall establish a monitoring and evaluation system using measurable indicators,
including reductions in post-harvest losses, increases in farmers' income, facility
utilization rates, value-added production, employment generation, market access,


                                                                                 8
investment generated, and overall program effectiveness. The Department shall
submit an annual accomplishment report to Congress.

      SEC. 15. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Department of Agriculture. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

      SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred
twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Agriculture,
in coordination with the concerned agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate
the Implementing Rules and Regulations necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act.

      SEC. 17. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 19. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
