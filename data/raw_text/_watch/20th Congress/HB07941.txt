                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution declares that “the State shall
protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness
among them.”

       Despite this constitutional guarantee and the passage of the Universal Health
Care Act (Republic Act No. 11223), many barangays across the country still face
persistent gaps in health funding, leading to inadequate primary care services, poor
preventive programs, and limited resources for Barangay Health Workers and health
stations.

       The barangay, as the basic political unit, is at the frontline of service delivery.
It serves as the first point of contact for health services, especially in rural and
underserved areas. However, without a mandatory and predictable funding stream

                                                                                          1
 for health, local health systems remain vulnerable to budget cuts or political
 discretion.

        This measure seeks to institutionalize a minimum allocation of ten percent
 (10%) of the Barangay National Tax Allotment (NTA) exclusively for health
 programs and services. The proposed Barangay Health Budget Mandate Act ensures
 that every barangay dedicates sufficient resources to disease prevention, health
 promotion, maternal and child care, nutrition programs, and other vital public health
 services.

    Specifically, the measure seeks to:

    1) Strengthen the delivery of primary health care at the barangay level by
       guaranteeing a dedicated health budget;
    2) Empower local health committees and Barangay Health Workers to plan and
       implement responsive health initiatives;
    3) Institutionalize accountability and transparency in the use of barangay health
       funds through COA and DILG oversight; and
    4) Support the objectives of the Universal Health Care Act and the Local
       Government Code by ensuring equitable access to essential health services for
       all Filipinos.

    By mandating a fixed percentage of the NTA for health, this bill reinforces the
 government’s commitment to health equity, strengthens community resilience, and
 ensures that no Filipino is denied basic health care due to lack of local funding.

    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Barangay Health
Budget Mandate Act.”


      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
consciousness among them, consistent with Article II, Section 15 of the 1987


                                                                                1
Constitution. Further, the State recognizes that the barangay, being the primary
implementing unit of government programs, plays a critical role in delivering
essential health services, promoting disease prevention, and ensuring equitable
access to primary care.


      In line with this constitutional mandate and the goals of Republic Act No.
7160, otherwise known as the Local Government Code of 1991, and Republic Act
No. 11223, or the Universal Health Care Act, it is imperative to strengthen barangay-
level health systems through the guaranteed allocation of resources dedicated to
health programs, projects, and services.


      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall mean:


   a) Barangay National Tax Allotment (NTA) refers to the share of the barangay
      from national taxes pursuant to Title III, Book II of the Local Government
      Code of 1991, as amended.
   b) Health Programs, Projects, and Services refer to any barangay initiative,
      intervention, or expenditure related to public health.
   c) (Barangay Health Station (BHS) refers to the primary health facility
      established at the barangay level, managed by local government and
      supervised by the city or municipal health office.
   d) Barangay Health Plan refers to the annual health action plan formulated by
      the Barangay Health Committee, consistent with the local development and
      investment plans, indicating health priorities, activities, and fund utilization.




                                                                                          2
       SECTION 4. Mandatory Health Allocation. – Each barangay shall allocate
not less than ten percent (10%) of its annual National Tax Allotment (NTA)
exclusively for the funding and implementation of health programs, projects, and
services as defined in this Act.


       The allocation shall be distinct from and in addition to appropriations for other
barangay expenditures such as peace and order, infrastructure, and administrative
operations.


       SECTION 5. Preparation and Utilization of the Barangay Health Budget –
The Barangay Health Committee, chaired by the Punong Barangay and composed
of the Barangay Kagawad for Health, Barangay Health Workers, and representatives
from the City or Municipal Health Office, shall prepare the Barangay Health Plan
and Budget, consistent with the annual barangay development plan.


       The Barangay Council shall approve the Barangay Health Budget as part of
its annual appropriation ordinance. Funds allocated under this Act shall be used
solely for health-related purposes and shall not be realigned or used for non-health
activities.


       SECTION 6. Monitoring, Reporting and Oversight. –


       A. The City or Municipal Health Office shall monitor the utilization and
implementation of the health allocation and submit a consolidated report to the
City/Municipal Mayor and the Local Sanggunian.




                                                                                      3
      B. The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and the
Department of Health (DOH) shall jointly issue guidelines for monitoring and
reporting compliance with this Act.


      C. The Commission on Audit (COA) shall audit the use of barangay funds to
ensure that the mandated 10% allocation is properly utilized for health purposes.


      D. The DILG shall submit to Congress an annual compliance report, detailing
the percentage of barangays meeting the required allocation and the health outcomes
achieved.


      SECTION 7. Incentives and Sanctions. – Barangays that fully comply with
this Act and demonstrate effective use of funds for measurable health outcomes may
be eligible for incentive grants under the DOH or DILG performance-based
programs.


      Failure to comply with the minimum allocation requirement shall constitute
administrative negligence, subjecting the responsible barangay officials to sanctions
under existing laws, including suspension of eligibility for certain national
government grants or programs.


      SECTION 8. Capacity Building and Technical Assistance. — The DOH and
DILG shall jointly provide capacity-building programs for barangay officials, health
committees, and BHWs in financial management, health planning, monitoring, and
reporting to ensure the efficient use of the allocated funds.




                                                                                    4
      SECTION 9: Appropriation. – The amounts necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
DOH and DILG. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.


      SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within
ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH and DILG, in consultation
with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), COA, and the League of
Barangays of the Philippines, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations
to carry out the provisions of this Act.


      SECTION 11. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision
of this Act shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, other parts of provisions thereof
not affected shall thereby continue to be in full force and effect.


      SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, letters of instruction, proclamations or administrative regulations that are
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or
modified accordingly.


      SECTION 13. Effectivity. - Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the
implementing rules and regulations, this Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
