                              AN ACT
     PROHIBITING THE DIRECT OR INDIRECT RECOVERY FROM WATER
    CONSUMERS OF COSTS ATTRIBUTABLE TO NON-REVENUE WATER,
   REQUIRING WATER SERVICE PROVIDERS TO REDUCE AND PUBLICLY
DISCLOSE WATER LOSSES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTION 63 OF
      PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 198, AS AMENDED, AND FOR OTHER
                             PURPOSES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Water is indispensable to life, health, sanitation, commerce, and the daily
functioning of every Filipino household. How water is measured and billed is therefore
not a mere matter of utility accounting; it is a question of consumer protection and
responsible stewardship of a vital natural resource.

       The scale of the need is stark. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority's
preliminary 2024 Annual Poverty Indicators Survey, only 53.1 percent of Filipino
families had access to safely managed drinking water service. When water is this
scarce and this essential, not a single drop lost to leaks, faulty meters, or
mismanagement should be quietly charged to the very families struggling to obtain it.

        Yet that is precisely what happens. Non-revenue water—the gap between
water entering a system and water actually billed—captures both real losses from
leaking pipes, reservoirs, and connections, and apparent losses from inaccurate
meters, unauthorized use, and billing errors. Authorized public uses such as
firefighting and disaster relief are treated separately when properly documented. The
Philippine Water Supply and Sanitation Master Plan 2019–2030 records the National
Water Resources Board's mandate that non-revenue water should not exceed 25.0
percent. That ceiling exists precisely because water loss is a manageable operating
condition — not an unlimited cost to be dumped onto consumers.
         This measure is the water counterpart to the reform now sweeping the power
sector. Just as Congress and the Executive move to stop electric utilities from passing
their system losses to consumers by amending Republic Act No. 7832, this bill stops
water providers from passing their water losses down the line. The principle is identical
across both utilities: the burden of losses belongs to those who control the
infrastructure, not to the households at the end of the pipe. The Fair Water Billing Act
is the companion measure to system-loss reform in electricity — one Congress, two
utilities, a single standard of fairness.

        The Constitution supplies a firm foundation. Article II, Sections 15 and 16 direct
the State to protect the rights to health and to a balanced and healthful ecology. Article
XII, Section 2 vests ownership and full control of the nation's waters in the State.
Presidential Decree No. 198 authorizes water districts to set cost-recovering rates, but
nowhere establishes the nationwide, progressively declining framework this bill
provides. Executive Order No. 22 (2023) recognized the sector's fragmentation and
created the Water Resources Management Office to harmonize it. The gap this bill fills
is therefore real and unaddressed by existing law.

       The Fair Water Billing Act delivers these principal reforms:

          1. Consumers do not pay for inefficiency. During the transition, Sec. 5
             bars recovery of non-revenue-water costs exceeding the efficiency
             benchmark. Even costs within it are recoverable only after audit, public
             hearing, and specific regulatory findings.
          2. The benchmark must keep falling. Sec. 6 caps the initial benchmark
             at 25 percent of system input (or any lower binding benchmark) and
             drives it to zero by the end of the fifth year after the implementing rules
             take effect.
          3. The transition is temporary, not a permanent allowance. On
             expiration, all recovery of non-revenue-water costs is prohibited.
          4. The rule covers the whole sector. Sec. 3 reaches water districts, LGU
             waterworks, MWSS and its concessionaires, private utilities,
             cooperatives, community and subdivision systems, bulk suppliers, and
             operators under concessions, joint ventures, and similar arrangements.
          5. Hidden recovery is barred. Secs. 7, 9, and 10 prevent prohibited costs
             from being buried in base rates, fixed charges, tariff adjustments, bulk-
             water contracts, affiliate transactions, management fees, or renamed
             billing lines.
          6. Loss-reducing investment stays recoverable—under safeguards.
             Sec. 7 permits prudent pipeline rehabilitation, leak detection, district
             metering, pressure management, and meter replacement where
             necessary, cost-effective, transparently procured, independently
             verified, and protected against double recovery.
          7. Consumers gain enforceable rights. Annual performance scorecards,
             disclosure of beneficial ownership and related-party transactions, public
             hearings, independent audits, refunds with legal interest, personal
             accountability for knowing violations, and whistleblower protection.

        The bill accounts for real differences among providers. A one-time extension of
not more than two years may be granted only to a qualified financially distressed
public, cooperative, community, or nonprofit provider, and only upon public hearing,
an independently audited rehabilitation plan, definite targets, quarterly reporting, and
a finding that its distress arose from neither fraud, gross negligence, fund diversion,
prohibited related-party dealings, nor willful neglect. A Water Loss Reduction and
Modernization Program further assists qualified public and community systems, with
assistance walled off from dividends, bonuses, management fees, refunds, and any
cost traceable to fraud or gross negligence.

         The principle is simple and fair: Ang tubig na hindi umabot sa gripo ay hindi
dapat umabot sa bayarin. Responsibility must rest with those who control the
pipelines, meters, reservoirs, billing systems, and investment decisions. By pairing a
defined transition, declining benchmarks, public scrutiny, and eventual zero recovery,
the Fair Water Billing Act protects consumers while preserving lawful and prudent
utility operations — completing, on the water side, the same promise of fairness now
being written into the nation's power bills.

       In view of the foregoing, the swift passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

   REQUIRING WATER SERVICE PROVIDERS TO REDUCE AND PUBLICLY

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Fair Water Billing
Act.”

        Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to:
       (a) Protect water consumers from unreasonable, unjustified, inefficient, and
excessive charges;
       (b) Ensure that consumers pay for water actually delivered to their service
connection and for other lawful services expressly authorized by law;
       (c) Treat non-revenue water exceeding the applicable efficiency benchmark,
and all non-revenue water upon the expiration of the transition period, as an operating
responsibility and business risk of the water service provider;
       (d) Establish progressively declining non-revenue water efficiency benchmarks
that lead to the eventual abolition of the recovery of non-revenue water costs from
consumers;
       (e) Promote the conservation, efficient utilization, and sustainable management
of the country’s water resources;
       (f) Encourage continuous investment in reliable, efficient, climate-resilient, and
properly maintained water supply and distribution systems;
       (g) Strengthen accountability, transparency, public participation, and
measurable performance among water service providers and regulatory bodies;
       (h) Provide appropriate technical and financial assistance to qualified public,
cooperative, community, and nonprofit water systems undertaking water-loss
reduction and modernization; and
       (i) Ensure that water rates remain just, reasonable, transparent, affordable,
nonconfiscatory, and consistent with the public interest.

        Sec. 3. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to all water service providers
engaged in the abstraction, production, treatment, transmission, bulk supply,
distribution, sale, or delivery of water to consumers, regardless of ownership, legal
form, franchise, service area, contractual arrangement, or regulatory classification.

      It shall include:
      (a) Local water districts;
      (b) Local government-owned or operated waterworks systems;
      (c) The Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System and its
      concessionaires, contractors, successors, or assignees;
      (d) Private water utilities and holders of certificates of public convenience;
      (e) Cooperatives, associations, community water systems, subdivision water
      systems, and developer-operated water systems;
      (f) Government-owned or -controlled corporations engaged in water supply or
      distribution;
      (g) Operators under concession agreements, joint ventures, public-private
      partnerships, management contracts, leases, or similar arrangements;
      (h) Bulk-water suppliers, insofar as their charges are passed on to retail water
      consumers; and
      (i) Any successor entity performing substantially similar functions.

     This Act shall likewise apply to every government agency, regulatory body, local
sanggunian, board, office, or entity authorized to approve, review, establish,
recommend, or regulate water rates and charges.

        Sec. 4. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:
        (a) “Applicable efficiency benchmark” refers to the maximum percentage of
non-revenue water that may be considered by the appropriate regulator during the
transition period, subject to the conditions and limitations prescribed under this Act;
        (b) “Apparent losses” refer to water volumes not properly billed because of
unauthorized consumption, illegal connections, water theft, meter inaccuracies, meter
tampering, customer-account errors, data-transfer errors, billing errors, or similar
commercial losses;
        (c) “Appropriate regulator” refers to the Water Regulatory Commission, once
created and organized by law, or, pending its creation, the government agency or body
legally authorized to regulate the concerned water service provider, including the
National Water Resources Board, the Local Water Utilities Administration, the
Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System Regulatory Office, the appropriate
local sanggunian, or any other authorized rate-setting or regulatory body;
        (d) “Authorized billed consumption” refers to water lawfully supplied to and
consumed by an authorized customer and billed on the basis of an accurate meter
reading or another lawful billing method approved by the appropriate regulator;
        (e) “Authorized unbilled public-use water” refers to water used for firefighting,
emergency response, disaster relief, necessary system flushing, public fountains, or
other public purposes specifically approved by the appropriate regulator and
separately measured or reasonably estimated, documented, and publicly disclosed;
        (f) “Beneficial owner” refers to the natural person who ultimately owns, controls,
exercises substantial influence over, or receives the principal economic benefit from a
juridical entity or transaction, whether directly or indirectly;
        (g) “Consumer” refers to any person, household, establishment, institution,
government office, or juridical entity receiving retail water service;
        (h) “Non-revenue water” refers to the difference between the volume of water
entering a water supply and distribution system and the volume constituting authorized
billed consumption, including real losses, apparent losses, and undocumented or
unauthorized unbilled consumption. Authorized unbilled public-use water shall not be
considered non-revenue water when it complies with paragraph (e) of this section;
        (i) “Real losses” refer to physical water losses from pressurized or non-
pressurized systems, including leakage from transmission and distribution mains,
service connections, storage facilities, reservoirs, tanks, treatment facilities, pumping
stations, and overflows occurring before the consumer’s service meter;
        (j) “Related party” refers to an affiliate, subsidiary, parent company, controlling
shareholder, beneficial owner, director, trustee, officer, or entity under common
ownership, control, management, or substantial influence with a water service
provider;
        (k) “Transition period” refers to the period during which progressively declining
efficiency benchmarks may be applied pursuant to Sec. 6 of this Act;
        (l) “Water-loss reduction investment” refers to a project specifically designed to
measure, prevent, detect, control, or reduce real or apparent losses, including pipeline
rehabilitation, district-metered areas, pressure-management systems, leak-detection
equipment, customer-meter replacement, bulk metering, digital monitoring,
supervisory control and data acquisition systems, and asset-management systems;
and
        (m) “Water service provider” refers to any public or private entity covered under
Sec. 3 of this Act.

        Sec. 5. Prohibition Against the Recovery of Non-Revenue Water Costs. —
During the transition period, no water service provider shall charge, bill, pass on,
collect, or otherwise recover from consumers any cost attributable to non-revenue
water exceeding the applicable efficiency benchmark prescribed under this Act.
      The applicable efficiency benchmark shall not constitute an automatic or
guaranteed recoverable allowance. Any cost attributable to non-revenue water within
the applicable benchmark may be considered in rate-setting only upon a finding by the
appropriate regulator, after audit and public hearing, that the cost:
      (a) Was actually and prudently incurred;
      (b) Was determined using the uniform methodology prescribed under this Act;
      (c) Is not attributable to fraud, willful misconduct, gross negligence,
      unreasonable delay, meter manipulation, billing error, inadequate maintenance,
      or failure to comply with an approved water-loss reduction plan;
      (d) Does not include any profit, markup, return on equity, penalty, or financing
      charge imposed on the value of water lost; and
      (e) Is consistent with the applicable benchmark and all other requirements of
      this Act.

        Upon the expiration of the transition period, no water service provider shall
directly or indirectly recover from consumers any cost attributable to non-revenue
water.

       Consumers may be billed only for:
       (a) Authorized billed consumption actually delivered to the consumer’s service
connection;
       (b) Reasonable fixed service charges representing prudent and efficient costs
unrelated to non-revenue water and expressly approved by the appropriate regulator;
       (c) Sewerage, sanitation, environmental, or other services actually provided
and lawfully approved; and
       (d) Other charges expressly authorized by law that are not intended to recover
the cost or value of non-revenue water.

       Any non-revenue water cost prohibited under this Act shall be treated as an
operating responsibility and business risk of the water service provider.

       Sec. 6. Progressively Declining Efficiency Benchmarks and Transition
Period. — Within one hundred twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the
appropriate regulators, coordinated by the Water Resources Management Office, shall
prescribe a uniform methodology for determining non-revenue water and
progressively declining efficiency benchmarks.

       The initial applicable efficiency benchmark shall not exceed twenty-five percent
(25%) of system input volume or an existing lower legally binding benchmark
applicable to the water service provider, whichever is lower.

       The appropriate regulator shall establish an annual benchmark for each water
service provider or comparable peer group. The benchmark shall:
       (a) Continuously decline every year;
       (b) Take into account the provider’s size, water source, system age, service
       area, operating conditions, existing binding standards, and demonstrated
       technical capacity;
       (c) Require measurable annual reductions supported by an audited water-loss
       reduction plan;
       (d) Never exceed the maximum national ceiling prescribed under this section;
       (e) Not be increased to compensate for a provider’s failure to meet a prior
       target; and
       (f) Reach zero not later than the end of the fifth (5th) year following the effectivity
       of the implementing rules and regulations.

       A water service provider that already operates below the applicable benchmark
shall be assigned a benchmark not higher than its most recently verified performance,
subject to continuing annual reduction.

       The appropriate regulator may grant a one-time extension not exceeding two
(2) years only to a financially distressed local water district, local government-operated
water system, cooperative, community water system, or nonprofit provider upon a
finding, after public hearing, that:
       (a) Immediate application of the zero benchmark would create a substantial and
documented risk of interruption of essential water service;
       (b) The provider has submitted an independently audited rehabilitation and
water-loss reduction plan;
       (c) The provider has exhausted reasonably available internal efficiency
measures and has applied for available technical or financial assistance;
       (d) The requested extension is supported by a definite implementation
schedule, annual reduction targets, and quarterly public reporting;
       (e) The benchmark will continue to decline during each year of the extension
and will reach zero upon its expiration; and
       (f) The provider’s condition is not attributable to fraud, willful misconduct, gross
negligence, diversion of funds, prohibited related-party transactions, or a willful failure
to maintain the system.

       No extension shall authorize recovery beyond the approved benchmark or
create an automatic entitlement to rate recovery.

       Sec. 7. Treatment of Non-Revenue Water in Rate-Setting. — In approving
or reviewing water rates, the appropriate regulator shall not:
       (a) Include in the revenue requirement any non-revenue water cost exceeding
the applicable efficiency benchmark;
       (b) Use a loss allowance, loss factor, efficiency factor, tariff gross-up, volume
adjustment, or similar mechanism that permits recovery beyond the applicable
benchmark;
        (c) Permit the recovery of raw-water, bulk-water, electricity, chemical,
treatment, pumping, transmission, distribution, or other operating costs attributable to
non-revenue water beyond the applicable benchmark;
        (d) Permit a water service provider to increase a base rate, volumetric rate,
minimum charge, fixed charge, adjustment mechanism, or other tariff component to
offset a prohibited non-revenue water cost;
        (e) Recognize non-revenue water as an automatic or guaranteed recoverable
cost; or
        (f) Permit any recovery of non-revenue water costs after the expiration of the
transition period.

       The appropriate regulator may approve the recovery of prudent water-loss
reduction investments upon a finding that:
       (a) The investment is necessary, technically sound, and cost-effective;
       (b) The investment was procured in accordance with applicable procurement,
competition, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and conflict-of-interest rules;
       (c) The investment is subject to measurable reduction targets and independent
performance verification;
       (d) The cost does not include the purchase, production, treatment, pumping,
financing, profit, markup, or value of water already lost;
       (e) The investment will not result in double recovery;
       (f) The provider has complied with the disclosure, audit, public-participation,
and reporting requirements of this Act; and
       (g) The proposed rate treatment reasonably allocates project risks between the
provider and consumers.

       Rates approved under this Act shall remain just and reasonable and shall afford
an efficient water service provider a reasonable opportunity, but not a guarantee, to
recover prudent costs unrelated to prohibited non-revenue water.

       Nothing in this Act shall be construed to require confiscatory rates or to prohibit
recovery of lawful and efficient costs that are not attributable to prohibited non-revenue
water.

     Sec. 8. Amendment of Sec. 63 of Presidential Decree No. 198, as
Amended. — Sec. 63 of Presidential Decree No. 198, as amended, is hereby further
amended by adding the following paragraphs at the end thereof:

       “Notwithstanding any provision of this section, no rate or charge shall include,
       directly or indirectly, any cost attributable to non-revenue water exceeding the
       applicable efficiency benchmark prescribed under the Fair Water Billing Act.

       Upon the expiration of the transition period under the Fair Water Billing Act, no
       rate or charge shall include any cost attributable to non-revenue water.
       Water-loss reduction investments may be included in rates only when approved
       in accordance with the standards, safeguards, public-participation
       requirements, performance conditions, and prohibition against double recovery
       prescribed under the Fair Water Billing Act.

       The review of any rate or charge shall include a determination of compliance
       with the applicable efficiency benchmark, beneficial-ownership and related-
       party disclosure requirements, consumer-refund obligations, and other
       requirements of the Fair Water Billing Act.”

        Sec. 9. Effect on Existing Rates, Tariffs, and Contracts. — Upon the
effectivity of this Act:
        (a) No new or increased non-revenue water charge or recovery mechanism
shall be approved except in accordance with the applicable efficiency benchmark and
the requirements of this Act;
        (b) Any separately identified non-revenue water charge exceeding the
applicable benchmark shall immediately cease to the extent of the excess;
        (c) No pending rate application shall be approved unless it complies with this
Act;
        (d) Within ninety (90) days from the issuance of the uniform methodology, every
water service provider shall submit to the appropriate regulator an unbundled
statement identifying every portion of its existing rate attributable to non-revenue
water;
        (e) Where a non-revenue water cost is embedded in an existing tariff and
cannot immediately be isolated, the appropriate regulator shall determine the amount
using the uniform methodology prescribed under this Act and shall implement the
corresponding rate adjustment within one hundred eighty (180) days from receipt of
complete data;
        (f) A water service provider shall not evade compliance by claiming that it has
no separately identified non-revenue water charge when such cost is embedded in its
revenue requirement, base rate, volumetric rate, fixed charge, minimum charge,
adjustment mechanism, or another tariff component;
        (g) No water service provider shall be entitled to an automatic rate increase
solely because recovery of non-revenue water has been reduced or prohibited; and
        (h) Existing contracts, concession agreements, loan agreements, and similar
arrangements shall be administered and implemented consistently with this Act,
subject to constitutional requirements and without impairing obligations unrelated to
the prohibited recovery from consumers.

        This Act shall apply to rates, charges, and collections imposed after its
effectivity. Nothing in this section shall prevent the regulator from reviewing collections
made before effectivity when authorized under existing law.
       Sec. 10. Prohibition Against Circumvention; Beneficial-Ownership and
Related-Party Disclosure. — No water service provider, affiliate, contractor, bulk-
water supplier, or other entity shall recover a prohibited non-revenue water cost by:
       (a) Changing the name or description of the charge;
       (b) Incorporating it into a base rate, minimum charge, fixed charge, commodity
charge, environmental charge, sewerage charge, sanitation charge, or other billing
component;
       (c) Establishing a rider, surcharge, automatic adjustment mechanism, or rate-
rebasing adjustment;
       (d) Increasing another regulated charge to offset the prohibition under this Act;
       (e) Passing the cost through a bulk-water supply agreement, affiliate
transaction, management fee, lease payment, or related-party contract;
       (f) Altering billing assumptions, volume projections, consumption bands, or tariff
denominators;
       (g) Reclassifying water losses as capital costs, regulatory assets, deferred
charges, extraordinary adjustments, or similar accounts; or
       (h) Adopting any other arrangement, device, or transaction intended to defeat
or circumvent this Act.

       Every water service provider shall disclose to the appropriate regulator:
       (a) The beneficial ownership and controlling interests of every bulk-water
supplier, contractor, affiliate, and entity receiving payments that are recovered or
proposed to be recovered through consumer rates;
       (b) All common directors, trustees, officers, beneficial owners, management
personnel, or controlling interests between the provider and such entity;
       (c) The material terms, pricing, volume commitments, escalation provisions,
guarantees, procurement method, and payment arrangements of the transaction; and
       (d) Any direct or indirect financial interest of a director, trustee, officer,
employee, regulator, consultant, or immediate family member in the transaction.

       A related-party transaction shall not be included in consumer rates unless the
appropriate regulator finds, after audit and public hearing, that it was transparently
procured, is prudent and necessary, was entered into on arm’s-length terms, and is
no less favorable to consumers than reasonably available alternatives.

       Failure to make a complete and truthful disclosure shall be sufficient ground to
disallow the related cost, without prejudice to other sanctions.




         Sec. 11. Duties of Water Service Providers. — Every water service provider
shall:
       (a) Prepare an annual water balance using the uniform methodology prescribed
under this Act;
        (b) Accurately measure system input volume, authorized billed consumption,
authorized unbilled public-use water, real losses, and apparent losses;
        (c) Establish annual and medium-term non-revenue water reduction targets
consistent with the applicable benchmark;
        (d) Implement active leak-detection and prompt leak-repair programs;
        (e) Establish district-metered areas where technically and economically
appropriate;
        (f) Implement pressure-management and reservoir-overflow controls;
        (g) Maintain, calibrate, test, and replace customer and bulk meters according
to prescribed standards;
        (h) Detect and act against illegal connections, meter tampering, and
unauthorized consumption in accordance with law and due process;
        (i) Maintain an updated asset registry and risk-based pipeline-replacement
program;
        (j) Use appropriate digital monitoring, supervisory control, data acquisition,
geographic-information, and smart-metering technologies where technically and
economically justified;
        (k) Maintain adequate records of water production, treatment, purchase,
transmission, distribution, consumption, and loss;
        (l) Implement transparent procurement, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and
conflict-of-interest safeguards for water-loss reduction projects;
        (m) Comply with water-quality, continuity, pressure, customer-service, and
other applicable performance standards;
        (n) Refrain from reducing service pressure, continuity, water quality, or
necessary maintenance for the purpose of appearing to meet an efficiency benchmark;
and
        (o) Submit accurate and timely reports required under this Act.

       Sec. 12. Annual Water Service Performance Scorecards. — The
appropriate regulator shall publish an annual performance scorecard for every water
service provider.

      The scorecard shall contain, at a minimum:
      (a) Total system input volume;
      (b) Authorized billed consumption;
      (c) Authorized unbilled public-use water;
      (d) Non-revenue water volume and percentage;
      (e) Estimated real and apparent losses;
      (f) The applicable efficiency benchmark;
      (g) The reduction or increase from the previous year;
      (h) The amount of non-revenue water cost allowed and disallowed in rates;
      (i) Water production and purchase costs;
      (j) Expenditures for leak detection, meter replacement, pipeline rehabilitation,
      pressure management, and other water-loss reduction measures;
       (k) Average hours of service;
       (l) Frequency and duration of service interruptions;
       (m) Water-pressure performance;
       (n) Water-quality compliance;
       (o) Number of reported and repaired leaks and average repair time;
       (p) Consumer complaints and their disposition;
       (q) Enforcement actions, penalties, refunds, or consumer credits;
       (r) Compliance with the provider’s water-loss reduction plan;
       (s) Material related-party transactions and beneficial-ownership disclosures;
       and
       (t) Other information necessary to assess efficiency, service quality,
       affordability, and accountability.

       Water service providers shall be classified into comparable peer groups based
on size, source, service area, system characteristics, and operating conditions. The
appropriate regulator shall publish comparative rankings within each peer group.

       All scorecards and underlying nonconfidential data shall be available in
searchable and downloadable formats and posted on the websites and in the principal
offices of the regulator and water service provider.

       Sec. 13. Public Participation in Rate Proceedings. — No rate application
involving non-revenue water costs, water-loss reduction investments, embedded tariff
adjustments, or related-party transactions shall be approved without:
       (a) Prior publication and public disclosure of the application and supporting
nonconfidential data;
       (b) Reasonable notice to affected consumers;
       (c) A meaningful opportunity for affected consumers, local government units,
consumer organizations, and other interested persons to submit written comments;
and
       (d) At least one accessible public hearing within the affected service area, with
remote participation where reasonably practicable.

       The application, audit results, water balance, applicable benchmark, proposed
investments, beneficial-ownership disclosures, related-party transactions, and
proposed consumer impact shall be made publicly available at least fifteen (15) days
before the hearing.

       The appropriate regulator shall issue a written decision stating its findings on
the material issues raised during the proceedings. No confidential treatment shall be
granted to information necessary to identify beneficial ownership, controlling interests,
related-party relationships, or the amount proposed to be recovered from consumers,
except to the extent expressly required by law.
       Sec. 14. Independent Audit and Verification. — The appropriate regulator
may require independent technical, financial, and performance audits to verify
reported non-revenue water, costs, investments, meter accuracy, reduction measures,
related-party transactions, and compliance with this Act.

      The auditor shall be independent of the water service provider, its affiliates,
contractors, beneficial owners, directors, trustees, officers, and management.
Government-owned water service providers shall remain subject to audit by the
Commission on Audit.

       The intentional falsification, material misstatement, concealment, destruction,
or manipulation of water-loss, meter, cost, billing, beneficial-ownership, or related-
party data shall constitute a separate violation of this Act, without prejudice to liability
under other laws.

       Sec. 15. Water Loss Reduction and Modernization Program. — A Water
Loss Reduction and Modernization Program is hereby established to provide targeted
technical and financial assistance to:
       (a) Financially distressed local water districts;
       (b) Local government-operated water systems;
       (c) Small cooperatives and community water systems;
       (d) Water systems serving geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas;
       and
       (e) Other nonprofit or public water service providers determined by the
       implementing agencies to require assistance.

       The Program shall prioritize:
       (a) Replacement of leaking and deteriorated pipelines;
       (b) Installation of bulk meters and district-metered areas;
       (c) Leak-detection and pressure-management equipment;
       (d) Replacement and calibration of inaccurate customer meters;
       (e) Digital mapping and asset-management systems;
       (f) Supervisory control and data acquisition systems;
       (g) Training and institutional capacity development; and
       (h) Emergency rehabilitation of systems suffering from exceptionally high
       physical losses.

      Assistance may consist of grants, concessional loans, technical assistance,
guarantees, or other lawful financing arrangements.

       No assistance shall be released unless the recipient has submitted an
independently audited water-loss reduction plan containing measurable annual
targets, implementation schedules, procurement arrangements, performance
indicators, beneficial-ownership disclosures, and public-reporting commitments.
       Assistance shall not be used for:
       (a) Dividends, profit distributions, bonuses, or management fees;
       (b) Payments to affiliates or related parties without competitive and transparent
       procurement;
       (c) Fines, penalties, or consumer refunds;
       (d) Costs arising from fraud, gross negligence, willful misconduct, or willful
       failure to maintain the system; or
       (e) Recovery of the cost or value of non-revenue water.

       Receipt of government assistance shall not authorize the recipient to pass
prohibited non-revenue water costs on to consumers.

       Sec. 16. Enforcement, Administrative Sanctions, and Personal
Accountability. — The appropriate regulator shall investigate violations of this Act
and, after notice and hearing, may:
       (a) Issue cease-and-desist orders;
       (b) Disallow or suspend the collection of prohibited rates and charges;
       (c) Direct the revision of tariffs, billing systems, and accounting records;
       (d) Order refunds or bill credits;
       (e) Deny, defer, or condition pending rate applications;
       (f) Require corrective-action and compliance plans;
       (g) Impose an administrative fine not exceeding one percent (1%) of the water
service provider’s annual gross revenue from water services for each serious violation,
taking into account the gravity, duration, recurrence, consumer impact, and financial
capacity of the provider;
       (h) Suspend or revoke a certificate, permit, or regulatory approval when
authorized by law;
       (i) Recommend the cancellation, suspension, or nonrenewal of a franchise,
concession, joint venture, or similar authority in cases of repeated or grave violations;
and
       (j) Impose other lawful sanctions necessary to enforce this Act.
No administrative fine, penalty, refund, legal expense arising from a violation, or similar
amount shall be treated as a recoverable operating expense or passed on to
consumers.

       Any director, trustee, officer, employee, agent, or representative of a public or
private water service provider who knowingly authorizes, approves, conceals,
implements, or causes the collection of a prohibited charge shall, after notice and
hearing, be personally subject to the applicable administrative sanctions, without
prejudice to civil or criminal liability under existing laws.

      Any member, officer, or employee of a regulatory or rate-setting body who
knowingly authorizes, approves, conceals, or facilitates a prohibited charge shall be
referred to the appropriate disciplinary authority, the Office of the Ombudsman, or
another competent body for proceedings under applicable laws.

        Personal liability under this section shall attach only upon a specific finding that
the person knowingly participated in, authorized, approved, concealed, or caused the
violation.

        Sec. 17. Consumer Refunds and Remedies. — Any amount collected in
violation of this Act shall be refunded to affected consumers.
The refund shall cover the full period beginning on the date the applicable prohibition
or efficiency benchmark became effective, or the date the unlawful collection
commenced, whichever is later, until the collection ceased.

        Legal interest shall be computed from the date of each unlawful collection until
full reimbursement at the rate prescribed under applicable law and prevailing
jurisprudence.

       Refunds or bill credits shall be completed within one hundred eighty (180) days
from the date the order directing the refund becomes final and executory, unless the
appropriate regulator prescribes a shorter period in the interest of consumers.

       Refunds may be made through:
       (a) Direct payment;
       (b) Automatic bill credits;
       (c) Reduction of succeeding bills; or
       (d) Another transparent method providing full value to the consumer.

       The water service provider shall provide each affected consumer with written
or electronic notice stating the refund period, principal amount, interest, manner of
payment, and schedule of release or credit.

       A refund shall not be conditioned on the filing of an individual complaint when
the affected class of consumers and the amount collected can be determined from the
provider’s records.

        Refunds due to disconnected account holders shall be paid directly upon
verification of identity and account records. Refunds due to deceased account holders
shall be released to the lawful estate, heirs, or authorized representative upon
submission of reasonable proof prescribed by the regulator.

       Unclaimed refunds shall be held in trust in a segregated account, shall not be
treated as income or assets available for distribution by the provider, and shall remain
payable to the entitled consumer or lawful successor. The appropriate regulator shall
prescribe procedures for notice, safekeeping, claims, and disposition consistent with
applicable law.

      The remedies under this Act shall be without prejudice to civil, criminal,
administrative, contractual, or other remedies available under existing laws.

        Sec. 18. Whistleblower and Anti-Retaliation Protection. — No employee,
contractor, consultant, consumer, auditor, witness, or other person who, in good faith,
reports a suspected violation of this Act, provides information, produces records,
participates in an investigation, or testifies in a proceeding shall be dismissed,
suspended, demoted, harassed, threatened, discriminated against, blacklisted, denied
water service, or subjected to retaliation.

       The identity of a reporting person shall be kept confidential to the extent
permitted by law and consistent with due process.

        Retaliation shall constitute a separate violation of this Act. A person subjected
to retaliation may be granted appropriate relief, including reinstatement, restoration of
benefits, correction of employment records, and other remedies available under law.

      This protection shall not apply to a person who knowingly submits false
information or acts with malice.

       Sec. 19. Regulatory Coordination and Institutional Transition. — Pending
the creation and organization of a Water Regulatory Commission by law, the existing
appropriate regulators shall exercise their respective powers and perform the duties
prescribed under this Act within their existing jurisdictions.

      The Water Resources Management Office shall convene and coordinate the
concerned regulators and implementing agencies to adopt uniform national definitions,
accounting methodologies, efficiency benchmarks, reporting standards, scorecards,
audit protocols, beneficial-ownership disclosure requirements, public-participation
procedures, and anti-circumvention rules.

       Upon the creation and organization of the Water Regulatory Commission, the
regulatory, enforcement, rate-review, reporting, and scorecard functions prescribed
under this Act shall be exercised by the Commission to the extent provided by its
enabling law.

       No regulatory transition, reorganization, transfer of functions, or vacancy in
office shall suspend or delay the implementation of the prohibitions and consumer
protections established under this Act.
       Sec. 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred twenty
(120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Environment and Natural
Resources, through the Water Resources Management Office, shall convene the
National Water Resources Board, Local Water Utilities Administration, Metropolitan
Waterworks and Sewerage System Regulatory Office, Department of the Interior and
Local Government, Department of Finance, Department of Budget and Management,
Commission on Audit, and other concerned agencies to promulgate the implementing
rules and regulations.

       The rules shall prescribe:
       (a) A uniform water-balance and non-revenue water accounting methodology;
       (b) Progressively declining efficiency benchmarks and procedures for
determining provider-specific targets;
       (c) A methodology for identifying non-revenue water costs embedded in
regulated rates;
       (d) Standards for determining costs that may be considered within the
benchmark during the transition period;
       (e) Standards for authorized unbilled public-use water;
       (f) Procedures for rate review, public hearings, refunds, consumer complaints,
and enforcement;
       (g) National scorecard and open-data standards;
       (h) Beneficial-ownership and related-party disclosure requirements;
       (i) Water-loss reduction planning and audit requirements;
       (j) Eligibility and performance conditions for modernization assistance;
       (k) Whistleblower confidentiality and anti-retaliation procedures; and
       (l) Other measures necessary to implement this Act.

      The implementing rules shall be issued after public consultation with
consumers, water service providers, local governments, water districts, civil society
organizations, technical experts, and other stakeholders.

        No implementing rule shall authorize any recovery beyond the limits or
transition period prescribed under this Act.

      Sec. 21. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
concerned agencies.

        Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation
of this Act and the Water Loss Reduction and Modernization Program shall be included
in the annual General Appropriations Act.
         Sec. 22. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

         Sec. 23. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
administrative issuances, franchises, local ordinances, rules, regulations, rate orders,
tariffs, methodologies, concession provisions, and other issuances inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       Sec. 24. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least one (1) newspaper of general
circulation.
