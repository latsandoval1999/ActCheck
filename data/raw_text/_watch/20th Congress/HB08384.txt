                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Discrimination in the workplace remains a persistent and often
underreported concern in the Philippines. According to global recruitment agency
Michael Page Philippines in its 2025 Talent Trends Report, approximately 9% of
464 respondents have personally experienced workplace discrimination.1 More
tellingly, a larger 35% of respondents reported witnessing discriminatory acts
against colleagues2 – an indication that many incidents remain unreported due to
fear of retaliation, stigma, or lack of effective grievance mechanisms. These

      Workplace discrimination manifests in various forms – ranging from age
bias and disability-based exclusion to unequal treatment on the basis of sex,
religion, ethnicity, health status, physical features and appearance, and political
affiliation, among others. Persistent gender pay gaps, barriers to employment for
older workers, prejudicial hiring practices, and discriminatory job advertisements
continue to undermine the constitutional guarantee of equal protection and the
fundamental right to work. In many instances, qualified individuals are denied
opportunities not because of merit, competence, or performance, but because of
characteristics unrelated to job requirements.

      Beyond its moral and legal implications, discrimination carries substantial
economic and social costs. It erodes employee morale, weakens productivity,
fuels workplace conflict, and limits innovation. Conversely, studies consistently
show that diverse and inclusive workplaces are more dynamic, resilient, and
competitive. Organizations that embrace diversity benefit from broader
perspectives, improved decision-making, and stronger employee engagement.
Equality and inclusion are not merely ideals – they are strategic imperatives in a
modern, globally competitive economy.

      The Philippines has long signified its commitment to international labor
standards, particularly through its adherence to key conventions of the
International     Labour   Organization    (ILO),    including    those   addressing
discrimination in employment and occupation. As a State Party to these
commitments, the Philippines bears the responsibility of translating international
principles into concrete, enforceable domestic protections that safeguard the
dignity of every worker.
        This measure seeks to institutionalize the protection of all employees
  against discrimination in their workplaces on the account of age, civil status,
  disability, ethnicity, health status, language, national origin, physical features and
  appearance, political affiliation or belief, pregnancy or parental status, religion,
  sex or gender, socioeconomic status, or any other status or characteristic, whether
  actual or perceived. This bill aims to ensure that employment decisions are based
  solely on merit, competence, qualifications, performance, and lawful job-related
  requirements.

        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
  earnestly sought.

                         AN ACT
      PROHIBITING DISCRIMINATION IN THE WORKPLACE,
             PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Anti-
Discrimination in Workplace Act.”


      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to uphold the constitutional guarantee of equal protection of the laws and to
promote full employment and equality of employment opportunities for all. To
this end, the State shall ensure that all persons are afforded dignity, respect, and
fairness in the workplace, free from discrimination and harassment.
       Consistent with the Philippines’ obligations under International Labor
Organization (ILO) Convention No. 100 (Convention concerning Equal
Remuneration for Men and Women Workers for Work of Equal Value, 1951),
ILO Convention No. 111 (Convention concerning Discrimination in Respect of
Employment and Occupation, 1958), and ILO Convention No. 122 (Employment
Policy Convention, 1964), the State shall eliminate discrimination in employment
and occupation and promote equality of opportunity and treatment.


       The State recognizes that discrimination may arise from prejudice,
stereotypes, stigma, or bias relating to age, civil status, disability, ethnicity, health
status, language, national origin, physical features and appearance, political
affiliation or belief, pregnancy or parental status, religion, sex or gender,
socioeconomic status, or any other status or characteristic, whether actual or
perceived, and shall ensure that employment decisions are based solely on merit,
competence, qualifications, performance, and lawful job-related requirements.


       SEC. 3. Definitions. – For purposes of this Act:


       (a) Discrimination refers to any distinction, exclusion, restriction,
           harassment, or preference in employment which has the purpose or
           effect of nullifying, impairing, or denying equality of opportunity or
           treatment;


       (b) Employment refers to all aspects of the employer-employee
           relationship in the private sector, including recruitment, hiring,
           selection, placement, promotion, transfer, training, apprenticeship,
           probation, compensation, benefits, job assignment, discipline,
           suspension, demotion, termination, retirement, and other terms and
           conditions of employment;
(c) Employer refers to any person, partnership, corporation, association,
   labor contractors, or other juridical entity engaged in private business,
   industry, or enterprise that employs one or more persons, and includes
   any person acting in the interest of such employer.


(d) Protected Characteristic refers to any personal attribute or status
   which is protected from discrimination under this Act, as follows:


      (i)     Age;
      (ii)    Civil status;
      (iii)   Disability;
      (iv)    Ethnicity;
      (v)     Health status;
      (vi)    Language;
      (vii) National origin;
      (viii) Physical features and appearance;
      (ix)    Political affiliation or belief;
      (x)     Pregnancy or parental status;
      (xi)    Religion;
      (xii) Sex or gender;
      (xiii) Socioeconomic status; or
      (xiv) Any other status or characteristic, whether actual or
              perceived, that is not directly related to the performance of
              lawful job-related requirements; and


(e) Perceived Status refers to characteristics attributed to a person,
   whether accurate or not.
      (f) Publisher refers to any person or juridical entity that publishes,
         circulates,   disseminates,    or   otherwise   makes       available   job
         advertisements or recruitment materials, whether in print, broadcast,
         online, or digital form, on behalf of or for the benefit of an employer.


      SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. – It shall be unlawful for any employer or any
person acting on behalf of an employer to:


     (1) Refuse to hire, employ, promote, assign, or retain an employee on the
        basis of any protected characteristic or perceived status;


     (2) Discriminate in compensation, wages, salary rates, allowances, bonuses,
        incentives, benefits, training opportunities, promotion, or other
        privileges of employment on the basis of any protected characteristic or
        perceived status rather than on merit, competence, seniority,
        performance, or lawful job-related criteria;


     (3) Limit, segregate, classify, assign, or otherwise treat employees or job
        applicants in a manner that would deprive or tend to deprive any person
        of employment opportunities, promotion, compensation, or favorable
        working conditions on the basis of any protected characteristic or
        perceived status;


     (4) Publish, circulate, or cause to be published or circulated any job
        advertisement, announcement, or recruitment material that expresses,
        directly or indirectly, any preference, limitation, exclusion, or
        discrimination based on any protected characteristic or perceived status,
        unless such requirement constitutes a bona fide occupational
        qualification under this Act;
     (5) Subject an employee to harassment, ridicule, hostility, or a hostile work
        environment based on protected characteristics or perceived status; or


     (6) Retaliate against any individual who files a complaint or participates in
        proceedings under this Act.


      SEC. 5. Equal Pay for Work of Equal Value. – Employees should be
given equal pay for work of equal value. To this end:


     (a) Employers shall provide equal remuneration for work of equal value and
        shall not discriminate in wages, salary rates, allowances, bonuses,
        incentives, benefits, or other forms of compensation on the basis of any
        protected characteristic or perceived status;


     (b) Work shall be considered of equal value when it requires substantially
        comparable levels of skill, effort, responsibility, and working
        conditions, regardless of job title or classification;


     (c) Any differentiation in compensation shall be permitted only when based
        on legitimate, objective, and job-related factors, including, but not
        limited to:


                (i)     A merit-based system;
                (ii)    A system measuring earnings by quantity or quality of
                        production;
                (iii)   A bona fide seniority system; or
                 (iv)   Any other reasonable and demonstrable business-related
                        factor    that   is   applied   consistently   and   without
                        discriminatory intent or effect; and


     (d) Employers shall maintain payroll records and job classification
         standards sufficient to determine compliance with this section, subject
         to inspection in accordance with the Labor Code and its implementing
         rules and regulations.


      Nothing in this section shall be construed to authorize an employer to
reduce the wages of any employee or to prevent collective bargaining agreements
from providing more favorable wage standards.


      SEC. 6. Bona Fide Occupational Qualification. – Nothing in this Act
shall prevent an employer from imposing qualifications reasonably necessary for
the normal operation of the enterprise, provided such qualifications are lawful,
proportionate, reasonable, and directly related to the job.


      SEC. 7. Enforcement and Jurisdiction. – Complaints alleging
administrative violations of this Act shall be filed with the Department of Labor
and Employment (DOLE) Regional Office.


      The filing of an administrative complaint under this Act shall not preclude
the filing of a separate civil action for damages or a criminal action before the
appropriate court or authority where warranted.


      In cases involving constructive dismissal or discriminatory dismissal, the
remedies under Article 294 of the Labor Code on illegal dismissal shall apply,
and such cases may be filed with the National Labor Relations Commission,
without prejudice to additional reliefs provided under this Act.


      Once a complainant establishes a prima facie case of discrimination, the
burden of proof shall shift to the employer to demonstrate that the challenged act
or practice was based on lawful, job-related, and non-discriminatory grounds.


      SEC. 8. Administrative Remedies. – Upon a finding that an employer has
committed an act of discrimination in violation of this Act, the DOLE may order
any or all of the following remedies:


      (a) Cease-and-desist orders directing the employer to refrain from
           discriminatory practices;


      (b) Removal or modification of discriminatory policies or practices,
           including payment of wage differentials, where compensation
           discrimination is established; and


      (c) Such other reliefs that may be granted by the DOLE within the scope
           of its authority, as may be just and equitable under the circumstances.


       In cases involving systemic or repeated discrimination, the DOLE may
order periodic compliance reporting for a specified period.


       The grant of remedies under this Act shall not diminish rights or benefits
granted under existing labor laws, collective bargaining agreements, or
company policies.
       SEC. 9. Penalties. – For any violation of this Act or its implementing rules
and regulations, or for any willful disobedience of the orders of the DOLE
pursuant to Section 8 of this Act, the aggrieved employee may, at the employee’s
option, file a case before the appropriate court, simultaneously with, or
notwithstanding the filing or pendency of, any administrative case under Section
8 of this Act.


       Any violation falling under this Section shall be punished with a fine of
not less than fifty thousand pesos (Php50,000.00) but not more than Five Hundred
Thousand Pesos (Php500,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than three (3)
months but not more than two (2) years, or both, at the discretion of the court.


       Where the offender is a corporation, partnership, association, or other
juridical entity, the responsible officers who knowingly authorized, participated
in, or tolerated the violation shall be held liable for their acts.


       SEC. 10. Non-Diminution of Rights. – This Act does not diminish or
extinguish any protection provided under Republic Act No. 9710 (Magna Carta
of Women), Republic Act No. 10911 (Anti-Age Discrimination in Employment
Act), Republic Act No. 7277 (Magna Carta for Disabled Persons), Republic Act
No. 11166 (Philippine HIV and AIDS Policy Act), Republic Act No. 11313 (Safe
Spaces Act), and other related laws addressing discrimination and workplace
equality.


       SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Labor and Employment, in
consultation with relevant government agencies, employer organizations, labor
groups, and civil society stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules
and regulations for the proper and effective implementation of this Act.
      SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any section or provision
of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other sections or provisions
which are not affected shall continue to be in full force and effect.


      SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations or parts thereof inconsistent herewith are
repealed, amended or modified accordingly.


      SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days from the
date of its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
