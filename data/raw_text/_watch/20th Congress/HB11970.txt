                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to institutionalize climate literacy and sustainability
education at all levels of the Philippine education system. It embeds
Sustainability Literacy Learning Competencies into the existing MATATAG
Curriculum learning areas for Grades 4 and 7, without creating new subjects,
consistent with the curriculum decongestion agenda of the Second Congressional
Commission on Education (EDCOM II).

      The Philippines is among the most disaster-prone nations in the world,
making the climate crisis a learning crisis. The Department of Education
estimates that learners lose an average of fifty-three (53) school days per school
year to class suspensions caused by typhoons and other disasters.1 The Second
Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II) has likewise reported that
the Department of Education incurs annual losses of seventeen point
ninety-eight (Php 17.98) billion pesos due to high hazard exposure.2

      Notwithstanding this exposure, the generation most affected by these
hazards is not being adequately prepared to understand them. This measure
addresses the gap on three fronts. It makes climate competencies explicit and
measurable at the grade levels most learners reach, ensures that learners outside
the formal school system are also included, and requires every graduate,
regardless of discipline, to understand the climate realities they will confront as
professionals and citizens.

      Filipino children already live through the climate crisis. They wade
through floods to reach school and lose weeks of learning each year. They
deserve an education that enables them to understand, adapt to, and act on the

                          AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING CLIMATE LITERACY AND SUSTAINABILITY
EDUCATION AT ALL LEVELS OF THE PHILIPPINE EDUCATION SYSTEM,
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

                                  CHAPTER I
                              GENERAL PROVISIONS

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Klima Kurriculum Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology, give
priority to education, and make quality education accessible to all. Recognizing that
the Philippines is among the countries most vulnerable to climate change, the State
affirms that climate literacy is a fundamental civic competence that every Filipino
must possess, regardless of age, status, or place of residence.

       Toward this end, the State shall institutionalize climate literacy and
sustainability education across basic education, the Alternative Learning System
(ALS), and higher education. This shall be done by integrating competencies into
existing curricula rather than adding new subjects, consistent with the curriculum
decongestion reforms of the Second Congressional Commission on Education
(EDCOM II).

       SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act seeks to achieve the following objectives:

          a. To embed climate literacy and sustainability education at critical entry
       points in the basic education curriculum, specifically Grade 4 and Grade 7,
       that precede the most acute dropout thresholds in the Philippine education
       system, so that no Filipino learner is denied a foundational climate education
       on account of early school leaving;​
       b.​ To integrate, rather than add, climate literacy into existing subjects and
    curriculum structures under the MATATAG Curriculum, consistent with the
    curriculum decongestion mandate established by the EDCOM II, and to do so
    through named, assessable Sustainability Literacy Learning Competencies
    embedded in approved Curriculum Guides;​

       c.​ To extend climate literacy to all learners outside the formal school
    system through the Alternative Learning System, with dedicated
    Klima-integrated modules in the relevant ALS Learning Strands and
    recognition of ALS completion as an equivalent form of climate literacy
    achievement;​

        d.​ To institutionalize a required, non-substitutable General Education
    course in climate literacy at the higher education level, namely Klima 101,
    following the precedent of Republic Act (RA) No. 1425, entitled “An Act to
    Include in the Curricula of All Public and Private Schools, Colleges and Universities
    Courses on the Life, Works and Writings of Jose Rizel, Particularly His Novels Noli
    Me Tangere and El Filibuterismo, Authorizing the Printing and Distribution
    Thereof, and for Other Purposes” and subject to minimum course outcomes
    prescribed by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED);​

       e.​ To ensure that all climate education content involving indigenous
    ecological knowledge and indigenous peoples’ climate practices is developed
    with the Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC) of the relevant indigenous
    peoples and cultural communities, in accordance with RA No. 8371,
    otherwise known as “The Indigenous Peoples Rights Act of 1997”, and that such
    content is co-authored with, not merely about, indigenous knowledge
    holders; and​

       f.​ To establish a statutory inter-agency coordination structure, an annual
    accountability        mechanism          to      Congress,        and        a
    student-learning-outcome-anchored monitoring and evaluation framework to
    ensure that this Act is not merely promulgated but actually implemented to
    measurable effect.

​   SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

       a.​ Climate literacy refers to the understanding of the climate system, the
    causes and consequences of climate change and variability, the relationship
    between climate and human and ecological well-being, and the capacity to
    apply this understanding in personal, civic, and professional
    decision-making. Climate literacy encompasses scientific, ethical, and
    practical dimensions and is understood as a component of fundamental 21st
    century civic competence;

        b.​ Klima 101 refers to the three (3)-unit required, non-substitutable
    General Education course in climate literacy and sustainability established for
    all baccalaureate and two (2)-year technical-vocational programs in all higher
education institutions (HEIs) pursuant to Chapter III of this Act;​

    c.​ Klima Kurriculum refers to the integrated framework of climate
literacy and sustainability education provided for under this Act, consisting
of the SLLCs for basic education, the ALS Coordination Track, and the Klima
101 General Education course for higher education, together with the
standards, assessment instruments, teacher development programs, and
inter-agency governance mechanisms established pursuant to this Act;​

    d.​ MATATAG Curriculum refers to the revised basic education
curriculum framework issued by the Department of Education (DepEd)
through the MATATAG Curriculum policy, designed to decongest and
strengthen the K to 10 basic education curriculum, and the related
Curriculum Guides issued for each learning area and grade level thereunder;​

    e.​ Sustainability Literacy Learning Competencies (SLLCs) refers to the
named, assessable learning competencies pertaining to climate literacy and
sustainability education that the Secretary of Education shall embed into the
MATATAG Curriculum Guides for Grade 4 and Grade 7 pursuant to this Act.
The SLLCs are not a standalone subject but an integrated set of measurable
learning outcomes distributed across existing subjects and learning areas at
each grade level;​

   f.​ ALS Coordination Track refers to the Klima-integrated modules for the
Alternative Learning System developed by the Bureau of Alternative
Learning System (BALS) pursuant to Section 9 of this Act, designed for
delivery within ALS Learning Strands 2 and 5, and recognized as an
equivalent form of climate literacy achievement for learners outside the
formal school system;​

    g.​ Integration refers to the embedding of climate literacy learning
competencies, content, and assessments into existing subjects, learning areas,
or curriculum guides, without creating a new, standalone subject or adding
net instructional hours beyond those already allocated to the subjects into
which integration occurs. Integration is the mode through which the SLLCs
are delivered at the basic education level under this Act;​

    h.​ Inter-Agency Advisory Group (IAAG) refers to the Klima Kurriculum
Inter-Agency Advisory Group created by Section 22 of this Act to coordinate
implementation across agencies, review and update SLLCs, and report
annually to Congress;​

   i.​ Indigenous People (IP) Communities refer to indigenous peoples and
indigenous cultural communities as defined under the IPRA, including their
communities, ancestral domains, and traditional institutions; and​

   j.​ Sustainability Education refers to education that develops the
knowledge, skills, values, and attitudes necessary to promote environmental
      integrity, social equity, and economic sustainability, while ensuring
      intergenerational equity and fostering resilient and just communities and
      societies. It encompasses the environmental, social, and economic dimensions
      of sustainable development, including, but not limited to, climate literacy.

      SEC. 5. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to:

         a.​ All public and private basic education institutions nationwide offering
      education from Kindergarten through Grade 12, including those operating
      under the K to 12 Basic Education Program established by RA No. 10533,
      otherwise known as the “Enhanced Basic Education Act of 2013”;​

          b.​ ﻿All higher education institutions, both public and private, including
      state universities and colleges, local universities and colleges, and private
      HEIs operating under the jurisdiction of the CHED pursuant to RA No. 7722,
      otherwise known as the “Higher Education Act of 1994”;

         c.​ ﻿All providers and facilitators of Alternative Learning System programs
      operating under the framework of RA No. 11510, otherwise known as the
      “Alternative Learning System Act”; and​

         d.​ ﻿All educational institutions and programs within the Bangsamoro
      Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao: Provided, That the implementation
      of this Act within the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao
      (BARMM) shall be coordinated with the Ministry of Basic, Higher, and
      Technical Education of the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim
      Mindanao (MBHTE-BARMM), in recognition of the autonomous powers of
      the Bangsamoro Government over education within its territorial jurisdiction
      under RA No. 11054, otherwise known as the “Organic Law for the Bangsamoro
      Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao”, and the provisions of this Act
      applicable to the BARMM shall be implemented through, and not in
      derogation of, the exercise of such autonomous powers: Provided, further, That
      the MBHTE-BARMM shall, in coordination with the DepEd and the CHED,
      develop and issue equivalent orders and guidelines to achieve the objectives
      of this Act within the Bangsamoro educational system.

                                 CHAPTER II
                         BASIC EDUCATION MANDATE

       SEC. 6. Grade 4 Climate Literacy Integration. – The DepEd shall embed SLLCs
into the following existing learning areas for Grade 4 under the MATATAG
Curriculum: Araling Panipunan, Science, Edukasyon sa Pagpapakatao or Good
Manners and Right Conduct/Edukasyon sa Pagpapakatao, and Health and Physical
Education. The integration shall be accomplished through the amendment of
existing MATATAG Curriculum Guides for these learning areas and shall not
require the creation of any new or standalone subject at the Grade 4 level.
       For the avoidance of legislative doubt, no new subject shall be created at the
Grade 4 level pursuant to this Act. The SLLCs for Grade 4 shall focus on place-based
climate literacy, including the learner’s direct relationship to local climate
conditions, water systems, food systems, and community resilience practices, and
shall be developmentally appropriate for the Grade 4 learner. The Bureau of
Curriculum Development (BCD) shall ensure that the Grade 4 SLLCs are aligned
with the learner-centered, age-appropriate, and localization principles of the
MATATAG Curriculum.

       SEC. 7. Grade 7 Climate Literacy Integration. – The DepEd shall embed SLLCs
into the following existing learning areas for Grade 7 under the MATATAG
Curriculum: Araling Panipunan, Science, Edukasyon sa Pagpapakatao and Music,
Arts, Physical Education, and Health (MAPEH). The integration shall build
explicitly upon and deepen the foundational climate literacy competencies
established at the Grade 4 level, advancing the learner’s capacity from place-based
awareness to analytical understanding of climate systems, climate justice, and
regional and national disaster risk. The integration shall be accomplished through
the amendment of existing MATATAG Curriculum Guides for these learning areas.

        For the avoidance of legislative doubt, no new subject shall be created at the
Grade 7 level pursuant to this Act. The BCD shall ensure continuity and
developmental progression between the Grade 4 and Grade 7 SLLCs so that the
latter explicitly expand upon competencies introduced at the former level.

        SEC. 8. Sustainability Literacy Learning Competencies. – The Secretary of
Education shall issue a Department Order embedding the Sustainability Literacy
Learning Competencies into the MATATAG Curriculum Guides for the applicable
Grade 4 and Grade 7 learning areas within one hundred eighty (180) days from the
effectivity of this Act. The SLLCs shall be named, assessable, and integrated into the
curriculum guides as official competencies subject to the same assessment processes
applicable to all other competencies in those learning areas.

        Prior to the issuance of the Department Order, the DepEd shall convene a
Technical Working Group (TWG), to be chaired by the BCD, which shall include
representatives from the Bureau of Learner Support Services (BLSS), the National
Educators Academy of the Philippines (NEAP), the Department of Science and
Technology (DOST), the Climate Change Commission (CCC), and at least two (2)
civil society organizations (CSOs) with demonstrated expertise in climate education
and two representatives from IP communities with expertise in indigenous
ecological knowledge.

      The TWG shall develop the SLLCs through a process that is consultative,
evidence-based, and anchored in Philippine climate science as certified by the CCC
pursuant to Section 24 of this Act.

      If the Department Order is not issued within the one hundred eighty (180)
day period prescribed in this section, the Secretary of Education shall appear before
the House Committee on Basic Education and Culture (CBEC) within thirty (30)
days thereafter to report on the reasons for the delay and the corrective measures
being undertaken. The obligation to issue the Department Order is not extinguished
by the lapse of the prescribed period.

       The Grade 4 SLLCs shall focus on place-based climate literacy, which shall
include, at minimum, competencies pertaining to understanding local climate
patterns and changes as experienced in the learner’s own community; the
relationship between climate, water, and food security as experienced at the
household and community level; the effects of natural hazards on communities and
the concept of community resilience; and the recognition of indigenous and local
practices for living with and adapting to climate variability.

      The Grade 7 SLLCs shall focus on analytical climate literacy, which shall
include, at minimum, competencies pertaining to understanding climate as a system
with causes and feedback mechanisms beyond the local scale; the concept of climate
and environmental justice and its application to the Philippine context; regional and
national disaster risk reduction and management frameworks and the role of science
in them; the contribution of indigenous ecological knowledge to climate
understanding and adaptation; and the individual, community, and institutional
dimensions of climate action.

        SEC. 9. Alternative Learning System Coordination Track. – The BALS shall,
within twelve (12) months from the effectivity of this Act, develop Klima-integrated
learning modules for ALS Learning Strand 2 or Scientific and Critical Thinking
Skills, and ALS Learning Strand 5 or Understanding the Self, Society, and the World.
The modules shall be designed to develop climate literacy competencies equivalent
in substance to the SLLCs for basic education, adapted appropriately to the
out-of-school youth and adult learner context.

       The modules shall be delivered in the mother tongue or primary language of
the learning community to the extent practicable, consistent with the language
policy of the ALS Act. No standalone ALS subject on climate literacy shall be
created; the Klima-integrated modules shall be embedded within the existing
learning strands. The MBHTE-BARMM shall issue an equivalent instrument for ALS
learners within the Bangsamoro in coordination with the BALS, within twelve (12)
months from the effectivity of this Act.

       SEC. 10. Teacher Capacity Development. – The DepEd, through the National
Educators Academy of the Philippines, shall develop and deliver a mandatory
in-service training program for all Grade 4 and Grade 7 teachers assigned to the
learning areas of Araling Panlipunan, Science, Edukasyon sa Pagpapakatao, Health
and Physical Education, and MAPEH covering, at minimum: foundational climate
science applicable to the Philippine context; the pedagogical approaches for
integrating climate literacy into existing learning areas; the localized SLLCs
applicable to the teacher’s own division; and strategies for place-based, indigenous
knowledge-inclusive, and inquiry-based climate education. Such training shall be
mandatory and not subject to the discretion of individual schools or divisions to
waive.
       The NEAP shall complete the initial training cycle for all target Grade 4 and
Grade 7 teachers within twenty-four (24) months of the issuance of the Department
Order under Section 8. A dedicated appropriation for teacher training under this Act
is established in Chapter VI. The Department shall not reduce the mandatory scope
of training to fit within existing professional development allocations; the
appropriation in Chapter VI is additional and dedicated. The initial training cycle
under this section shall prioritize teachers in Schools Division Offices (SDOs)
designated as climate-exposed priority areas under Section 11, with full national
coverage to be completed within three (3) years of the effectivity of this Act.

       SEC. 11. Localization. – SDOs throughout the country shall contextualize the
SLLCs and any supporting learning materials to the specific local climate conditions,
disaster history, indigenous territories, and dominant livelihood systems of their
respective divisions. Localization shall be a process of adapting the nationally
prescribed SLLCs to local realities, not a discretionary opt-out mechanism. The
SDOs in Sultan Kudarat, the divisions within the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region
in Muslim Mindanao, Bicol Region, Eastern Visayas, and other Divisions in regions
identified by the CCC and DOST-PAGASA as climate-exposed priority areas shall be
treated as priority areas for localization support, technical assistance, and
supplementary appropriation. The BCD, in coordination with the BLSS, shall
develop a localization guide within twelve (12) months from the effectivity of this
Act to support SDOs in developing locally contextualized climate literacy materials
consistent with the national SLLCs.

       SEC. 12. Monitoring in Basic Education. – The DepEd shall conduct an annual
assessment of SLLC learning outcomes in Grade 4 and Grade 7 as part of its regular
educational performance monitoring. Such monitoring shall be anchored in actual
student learning outcome data and shall not be limited to administrative or
compliance documentation. The methodology for SLLC learning outcome
assessment shall be developed by the BCD in coordination with the Philippine
Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) within twelve (12) months from the
issuance of the Department Order under Section 8. The Secretary of Education shall
submit an annual implementation and learning outcomes report to the House CBEC
and the Senate Committee on Basic Education (CBE) in accordance with the
provisions of Chapter VII of this Act.

                               CHAPTER III
                       HIGHER EDUCATION MANDATE

        SEC. 13. Klima 101 as Required General Education Component. – The CHED shall
issue a CHED Memorandum Order within one hundred eighty (180) days from the
effectivity of this Act establishing Klima 101 or Climate Literacy and Sustainability
as a three-unit required, non-substitutable General Education component for all
baccalaureate programs and two-year technical-vocational programs offered by all
public and private HEIs in the Philippines.
       Klima 101 shall not be offered as an elective, shall not be substituted by any
other course, and shall not be waivable on the basis of a student’s field of study,
degree program, or institutional arrangement. In this regard, the Rizal course
established under RA No. 1425 serves as the explicit legislative precedent for the
non-substitutable, universally required structure of Klima 101.

        Nothing in this section shall be construed to prevent HEIs from offering
additional, supplementary courses in climate science, environmental studies, or
sustainability as electives or program-specific requirements. Provided, That pending
the issuance of the CHED Memorandum Order, all HEIs shall offer Klima 101 using
the minimum course outcomes enumerated in Section 14 as the operative standard,
effective Academic Year 2028-2029, and the absence of a Memorandum Order shall
not excuse any HEI from this obligation.

      SEC. 14. Minimum Course Outcomes for Klima 101. – The CHED Memorandum
Order issued pursuant to Section 13 shall specify minimum course outcomes for
Klima 101. At minimum, the course shall address the following areas of learning:

          a.​ ﻿Philippine climate science and vulnerability, covering the physical
      science of climate, Philippine climate patterns and projections, and the
      disproportionate vulnerability of specific regions, communities, and sectors in
      the Philippines;​

          b.​ Climate and economic systems, covering the intersection of climate
      change with agriculture, fisheries, infrastructure, finance, labor, and poverty
      in the Philippine context;​

          c.​ Environmental and climate justice, covering the differential impacts of
      climate change on marginalized communities, women, youth, indigenous
      peoples, and persons with disabilities, and the ethical dimensions of climate
      response;​

         d.​ ﻿Indigenous ecological knowledge, covering the climate knowledge,
      adaptive practices, and ecological stewardship systems of Philippine
      indigenous peoples and cultural communities, to be developed and delivered
      with the Free, Prior and Informed Consent of the relevant communities and
      with the involvement of indigenous knowledge holders as course resource
      persons: Provided, That no content constituting the intellectual heritage or
      cultural property of IP communities shall be included in the Klima 101
      framework without the written consent of the relevant community and
      appropriate recognition and benefit-sharing arrangements as may be required
      under IPRA;​

          e.​ Climate and wellbeing, covering the mental health, food, health, and
      livelihood dimensions of climate vulnerability and the concept of adaptive
      wellbeing;​
          f.​ ﻿Civic and professional responsibility in the climate crisis, covering the
      roles of individuals, professionals, institutions, governments, and civil society
      in climate action, adaptation, and mitigation, with specific attention to the
      professional ethics of climate responsibility across disciplines; and​

          g.​ ﻿Applied climate action, developed through a community-based,
      field-based, or policy-advocacy project as required under Section 17 of this
      Act.

       SEC. 15. Faculty Qualifications and Development. – The CHED shall include in
the Memorandum Order issued pursuant to Section 13 the minimum qualifications
for faculty members authorized to teach Klima 101, which shall take into account the
interdisciplinary nature of the course and shall not be limited to faculty holding
degrees in a single discipline. The CHED shall, through its College Faculty
Development Fund, establish Klima 101 Faculty Development Grants for faculty
members in state universities and colleges to undertake training, research, and
content development in climate literacy and sustainability education. The Faculty
Development Grants shall prioritize faculty in SUCs located in climate-exposed and
geographically isolated regions, including those in Mindanao and the BARMM.

       SEC. 16. Phased Implementation. – The implementation of the Klima 101
requirement shall proceed in two phases. In the first phase, which shall commence
in Academic Year 2027-2028, all state universities and colleges shall be required to
offer Klima 101 as a required General Education component. In the second phase,
which shall commence in Academic Year 2028-2029, the Klima 101 requirement shall
apply to all HEIs in the Philippines, both public and private.

       The CHED shall issue the necessary implementing guidelines for each phase
not later than one (1) year before the commencement of each phase. HEIs are
encouraged, but not required, to begin offering Klima 101 on a pilot or voluntary
basis prior to the commencement of the first phase.

       SEC. 17. Assessment Requirements for Klima 101. – Klima 101 shall not be
assessed through lecture-examination format alone. The CHED Memorandum Order
shall require that each student’s performance in Klima 101 include an applied civic
action component, which may be satisfied through a community-based project, a
policy advocacy activity, a field-based investigation, or an equivalent undertaking as
prescribed in the Memorandum Order. Portfolio-based or project-based assessment
shall be the primary mode of final assessment in Klima 101. HEIs may develop their
own assessment instruments consistent with the minimum requirements established
in the Memorandum Order.

       SEC. 18. BARMM Higher Education. – The Ministry of Basic, Higher, and
Technical Education of the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao
shall, within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, adopt an
equivalent ministerial order establishing Klima 101 or its equivalent as a required
General Education component for all Bangsamoro HEIs operating under
MBHTE-BARMM jurisdiction, with minimum course outcomes and assessment
requirements consistent with those prescribed in the CHED Memorandum Order
issued pursuant to this Chapter.

                           CHAPTER IV
             INDIGENOUS PEOPLES AND EQUITY PROVISIONS

       SEC. 19. Free, Prior, and Informed Consent. – All climate education content
related to indigenous ecological knowledge, indigenous climate practices, ancestral
domain-based resource management, or any other knowledge, cultural expression,
or practice held by indigenous peoples and cultural communities shall be developed
under this Act only with the Free, Prior, and Informed Consent of the relevant IP
communities, as required under IPRA and the rules and regulations of the National
Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP).

      The NCIP shall serve as the coordinating body for all FPIC processes
undertaken in connection with the development of SLLCs, Klima 101 course content,
ALS Coordination Track modules, and any supplementary learning materials under
this Act that involve indigenous ecological knowledge. All content involving
indigenous knowledge shall be co-authored with, and shall expressly credit, IP
knowledge holders from the communities whose knowledge is represented.

      No central generation of content claiming to represent indigenous ecological
knowledge without the participation of the relevant communities shall be permitted.
The Inter-Agency Advisory Group shall include a protocol for FPIC compliance
review in its standard review cycle for the SLLCs.

       SEC. 20. IP Community-Based Climate Education. – Indigenous peoples
communities may organize and conduct community-based climate education
sessions as part of their own community learning and governance processes.
Learners from IP communities who complete community-based climate education
sessions that meet standards jointly developed by the BALS and the NCIP shall be
recognized as having fulfilled their ALS Coordination Track requirements under
Section 9 for purposes of ALS equivalency credit.

       The BALS and the NCIP shall issue the joint standards referred to in this
section within eighteen (18) months from the effectivity of this Act. Nothing in this
section shall be construed to subject community-based climate education activities of
IP communities to external curriculum approval requirements.

       SEC. 21. Equity Safeguards. – ALS learners who have completed the Klima-
integrated modules under the ALS Coordination Track shall be recognized as having
attained equivalent climate literacy competency for all purposes under this Act and
shall not be subject to any inferior treatment in any context in which climate literacy
credentials may be relevant.

       No learner shall be excluded from access to climate literacy education on the
basis of formal or non-formal enrollment status, geographic remoteness, disability,
or any other ground. DepEd and the CHED shall each develop and publish, within
twenty-four (24) months from the effectivity of this Act, strategies for reaching
learners in areas with limited access to schooling, including geographically isolated
and disadvantaged areas, conflict-affected areas, and areas with high dropout rates,
to ensure that the equity objectives of this Act are pursued in practice and not
merely declared in principle.

                               CHAPTER V
                       INTER-AGENCY COORDINATION

       SEC. 22. Klima Kurriculum Inter-Agency Advisory Group. – There is hereby
created a Klima Kurriculum Inter-Agency Advisory Group, hereinafter referred to as
the IAAG, which shall serve as the coordinating, advisory, and accountability body
for the implementation of this Act. The IAAG is created by this Act as a statutory
inter-agency body and its existence, mandate, and composition shall not be altered
or dissolved by executive order without an express act of Congress.

      The IAAG shall be composed of the following members, or their duly
authorized representatives of not lower than the rank of Undersecretary:

          a.​ The Secretary of Education, as Chairperson;

          b.​ The Chairperson of CHED;

        c.​ The Director-General of          the   Technical   Education   and   Skills
      Development Authority (TESDA);

          d.​ The Secretary of Environment and Natural Resources;

         e.​ The Administrator of the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and
      Astronomical Services Administration under the DOST;

          f.​ The Vice-Chairperson of the CCC;

          g.​ The Director-General of the NEAP;

          h.​ The Chairperson of the NCIP;

          i.​ The Secretary of Social Welfare and Development;

         j.​ The Minister of Basic, Higher, and Technical Education of the BARMM;
      and

          k.​ Two (2) CSO representatives, one with demonstrated expertise in
      climate science and advocacy, and one with demonstrated expertise in basic
      or higher education policy, to be appointed by the President upon the
      recommendation of the Chairperson of the IAAG from nominations
      submitted by accredited CSOs.
      The Secretariat of the IAAG shall be hosted by and funded within the
DepEd’s budget allocation under this Act. The IAAG shall convene at least once
every quarter.

       Failure to convene for two (2) consecutive quarters shall be reported by the
Secretariat to the House CBEC and the Senate CBE within fifteen (15) days of the
lapse of the second consecutive missed quarter.

        SEC. 23. Functions of the Inter-Agency Advisory Group. – The IAAG shall have
the following functions:

         a.​ To review and recommend updates to the SLLCs every three (3) years
      from the initial issuance of the SLLCs, to ensure that climate education
      content remains current, scientifically accurate, and responsive to evolving
      Philippine climate realities;​

         b.​ ﻿To coordinate with the CCC to align the substance and priorities of the
      Klima Kurriculum with the current National Climate Change Action Plan and
      with Philippine commitments under the United Nations Framework
      Convention on Climate Change and the Paris Agreement;​

         c.​ ﻿To receive and review the annual implementation reports submitted by
      the DepEd and the CHED under Chapter VII of this Act;​

         d.​ ﻿To consolidate such reports and submit a single Annual Consolidated
      Implementation Report to the House CBEC, the House Committee on Higher
      and Technical Education (CHTE), and the corresponding Senate Committees
      not later than June 30 of each year; and​

         e.​ ﻿To recommend climate literacy education budget priorities to the
      Department of Budget and Management (DBM) in connection with the
      preparation of the annual National Expenditure Program, including any
      recommendation for adjustments to the appropriation levels established
      under Chapter VI of this Act.

        SEC. 24. Coordination with the Climate Change Commission. – The Climate
Change Commission shall certify the climate science accuracy and currency of the
SLLCs developed by the TWG under Section 8 and the minimum course outcomes
for Klima 101 established in the CHED Memorandum Order under Sections 13 and
14. The CCC certification required under this section is a prerequisite to the
effectivity of the Department Order referred to in Section 8 and the CHED
Memorandum Order referred to in Section 13.

       The CCC shall act on requests for certification within forty-five (45) days of
receipt of the draft content submitted for certification. A failure to act within the
prescribed period shall be deemed a certification of no scientific objection, which
shall permit the issuing agency to proceed without prejudice to any specific
scientific comments subsequently submitted by the CCC. The CCC shall also review
and certify any updated SLLCs recommended by the IAAG under Section 23(a)
prior to their adoption.

        SEC. 25. Civil Society and Academic Participation. – Civil society organizations,
foundations, non-government organizations, and academic institutions with
expertise in climate education, environmental science, sustainability, or related fields
may serve as curriculum resource partners of the DepEd and the CHED in the
development, enrichment, localization, and contextualization of climate literacy
learning materials and Klima 101 course resources. Curriculum resource partners
shall not be vested with authority over official curriculum content, teacher
certification standards, or any other function reserved by law to the DepEd and the
CHED. The DepEd and the CHED each retain full and exclusive curricular authority
over their respective domains under this Act. Participation as a curriculum resource
partner shall be formalized through written agreements specifying the scope of
participation, intellectual property terms, and conditions consistent with this Act
and applicable procurement and transparency laws.

       Open-access digital curriculum resources developed by CSOs and hosted on
accredited educational platforms prior to the effectivity of this Act, which have been
validated for alignment with the SLLCs by the TWG under Section 8, shall be
recognized as primary curriculum resources for the implementation of this Act. No
appropriation under this Act shall be used to develop learning content that
duplicates validated open-access curriculum resources already available for the
relevant SLLCs.

                             CHAPTER VI
                 APPROPRIATIONS AND FISCAL PROVISIONS

       SEC. 26. Initial Appropriation. – The sum of One hundred million pesos (Php
100,000,000.00) is hereby appropriated from the unappropriated portion of the
national government funds for the initial implementation of this Act. The funds shall
be allocated as follows, with the specific breakdown to be detailed in Section 35 of
this Act:

           a.​ ﻿To the DepEd through the NEAP, for the design, development, and
       delivery of the mandatory in-service training program for Grade 4 and Grade
       7 teachers under Section 10 of this Act, including the development of training
       materials, trainer-of-trainers programs, and the deployment of training
       programs to all SDOs;​

          b.​ To the DepEd through the BALS, for the development of the
       Klima-integrated ALS modules under the ALS Coordination Track, the
       production and distribution of learning materials to Community Learning
       Centers, and the training of ALS facilitators;​

          c.​ ﻿To the CHED, for the establishment and administration of the Klima
       101 Faculty Development Grants for state university and college faculty
       under Section 15 of this Act, including grants for training, curriculum
      development, and research;​

          d.​ ﻿To the DepEd as Secretariat of the IAAG, for the organization,
      operational costs, and secretariat functions of the Klima Kurriculum IAAG
      established under Section 22 of this Act; and​

         e.​ ﻿To the PIDS for the baseline assessment study and the design of the
      student learning outcome monitoring and evaluation instruments required
      under Sections 12, 30, and 31 of this Act.

       The failure or delay in the release of funds appropriated under this section
shall not suspend, excuse, or otherwise affect the obligation of the DepEd to embed
the SLLCs under Sections 6 and 7 of this Act or the obligation of the CHED to issue
the Memorandum Order under Section 13. The curriculum integration mandate of
this Act is operative upon the effectivity of the relevant Department Order and
Memorandum Order and is independent of appropriation release.

       SEC. 27. Subsequent Appropriations. – The annual appropriations necessary to
sustain the full implementation of this Act after the initial appropriation shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act, upon the recommendation of
the implementing agencies and the DBM. The sum appropriated annually for the
implementation of this Act shall not fall below the initial appropriation of One
hundred million pesos (Php 100,000,000.00) without a specific act of Congress
expressly reducing such amount. The IAAG shall include budget recommendations
in its Annual Consolidated Implementation Report pursuant to Section 23(e) to
guide Congress in making annual appropriations consistent with actual
implementation needs.

      SEC. 28. Complementary Fiscal Resources. – The existing disaster preparedness
and climate resilience budget lines within the DepEd’s annual budget and the
resources available to the Philippines under the World Bank Philippines Disaster
Risk Management and Climate Development Program and any successor instrument
are hereby recognized as complementary fiscal resources for climate literacy
education consistent with this Act.

       The IAAG shall coordinate among implementing agencies to identify, map,
and rationalize the use of such complementary resources to prevent duplication of
expenditure and to maximize coverage. Implementing agencies shall report to the
IAAG on the use of complementary resources as part of their annual
implementation reports. Nothing in this section shall be construed to reduce the
obligation to appropriate dedicated funds under this Act.

                           CHAPTER VII
           MONITORING, EVALUATION, AND ACCOUNTABILITY

      SEC. 29. Annual Report. – The Secretary of Education and the Chairperson of
the CHED shall each submit a separate Annual Implementation Report to the House
CBEC, the House CHTE, and the corresponding Senate Committees not later than
March 31 of each year commencing in the year following the first full school year in
which SLLCs and Klima 101, respectively, are in operation. Each Annual
Implementation Report shall include, at minimum, the following data:

          a.​ ﻿The number and percentage of public and private schools and HEIs in
      full compliance with the requirements of this Act;​

         b.​ ﻿The number of teachers trained under the program established by
      Section 10, disaggregated by division, learning area, and grade level;​

         c.​ ﻿Student learning outcome data on SLLC competency attainment,
      disaggregated to the extent practicable by region, division, school type, and
      sex;​

         d.​ ﻿ALS enrollment and completion rates in the Klima-integrated modules
      under the ALS Coordination Track, disaggregated by region and learner type;
      and​

         e.​ ﻿Budget utilization data, including amounts obligated and disbursed
      from appropriations under this Act and from complementary resources
      identified under Section 28.

       SEC. 30. Student Learning Outcomes Focus. – The monitoring and evaluation of
this Act’s implementation shall be anchored primarily in actual student learning
outcomes and shall not be satisfied by administrative compliance documentation
alone. The BCD of the DepEd shall, in coordination with the PIDS, develop
outcome-based monitoring and evaluation instruments for the SLLCs within twelve
(12) months of the issuance of the Department Order under Section 8. These
instruments shall be capable of generating nationally comparable, division-level
disaggregated data on student attainment of SLLC competencies and shall be
integrated into existing assessment systems to the extent practicable. The CHED
shall develop equivalent outcome-based assessment frameworks for Klima 101
within twelve (12) months of the issuance of the Memorandum Order under Section
13.

        SEC. 31. Independent Evaluation. – The PIDS shall conduct an independent
evaluation of the implementation of the Klima Kurriculum Act, covering both the
basic education and higher education components, no later than three (3) years after
the full rollout of Klima 101 under Section 16. The evaluation shall assess, among
other matters: the degree of actual integration of SLLCs into classroom instruction;
the measurable improvement, if any, in student climate literacy outcomes; the equity
of implementation across regions, income groups, and learner categories; the
effectiveness of the ALS Coordination Track; and the quality and consistency of
Klima 101 delivery across HEIs. The PIDS evaluation findings shall be submitted to
Congress and shall be published in full. The results of the PIDS evaluation shall
inform the first major review of the SLLCs by the IAAG and shall be used as basis
for any legislative amendment to this Act as Congress may deem appropriate.
       SEC. 32. Compatibility with EDCOM II Framework. – The implementing rules,
Department Orders, Memorandum Orders, and all agency actions pursuant to this
Act shall ensure that the basic education provisions of this Act are implemented
exclusively through integration into existing MATATAG Curriculum Guides and
shall not create new standalone subjects at the Grade 4 or Grade 7 level, either
directly or through any restructuring of learning areas.

       This provision expressly acknowledges and is consistent with the curriculum
decongestion mandate confirmed by the EDCOM II, and shall be interpreted in a
manner that reinforces, and does not undermine, the goals of curriculum
decongestion, teacher workload reduction, and learner-centered education reform
that EDCOM II has established as national education priorities.

                              CHAPTER VIII
                   PENAL AND ENFORCEMENT PROVISIONS

        SEC. 33. Non-Compliance by Educational Institutions. – Public and private basic
education schools that fail to implement the SLLCs in the prescribed learning areas
after the expiration of the prescribed implementation period shall be subject to
administrative sanctions by the DepEd, to be administered through the relevant
Schools Division Office. Such sanctions shall be proportional and corrective in
nature and shall include, at minimum, a written notice of non-compliance, a
mandatory corrective action plan, and technical assistance from the Schools Division
Office.

       Repeated or willful non-compliance may be referred to the Office of the
Secretary for appropriate action, including sanctions applicable to private schools
under the relevant provisions of RA No. 10533 and the Manual of Regulations for
Private Schools. Private higher education institutions that fail to offer Klima 101 as a
required General Education component after the commencement of the second
phase of implementation in Academic Year 2028-2029 shall have this failure noted in
the CHED accreditation and recognition proceedings applicable to such institution,
and the Commission may impose such additional sanctions as are within its
authority under RA No. 7722.

      SEC. 34. Misuse of Funds. – Any official, officer, employee, or other person
who misuses, misappropriates, converts, or otherwise unlawfully disposes of any
funds appropriated or disbursed under this Act shall be subject to the applicable
penal and administrative provisions of RA No. 3019, entitled the “Anti-Graft and
Corrupt Practices Act”; the malversation of public funds provisions of Act No. 3815,
otherwise known as the “Revised Penal Code”, and such other applicable laws as may
govern the conduct of public officers.

      The Commission on Audit (COA) shall exercise full audit jurisdiction over all
funds appropriated under this Act, and all implementing agencies shall submit to
the audit procedures and reporting requirements of the COA. The Office of the
Ombudsman shall have full jurisdiction over complaints arising from the misuse of
funds appropriated under this Act.
                                   CHAPTER IX
                                FINAL PROVISIONS

        SEC. 35. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Secretary of Education and
the Chairperson of CHED, in coordination with the CCC, TESDA, and the NCIP,
shall jointly promulgate the Implementing Rules and Regulations of this Act within
ninety (90) days from its effectivity. The IAAG shall be consulted in the development
of the IRR and its written comments shall be addressed in the final version. The IRR
shall include, among other matters, the specific budget allocation formula for the
initial appropriation under Section 26; the standards and procedures for FPIC
compliance in content development; the criteria for designation of priority
localization areas under Section 11; the terms of reference for the TWG under Section
8; the procedures for CCC certification under Section 24; and the standards for IP
community-based climate education recognized for ALS equivalency credit under
Section 20. In case of conflict between the IRR and the text of this Act, the text of this
Act shall prevail.

       SEC. 36. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

        SEC. 37. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly: Provided, That the provisions of RA No.
9512, otherwise known as the “National Environmental Awareness and Education Act of
2008” and the provisions of Section 14 of RA No. 10121, otherwise known as the
“Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010” pertaining to the
integration of disaster risk reduction and management into the school curriculum
are expressly preserved and shall continue in full force and effect and shall be
treated as complementary to and not superseded or diminished by this Act: Provided,
further, That where any provision of this Act and any provision of RA No. 9512 or
Section 14 of RA No. 10121 address the same subject matter. They shall be construed
together and applied harmoniously to the maximum extent possible, and this Act
shall be deemed to supplement and strengthen, not replace, the earlier enactments.

      SEC. 38. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
