                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Traffic congestion in Metro Manila causes the country an estimated Php3.5
billion in economic losses daily.1 According to the Japan International Cooperation
Agency (JICA), as traffic congestion continues to worsen, without an effective
intervention, the associated costs are likely to increase to Php6 billion by 2030. 2
Among the primary drivers of traffic congestion is the country’s poor public transport
system, which remains fragmented, unreliable, and underfunded.3

        As such, many Filipino commuters have explored an alternative mode of
transportation known as motorcycle taxis, similar to habal-habal in rural areas.
Compared to traditional modes of public transportation, such as jeepneys, buses, and
trains, which either require passengers to wait in long lines, sit through the traffic
congestion, or both, motorcycles are more efficient, convenient, and cost-effective.4

      As more Filipino commuters rely on motorcycle taxis for their daily
transportation, and more bikers depend on this mode of transportation for their daily
income, the government has to step in and provide a more responsive and robust

       This bill seeks to recognize motorcycles as legitimate public utility vehicles
(PUVs). Motorcycles-for-hire shall be placed under the regulatory authority of the Land
Transportation Office (LTO), the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory
Board (LTFRB), and concerned local government units (LGUs). This will ensure the
motorcycle’s roadworthiness, promote the safety and protection of passengers and
other road users, and guarantee the biker’s compliance with the necessary training
and licensing standards, with due regard to fair pricing schemes and reasonable
operating rules.

 1           SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Motorcycle-for-Hire
 2   Act.”
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the importance of
 5   transportation in economic development. The recent innovations in technology have
 6   allowed the riding public access to alternative modes of public conveyance, such as
 7   motorcycles-for-hire, which have emerged as a reliable and accessible mode of
 8   transportation. However, despite its promise of convenience and fast service,
 9   motorcycles-for-hire must be regulated to ensure that the interests of the general
10   public are protected. Thus, the State shall allow the regulated operation of
11   motorcycles-for-hire to provide the public with an alternative mode of public
12   transportation that is safe, secure, and economical. Towards this end, the State shall
13   establish mechanisms and guidelines for regulating all operational aspects of
14   motorcycles-for-hire.
15
16           SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
17
18           (a) Biker refers to the driver of a motorcycle-for-hire;
19
20           (b) Certificate of Public Convenience or CPC refers to the authority issued by
21   the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) to Motorcycle
22   Taxi Operators (MTO) so that they may authorize bikers to operate motorcycles-for-
23   hire or bikers to operate motorcycles-for-hire;

                                                  1
 1         (c) Customer or user refers to a natural person requesting the services of a
 2   motorcycle-for-hire on a digital platform or through other means;
 3
 4         (d) Digital platform refers to an information and communications technology-
 5   enabled mechanism that connects and integrates providers and users in online
 6   environments where services are requested, developed, and sold, and data is
 7   generated and exchanged;
 8
 9          (e) Motorcycle refers to any two (2)-wheeled motor vehicle without appendages;
10
11         (f) Motorcycle Taxi Operator or MTO refers to any natural or juridical person
12   that makes available pre-arranged transportation services of motorcycles-for-hire,
13   whether on a digital platform or through other means, and connects its customers or
14   users with bikers to facilitate the consummation of a contract of carriage for
15   compensation;
16
17            (g) Motorcycle-for-hire refers to a motorcycle, duly registered as such under this
18   Act, used in the provision of services offered to the public for the carriage of
19   passengers on a for-hire basis. A motorcycle-for-hire shall be considered a public
20   utility vehicle and its operation a public utility;
21
22          (h) Motorcycle-for-hire Operator's Permit or MOP refers to the authority issued
23   to bikers by LGUs so that they may operate a motorcycle-for-hire;
24
25         (i) Multi-homing refers to an approach where a particular biker may be
26   accredited by more than one MTO, allowing such biker to utilize the services of more
27   than one MTO in the provision of transportation services; and
28
29          (j) Passenger refers to a natural person being transported from point to point by
30   a biker using a motorcycle-for-hire.
31
32          SEC. 4. Scope and Application. – This Act shall apply to all aspects of the
33   operation of motorcycles used as a common carrier for the transportation of
34   passengers.
35
36          SEC. 5. Recognition of Motorcycles-for-Hire as a Viable Mode of Public
37   Transportation. – Motorcycles-for-hire shall be recognized as a viable mode of public
38   transportation and, for this purpose, shall be included as a mode of transport in the
39   Local Public Transport Route Plan (LPTRP) to be developed by LGUs and approved
40   by the DOTr and LTFRB, as well as in separate manuals or guidelines issued by LGUs
41   on any public transportation plans concerning any province, city, or municipality based
42   on local mobility requirements.

 4          SEC. 6. Motorcycles-For-Hire as Common Carriers. – The operation of
 5   motorcycles-for-hire is imbued with public interest. As such, the same shall be
 6   governed by the applicable provisions of Republic Act No. 386, otherwise known as
 7   the "Civil Code of the Philippines," and other pertinent laws, rules, and regulations on
 8   common carriers, particularly on the required degree of diligence to be observed by
 9   bikers in the course of transporting passengers and on the presumption of culpability
10   in case of death or injuries to passengers.
11
12          SEC. 7. Registration for Motorcycles-For-Hire. – A motorcycle intended to be
13   used as a motorcycle-for-hire must be duly registered with the Land Transportation
14   Office (LTO) in accordance with the requirements set forth in Section 11 of this Act.
15   The LTO shall ensure the roadworthiness of all motorcycles-for-hire before their
16   registration, or any renewal thereof.
17
18         SEC. 8. Standards and Specifications of Motorcycles-For-Hire. – The DOTr,
19   upon the recommendation of the LTO, shall prescribe standards and specifications of
20   motorcycles that may be allowed to operate as motorcycles-for-hire, taking into
21   consideration the safety of the passengers and bikers.
22
23           No modifications shall be made on any motorcycle-for-hire except for the
24   installation of appropriate accessories, such as a step board, footrest, speed limiter,
25   or monitoring devices, in accordance with the standards and specifications to be
26   prescribed by the DOTr.
27
28          SEC. 9. Accreditation Requirements for MTOs. – An individual or juridical entity
29   duly registered with the appropriate government agencies may be accredited by the
30   LTFRB to operate as an MTO.
31
32          MTOs must comply with the nationality requirements under applicable laws:
33   Provided, That, where permitted, foreign nationals shall not be allowed to directly or
34   indirectly own more than forty percent (40%) of the capital of any MTOs unless the
35   country of such foreign nationals accords reciprocity to Philippine nationals as may be
36   provided by foreign law, treaty, or international agreement.
37
38          The accreditation shall be valid for five (5) years and may be renewed:
39   Provided, That an MTO applying for renewal shall have maintained an average safety
40   rating of at least ninety-five percent (95%) as certified by an independent auditor for
41   the validity period of its previous accreditation.

20             SEC. 18. Liabilities for Death, Injuries, or Damage to Property. – The biker and
21   the MTO that facilitated the transportation service at the time when the death, injury,
22   or damage to persons or property occurred in the course of the operation of a
23   motorcycle-for-hire shall be jointly and solidarily liable for civil liabilities arising from
24   such death, injury, or damage, with rights of subrogation against any party at fault:
25   Provided, That the liability of the MTO shall not exceed the amount of the insurance
26   coverage of the biker: Provided further, That the following provision on civil liability is
27   without prejudice to the imposition of appropriate criminal and/or administrative
28   liabilities on the driver at fault.

30          SEC. 19. Accident Quick Response Team. – MTOs shall establish and maintain

34   a person injured by the operation of a motorcycle-for-hire is prevented from receiving
35   medical care due to financial constraints, the MTO shall advance a reasonable amount
36   necessary for the provision of such medical care and, if necessary, the admission of
37   such injured person to a hospital or medical facility up to the maximum amount of the
38   insurance coverage of the biker.

 4          SEC. 23. Protection Against Anti-Competitive Conduct. – The LTFRB shall
 5   protect MTOs, bikers, and the riding public from monopolistic conduct, anti-competitive
 6   conduct, and unfair trade practices, and shall exercise all powers and functions
 7   consistent with this Act, to ensure such protection.
 8
 9          (a) The following acts by MTOs shall be prohibited under this Act:
10
11             i.    Consummating a merger or acquisition that will give rise to a change in
12                   control of the MTO without securing the prior endorsement of the DOTr.
13                   This requirement shall be separate from and in addition to the
14                   notification requirements under Republic Act No. 10667, otherwise
15                   known as the "Philippine Competition Act."
16
17            ii.    Engaging in any anti-competitive conduct that has the object or effect of
18                   substantially preventing, restricting, or lessening competition in the
19                   market for motorcycle-for-hire services.
20
21            iii.   Leveraging network effects in a separate market to gain undue
22                   advantage or dominance in the market for motorcycle-for-hire services.
23                   Network effects arise when the value of a product or service increases
24                   the more people use such product or service. In markets that depend
25                   on two or more user groups, network effects occur when the number of
26                   users in one group affects the value derived by users of the other group
27                   from the product or service.
28
29           iv.     Using dominance in a separate market to gain advantage in the market
30                   for motorcycle-for-hire services. There is a presumption of dominance if
31                   the market share of an MTO in the separate market is at least fifty
32                   percent (50%).

10           SEC. 25. Penalties. – The LTFRB may impose a fine of not less than One
11   million Philippine Pesos (PHP1,000,000.00) but not more than Ten million Philippine
12   Pesos (PHP10,000,000.00) upon any MTO that has committed any of the prohibited
13   acts under Section 24 of this Act. A biker found liable for any of the prohibited acts
14   under Section 24 of this Act shall be blacklisted from operating motorcycles-for-hire.
15
16          In addition to the foregoing penalties, any biker who operates a motorcycle-for-
17   hire in violation of traffic rules and regulations, carrying capacity, and licensing
18   requirements shall suffer the appropriate penalty imposed under the applicable law,
19   ordinance, rule, or regulation for such violation. The LTO shall impose the appropriate
20   penalty on an erring biker commensurate with the gravity of the offense and frequency
21   of commission.
22
23          SEC. 26. Taxation of MTOs. – The gross remittances of MTOs to bikers for
24   services sold through their digital platforms or through other means used to connect
25   such bikers with customers or users shall not form part of the taxable income of the
26   MTO and shall not be subject to income tax or withholding tax on the part of the MTO.
27   The term gross remittance shall refer to the total amount of the value of the services
28   of a biker net of any commissions or service fees due to and deducted by the MTO
29   that is collected by the MTO on behalf of such biker, regardless of whether the
30   payment for the services is in cash or made through electronic payment channels, and
31   remitted by the MTO to the biker. An MTO shall be liable for value-added tax only for
32   its commissions or service fees.
33
34           SEC. 27. Incentives. – The following incentives shall be made available to
35   MTOs:
36
37          (a) Income Tax Holiday. – In order to promote the expansion of transportation
38   services, pioneer MTOs shall be granted an exemption from payment of income tax
39   for a period of five (5) consecutive years from the effectivity of this Act, and non-
40   pioneer MTOs shall be granted an exemption from payment of income tax for a period
41   of three (3) years from the date of their registration. A pioneer MTO refers to operators
42   who were accredited under the pilot program for motorcycle taxis before 2020. The
43   period for exemption may be extended for an additional period of two (2) years,

 4          (b) Zero-Rated Value-Added Tax on Services. – Services provided by
 5   registered MTOs shall be subject to zero percent (0%) value-added tax.
 6
 7         (c) Regional Development Incentives. – MTOs that establish operations in rural

13
14          SEC. 28. Income Tax Rates for Bikers. – For a period of two (2) consecutive
15   years from the effectivity of this Act, bikers shall enjoy a preferential tax rate of five
16   percent (5%) on their gross income in excess of Three Hundred Fifty Thousand Pesos
17   (PhP350,000.00) derived from their operations as a motorcycle-for-hire: Provided,
18   That their gross income does not exceed the value-added tax threshold provided in
19   Section 109(BB) of Republic Act No. 8424, otherwise known as the "National Internal
20   Revenue Code of 1997," as amended. Upon the expiration of the two (2)-year period,
21   bikers shall be subject to the income tax rates set out in Section 24 of the National
22   Internal Revenue Code of 1997.
23
24          SEC. 29. Exemption of Technology Professionals from Income Tax. – To
25   incentivize recruitment and retention of skilled technology professionals in recognition
26   of the importance of fostering growth and innovation in the technology sector and in
27   support of technology entrepreneurship in the country, technology professionals,
28   including but not limited to software developers, engineers, and IT specialists, shall be
29   exempt from the payment of income tax for a period of five (5) consecutive years from
30   the effectivity of this Act. This exemption shall apply only to compensation income and
31   shall not extend to other sources of income.
32
33          In order to qualify for the exemption, the technology professional must be a
34   regular full-time employee and hired to work in a position directly related to technology
35   or essential digital platform operations. MTOs employing technology professionals
36   benefitting from the exemption shall be required to report the employment status and
37   aggregate income information of their technology professionals to the BIR on an
38   annual basis. Any changes in employment status or employing MTO must also be
39   reported by the MTO and technology professionals to the BIR.
40
41          SEC. 30. Appropriations. – The amount needed for the initial implementation of
42   this Act shall be taken from the current year's appropriation of the LTFRB. Thereafter,
43   such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in
44   the annual General Appropriations Act.

 3   full implementation of this Act, operators that are accredited under the pilot program
