                                             EXP L A N A T O R Y                    NOTE

The 1987 Philippine Constitution serves as the primary guardian of human dignity and labor
rights. Under Article II, Section 11, the State is mandated to value the dignity of every human
person and guarantee full respect for human rights. This is further reinforced by Section 18, which
affirms labor as a "primary social economic force" and directs the State to protect the rights of
workers and promote their welfare. Furthermore, Article XIII, Section 3 explicitly commands the
State to afford full protection to labor, local and overseas, and guarantee humane conditions of
work. Despite these robust constitutional safeguards, a significant segment of the Filipino
workforce remains trapped in conditions that mirror modern-day slavery.1

    Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
    assembled:

1   SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Prevention and Prosecution of Forced
2   Labor Act of 2026."

3   - SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to afford full protection to labor,
4   promote full employment, and ensure equality of employment opportunities. Recognizing that
5   forced labor is a profound affront to human dignity and a violation of the 1987 Constitution and
6   the ILO Forced Labour Convention (No. 29), the State shall ensure that no person is compelled to
7   work under the menace of any penalty or without their free, prior, and informed consent. The State
8   further recognizes that forced labor may exist independently of the elements of "recruitment" or
9   "transportation" required in trafficking cases.
