                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) affects many Filipino individuals and
families who continue to face significant challenges in accessing timely
diagnosis, intervention, therapy, education, healthcare, and community support
services. Limited public awareness, shortage of trained specialists, financial
barriers, and unequal access to developmental services—particularly in
underserved areas—often result in delayed intervention and reduced
opportunities for persons on the autism spectrum.

      This bill seeks to establish a comprehensive and inclusive national
framework for autism care and support through the creation of regional Autism
Centers, early detection and intervention programs, inclusive education support,
professional training, research initiatives, and accessible community-based
services. The measure likewise strengthens coordination among healthcare,
education, and social welfare institutions to ensure continuity of care and long-
term support for individuals with autism and their families.

      Consistent with the Magna Carta for Persons with Disability, the Mental
Health Act, and the Universal Health Care Act, this measure promotes a rights-
based, evidence-informed, and family-centered approach to autism care while
  encouraging inclusion, accessibility, dignity, and equal opportunity for persons
  on the autism spectrum.

       By improving access to developmental services, strengthening public
  awareness, and supporting families and communities, this measure seeks to
  enhance the quality of life and full participation of persons with autism in society.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Autism
Care and Support Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the rights, dignity, inclusion, and well-being of persons with
disabilities, including persons on the autism spectrum, consistent with the
Constitution, Republic Act No. 7277 or the “Magna Carta for Persons with
Disability,” Republic Act No. 11036 or the “Mental Health Act,” Republic Act
No. 11223 or the “Universal Health Care Act,” and other relevant laws.
The State shall adopt a comprehensive, inclusive, family-centered, community-
based, and evidence-informed approach toward autism care through accessible
screening, early detection, intervention, healthcare, education, rehabilitation,
employment support, and social inclusion programs for individuals with Autism
Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and their families.


SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
hereby defined:
   a) “Autism     Spectrum      Disorder     (ASD)”    refers    to    a    range   of
      neurodevelopmental        conditions    characterized     by    differences   in
      communication, social interaction, sensory processing, and patterns of
      behavior, with varying levels of support needs.
   b) “Autism Centers” refer to specialized facilities or designated units within
      healthcare institutions that provide developmental screening, diagnostic
      assessment,    therapy,     rehabilitation,   family    support,     educational
      coordination, and community-based services for persons with ASD.
   c) “Early Detection and Intervention Program” refers to a coordinated system
      of developmental screening, assessment, referral, and evidence-informed
      intervention services designed to identify and support children with
      developmental concerns at the earliest possible stage.
   d) “Persons on the Autism Spectrum” refer to individuals diagnosed with
      Autism Spectrum Disorder or related developmental conditions requiring
      support services.
   e) “Individualized Education Plan (IEP)” refers to a learner-centered
      educational support plan designed to address the specific developmental,
      behavioral, communication, and learning needs of a learner with ASD.

SEC. 4. Establishment of Regional Autism Centers. – The Department of Health
(DOH), in coordination with local government units (LGUs), government
hospitals, state universities and colleges (SUCs), and accredited non-government
organizations (NGOs), shall establish or designate at least one (1) Autism Center
in every region.


Priority shall be given to underserved and geographically disadvantaged areas.


The Autism Centers shall provide accessible and affordable services including:
   a) Developmental screening, diagnostic assessment, and referral services;
   b) Occupational therapy, speech therapy, behavioral therapy, physical
      therapy, psychosocial support, and related interventions;
   c) Parent, caregiver, and family counseling and training;
   d) Educational assessment and Individualized Education Plan (IEP) support
      in coordination with schools and the Department of Education (DepEd);
   e) Community-based rehabilitation and support services;
   f) Referral and coordination with healthcare providers, social welfare
      agencies, educational institutions, and employment support programs.


The DOH may accredit existing qualified facilities as Autism Centers subject to
standards established under this Act.


SEC. 5. National Early Detection and Intervention Program. – The Department
of Health, in coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd),
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), local government
units, and other relevant agencies, shall establish a nationwide Early Detection
and Intervention Program for Autism Spectrum Disorder.



                                                                                 3
The Program shall:
   a) Integrate developmental screening and autism risk assessment into existing
      maternal, child health, and immunization programs in public healthcare
      facilities;
   b) Train barangay health workers, nurses, midwives, pediatricians, teachers,
      daycare workers, and community healthcare personnel to identify
      developmental delays and autism-related indicators;
   c) Establish     clear   referral pathways for children requiring further
      developmental assessment and intervention;
   d) Ensure timely access to evidence-informed interventions and support
      services;
   e) Conduct nationwide information, education, and anti-stigma campaigns on
      autism awareness and inclusion.


Developmental screening under this Act shall respect parental participation,
informed consent, and child rights standards.


SEC. 6. Inclusive Education and Community Support. – The Department of
Education (DepEd), in coordination with the DOH, CHED, TESDA, and LGUs,
shall strengthen inclusive education and community support systems for learners
and individuals on the autism spectrum by:
   a) Expanding access to Special Education (SPED) and inclusive education
      programs;
   b) Providing training for teachers, guidance counselors, and school personnel
      on autism inclusion, classroom accommodations, and learner support
      strategies;
   c) Developing inclusive learning materials and reasonable accommodation
      guidelines;

                                                                               4
   d) Supporting transition programs for adolescents and young adults with ASD
      toward higher education, skills training, employment, and independent
      living;
   e) Promoting autism-friendly schools, workplaces, and public spaces.


LGUs and communities are encouraged to establish support networks and
inclusive community programs for persons with ASD and their families.


SEC. 7. Capacity Building and Workforce Development. The DOH, PRC,
CHED, TESDA, and relevant professional organizations shall:
   a) Develop and strengthen training, certification, fellowship, and continuing
      professional education programs related to autism care and developmental
      services;
   b) Encourage the expansion of developmental and behavioral healthcare
      specialization programs;
   c) Provide scholarships, grants, incentives, or return-service programs for
      healthcare workers, therapists, educators, and specialists        serving
      underserved areas;
   d) Promote interdisciplinary and community-based approaches to autism care
      and support.


SEC. 8. Research, Data Management, and Policy Development. – The DOH, in
coordination with the Philippine Council for Health Research and Development
(PCHRD), academic institutions, and relevant agencies, shall:
   a) Establish and maintain a national autism database and information system
      consistent with Republic Act No. 10173 or the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”;
   b) Support research on autism prevalence, intervention outcomes, inclusive
      education, mental health, employment, and family support systems;

                                                                               5
   c) Monitor and evaluate the effectiveness, accessibility, and regional
      coverage of autism-related services and programs;
   d) Develop evidence-informed policies and recommendations for autism
      inclusion and support.


Personal information collected under this Act shall be protected and processed in
accordance with applicable data privacy and child protection laws.


SEC. 9. Financial Accessibility and Health Coverage. – The DOH and
PhilHealth shall endeavor to improve access to affordable developmental,
therapeutic, rehabilitative, and mental health services for persons on the autism
spectrum consistent with existing laws and available resources.


The government shall encourage the inclusion and expansion of appropriate
autism-related benefit packages under PhilHealth and other public healthcare
financing mechanisms.


Indigent and financially vulnerable families shall be prioritized for subsidized
services and assistance programs.


SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual appropriations of the Department of Health
and participating agencies under the General Appropriations Act.


Local government units may allocate counterpart funding, facilities, personnel,
equipment, and logistical support for the implementation of this Act.




                                                                                6
The Program may also receive grants, donations, and development assistance
from lawful domestic or international sources subject to existing laws, rules, and
regulations.


SEC. 11. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Accountability. – The DOH shall
establish a monitoring and evaluation framework for the implementation of this
Act and shall submit annual reports to Congress and the Office of the President
containing:
   a) Number and distribution of Autism Centers established or accredited;
   b) Coverage of developmental screening and intervention services;
   c) Availability of trained professionals and support personnel;
   d) Budget utilization and program outcomes;
   e) Challenges, recommendations, and policy updates.


Periodic independent program reviews may likewise be conducted.


SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with DepEd, DSWD, CHED,
TESDA,     PRC,    PhilHealth,     autism   advocacy   organizations,   healthcare
professionals, educators, and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the
necessary implementing rules and regulations.


SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or

SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
