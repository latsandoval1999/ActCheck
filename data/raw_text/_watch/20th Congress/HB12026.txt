                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

     “Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it.”
                                                     - Psalm 127:1


      The 1987 Constitution places the family at the center of national life. Article II,
Section 12 declares that the State recognizes the sanctity of family life and shall
protect and strengthen the family as a basic autonomous social institution, and that it
shall equally protect the life of the mother and the life of the unborn from conception.
Article XV, Section 1 recognizes the Filipino family as the foundation of the nation and
directs the State to strengthen its solidarity and actively promote its total development.
These are not mere declarations of sentiment. They are standing commands that
should shape how the State legislates, plans, budgets and delivers services.

      Yet in practice, the family is often treated as an incidental beneficiary of public
policy rather than as a deliberate point of reference. Laws and programs are commonly
designed around individuals, sectors or agencies. Their combined effects on the
household, whether they strengthen or strain the bonds between spouses, parents
and children, and among generations living under one roof, are seldom examined as
a matter of course. When the family is not consciously considered, its most vulnerable
members are the first to be left behind: the unborn and mothers facing crisis
pregnancies, children and youth, solo parents, persons with disabilities, and the elderly.

       Family mainstreaming means consciously integrating the well-being of the
Filipino family into the design, implementation and evaluation of laws, policies and
programs across government. The bill anchors this observance on a clear declaration
of State policy. It affirms the sanctity of family life and the State’s duty to protect it as
the foundation of the nation. It commits the State to equally protect the life of the
mother and of the unborn from conception, with particular attention to at-risk and crisis
pregnancies, so that both lives are safeguarded. It extends protection to every family
and household member across the life cycle, from the unborn to the elderly, against
abuse, neglect, exploitation, violence and discrimination. It upholds the natural and
primary right and duty of parents in rearing the youth for civic efficiency, moral

character and spiritual development, while supporting spouses and parents in building
healthy, stable and resilient marriages and homes.

      This bill seeks to declare the month of September of every year as the National
Family Mainstreaming Month. It chooses the month of September to complement the
annual of celebration of National Family Week (every last week of September) by
virtue of Proclamation No. 60 (s. 1992), and Kainang Pamilya Mahalaga Day which is
established under Proclamation No. 326 (s. 2012).

      The bill further declares the policy of institutionalizing family mainstreaming
across all branches of government and local government units through conscious
implementation, rigorous monitoring and annual Family Impact Assessments of
existing laws and programs. It also calls for a holistic, evidence-based Bio-Psycho-
Social-Spiritual approach that engages the whole nation. The private sector, non-
governmental organizations, civil society organizations and faith-based organizations
already serve Filipino families in countless communities, and they are recognized as
partners of the State in this work.

       The annual observance is intended to give government, at the national and local
levels, a fixed occasion each year to examine how its laws and programs affect Filipino
families, to account for their impact, and to renew its commitment to the household as
the first school of values and the first line of social protection. It is also intended to rally
families themselves, together with schools, churches, communities and the private
sector, around a shared national commitment to strengthen the institution on which
the nation is built. By giving the constitutional mandate on the family a visible and
recurring place in the national calendar, this measure helps ensure that the family is
no longer an afterthought in public policy but a conscious priority.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
DECLARING EVERY MONTH OF SEPTEMBER OF EACH YEAR AS THE
NATIONAL FAMILY MAINSTREAMING MONTH


    Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

    SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National
Mainstreaming Month Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to:
      (a) Recognize the sanctity of family life, cherish and protect the family as a basic
autonomous social institution and the foundation of the nation, strengthen its solidarity,
and actively promote its total development pursuant to Article II, Section 12 and Article
XV, Sections 1 to 3 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution;

        (b) Equally protect the life of the mother and the life of the unborn from the
moment of conception or fertilization, affirming that the unborn child is an integral
member of the Filipino family entitled to full legal protection, care, and support, and
intentionally securing measures that safeguard the quality of life of both mothers and
the unborn—especially in at-risk and crisis pregnancies—to prevent abortion and its
fatal consequences;

       (c) Safeguard the rights, dignity, and well-being of all family and household
members across the entire life cycle—specifically fathers, mothers, the unborn,
children, youth, solo parents, persons with disabilities, and the elderly up to the natural
end of life—ensuring that no vulnerable member is left behind or subjected to abuse,
neglect, exploitation, violence, or discrimination;

       (d) Uphold the natural and primary right and duty of parents in the rearing of the
youth for civic efficiency, moral character, and spiritual development, while equipping

                                            3
parents and spouses to build healthy, stable, and resilient marriages and households;

      (e) Institutionalize family mainstreaming across all branches of government and
local government units (LGUs) through conscious implementation, rigorous
monitoring, and annual Family Impact Assessments (FIA) of existing laws and
programs; and

      (f) Adopt a holistic, evidence-based Bio-Psycho-Social-Spiritual (BPSS) and
whole-of-nation approach by actively engaging the private sector, non-governmental
organizations (NGOs), civil society organizations (CSOs), and faith-based
organizations (FBOs) in strengthening Filipino families and households.

     SEC. 3. National Family Mainstreaming Month. – Every month of September
of each year is hereby declared as the National Family Mainstreaming Month.

      For this purpose, all heads of government agencies and instrumentalities,
including government-owned and -controlled corporations as well as local government
units and employers in the private sector, are hereby mandated to conduct activities
designed to strengthen Filipino families and reinforce the mainstreaming of family as
the focus of government policies, programs and projects.

     The Office of the President is hereby designated as the lead agency for the
implementation of this Act.

     SEC. 4. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
