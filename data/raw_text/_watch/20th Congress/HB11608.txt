                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Motorcycle delivery riders and drivers play a vital role in the Philippines’
transportation, delivery, and digital-economy sectors. Through their daily work,
they transport passengers and deliver food, medicine, documents, and other
essential goods to individuals, households, and businesses throughout the
country. Their services help sustain commerce, support local businesses,
improve access to essential products, and provide convenience to the public.

       Despite their important contribution, many riders and drivers continue
to face difficult and uncertain working conditions. They may also be exposed to
road accidents, harassment, discrimination, and other forms of abuse. Many
also lack sufficient access to social protection, health and accident insurance,
emergency assistance, and other employment-related benefits.

      These conditions demonstrate the need for a clear legal framework that
recognizes the essential role of motorcycle delivery riders and drivers and
protects them from exploitation and unfair treatment. This bill seeks to
establish their rights in fulfillment of the State’s constitutional duty to protect
labor, promote social justice, and ensure decent and humane working
conditions.

      In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF MOTORCYCLE TAXI
    RIDERS AND DELIVERY RIDERS, PROMOTING THEIR RIGHTS AND
               WELFARE AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta of
Delivery Riders and Drivers Act.”

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to afford full
protection to labor, promote decent work, guarantee equal protection of the law,
and ensure humane conditions of work. The State recognizes motorcycle
delivery Riders and Drivers as essential workers.

Section 3. Objectives. — This Act seeks to protect motorcycle delivery riders
and drivers by ensuring fair pay, safe working conditions, access to labor and
social-protection benefits, and protection from exploitation, discrimination,
harassment, unlawful deductions, and retaliation. It also sets rules for
suspension, termination, and deactivation; promotes road and occupational
safety; and provides riders with effective legal and administrative remedies.

Section 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

      a. Motorcycle Delivery Rider and Drivers refers to any individual who
         uses a motorcycle or similar powered two-wheeled vehicle to deliver
         goods or passengers for compensation.
      b. Delivery Service Provider refers to any corporation, partnership,
         cooperative, agency, or entity that directly engages riders and
         operates a delivery business.

Section 5. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to all Motorcycle Delivery Riders
and Drivers operating in the Philippines, whether the delivery service involves
transport of passengers and delivery of goods. Further, this Act shall apply
regardless of whether the rider is designated as an employee, independent
contractor, partner, franchisee, service provider, or by any other contractual
description.
Section 6. Rights of Motorcycle Delivery Riders and Drivers. — Every
Motorcycle Delivery Riders and Drivers covered by this Act shall have the
following rights:

   1. the right to fair, timely, and transparent compensation;

   2. the right to safe and healthful working conditions;

   3. the right to refuse an unsafe, unlawful, or dangerous delivery assignment
      without retaliation;

   4. the right to reasonable        protection   against   road   accidents    and
      occupational injury;

   5. the right to mandatory coverage on social protection programs, including
      SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG coverage where legally applicable;

   6. the right to security of tenure where an employer-employee relationship
      exists;

   7. the right to due process before suspension, termination, or deactivation;

   8. the right to organize, form, join, or assist a union or riders’ association;

   9. the right to privacy and protection of personal data;

   10.     the right to access records relating to compensation, ratings,
      complaints, and disciplinary action;

   11.       the right to be free from discrimination, harassment, violence, and
      retaliation;

Section 7. Employment Contracts. —All motorcycle delivery riders and
drivers shall be entitled to a written employment contract setting forth the
complete terms and conditions of their engagement, including, in particular,
the rights guaranteed under Section 6 of this Act.

Section 8. Determination of Employment Relationship. — The existence of
an employer–employee relationship shall be determined in accordance with
applicable labor laws, rules, and regulations, including the four-fold test and
the control test, and shall not be determined solely by the designation or terms
stated in the parties’ contract. The actual nature of the working relationship
and the degree of control exercised over the rider shall prevail. Where an
employer–employee relationship is established, the rider shall be entitled to all
rights, benefits, and protections granted under the Labor Code and other
applicable laws.

Section 9. Training and skills development. —All Motorcycle Delivery Riders
and Drivers shall be entitled to training and skills development programs
under the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) and
other accredited providers. Among the programs covered should include road
safety, customer service, digital literacy, and other skills in relation to their
occupation.

Section 10. Delivery Drivers Welfare Fund. — A Delivery Drivers Welfare
Fund shall be established, to be administered by the DoTR, sourced from
government appropriations, to provide assistance to motorcycle delivery drivers
and riders.
The Fund may be used for the following:

   1. Emergency and medical assistance;

   2. Training and skills-development programs;

   3. Assistance during disasters, public emergencies, and other crises;

   4. Fuel subsidies and other support for work-related expenses; and

   5. Other similar programs that promote the safety, welfare, and livelihood of
      drivers and riders.

Section 11. Grievance Mechanism. —The DoTR, in coordination with the
land Transportation office, shall establish a grievance mechanism to address
and resolve complaints raised by motorcycle riders and drivers in violations of
this Act.

Section 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DoTR, in consultation with, DOLE, DICT,
LTFRB, LTO and other relevant agencies, shall promulgate the Implementing
Rules and Regulations (IRR) necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act.

Section 13. Appropriation. — The Amount necessary to implement this Act
shall be included in the General Appropriations At of the year following its
enactment and thereafter.

Section 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions which are not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

Section 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, rules and
regulations, memoranda, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent
with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

Section 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved.
