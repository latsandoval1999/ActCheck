13                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
14
15   Article 365 of the Revised Penal Code governs criminal liability arising from
16   imprudence and negligence. Its present text, however, contains different and
17   potentially conflicting rules on the application of penalties and modifying
18   circumstances.
19
20   In Noli Z. Ilon v. People of the Philippines, G.R. No. 260538, promulgated on
21   November 11, 2025 and uploaded to the Supreme Court website on May 7, 2026, the
22   Supreme Court held that Article 64 of the Revised Penal Code applies to reckless
23   imprudence resulting in homicide committed through the use of a motor vehicle
24   under paragraph six (6), subparagraph two (2) of Article 365. The Court also ruled
25   that failure to render assistance is a qualifying circumstance which must be
26   specifically alleged in the information and proved beyond reasonable doubt.
27
28   The Court observed that the present wording of Article 365 produces an inequitable
29   distinction. Cases falling under paragraph six (6) may be mitigated or aggravated
30   pursuant to Article 64, while cases falling under the first four paragraphs may not be
31   mitigated because of paragraph five (5), even though they may still be subjected to
32   the penalty-increasing circumstance of failure to render assistance. The Court
33   emphasized that the determination of penalties is a matter of legislative policy and
34   furnished copies of its Decision to the President, the Senate President, and the
35   Speaker of the House of Representatives pursuant to Article 5 of the Revised Penal
36   Code.
37
38   This measure addresses the statutory incongruence identified by the Supreme Court.
39   It provides for the uniform appreciation of mitigating and aggravating circumstances
40   in prosecutions under Article 365, in accordance with Articles 64 and 65 of the
41   Revised Penal Code, while preserving judicial discretion in fixing the duration of the
42   penalty within the proper period based on the degree of negligence and the
43   circumstances of each case.
44

 1   The bill likewise removes the ambiguous statement that the provisions of Article 365
 2   shall not apply to certain cases. Instead, it specifies that only the general penalties
 3   contained in the first two paragraphs shall not govern the exceptional cases
 4   enumerated therein.
 5
 6   Finally, the measure expressly provides that failure to render assistance is a qualifying
 7   circumstance which must be alleged in the information and proved beyond
 8   reasonable doubt. It also codifies the rule that contributory negligence does not
 9   extinguish criminal liability, although it may be appreciated as an analogous
10   mitigating circumstance and considered in determining civil liability.
11
12   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

14                           AN ACT
15   AMENDING ARTICLE 365 OF ACT NO. 3815, AS                                AMENDED,
16   OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE REVISED PENAL CODE
17
18   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
19   Congress assembled:
20
21   SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Vehicular Reckless
22   Imprudence Penalty Harmonization Act.”
23
24   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to ensure
25   consistency, fairness, and proportionality in the imposition of penalties for quasi-
26   offenses under Article 365 of the Revised Penal Code, particularly those involving
27   death or serious physical injuries caused through the operation of motor vehicles.
28
29   The State likewise recognizes the need to harmonize the provisions of Article 365 with
30   Article 64 of the Revised Penal Code in light of evolving jurisprudence, ensure fair
31   notice to the accused, preserve due process rights, and establish clearer statutory
32   standards governing mitigating and aggravating circumstances in cases of reckless
33   imprudence.
34
35   SECTION 3. Article 365 of Act No. 3815, as amended, otherwise known as the
36   Revised Penal Code, is hereby further amended to read as follows:
37
38         “ART. 365. IMPRUDENCE AND NEGLIGENCE. — ANY PERSON
39         WHO, BY RECKLESS IMPRUDENCE, SHALL COMMIT ANY ACT
40         WHICH, HAD IT BEEN INTENTIONAL, WOULD CONSTITUTE A GRAVE
41         FELONY, SHALL SUFFER THE PENALTY OF ARRESTO MAYOR IN ITS
42         MAXIMUM PERIOD TO PRISION CORRECCIONAL IN ITS MEDIUM
43         PERIOD; IF IT WOULD HAVE CONSTITUTED A LESS GRAVE FELONY,
44         THE PENALTY OF ARRESTO MAYOR IN ITS MINIMUM AND MEDIUM
45         PERIODS SHALL BE IMPOSED; IF IT WOULD HAVE CONSTITUTED A
46         LIGHT FELONY, THE PENALTY OF ARRESTO MENOR IN ITS
47         MAXIMUM PERIOD SHALL BE IMPOSED.

35   SECTION 4. Retroactive Application. — This Act shall have retroactive effect
36   insofar as it is favorable to an accused or to a person serving sentence by final
37   judgment, consistent with Article 22 of the Revised Penal Code.
38
39   SECTION 5. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
40   unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
41   remain in full force and effect.
42
43   SECTION 6. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
44   regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
45   repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
