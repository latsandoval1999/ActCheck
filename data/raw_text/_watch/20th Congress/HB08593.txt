CREATING THE PHILIPPINE BUSINESS REGISTRY DATABANK, ESTABLISHING A
FRAMEWORK FOR INTEROPERABILITY WITH EXISTING GOVERNMENT

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) are the backbone of the Philippine
economy. Yet many businesses still struggle to ind partners, suppliers, and new
markets because reliable information about companies is scattered across different
government of ices and local government units. When business data is fragmented or
hard to access, entrepreneurs lose opportunities to connect, grow their networks, and
expand their operations.

Several laws have already taken steps to improve the business information system.
The Ease of Doing Business Act created a Central Business Portal and a Philippine
Business Databank to help government agencies verify registered businesses. Other
laws have also recognized the need for a national repository of business information
and an online database for online transactions. These efforts are important, but they
mainly focus on government veri ication and regulation, and they do not fully address
the need for a platform that helps businesses discover and connect with one another.

This bill ills that gap by creating a Philippine Business Registry Databank under the
Department of Trade and Industry. The proposed system will provide veri ied and
standardized business pro iles that make it easier for companies, especially MSMEs,

to ind potential partners, suppliers, and markets. It will also work with existing
government databases so that information can be shared ef iciently without requiring
businesses to repeatedly submit the same documents.

At the same time, the measure ensures strong protection for privacy, trade secrets,
and sensitive information. Only necessary data will be collected, and safeguards will
be put in place to prevent misuse. By improving access to reliable business
information while protecting con identiality, this bill aims to strengthen business
connections, support MSME growth, and promote greater economic opportunities
across the country.

respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                              AN ACT
CREATING THE PHILIPPINE BUSINESS REGISTRY DATABANK, ESTABLISHING A
FRAMEWORK FOR INTEROPERABILITY WITH EXISTING GOVERNMENT

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Business
Registry Databank Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote ease of
doing business, trade and investments, MSME development, and evidence-based
economic governance through secure electronic networking and information
services, while protecting personal information and con idential business
information in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 and applicable jurisprudence.

SECTION 3. Creation of the Philippine Business Registry Databank (PBRD). —
There is hereby created a Philippine Business Registry Databank (PBRD), which shall
be a specialized national databank intended to: (a) provide veri ied business pro iles
for business-to-business (B2B) discovery and matchmaking; (b) facilitate access to
government and private sector business development services, including training and
market information; and (c) support trade and investment promotion through
electronic networking.

                                                                           3|Page
The PBRD shall not replace the Philippine Business Databank (PBD) mandated under
Republic Act No. 11032, but shall operate as a distinct system with a separate purpose
and dataset, interoperable with the PBD and other relevant government systems.

SECTION 4. Relationship with the Philippine Business Databank (PBD), Central
Business Portal (CBP), and Other Government Databases. —

   (a) Interoperability. — The PBRD shall be designed for interoperability with the
       PBD and CBP under Republic Act No. 11032, and with other government
       databases that contain business registration and permitting information,
       subject to lawful sharing and data minimization.

   (b) No Duplication of Core Registration Data. — The PBRD shall rely on the PBD
       (or the originating agency databases linked thereto) for verification of the
       existence and registration status of a business, to avoid duplicative collection
       of core registration information.

   (c) Trade Secrets and Competition. — Data sharing and publication under this
       Act shall at all times protect trade secrets and promote competition.

SECTION 5. Lead Implementing Agency; System Owner. — The Department of
Trade and Industry (DTI) shall be the lead implementing agency and system owner of
the PBRD, given its mandate on trade, investment promotion, and MSME
development. The DTI shall coordinate with the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT) to ensure interoperability standards,
cybersecurity, and linkage with the PBD and CBP.

SECTION 6. Philippine Business Registry Governing Council. — A Philippine
Business Registry Governing Council (Council) is hereby created to set policy
direction and oversee implementation, composed of: (a) DTI, as Chair; (b) DICT, as Co-
Chair; (c) Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG); (d) Securities and
Exchange Commission (SEC); (e) Cooperative Development Authority (CDA); (f)
Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA); (g) National Privacy Commission (NPC), as
advisory member on privacy compliance; and (h) representatives from accredited
business organizations with nationwide membership, as may be provided in the IRR,
as non-voting private sector advisers.

SECTION 7. Data to be Contained in the PBRD; Data Minimization. — The PBRD
shall contain only data that are necessary to accomplish the purposes of Section 3. It
may include:

   (a) business name and trade name;
   (b) type of entity and primary registration reference numbers (as veri ied through
       PBD/originating agencies);
   (c) city/municipality of principal business address;
   (d) industry classi ication and products/services categories;
   (e) point-of-contact information for business inquiries, subject to consent and the
       Data Privacy Act;
   (f) certi ications, quality standards, and capability statements voluntarily
       submitted by the business;
   (g) participation in government MSME programs, where disclosure is authorized
       by law or by the data subject; and
   (h) other ields as may be necessary and narrowly tailored under the IRR.

SECTION 8. Voluntary Enrollment and Consent-Based Fields. — Except for ields
that may be sourced from existing government registries for veri ication (e.g.,
business existence/status), participation in the PBRD and publication of business
pro ile ields shall be voluntary. Personal information ields, if any, shall be processed
on the basis of consent or other lawful criteria under Republic Act No. 10173 and its
IRR.

SECTION 9. Access and Publication Rules. —

   (a) Public Portal. — The DTI may operate a public portal that allows searching and
       viewing of limited business pro ile information intended for business
       matchmaking and trade promotion.

   (b) Restricted Government Access. — Government access shall be role-based and
       limited to of icial functions.

   (c) Prohibited Disclosures. — The PBRD shall not publish:

          i.   government-issued identi ication numbers;
         ii.   personal addresses of natural persons;
        iii.   con idential business information and trade secrets; and
        iv.    other information protected by law.


                                                                            5|Page
SECTION 10. Data Privacy, Security, and Audit. — The DTI and DICT shall
implement appropriate organizational, physical, and technical measures to protect
PBRD data, including authentication, encryption, access logs, retention schedules, and
breach response measures, consistent with Republic Act No. 10173 and its IRR.

SECTION 11. Role of LGUs and Government Agencies. — LGUs and concerned
NGAs shall cooperate with the DTI and DICT by: (a) enabling veri ication through
linkage with the PBD and/or their databases consistent with Republic Act No. 11032;
and (b) designating focal persons for technical coordination, data standards
alignment, and quality assurance.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize the PBRD to require LGUs to
impose new fees or surcharges on business permits.

SECTION 12. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. — The IRR shall provide
administrative and other appropriate sanctions for unauthorized access, misuse, or
disclosure of PBRD information, without prejudice to the application of Republic Act
No. 10173 and other relevant laws.

SECTION 13. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
DTI, subject to budgeting, programming, and auditing rules. Thereafter, such sums as
may be necessary shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from effectivity, the DTI, in coordination with the DICT, DILG, SEC, CDA, PSA, and NPC,
and after stakeholder consultations, shall promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations necessary to carry out this Act. The IRR shall include interoperability
standards, consent and lawful processing rules, publication limits, security controls,
and audit mechanisms.

SECTION 15. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed or modi ied accordingly. This Act shall be construed in
harmony with Republic Act No. 11032.
