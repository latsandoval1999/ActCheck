     TAXPAYERS TO FOUR HUNDRED FIFTY THOUSAND (P450,000.00)

    THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED

                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution mandates that Congress evolve a progressive system of taxation.1
Pursuant to this, Congress enacted the National Internal Revenue Code, which
provides for income tax rates that increase as the person’s taxable income increases.
Currently, the law provides that individuals with an annual taxable income of P250,000
and below are exempt from income tax.2 As such, those earning around P20,800 a
month do not have to pay income tax.

In 2017, when the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) Law was
passed providing for the current tax exemption ceiling, the ceiling covered those in the
low income and lower-middle income sectors.3 However, over the years, the range of
family income belonging to the different economic classes have also changed. Some
who were previously classified as being part of the middle middle- income class in
2017 eventually belonged to the lower-middle income class in 2021.4 The purchasing

In addition, the effects of inflation, while felt by all Filipinos, are harder to bear for low-
income families who spend the majority of their income on food, transportation, and
other basic necessities.6 As the prices of goods and services increase, lower-income
households experience a decline in purchasing power and an inability to meet the
increasing costs. Notably, there have also been inflation spikes since the passage of
the TRAIN Law, particularly in the years 2018 and 2022.7

This demonstrates that the current tax exemption ceiling no longer protects those most
vulnerable to income poverty, particularly the low income and lower-middle income
class.8 Specifically, lower-middle income families face the risk of falling into poverty
due to unforeseen and sudden economic shocks, such as a family member losing
employment or medical emergencies.

This measure aims to make our tax law more responsive to the changing economic
times and the value of the Philippine Peso by increasing the tax exemption ceiling for
individual taxpayers from P250,000 to P450,000. This would cover individuals earning
P37,500 each month, as opposed to exempting only those earning around P20,800 a
month. Under this revised ceiling, those earning P450,000 a year can save up to
P32,500 annually or over P2,700 a month. This higher take-home pay for ordinary
Filipinos would significantly ease their burden, allow for greater spending on food and
basic necessities, and ultimately support the most vulnerable sectors of our society.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

SEC. 5. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following
