                                   AN ACT
    REQUIRING LAND, AIR, AND SEA TRANSPORT PROVIDERS TO
     PROVIDE PRIORITY SEATING FOR THE ELDERLY, FURTHER
     AMENDING FOR THIS PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7432, AS
    AMENDED BY REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9257 AND 9994, OTHERWISE
    KNOWN AS THE “EXPANDED SENIOR CITIZEN’S ACT OF 2010”

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      It is the duty of the State to ensure that public utilities and
transportation services are accessible to all people, especially those in
vulnerable groups such as senior citizens and persons with disabilities.

        In furtherance of this mandate, Batas Pambansa Bilang 344, or the
Accessibility Law, and Republic Act No. 9994, or the Expanded Senior Citizens
Act of 2010, were enacted to improve access to public transportation for
persons with disabilities and senior citizens. Under these laws, public
transport providers are required to designate seats for persons with
disabilities, and the Department of Transportation is directed “to develop a
program to assist senior citizens to fully gain access to public transport
facilities.”

       Administrative issuances, including Department of Transportation
Department Order No. 2014-013, likewise provide for reserved or priority
seating for persons with disabilities, senior citizens, and pregnant women in
certain public transportation facilities. However, existing law and regulations
do not establish a comprehensive statutory requirement expressly prescribing
priority seating for senior citizens across all modes of public transportation—
land, rail, air, and sea—along with uniform standards on seat designation,
labeling, reservation, enforcement, and penalties.

      As a result, senior citizens continue to experience difficulties in
accessing priority seats. They often find themselves standing in cramped,
moving vehicles while non-eligible passengers occupy seats that should be
available to them. The lack of clear statutory rules and consistent enforcement
undermines the intent of existing accessibility and senior-citizen laws.

       In recognition of the invaluable contributions of senior citizens to
nation-building, it is only right and just to provide them with additional
privileges, particularly in public transportation. By ensuring priority seating
for the elderly, this measure promotes their mobility and access, enabling
them to continue participating actively in the life of their communities.

       This bill seeks to strengthen the Expanded Senior Citizens Act by
expressly requiring priority seating for senior citizens in land, air, and sea
transport, including rail services. It further provides for appropriate penalties
for public transport operators who fail to comply with this requirement. It is
the hope of this measure to mainstream mobility and access for senior citizens
in public transportation, so that we may continue to support them in their
daily lives and in their ongoing efforts to contribute to nation-building.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                                    AN ACT

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Priority Seating
for the Elderly Act.”

SECTION 2. Amendatory Provisions. – For the purposes of this Act, the
following provisions of Republic Act No. 7432, as amended by Republic Act
No. 9257 and 9994, otherwise known as the “Expanded Senior Citizens Act of
2010,” is hereby further amended to read as follows:
      a) Section 5, Paragraph (f) of Republic Act No. 7432, as amended, is
         hereby further amended as follows:

         “SEC. 5. Government Assistance. – x x x

         “(a) Employment

         xxx

         “(f) Access to Public Transport

         The Department of Transportation [and Communication] (DOTr) shall
         develop a program to assist senior citizens to fully gain access to public
         transport facilities.

         PRIORITY SEATING FOR THE ELDERLY ALL PUBLIC UTILITY
         VEHICLES (PUV), PUBLIC UTILITY BUSES (PUB), RAIL
         TRANSPORT PROVIDERS, AND AIR AND SEA TRANSPORT
  PROVIDERS, SHALL RESERVE AND DESIGNATE THE BEST
  POSSIBLE SEATS FOR SENIOR CITIZENS AS DETERMINED BY
  THE DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION (DOTR), IN
  COORDINATION     WITH    THE  LAND     TRANSPORTATION
  FRANCHISING AND REGULATORY BOARD (LTFRB), THE
  PHILIPPINE NATIONAL RAILWAYS (PNR), THE LIGHT RAIL
  TRANSIT AUTHORITY (LRTA), THE MARITIME INDUSTRY
  AUTHORITY (MARINA), THE CIVIL AVIATION AUTHORITY OF
  THE PHILIPPINES (CAAP), AND THREE (3) REPRESENTATIVES
  FROM REPUTABLE AND CONCERNED CIVIL SOCIETY
  ORGANIZATIONS, AS DETERMINED BY THE DOTR.

    THE PRIORITY SEATS SHALL BE CLEARLY LABELLED TO
  INDICATE THAT PASSENGERS MAY USE THEM BUT MUST
  VACATE THEM IF THEY ARE NEEDED BY AN ELDERLY
  PASSENGER: PROVIDED, THAT PASSENGERS WHO ARE
  PREGNANT OR DISABLED SHALL BE EXEMPTED FROM THIS
  PROVISION.

    COMMERCIAL AIR AND SEA TRANSPORT PROVIDERS SHALL
  ENSURE THAT PRIORITY SEATS ARE MADE AVAILABLE TO
  ELDERLY PASSENGERS DURING THE RESERVATION OF SEATS.
  ELDERLY PASSENGERS MAY NOT BE TRANSFERRED TO
  OTHER SEATS OR BUMPED OFF WITH AT LEAST ONE (1) WEEK
  OF PRIOR NOTICE AND WITHOUT REASON.

     x x x”

SECTION 3. Appropriations. – Such funds necessary to carry out the
provisions of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.

SECTION 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DOTR, in
coordination with the LTFRB, PNR, LRTA, MARINA, CAAP, and three (3)
representatives from reputable and concerned civil society organizations, as
determined by the DOTR, shall promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations of this Act within fifteen (15) days from its effectivity.

SECTION 5. Non-Impairment Clause. – Nothing in this Act shall be
construed as to diminish, impair, or repeal rights recognized, granted, or
made available to disadvantaged, marginalized, or specific sectors under
existing laws.

SECTION 6. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall continue
to be in full force and effect.

SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees, presidential proclamations, letters of instruction, rules and
regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 8. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect within fifteen (15) days
following its publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of
general circulation in the Philippines.
