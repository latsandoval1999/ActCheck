EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines faces significant exposure to a broad spectrum of natural,
biological, technological, and human-induced hazards. These include tropical cyclones,
floods, storm surges, landslides, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, tsunamis, drought,
extreme heat, epidemics, pandemics, and other threats that jeopardize lives, livelihoods,
infrastructure, public services, and economic development.

Republic Act No. 10121, otherwise known as the “Philippine Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Act of 2010,” established a comprehensive disaster risk
reduction and management system and institutionalized the National Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Plan. The law likewise established the National Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) and vested it with functions relating to
disaster risk assessment, planning, coordination, preparedness, response, recovery, and the
implementation of disaster risk reduction and management policies and programs.

Despite the presence of these mechanisms, further strengthening of disaster risk
reduction and management is necessary. This can be achieved through a specific, time-
bound, measurable, and continuously monitored action plan that addresses disasters and
risks which can be reasonably anticipated using scientific evidence, historical data, hazard
assessments, risk maps, climate projections, early warning systems, and other reliable
sources.

A lack of a sufficiently integrated, long-term operational mechanism may lead to
fragmented preparedness activities, program duplication, gaps in inter-agency
coordination, inadequate resource allocation, and delays in implementing preventive and
anticipatory measures.

This measure seeks to institutionalize a Five-Year Action Pian for the Identification,
Prevention, Preparedness, and Response to Foreseeable Disasters as an integral and
complementary component of the existing national disaster risk reduction and management
framework.

The proposed Five-Year Action Plan will operationalize national disaster risk
reduction and management policies by establishing concrete and measurable programs,
projects, activities, targets, timelines, responsibilities, and resource requirements. It will

The proposed measure is not intended to replace the National Disaster Risk

other stakeholders to anticipate and manage disaster risks.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Five-Year Disaster

Preparedness and Action Plan Act.”
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. —\t is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

(a) Protect the life, safety, dignity, property, livelihood, and well-being of the Filipino

people from the adverse effects of disasters;

(b) Strengthen the country's capacity to identify, prevent, mitigate, prepare for, anticipate,

respond to, and recover from disasters;

(c) Promote a proactive, science-based, risk-informed, inclusive, and whole-of-

government approach to disaster risk reduction and management;

(d) Ensure that foreseeable disaster risks are identified and addressed through timely,

coordinated, measurable, and adequately resourced government action;

SEC. 4. Integration with Existing National Plans. — The Five-Year Action Plan shall be
harmonized with the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plan and other existing

national government plans, programs, and policies.

It shall complement and operationalize, and shall not replace or diminish, existing disaster
risk reduction and management plans, contingency plans, response plans, climate change

adaptation plans, development plans, and other applicable government programs.

In case of conflict between the Five-Year Action Plan and an existing law, the provisions

of the existing law shall prevail.

SEC. 5. Funding. — The amount necessary for the implementation of this Act shall be
included in the appropriations of the concerned national government agencies and local
government units, subject to existing budgeting, may be supported by the National Disaster Risk

Reduction and Management Fund, Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Funds, regular

SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred twenty (120) days

academic institutions, civil society organizations, private sector representatives, and other relevant
stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation

of this Act.

The implementing rules and regulations shall establish, among others, standards and

procedures for:
(a) The formulation and approval of the Five-Year Action Plan;
(b) The preparation of Annual Implementation Plans;
(c) Monitoring and evaluation;
(d) Inter-agency coordination;
(e) Local government integration;
(f) Public reporting and transparency; and
(g) Periodic review and updating.
