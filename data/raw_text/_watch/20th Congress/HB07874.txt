AN ACT RATIONALIZING THE WITHHOLDING TAX RATE ON REFINED
PETROLEUM PRODUCTS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTION 57
OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED


                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

The downstream oil industry plays a vital role in sustaining economic activity in the
Philippines. Petroleum products fuel public transportation, power generation,
logistics, agriculture, and industrial production. Any distortion in the tax structure
affecting this sector inevitably reverberates throughout the economy and impacts
consumer prices.

Under current tax regulations, income derived from the sale of refined petroleum
products is subject to a one percent (1%) creditable withholding tax (CWT) on gross
sales. While intended as an advance collection mechanism, this rate has resulted in a
persistent and significant accumulation of excess or unused tax credits among oil
industry players. As reflected in audited financial statements in recent years, major oil
companies have consistently reported increasing levels of unutilized CWTs due to the
mismatch between the withholding rate and their actual income tax liabilities.

The downstream oil industry typically operates on thin margins. The imposition of a
relatively high withholding tax on gross sales—rather than net income—creates cash
flow constraints that tie up substantial working capital. Funds that could otherwise be
deployed for infrastructure investments, storage expansion, logistics modernization,
and distribution efficiency remain immobilized in the form of idle tax credits.

This measure seeks to rationalize the withholding tax rate applicable to refined
petroleum products by reducing it to a rate not exceeding one-tenth of one percent
(0.1%). The proposal does not remove the withholding mechanism; rather, it aligns
the rate more closely with the sector’s actual tax profile and economic realities.



                                                  1
By easing cash flow pressures, this reform is expected to:

   ● Improve liquidity within the downstream oil industry;

   ● Encourage reinvestment in infrastructure and operational efficiency;

   ● Reduce the need to pass financing costs to consumers; and

   ● Contribute, over time, to more stable and competitive fuel pricing.


Importantly, the measure does not diminish the government’s power to assess and
collect income taxes. It merely corrects a structural imbalance in advance withholding
that results in excessive credit accumulation.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

Section 1. Section 57 of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC), as amended, is
hereby further amended to read as follows:

        Sec. 57. Withholding Tax at the Source.

        xxx

        (B) Withholding of Credible Tax at the Source. – Upon the recommendation of
        the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, the Secretary of Finance may require
        any payor corporation or person to withhold tax on income paid to individuals
        or entities residing in the Philippines, FOR ITEMS OF INCOME WHERE
        WITHHOLDING IS AUTHORIZED UNDER EXISTING TAX LAWS.
        The amount withheld shall NOT EXCEED FIFTEEN PERCENT (15%) of
        the income and shall be credited against the recipient’s income tax liability for
        the year. Provided, however, that for income derived from the SALE OF
        REFINED PETROLEUM PRODUCTS, the withholding tax SHALL NOT
        EXCEED 0.1%.

        Sec. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations – Within ninety (90) days from
        the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance (DOF), in consultation
        with the relevant government agencies, shall issue the rules and regulations
        needed to implement this Act effectively.



                                                  3
Sec. 3. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
