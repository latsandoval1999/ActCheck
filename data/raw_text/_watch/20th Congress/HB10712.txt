                               AN ACT
    INCREASING BY FIFTY PERCENT (50%) THE EXCISE TAX ON SWEETENED
      BEVERAGES, IMPOSING AN EXCISE TAX ON REFINED SUGAR, AND
      AMENDING SECTION 150-B OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE
                      CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED

                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Constitution recognizes the people’s right to health and directs the State to
adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to health development. Consistent
with this policy, Section 150-B of the National Internal Revenue Code imposes an
excise tax on sweetened beverages.

       In carrying out this constitutional policy, legislation should go beyond
regulating conduct. It should also help people understand the reasons behind public
health measures and encourage them to make informed choices. This finds a fitting
parallel in 1 Corinthians 2:13 (KJV), which speaks of …speaking, teaching, and
comparing…” These interconnected acts suggest a process of learning,
comprehension, and appreciation, and right application. Applied to public health, it
is not enough for people to simply know the risks of excessive sugar consumption.
They should understand those risks, appreciate their consequences, and be able to use
that knowledge in making healthier choices.

       At present, the law imposes ₱6.00 per liter on beverages using caloric or non-
caloric sweeteners, or a combination of both, and ₱12.00 per liter on beverages using
high-fructose corn syrup. Beverages using purely coconut sap sugar or purely steviol
glycosides are exempt.1

        This bill increases these rates by fifty percent (50%), from ₱6.00 to ₱9.00 per
liter and from ₱12.00 to ₱18.00 per liter, respectively. Except for the rate adjustments,
it retains the existing coverage, definitions, exemptions, labeling requirements,

       The bill also imposes a new excise tax of ₱5.00 per kilogram on refined sugar
produced in the Philippines for domestic consumption and on imported refined sugar
released for domestic consumption.

       For this purpose, refined sugar is principally identified by a polarimeter
reading of 99.5° or higher. Color, production process, refinery status, laboratory
results, and Sugar Regulatory Administration documentation may be used as
supporting evidence under the implementing rules. Existing classifications likewise
recognize sugar with a polarimeter reading of 99.5° or higher and/or a color of 800
ICU or less as refined sugar.3

       The refined-sugar tax applies to sugar as the product subject to tax. Its
subsequent use as an ingredient does not result in a second tax under the same
provision. A finished beverage remains subject to the sweetened-beverage tax if it
independently falls within Section 150-B.

       Exports and qualified uses under bond shall be treated as tax suspensions. The
tax shall become due upon diversion to domestic consumption or failure to account
for the sugar. The Bureau of Internal Revenue, Bureau of Customs, and Sugar
Regulatory Administration shall harmonize classification, testing, documentation,
bonded transfers, and enforcement procedures.

       Before the refined-sugar excise tax takes effect, the Department of Finance, in
consultation with the concerned agencies, shall submit to Congress a study on its
projected revenue, price, health, industry, and enforcement effects, including its
impact on consumers, small businesses, sugarcane farmers, smuggling, and the
domestic sugar industry.

        Congress shall review the tax and its implementation three (3) years after
effectivity and determine whether the rate and regulatory framework should be
retained, amended, or repealed.

       Revenues from the existing sweetened-beverage excise tax shall continue to be
governed by Section 288-A of the National Internal Revenue Code. Fifty percent (50%)
of such revenues shall remain earmarked for PhilHealth, medical assistance, and the
Health Facilities Enhancement Program. This statutory allocation shall not be altered,
reduced, transferred, or repurposed.4

     Revenues from the new refined-sugar excise tax shall accrue to the General
Fund and shall be subject to appropriation through the annual General
Appropriations Act.

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Healthy Beverages
and Refined Sugar Tax Act.”

       SEC. 2. Amendment of Section 150-B(A) of the National Internal Revenue Code of
1997, as amended.

            Section 150-B(A) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as
       amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:


               “SEC. 150-B. Sweetened Beverages. —

               “(A) Rate and Base of Tax. —

               “(1) A tax of [Six pesos (₱6.00)] NINE PESOS (₱9.00) per liter of volume
               capacity shall be levied, assessed, and collected on sweetened beverages
               using purely caloric sweeteners, purely non-caloric sweeteners, or a mix
               of caloric and non-caloric sweeteners: Provided, That this tax rate shall
               not apply to sweetened beverages using high-fructose corn syrup:
               Provided, further, That sweetened beverages using purely coconut sap
               sugar and purely steviol glycosides shall be exempt from this tax; and

               “(2) A tax of [Twelve Pesos (₱12.00)] EIGHTEEN PESOS (₱18.00) per
               liter of volume capacity shall be levied, assessed, and collected on
               sweetened beverages using purely high-fructose corn syrup or high-
               fructose corn syrup in combination with any caloric or non-caloric

      SEC. 3. Preservation of Existing Sweetened-Beverage Provisions.

        Except for the amendments expressly made by this Act, all provisions of
Section 150-B of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, shall remain
in full force and effect.

        The existing statutory definition of sweetened beverages, including its
coverage of liquid, powdered, and concentrated products, shall remain applicable.
The existing exclusions for milk products, qualifying natural fruit and vegetable
juices, meal-replacement and medically indicated beverages, and coffee products shall
likewise remain in force.

The existing provisions on filing and payment under Sections 130 and 131, penalties,
FDA labeling and post-marketing surveillance, and BIR identification markings shall
continue to apply to sweetened beverages.

      SEC. 4. Creation of Section 150-C of the National Internal Revenue Code.

       A new Section 150-C is hereby inserted into Chapter VI, Title VI of the National
Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, to read as follows:


             “SEC. 150-C. EXCISE TAX ON REFINED SUGAR. —

             “(A) Imposition of Tax. — A specific excise tax of Five pesos (₱5.00) per
                  kilogram shall be levied, assessed, and collected on refined sugar
                  manufactured or produced in the Philippines for domestic
                  consumption and on refined sugar imported into the Philippines
                  for domestic consumption.

             “(B) Definition of Refined Sugar. — For purposes of this Section,
                  refined sugar refers to sugar whose sucrose content by weight, in
                  the dry state, corresponds to a polarimeter reading of 99.5 degrees
                  or above.

                   “Color, production process, refinery status, production-line
                   accreditation, laboratory results, Sugar Regulatory Administration
                   certification, and other technical documentation may be used as
                   supporting evidence or presumptive indicators of refined-sugar
                   classification in accordance with the rules and regulations issued
                   under this Act.

             “(C) Raw Cane Sugar. — Raw cane sugar refers to sugar extracted from
                  sugarcane through a simple mechanical process, including

     pressing for juice, boiling to crystallize, filtering through a
     centrifuge, and drying, resulting in crystallized sugar containing
     natural molasses.

     “Raw cane sugar shall be produced through only one (1) stage of
     filtering and centrifuging, without further processing such as
     washing or bleaching, and shall have a polarimeter reading of less
     than 99.5 degrees and a color greater than 800 ICU.

     “The term includes muscovado sugar meeting the applicable
     technical specifications.

“(D) Presumption of Refined Sugar. — Cane sugar shall be prima facie
     presumed to be refined sugar if it is:

      “(1) A product of a refining process;
      “(2) A product of a sugar refinery; or
      “(3) A product of a production line accredited by the Bureau of
           Internal Revenue as producing or capable of producing
           sugar with a polarimeter reading of 99.5 degrees or above,
           where the corresponding quedan, as verified by the Sugar
           Regulatory Administration, identifies the sugar accordingly.

“The presumption may be rebutted through laboratory testing, Sugar
Regulatory Administration certification, or other evidence recognized
under the implementing rules.

“(E) Taxable Events. — The tax shall accrue:

      “(1) Upon removal of refined sugar from the place of production,
            mill, refinery, or factory for domestic consumption; or
      “(2) Upon release of imported refined sugar from customs
            custody for domestic consumption.

“(F) Tax Suspensions and Exclusions. — The following shall not be
subject to the tax imposed under this Section:

      “(1) Raw cane sugar, including muscovado sugar, that meets the
            requirements of subsection (C);
      “(2) Refined sugar actually exported from the Philippines, subject

            and revenue requirements;
      “(3) Refined sugar removed under bond for actual exportation or
            for transfer to a registered facility or qualified end-user
            under conditions prescribed by the Secretary of Finance; and

       SEC. 5 Classification and Interagency Coordination.
       The Secretary of Finance shall, upon the joint recommendation of the
Commissioner of Internal Revenue, the Commissioner of Customs, and the
Administrator of the Sugar Regulatory Administration, and in consultation with the
Secretary of Agriculture, issue rules for the classification of refined sugar and raw cane
sugar subject to Section 150-C.

The implementing rules shall provide for:

   1.   Polarimeter and color testing;
   2.   Laboratory methods, sampling, and certificates of analysis;
   3.   Accreditation of production lines;
   4.   Verification and marking of quedans;
   5.   Submission of Sugar Regulatory Administration quality-test results to the
        Bureau of Internal Revenue;


                                        Page 7 of 10
   6. Classification and testing of imported sugar;
   7. Procedures for disputed classification and taxpayer challenges;
   8. Transfers between registered mills, refineries, warehouses, and manufacturing
       facilities;
   9. Tax-suspension documentation and inventory reconciliation; and
   10. Prevention of double taxation upon transfers between registered facilities.


Tariff classification under customs laws shall remain distinct from the determination
of excise-tax liability under Section 150-C. The Bureau of Customs shall collect the tax
on imported refined sugar upon release for domestic consumption based on the
technical definition and implementing rules under this Act.

       SEC. 6. Identification Markings and Records.
The Commissioner of Internal Revenue may prescribe materially unique, secure, and
nonremovable identification markings, codes, stamps, or other control measures for
refined sugar subject to tax under Section 150-C.

Manufacturers, producers, importers, warehouses, refineries, and qualified end-users
shall maintain records sufficient to establish:

   1.   The source and classification of the sugar;
   2.   The quantity produced, received, transferred, exported, or removed;
   3.   The applicable polarimeter and color results;
   4.   The destination and end-use of sugar removed under bond; and
   5.   The payment, suspension, or exemption of the excise tax.

       SEC. 7. Filing, Payment, and Enforcement.
The filing and payment of excise taxes on sweetened beverages shall continue to be
governed by Sections 130 and 131 of the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended.

The same provisions shall apply, as appropriate, to refined sugar. Misdeclaration,
misclassification, unlawful removal, diversion, failure to account for products
removed under bond, and other violations shall be subject to the applicable penalties
under the National Internal Revenue Code and other relevant laws.

       SEC. 8. Revenue Allocation.
The revenues collected from the excise tax on sweetened beverages shall continue to
be allocated in accordance with Section 288-A of the National Internal Revenue Code,
as amended.

Nothing in this Act shall alter, reduce, transfer, or repurpose the statutory allocation
of sweetened-beverage excise-tax revenues. Fifty percent (50%) of the total revenues
collected from the excise tax on sweetened beverages shall continue to be allocated
and used exclusively in the manner provided by Section 288-A, including:



                                       Page 8 of 10
Eighty percent (80%) to the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation for the
implementation of the Universal Health Care Act; and

Twenty percent (20%) nationwide, based on political and district subdivisions, for
medical assistance and the Health Facilities Enhancement Program.

The revenues collected from the excise tax on refined sugar shall accrue to the General
Fund and shall be subject to appropriation through the annual General
Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 9. Revenue, Health, and Industry Impact Study.
Within one hundred twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary
of Finance shall submit to Congress a revenue, health, industry, and enforcement-
impact study prepared in consultation with the Secretary of Agriculture, the Secretary
of Trade and Industry, the Secretary of Health, the Commissioner of Internal Revenue,
the Commissioner of Customs, and the Administrator of the Sugar Regulatory
Administration.

The study shall include:

   1.  Annual domestic production of refined sugar;
   2.  Annual imports of refined sugar;
   3.  Estimated taxable volume in kilograms;
   4.  Projected gross collections from the ₱5.00-per-kilogram tax;
   5.  Collection, monitoring, and enforcement costs;
   6.  Expected effects on consumer prices and inflation;
   7.  Effects on low-income households, small businesses, food manufacturers, and
       retailers;
   8. Effects on sugarcane farmers, sugar mills, refineries, and the domestic sugar
       supply chain;
   9. Risks of smuggling, underdeclaration, misclassification, and substitution;
   10. The availability and reliability of BIR, Customs, SRA, and industry data;
   11. Expected effects on sugar consumption and health outcomes; and
   12. Recommendations on whether the ₱5.00-per-kilogram rate, exemptions, and
       classification rules remain appropriate.

The Secretary of Finance shall make the study publicly available, subject to applicable
laws on confidential taxpayer and customs information.

        SEC. 10. Effectivity of the Refined-Sugar Tax.
The tax imposed under Section 150-C shall take effect one hundred eighty (180) days
after the effectivity of this Act.

The increased rates under Section 150-B(A), as provided in Section 2 of this Act, shall
take effect fifteen (15) days after publication under Section 14 of this Act.



                                       Page 9 of 10
      SEC. 11. Periodic Legislative Review.
Congress shall review the rates, classifications, exemptions, collections,
administrative costs, enforcement performance, price effects, and health effects of this
Act every three (3) years from its effectivity.

The review shall consider whether:

   1.   The ₱5.00-per-kilogram refined-sugar rate remains appropriate;
   2.   The sweetened-beverage rates require further adjustment;
   3.   The exemptions remain justified;
   4.   The classification standards remain technically and administratively workable;
   5.   The tax causes material substitution or smuggling; and
   6.   The measure produces its intended health and revenue objectives.

   SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations.
   Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance
shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations upon recommendation of
the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, the Commissioner of Customs, and the
Administrator of the Sugar Regulatory Administration, with the participation of the
Department of Agriculture, Department of Trade and Industry, Department of Health,
and Food and Drug Administration where appropriate.

The implementing rules shall cover product classification, testing, accreditation,
importation, bonded removals, exportation, medical-use documentation, inventory
controls, tax payment, identification markings, records, audit, disputes, and
enforcement.

        SEC. 13. Separability Clause.
If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions
not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 14. Repealing Clause.
All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, regulations, and other issuances, or parts
thereof, inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

        SEC. 15. Effectivity.
This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette
or in a newspaper of general circulation.


        Approved.
