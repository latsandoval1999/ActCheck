                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The rapid expansion of social media use among children has raised significant and
well-documented concerns regarding exposure to harmful and inappropriate content,
cyberbullying, exploitation risks, mental health impacts, data privacy violations, and the
effects of algorithm-driven and engagement-based platform design that may contribute
to addictive use patterns.

      Current platform incentives are largely directed toward maximizing user
engagement and time spent on platforms, which may not necessarily align with the
developmental needs and welfare of children.

       International policy developments, including Australia’s Online Safety Amendment
(social media Minimum Age) Bill 2024, reflect a growing recognition of the need for a
systems-based regulatory approach that places responsibility on social media service
providers to implement safeguards against underage access, rather than relying solely on
user or parental compliance.

        This measure seeks to establish a minimum age requirement for social media
account access and to mandate that Covered Social Media Service Providers implement
reasonable and proportionate age assurance measures to prevent access by children
fifteen (15) years of age and below.

      This bill does not intend to restrict access to digital learning platforms, educational
technologies, or private messaging services, but rather to regulate access to social media
platforms that facilitate public or semi-public user-generated content and algorithm-
driven content distribution.

        In line with Article II, Section 13 of the 1987 Constitution, which mandates the
State to protect the youth and promote their physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and
social well-being, this measure establishes regulatory accountability on social media
service providers operating within the Philippines for ensuring safer digital environments
for children.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                  AN ACT
     RESTRICTING SOCIAL MEDIA ACCESS TO CHILDREN FIFTEEN (15) YEARS OLD
     AND BELOW, MANDATING AGE VERIFICATION MECHANISMS FOR SOCIAL
     MEDIA PLATFORMS, PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR NON-COMPLIANCE, AND
     FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1          SECTION 1. Short Title - This Act shall be known as the “Social Media Age
 2   Restriction Act of 2026”
 3
 4          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   protect and promote the physical, mental, emotional, and social well-being of children by
 6   addressing the risks associated with unrestricted access of minors fifteen (15) years of
 7   age and below to algorithm-driven social media platforms. The State recognizes that such
 8   exposure may subject children to foreseeable harms, including harmful content exposure,
 9   online exploitation, addictive platform design, and adverse psychological effects.
10
11         In pursuit of these goals, the State shall:
12
13         (a) Institutionalize a preventive and systems-based regulatory framework
14         requiring Covered Social Media Service Providers, as a condition for operating
15         within the Philippines, to implement reasonable and proportionate measures to
16         prevent the creation, registration, and access of social media accounts by children
17         below the prescribed minimum age;
 1
 2         (b) Ensure that primary responsibility for enforcing age restrictions and
 3         implementing age assurance mechanisms rests upon Covered Social Media Service
 4         Providers, consistent with the principle that entities which design, operate, and
 5         derive economic benefit from digital platforms must likewise be accountable for
 6         foreseeable user harms;
 7
 8         (c) Require that age assurance and access restriction measures be implemented
 9         in a manner that is privacy-preserving, proportionate, and consistent with
10         constitutional rights, including the right to privacy of communications and the right
11         to data privacy under existing laws;
12
13         (d) Promote age-appropriate digital participation through the strengthening of
14         digital literacy programs, parental guidance mechanisms, and institutional support
15         systems, while recognizing that unrestricted access to social media platforms is
16         not essential to meaningful digital participation by minors; and
17
18         (e) Ensure that nothing in this Act shall be construed -as restricting access to
19         educational technologies, messaging services, and other digital tools not primarily
20         designed for public or semi-public social interaction.
21
22          By adhering to these principles, the State commits to fostering a safe, rights-
23   respecting, and developmentally appropriate digital environment that safeguards children
24   while enabling responsible and inclusive participation in the digital age.
25
26         SECTION 3. Definition of Terms - For purposes of this Act, the following terms
27   are hereby defined:
28
29         (a) Account Holder - a natural person who owns, registers, controls, maintains, or
30         operates a Social Media Account.
31
32         (b) Age Assurance Measure - any technological, administrative, procedural, or
33         documentary method designed to reasonably determine whether a person satisfies
34         the minimum age requirement prescribed under this Act.
35
36         (c) Child - any person fifteen (15) years of age and below.
37
38         (d) Circumvention - any act, omission, device, software, mechanism,
39         misrepresentation, or arrangement intended to evade, defeat, manipulate, or
40         bypass an Age Assurance Measure or any requirement imposed under this Act.
41
42         (e) Covered Social Media Service - an online platform, website, application, or
43         electronic service that:
44
 1          (1) allows users to create or maintain accounts or profiles;
 2
 3          (2) enables users to generate, upload, publish, share, view, or interact with
 4          user-generated content; and
 5
 6          (3) facilitates social interaction, communication, networking, content
 7          discovery, or engagement among users.
 8
 9   (f) Covered Social Media Service Provider - any natural or juridical person,
10   corporation, partnership, association, organization, or other entity that owns,
11   operates, controls, administers, or makes available a Covered Social Media Service
12   to users located within the Philippines, whether organized under Philippine law or
13   foreign law.
14
15   (g) Department - refers to the Department of Information and Communications
16   Technology (DICT).
17
18   (h) Minor - a person below eighteen (18) years of age as defined under Philippine
19   law.
20

27
28          (3) available technologies and industry standards;
29
30          (4) privacy and data protection obligations; and

32          (5) the costs and feasibility of implementation.

35   presence that enables access to or participation in a Covered Social Media Service.
36
37   (k) User - any individual who accesses, browses, registers for, maintains, or uses

39
40   (l) User-Generated Content - any text, image, video, audio, livestream, comment,

42   or disseminated by a user through a Covered Social Media Service.

18          SECTION 4. Minimum Age Requirement - A Covered Social Media Service Provider
19   shall not permit a Child to create, register for, hold, maintain, or operate a Social Media
20   Account on a Covered Social Media Service.
21
22         Every Covered Social Media Service Provider shall take reasonable and
23   proportionate steps to prevent Children from creating, registering for, holding,
24   maintaining, or operating Social Media Accounts on its service.
25
26         For purposes of this Act, the minimum age for the creation, registration, holding,
27   maintenance, or operation of a Social Media Account shall be sixteen (16) years of age.
28
29          SECTION 5. Obligations of Social Media Platforms - To comply with the minimum
30   age requirement established under this Act, every Covered Social Media Service Provider
31   shall:
32
33          (a) implement and maintain age assurance measures reasonably designed to
34          prevent a Child from creating, registering for, holding, maintaining, or operating a
35          Social Media Account;
36
37          (b) take reasonable and proportionate steps to identify and restrict access to Social
38          Media Accounts reasonably determined or verified through available information
39          and system indicators to be held or operated by a Child;
40
41          (c) establish clear procedures for the review, restriction, suspension, or removal
42          of accounts found to be in violation of the minimum age requirement, subject to
43          notice and internal review mechanisms;
44
 1         (d) adopt measures reasonably designed to prevent the circumvention or evasion
 2         of age assurance measures implemented under this Act; and
 3
 4         (e) submit compliance reports, information, and records as may be required by
 5         the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) under the
 6         Implementing Rules and Regulations.
 7
 8          Compliance with this Section shall not require absolute prevention of underage
 9   access, provided that the Covered Social Media Service Provider demonstrates that it has
10   taken reasonable and proportionate steps consistent with this Act and its IRR.

12          SECTION 7. Prohibited Acts - It shall be unlawful for any Covered Social Media
13   Service Provider to:
14
15          (a) fail to take reasonable and proportionate steps to prevent a Child from creating,
16          registering for, holding, maintaining, or operating a Social Media Account in
17          violation of this Act;
18
19          (b) fail to implement and maintain age assurance measures required under this
20          Act and its IRR;
21
22          (c) knowingly permit the continued operation of a Social Media Account after
23          becoming aware, or having sufficient basis under system data or verification
24          indicators to reasonably determine, that such account is held or operated by a
25          Child;
26
27          (d) design, implement, or maintain systems, processes, or features that are
28          reasonably expected or intended to circumvent or undermine age assurance
29          measures required under this Act; and
30
31          (e) collect, retain, process, disclose, or use personal information obtained for age
32          assurance purposes beyond what is necessary and proportionate for compliance
33          with this Act and Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012).
34
35           SECTION 8. Penalties - Any Covered Social Media Service Provider found in
36   violation of this Act shall be subject to the following penalties, without prejudice to civil,
37   criminal, or administrative liability under existing laws:
38
39          (a) Administrative Fines - ₱1,000,000.00 to ₱5,000,000.00 per violation instance,
40          taking into account the nature, gravity, duration, and number of affected users;
41
42          (b) Continuing non-compliance - additional daily fines may be imposed until full
43          compliance is achieved;
44
 1         (c) Suspension of Operations - in cases of repeated, willful, or gross violations,
 2         subject to due process and coordination with the National Telecommunications
 3         Commission (NTC);
 4
 5         (d) Blocking or Restriction - in grave and persistent violations, the DICT may
 6         recommend blocking or restriction of access through appropriate regulatory and
 7         judicial mechanisms;

 9         (e) Revocation of Registration - applicable to entities registered with SEC, DTI, or
10         other agencies, subject to due process; and

15          SECTION 9. Exemptions - This Act shall not apply to the following services,
16   provided that they do not meet the definition of a Covered Social Media Service under
17   Section 3 of this Act, and provided further that the determination of coverage shall be
18   based on the actual functions and features of the service, rather than its stated purpose
19   or marketing representation:
20
21         (a) Educational Platforms - digital services primarily designed and operated for
22         instructional, academic, training, or educational purposes, where user interaction
23         is limited to educational or course-related communication, and which do not
24         principally enable public or semi-public user-generated content sharing,
25         algorithmic content feeds, or social networking features;
26
27         (b) Messaging Services - electronic communication services whose primary
28         function is the exchange of direct, private, or group messages between identified
29         users, and which do not principally provide public or semi-public content feeds,
30         algorithmic recommendation systems, content broadcasting mechanisms, or open-
31         ended user discovery features;
32
33         (c) Government-Designated Child Protection or Educational Platforms - digital
34         services officially designated by the Department of Information and
35         Communications Technology (DICT), in coordination with the Department of
36         Education (DepEd) and the Department of Social Welfare and Development
37         (DSWD), as primarily serving child protection, educational, or developmental
38         purposes, pursuant to standards and criteria to be issued in the Implementing
39         Rules and Regulations.
40
41         Provided, that a service otherwise exempted under this Section shall be deemed a
42   Covered Social Media Service, and shall become subject to this Act, if it introduces,
43   enables, or materially expands functionalities that allow:
44
 1                 (1) public or semi-public user-generated content sharing;
 2
 3                 (2) algorithmic content recommendation or ranking systems;
 4
 5                 (3) social networking, follower-based interaction, or content broadcasting
 6                 to undefined audiences.

 8           Provided further, that the DICT shall have the authority, upon notice and due
 9   determination, to reclassify any service as a Covered Social Media Service if its actual

16
17          (a) Responsible Digital Citizenship Education - The Department of Education
18          (DepEd) shall integrate age-appropriate modules on responsible internet use,
19          online safety, digital ethics, and awareness of risks associated with social media
20          use into the basic education curriculum;
21
22          (b) Parental Guidance and Control Awareness - The Department of Information
23          and Communications Technology (DICT), in coordination with the Department of

26          parental control tools, safe internet practices, and informed supervision of

28
29          (c) School-Based Online Safety Programs - DepEd shall ensure the implementation
30          of structured school-based programs, seminars, and orientation activities on online
31          safety, cyber risks, and responsible digital behavior for learners, parents, and

35          agencies shall coordinate with local government units, civil society organizations,
