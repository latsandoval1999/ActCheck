                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Section 15, Article II of the 1987 Constitution provides that the State shall
protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness
among them.1 Alongside this duty, Republic Act No. 9593, otherwise known as the
"Tourism Act of 2009," declares tourism to be an indispensable element of the
national economy and an industry of national interest and importance.2 Medical
tourism lies at the meeting point of the two, and rightly developed it serves both ends
at once: it earns for the economy while building the very facilities, equipment, and
expertise on which Filipino patients themselves depend. This bill accordingly
declares it the policy of the State to responsibly develop, regulate, and promote the
Philippines as a safe, ethical, accessible, and internationally competitive medical
tourism destination, upon the express condition that the growth of the industry shall

       The country's advantages are not in doubt: a large pool of internationally
trained, English-speaking health professionals, competitive costs, and an established
tourism industry. What is missing is a law. The Philippines has no statute and no
lead body for medical tourism, only dispersed agency efforts without a unified
strategy, a common standard of accreditation, or dedicated funding, and the cost of
that omission is measurable. The Department of Tourism itself places the country
third in Southeast Asia and within the global top twenty,3 well behind neighbours
that institutionalised the sector: Malaysia created a national healthcare travel council
in 2005 and grew from 640,000 medical tourists in 2011 to more than a million by
2019,4 while Thailand and India have each paired dedicated promotion with fiscal
incentives and simplified entry for foreign patients. 56 The market they are capturing
is expanding at roughly twelve percent a year. 7 Nor is the opportunity merely
commercial. Foreign patients stay longer and travel with companions, sustaining
employment across health care, transport, accommodation, and allied services, and
the investment they draw finances the equipment, specialty centers, and subspecialty
training that serve Filipino patients and give Filipino professionals reason to build
their careers at home. Properly regulated, medical tourism does not divert the
country's health resources. It enlarges them.

       To these ends, the bill creates the Philippine Medical Tourism Council who
must identify the Priority Areas, the fields in which the country genuinely competes,
so that promotion and public funds are concentrated. It establishes a voluntary
accreditation system, granted for each Priority Area and built on standards of
competence, informed consent, transparent billing, and continuity of care once the
patient goes home, giving Philippine facilities a credential that foreign patients and
insurers can trust. Because those standards must be proportionate to the size of the
facility, dental clinics and day-surgery centers can qualify alongside hospitals.

        The Philippines has long possessed the professional talent, the language, and
 the hospitality for which medical tourists search abroad. What it has lacked is the
 institutional framework to organize these advantages, assure their quality, and
 present them to the world with credibility. This measure supplies that framework,
 and it does so while holding the interests of Filipino patients paramount.

          In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Section 1. Short Title.    This Act shall be known as the "Philippine Medical
Tourism Development Act."

      Section 2. Declaration of Policy.         Pursuant to Section 15, Article II of the
1987 Constitution, which provides that the State shall protect and promote the right
to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them, and consistent

State to responsibly develop, regulate, and promote the Philippines as a safe, ethical,

      Towards this end, the State shall develop and promote the Philippines as a
competitive and trusted destination for medical tourism by strengthening the quality,
safety, accessibility, and international competitiveness of Philippine healthcare
services, particularly in Priority Areas, while ensuring that the development of
medical tourism shall complement and not prejudice the availability and
accessibility of healthcare services for Filipinos.

      Section 3. Definition of Terms.        As used in this Act:

      (a) Accredited Health Facility shall refer to a hospital, infirmary, ambulatory
or day-surgery center, dental clinic, or other health facility or clinic, whether or not
hospital-based and regardless of size or ownership structure, holding a valid
accreditation under Section 5 in respect of one or more Priority Areas;

      (b) Council shall refer to the Philippine Medical Tourism Council created
under Section 4;

      (c) Covered Services shall refer to health care services rendered by an
Accredited Health Facility to a Foreign Patient within a Priority Area covered by its
accreditation;

      (d) Foreign Patient shall refer to a foreign national, or a Filipino citizen
residing abroad, who travels to the Philippines principally to obtain planned health
care services in a Priority Area, and their accompanying dependents; and

      (e) Priority Areas shall refer to the fields of health care identified by the
Council under Section 4.

      Section 4. Philippine Medical Tourism Council.        There is hereby created a
Philippine Medical Tourism Council, attached to the Department of Health (DOH),
which shall be the lead body for the implementation of this Act.

      It shall be composed of the Secretary of Health as Chairperson, the Secretary
of Tourism as Co-Chairperson, and the Secretaries of Finance, Foreign Affairs, and
Trade and Industry, the Commissioner of Immigration, and 3 (three) representatives
from the private health care and tourism sectors as members.

      Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the
Council shall identify and publish as Priority Areas those fields of health care in
which the country possesses recognized expertise and competitive advantage,
considering for this purpose clinical capacity and outcomes, availability of
specialists, international demand, cost competitiveness, and the capacity to serve
Foreign Patients without prejudice to Filipino patients.

      The Council shall also formulate and implement a unified national program
to:

         a. promote Philippine health services in Priority Areas to international
             markets;
         b. encourage investment in health facilities and technology serving
             Priority Areas;
         c. provide training, continuing education, and technical assistance to
             health facilities and their personnel in Priority Areas, including
             assistance to facilities seeking or maintaining accreditation;


                                                                                   3
          d. administer the accreditation system established under this Act; and
          e. ensure streamlined and patient-centered arrangements from pre-trip
             planning through post-treatment follow-up.

      Section 5. Accreditation of Participating Health Facilities.        The Council
shall establish and administer a system for the accreditation of health facilities
providing or actively promoting planned health care services to Foreign Patients in
Priority Areas. The Council shall ensure a simplified accreditation system for
facilities that do not provide in-patient care, or whose services in a Priority Area are
limited in scope or complexity. It shall be granted separately for each Priority Area
and shall be voluntary.

      Section 6. Accreditation Standards.      The Council shall set and periodically
update the standards for accreditation in each Priority Area, which shall cover, at
minimum:

          a. Qualified personnel, facilities, equipment, and protocols appropriate to
             the Priority Area concerned;
          b. Capacity to deliver care consistent with applicable national and
             international standards;
          c. Disclosure, in a language understood by the patient, of the treatment,
             the credentials of the attending professionals, risks, expected outcomes,
             recovery period, total costs, and insurance coverage, sufficient for
             informed consent;
          d. Culturally sensitive and non-discriminatory care;
          e. Transparent billing, payment, and insurance claims arrangements;
          f. Streamlined arrangements for arrival, transportation, accommodation,
             treatment, and follow-up;


                                                                                      4
          g. Discharge instructions, secure transfer of medical records, and
             continuity of care after the patient leaves the Philippines;
          h. Compliance with laws on data privacy and medical confidentiality; and
          i. Compliance with all other applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

      The standards shall be proportionate to the scope, complexity, and risk of the
services offered and to the size of the facility, and shall not impose on facilities
without in-patient care requirements appropriate only to hospitals.

      Section 7. Incentives to Accredited Health Facilities.          Accredited Health
Facilities shall, with respect to Covered Services, be entitled to:

          a. Inclusion in official directories, platforms, and medical promotional
             campaigns of the government;
          b. Priority access to government-supported training and quality-
             improvement programs;
          c. Marketing development assistance from the Council, by way of cost-
             sharing for participation in international trade fairs, health missions,
             and other promotional activities abroad, under such guidelines and
             ceilings as the Council may issue;
          d. An additional deduction from taxable income equivalent to one
             hundred percent (100%) of expenses actually incurred in obtaining,
             renewing, or maintaining accreditation under this Act, as certified by
             the Council; and
          e. Registration with the Board of Investments of the rendering of Covered
             Services as a registered project or activity, which the Council shall
             endorse for inclusion in the Strategic Investment Priority Plan, entitling
             the facility to the incentives available under Title XIII of the National

             Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, including exemption from
             customs duties and from value-added tax on the importation of capital
             equipment directly attributable to the registered project or activity.

       Income and expenses attributable to Covered Services shall be separately
accounted for, and revenue from services rendered to persons other than Foreign
Patients shall not be included in the revenue eligible for incentives under subsection
(e).

       The incentives under subsections (c) and (d) shall be availed for a period of
ten (10) years from the effectivity of this Act; those under subsection (e) shall be
governed by the periods of availment under Title XIII. No expense shall be claimed
twice under this Act and any other law.

       Section 8. Entry and Stay of Foreign Patients. The Bureau of Immigration
and the Department of Foreign Affairs, in coordination with the Council, shall
expedite immigration processing for Foreign Patients, along with their dependents
and caregivers, endorsed by an Accredited Health Facility for treatment in a Priority
Area covered by their accreditation.

       Section 9. Appropriations.         The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
DOH. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary, including a dedicated fund for
international promotion and for the Secretariat, shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

       Section 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Council shall promulgate the necessary
implementing rules and regulations.
