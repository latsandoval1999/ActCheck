                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The integrity of the electoral process is the foundation of a representative democracy.
The right of the people to choose their leaders through free, orderly, honest, peaceful, and
credible elections must be protected through systems that promote accuracy, transparency,
and public confidence.

      Republic Act No. 8436, as amended by Republic Act No. 9369, authorized the
adoption of an Automated Election System (AES) in the Philippines to modernize the
conduct of elections and ensure the efficient counting, consolidation, and transmission of
election results. Since its implementation, automation has significantly improved the speed
by which election results are processed and delivered to the public.

      However, while technological advancements have enhanced the efficiency of
elections, it remains equally important to ensure that transparency and verifiability are
preserved at every stage of the electoral process. Public trust in election results depends not
only on the speed of the system but also on the ability of stakeholders to verify and observe
the accuracy of the results generated.

      This bill seeks to strengthen the Automated Election System by requiring a parallel
precinct-level manual count of votes. This additional safeguard shall serve as a transparency
and verification mechanism while maintaining the benefits of automated counting,
consolidation, and transmission of election results.

      By providing an additional layer of accountability, the measure seeks to reinforce
public confidence in the electoral process and uphold the fundamental principle that election
results must accurately reflect the genuine will of the Filipino people.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

        AN ACT AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8436, ENTITLED "AN ACT
          AUTHORIZING THE COMMISSION ON ELECTIONS TO USE AN
        AUTOMATED ELECTION SYSTEM IN THE MAY 11, 1998 NATIONAL
         OR LOCAL ELECTIONS AND IN SUBSEQUENT NATIONAL AND
        LOCAL ELECTORAL EXERCISES, PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR
        AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”, AS AMENDED BY REPUBLIC ACT
           NO. 9369, PROVIDING FOR A PARALLEL PRECINCT-LEVEL
          MANUAL COUNT OF VOTES IN THE AUTOMATED ELECTION
                     SYSTEM AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:


     SECTION 1. “Section 1 of Republic Act No. 8436 is amended to read as follows:

     “SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to ensure free,
     orderly, honest, peaceful, credible and informed elections, plebiscites, referenda,
     recall and other similar electoral exercises by improving on the election process and
     adopting systems which shall involve the use of an automated election system that
     will ensure the secrecy and sanctity of the ballot and all election documents, provide
     for the automated counting, consolidation, and transmission of election results, and
     establish a parallel precinct-level manual count of votes to promote transparency
     and credibility, in order that the results shall be fast, accurate, and reflective of the
     genuine will of the people.”


     SEC. 2. “Section 2 of Republic Act No. 8436 is amended to read as follows:

     “SEC.2. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act, the following terms shall mean:

     “XXX”
      "4. Election returns – a document in electronic and printed form directly produced by
      the counting or voting machine, and in manually-written tally and total number of
      votes, showing the date of the election, the province, municipality and the precinct
      in which it is held and the votes in figures for each candidate in a precinct in areas
      where AES is utilized;”

      SEC. 3. ”Section 21 of Republic Act No. 8436 is amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 21. Counting Procedure. - The Commission shall prescribe the manner and
      procedure for the expedited parallel precinct-level manual counting of votes
      recorded in manually-prepared election returns in addition to the automatic counting
      of votes generated under the automated system: Provided, That apart from the
      electronically stored result, thirty (30) copies of the election return are printed."


      SEC. 4. “Section 22 of Republic Act No. 8436 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 22. Election Returns. – Both the Precinct Manual Count Election Returns and
      the printed election returns shall bear appropriate control marks to determine the
      time and place of printing. Each copy shall be signed and thumb marked by all the
      members of the board of election inspectors and the watchers present. If any
      member of the board of election inspectors present refuses to sign, the chairman of
      the board shall note the same in each copy of the printed election returns. The
      member of the board of election inspectors concerned refusing to sign shall be
      compelled to explain his or her refusal to do so. Failure to explain an unjustifiable
      refusal to sign each copy of the printed election return by any member of the board
      of election inspectors shall be punishable as provided in this Act. The chairman of
      the board shall then publicly read and announce the total number of registered
      voters, the total number of voters who actually voted and the total number of votes
      obtained by each candidate based on the election returns.”

      “XXX”

      "The election returns transmitted electronically and digitally signed shall be
      considered as official election results and shall be used as the basis for the
      canvassing of votes and the proclamation of a candidate. Provided, that in case of
      variance in any manual election returns vis a vis the corresponding electronically
      printed election returns, the Commission, motu proprio or upon motion of any
      interested party, shall proceed to conduct summary verification to reconcile the
      results; provided further, that should the variance be substantial, the Commission
      shall motu proprio suspend the proclamation of the winning candidate.”

        SEC. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations - Within ninety (90) days from the
