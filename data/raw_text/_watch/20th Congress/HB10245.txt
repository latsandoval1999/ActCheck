                                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Agriculture remains a critical pillar of the Philippine economy, yet small farmers
and fisherfolk continue to face persistent structural constraints. Despite existing
financing mandates under Republic Act No. 11901, known as the The Agriculture,
Fisheries And Rural Development Financing Enhancement Act Of 2022, many rural
producers still lack access to affordable, appropriate, and well-integrated financial
services. Credit is often extended as stand-alone lending, disconnected from markets,
postharvest systems, insurance, and other support necessary for sustainable income
generation.

       Recent data underscore the urgency of reform. Farmers and fisherfolk remain
among the poorest sectors in the country, with poverty incidence rates significantly
higher than the national average.1 At the same time, Philippine agriculture continues to
face productivity challenges, climate risks, and supply chain inefficiencies, all of which
weaken the ability of borrowers to repay loans and discourage financial institutions from
deeper engagement in the sector.

       This measure seeks to address these gaps by strengthening smallholder
agricultural value chain financing (AVCF) within the framework of RA 11901. Rather
than creating a new system, the bill operationalizes what the law already envisions:
financing that is linked to the full agricultural cycle—from input supply and production
to aggregation, processing, and market access.

       International experience demonstrates that this approach is more effective than
traditional credit models. The Food and Agriculture Organization notes that value chain

       The proposed amendments are likewise consistent with developments across
ASEAN. Regional frameworks recognize that smallholder farmers must be integrated
into value chains involving cooperatives, agribusinesses, financial institutions, and
institutional buyers. The Association of Southeast Asian Nations has explicitly
emphasized responsible and inclusive agricultural investment that connects smallholders
to markets and supply chains.4

       In practice, several ASEAN countries have already moved in this direction.
Vietnam has supported value chain-based financing arrangements linked to export-
oriented agricultural sectors such as rice and coffee, enabling farmers to access credit
backed by supply contracts and market demand.5 Indonesia has expanded agricultural
financing programs that incorporate cooperatives, off-takers, and agribusiness firms,
improving credit access through structured market linkages. 6 Thailand, through its
specialized agricultural bank, has long combined credit with marketing support,
cooperative strengthening, and value chain development.7 These experiences show that
integrating finance with markets and support services improves both productivity and
repayment performance.

       This bill builds on these lessons by clarifying definitions, expanding eligible
financing activities, promoting market linkage arrangements, strengthening reporting
requirements, and supporting capacity-building for farmers’ organizations, cooperatives,
and rural financial institutions. It also encourages the use of digital payments, insurance,

        SEC. 10. Monitoring and Review. - The ACPC, in coordination with the BSP, DA,
DAR, LBP, DBP, PCIC, CDA, and other relevant agencies, shall conduct a periodic review
of the implementation of smallholder agricultural value chain financing under this Act.
The review shall assess whether financing programs are improving access to credit,
market access, repayment capacity, insurance coverage, savings participation, and
income outcomes among rural community beneficiaries.

     The results of the review shall be included in the annual report submitted to
Congress under Section 8 of Republic Act No. 11901, as amended.

        SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the BSP, in consultation with the ACPC, DA, DAR, LBP, DBP, PCIC,
CDA, banking industry, rural financial institutions, microfinance institutions,
cooperatives, farmers’ and fisherfolk’s organizations, and other relevant stakeholders,
shall amend or supplement the implementing rules and regulations of Republic Act No.
11901 to carry out the provisions of this Act.

        SEC. 12. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

       SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 14. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
