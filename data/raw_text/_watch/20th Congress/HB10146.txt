                          AN ACT
  PROVIDING A FREE CHILDCARE CENTER IN BARANGAY FAIRVIEW,
        QUEZON CITY, AND PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State recognizes its constitutional duty to defend and strengthen
the right of children to assistance, including proper care and nutrition, and
special protection from all forms of neglect, abuse, cruelty, exploitation, and
other conditions prejudicial to their development.

       As such, the government enacted into law the Republic Act 6972,
otherwise known as the Barangay-Level Total Development and Protection of
Children Act, as well as signed the ratification of the Convention on the Rights
of the Child in 1990.

       While these laws support the welfare of children, there is currently a gap
in existing legislation that needs to be filled as regards the well-being of the
parents of infants and toddlers who are struggling to find a balance between
caring for their children while pursuing their respective careers.

       It has been the primary goal of existing barangay daycare centers to
prepare the children for formal schooling. With said primary goal in mind,
existing barangay daycare centers have since only accommodated children
between the ages of three years and four years and eleven months, despite
being intended for all children below the age of five.

      As a result of placing a minimum age to avail of the services of the
designated barangay daycare centers, the parents of children below the age of
three are forced to request help from their family members, if not hire domestic
helpers to care for their child, while those who are not as fortunate are forced
to stay at home and neglect their careers.
      Recent World Bank and International Labor Organization estimates
show that female labor force participation in the country remains at around
49% as of 2024-2025, reflecting minimal improvement over the past decades.
Despite gains in education, a persistent gender gap of over 20 percentage
points compared to men continues to characterize the Philippine labor market.

       A large proportion of women are held back from gainful employment
opportunities due to responsibilities to their respective families, more
particularly, the caring of their children between the time they are born until
two years of age1. For women who cannot afford the cost of early childhood
care, the most convenient option is to forego any economic opportunities to
care of their infants and toddlers.

       As the disparity in women’s participation in the labor sector has been a
constant hurdle in the State’s goal of achieving gender equality, the present
bill seeks to address this gap by providing a free childcare center to women
and parents or guardians who are solo-parents, indigent, temporarily
displaced, and/or are looking for job opportunities in Brgy. Fairview, Quezon
City. By establishing such infrastructure, infants and toddlers are given the
care they are entitled to while giving their parents a fair chance to advance
their respective careers.

      It is the hope of this bill that the Philippines will eventually close the
gender disparities in the country by providing full support to childcaring
services. It is the intention of this bill to afford safe and nurturing spaces for
infants and toddlers in our District, while giving the parents peace of mind as
they work for a brighter future for their children.

        In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is urgently sought.

                          AN ACT
  PROVIDING A FREE CHILDCARE CENTER IN BARANGAY FAIRVIEW,
        QUEZON CITY, AND PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Barangay Fairview
Childcare Center Act”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to recognize
the sanctity of family life and to protect and strengthen the family as a basic
autonomous social institution.

Further, under Article XV Section 3 (2) of the Constitution, the State is
responsible for defending the right of children to assistance, including proper
care and nutrition, and special protection from all forms of neglect, abuse,
cruelty, exploitation, and other conditions prejudicial to their development.

SECTION 3. Establishing a Free Childcare Center in Barangay Fairview,
Quezon City. – There is hereby established a free childcare center (hereinafter
“Center”) in Barangay Fairview, Quezon City, for all qualified parents and/or
legal guardians with children below three (3) years old, who are not yet fit for
daycare centers. The Center may be established in the barangay hall or a
separate facility equipped with basic facilities and shall be manned by trained
and equipped personnel from the local government unit.

Provided, that the Center shall be under the control and supervision of the
local government, which shall likewise ensure a sustainable program for the
children, the administrative personnel, and the Center. The LGU may call
upon private volunteers, who are responsible members of the community, and
utilize them to assist in the care of children and provide consultative services
for medical, educational, and other needs of the children;
Provided further, that the LGU personnel and private volunteers undergo or
have undergone the selection process and obtain all qualifications which have
been set by the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD).

SECTION 4. Free Childcare Services. – The Free Childcare Services shall
ensure that:

   1) Parents and/or legal guardians who are indigent, solo-parents, or
      temporarily unemployed shall be afforded safe, adequate, and proper
      childcare for their children while they are at work or looking for work;
   2) Children below three years old of qualified working parents and/or legal
      guardians are entitled to the following:

         a. Enhancement of the physical, social, emotional, cognitive, and
            language development;
         b. Proper and adequate feeding and sustenance;
         c. Safe and secure space fit for childcare and development;

SECTION 5. Functions of the Department of Social Welfare and
Development. – The Department shall formulate the criteria for the selection
and qualification of:

         a. the childcare workers employed by the LGU and the private
            volunteers called upon by the LGU; and
         b. parents who may avail of the services of the childcare facility

Further, the Department shall formulate the minimum standards for childcare
services and facilities to be implemented by the LGU;

Finally, to safeguard the proper implementation of the program, the DSWD
shall conduct regular monitoring and set up a feedback mechanism for the
program.

SECTION 6. Funding. – The funds for the establishment of the childcare
center shall be appropriated from the national budget and shall be included
in the General Appropriations Act as part of the budget of the Department of
Social Welfare and Development. The maintenance of the Center established
shall be the responsibility and shall be charged to the barangay. The LGUs
concerned, in consultation with the DSWD, shall set aside from any available
local revenue an amount deemed appropriate as annual counterpart fund to
support the maintenance and operation of the Center herein established.

SECTION 7. Non-Impairment Clause. – Nothing in this Act shall be
construed as to diminish, impair, or repeal rights recognized, granted, or
made available to disadvantaged, marginalized, or specific sectors under
existing laws.
SECTION 8. Separability Clause. If any provision or section of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions and sections not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, or issuances or any part thereof which are inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 10. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or any newspaper of general circulation.
