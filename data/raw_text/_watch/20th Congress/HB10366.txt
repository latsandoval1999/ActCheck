                        AN ACT
  PROTECTING CONSUMERS FROM UNFAIR, DECEPTIVE, AND
  ABUSIVE ONLINE LENDING PRACTICES, ESTABLISHING THE
VICTIM COMPENSATION BOARD FOR ONLINE LENDING ABUSES
 FOR THE PURPOSE, PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS
           THEREOF, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

The rapid growth of digital technology has created a new market for money
lending in the Philippines. Through mobile applications, websites, and digital
installment platforms, Filipinos gain easy access to emergency funds as
needed. With just a phone or mobile device and genuine identification, any
Filipino can loan from recognized online lending platforms. For many Filipinos
in need, online lending has become an accessible and convenient source of
funds.

The online lending market is not entirely unregulated. Existing laws already
provide an important baseline framework for the operation of lending
businesses and the protection of borrowers. Republic Act No. 3765, or the Truth
in Lending Act, requires creditors to disclose all charges and costs of the loan,
including interest rates, charges, and the total amount payable. This
information is given to enable borrowers to make informed financial decisions.
Republic Act No. 9474, or the Lending Company Regulation Act of 2007,
governs the establishment and operation of lending companies and requires
their registration and supervision by the Securities and Exchange
Commission. Republic Act No. 11765, or the Financial Products and Services
Consumer Protection Act, imposes standards of fair dealing, transparency,
responsible pricing, and consumer protection in the offering of financial

products and services. Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012,
regulates the collection, processing, storage, use, disclosure, and protection of
personal information or sensitive personal information, including borrower
information obtained through digital lending platforms.

However, the digital lending market still remains vulnerable to abuse. Despite
the existing laws and other regulations, predatory practices continue to persist
on a disturbing scale.1 These include threats, the use of obscene or abusive
language, public shaming, the dissemination of personal information,
fabricated legal notices, and the use of borrowers' contact lists or private data
to intimidate family members, friends, co-workers, and employers.2 Borrowers
remain exposed to excessive and unconscionable interest rates, deceptive
pricing structures, hidden service charges, and loan designs that trap them in
cycles of rollover and over-indebtedness.

The continued proliferation of unregistered online lending applications further
aggravates the problem. According to the Cybercrime Investigation and
Coordinating Center, from January to February 2026 alone, more than ₱8
million has already been lost from the pockets of citizens due to abusive and
fraudulent online lending applications.3

In light of these troubling circumstances, the law must evolve to address the
realities of modern credit delivery. It is no longer sufficient to regulate
electronic transactions and to require only formal disclosures. The State must
also ensure that digital loan or lending products are fair in structure,
transparent in cost, proportionate in their collection and use of personal
information, and humane in enforcement.

Further, individuals most affected by abusive online lending practices are
frequently those with limited financial resources and financial literacy. Among
them are domestic workers, construction workers, vendors, and drivers. Given
their limited access to legal and financial information, it is imperative that

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

Section 1. Short Title. - This Act will be known as the “Anti-Online Lending
Abuse Act.”

                                 CHAPTER I

                         GENERAL PROVISIONS

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. —It is the policy of the State that:

   a. The dignity, privacy, and financial welfare of borrowers must be
      protected from abusive, deceptive, and predatory practices in the
      provision and collection of loans.
   b. Online lending services must operate within a fair, transparent, and
      accountable regulatory framework.
   c. Effective safeguards must be in place against unlawful debt collection
      practices and the misuse of personal information or sensitive personal
      information.
   d. Online lending platforms must promote financial inclusion while
      adhering to consumer protection standards and responsible lending
      practices.


                                                                              1
Section 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall mean:

a. Borrower - refers to any natural person who obtains or applies for a
  loan through an online lending platform.

b. Collection agent - refers to any natural or juridical person authorized,
  contracted, or engaged by an online lender to collect payments,
  communicate with borrowers regarding outstanding obligations, or
  otherwise perform debt collection activities on behalf of the online lender.

c. Covered online lending activities - refer to the offering, marketing,
  facilitation, origination, servicing, or collection of loans through an
  online lending platform where the borrower resides within the
  Philippines.

d. Hidden charges - refer to any fee, charge, or cost imposed in connection
  with a loan that is not clearly and fully disclosed to the borrower prior to
  the execution of the loan agreement or prior to the disbursement of the
  loan proceeds.

e. Online lender - refers to any natural or juridical person or entity
  engaged in the business of extending credit through an online lending
  platform, including lending or financing companies or any entity that
  holds itself out to the public as offering or facilitating loans through
  digital means.

f. Online lending platform - refers to any mobile lending application,
  website, or other financial technology-enabled program or system where
  the services and products of a financing or lending company are made
  available.

g. Personal information - refers to any information whether recorded in
  a material form or not, from which the identity of an individual is
  apparent or can be reasonably and directly ascertained by the entity
  holding the information, or when put together with other information
  would directly and certainly identify an individual.

h. Sensitive personal information refers to personal information:




                                                                            2
            (1) About an individual’s race, ethnic origin, marital status, age,
            color, and religious, philosophical or political affiliations;

            (2) About an individual’s health, education, genetic or sexual life
            of a person, or to any proceeding for any offense committed or
            alleged to have been committed by such person, the disposal of
            such proceedings, or the sentence of any court in such proceedings;

            (3) Issued by government agencies peculiar to an individual which
            includes, but not limited to, social security numbers, previous or
            current health records, licenses or its denials, suspension or
            revocation, and tax returns; and

            (4) Specifically established by an executive order or an act of
            Congress to be kept classified.

Section 4. Scope and Coverage. - The provisions of this Act apply to all
covered online lending activities.

This Act does not apply to banks and financial institutions regulated by the
Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), except insofar as such institutions operate
online lending platforms that function independently from their regulated
banking activities or that are offered through separate digital lending
applications.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to prevent or limit the exercise of
regulatory authority over online lending activities by the Securities and
Exchange Commission (SEC), the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI),
the National Privacy Commission (NPC), or any other relevant agencies.

                                 CHAPTER II

                       RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS

Section 5. Rights of Borrowers. - Borrowers have the following rights:

   a. Right to Transparent Loan Terms. - The borrower has the right to clear
      and transparent loan terms, including but not limited to the duration of
      the loan agreement, the applicable interest rate, the mode and schedule
      of payment, and all applicable fees and charges.




                                                                              3
   b. Right to Data Transparency and Protection. - The borrower has the right
      to be informed, in concise language they are familiar with, of the purpose
      of the collection of their personal information or sensitive personal
      information, the manner in which such data will be stored or processed,
      and the identity of the entity responsible for storing or processing such
      data. In no case shall the data be disseminated to collection agents or
      any other third party without the borrower’s express lawful consent,
      except as may be authorized by law.

   c. Right to Proper Accounting of Obligations. - The borrower has the right
      to receive, before and during the loan, a clear and itemized disclosure of
      all payments due, including the principal, interest rate, penalties, and
      all other charges imposed in a manner that allows full understanding of
      the cost of credit.

   d. Right to Fair and Dignified Debt Collection. - The borrower has the right
      to be treated with dignity and respect in the collection of debts and shall
      not be subjected to any acts of harassment, coercion, public shaming,
      threats, intimidation, or other abusive collection practices.

   e. Right to a Cap on Total Loan Cost. - In no case shall a borrower be
      required to pay, in the aggregate, interest, fees, charges, penalties, and
      any other amounts and hidden charges incident to the loan in an amount
      exceeding one hundred percent (100%) of the original principal actually
      extended to and received by the borrower. Accordingly, the total amount
      payable by the borrower for any online loan, inclusive of principal and
      all interest, fees, charges, penalties, and other amounts and hidden
      charges, shall not exceed two hundred percent (200%) of the original
      principal. Any stipulation or collection in excess thereof is void, and the
      excess must be refunded or credited in favor of the borrower.

Section 6. Duties and Responsibilities of Lenders. - Online lenders shall
observe the following duties and responsibilities:

   a. Duty of Fair Dealing. - Online lenders shall deal fairly and in good faith
      with borrowers at all stages of the lending transaction, including
      marketing, loan approval, disbursement, servicing, and collection. They
      shall not employ any false, misleading, deceptive, or unfair act or
      omission that is likely to mislead or prejudice a borrower.

                                                                               4
   b. Duty of Full Disclosure. - Prior to the consummation of any online loan,
      every online lender shall clearly and conspicuously disclose to the
      borrower, in concise language that is understandable to the borrower
      and in an accessible format, the true cost of credit. The disclosure must
      include the stated interest rate and all fees, charges, penalties,
      deductions, or other costs or hidden charges required as a condition for
      the approval, disbursement, renewal, restructuring, servicing, or
      collection of the loan.

   c. Duty to Assess Ability to Repay. - Online lenders must establish and
      implement reasonable procedures, including automated checks where
      appropriate, to determine prior to loan approval whether the borrower
      has the capacity to repay the loan without suffering serious financial
      hardship. The extent of such assessment should be proportionate to the
      size, duration, and repayment structure of the loan, as well as the
      borrower’s borrowing pattern.

   d. Duty to Obtain Informed Consent. - Online lenders must obtain the
      borrower’s express and informed consent to all material loan terms, and
      such consent should be recorded and made retrievable for verification.
      Where personal information or sensitive personal information is
      collected, the lender shall clearly disclose the purpose of such collection,
      using concise language the borrower is familiar with, and shall ensure
      that the data will not be disclosed to any collection agent or third party
      without the express lawful consent of the borrower, except as may be
      required by law.

                                CHAPTER III

                     PROHIBITED AND UNLAWFUL ACTS

Section 7. Prohibited Acts in Online Lending - It shall be unlawful for any
online lender or any director, trustee, officer, partner, employee, agent, or
third-party service provider thereof to commit or cause the commission of any
of the following acts:

   a. Operating without a license;

   b. Misrepresentation regarding the interest, fees, charges, penalties, or any
      hidden charges on an online loan; or

                                                                                5
   c. Rollover or automatic extensions without the borrower’s prior specific,
      informed, and express consent given after full disclosure of the revised
      loan terms.

Any automatic rollover or renewal made in violation of this Section is void,
without prejudice to the borrower’s liability for the unpaid principal and lawful
charges under the original loan agreement.

Any permissible extension or refinancing shall require a separate and
affirmative act of consent by the borrower, preceded by clear disclosure of the
total amount due, the additional interest, fees, charges, penalties, and hidden
charges to be imposed, the new due date or schedule of payments, and the
borrower’s right to refuse the rollover and instead settle the obligation under
the original terms or avail of lawful restructuring options.

Section 8. Abusive collection methods - It shall be unlawful for any person
or entity, including the online lender or its collection agent, to commit,
authorize, tolerate, or cause the commission of any act of harassment,
intimidation, coercion, public humiliation, or other abusive conduct against a
borrower in connection with the payment or collection of an online loan,
including the following:

       1.   Calling, messaging, chatting with, or otherwise making contact
            with the borrower outside business hours from eight o’clock (8:00)
            in the morning to five o’clock (5:00) in the afternoon;

       2.   Public shaming or humiliation;

       3.   Unauthorized access, use, disclosure, or processing of personal
            information or sensitive personal information;

       4.   Calling, messaging, chatting with, or otherwise making contact
            with any person other than the borrower in connection with the
            borrower’s debt, except solely for the limited purpose of obtaining
            or verifying the borrower’s contact information or address; or

       5.   Threatening the borrower, his or her relatives, or any of the
            borrower’s contacts, verbally or in writing, including through text
            messages or social media, with criminal charges or prosecution,
            including estafa, swindling, and other deceits under the Revised


                                                                               6
            Penal Code or any other special laws, if it can be reasonably
            inferred that the same will not prosper.

                                  CHAPTER IV

                   VICTIM COMPENSATION BOARD

Section 9. Creation of the Board. There is hereby created a Victim
Compensation Board for Online Lending Abuses (VCBOLA), hereinafter
referred to as the “Board,” which shall be attached to the SEC for
administrative supervision.

Section 10. Composition of the Board. - The Board shall be composed of
the following:

   a. The SEC Chairperson as Chairperson of the Board;

   b. The DTI Secretary, or an Undersecretary;

   c. The Department of Information and Communications Technology
      (DICT) Secretary, or an Undersecretary;

   d. The NPC Commissioner, or a Deputy Commissioner;

   e. The BSP Governor, or a Deputy Governor;

   f. One (1) representative from a consumer protection organization; and

   g. One (1) representative from the Integrated Bar of the Philippines (IBP).

Section 11. Powers and Functions. — The Board shall have the following
powers and functions:

   a. Receive and adjudicate claims for monetary compensation filed by
      borrowers who are victims of unlawful, abusive, deceptive, or predatory
      online lending practices;

   b. Determine the entitlement of borrowers to compensation, including
      actual damages, moral damages, exemplary damages, and other
      appropriate reliefs, based on substantial evidence;

   c. Order restitution or refund of unlawfully collected amounts in favor of a
      borrower, including interest, fees, penalties, charges, and other hidden
      charges imposed in violation of this Act;

                                                                             7
   d. Administer the Victim Compensation Fund as provided under this Act;

   e. Issue subpoenas and require the production of documents, digital
      records, and other relevant evidence;

   f. Require the assistance of government agencies for the enforcement of its
      orders; and

   g. Promulgate rules of procedure for the expeditious disposition of claims.

Section 12. Victim Compensation Fund. — A Victim Compensation Fund
is hereby established, to be administered by the Board, which shall be used
exclusively for the payment of approved claims.

The Fund shall be sourced from:

   a. Fines, penalties, and administrative sanctions collected under this Act;

   b. A mandatory contribution equivalent to one percent (1%) of gross loan
      disbursements from all registered online lenders, as may be adjusted by
      the SEC;

   c. Proceeds from forfeited assets of entities found to have engaged in illegal
      online lending operations; and

   d. Such appropriations as may be provided by Congress.

Section 13. Eligibility and Filing of Claims. — Any borrower who has
suffered loss, injury, harassment, unauthorized processing of personal
information or sensitive personal information, or financial damage arising
from a violation of this Act may file a claim before the Board within three (3)
years from the occurrence of the violation.

Section 14. Nature of Proceedings. — Proceedings before the Board shall be
summary and non-litigious. They shall not be strictly bound by technical rules
of evidence, provided that administrative due process is observed.

Section 15. Relief and Awards. — The Board may grant:

   a. Refund of excess payments;

   b. Compensation for actual financial loss;




                                                                               8
   c. Moral and exemplary damages in cases involving harassment, coercion,
      or public shaming;

   d. Attorney’s fees;

   e. Cease-and-desist orders;

   f. Protection orders; and

   g. Such other reliefs as may be just and equitable for the protection of
      borrowers.

Section 16. Enforceability. — Decisions of the Board shall be final and
executory after fifteen (15) days from notice to the parties. A final decision may
be enforced by way of a writ of execution.

Section 17. Non-Exclusivity of Remedy. — Recourse to the Board shall not
preclude the filing of civil, criminal, or administrative actions under existing
laws: Provided, That the borrower cannot recover damages twice for the same
act or omission.

                                 CHAPTER V

                      PENALTIES AND LIABILITIES

Section 18. Criminal Liability. Any person who violates the provisions of
this Act shall, upon conviction, be punished with imprisonment of not less than
three (3) years but not more than six (6) years or a fine of not less than One
hundred thousand pesos (₱100,000.00) but not more than One million pesos
(₱1,000,000.00), or both at the discretion of the court. If the harassment leads
to severe psychological injury, loss of livelihood, or loss of life, the court may
impose double the penalties provided herein.

If the offender is a corporation, partnership, or other entity, the penalty shall
be imposed upon the responsible officers, directors, partners, or agents.

If the offender made use of an unlicensed or unregistered online lending
platform in the commission of the offense, the maximum penalty of
imprisonment of six (6) years and a fine of One million pesos (₱1,000,000.00)
shall be imposed.

Section 19. Administrative Sanctions. The SEC and the BSP may suspend
or permanently revoke the registration or certificate of authority of an online
                                                                                9
lender found to have committed a violation of this Act or its implementing rules
and regulations.

The NPC may also impose fines for any violation of the Data Privacy Act of
2012, including the malicious act of publicly revealing personal information or
sensitive personal information and the unauthorized harvesting of contact
lists.

Section 20. Civil Liability and Borrower Redress.

   a. Any provision in a loan agreement found to be unfair, including
         unconscionable interest rates, hidden charges, or automatic rollovers
         without prior specific, informed, and express consent shall be rendered
         void and unenforceable.

   b. Any loan agreement entered into by an unlicensed or unregistered online
         lending platform shall be deemed unenforceable.

   c. The courts shall have the power to reopen or set aside unjust
         transactions where the online lender failed to assess if the loan was
         affordable or suitable for the borrower.

Section 21. Intelligence Gathering. The Presidential Anti-Organized Crime
Commission (PAOCC), or its organizational successor-in-interest, is mandated
to actively gather intelligence on online lending platforms for the speedy
investigation and prosecution of unlicensed or unregistered online lenders in
the Philippines. Solely for this purpose, the Philippine National Police, the
National Bureau of Investigation, the DICT, and the SEC are mandated to
maintain a secure, real-time, and interoperable information-sharing system
with PAOCC, which shall include the sharing of information based on
complaints and reports received by the agencies. PAOCC is also authorized to
coordinate with and enlist the assistance of any department, agency, or
instrumentality of government, including government-owned and -controlled
corporations, local government units, the private sector, and civil society
organizations for the purpose of intelligence gathering under this Section.

Section 22. Cease-and-Desist Order. Upon its finding that an online lending
platform is being operated by an online lender in the Philippines without the
registration, license, certificate of authority, or other authorization required by
law, the SEC shall issue a cease-and-desist order and serve the same upon the

                                                                                10
application marketplace, internet intermediary, or other service provider
facilitating the availability of such online lending platform in the Philippines.
The SEC shall likewise coordinate with the DICT and relevant agencies to
cause the delisting, removal, disabling, blocking, or restriction of access to such
online lending platform.

                                 CHAPTER VI

                             FINAL PROVISIONS

Section. 23. Appropriations. – This Act will be implemented initially within
the framework of the budgets of the SEC, BSP, DTI, DICT, and NPC, for the
year it is enacted into law. Thereafter, the amounts as may be necessary for
the implementation of this Act will be included in the budget of these
government agencies in the annual General Appropriations Act.

Section. 24. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the SEC, BSP, DTI, DICT, NPC, PAOCC,
and representatives of consumer protection organizations must promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations to implement its provisions.

Section 25. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, other parts or provisions thereof not
affected thereby will continue to be in full force and effect.

Section 26. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, rules, and regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.

Section 27. Effectivity. – This Act takes effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
