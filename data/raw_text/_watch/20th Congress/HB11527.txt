20                                              EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   The Philippines is a home to many unique and threatened wildlife species. However,
23   wildlife continues to be threatened by illegal hunting, collection, transport, trade, and
24   destruction of their habitats. While Republic Act No. 9147, or the Wildlife Resources
25   Conservation and Protection Act, already provides the current basic law for the
26   protection and conservation of wildlife, the changing nature and increasing scale of
27   wildlife crimes require stronger penalties and better enforcement measures.
28
29   Hence, this proposed measure now seeks to strengthen and update the existing wildlife
30   protection law, particularly by providing clearer and heavier penalties for serious
31   violations. The bill also adds important enforcement measures, including presumptions
32   in certain wildlife cases, stronger administrative powers to confiscate and forfeit wildlife
33   and equipment used in violations, Wildlife Enforcement Agents, Wildlife Traffic
34   Monitoring Units, and a wildlife law enforcement information system.
35
36   Through these measures, the bill seeks to provide stronger penalties, better
37   enforcement, and greater government support for the protection and conservation of
38   Philippine wildlife.
39
40   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

20                                  AN ACT
21        PROVIDING STRONGER MEASURES FOR THE CONSERVATION AND
22       PROTECTION OF WILDLIFE RESOURCES AND THEIR HABITATS, AND
23       APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, REPEALING FOR THE PURPOSE
24     REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9147 OR THE “WILDLIFE RESOURCES CONSERVATION
25                           AND PROTECTION ACT”
26
27
28   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
29   Congress assembled:
30
31        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Revised Wildlife
32   Resources Conservation and Protection Act”.
33
34          SECTION. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It shall be the policy of the State to
35   conserve and protect the country's wildlife resources and their habitats for sustainability
36   and to promote ecological balance, enhance biological diversity, and ensure the
37   provision of ecosystem services. In the pursuit of this policy, this Act shall have the
38   following objectives:
39
40           (a) Regulate the collection, possession, use, and trade of wildlife, wildlife by-
41           products and derivatives;
42
43           (b) Pursue, with due regard to the national interest, Philippine commitments
44           to international conventions, treaties, and agreements on the protection of
45           wildlife and their habitats, and on the fair and equitable sharing of the
46           benefits from the lawful utilization of wildlife genetic resources;
47
48           (c) Initiate or support scientific studies on the conservation of biological
49           diversity and sustainable utilization of wildlife resources.
50
51           (d) Promote full awareness and widest dissemination of policies on wildlife
52           conservation and protection; and
 1          (e) Strengthen the global effort to eradicate the transnational organized crimeof
 2          wildlife trade and trafficking.
 3
 4          SECTION. 3. Scope of Application. – This Act applies to all wildlife species
 5   found in all areas of the country, including exotic species which are subject to
 6   trade, are bred or cultured in captivity or propagated in the country.
 7
 8          SECTION. 4. Jurisdiction of the Department of Environment and Natural
 9   Resources, the Department of Agriculture, and the Palawan Council for
10   Sustainable Development. – The Department of Environment and Natural Resources
11   (DENR) shall have jurisdiction over all terrestrial plant and animal species including
12   turtles and tortoises, wetland species, including crocodiles, waterbirds and all
13   amphibians, and the Dugong Dugon or Dugong. The Department of Agriculture (DA)
14   shall have jurisdiction over all declared aquatic critical habitats, all aquatic resources
15   including all fishes, aquatic plants, invertebrates and all marine mammals, except the
16   Dugong. The Secretaries of the DENR and the DA shall review and, by joint
17   administrative order, revise and regularly update the list of species under their
18   respective jurisdictions and make the list readily available to all concerned government
19   agencies and local government units. In the Province of Palawan, the jurisdiction is
20   vested on the Palawan Council for Sustainable Development (PCSD) pursuant to
21   Republic Act (RA) No. 7611, otherwise known as the “Strategic Environmental Plan
22   (SEP) for Palawan Act”.
23

48                           CHAPTER II
49        CONSERVATION AND PROTECTION OF WILDLIFE RESOURCES

51                                         ARTICLE I
 1                                       General Provisions
 2
 3       SECTION. 6. Wildlife Information. – All activities described in this Chapter shall
 4   be authorized by the DENR, DA, or PCSD upon proper evaluation of best available
 5   information or scientific data showing that the activity or its intended purpose is not
 6   detrimental to the survival of the species or subspecies involved and their habitat.
 7   For this purpose, the DENR, DA, or PCSD shall regularly update wildlife information
 8   through research.
 9
10        The DENR, DA, and PCSD shall establish their respective National Wildlife
11   Management Committee (NWMC) to provide technical and scientific advice on
12   wildlife conservation and protection. Each NWMC shall be composed of
13   representatives from the DENR, DA, or PCSD, other concerned government
14   agencies, and local scientists with expertise on various fields of discipline on wildlife.
15   The NWMC shall invite other stakeholders as resource persons and other experts,
16   when necessary. The DENR, DA, or PCSD shall each designate the Chairpersons
17   of their respective NWMCs.
18
19       The DENR and DA shall establish a Regional Wildlife Management Committee
20   in every administrative region to provide technical and scientific advice on wildlife
21   conservation and protection to DENR and DA field offices.
22
23       SECTION. 7. Biosafety – All activities dealing with genetic engineering and
24   pathogenic organisms in the Philippines, as well as activities requiring the
25   importation, introduction, field release and breeding of organisms shall be reviewed
26   by the DENR, DA, or PCSD in accordance with the biosafety guidelines designed to
27   ensure public welfare and the protection and conservation of wildlife and their
28   habitats.
29
30       SECTION. 8. Economically Important Species. – The DENR, DA, and PCSD
31   shall establish a list of economically-important species. Where appropriate, a
32   population assessment of such species shall be conducted within a reasonable
33   period and shall be regularly reviewed and updated.
34
35       The collection of certain species shall only be allowed when the results of the
36   assessment show that, despite certain extent of collection, the population of such
37   species can still remain viable and capable of recovering its numbers. For this
38   purpose, the DENR, DA, and PCSD shall establish a schedule and volume of
39   allowable harvests.
40
41   Whenever an economically important species becomes threatened, any form of
42   collection thereof shall be prohibited except for scientific, educational, or breeding or
43   propagation purposes, pursuant to the provisions of this Act.
44
45        SECTION. 9. Invasive Alien Species. – The DENR, DA, and PCSD shall
46   manage IAS and formulate a National Invasive Alien Species Strategy and Action
47   Plan (NISSAP) on the prevention, detection, eradication, control of and rehabilitation
48   from invasive alien species, particularly those having the greatest impact on the
49   environment, biodiversity, human health, and livelihood. Within one (1) year after the
50   effectivity of this Act, the DENR, DA, and the PCSD shall jointly establish the list of
51   invasive and potentially invasive alien species.
 1        The list of IAS and the NISSAP shall be periodically reviewed and updated.
 2   Copies of the same shall be made readily available to concerned government
 3   agencies and local government units (LGUs). All national government agencies and
 4   LGUs shall coordinate with the DENR, DA, and PSCD in the implementation and
 5   localization of the strategy and action plan.
 6
 7        SECTION. 10. Designation of Management and Scientific Authorities for
 8   International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora. – The
 9   Biodiversity Management Bureau of the DENR shall be the management authority in
10   the implementation of international agreements on international trade in endangered
11   species of wild fauna and flora and terrestrial resources, while the Bureau of Fisheries
12   and Aquatic Resources of the DA shall be the authority with respect to aquatic
13   resources. Finally, pursuant to RA 7611, the PCSD shall manage the implementation
14   of international agreements on international trade in endangered species of wild
15   fauna and flora, terrestrial resources and aquatic resources in the province of
16   Palawan.
17
18        Scientific authorities shall provide advice to these management authorities. The
19   scientific authorities for the terrestrial species are the Ecosystems Research and
20   Development Bureau (ERDB) of the DENR, the University of the Philippines (U.P.)
21   Institute of Biological Sciences, U.P. Institute of Biology, the National Museum, and
22   other agencies as may be designated by the Secretary of the DENR or DA. The
23   scientific authorities for the marine and aquatic species are the National Fisheries
24   Research and Development Institute (NFRDI), U.P Marine Science Institure, U.P
25   Visayas, Silliman University, the National Museum and other agencies as may be
26   designed by the Secretary authorities for terrestrial species while the NFRDI shall be
27   convenor of the scientific authorities for marine and aquatic species.

29                                       ARTICLE II
30                                   Permissible Activities
31
32       SECTION. 11. Collection of Wildlife. – The collection of wildlife may be
33   allowed in accordance with Section 6 of this Act subject to the following conditions:
34
35       (a) Appropriate and acceptable wildlife collection techniques with least or no
36       detrimental effects to existing wildlife populations and their habitats shall be
37       observed;
38
39       (b) The collection of wildlife by ICCs/IPs in their ancestral land or domain may be
40       allowed for traditional use and not primarily for profit;
41
42       (c) The collection of wildlife resources by ICCs/IPs must be undertaken in
43       accordance with the guidelines established by the Secretary of DA: Provided,
44       That the collection of threatened wildlife shall be governed by Section 21 of this
45       Act.
46
47       SECTION. 12. Possession of Wildlife. – No person or entity shall be allowed
48   possession of wildlife unless such person or entity can prove financial and technical
49   capability, including scientific knowledge, and possesses the appropriate facility to
50   maintain said wildlife: Provided, That such wildlife was not obtained in violation of this
51   Act or other laws.
 1       SECTION. 13. Collection or Possession of By-Products and Derivatives. –
 2   The collection or possession of wildlife by-products and derivatives may be allowed:
 3   Provided, That the same was not obtained in violation of this Act or other laws.
 4
 5       SEC. 14. Local Transport of Wildlife, By-Products and Derivatives. – Local
 6   transport of wildlife, wildlife by-products and derivatives legally collected or
 7   possessed shall be authorized by the concerned agency unless the same is
 8   prejudicial to wildlife and public health.
 9
10       SECTION 15. Exportation or Importation of Wildlife, By-Products and
11   Derivatives – Wildlife, wildlife by-products, and derivatives may be exported to
12   imported from another country as may be authorized by the Secretaries of the DENR
13   and DA, the PCSD Chairperson, or their designated representatives, subject to strict
14   compliance with the provisions of this Act and the rules and regulations promulgated
15   pursuant thereto: Provided, That the recipient of live wildlife has the technical and
16   financial capacity to maintain it. However, the importation of IAS shall not be allowed.
17
18        SECTION. 16. Introduction, Reintroduction or Restocking of Endemic or
19   Indigenous Wildlife. – The introduction, reintroduction, or restocking of endemic
20   and indigenous wildlife shall be allowed only for population enhancement or recovery
21   purposes, subject to prior clearance from the Secretary of the DENR or the DA or
22   their authorized representatives pursuant to Section 6 of this Act. Any proposed
23   introduction shall be subjected to a scientific study which shall focus on the natural
24   history of the specific wildlife sought to be introduced and its bio-ecological impact,
25   as well as the conduct of public consultations with concerned individuals or entities.
26
27        SECTION. 17. Introduction of Exotic Wildlife. –No exotic species shall be
28   introduced into the country without clearance from the DENR, DA, or PCSD or their
29   authorized representatives. In no case shall exotic species be introduced into
30   protected areas under RA 7586, as amended by RA 11038 and to critical habitats
31   referred to under Section 28 hereof: Provided, That the introduction of such exotic
32   species shall not pose any adverse impacts on local ecology and that further
33   environmental impact study shall be undertaken which shall focus on the bio-
34   ecology, natural history, socioeconomic and related aspects of the area where the
35   species will be introduced. The proponent shall also be required to secure the free
36   and prior informed consent from concerned ICCs/IPs and to conduct public
37   consultations with local stakeholders.
38
39       SECTION. 18. Bioprospecting. – Bioprospecting shall be allowed upon
40   execution of an undertaking by any proponent, which shall stipulate, among others,
41   that the proponent shall comply with reasonable terms and conditions that may be
42   imposed by the Secretaries of the DENR and DA, and the PCSD Chairperson to
43   protect biological diversity and ensure fair and equitable sharing of benefits derived
44   from the utilization of Philippine genetic resources.
45
46        Before any permit shall be granted by the Secretaries of the DENR and DA, the
47   PCSD Chairperson, or their authorized representatives, the proponent shall secure
48   the free, prior and informed consent of the concerned ICCs/IPs, pursuant to RA 8371
49   or “The Indigenous Peoples’ Rights Act of 1997,” and of the Protected Area
50   Management Board under RA 7586, as amended by RA 11038, or of any affected
51   local communities or persons. The applicant shall fully disclose the intent and scope
 1   of the bioprospecting activity in a language and process understandable to the
 2   community.
 3
 4        Whenever applicable and appropriate, a local institution shall be tapped to be
 5   involved in the research, collection, and technological development of the product or
 6   products derived from the wildlife biological and genetic resources. Upon submission
 7   of the complete requirements, the Secretaries of the DENR and DA, and PCSD
 8   Chairperson shall act on the research proposal within a reasonable period.
 9
10         SECTION. 19. Scientific Researches on Wildlife. – The collection and
11   utilization of biological resources for scientific research, not for commercial purposes,
12   shall be allowed upon execution of an undertaking or agreement or the issuance of a
13   gratuitous permit by the Secretaries of the DENR and DA, or their authorized
14   representative: Provided, That clearance from concerned bodies shall be secured
15   before the issuance of the gratuitous permit: Provided, further, That special conditions
16   may be provided by either the Secretary of the DENR or DA, or the Chairperson of
17   the PCSD to facilitate the conduct of scientific research by Filipino students.
18
19       Whenever applicable and appropriate, a local institution shall be tapped to
20   undertake or assist in the research, collection, and technological development of the
21   product or products derived from the biological and genetic resources.
22
23        SECTION. 20. Commercial Breeding or Propagation of Wildlife Resources.
24   – Breeding or propagation of wildlife for commercial purposes shall be allowed by the
25   Secretary of the DENR or DA, the PCSD Chairperson, or their authorized
26   representatives pursuant to Section 6 of this Act through the issuance of a wildlife
27   farm or culture permit: Provided, That only propagated flora, progenies of wild fauna
28   raised, or specimens therefrom, as well as unproductive parent stock shall be utilized
29   for trade: Provided, further, That commercial propagation and breeding operations
30   for wildlife, whenever appropriate, shall be subject to an environmental impact study.
31
32        SECTION. 21. Collection of Threatened Wildlife, By-Products and
33   Derivatives. – The collection of threatened wildlife, as determined and listed
34   pursuant to this Act, including its by-products and derivatives, shall be allowed only
35   for scientific, or breeding or propagation purposes in accordance with Section 6 of
36   this Act. Only persons accredited by the Secretary of the DENR or DA, or the PCSD
37   Chairperson shall be allowed to collect wildlife for conservation breeding or
38   propagation purposes.
39
40       SECTION. 22. Conservation Breeding or Propagation of Threatened
41   Species. – Conservation breeding or propagation of threatened species shall be
42   encouraged in their natural habitats. It shall be done simultaneously with the
43   rehabilitation and protection of the habitat where the captive-bred or propagated
44   species shall be released, reintroduced, or restocked.
45
46       SECTION. 23. Commercial Breeding or Propagation of Threatened Species
47   – Commercial breeding or propagation of threatened species may be allowed:
48   Provided, That these following minimum requirements are met by the applicant:
49       (a) Proven effective breeding and captive management techniques for the
50       species; and
 1         (b) Commitment to simultaneously undertake conservation breeding and
 2         commercial breeding in accordance with Section 20 of this Act.
 3   The Secretary of the DENR or DA, or PCSD Chairperson shall prepare a list of
 4   threatened species for commercial breeding and shall regularly revise or update such
 5   list or as the need arises.

 8                               Permits, Fees and Charges
 9
10        SECTION. 24. Issuance and Validity of Permits. - The Secretaries of the
11   DENR and DA, and the PCSD Chairperson, or their duly authorized representatives,
12   shall issue permits, certifications, or clearances with corresponding periods of
13   validity as follows:
14
15       (a) Wildlife farm or culture permit              three (3) to five (5) years;

17       (c) Gratuitous permit                            one (1) to three (3) years;
18       (d) Local transport permit;                      not more than three (3) months;
19       (f) Wildlife Local Trade Permit                  one (1) year;
20       (g) Wildlife Special Use Permit                  one (1) to three (3) years
21       (h) Export/Import/Re-export Permit               one (1) to six (6) months

23
24       The permits may be renewed subject to the guidelines to be issued by the
25   appropriate agency and upon consultation with concerned groups.
26
27        SECTION. 25. Authority to Collect Fees and Charges. – The Secretaries of
28   the DENR, and DA, and the PCSD Chairperson are hereby authorized to impose and
29   collect reasonable fees and charges for the issuance of permits enumerated in the
30   preceding section, as may be determined upon consultation with the concerned
31   groups, and in the amount fixed by the DENR, DA, or PCSD.
32
33        For the export of wildlife species, an export permit fee of not more than five
34   percent (5%) of the export value, excluding transport costs, shall be charged:
35   Provided, That in the determination of aforesaid fee, the production costs shall be
36   given due consideration. Cut flowers, leaves, and the like produced from farms shall
37   be exempted from the said export fee. These fees and charges shall be reviewed by
38   the Secretaries of DENR and DA every two (2) years or as the need arises and
39   revisions shall be made accordingly, subject to consultations with concerned sectors.
40
41       The Secretary of the DENR or DA, or the PCSD Chairperson may impose an
42   import permit fee based on the import value of wildlife species and a valuation of the
43   potential risks they pose to biodiversity and human health. Fees and charges for such
44   importation stipulated under international agreements shall be borne by the applicant.

46                                      ARTICLE IV
47                           Protection of Threatened Species
48
49        SECTION. 26. Determination of Threatened Species. - The Secretaries of the
50   DENR and DA, or the PCSD Chairperson shall accordingly determine whether any
51   wildlife species or subspecies is threatened, and classify the same as critically
 1   endangered, endangered, vulnerable, or under other accepted category based on the
 2   best scientific data and with due regard to internationally-accepted criteria, including
 3   the following conditions:
 4
 5       (a) Its habitat or range has been destroyed or is under threat to be modified or
 6       curtailed;
 7       (b) It is over-utilized for commercial, recreational, scientific or educational
 8       purposes; and
 9       (c) Other natural or man-made factors that negatively affect the existence of
10       wildlife.
11
12        The Secretaries of the DENR and DA, and the PCSD Chairperson shall
13   accordingly review, revise, and publish the list of categorized threatened wildlife
14   within one (1) year after effectivity of this Act. Thereafter, the list shall be updated
15   regularly or as the need arises: Provided, That a species listed as threatened shall
16   not be removed therefrom within three (3) years of its initial inclusion in the list.

19   evaluate the status of the species that are the subject of a petition filed by any person
20   seeking for the addition or deletion of such species from the list and act on said
21   petition based on the criteria stated herein within a reasonable period.

23       SECTION. 27. Registration of Threatened and Exotic Wildlife in the
24   Possession of Private Persons. – Threatened and exotic wildlife possessed without

30       applicant, including wildlife import permits or certifications, wildlife collector’s

34       submission of proof of legal acquisition: Provided, That the sale or disposition of

46                                        ARTICLE V
47                                      Critical Habitats
48
49       SECTION. 28. Establishment of Critical Habitats. – The Secretaries of the
50   DENR and DA, and the PCSD Chairperson shall designate critical habitats where
51   threatened species are found outside protected areas under RA 7586, as amended
 1   by RA 11038 and other existing laws. Such designation shall be made on the basis
 2   of the best scientific data taking into consideration species endemicity or richness,
 3   and presence of man-made pressures and threats to the survival of wildlife living in
 4   the area, among others.
 5
 6       All designated critical habitats shall be protected, in coordination with the LGUs,
 7   ICCs/IPs, and other concerned groups, from any form of exploitation or destruction
 8   which may be detrimental to the survival of the threatened species dependent therein.
 9   For such purpose, the Secretary of the DENR or DA or the PCSD chairperson may
10   acquire by purchase, donation, or expropriation lands, or interests therein, including
11   the acquisition of usufruct, establishment of easements or other undertakings
12   appropriate in protecting the critical habitat.
13
14       To complement these habitats, the Secretary of the DENR, or DA, or the PCSD
15   Chairperson may also identify for protection a geographically-defined area other than
16   a protected area, which is governed and managed in ways that shall produce positive
17   and sustained long-term outcomes for the in-situ conservation of biodiversity, with
18   associated ecosystem functions and services, and where, cultural, spiritual,
19   socioeconomic, and other locally relevant values can be applied or observed.

26        SECTION. 29. Illegal Acts. – It shall be unlawful for any person to willfully and
27   knowingly undertake, or induce, solicit, or employ another person to undertake, the
28   following acts:
29
30   (a) Killing or destroying wildlife species, including causing death or destruction as a
31   result of any violation of this Act, except in the following instances:
32

42       human being;

45       research or experiments; and
46
47       (vi.) When done for the purpose of wildlife population management or
48       eradication of invasive alien species, as authorized by the DENR, DA or PCSD.
49
50   (b) Injuring, crippling, or impairing the reproductive system of wildlife species;
51
 1   (c) Committing any of the following acts in critical habitats:
 2

 4
 5       (ii.) Squatting or otherwise illegally occupying any portion of the critical habitat;
 6
 7       (iii.)   Mineral exploration or extraction, treasure-hunting or prospecting;

10
11       (v.)     Logging;
12
13       (vi.)    Quarrying;

15       (vii.)   Land reclamation;

21       made or anthropogenic material, agent, or medium that endangers the
22       habitat or renders a habitat no longer viable for wildlife;
23
24   (d) Introduction, reintroduction or restocking of wildlife resources;

26   (e) Trading or attempting to trade wildlife, wildlife by-products and derivatives,

45
46   (c) herein, or a combination thereof, that results in the cessation or impairment

50       SECTION 30. Wildlife Trafficking. – When any of the violations of
51   paragraphs (a), (e), (f), (i), and (j) in Sec. 29 of this Act are committed by a
 1   syndicate or in large scale, or the wildlife involved is to be exported or is actually
 2   exported to another country, or is imported from another country by the person
 3   in connivance with the persons from whom the wildlife was apprehended, the
 4   offense committed is wildlife trafficking and shall be penalized accordingly.
 5
 6       Wildlife trafficking is also committed under any of the following
 7   circumstances, whether domestic or transnational:
 8
 9   (a) When one or more persons agree to commit any of the aforementioned
10   illegal acts for a purpose relating directly or indirectly to obtaining of financial or
11   other material benefit from any crime defined in this Act, including any act
12   undertaken by one of the participants in furtherance of the agreement or
13   involving an organized criminal group;
14
15   (b) When a person who, with knowledge of either the aim and general criminal
16   activity of an organized criminal group or its intention to commit the crimes in
17   question, takes an active part in criminal activities of the organized criminal
18   group or other activities of the organized criminal group in the knowledge that
19   the participation will contribute to the achievement of the above-described
20   criminal aim; and
21
22   (c) When a person organizes, directs, aids, abets, facilitates or provides
23   counsel in the commission of any of the illegal acts defined under this Act
24   involving an organized criminal group.

 3
 4   (a) Possession of any hunting, trapping or collecting paraphernalia along with

13   the wildlife species involved;

16   hunting, trapping, or collecting paraphernalia shall be             a disputable
17   presumption that the person intends to collect, hunt, or gather wildlife resources
18   thereat; and
19
20   (d) Failure on the part of any airfreight, shipping, trucking or forwarding
21   company, or any parcel or mail delivery service providers from whose
22   possession the wildlife, wildlife by-products or derivatives are discovered or
23   seized to fully cooperate in the investigation on the matter by concerned
24   government authorities shall create a presumption that there is connivance or
25   conspiracy between the company or service provider and the shipper to violate
26   the provisions of this Act.

29   committed on each wildlife specimen shall be counted separately. The
30   imposition of penalties shall be qualified and the maximum penalty shall be
31   imposed if the prohibited act committed involves:
32
33   (a) More than one (1) specimen of a critically endangered species;
34   (b) More than five (5) specimens of an endangered species;
35   (c) More than eight (8) specimens of a vulnerable species;
36   (d) More than ten (10) specimens of a near threatened species;
37   (e) More than fifteen (15) specimens of other wildlife species;
38   (f) More than twenty (20) wildlife specimens of any classification.
39
40       If the prohibited act is committed in a large scale or by a syndicate, the
41   penalty of twice the maximum penalty shall be imposed.       A prohibited act
42   is deemed large scale when the total number of wildlife specimen involved is
43   more than thirty (30). A prohibited act is deemed committed by a syndicate if
44   more than three (3) persons are involved.

47   maximum penalty shall be imposed.

50   penalty shall be imposed upon a recidivist who commits any of the illegal acts

 2   judgment of the same or another violation of this Act.

14   confiscation, and forfeiture powers, including the power to cite in contempt, in
15   all cases of violations of this Act. They may also impose administrative
16   sanctions such as payment of fines, or order the suspension or cancellation of
17   existing wildlife permits and disqualification from issuance of future permits, the
18   closure of establishment, or the confiscation and forfeiture of all wildlife, wildlife
19   by-products and derivatives, and all paraphernalia, tools and conveyances

30                                 CHAPTER IV
31                        TRANSNATIONAL WILDLIFE CRIMES
32
33       SECTION. 40. Transnational Character of Wildlife Crime. – Consistent
34    with the provisions of the United Nations Convention on Transnational
35    Organized Crime (UNCTOC) to which the Philippines is a Party, and which is
36    considered part of the law of the land through incorporation, a wildlife crime
37    acquires a transnational character if committed:
38       (a) In more than one State;
39       (b) In one State, but a substantial part of its preparation, planning, direction
40       or control takes place in another State;
41       (c) In one State, but involves an organized criminal group that engages in
42       criminal activities in more than one State; or
43       (d) In one State but has substantial effects on another State.
44
45        SECTION. 41. International Cooperation. –Upon the receipt of a request
46    from another State Party of UNCTOC for the confiscation of proceeds of
47    crime, property, equipment, or other instrumentalities from the commission of
48    a transnational wildlife crime, the Philippine Center on Transnational Crime
49    (PCTC) shall submit the request to the implementing agencies of this Act for
50    the purpose of obtaining an order of confiscation, and if such an order is
51    granted, shall enforce such order.
 1
 2       In addition, upon receipt of such a request, the PCTC shall, in coordination
 3    with the implementing agencies of this Act, take measures to identify, trace,
 4    freeze, seize property, equipment, other instrumentalities and the proceeds of
 5    the transnational wildlife crime.
 6
 7         SECTION. 42. Collection, Exchange, and Analysis of Information on
 8   the Nature of Transnational Wildlife Crime. – The PCTC shall, in
 9   consultation with the scientific and academic communities and other relevant
10   international and regional organizations, including International Criminal Police
11   Organization (INTERPOL) and Association of Southeast Asian Nation National
12   Police (ASEANAPOL), consolidate and analyze the trends in organized crime
13   in its territory, the circumstances in which organized crime operates, as well as
14   the professional groups and technologies involved. The PCTC shall also
15   consider developing and sharing analytical expertise concerning organized
16   criminal activities with other international and regional organizations. For these
17   purposes, common definitions, standards and methodologies should be
18   developed and applied, as appropriate.
19
20        SEC. 43. Policies and Other Measures to Address Transnational
21    Wildlife Crime. – The PCTC shall work with the DENR, DA, PCSD and the
22    Office of the Special Envoy for Transnational Crimes, and consult with
23    international and regional organizations, including INTERPOL, ASEANAPOL,
24    and the ASEAN Centre for Biodiversity, to develop policies and measures
25    conducive to the optimal implementation of the UNTOC in relation to wildlife
26    crime, through regional and international cooperation, taking into account the
27    negative effects of organized crime on society in general, and on sustainable
28    development in particular.

30                                     CHAPTER V
31
32                          WILDLIFE LAW ENFORCEMENT
33
34       SECTION. 44. Creation of Plantilla Positions for Wildlife Law
35    Enforcement. – The DA, DENR, and PCSD are hereby authorized to create,
36    Subject to existing guidelines, permanent plantilla positions of Wildlife
37    Enforcement Agents, or designate their existing permanent employees as
38    such from their respective enforcement units. They shall have full authority to
39    enforce the provisions of this Act such as the conduct of surveillance activities,
40    investigation, application and service of search warrants, arrests of violators
41    and seizures of illegally possessed, collected, traded or transported wildlife,
42    their by- products and derivatives including the conveyances, tools and
43    implements used thereto. Wildlife Enforcement Agents are authorized to carry
44    their agency's badge and government firearms in the conduct of their duties,
45    subject to the existing rules on firearms and after proper training from any
46    government facility.
47
48        SEC. 45. Deputation of Wildlife Enforcement Officers. – The
49    Secretaries of the DENR and DA, and the PCSD Chairperson shall deputize
50    wildlife enforcement officers from non-government organizations, citizens’
51    groups, community organizations, LGUs and other volunteers who have
 1   undergone necessary training for this purpose. The Philippine National Police,
 2   the National Bureau of Investigation, the Bureau of Customs and other law
 3   enforcement agencies shall designate wildlife enforcement officers. As such,
 4   the wildlife enforcement officers shall have the full authority to seize illegally
 5   traded wildlife and arrest violators of this Act, subject to existing laws, rules
 6   and regulations on arrest and detention. These agencies may also seek the
 7   cooperation of international and regional organizations in the conduct of their
 8   investigation and enforcement actions.
 9
10       SEC. 46. Creation of Wildlife Traffi Monitoring Units. – The
11   Secretaries of the DENR and DA shall create Wildlife Traffic Units (WTMUs)
12   in strategic air and seaports all over the country to ensure the strict compliance
13   and effective implementation of all existing wildlife laws, rules and regulations,
14   including pertinent international agreements. For this purpose, the DENR, DA
15   and PCSD are authorized to provide for in their organizational structure such
16   number of Wildlife Inspector positions as may be necessary, the appointees
17   of which shall be assigned to WTMUs, and who shall have the same powers
18   and duties as Wildlife Enforcement Officers. National government agencies
19   with mandates related to transportation and local government units shall
20   provide WTMUs the necessary assistance for the effective implementation of
21   the latter’s duties and functions.
22
23       Customs officers and the authorized representatives of other government
24   agencies or instrumentalities such as, those from the Office for Transportation
25   Security, the Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines, assigned at air or
26   seaports who may have discovered or intercepted wildlife commodities in the
27   discharge of their official functions shall bring such discovery to the attention
28   of, and turn over the intercepted wildlife, wildlife by-products and derivatives
29   to the wildlife traffic monitoring unit assigned in the area.
30
31      SECTION. 47. Wildlife Regulatory and Law Enforcement Management
32   Information System. – The DENR, DA, and PCSD shall develop, establish,
33   and maintain a wildlife law enforcement management information system to
34   aid in the monitoring, regulation, control, and surveillance of activities involving
35   wildlife, particularly the possession, transport and trade of wildlife, wildlife by-
36   products and derivatives.
37
38         SECTION. 48. Role of Local Government Units. – Local government
39   units shall support the DENR, DA, and PCSD in the implementation of this
40   Act. LGUs shall require the presentation of the appropriate and duly issued
41   wildlife permits or clearances by persons engaged in business activities
42   involving wildlife as a prerequisite for the issuance or renewal of business
43   permits and other applicable local government permits and clearances to such
44   persons.
45
46         SECTION. 49. Public Participation. - The participation of private
47   citizens in reporting and providing information on illegal wildlife trade shall be
48   encouraged. Any private person who shall provide any information leading to
49   the apprehension, prosecution, and conviction of any offender for any violation
50   of this Act and any rule or regulation promulgated to implement it, or the
51   confiscation of wildlife, its derivatives or by-products, and all paraphernalia,
 1   tools and conveyances used in connection with the violation, may be given a
 2   reward in an amount to be determined through a policy guideline issued by
 3   the DENR, DA, or the PCSD.
 4
 5        SECTION. 50. Strategi Lawsuit Against Public Participation in the
 6   Enforcement of this Act. – A legal action filed to harass, vex, exert undue
 7   pressure, or stifle any legal recourse that any person, institution, or the
 8   government has taken or may take in the enforcement of this Act shall be
 9   treated as a strategic lawsuit against public participation. The hearing on the
10   defense of a strategic lawsuit against public participation shall be summary in
11   nature. The affirmative defense shall be resolved within thirty (30) days after
12   the summary hearing. If the court dismisses the action, the court may award
13   damages, attorney’s fees, and costs of suit under a counterclaim if such has
14   been filed.
15
16      If the court rejects the defense of a strategic lawsuit against public
17   participation, the evidence adduced during the summary hearing shall be
18   treated as evidence of the parties on the merits of the case. The action shall
19   proceed in accordance with the Rules of Court.
20
21       The Rules of Procedure for Environmental Cases shall govern the
22   procedure in civil, criminal, and special civil actions involving the enforcement
23   or violations of this Act, including actions treated as a strategic lawsuit against
24   public participation as provided in this section.

27                         MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS
28
29         SECTION. 51. Wildlife Management Fund. – There is hereby
30   established a Wildlife Management Fund to be administered separately by
31   the DENR, DA, and PCSD as a special account in the National Treasury. It
32   shall be utilized to finance rehabilitation or restoration of habitats affected by
33   acts committed in violation of this Act and support scientific research,
34   enforcement and monitoring activities, procurement of vehicles, firearms and
35   ammunition, as well as enhancement of capabilities of relevant agencies.
36
37       The Fund shall be derived from administrative and criminal fines imposed,
38   civil liabilities and damages awarded, proceeds from allowable disposition of
39   wildlife, wildlife by-products and derivatives, fees, charges, donations,
40   endowments, administrative fees or grants in the form of contributions.
41
42       Contributions to the Fund shall be exempt from the payment of the
43   donor’s tax and all other tax charges or fees imposed by the government. The
44   administrative and criminal fines imposed, civil liabilities and damages
45   awarded shall accrue to the Wildlife Management Fund of the DA, DENR, or
46   the PCSD, as the case may be.
47
48        SECTION. 52. Tax Exemption. – All grants, bequests, endowments,
49   donations and contributions which may be made to non-government
50   organizations and people’s organizations engaged in wildlife conservation,
51   protection and law enforcement duly registered with the Securities and
 1   Exchange Commission or the Cooperative Development Authority, as
 2   certified by the local government unit, the DENR, DA, or PCSD, for the
 3   conservation and protection of wildlife resources and their habitats shall be
 4   exempt from donor’s tax and the same shall constitute as allowable
 5   deductions from gross income for purposes of computing the taxable income
 6   of the donor in accordance with the provisions of the NIRC of 1997, as
 7   amended.
 8
 9      Likewise, all grants, bequests, endowments, donations, and contributions
10   which may be made to the DENR, DA, or PCSD shall also be exempted from
11   donor’s tax in accordance with the provisions of the National Internal
12   Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.
13
14      These national government agencies may also avail of the tax expenditure
15   subsidy administered by the Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB), subject
16   to the provisions of Title XIII (Tax Incentives) of the National Internal Revenue
17   Code of 1997, as amended, Executive Order No. 93, as amended and the
18   General Appropriations Act.
19
20         SECTION. 53. Wildlife Rescue Center. – The Secretaries of the DENR
21   and DA shall establish or designate wildlife rescue centers which shall take
22   temporary custody and care of all confiscated, abandoned, or donated wildlife
23   to ensure their welfare and well-being. All wildlife rescue centers shall be
24   situated in appropriate facilities that are equipped with the necessary tools
25   and apparatus, and staffed by regular, qualified and properly-trained
26   personnel to ensure that all rescued wildlife are properly cared for. They shall
27   formulate guidelines for the disposition of wildlife from these rescue centers.
28
29          SECTION. 54. Establishment of National Wildlife Research
30   Centers. – The Secretaries of the DENR and DA and the PCSD Chairperson
31   shall establish national and local wildlife research centers for terrestrial and
32   aquatic species that shall lead in the conduct of scientific researches on the
33   proper strategies for the conservation and protection of wildlife, including
34   captive breeding or propagation. Such research centers shall be staffed by
35   qualified and properly-trained personnel, and shall be outfitted with the tools,
36   machines and equipment necessary in the proper conduct of forensic
37   analyses, and such other related capabilities necessary in effective wildlife
38   law enforcement. To further enrich scientific studies and explorations in the
39   area of wildlife protection and conservation, the DENR, DA, and PCSD shall
40   establish partnerships with experts from academic and research institutions
41   and legitimate wildlife trade industry.
42
43          SECTION. 55. Flagship Species. – Local government units shall
44   initiate conservation measures for wildlife species in their areas. For this
45   purpose, they may adopt flagship species such as the Cebu black shama
46   (Copsychus cebuensis), Tamaraw (Bubalus mindorensis), Philippine tarsier
47   (Tarsius syrichta), or the Philippine teak tree (Tectona philippinensis), which
48   shall serve as emblems of conservation for the local government concerned.
49   The Secretary of the DENR or DA, or the PCSD Chairperson, or their
50   authorized representatives may provide guidelines on the selection of
51   flagship species.

 2         SECTION. 56. Heritage Trees. – In coordination with and with

 6   Chairperson, or their authorized representatives may provide guidelines on
 7   the selection of heritage trees.

14

18   specimens with the DENR, DA, or PCSD, as the case may be.

20         SECTION. 59. Communication, Education, Public                Awareness,
21   and Information Sharing. – The DENR, DA, PCSD and the Office of Special
22   Envoy for Transnational Crimes shall undertake wildlife information

24   national agencies and LGUs. These agencies shall facilitate information
25   sharing with other national agencies and pertinent agencies of the ASEAN
