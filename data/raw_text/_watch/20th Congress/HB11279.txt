                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to exempt qualified indigent Filipino graduates from paying
review fees for their first board or bar examination. It helps remove what may
prevent academically qualified graduates from obtaining the review preparation
necessary to ace the exams.

       According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (2026)1, eight hundred and
eighty-one (881) out of one thousand six hundred eleven (1,611) cities and
municipalities recorded poverty incidence levels of twenty percent (20%) or
lower in 2023, while seven hundred and twenty two (722) localities continued to
register poverty incidence above twenty percent (20%), including areas classified
under higher poverty levels. Fresh graduates invest for review courses and
examination materials after completing their degrees. For indigent graduates
from economically disadvantaged backgrounds, these costs may delay their first
attempt at the board or bar examination, thus limiting professional opportunities
that their education would otherwise provide.

        The proposed measure provides assistance to indigent graduates who are
first-time license exam takers who have demonstrated academic excellence. It
shall cover review courses and related preparatory materials for board and bar
examinations.

       Expanding opportunities for qualified indigent graduates to obtain
professional licenses strengthens the country’s human capital and allows more
Filipinos to contribute their knowledge and expertise to national development.
Behind every aspiring professional is a family that invests years of sacrifice,
perseverance, and hope toward achieving a better future. This represents a

                         AN ACT
EXEMPTING THE PAYMENT OF REVIEW FEES FOR BOARD AND BAR
EXAMINATIONS FOR QUALIFIED INDIGENTS ON THEIR FIRST TAKE

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Professional
Advancement of Indigents Act”.

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote equitable access to education and to provide opportunities for all,
regardless of socio-economic status, to excel in their chosen fields. The State
recognizes that the cost of review materials and fees for board and bar
examinations can be a significant barrier for indigent families. Towards this end,
the State shall remove this barrier and enable deserving students from
disadvantaged backgrounds to pursue their dreams and contribute to the
development of our nation.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

         a. Qualified indigents refer to persons who have no visible means of
      income or whose income is insufficient for the subsistence of his or her
      family as identified by the Department of Social Welfare and Development
      (DSWD), or those who are legal dependents from families which have a
      combined annual income below the poverty threshold as determined by
      the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), and
      who meets the eligibility criteria set forth in Section 5 of this Act;

          b. Bar Examination refers to the licensure examination administered
      by the Supreme Court of the Philippines, and is one (1) of the prerequisites
      for professional admission to the Philippine Bar;

         c.​ Board Examinations refer to licensure examinations administered by
      the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), including but not limited
      to examinations for nursing, engineering, medicine, and other regulated
      professions; and

         d.​ Review fees refer to fees charged by review centers and educational
      institutions for review courses, whether offered in-person, online, or a
      combination of the same, for Board and Bar Examinations, including fees
      for review and exam preparatory materials separately offered by review
      centers for the course in which each qualified indigent is officially
      enrolled.

       SEC. 4. Exemption from Review Fees. – Subject to the exceptions provided in
Section 6 of this Act, qualified indigents shall be exempt from paying review fees
for board and bar examinations; Provided, That they meet the criteria set forth in
Section 5 of this Act; Provided, further, That in order to avail of the benefits under
this Act, the certifications under the succeeding section shall be presented by
qualified indigents to their respective review centers or educational institutions
upon their enrollment therein.

       SEC. 5. Eligibility Criteria. – To qualify for the exemption under Section 4,
qualified indigents must meet the following criteria:

         a.​ Must provide documentary evidence of their indigency, as
      determined by the DSWD;

         b.​ Must be first-time board or bar examination takers, as certified by
      the Supreme Court of the Philippines or the PRC, as may be appropriate;

          c.​ Must be Filipino citizens; and

          d.​ Must either be:

                 1.​ Higher education graduates with Latin Honors; or

                2.​ Academic Scholarship grantees, as certified by the applicants’
             higher educational institutions.

      SEC. 6. Exceptions. – The exemption from review fees under this Act shall
not include those charged in connection with the review for other examinations
not specified herein such as, but not limited to, the Civil Service Commission
Examination or the Career Service Examination for Foreign Service Officer.

       SEC. 7. Funding. – The necessary funds for the implementation of this Act
shall be allocated by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) in the
General Appropriations Act (GAA).

       SEC. 8. Reporting. – The PRC and the Supreme Court of the Philippines
shall submit an annual report to Congress detailing the number of indigent
students who benefited from this Act, the amount disbursed, and any other
relevant information.

      SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Supreme Court of the
Philippines, PRC, DSWD, and other relevant government agencies shall
formulate and promulgate necessary rules and regulations to effectively
implement this Act within ninety (90) days from its effectivity.

       SEC. 10. Separability Clause. – If any part, section, or provision of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions not affected shall remain in full
force and effect.

      SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations
or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or amended
accordingly.

       SEC. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
