FOR INDIGENT PATIENTS WHO INCUR OUT-OF-POCKET

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 15 of Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that
the State shall protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill
health consciousness among them. Complementing this constitutional mandate,
Section 11 of Article XIII directs the State to adopt an integrated and
comprehensive approach to health development that shall endeavor to make
essential goods, health, and other social services available to all people at
affordable cost, with priority for the underprivileged, sick, elderly, persons with
disabilities, women, and children. In furtherance of these constitutional mandates,
the government enacted Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the
Universal Health Care Act, and Republic Act No. 11463, otherwise known as the
Malasakit Centers Act, to strengthen financial risk protection and improve access
to healthcare services. Despite these reforms, recent evidence shows that many
Filipino households continue to experience financial hardship due to high out-of-
pocket healthcare expenditures, particularly among poor, elderly, and rural
populations, indicating that financial protection remains incomplete.1

       The prioritization of indigent patients under this measure is therefore
grounded in an express constitutional policy. Indigent patients are particularly
vulnerable to the financial consequences of medical service deficiencies because
they have limited or no capacity to absorb unexpected health expenditures
without compromising the subsistence of their families. When a PhilHealth-
covered or government-funded medicine, medical supply, diagnostic service,
procedure, or treatment is unavailable in an accredited public health facility, the
resulting expense may be transferred to the patient who is least able to bear it.
The State's constitutional duty to prioritize the underprivileged and sick
consequently provides a direct policy basis for extending additional financial
protection to qualified indigent patients in circumstances where they are
compelled to shoulder expenses that should otherwise have been covered.

       Recent local studies likewise reveal that out-of-pocket payments continue
to comprise a significant share of healthcare financing in the Philippines despite
expanded insurance coverage. Findings from the Philippine Institute for
Development Studies (PIDS) indicate that existing financing mechanisms remain
insufficient to fully protect vulnerable households from catastrophic health
expenditures, especially when medicines, medical supplies, diagnostic services,
and treatments are unavailable in public health facilities.2 International evidence
supports these findings. The World Health Organization emphasizes that
excessive reliance on out-of-pocket payments remains one of the greatest barriers
to Universal Health Coverage.3 While the World Bank reports that households in
many developing countries, including the Philippines, continue to shoulder a
substantial portion of healthcare costs despite government health financing
reforms.4
       Although existing laws provide insurance benefits, subsidies, and financial
assistance, no Philippine law establishes a statutory right to reimbursement for
indigent patients who have already incurred out-of-pocket medical expenses

public health facilities. Consequently, operational deficiencies such as medicine
stock-outs, equipment failures, and service unavailability continue to transfer the
financial burden of healthcare to indigent patients, contrary to the constitutional
principles of social justice, equitable access to healthcare, and financial risk

       This bill seeks to address these policy gaps by establishing a mandatory
reimbursement mechanism for qualified indigent patients who incur out-of-
pocket medical expenses due to the unavailability of covered services in
accredited public health facilities. The proposed measure institutionalizes a
simplified reimbursement process, strengthens institutional accountability
through monitoring and reporting mechanisms, and complements the financial
protection objectives of the Universal Health Care Act and the Malasakit Centers
Act. By ensuring that indigent patients are reimbursed for healthcare expenses
they were compelled to shoulder because of service delivery failures, the measure
reinforces the State's constitutional duty to protect the right to health and promote
equitable access to quality healthcare for all Filipinos.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:
      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Indigent
Patients' Out-of-Pocket Medical Expense Reimbursement Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right to health of the people and to ensure that
no indigent patient is deprived of essential health services due to financial
hardship arising from the unavailability of PhilHealth-covered or government-
funded medicines, medical supplies, diagnostic services, procedures, or
treatments in accredited public health facilities.

                                                                                4
      Consistent with Section 11, Article XIII of the 1987 Constitution and
Section 2 of Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the “Universal Health
Care Act,” the State shall give priority to the health needs of underprivileged and
sick persons and shall endeavor to ensure access to essential health services
without causing financial hardship.

      Towards this end, the State shall establish a mandatory reimbursement
mechanism to provide prompt financial protection to qualified indigent patients
who are compelled to incur out-of-pocket medical expenses because covered
health services are unavailable in accredited public health facilities.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Accredited Public Health Facility refers to any hospital, infirmary, rural
      health unit, specialty center, or other healthcare institution owned or
      operated by the National Government, local government units, or
      government-owned and controlled corporations that is accredited by the
      Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth).

      (b) Indigent Patient refers to a patient who has no visible means of income,
      or whose income is insufficient for the subsistence of his or her family, as
      assessed by the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD),
      local government social worker, or medical social worker of the health
      facility, consistent with Republic Act No. 11463, otherwise known as the
      “Malasakit Centers Act,” and other applicable laws and regulations.

      (c) Out-of-Pocket Medical Expense refers to the actual amount personally
      paid by an indigent patient for medicines, medical supplies, laboratory
      tests, diagnostic services, procedures, or treatments that should have been
      fully covered by PhilHealth or existing government-funded health
      programs.
                                                                                  5
      (d) Unavailability of Covered Services refers to the temporary or actual
      absence, shortage, stock-out, equipment failure, or operational inability of
      an accredited public health facility to provide a PhilHealth-covered or
      government-funded medicine, medical supply, diagnostic service,
      procedure, or treatment at the time it is medically required, including the
      failure of the facility to timely secure such service through authorized
      contracting arrangements.

      (e) Certification of Unavailability refers to the official written certification
      issued by the authorized public health facility confirming that the required
      medicine, supply, diagnostic service, procedure, or treatment was
      unavailable at the time of the patient's medical need.

      SEC. 4. Mandatory Reimbursement Guarantee. – Any qualified indigent
patient who incurs out-of-pocket medical expenses due to the unavailability of
covered services in an accredited public health facility shall be entitled to full
reimbursement of the actual amount personally paid. The Department of Health
(DOH) shall establish and administer a reimbursement mechanism to ensure the
prompt payment of approved claims under this Act.

      No qualified reimbursement claim shall be denied solely on the ground that
the patient procured the required medicine, supply, or service from an authorized
private provider when such procurement resulted from the certified unavailability
of the covered service in the public health facility.

      SEC. 5. Relationship with Existing Health Assistance Programs. – The
reimbursement mechanism established under this Act shall complement and
strengthen existing health financing programs under Republic Act No. 11223,
otherwise known as the "Universal Health Care Act," Republic Act No. 11463,
otherwise known as the "Malasakit Centers Act," and other applicable laws.

                                                                                    6
      The benefits provided under this Act shall apply only when existing
government assistance mechanisms fail to prevent an indigent patient from
personally paying for covered services due to their unavailability in an accredited
public health facility.

      SEC. 6. Reimbursement Procedure. – The DOH, in coordination with
PhilHealth and the DSWD, shall establish a simplified and fast-tracked
reimbursement procedure, which shall include the following minimum
requirements:

      (a) A Certification of Unavailability issued by the attending physician,
      hospital pharmacist, or authorized hospital official;

      (b) Proof of indigent status;

      (c) Official receipts or equivalent proof of payment covering the actual out-
      of-pocket medical expense; and

      (d) Such other documents as may be strictly necessary under the
      implementing rules and regulations.

      Complete reimbursement claims shall be processed within fifteen (15)
working days from submission of complete documentary requirements.

      Claims shall be filed within sixty (60) days from the date the out-of-pocket
medical expense was incurred.

      SEC. 7. Appeal. – Any claimant whose reimbursement application is
denied may appeal before the appropriate DOH Regional Office within fifteen
(15) days from receipt of the written notice of denial.




                                                                                 7
      The decision of the DOH Regional Office may be appealed to the Secretary
of Health, whose decision shall be final without prejudice to judicial remedies
available under existing laws.

      SEC. 8. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting. – All accredited public
health facilities shall submit quarterly reports to the DOH on reimbursement
claims filed, approved, denied, pending, and paid under this Act. The DOH shall
maintain a centralized monitoring system and annually submit to the Office of
the President, the Senate Committee on Health, and the House of Representatives
Committee on Health a report containing:

      (a) the number of reimbursement claims received;

      (b) the amount reimbursed;

      (c) average processing time;

      (d) recurring causes of service unavailability;

      (e) corrective measures undertaken; and

      (f) recommendations for legislative or administrative improvements.

      SEC. 9. Administrative and Penal Provisions. – Any public official or
employee who, without lawful justification:

      (a) knowingly refuses to issue a Certification of Unavailability;

      (b) knowingly delays or denies a valid reimbursement claim;

      (c) knowingly issues a false Certification of Unavailability; or

      (d) otherwise abuses authority in implementing this Act,




                                                                              8
      shall, after due notice and hearing, be administratively liable in accordance
with existing civil service laws, rules, and regulations, without prejudice to
criminal prosecution under Republic Act No. 3019 and other applicable laws.

      Any person who knowingly commits fraud or misrepresentation to obtain
reimbursement under this Act shall:

      (1) forfeit all benefits under this Act;

      (2) refund twice the amount unlawfully received; and

      (3) suffer the penalty of imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but
      not more than two (2) years.

      SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Department of Health.

      Thereafter, the amounts necessary for the continued implementation of this
Act, including the establishment and operation of the Indigent Patients
Reimbursement Guarantee Fund, shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act. All reimbursements shall be subject to existing government
accounting and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.

      SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with PhilHealth, the
DSWD, the Department of Budget and Management, and the Commission on
Audit, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

      SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
