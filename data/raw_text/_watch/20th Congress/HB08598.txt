                             AN ACT
PROTECTING CHILDREN AND MINORS IN SOCIAL MEDIA PLATFORMS,
STRENGTHENING TRANSPARENCY AND USER CONTROLS, AND ENSURING
PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION IN THE USE OF SOCIAL MEDIA

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Social media has become an important part of everyday life. It allows people to
communicate, learn, do business, and take part in public discussions. However, it also
exposes children and minors to serious risks such as online sexual abuse and
exploitation, grooming, privacy violations, and unhealthy or excessive use. Because
social media platforms collect and process large amounts of personal data, these risks
can grow quickly and affect many young users. There is therefore a clear need to
strengthen protections for children in the online environment.

Philippine law already recognizes the duty of the State to protect children online. The
Anti-Online Sexual Abuse or Exploitation of Children and Anti-Child Sexual
Abuse or Exploitation Materials Act (Republic Act No. 11930) requires internet
platforms and intermediaries to adopt measures that prevent, detect, and report
online sexual abuse and exploitation of children. At the same time, the Data Privacy
Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173) protects personal data and gives the National
Privacy Commission the authority to enforce privacy standards. This bill builds on
these existing laws by strengthening the responsibility of social media platforms to
provide safer online spaces for children while respecting privacy rights and freedom
of expression.

Accordingly, this measure seeks to establish clear and practical protections for
children and minors using social media platforms. These include age-appropriate
safety measures, stronger privacy settings for minors by default, tools for parents and
guardians, easier reporting systems for harmful content, and greater transparency
about how platforms collect and use children’s data. By promoting safer design and
responsible data practices, the bill aims to reduce online abuse, protect young users’
privacy, and empower families to better manage children’s digital experiences.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Social Media Child
Protection and Privacy Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect children
and minors from online risks, strengthen consumer transparency and user control in
social media use, and uphold the constitutional rights to privacy and freedom of
expression.

Regulation under this Act shall be content-neutral, narrowly tailored to legitimate
State interests, and implemented with clear standards and safeguards to avoid
overbreadth and chilling effects.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) Social Media Platform refers to an online service or application that
       primarily enables users to create accounts or pro iles and to create, share,
       view, or interact with content and other users.


                                                                           3|Page
   (b) Child refers to a person below thirteen (13) years of age.

   (c) Minor refers to a person thirteen (13) years of age but below eighteen (18)
       years of age.

   (d) Parent/Legal Guardian refers to a parent, legal guardian, or person
       exercising substitute parental authority under applicable laws.

   (e) Personal    Information, Sensitive    Personal       Information, Personal
       Information Controller, and Personal Information Processor shall have
       the meanings provided under the Data Privacy Act of 2012.

SECTION 4. Duties of Social Media Platforms; Minimum Child and Minor
Protections. — All Social Media Platforms operating in the Philippines and offering
services to Philippine users shall implement the following minimum measures:

   (a) Age-Appropriate Access and Safeguards. Platforms shall implement
       reasonable and proportionate age-assurance measures and user experience
       controls to protect Children and Minors.

   (b) Default Privacy and Safety Settings for Minors. Accounts reasonably
       determined to belong to Minors shall, by default, be set to the highest available
       privacy settings, including limitations on discoverability, direct messaging by
       non-contacts, and public visibility, subject to user/parental adjustments.

   (c) Enhanced Protections for Children. Platforms shall take reasonable
       measures to prevent or restrict the creation and use of accounts by Children
       and shall provide clear pathways for reporting and rapid action on suspected
       child accounts.

   (d) Parental Tools. Platforms shall provide optional parental/guardian tools for
       Minors, including at least: activity summaries, privacy setting guides, and
       reporting channels.

   (e) Time-Use Transparency Tools. Platforms shall provide an in-app dashboard
       that allows users to view time spent on the platform and to set voluntary
       reminders or limits. For Minors, such reminders shall be enabled by default,
       subject to adjustment.



                                                                            4|Page
   (f) Limits on Behavioral Pro iling of Minors. Platforms shall not engage in
       targeted advertising or behavioral pro iling of Minors based on sensitive
       personal information and shall adopt privacy-by-design measures appropriate
       for minors.

   (g) Ban on Collection of Children’s Personal Data Without Proper
       Basis. Platforms shall not collect or process personal information of Children
       except in accordance with the Data Privacy Act and its Implementing Rules and
       Regulations, including rules on lawful criteria and protection of data subjects.

SECTION 5. Data Privacy; Non-Exemption Clause. — Nothing in this Act shall be
construed as an exemption from the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and its Implementing
Rules and Regulations.

SECTION 6. Complaints and Enforcement Framework. — The National Privacy
Commission (NPC) with its function to monitor and ensure compliance of the country
with international standards set for data protection in accordance with Republic Act
No. 10173 or the Data Privacy Act of 2012 shall:

   (a) National Privacy Commission (NPC). For matters involving the processing of
       personal data, including unlawful collection, processing, retention, disclosure,
       or security incidents, the NPC shall exercise its powers under the Data Privacy
       Act, including rulemaking, compliance monitoring, and issuance of
       appropriate orders and remedies.

   (b) DICT Coordinating Role. The DICT shall serve as the coordinating agency for
       platform compliance reporting and public digital literacy initiatives relevant to
       this Act, without prejudice to the NPC’s regulatory authority over personal
       data protection.

   (c) Referral and Inter-Agency Coordination. The NPC, DICT, and other
       concerned agencies may enter coordination mechanisms for complaints
       referral, technical assistance, and public advisories, consistent with their
       mandates.

SECTION 7. Research Program on Child and Adolescent Digital Well-Being. —
The Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with relevant agencies and
academic institutions, shall establish a research program on the health and
developmental effects of social media use among children and minors, and shall issue
evidence-based public health advisories.

SECTION 8. Transparency Reports. — Social Media Platforms shall publish periodic
transparency reports, in such form as may be prescribed in the IRR, including
aggregated data on: (a) child/minor safety reports received; (b) actions taken; (c)
average response times; and (d) user education measures.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the DICT, in coordination with the NPC, DOH, and other concerned
stakeholders,      shall    promulgate     the      implementing     rules     and
regulations: Provided, That rules affecting personal data processing shall be
consistent with the Data Privacy Act and NPC’s rule-making and advisory functions.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. — If any portion of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the same shall not affect the validity and effectivity of the other
provisions not affected hereby.

SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances, rules and
regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
