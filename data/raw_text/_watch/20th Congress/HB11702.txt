                            EXPLANATORY NOTE


      Military retirees remain entitled to the country’s care after their active
service ends. Their retirement should bring dependable access to health care,
family protection, and opportunities to participate productively in civilian life.
Recognition of their service must be reflected in benefits that are clear, accessible,
and adequate.

      The existing veterans’ benefits framework has developed through
successive amendments to Republic Act No. 6948. This bill replaces that
framework with a single comprehensive Military Retirees and Veterans Welfare
Act. It expressly covers honorably retired AFP officers and enlisted personnel,
while preserving the recognition and accrued rights of other veterans and
historical beneficiaries.

      Military retirees shall not be required to establish wartime or campaign
service to receive ordinary welfare benefits. Health and reintegration services
shall be available upon retirement, while age-based, disability, and income-tested
benefits shall remain subject to the conditions appropriate to each benefit. AFP
retirement pensions and distinct welfare benefits are preserved as separate
entitlements.

      The measure retains the increased ordinary old-age and surviving-spouse
pensions, enhanced death pensions, burial assistance, transition grants, livelihood
assistance, care allowances, and emergency welfare support. It carries forward
disability pension protection and the special historic-war pension within the
replacement law, together with educational assistance, employment incentives,
discounts, and safeguards for dependents and survivors.

      Regular and standby inflation adjustments shall preserve purchasing
power. A cumulative five percent increase in the prescribed consumer price index
shall trigger an earlier adjustment without waiting for the regular review.
Appropriation requirements and transparent fiscal reporting shall support
responsible implementation.

      The bill expressly repeals Republic Act Nos. 6948, 7696, 9396, 10649,
11164, and 11958, while preserving accrued entitlements, pending claims,
institutions, records, and lawful proceedings. Separate AFP retirement laws and
the organizational laws of PVAO and VMMC shall remain operative.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

SECTION 1. Short Title — This Act shall be known as the “Military Retirees
and Veterans Welfare Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State recognizes its continuing
responsibility toward military retirees, veterans, and their dependents and
survivors in consideration of their service to the Republic.
      The State shall provide a comprehensive, accessible, equitable, and
financially sustainable system of benefits and services that protects veterans
against poverty, disability, illness, and social exclusion; supports their transition
to civilian life; and safeguards the welfare of their dependents and survivors.

      Military retirement is a transition to civilian life that requires continuing
protection of health, family welfare, economic security, and opportunities for
productive participation. Military retirees shall be expressly included in every
generally applicable benefit under this Act, subject only to the specific age,
disability, income, or other conditions stated for that benefit. Wartime or combat
service shall not be required for ordinary retiree welfare benefits.

To these ends, the State shall:

   a. Preserve earned benefits and provide timely access to pensions, health
      care, rehabilitation, and appropriate social assistance;
   b. Support civilian reintegration through education, recognition of military
      learning and experience, employment services, entrepreneurship, and
      housing assistance;
   c. Protect dependents and survivors against the disruption of family income,
      education, care, and housing resulting from military service, disability, or
      death;
   d. Give particular attention to service-connected disability, elderly veterans,
      low-income      households,    women      veterans,    and   beneficiaries    in
      geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas;
   e. Maintain accountable administration, adequate appropriations, effective
      coordination, and fair procedures for the determination of claims; and
   f. Encourage      responsible    participation   by      employers,   educational
      institutions, health facilities, local government units, and civil society.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   a. Military retiree refers to an officer or enlisted member of the Armed Forces
      of the Philippines whose honorable retirement has been approved or has
      taken effect under a law governing compulsory, optional, disability, or
      special military retirement. Entitlement shall be established by the
      retirement order or other competent official record. Actual receipt of a
      retirement pension shall not be required to establish retiree status.
      Discharge or separation without lawful retirement shall not, by itself,
      confer such status.
   b. Retirement refers to the lawful termination of active military service with
      retirement status under an applicable retirement law. Separation refers to
      termination of active service by discharge, resignation, or another lawful
      mode, whether or not accompanied by retirement. These terms shall not be
      used interchangeably in determining eligibility.
   c. Qualified dependent refers to any of the following persons who satisfies
      the applicable dependency requirement:
               1. The lawful spouse who relies upon the military retiree or veteran
                  for substantial support;
               2. A child entitled to support under law who is below eighteen (18)
                  years of age;
               3. An unmarried child who is eighteen (18) years of age or older but
                  below twenty-five (25) years of age, is enrolled in a government-
                  recognized educational or training program, and relies upon the
                  military retiree or veteran for substantial support;

                                                                                  3
            4. A child, regardless of age, who is incapable of self-support
                because of a disability that arose before attaining twenty-five
                (25) years of age or while already recognized as a qualified
                dependent under this Act; or
            5. A parent who relies upon the military retiree or veteran for
                substantial support.

      Substantial support means a regular contribution necessary to meet
essential living, educational, or care needs. It shall not require the complete
absence of other income or assistance. PVAO shall prescribe uniform evidentiary
requirements appropriate to the benefit claimed and shall accept credible
alternative evidence where ordinary records are unavailable.

      The circumstances of a child’s birth shall not be a ground for
discrimination. Qualified dependent status shall not, by itself, confer pension
entitlement, which shall be determined under the specific pension provisions.

   d. Qualified survivor refers to a surviving spouse, child, or parent who
      satisfies the eligibility requirements of a particular survivor benefit under
      this Act. Recognition as a dependent during the deceased’s lifetime shall
      be relevant evidence but shall not replace the conditions expressly
      prescribed for that survivor benefit.
   e. Service-connected disability refers to a medically established physical or
      mental impairment resulting from an injury, wound, illness, or disease
      incurred or aggravated in line of duty. Aggravation refers to a lasting
      worsening attributable to military service beyond the ordinary progression
      of the condition. Service connection and the degree of disability shall be
      determined by the Disability Rating Board under this Act.
   f. Reintegration refers to the process through which a military retiree or
      honorably separated service member adjusts to civilian               family,

SEC. 4. Veteran Status and Eligibility. — A veteran, for purposes of this Act, is
a person falling within any of the following categories:

   a. A military retiree as defined in Section 3(a), regardless of whether the
      person served in wartime, combat, or a particular military campaign;
   b. A person whose qualifying military service in the Philippine Revolution,
      the Philippine American War, or World War II has been recognized by
      competent authority, including qualifying service by Filipino citizens with
      Allied forces in Philippine territory and recognized guerrilla service;
   c. A member of the Philippine Expeditionary Forces to Korea, the Philippine
      Civic Action Group, or the Philippine Contingent in Vietnam whose
      qualifying service is established by competent records;
   d. A former AFP member honorably discharged or separated after at least
      twenty (20) years of cumulative active service, whether or not retired;
   e. A former AFP member honorably separated before completing the service
      period in paragraph (d) because of a service-connected disability; or a
      member whose death in line of duty establishes the entitlement of qualified
      survivors under this Act; or
   f. A person who entered military service before the effectivity of Republic
      Act No. 9396 and qualifies under the six-year cumulative active-service
      rule or another eligibility rule applicable to that person before the
      effectivity of that law.

   Veteran status lawfully established under the laws repealed by this Act shall
be preserved. Historical beneficiaries, including foreign nationals or members of
earlier military formations lawfully recognized under the previously applicable
framework, shall not lose recognized status solely because their service category
is not separately enumerated herein.



                                                                                8
   PVAO shall determine status from authenticated service records and other
competent evidence. Military retirees shall be recognized upon verification of
lawful honorable retirement and shall not be required to prove separate campaign
or combat service. Missing historical records shall be addressed through
reasonable alternative verification and shall not alone justify denial.

   Veteran status establishes membership in the covered class. Entitlement to
each pension, allowance, discount, scholarship, or service shall remain subject to
the conditions expressly prescribed for that benefit. Recognition of a deceased
member under paragraph (e) shall establish only the rights lawfully available to
qualified survivors and shall not create a pension payable to the deceased.

SEC. 5. Coverage and Express Entitlement of Military Retirees — This Act
shall cover military retirees and other veterans, their qualified dependents and
survivors, and transition beneficiaries to the extent expressly provided.

      Every military retiree shall be eligible to apply for health care,
rehabilitation, educational assistance, reintegration, employment and livelihood
services, discounts, identification, and other generally applicable welfare benefits
without having to establish separate wartime or campaign veteran status.

      Old-age pension shall commence at the prescribed age. Disability and
death pensions require the corresponding statutory contingency. Income tests
shall apply only where expressly prescribed. These conditions shall not be used
to deny unrelated welfare services.

      AFP retirement pensions, gratuities, survivorship benefits, and retirement
adjustment mechanisms under applicable retirement laws are distinct from the
welfare entitlements under this Act. Nothing herein reduces, repeals, or
substitutes for those retirement benefits.


                                                                                   9
      Receipt of an AFP retirement pension shall not bar the ordinary old-age
pension, disability pension, health coverage, education assistance, discounts, or
other distinct benefits under this Act. The special historic-war pension shall
remain subject to its expressly stated restriction.

SEC. 6. Administration and Retiree Services — PVAO shall administer this Act
and maintain a dedicated military retiree service function within its existing
organizational structure, subject to approved staffing and appropriations.

      PVAO and the AFP retirement administering offices shall establish
coordinated intake, pension and service verification, pre-retirement orientation,
and continuing referrals. A retiree shall not be required to repeat an application
or submit a service record already verified by the responsible agency.

      Service standards and annual reports shall separately identify military
retirees, disability retirees, other veterans, dependents, and survivors to permit
assessment of coverage and unmet needs.

      Existing powers of PVAO under its organizational laws shall continue. Its
legal officers, investigators, and duly authorized assistance officers may
administer oaths and receive testimony necessary for claims and investigations
under this Act.

SEC. 7. Educational Benefits. — Every veteran shall be entitled to educational
assistance for one (1) approved program leading to a first undergraduate degree,
a recognized technical-vocational qualification, or an equivalent occupational
qualification.

      An eligible veteran may assign the unused benefit, in whole or in part, to a
qualified spouse or child. A surviving spouse may apply on behalf of an eligible



                                                                                10
child of a deceased veteran. A lawful guardian may apply where no surviving
spouse is available or capable of acting.

The following shall govern:

   a. Assistance shall cover tuition and mandatory school fees, required learning
      materials, and reasonable support for transportation, connectivity, and
      subsistence;
   b. In public institutions, tuition and fees already covered by another
      government program shall not be paid twice. Veterans’ assistance shall
      cover eligible expenses remaining after application of such coverage;
   c. Assistance in private institutions shall be subject to published ceilings
      based on reasonable program costs, established jointly by PVAO, CHED,
      TESDA, and DBM. Any amount exceeding the ceiling shall be disclosed
      before enrollment;
   d. Each veteran’s entitlement shall ordinarily be limited to the published
      duration of the approved program, with an extension of up to two (2)
      academic years for disability, illness, caregiving, displacement, or other
      circumstances beyond the beneficiary’s reasonable control;
   e. An unused portion may be reassigned once to another qualified spouse or
      child. Total assistance shall not exceed the original program entitlement,
      except as expressly provided herein;
   f. Beneficiaries shall satisfy lawful admission requirements and reasonable
      academic       standards.   Disability-related   accommodations   and   an
      opportunity for academic recovery shall be provided before assistance is
      terminated;
   g. Approved flexible learning, modular programs, microcredentials, and
      recognition of prior learning may be supported when they form part of an
      approved qualification pathway;

conditions herein, and shall be included in annual budget proposals.

Educational assistance shall be paid directly to the institution, except allowances
properly payable to the beneficiary. Institutions shall disclose charges and refund
unearned payments.

Previously received educational assistance shall be credited only against
substantially equivalent expenses or periods of study. An unrelated training grant
shall not automatically extinguish eligibility.

SEC. 8. Disability Pension and Age-Based Benefit — A veteran, including a
military retiree, with a service-connected disability shall receive a monthly
disability pension according to the following disability-rating schedule:

   a. Ten to thirty percent (10%–30%): Four thousand five hundred pesos
      (₱4,500.00);
   b. Thirty-one to forty percent (31%–40%): Five thousand three hundred pesos
      (₱5,300.00);
   c. Forty-one to fifty percent (41%–50%): Six thousand one hundred pesos
      (₱6,100.00);
   d. Fifty-one to sixty percent (51%–60%): Six thousand nine hundred pesos
      (₱6,900.00);
   e. Sixty-one to seventy percent (61%–70%): Seven thousand seven hundred
      pesos (₱7,700.00);
   f. Seventy-one to eighty percent (71%–80%): Eight thousand five hundred
      pesos (₱8,500.00);
   g. Eighty-one to ninety percent (81%–90%): Nine thousand three hundred
      pesos (₱9,300.00); and
   h. Ninety-one to one hundred percent (91%–100%): Ten thousand pesos
      (₱10,000.00), plus One thousand pesos (₱1,000.00) for the spouse and each
      eligible child.

   A veteran aged seventy (70) or older who is not receiving the foregoing
disability pension shall receive an age-based benefit of One thousand seven
hundred pesos (₱1,700.00) monthly. Age alone shall not establish service-
connected disability or entitlement to the higher rating-based schedule.

   These benefits are payable to living eligible beneficiaries and are not
transferable after death. Accrued unpaid installments shall be settled under the


                                                                              13
applicable rules on estates and lawful representatives. The amounts shall be
subject to the inflation adjustment provided herein.

   AFP retirement income shall not disqualify a military retiree from this
disability pension. Disability and survivor entitlements already recognized for

beneficiaries shall continue.

SEC. 9. Disability Assessment and Re-evaluation. — The Disability Rating
Board shall determine service connection and disability ratings through
accessible, evidence-based procedures.

      Assessment shall consider physical and mental conditions, functional
limitations, and the combined effect of multiple service-connected impairments.
Mental health conditions shall be assessed according to appropriate clinical
standards.

      Official service and medical records shall be obtained directly from the
responsible government office whenever available. Missing records shall not, by
themselves, justify denial where credible alternative evidence establishes
entitlement.

      Where the evidence for and against a material issue is approximately
balanced, the issue shall be resolved in favor of the claimant.

      An adverse decision shall state the evidence considered, applicable criteria,
reasons, and available remedies. The claimant shall receive a copy without
charge.

      Examinations may be conducted through accredited regional facilities or
appropriate telemedicine arrangements. Necessary transportation assistance shall
be provided to lowincome claimants required to attend an examination.
                                                                                 14
      Re-evaluation shall not be required for disabilities exempt under Section
10. No existing pension shall be reduced without notice, access to relevant
findings, and an opportunity to contest the proposed reduction.

A dependent supplement payable under Section 8 for a child shall likewise cover
a child described in Section 3(c)(3) or (4), at the applicable statutory rate. No
duplicate supplement shall be paid for the same child for the same period.

SEC. 10. Exemption from Repeated Disability Examination — A beneficiary
shall not be subjected to periodic examination or re-rating where the disability is
static, permanent with no reasonable likelihood of improvement, or the
beneficiary is fifty-seven (57) years of age or older.

      This exemption shall not prevent an investigation based on specific
credible evidence of fraud or identity error, subject to notice and due process.

SEC. 11. Old-Age Pension. — A veteran qualified under Section 4 of this Act
who has attained the age of sixty-five (65) years shall receive an old-age pension
of Ten thousand pesos (₱10,000.00) per month for life, subject to the coordination
of benefits under Section 37.

      The higher old-age pension granted to eligible senior veterans under
Section 12 shall remain applicable, together with its statutory eligibility
requirements and restrictions. No beneficiary shall receive a lower amount by
reason of this Act.

      The pension prescribed herein shall be subject to adjustment under Section
48.

SEC. 12. Special Old-Age Pension for Historic-War Veterans — A living
eligible veteran of World War II, the Korean War, or the Vietnam War shall


                                                                                   15
receive a special old-age pension of Twenty thousand pesos (₱20,000.00)
monthly, instead of the ordinary old-age pension.

      A Korean or Vietnam War veteran receiving an AFP retirement pension
shall not qualify for this special rate, but may receive the ordinary old-age pension
if otherwise eligible.

      The special pension shall not be transferred to dependents or family
members. Eligible survivors shall have their separate entitlements under this Act.
The special pension shall be subject to the inflation provision of this Act. More
favorable amounts already lawfully awarded shall be preserved.

SEC. 13. Pension of Surviving Spouse. — The surviving spouse of a veteran
shall, regardless of age, receive a monthly pension of not less than Ten thousand
pesos (₱10,000.00), or the higher amount lawfully applicable to that beneficiary.

      The pension shall continue until the surviving spouse dies or remarries.

      This pension is separate from the death pension under Section 14.
Eligibility shall not depend upon whether the deceased veteran had reached the
age required for old-age pension.

      A pension expressly limited by law to a living veteran shall not be
transferred by reason of this section. In particular, the special historic-war pension
under Section 12 shall remain subject to its statutory limitations.

Restrictions concerning substantially identical survivor pensions shall be
governed by Section 37.

SEC. 14. Death Pension. — Where military personnel die in line of duty, or a
veteran dies after honorable discharge or separation as a result of a service-



                                                                                   16
connected injury, wound, illness, or disease, the following monthly death
pensions shall be payable:

   a. Fifteen thousand pesos (₱15,000.00) to the surviving spouse, until death or
      remarriage;
   b. Seven thousand five hundred pesos (₱7,500.00) to each eligible child; and
   c. In the absence of an eligible surviving spouse and eligible children, Seven
      thousand five hundred pesos (₱7,500.00) to each eligible surviving parent.

   A child’s pension shall continue until eighteen (18) years of age; or until
twenty-five (25) years of age while unmarried, dependent, and enrolled in a
recognized educational or training program. It shall continue regardless of age for
a child incapable of self-support under Section 3(c)(4), subject to appropriate
verification.

   A temporary interruption of study due to illness, disability, displacement, or
comparable hardship shall not immediately terminate eligibility.

Parents shall retain the right of accretion recognized under this Act. A dependent
parent shall become eligible when entitlement of all surviving spouses and
children has ceased.

      Entitlements of beneficiaries of deceased political prisoners already
recognized under this Act shall be preserved, with the applicable upgraded rates.

No distinction shall be made between combat and noncombat deaths where death
occurred in line of duty. Death attributable to the individual’s willful misconduct
shall not be considered a line-of-duty death.

      Duplicate pension coverage for the same death and beneficiary shall be
governed by Section 37. This limitation shall not affect the distinct surviving-
spouse pension under Section 13.
                                                                                 17
SEC. 15. Commencement and Accrued Pension Obligations — For a new
service – connected disability claim filed within one (1) year after retirement or
separation, entitlement shall commence on the following day; if filed later, it shall
commence upon receipt of the application. New benefits under this Act shall not
accrue earlier than its effectivity. Historical claims and arrears already governed
by a prior law shall retain their lawful commencement dates.

       For ordinary old-age pension, entitlement shall commence upon reaching
sixty-five (65) years if the application is filed within one (1) year thereafter;
otherwise upon receipt of the application. For a beneficiary already aged sixty-
five (65) at effectivity, a claim filed within one (1) year shall commence upon
effectivity.

       For new survivor pensions, entitlement shall commence upon death if a
claim is filed within one (1) year thereafter; otherwise upon receipt of the
application, without prejudice to a more favorable accrued right under prior law.

       Upgraded rates for existing pensioners shall apply from effectivity without
a new application. Disability supplements and age-based benefits shall
commence when the corresponding eligibility conditions are met and verified.

       Payment delays shall not extinguish accrued installments. The prior
replacement of death gratuities under Republic Act Nos. 573 and 610 by death
pensions shall not be reversed by this Act; previously settled options and lawful
refund arrangements shall be honored. Employees’ compensation rights and
benefits under distinct AFP retirement laws shall remain unaffected.

SEC. 16. Health Care and Rehabilitation. — Veterans and their qualified
dependents shall have access to an integrated health care and rehabilitation
program administered by PVAO in coordination with the Department of Health,
PhilHealth, the Veterans Memorial Medical Center, and participating facilities.
                                                                                  18
      The program shall include preventive services, outpatient and inpatient
care, necessary medicines, diagnostics, surgery, dental care, rehabilitation,
prostheses, assistive devices, mental health services, and palliative care.

      Necessary treatment for a veteran’s service-connected condition shall be
provided without out-of-pocket payment for medically necessary services within
the approved treatment package. Medically justified exceptions to standard
packages shall be subject to an expedited review procedure.

      For other conditions and for qualified dependents, PhilHealth and other
applicable health benefits shall be applied first. PVAO shall finance approved
remaining charges according to published schedules, with full coverage of
approved necessary services for low-income beneficiaries.

      Surviving spouses, eligible children, and eligible dependent parents of
veterans who died in line of duty shall remain covered. Other existing medical
entitlements of survivors shall not be diminished.

Emergency treatment shall not be delayed for prior certification. Necessary
emergency care in a nonparticipating facility shall be eligible for reimbursement

Coverage shall include clinically appropriate treatment of post-traumatic stress,
depression, substance use disorders, and other service-related mental health
conditions. Confidentiality shall be protected under applicable law.

No facility shall be reimbursed twice for the same expense. Charges for optional
amenities and medically unnecessary services may be excluded after proper
disclosure.

SEC. 17. Veterans Memorial Medical Center and Continuing Care — The

                                                                               19

SEC. 18. Veterans Health Network. — PVAO and the Department of Health

include a veterans assistance desk, referral arrangements, and an appropriate
allocation of beds according to actual demand.

      An aggregate designated capacity of at least two thousand (2,000) veterans
beds shall be maintained, without requiring beds to remain idle when needed by
other patients.

SEC. 19. Burial Flag and Military Honors — PVAO shall provide a Philippine
flag for the casket of each deceased military retiree or veteran, to be presented to
the next of kin after interment. AFP shall provide appropriate military honors
according to uniform rules and logistical arrangements.

SEC. 20. Burial Assistance. — The person who actually defrayed the funeral
expenses of a deceased veteran shall receive burial assistance of Seventy-five
thousand pesos (₱75,000.00), subject to the requirements of this section and
adjustment under Section 48.

      Only one (1) award shall be paid for each deceased veteran. Where
expenses were shared, payment shall be distributed according to documented
contributions or a written agreement among the persons concerned.

      An application shall be filed within three (3) years from death. PVAO may
allow a further extension of up to two (2) years where incapacity, disaster,

Flag entitlement under Section 19 shall be preserved. Military honors shall be

SEC. 21. National Veterans Welfare and Reintegration Program. — PVAO
shall establish an integrated program covering transition planning, education,
employment, livelihood, family support, housing assistance, health referrals, and
community participation.

Every eligible applicant shall receive an assessment and an individualized
assistance plan where multiple or continuing needs are identified.

Services shall be accessible through regional offices, participating local service
centers, mobile teams, and digital channels. Personal appearance shall be required
only when reasonably necessary.

Priority shall be given to veterans with service-connected disabilities, low-
income households, elderly veterans living alone, and survivors of personnel who
died in line of duty.

SEC. 24. Employment Assistance and Public Employment Preference. —

Preference shall not waive qualification standards, eligibility requirements, or
constitutional merit and fitness principles. Military rank shall not automatically

Military service shall not, by itself, be a ground for denying employment.
Employers shall provide reasonable accommodation required under applicable
disability laws.

SEC. 29. Discounts and VAT Exemption for Veterans. — A veteran certified
by PVAO shall be entitled to a twenty percent (20%) discount and exemption
from value-added tax, if applicable, on the following purchases for the veteran’s
exclusive use:

   a. Medicines, medically necessary supplies, prostheses, and assistive devices;
   b. Medical, dental, rehabilitation, diagnostic, and laboratory services,
      including professional fees;
   c. Actual passenger fares for domestic land, sea, and air transportation;
   d. Accommodation and meals supplied by hotels, lodging establishments,
      restaurants, and comparable food establishments; and
   e. Admission fees charged by cinemas, theaters, museums, and recreational
      facilities.

   The benefit shall apply to purchases made in person or through online
platforms. Delivery and intermediary charges separately imposed by third parties
shall be excluded.

   For shared meals, rooms, or services, the discount and exemption shall apply
only to the veteran’s identifiable share. Commercial purchases, resale, and
expenses principally incurred for another person shall be excluded.

   A five percent (5%) discount shall also apply to basic necessities and prime
commodities purchased for personal or household consumption, up to an
aggregate purchase ceiling of Two thousand five hundred pesos (₱2,500.00) per
week. Unused weekly entitlement shall not accumulate.



                                                                               28
   A five percent (5%) discount shall apply to residential electricity and water
bills where the account is in the veteran’s name, the veteran actually resides in
the dwelling, and monthly consumption does not exceed one hundred kilowatt-
hours (100 kWh) of electricity and thirty cubic meters (30 m³) of water. Only one
(1) household discount per utility account shall be allowed.

The five percent (5%) discounts shall not independently create a VAT exemption.

Where another law or promotional offer provides a discount for the same
purchase, the beneficiary may choose the more favorable benefit. Discounts shall
not be compounded. A VAT exemption independently available under another
law shall remain applicable.

The seller shall compute the twenty percent (20%) discount on the VAT-
exclusive selling price. No VAT shall be charged on a transaction exempted under
this section.

SEC. 30. Essential Discounts for Dependents and Survivors. — The following
shall receive a ten percent (10%) discount on medicines and medically necessary

on the same charge.

   Qualified survivor status for these discounts shall cease upon the termination
of the corresponding eligibility, including remarriage where applicable.

SEC. 31. Tax Treatment of Discounts and Donations. — An establishment
granting a mandatory discount under Sections 29 or 30 may deduct the actual
discount from gross income for income tax purposes in the taxable year granted,
subject to adequate records.

The deduction shall equal the actual mandatory discount, exclusive of VAT, and
shall not be treated as a tax credit. The same amount shall not be deducted twice
or included in another deduction in a manner resulting in duplicate relief.

Qualified donations to the National Government specifically accepted for
programs under this Act shall be deductible to the extent and under the conditions
authorized by the National Internal Revenue Code. Donor’s tax exemption shall
apply only where authorized by that Code.

Nothing herein shall create an exemption for unrelated business income,
commercial transactions, or assets of a veteran or veteran-owned enterprise.

SEC. 33. Emergency Welfare Assistance. — A low-income veteran or qualified

SEC. 35. National Veterans and Beneficiaries Registry. — PVAO shall

SEC. 37. Coordination and Nonduplication of Benefits. — Receipt of an AFP
retirement pension, GSIS or SSS benefit, employment income, or private
insurance payment shall not by itself disqualify a person from benefits under this
Act.

         The express restriction on the special historic-war pension under Section

to an otherwise eligible military retiree receiving an AFP retirement pension.

         Where an existing provision excludes a substantially identical pension
from another government or foreign veterans program for the same contingency
and beneficiary, PVAO shall determine the amount otherwise payable under this
Act and pay any positive difference. The beneficiary may elect the more favorable
benefit where simultaneous receipt is prohibited.

         Benefits serving different purposes may be received concurrently. Old-age
pension, serviceconnected disability pension, education assistance, and health
benefits shall not be treated as identical solely because they originate from public
funds.

         Reimbursement for a particular expense shall not exceed the actual eligible
expense. Lumpsum private insurance proceeds shall not be treated as
reimbursement unless expressly designated as such.

         Only the assistance expressly made income-tested under this Act shall be
subject to an income test.

                                                                               34
SEC. 40. Applications and Adjudication. — PVAO shall operate a single intake

      An adverse decision may be appealed within thirty (30) days to a Veterans

SEC. 48. Preservation of the Real Value of Benefits. — The monetary benefits
under this Act shall be adjusted to preserve their purchasing power through a
regular review and a standby inflation adjustment mechanism.

                                                                                 39
   a. Covered benefits. — This section shall apply to old-age, disability,
      surviving-spouse, and death pensions; dependent pension supplements;
      burial assistance; transition grants; livelihood grant ceilings; care
      allowances; and emergency welfare assistance ceilings prescribed under
      this Act.

   The age-based disability benefit payable under Section 8 shall likewise be
covered. The special historic-war pension established by this Act shall likewise
be covered.

   b. Reference index. — Adjustments shall use the national all-items
      Consumer Price Index published by the Philippine Statistics Authority. A
      rebasing of the index shall not interrupt or alter the calculation; the
      corresponding officially linked series shall be used.
   c. Initial reference period. — For a benefit amount prescribed by this Act,
      the initial reference index shall be the index for the month immediately
      preceding its effectivity.

   For an existing benefit amount retained by this Act, the same reference month
shall apply for prospective adjustments, without prejudice to any adjustment
already due under another law.

   d. Regular adjustment. — On January 1 following the third anniversary of
      the effectivity of this Act, and every three (3) years thereafter, covered
      amounts shall be increased by the positive cumulative percentage change
      between the applicable reference index and the latest September index
      available before the adjustment date.

   An intervening adjustment under paragraph (e) shall reset the reference index
for the affected benefit but shall not postpone the scheduled regular review.


                                                                                40
   e. Standby inflation adjustment. — Notwithstanding              the regular
       adjustment schedule, an earlier adjustment shall automatically become due
       when the three-month average of the Consumer Price Index is at least five
       percent (5%) higher than the applicable reference index.

   The increase shall equal the full positive cumulative percentage change
between that reference index and the three-month average that triggered the
adjustment.

   PVAO shall determine whether the threshold has been reached upon each
monthly release of the Consumer Price Index. An adjustment shall take effect on
the first day of the second calendar month following publication of the index
completing the triggering three-month period.

   After adjustment, the triggering three-month average shall become the new
reference index for the affected benefit.

   f. Coordination with other increases. — An increase in the same benefit
       granted by law during the applicable reference period shall be credited
       against the inflation adjustment, so that compensation for the same
       inflationary period is not duplicated.

   Where the intervening increase equals or exceeds the inflation adjustment
otherwise due, no additional increase shall be required for that period, and the
reference index shall be reset to the index for the month immediately preceding
the effectivity of that increase.

   An increase expressly designated by law as additional to inflation adjustment
shall not be credited against it.




                                                                              41
   g. No reduction. — No benefit shall be reduced because of a decline in the
      Consumer Price Index. An adjustment shall become part of the statutory
      amount for purposes of subsequent calculations.

Adjusted amounts shall be rounded upward to the nearest Fifty pesos (₱50.00).

   h. Grants and ceilings. — For a grant expressed as a maximum amount,
      adjustment shall increase the ceiling and shall not require payment of the
      maximum where assessed need is lower.

   The adjusted amount for a one-time grant shall apply to qualifying events
occurring on or after the adjustment’s effective date. Continuing pension and
allowance payments shall use the adjusted amount from that date.

   i. Certification and publication. — Within thirty (30) days from the release
      of the relevant index, PVAO, in coordination with DBM, shall publish the
      calculation, adjusted benefit schedule, and effective date.

   Certification and publication shall be ministerial. Their delay shall not
extinguish an adjustment that has become due under this section.

   j. Funding. — DBM shall include provision for regular adjustments and a
      reasonable standby inflation reserve in the annual budget proposal.

   Payments shall be charged against appropriations lawfully available for the
corresponding benefit. Where these are insufficient, DBM shall promptly

SEC. 49. Preservation of Rights and Transition to the Replacement
Framework. — Existing pensions, approved scholarships, service recognition,
medical coverage, dependent determinations, and accrued claims under the
repealed laws shall continue under this Act without reapplication, except for
reasonable verification of eligibility for a new or expanded benefit.

      No existing beneficiary shall receive a lower lawful amount or lose an
accrued right solely because of repeal. A more favorable existing award shall
remain payable until the corresponding amount under this Act equals or exceeds
it, subject to lawful termination conditions.

      Pending applications shall be decided under this Act, with preservation of
rights and commencement dates already accrued under prior law. New benefits
and increases shall operate prospectively.

      Existing    PVAO        offices,   VMMC     operations,   records,   assets,
appropriations, liabilities, personnel, agreements, and lawful proceedings shall
continue. The repeal does not abolish PVAO, VMMC, or the Veterans Federation
of the Philippines.

SEC. 50. Fiscal Assessment and Implementation Plan. — Within one hundred
eighty (180) days, PVAO, DBM, and DOF shall submit to Congress a five-year
fiscal and implementation plan identifying:

   a. Eligible populations and expected application rates;
   b. Pension and survivor obligations;
   c. Education, health, care, and reintegration costs;
   d. Administrative and information-system requirements;
   e. Estimated revenue effects of discounts and tax incentives; and

   f. Measures to prevent duplicate payments and improve access.

   The assessment shall inform appropriations and subsequent legislative review.
It shall not authorize an administrative reduction of benefits fixed by law.

SEC. 51. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for initial implementation
shall be charged against available appropriations of PVAO and participating
agencies for the relevant purposes, subject to budgeting and auditing laws.

       Thereafter, amounts necessary for continuing implementation shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act under clearly identified
program items.

       No pension appropriation shall be diverted to discretionary livelihood or
administrative programs in a manner that prevents payment of due pension
obligations.

       Acceptance of donations shall comply with government accounting,
procurement, conflict-ofinterest, and auditing requirements. Donations shall not
substitute for statutory appropriations.

SEC. 52. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty
(180) days, DND, through PVAO, shall issue implementing rules in coordination
with participating agencies and after consultation with veterans, dependents,
survivors, employers, educational institutions, health providers, and local
governments.

       DOF and BIR shall issue the necessary tax regulations. Competent
agencies shall issue sector-specific discount and service rules consistent with this
Act.

         Implementing rules shall establish clear documentary requirements,

SEC. 53. Legislative Review. — Five (5) years after effectivity, the appropriate
committees of Congress shall review coverage, adequacy, access, outcomes, and
fiscal sustainability.

         The review shall consider updated data on veteran populations, disability
and survivor needs, employment outcomes, educational completion, and the
revenue effects of incentives.

No benefit shall lapse solely because the review has not been completed.

SEC. 54. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected shall remain in
force.

SEC. 55. Express Repeal and Relationship to Other Laws. — Republic Act No.
6948, Republic Act No. 7696, Republic Act No. 9396, Republic Act No. 10649,
Republic Act No. 11164, and Republic Act No. 11958 are hereby expressly
repealed and replaced by this Act, subject to its savings and transition provisions.

         The repeal shall not revive provisions previously repealed by those laws,
including superseded pension and death-gratuity provisions. Recognition,
accrued obligations, valid prior awards, and lawful proceedings shall be
preserved as provided herein.
