                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Every glass of milk that a Filipino child drinks in school should be a source
of health for the child and of livelihood for a Filipino farmer. Today, it is almost
always neither Filipino-made nor Filipino-owned. For more than three decades,
the country produced only around one percent of the milk it consumes. The rest,
mostly powdered, was imported from abroad.

      There is good news. Our local dairy farmers are proving what they can do
when given support. According to the National Dairy Authority (NDA), local milk
production reached a record 43.3 million liters in 20251. The NDA now targets
3.3 percent in 2026 and 5 percent by 2028, and is expanding the national dairy
herd through imported dairy cattle. Carabao and goat milk, produced mostly by
our smallholders in the provinces, grew the fastest of all.

       But the law that governs the industry is thirty years old. Republic Act No.
7884, the National Dairy Development Act of 1995, created the NDA and the
Dairy Development Fund at a time when the industry was very different. Its fund
levels have not been updated since 1995, its governing board has no seat for the
smallholder dairy farmers who now drive production growth, and it does not
require government feeding programs to buy from our own farmers. Republic Act
No. 11037, the Masustansyang Pagkain para sa Batang Pilipino Act, encourages
the use of locally produced milk in school feeding, but only “as far as
practicable.”

       This bill strengthens Republic Act No. 7884. It writes current self-
sufficiency targets into law and requires a national dairy roadmap aligned with
the Philippine Development Plan. It expands the powers of the NDA over animal
health and welfare, model farms, milk collection and chilling centers, and market

Board, increases the Dairy Development Fund with a guaranteed share for

importantly, it makes government feeding programs a steady market for Filipino
milk.

     The carabao is a symbol of the Filipino farmer’s strength. With the right
support, it can also be a symbol of our nutrition and our prosperity in the
countryside.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
      STRENGTHENING THE NATIONAL DAIRY AUTHORITY AND
   ACCELERATING THE DEVELOPMENT OF THE PHILIPPINE DAIRY
INDUSTRY TOWARD SELF-SUFFICIENCY, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
  REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7884, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “NATIONAL
  DAIRY DEVELOPMENT ACT OF 1995,” AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Dairy
Industry Development Act of 2026.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes its duties of
pushing for agricultural development, particularly in terms of self-sufficiency,
and promoting public health, with emphasis on better nutritional options for all.
As such, the State acknowledges the necessity to enact measures for bringing
the dairy industry’s development back on track.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Dairy animals refer to cattle, carabaos, and goats raised for milk
          production;
      (b) Dairy self-sufficiency refers to the ratio of the volume of locally
          produced milk to the total national requirement for milk and dairy
          products, expressed as a percentage and computed in accordance with
          the methodology adopted by the National Dairy Authority (NDA);
      (c) Locally produced milk refers to fresh milk produced from dairy animals
          raised in the Philippines, and dairy products processed from such
          milk;
      (d) Smallholder dairy farmer refers to a dairy farmer whose herd size does
          not exceed the threshold set by the NDA; and
      (e) Dairy cooperative refers to a cooperative registered with the Cooperative
          Development Authority whose members are primarily engaged in dairy
          production.

      SECTION 4. Philippine Dairy Industry Roadmap. – Within six (6) months
from the effectivity of this Act, the NDA, in consultation with the Dairy Industry
Board, the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development, and dairy
stakeholders, shall formulate and adopt a Philippine Dairy Industry Roadmap
consistent with the Philippine Development Plan. The Roadmap shall:

      (a) Set annual targets for dairy self-sufficiency, which shall be not lower
          than five percent (5%) by 2028 and shall progressively increase
          thereafter;
      (b) Provide for the build-up of the national herd of dairy cattle, carabaos,
          and goats, including through local breeding and genetic improvement
          programs of the NDA and the Philippine Carabao Center;
      (c) Identify dairy zones and priority provinces for dairy development; and
      (d) Set targets for the share of locally produced milk in government feeding
          programs pursuant to Section 7 of this Act.

      The Roadmap shall be reviewed and updated every three (3) years.

      SECTION 5. Additional Powers and Functions of the NDA. – In addition to
the powers and functions provided under Section 6 of Republic Act No. 7884,
the NDA shall:

      (a) Implement programs to reduce dairy animal mortality and improve
          animal health, nutrition, and welfare, in coordination with the Bureau
          of Animal Industry and consistent with Republic Act No. 8485,
          otherwise known as “The Animal Welfare Act of 1998,” as amended;
      (b) Identify and accredit model dairy farms, which shall serve as centers
          for on-site training, mentorship, and technology demonstration for new
          and expanding dairy farmers;
      (c) Establish, or assist dairy cooperatives and local government units
          (LGUs) in establishing, village-level milk collection and chilling centers;
      (d) Monitor and publish, at least quarterly, the farmgate prices of raw milk
          and the volume and prices of imported milk and dairy products, and
          recommend to the Department of Agriculture (DA) appropriate
          measures under Republic Act No. 8800, otherwise known as the
          “Safeguard Measures Act,” when warranted;
      (e) Implement, in partnership with state universities and colleges, a
          scholarship program for dairy technicians and veterinarians with a
          return service obligation in the local dairy industry; and
      (f) Maintain a national dairy information system on dairy farms, herds,
          production, and markets.

      SECTION 6. Amendment to the Composition of the Dairy Industry Board. –
Section 5 of Republic Act No. 7884 is hereby amended to include as additional
members of the Dairy Industry Board the Secretary of Social Welfare and
Development and two (2) representatives of smallholder dairy farmers, at least
one (1) of whom shall be a woman, to be appointed by the President from among
the nominees of dairy cooperatives and smallholder dairy farmers’ organizations.
The additional representatives shall serve for a term of three (3) years without
reappointment. References in the said Section to the Secretary of Education,
Culture and Sports shall be understood to refer to the Secretary of Education.

       SECTION 7. Locally Produced Milk in Government Feeding Programs. – The
Department of Education and the Department of Social Welfare and
Development shall give priority to locally produced milk in the milk component
of the feeding programs under Republic Act No. 11037, otherwise known as the
“Masustansyang Pagkain para sa Batang Pilipino Act,” and shall progressively
increase its share in accordance with the targets set in the Roadmap. For this
purpose, the said agencies and implementing LGUs may procure locally
produced milk directly from accredited dairy cooperatives and other farmers and
fisherfolk enterprises pursuant to Republic Act No. 11321, otherwise known as
the “Sagip Saka Act,” subject to food safety standards.

     SECTION 8. Partnership with Local Government Units. – LGUs are
encouraged to support dairy farmers within their territorial jurisdiction by:

      (a) Identifying and developing communal grazing and pasture areas;
      (b) Promoting locally produced milk and dairy products as specialty
          products of the locality; and
      (c) Providing counterpart support for milk collection and chilling centers.

     In all such undertakings, LGUs shall give priority to smallholder dairy
farmers and dairy cooperatives.

      SECTION 9. Credit and Insurance. – The NDA shall coordinate with the
Land Bank of the Philippines, the Agricultural Credit Policy Council, and other
government financial institutions to establish accessible credit windows for
smallholder dairy farmers and dairy cooperatives, including under Republic Act
No. 11901, otherwise known as the “Agriculture, Fisheries and Rural
Development Financing Enhancement Act of 2022.” The Philippine Crop
Insurance Corporation shall provide free or subsidized insurance coverage for
dairy animals of smallholder dairy farmers, subject to its guidelines.
      SECTION 10. Dairy Development Fund. – The annual allocation for the
Dairy Development Fund under Section 14 of Republic Act No. 7884 is hereby
increased to not less than Five hundred million pesos (₱500,000,000.00):
Provided, That at least fifty percent (50%) of the Fund shall be allocated to
programs that directly benefit smallholder dairy farmers and dairy cooperatives.

      SECTION 11. Reporting. – The NDA shall submit an annual report to the
President and to the appropriate committees of the Senate and the House of
Representatives on the implementation of this Act, including the progress toward
the self-sufficiency targets in the Roadmap and the share of locally produced
milk in government feeding programs.

      SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the NDA, in coordination with the DA, the
Department of Education, the Department of Social Welfare and Development,
the Department of the Interior and Local Government, and dairy stakeholders,
shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the implementation of
this Act.

      SECTION 13. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations
of the NDA. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SECTION 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts not affected
shall remain in full force and effect.

       SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules, and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
