                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The 1987 Philippine Constitution expressly enshrines the State’s commitment to
afford full protection to labor, both local and overseas. In fulfilling this mandate, the
Philippine government has long institutionalized mechanisms for legal and consular
support through the Legal Assistance Fund (LAF) and the Agarang Kalinga at Saklolo
para sa mga OFWs na Nangangailangan (AKSYON) Fund.

        However, despite these mechanisms, the delivery of justice is often hindered by
bureaucratic limitations, funding inflexibility, and procurement laws that delay the hiring
of foreign legal representation during critical times of crisis.

       Currently, Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) frequently face legal and judicial
challenges abroad. These range from investigation and detention to arrest and
indictment, and are often without the benefit of immediate legal assistance. As
emphasized in our own Constitutional tenets, any person under investigation for the
commission of an offense has the fundamental right to be informed of their right to
remain silent and to have competent and independent counsel. When our OFWs are
subjected to foreign custodial investigations, the lack of immediate, culturally sensitive,
and language-appropriate legal counsel puts them at a severe systemic disadvantage.
Funding flexibility alone is inherently reactive. What is currently lacking in our legal
assistance framework is a proactive and systemic first-response mechanism.

       This proposed measure, the "Overseas Filipino Protection and Legal Assistance
Enhancement Act," adopts the vital funding expansions proposed in existing measures,
while introducing two unique, groundbreaking provisions:
   1.​ The Establishment of a Global Network of Retained Foreign Legal
       Counsels. Instead of scrambling to procure foreign lawyers only after an OFW is
       arrested, the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) and the Department of Migrant
       Workers (DMW) shall be mandated to pre-vet and retain a standing network of
       foreign law firms in countries with high OFW populations.

   2.​ A 24/7 Overseas Legal Crisis Hotline. A dedicated, round-the-clock hotline
       manned by Philippine legal officers who can provide immediate, preliminary legal
       advice (such as the translation of their right against self-incrimination) while the
       retained foreign counsel is being dispatched.

       By marrying expanded financial flexibility with a proactive network of pre-retained
legal defenders, this Act ensures that the Philippine government's promise of protection
is not just a constitutional ideal, but a highly operational, real-time reality for our
modern-day heroes.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                              AN ACT
 EXPANDING THE USE OF THE LEGAL ASSISTANCE FUND AND THE AGARANG
    KALINGA AT SAKLOLO PARA SA MGA OFWs NA NANGANGAILANGAN
 (AKSYON) FUND, ESTABLISHING A GLOBAL NETWORK OF RETAINED LEGAL
    COUNSELS AND A 24/7 LEGAL CRISIS HOTLINE, AMENDING FOR THE
 PURPOSE SECTION 26 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8042, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
   THE “MIGRANT WORKERS AND OVERSEAS FILIPINOS ACT OF 1995,” AS
   AMENDED, AND SECTION 14 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11641, OTHERWISE
       KNOWN AS THE “DEPARTMENT OF MIGRANT WORKERS ACT”


Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

                                 CHAPTER I
                             GENERAL PROVISIONS

      Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Overseas Filipino
Protection and Legal Assistance Enhancement Act.”

        Section 2. Amendment of the Uses of the Legal Assistance Fund. Section 26
of Republic Act No. 8042, otherwise known as the "Migrant Workers and Overseas
Filipinos Act of 1995," as amended, is hereby further amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 26. Uses of the Legal Assistance Fund. - The Legal Assistance Fund
      created under the preceding section, WHICH HAS BEEN RETAINED BY THE
      DEPARTMENT OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS (DFA) PURSUANT TO SECTION 14 OF
      REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11641, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE 'DEPARTMENT OF
     MIGRANT WORKERS ACT', shall be used to provide legal AND CONSULAR
     ASSISTANCE services to OTHER overseas Filipinos in accordance with the
     guidelines, criteria and procedures TO BE promulgated BY THE DFA. The
     expenditures to be charged against the LEGAL ASSISTANCE Fund shall include
     the fees for the foreign lawyers to be hired to represent OVERSEAS FILIPINOS
     facing charges, EXPENSES FOR THE filing OF LABOR AND/OR CRIMINAL
     cases against erring or abusive employers OR OTHER PERSONS abroad, bail
     bonds to secure temporary releases, and other litigation expenses:

     PROVIDED, THAT THE LEGAL ASSISTANCE FUND SHALL, AT ALL TIMES,
     BE MADE AVAILABLE FROM THE TIME OF THE INVESTIGATION,
     DETENTION, ARREST OR INDICTMENT OF THE OVERSEAS FILIPINOS ALL
     THE WAY TO THE TRIAL PROPER, AND AT ALL LEVELS OF APPEAL
     THEREAFTER:

     Provided, FURTHER, That at the end of every year, the Department of Foreign
     Affairs shall include in its report to Congress the status of the Legal Assistance
     Fund, including the expenditures from the said fund duly audited by the
     Commission on Audit (COA):

     Provided, FURTHERMORE, That the hiring of foreign legal counsels AND
     OTHER RELEASES OF FUNDS FOR OTHER EXPENSES RELATED TO THE
     PROVISIONS OF THIS SECTION, when circumstances warrant urgent action,
     shall be exempt from the coverage of Republic Act No. 9184 or the Government
     Procurement Reform Act IN COUNTRIES AND TERRITORIES WHERE THERE
     ARE NO MIGRANT WORKERS OFFICES YET, THE DFA SHALL EXTEND
     LEGAL AND OTHER FORMS OF ASSISTANCE TO OFWS LOCATED IN SAID
     AREAS."

       Section 5. Establishment of a Global Network of Retained Foreign Legal
Counsels. To ensure that the constitutional right to competent and independent counsel
is universally afforded to Filipinos abroad from the exact moment of custodial
investigation, the DFA and the DMW shall jointly establish a "Global Network of
Retained Foreign Legal Counsels."

       The DFA and DMW are hereby authorized to enter into standing retainer
agreements with reputable foreign law firms and legal practitioners in countries with a
high concentration of Overseas Filipinos and OFWs. These retained counsels shall act
as designated first-responders to immediately represent Filipinos during sudden police
raids, arrests, and preliminary investigations, bypassing the delays of ad-hoc
procurement.

       The following shall serve as the Minimum Standards for Accreditation and Hiring:
   (a)​Proven expertise in criminal, labor, or immigration law in the host country;
   (b)​Good standing with the relevant bar or legal authority;
   (c)​Demonstrated experience in handling cases involving foreign nationals;
   (d)​Capacity to provide immediate response within a reasonable time frame;
   (e)​No record of professional misconduct or human rights violations;
   (f)​ Ability to coordinate with Philippine diplomatic and migrant offices.

       The DMW shall maintain a review and audit mechanism to ensure quality,
accountability, and cost-efficiency of legal services rendered. Retained counsels shall
act as first responders during arrests, detention, or investigations, ensuring immediate
legal representation without procurement delays.

       Furthermore, the DMW shall establish a performance monitoring system to
evaluate retained counsels based on responsiveness, case outcomes, ethical
compliance, and client feedback. Retainer agreements shall include termination clauses
for non-performance, negligence, or misconduct.

       Section 6. Establishment of a 24/7 Overseas Legal Assistance and Crisis
Hotline. The DMW, in coordination with the DFA, shall establish a 24/7 digital and
telephonic Overseas Legal Crisis Hotline. The hotline shall be manned by
Philippine-licensed lawyers and trained paralegals who shall provide immediate, remote
preliminary legal advice to distressed OFWs.

        When an OFW is subjected to a foreign custodial investigation, the Hotline Legal
Officers shall be responsible for:
    (a)​Immediately dispatching the nearest retained foreign legal counsel from the
        Global Network;
    (b)​Translating and explaining fundamental rights, such as the right to remain silent
        and the right against self-incrimination, to the detained OFW; and
    (c)​Providing real-time updates and psychosocial support coordination to the OFW's
        family in the Philippines.

       Section 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Migrant Workers, as the lead agency, in
consultation with the Department of Foreign Affairs, shall promulgate the necessary
implementing rules and regulations to ensure the effective enforcement of this Act.

       Section 8. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department of
Migrant Workers and the Department of Foreign Affairs.
       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        Section 9. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall continue to be in full force and
effect.

       Section 10. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

       Section 11. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
