                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Poverty, youth attrition in education, and limited access to
socioeconomic opportunities continue to expose many Filipino families
to persistent vulnerabilities. Young people from low-income families are
often compelled to discontinue their education or forego skills
development due to financial constraints, while parents struggle to
adequately support their children’s learning and wellbeing. These
interrelated challenges weaken family resilience and limit opportunities
for upward social mobility.

      The Constitution recognizes the vital role of the youth in nation-
building. Article II, Section 13 mandates the State to “promote and
protect their physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social
wellbeing.”

      The Tara, Basa! Tutoring Program (TBTP), hereinafter referred to
as the Program, was established as a flagship initiative of the National
Government through Executive Order No. 76, series of 2024, to directly
address these vulnerabilities through a youth-centered and family-
focused social protection approach. At its core, the Program empowers
youth from poor or vulnerable families by providing them
with capability-building and subsidies, while engaging them in
meaningful community-based roles. The Program enhances partner-
beneficiaries’ soft and life skills, including leadership, communication,
emotional intelligence, adaptability, and self-motivation. In doing so,
the Program supports youth retention in tertiary education or re-
engagement in productive activities, while mobilizing them as partners
in community development and nation-building. This advances the
Administration’s vision of a Bagong Pilipinas where every young Filipino
is empowered to contribute to nation-building.

      Since its pilot implementation in 2023, the Program, implemented
by the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), in
partnership with the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), Higher
Education Institutions (HEIs), Department of Education (DepEd), Local
Government Units (LGUs), National Youth Commission (NYC), and
other stakeholders, has demonstrated its effectiveness as an integrated
social protection intervention. It aligns with the Philippine Development
Plan 2023–2028, the Philippine Youth Development Plan 2023–2028,
and the State’s commitment to inclusive growth and human capital
development.

The Program also supports Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) Nos.
1, 4, and 8, which respectively aim to end poverty, ensure inclusive and
quality education, and promote full and productive employment and
decent work for all.. Complementing youth empowerment, the Program
extends targeted support to parents or guardians through participation
incentives and structured sessions designed to enhance parenting
capabilities. These interventions aim to fortify the family support
system, thereby securing human capital investment in children’s
education and ensuring their holistic growth and development. As an
incidental yet important benefit of strengthened youth and family
engagement, the Program provides tutorial services to children who are
struggling readers or non-readers, contributing to improvements in
foundational literacy.

      At present, the Program is implemented through existing policies
that enabled pilot testing and expansion. However, a permanent legal
framework is necessary to ensure stable funding, nationwide coverage,
and strengthened accountability. Institutionalizing the Program
through legislation will firmly establish it as a national social protection
program that prioritizes youth empowerment, family resilience, and
community engagement, with educational support as a complementary
outcome.

      In view of the foregoing, the government shall institutionalize a
program that is holistic, inclusive, evidence-driven, and community-
based that empowers youth and families alike, while combating
learning gaps.

      Hence, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                        AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING “TARA, BASA! TUTORING PROGRAM” AS A
 NATIONAL THREE-PRONGED SOCIAL PROTECTION PROGRAM
CATERING TO THREE BENEFICIARIES: YOUTH BENEFICIARIES,
  PARENTS OR GUARDIANS, AND CHILDREN BENEFICIARIES,
  PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Title

This Act shall be known as the “Tara, Basa! Act of 2026.”


SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy

Article II, Section 9 of the 1987 Constitution declares that “the State
shall promote a just and dynamic social order that will ensure the
prosperity and independence of the nation and free the people from
poverty through policies that provide adequate social services, promote
full employment, a rising standard of living, and an improved quality of
life for all.”

Article II, Section 13 of the 1987 Constitution declares that “the State
recognizes the vital role of the youth in national building and shall
promote and protect their physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and
social wellbeing. It shall inculcate in the youth patriotism and
nationalism, and encourage their involvement in public and civic
affairs.”

Article XIV, Section 2 (4) of the 1987 Constitution declares the “State
shall encourage non-formal, informal, and indigenous learning
systems, as well as self-learning, independent, and out-of-school study
programs particularly those that respond to community needs.”
Pursuant to the mandates of the Constitution, it is hereby declared the
policy of the State to reduce the vulnerabilities of poor and
disadvantaged individuals and families by adopting a community-
based, intergenerational, and evidence-informed approach that
prioritizes youth development and empowerment through appropriate
social protection programs; strengthens parental participation and
family resilience; and provides educational support services for children
as an enabling and complementary intervention.

Executive Order (EO) No. 123 (s. 1987) as amended by EO No. 292 or
the "Administrative Code of 1987," mandates the Department of Social
Welfare and Development (DSWD) to formulate, develop, and implement
plans, programs, and projects on social welfare and development aimed
at providing assistance to the most vulnerable members of society.

SECTION 4. Establishment of the “Tara, Basa! Tutoring Program Act of
2026”

There is hereby established the Tara, Basa! Tutoring Program,
hereinafter referred to as the Program, as a developmental and
transformative educational assistance initiative of the DSWD. The
Program shall contribute to the reduction of the vulnerabilities of its
beneficiaries through the provision of social protection services,
including, but not limited to, capability-building activities, subsidies,
and case management.

The Program shall adopt a three-pronged social protection approach
and shall cater to the following categories of partner beneficiaries: (a)
youth beneficiaries; (b) parents or guardians; and (c) children
beneficiaries.


SECTION 5. Objectives

The overall objective of the Program is to reduce the vulnerabilities of
low-income individuals by providing socioeconomic opportunities for
youth beneficiaries and parents or guardians through the provision of
subsidies, while fostering community engagement and educational
support. Specifically, the Program aims to:

  A. Empower youth beneficiaries by providing capability-building
     activities and subsidies to reduce their socioeconomic
     vulnerabilities and strengthen their participation in nation-
     building;

  B. Reduce the vulnerabilities of parents or guardians through
     participation incentives and interventions that enhance their
     capacities to support their children’s overall wellbeing;

  C. Contribute to the improvement of reading proficiency and
     foundational literacy of children beneficiaries who are struggling
     readers or non-readers, as a supportive outcome of strengthened
     youth and family engagement; and

  D. Strengthen collaboration among NGAs, LGUs, academic
     institutions, and other stakeholders to enhance Program support,
     resource mobilization, and community participation.


SECTION 6. Coverage

The Program shall adopt a three-generation targeting approach,
focusing on youth beneficiaries, parents or guardians, and children
beneficiaries, collectively referred to as “partner beneficiaries” to ensure
comprehensive support across the family unit.

The Program shall cover the following:

  A. The primary beneficiaries shall be youth beneficiaries, who are
     individuals aged eighteen (18) years old or older from low-income
     or vulnerable families, including enrolled students, out-of-school
     youth, or unemployed graduates, who may be engaged as tutors,
     youth development workers, or in other roles under the Program;
     and

  B. The secondary beneficiaries shall be (1) parents or guardians of
     children beneficiaries, particularly those belonging to low-income
     or vulnerable families; and (2) children beneficiaries, who are
     below 18 years old endorsed by implementing partners of the
     Program.


SECTION 7. Targeting Mechanism for Program Sites

The following shall be the basis for selecting the Regional and Local
implementation areas:

  A. Areas with high attrition rate, poverty incidence, and low literacy
     rates;

  B. Geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs);

  C. Indigenous Peoples (IP) and urban poor communities; or

  D. Areas where NGAs, Faith-Based Organizations (FBOs), Civil
     Society Organizations (CSOs), Social Welfare and Development
     Agencies (SWDAs), LGUs, Non-Government Organizations (NGOs),
     CRCFs, or implementing partners express readiness and
     commitment to co-implement the Program.


SECTION 8. Program Components

The Program shall include the following core components:


  A. Capability-building activities for youth beneficiaries to equip
     them with the necessary skills and knowledge to effectively
     conduct the sessions;

  B. Parenting sessions facilitated by youth beneficiaries, particularly
     youth development workers (YDWs) for parents or guardians to
     further strengthen their full potentials and capacities to ensure
     the wellbeing of their children;

  C. Tutorial sessions conducted by youth beneficiaries, particularly
     tutors, that are child-centered, age-appropriate, culture-
     sensitive, and gender-responsive;

  D. Provision of direct subsidies to partner-beneficiaries, subject to
     existing government accounting and auditing rules;
  E. Development and distribution of knowledge products and
     learning kits to partner-beneficiaries to aid in the conduct of
     sessions; and/or

  F. Conduct of case management provided to partner-beneficiaries,
     such as referral services to other social protection and
     development programs, as may be deemed necessary and
     appropriate.


SECTION 9. Settings for the Sessions

The sessions shall be conducted in the following settings, based on the
profile, needs, and circumstances of the partner-beneficiaries:

  a. School/classroom-based implementation- in partnership with
     the DepEd the tutorial sessions shall be conducted at the
     elementary schools of struggling readers and non-readers
     identified through DepEd’s assessment tools or Madrasah;

  b. Community-based implementation - Out-of-school children
     beneficiaries or learners at risk of dropping out, or Children in
     Need of Special Protection (CNSP) endorsed by the LGUs or
     implementing partners; and/or

  c. Center or facility-based implementation - children beneficiaries
     needing tutorial sessions based on the assessment of case
     managers or Social Workers, and endorsed by the Center Head.


SECTION 10. Monitoring, Evaluation, Reporting, and Congressional
Oversight

The DSWD, in coordination with implementing partners, shall establish
and maintain a results-based monitoring and evaluation system to
assess the outputs, outcomes, and impact of the Program.

The DSWD shall submit to Congress an annual report on the
implementation of this Act, including but not limited to: (a) number and
profile of partner-beneficiaries served; (b) utilization of funds and
subsidies released; (c) Program outputs, outcomes, and impact
indicators, including literacy improvement and youth retention in
education; (d) challenges encountered and corrective actions taken; and
(e) policy and budgetary recommendations.

A Joint Congressional Oversight Committee on the Program is hereby
created, composed of representatives from the House of Representatives
and the Senate, to oversee the implementation of this Act and ensure
faithful compliance with its objectives.


SECTION. 11. Safeguarding Policies

      All activities under the Program shall adhere to existing child
protection laws, gender sensitivity policies, and safeguarding
standards. Youth beneficiaries and implementers shall undergo
mandatory orientation on child protection and ethical conduct.
Accessible grievance and reporting mechanisms shall be established
and enforced.


SECTION 12. Implementing Agencies and Institutional Arrangements

      The DSWD shall serve as the lead implementing agency of the
Program and shall coordinate with CHED, HEIs, NYC, DepEd, LGUs,
and other relevant stakeholders for the effective implementation.

     The DSWD shall strengthen its existing National Program
Management Office that oversee policy development, implementation,
monitoring, and evaluation of the Program and Regional Program
Management Offices that manage, coordinate, and oversee the
implementation of the Program at the regional level.


SECTION 13. Appropriations

      The funds necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DSWD
and included in the annual General Appropriations Act which is
necessary for the continued implementation of this Act.


SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations

     The DSWD, in consultation with CHED, HEIs, NYC, DepEd, LGUs,
and other relevant stakeholders, shall issue the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act within ninety (90)
days from its effectivity.


SECTION 15. Repealing Clause.

      All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, rules and
regulations and other issuances or part or parts thereof, which are
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.
SECTION 16. Separability Clause

      If for any reason, any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in force and effect.


SECTION 17. Effectivity Clause

      This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
