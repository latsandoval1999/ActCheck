20                                              EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   The country’s peatlands are more than ecosystems to be protected. In July 2025, a
23   peatland fire in the Agusan Marsh affected approximately 152 hectares. In 2026,
24   another peatland fire burned for nearly two weeks, although the exact area affected had
25   yet to be determined. These incidents show the continuing vulnerability of peatlands to
26   fire, drainage, land conversion, and other human activities.
27
28   Despite their importance, peatlands have received less attention in environmental and
29   climate policies compared with other ecosystems. Their value as carbon sinks, water
30   regulators, and habitats for biodiversity is often overlooked. This measure aims to
31   address this gap by providing specific rules for the protection, restoration, and proper
32   management of peatlands. It provides for the mapping and monitoring of peatlands, the
33   prevention and control of peatland fires, the restoration of damaged areas, and the
34   regulation of activities such as drainage, conversion, reclamation, and peat extraction.
35
36   By protecting our peatlands, we protect one of nature’s most important stores of carbon.
37
38   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

20                                AN ACT
21      PROVIDING THE REGULATORY FRAMEWORK TO CONSERVE, PROTECT,
22         RESTORE, AND SUSTAINABLY MANAGE PEATLANDS AND THEIR
23       RESOURCES TO ENHANCE THE COUNTRY’S RESILIENCE TO CLIMATE
24                               CHANGE
25
26   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
27   Congress assembled:
28
29        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Peatland
30   Conservation Act”.
31
32          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to promote the
33   conservation, restoration, and sustainable development of the country’s peatland and
34   peatland resources; enhance the resilience of the Filipino people towards climate
35   change adaptation and mitigation, disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM)
36   consistent with the principles of sustainable and inclusive economic development,
37   poverty reduction, and biodiversity conservation, while advancing the right to a balanced
38   and healthful ecology in harmony and rhythm of nature.
39
40         SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. — This Act shall apply to all peatland and
41   peatland resources nationwide within public and private lands.
42
43            SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:
44
45          (a) Adaptation refers to the adjustment in natural or human systems in response
46   to actual or expected climatic stimuli or their effects, which moderates harm or exploits
47   beneficial opportunities.
48
49          (b) Climate change refers to a change of climate which is attributed directly or
50   indirectly to human activity that alters the composition of the global atmosphere and
51   which is in addition to natural climate variability observed over comparable time periods.
52          (c) Carbon sink refers to carbon reservoirs and conditions that take in and store
53   more carbon than they release.
 1          (d) Disaster Risk Reduction and Management refers to the systematic process
 2   of using administrative directives, organizations, and operational skills and capacities to
 3   implement strategies, policies, and improved coping capacities in order to lessen the
 4   adverse impacts of hazards and the possibility of disaster.
 5
 6          (e) Ecosystem-based approaches refer to the use of biodiversity and ecosystem
 7   ®) functions and services as part of an overall adaptation strategy to help people adapt
 8   to the adverse effects of climate change. This term may refer to a wide range of
 9   ecosystem management activities to increase the resilience and reduce the vulnerability
10   of people and the environment, including climate change and disasters.
11
12         (f) High conservation value refers to the biological, ecological, social, or cultural
13   values of outstanding significance at the national, regional, or global level or of critical
14   importance at the local level that need to be appropriately managed and maintained or
15   enhanced.
16
17          (g) Mitigation, in the context of climate change, refers to human intervention to
18   address anthropogenic emissions by sources and removals by sinks of all greenhouse
19   gas, including ozone-depleting substances and their substitutes.
20
21           (h) Nature-based solutions refer to actions that can be taken to protect, conserve,
22   restore, sustainably use, and manage natural or modified terrestrial, freshwater, coastal,
23   and marine ecosystems which address social, economic, and environmental challenges
24   effectively and adaptively, while simultaneously providing human well-being ecosystem
25   services, resilience, and biodiversity benefits.
26
27          (i) Peat refers to dead and partially decomposed plant material that has
28   accumulated under high water saturation or waterlogged conditions. Peat soils are
29   histosols or organic soils with cumulative organic layers comprising more than half of
30   the upper 80 centimeters of the soil surface containing 35% or more organic matter
31   (35% or more loss on ignition) or 18% or more organic carbon.
32
33          (j) Peatlands refer to wetland ecosystems characterized by the accumulation of
34   peat at the surface and subsurface of the soil. Major characteristics of peatlands are the
35   presence of peat and permanent water logging. Peatlands may have vegetation in the
36   form of trees, shrubs, grasses, and mosses.
37
38         (k) Wetlands refer to a wide variety of inland habitats such as marshes,
39   peatlands, floodplains, rivers and lake; and coastal areas such as saltmarshes,
40   mangroves, intertidal mudflats and seagrass beds, and also coral reefs and other
41   marine areas no deeper than six (6) meters at low tide; and as well as human-made
42   wetlands such as dams, reservoirs, rice paddies, and wastewater treatment ponds and
43   lagoons.
44
45         (l) Wise use refers to the maintenance of the ecological character of peatlands
46   achieved through the implementation of nature-based solutions and ecosystembased
47   approaches within the context of sustainable development.
48
49          SEC. 5. Peatlands as Carbon Sinks. — The conservation and restoration of
50   peatlands sought to be enhanced under this Act shall be aimed at enabling the country
51   to manage peatlands sustainably, taking into account their value as natural solutions to
 1   address climate change such as by functioning as carbon sinks, thus providing
 2   opportunities for the country to achieve multiple benefits for biodiversity and climate
 3   resiliency, including financing through market-based instruments and mechanisms.
 4
 5           SEC. 6. Implementing and Monitoring Agencies. — The Department of
 6   Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), through the Biodiversity Management
 7   Bureau (BMB), shall be the lead agency in monitoring the overall implementation and
 8   compliance with this Act. The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
 9   through the Bureau of Local Government Supervision, shall assist in the implementation
10   of this Act, in coordination with the Department of Agriculture — Bureau of Soils and
11   Water Management, Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical, and Astronomical Services
12   Administration, Department of Agrarian Reform, DILG — Bureau of Fire Protection,
13   DILG — Bureau of Local Government Development, Department of Public Works and
14   Highways, Climate Change Commission (CCC), Department of Tourism, Department of
15   Science and Technology, Philippine Council for Agriculture, Aquatic and Natural
16   Resources Research and Development, National Commission on Indigenous Peoples,
17   National Water Resources Board, National Museum, concerned DENR Offices,
18   academe, and concerned local government units (LGUs) for specific peatland areas.
19
20           The DENR, in coordination with the above-mentioned agencies, shall exercise
21   the following powers and functions within their mandates:
22
23         (a) Formulate, implement and monitor policies, rules, regulations, plans and
24   programs;
25         (b) Enter into partnership and engagements with interested partners;
26         (c) Develop best practices on the wise use of peatland areas;
27         (d) Strengthen and implement communication, education and public awareness
28   programs on peatland conservation; and
29         (e) Convene regular meetings to update and share technical progress report to
30   be shared among the coordinating agencies.
31
32           SEC. 7. Role of Local Government Units. — The municipal, city, or provincial
33   LGUs having territorial jurisdiction over the peatland and its resources shall prioritize
34   peatlands for protection under Republic Act (RA) No. 7586 or the “National Integrated
35   Protected Areas System Act of 1992”, as amended by RA No. 11038, declaration as
36   Critical Habitat under RA No. 9147 or the “Wildlife Resources Conservation and
37   Protection Act”, or as local conservation area or other effective area-based conservation
38   measures through the ordinances of LGUs. They shall also include the protection and
39   conservation of peatlands and their resources in their respective Comprehensive Land
40   Use Plans, Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plans, Local Climate
41   Change Adaptation Plans, and other local plans in consultation with concerned
42   agencies. They shall coordinate with the DENR and the DILG in the monitoring of
43   peatlands and their resources within their jurisdiction and in compliance with this Act.
44   The DENR and the DILG shall provide the technical and financial support to all
45   concerned LGUs for the management and protection of peatlands within their territorial
46   boundaries.
47
48          SEC. 8. Integration and Mainstreaming of Peatland Protection and
49   Sustainable Use. — All national government agencies and offices and LGUs shall
50   integrate and mainstream peatland protection, conservation, restoration, and
51   sustainable use into their policies, ordinances, rules and regulations, plans, programs,
 1   projects, and development planning processes. Peatland conservation and restoration
 2   targets shall be identified in all plans related to biodiversity, forestry, land degradation,
 3   climate change and disaster risk reduction and management, as applicable. LGUs shall
 4   conduct mapping, assessment, delineation, and inventory of potential peatlands in their
 5   jurisdiction. Concerned LGUs shall also directly consult with the DENR, the CCC, and
 6   affected communities in the development and implementation of their plans, programs,
 7   and projects within or having impact on peatlands, especially on their ecosystem
 8   services, including their value as effective carbon sinks, consistent with the Nationally
 9   Determined Contributions of the Philippines under the Paris Agreement on Climate
10   Change.
11
12          SEC. 9. Development of a National Peatland Conservation and Restoration
13   Program. — A National Peatland Conservation and Restoration Program, aligned with
14   existing international commitments and national policies, plans, and programs, shall be
15   developed by the DENR, in coordination with other concerned agencies, sectors, and
16   stakeholders, within one year from the effectivity of this Act, to provide direction,
17   support, and guidance to the LGUs and stakeholders in the development and
18   implementation of their local peatland conservation and restoration programs. The
19   National Peatland Conservation and Restoration Program shall define national targets
20   for the conservation and restoration of peatlands and the development of its national
21   coordinating mechanism.
22
23          The Program shall include the following strategies:
24
25          (a) Inventory, mapping, delineation, assessment, and monitoring of peatlands.
26   All peatlands in the country shall be inventoried and assessed. Peatland assessment
27   including bio-capacity, carrying capacity, carbon storage, contributions to ecological
28   connectivity, and ecosystem services assessment and valuation as a natural capital of
29   peatland ecosystems shall be periodically conducted to measure the full extent of the
30   value of peatlands and guide agencies on their conservation, restoration, and
31   sustainable use;
32
33          (b) Assessment of vulnerability to climate change and disaster risk management;
34
35         (c) Peatland carbon stock assessment, accounting, verification, and crediting.
36   The nationwide assessment of carbon stocks shall be completed three (3) years after
37   the completion of a national peatland inventory;
38
39          (d) Assessment and monitoring of greenhouse gas flux and emissions. Results
40   of nationwide assessment of greenhouse gas fluxes from peatlands shall be included
41   in the reports submitted to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate
42   Change;
43
44         (e) Restoration and rehabilitation of degraded peatlands. Nature-based solutions
45   and ecosystem-based approaches shall be primarily employed in the restoration of the
46   ecosystem services lost from peatland degradation;
47
48          (f) Regulation against peatland drainage, conversion and reclamation, peat
49   extraction, and deliberate setting of fire within the peatland or immediately adjacent
50   areas that may affect the peatland;
 1         (g) Enforcement of legal easement zones, danger zones, and other land use
 2   setbacks. All concerned government agencies and LGUs shall strictly implement land
 3   use setback provisions imposed by law;
 4
 5          (h) Peat fire early warning systems, prevention, control, and monitoring;
 6
 7           (i) Research, development, and extension. Extensive researches that will
 8   determine the value of peatlands and peatland resources as well as best practices that
 9   will sustain these values shall be recognized as integral part of management strategies.
10   The use of innovative technologies on sustainable peatland management and practices
11   on peatland ecosystem-based adaptation shall also be promoted and made accessible
12   particularly to vulnerable communities;
13
14          (j) Knowledge management and peatland information system;
15
16           (k) Implementation of soil and water conservation research and development
17   efforts and technologies;
18
19          (l) Measures to address invasive alien species in peatlands;
20
21         (m) Establishment and promotion of peatland best management practices and
22   capacity building, including Public-Private Partnership;
23
24         (n) Communication, education, participation, capacity-building, and awareness
25   program;
26
27          (o) Collaboration with indigenous peoples and local communities in the
28   management and wise use of the peatlands including providing sustainable livelihood
29   for peatland dependent communities, in coordination with the Department of Trade and
30   Industry, Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, and other concerned
31   agencies;
32
33         (p) Harmonization and integration of local and traditional ecological knowledge
34   and practices with science-based knowledge systems; and
35
36          (q) Environmental impact assessments for any development activity proposed
37   within the peatland or adjacent areas or the catchment of the peatland which may
38   potentially have a negative impact on the peatland.
39
40          SEC. 10. Land Classification of Peatland. — Peatlands that are classified as
41   agricultural lands shall be reclassified to either forest lands or national parks if, upon the
42   recommendation of DENR based on a suitability assessment, such peatlands have high
43   conservation value and still provide ecosystems services particularly water and climate
44   regulation.
45
46           All peatlands that have been alienated and disposed for agricultural purposes
47   after classification as agricultural lands but are not being utilized for such purpose shall
48   be rehabilitated, restored, protected, and conserved. For purposes of rehabilitation,
49   restoration, protection, and conservation, based on suitability assessment, the
50   Secretary of the DENR may acquire by purchase, donation, or expropriation, lands or
51   interests therein including the acquisition of usufruct, establishment of easements or
 1   other undertakings appropriate in protecting the peatlands. Peatlands with high
 2   conservation value and which provide ecosystem services, particularly water and
 3   climate change regulation, shall be recommended for such undertakings.
 4
 5         SEC. 11. Prohibited acts. — In addition to acts or omissions already penalized
 6   under existing laws, the following offenses in peatlands within public domain shall be
 7   punishable under this Act:
 8
 9          (a) Drainage of peatlands for agriculture development, industrial, urban, illegal
10   logging, transport, infrastructure, and other land use;
11
12           (b) Forest clearance and degradation for peatland conversion to agriculture,
13   mining, residential, or any other kind of conversion such as logging, human-induced fire,
14   illegal peat reclamation, illegal peat dewatering, illegal peat quarrying, or illegal
15   harvesting of wildlife;
16
17         (c) Peatland deforestation and fires for land clearance, land claims and conflicts,
18   and resource extraction;
19
20         (d) Peatland clearing or earth moving for conversion to fish ponds or heavy
21   developments including recreational facilities that are not suitable to peatlands;
22
23          (e) Dumping of wastes and sediments from mining, factories, quarrying
24   operations, and sewer, as well as farm water carrying pesticide residues or pollutants
25   such as swine or livestock effluents thereby causing pollution to the peatlands; and
26
27          (f) Introduction of invasive alien species in peatlands.
28
29          SEC. 12. Penalties. — Any person who violates the provision/s of this Act shall,
30   upon conviction, be punished with a fine of not less than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos
31   (P200,000.00) or imprisonment of not less than six (6) months, or both, at the discretion
32   of the court.
33
34          SEC. 13. Reporting Responsibility. — The DENR Regional Executive Director
35   (RED) shall submit to the DENR Secretary, through the BMB, an annual
36   accomplishment report on peatlands located within the territorial jurisdiction of the RED.
37   The report shall include the conditions and benefits of the biological resources and
38   ecosystem services of the peatland to be submitted by the RED, through channels, to
39   the Secretary of the DENR every five (5) years. Consequently, the BMB shall, likewise,
40   prepare a report on the implementation of the National Action Plan for the Protection
41   and Sustainable Use of Philippine Peatlands every five (5) years and shall submit the
42   same to the President of the Philippines and both Houses of Congress. The report on
43   the status of peatlands shall contain an updated national action plans on biodiversity,
44   forestry, land degradation, climate change, and disaster risk reduction and
45   management, among others.
46
47         SEC. 14. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
48   implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year's appropriations of
49   the DENR. Thereafter, such amount shall be included in the annual General
50   Appropriations Act.

 2   the effectivity of this Act, the DENR shall, in close coordination with concerned agencies
 3   and stakeholders, issue rules and regulations necessary to implement the provisions
 4   hereof.
 5

 8   be in full force and effect.
 9

13
