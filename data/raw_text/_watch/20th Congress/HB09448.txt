                                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The rapid expansion of digital platforms has fundamentally reshaped how Filipino
children learn, communicate, and participate in society. While online spaces offer
educational and social benefits, they also expose children to serious risks such as
online sexual exploitation, cyberbullying, harmful content, algorithmic manipulation, and
the misuse of personal data. Recent international developments, including Australia’s
decision to restrict social media access for children under sixteen1, underscore the
growing global consensus that stronger state action is necessary to protect minors in
digital environments.

       In the Philippines, existing laws recognize the special protection owed to
children, including Republic Act No. 7610 or the Special Protection of Children Against
Abuse, Exploitation, and Discrimination Act, and Republic Act No. 11930 or the
Anti-Online Sexual Abuse or Exploitation of Children (Anti-OSAEC) Act. However, these
laws were enacted before the widespread dominance of algorithm-driven platforms,
immersive digital environments, and large-scale data profiling of minors. There remains
a regulatory gap in imposing affirmative, child-specific duties on digital platforms
operating in the country.

       Children today are growing up in a digital environment where online platforms,
social media applications, games, educational tools, and content-sharing services play
an increasingly central role in their daily lives. Filipino children are among the most
active internet users in Southeast Asia. According to UNICEF2, Filipino children spend

       Recent global research underscores the scale of the problem. A 2023 report by
the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) found that digital
platforms routinely collect extensive data on minors, including location data, browsing
behavior, biometric identifiers, and inferred psychological traits, often without meaningful
parental awareness or consent.

       In the Philippine context, online harms affecting children are rapidly increasing.
Data from the Philippine National Police Anti-Cybercrime Group shows a sharp rise in
online sexual exploitation of children, identity misuse, and data-driven grooming cases
since the COVID-19 pandemic. The Department of Justice has likewise noted that
social media platforms and messaging services are frequently used to collect, store,
and disseminate children’s personal information without adequate safeguards. These
risks are exacerbated by algorithm-driven content systems that amplify harmful material
and normalize intrusive data collection practices.

        While the Republic Act No. 10173, also known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012,
establishes a general framework for the protection of personal information, it does not
contain explicit provisions addressing the unique vulnerabilities of children in digital and
online environments. The Act treats children’s data largely the same as adult data,
relying on general consent and security standards that are insufficient in the context of
algorithmic profiling, targeted advertising, and large-scale data processing by global
technology platforms. In contrast, international best practices now recognize children as
a distinct category of data subjects requiring heightened protection.

       For example, the European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)3
mandates enhanced safeguards for children, including limits on profiling, stricter
consent requirements, and child-specific transparency obligations. The United
Kingdom’s Age Appropriate Design Code4 further requires platforms to design systems
with the best interests of the child as a primary consideration. These measures reflect a
growing global consensus that child data protection must be proactive, preventive, and
embedded in system design.

        Amending the Data Privacy Act to explicitly integrate child-specific data
protection standards will align Philippine law with these international norms while
strengthening accountability for digital platforms operating in the country. By imposing
heightened duties of care, requiring child-focused data protection impact assessments,
restricting harmful data practices such as behavioral profiling and targeted advertising,
and empowering the National Privacy Commission to enforce child-centered standards,
the proposed amendment seeks to close critical regulatory gaps.

      Ultimately, protecting children’s personal data is not merely a privacy issue but a
matter of child welfare, digital safety, and human rights. In an era where data-driven
technologies increasingly shape children’s experiences, opportunities, and
vulnerabilities, the State has a duty to ensure that the legal framework evolves
accordingly. The immediate passage of this measure is therefore earnestly sought.

        This measure does not seek to prohibit or ban children from accessing social
media or digital platforms. Instead, it adopts a child-rights-based and proportionate
approach by imposing stringent safety, transparency, and data protection obligations on
platforms, enhancing parental controls, and strengthening government oversight. By
shifting responsibility toward platform accountability and informed supervision, this Act
ensures that children can safely benefit from digital technologies without sacrificing their
rights to protection, privacy, and development.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                              AN ACT
  STRENGTHENING THE PROTECTION OF CHILDREN’S PERSONAL DATA IN
   DIGITAL AND ONLINE ENVIRONMENTS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
 REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10173, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “DATA PRIVACY ACT
                              OF 2012”

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

                                    CHAPTER I
                                GENERAL PROVISIONS

      Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Child Online Data
Protection and Safety Amendment Act.”

       Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - Consistent with Article II, Sections 11 and 13
of the 1987 Constitution, the State recognizes the dignity of every human person and
affirms its duty to protect children from all forms of abuse, exploitation, and harm,
including those arising in digital environments.

       It is well within the policy of the State to ensure that children are afforded special
protection online by strengthening platform accountability, imposing heightened duties
of care on digital service providers, and institutionalizing safeguards that promote safe,
age-appropriate, and rights-respecting digital spaces for minors. Regulation shall be
exercised in a manner that promotes transparency, innovation, and freedom of
expression, while ensuring that the best interests of the child remain paramount.
        The State recognizes that children are particularly vulnerable in digital
environments and that online platforms have a heightened responsibility to prevent
harm, misuse of personal data, and exposure to age-inappropriate or exploitative
content. Consistent with this policy, the State shall adopt regulatory measures that
ensure child-safe digital environments while respecting freedom of expression, access
to information, and parental authority.

​     Section 3. Amendment of Section 3 of Republic Act No. 10173 (Definition of
Terms). Section 3 of Republic Act No. 10173 is hereby amended by adding new
paragraphs to read as follows:

             “(m) Child – refers to any person whose age is below eighteen (18) years
             old.”
             “(n) Children’s Personal Data – refers to any personal information
             relating to a child, including but not limited to identifying information, online
             identifiers, behavioral data, location data, biometric data, educational
             records, and any data generated through a child’s use of digital or online
             services.”
             “(o) Digital Platform Accessible to Minors – refers to any online
             service, application, website, or system that is likely to be accessed or
             used by children, whether intentionally designed for children or not.”

​     Section 4. Amendment of Section 12 of Republic Act No. 10173 (Criteria for
Lawful Processing of Personal Information). Section 12 of Republic Act No. 10173 is
hereby amended by adding new paragraphs to read as follows:

             “In the case of children’s personal data, lawful processing shall require the
             consent of a parent or legal guardian, except where processing is
             necessary to protect the vital interests of the child or as otherwise
             provided by law. Such consent shall be obtained through age-appropriate
             and verifiable mechanisms.”

​     Section 5. Amendment of Section 13 of Republic Act No. 10173 (Sensitive
Personal Information and Privileged Information). Section 13 of Republic Act No.
10173 is hereby amended by adding new paragraphs to read as follows:

             “Children’s personal data shall be presumed to require a higher level of
             protection. Digital platforms accessible to minors shall be prohibited from
             processing such data for purposes of targeted advertising, behavioral
             profiling, or automated decision-making that may adversely affect the
             rights, safety, or well-being of the child.”

​     Section 6. Amendment of Section 16 of Republic Act No. 10173 (Rights of
the Data Subject). Section 16 of Republic Act No. 10173 is hereby amended by adding
new paragraphs to read as follows:

             “The rights of a child as a data subject shall be exercisable by the child’s
             parent or legal guardian, without prejudice to the evolving capacities of the
             child, and personal information controllers shall ensure that such rights are
             practically accessible and effective.”

​     Section 7. Amendment of Section 20 of Republic Act No. 10173 (Security of
Personal Information). Section 20 of Republic Act No. 10173 is hereby amended by
adding new paragraphs to read as follows:

             “Where personal information relates to children, personal information
             controllers shall adopt enhanced security measures commensurate to the
             heightened risks faced by minors in digital environments.”

​      Section 8. Insertion of a New Section on Heightened Duties of Care for
Children. A new section is hereby inserted after Section 20 of Republic Act No. 10173,
to read as follows:

             “SEC. 20-A. Heightened Duties of Care for Children’s Personal Data.
      Personal information controllers and processors whose services are accessible
      to minors shall:
         (a)​Design and operate their systems with the best interests of the child as a
             primary consideration;
         (b)​Implement enhanced organizational, physical, and technical safeguards
             specifically calibrated to protect children from data misuse, exploitation,
             and digital harm;
         (c)​Collect and process only such children’s personal data as is strictly
             necessary for the provision of the service;
         (d)​Provide privacy notices, terms of service, and consent requests in clear,
             age-appropriate, and child-friendly language; and
         (e)​Establish accessible mechanisms for parents, guardians, and children to
             exercise data subject rights.”
​    Section 9. Insertion of a New Section on Data Protection Impact
Assessments for Children. A new section is hereby inserted after Section 21 of
Republic Act No. 10173, to read as follows:

              “SEC. 21-A. Child Data Protection Impact Assessments. Personal
       information controllers operating digital platforms accessible to minors shall
       conduct periodic Child Data Protection Impact Assessments to identify, evaluate,
       and mitigate risks to children arising from the processing of their personal data,
       including risks from profiling, algorithmic content recommendation, and data
       sharing practices.”


​     Section 10. Role of the National Privacy Commission. Section 7 of Republic
Act No. 10173 is hereby amended by adding a new paragraph to read as follows:

       “The National Privacy Commission shall promulgate rules, in coordination with
       the Department of Information and Communications Technology and other
       relevant agencies, to enforce child-specific data protection standards for digital
       platforms accessible to minors.”

​      Section 11. Penalties. Any violation of the provisions relating to the protection of
children’s personal data shall be subject to the penalties provided under Republic Act
No. 10173, without prejudice to additional liabilities under special laws protecting
children.

      Section 12. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriation of the
National Privacy Commission. Thereafter, the amount needed for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       Section 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the National Privacy Commission, in coordination with the
Department of Information and Communications Technology, shall promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

      Section 14. Separability Clause. If for any reason, any section, subsection,
clause or term of this Act is held invalid or unconstitutional, such parts not affected by
such declaration shall remain in full force and effect.
       Section 15. Repealing clause. All existing laws, orders, decrees, rules and
regulations or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
amended, modified or repealed accordingly.

       Section 16. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
