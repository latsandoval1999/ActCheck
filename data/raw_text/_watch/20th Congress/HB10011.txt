                              EXPLANATORY NOTE


        The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), through PSA Board Resolution No.
18, Series of 2025, approved and adopted the definition of the “informal sector” as
comprising “economic units whose production is mainly intended for the market for
the purpose of generating income and profit, but which are not formally recognized as
producers of goods and services separate from the own-use production activities of
the owner-operators’ households (informal household unincorporated market
enterprises).”

        Based on the May 2026 Labor Force Survey, 36.2% of the country's total
working population or 17.8 million are informal sector workers. We cannot discount
the significance of the informal sector's contribution to the Philippine economy. Yet,
the informal sector is unseen in government programs, services, and social protection,
and struggling at the margins of Philippine society, vulnerable and exposed to the
vagaries of poverty and inequality. They are the faceless unaccounted informal sector
workers or workers in an informal employment arrangement. They are the
self-employed, home workers, industrial workers, ambulant vendors, small transport
operators, small and landless farmers, artisanal fisherfolk, intermittent non-corporate
construction workers, waste pickers and recyclers, petty retailers, barter traders,
small-scale miners, and quarry workers, entertainers, beauticians, laundry persons,
hairdressers, on-call domestic helpers, barkers, cargo-handlers, farmers, fisherfolks,
and all other livelihood that fall under the definition of PSA Board Resolution No. 18.
At various government instrumentalities, they are the forever job order workers, the
barangay health workers, barangay tanod, barangay nutrition scholars, barangay
daycare workers, and volunteers during disasters. They are the informal economy
workers in the public sector.

       They are not covered by the Labor or Civil Service Code. They live in
substandard conditions and are vulnerable to exploitation, working long hours at
meager wages, without benefits, and under deplorable working conditions. They have
limited access to productive resources, justice, and preventive programs for
work-related diseases and risks and are underrepresented in policy-making bodies
relevant to their issues and welfare. They lack contributory social protection such as
those provided by the Social Security System (SSS), Philippine Health Insurance


                                                                                     1
Corporation (PhilHealth), and affordable housing programs such as through the
Pag-IBIG Fund. At most, they do not even qualify to tax based social protection
programs.

       There have been several attempts to enact the Magna Carta for Workers in the
Informal Sectors. Therefore, it is high time that the 20th Congress will finish the
work to provide rights and benefits to workers in the informal economies, provide
them with affordable social protection and security in the workplace, among others.

                                 CHAPTER 1
                             GENERAL PRINCIPLES

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Workers in the Informal
Economy (WIE) Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to promote a just and
dynamic social order that shall ensure the prosperity and independence of the nation
and free the people from poverty through policies that provide adequate social
services, and promote decent, environmentally sound and full employment, resilient
and sustainable informal economic units, a rising standard of living, and an improved
quality of life for all.

       Towards this end, the State, in order to help uplift the lives of workers in the
informal economy, shall:

       (a) Protect, promote, and fulfill the rights of every worker and economic
           operators, including gender equality, non-discrimination, right to
           self-organization, just and humane conditions of work, access to inclusive
           and adequate social protection programs and services, access to
           productive resources, institutional market, and justice, security in the
           workplaces, and the right to represent their organizations in a continuing
           process of consultation and social dialogue, with equitable membership in
           relevant tripartite bodies and additional membership distinct for informal
           sector in the local government unit (LGU) local special bodies;

(b)​ Establish and fully implement an integrated and coherent policy with the
     just transition guidelines at all levels of government, progressive
     formalization of informal economic units and informal economy workers
     and make them visible in all relevant national and local statistics;

(c)​ Develop and enhance their technical, special, entrepreneurial, and other
     skills and capabilities necessary towards their resilience, empowerment
     and enjoyment of benefits and entitlements associated with the formal
     economy, and become more productive and self-reliant citizens, thereby
     ensuring participation in mainstream economic activities based on their
     own pace and self-determination;

(d)​ Progressively eliminate child labor through the creation of more quality
     jobs and resilient and sustainable enterprises for adults, effective
     enforcement of laws against child labor, elimination of gender-based
     discrimination against child workers, improved access to universal
     education and social protection, and elimination of cultural factors that
     directly or indirectly tolerate, and even accept child labor;

(e)​ Develop the local economy by maximizing the potential and contribution
     of informal economy enterprises and business activities through the
     establishment of an “Inclusive Enterprise Ecosystem Framework” that not

(f)​ Encourage and support workers and units in the informal economy to form

(g)​ Advance the social, political, economic, and reproductive rights of women

(h)​ Include the concerns of workers in the informal economy in the policies,

     agencies, government financial institutions, and local government units;

(i)​ Advance the autonomy and empowerment of local government units and
     their capability to be more responsive to the needs of the informal sector
     and of the workers in the informal economy among their constituents;

(j)​ Amend the taxation power by incorporating tax and business permit
     incentives to encourage registration of economic units and workers and
     workers alongside a toolkit to guide the local government units (LGUs) in
     imposing taxes and permits to the transitioning informal economic units
     and workers;

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

       (a) Economic units refer to informal economies such as:

           1. Market units (corporations, quasi-corporations, and household market
       enterprises);
           2. Non-market units (government and non-profit institutions serving
       households); and
           3. Households that produce goods or services for own final use
       (households).

         (b) Employer refers to a natural person or group or partnership of people or a
corporation for whom a paid worker renders productive employment or service;
         ​
         (c) Informal economy refers to the economic activities by workers and
economic units that are, in law or practice, not covered or insufficiently covered;
         ​
         (d) Informal economy workers’ organization refers to a group or groups of
informal workers, whether formally constituted or otherwise, organized with the
primary objective of promoting the rights and welfare of workers in the informal
economy;
         ​
         (e) Informal employment refers to any activity of persons to produce goods or
provide services for pay or profit that is not effectively covered by formal
arrangements such as commercial laws, procedures to report economic activities,
income taxation, labour legislation and social security laws and regulations providing
protection against economic and personal risks associated with carrying out the
activities;
         ​
         (f) Informal sector comprises economic units whose production is mainly
intended for the market with the purpose of generating income and profit, but that are
not formally recognized as producers of goods and services distinct from the own-use
production by the owner-operators’ household (informal household unincorporated
market enterprises);

        (g) Own-account workers refer to persons who, working on their own account
or with one or more partners, hold the type of job defined as a self-employed job, and
have not engaged continuously any employees to work for them during the reference
period;
        ​

        (h) Security of and at the workplace refers to the right of every worker and
informal economic unit to an enabling environment that guarantees and protects the
spaces, facilities, and structures to undertake their work, including the right to feel
safe in one’s workspace, legal security of tenure and freedom from discrimination,
risk, danger, doubt, anxiety, or fear of being removed, evicted or prevented from
working;
        ​
        (i) Self-employed worker refers to any person who has no employer and who
works for oneself by producing goods or services for the market;​

        (j) Social protection refers to policies and programs that seek to reduce
poverty and vulnerability to risks and enhance the social status of the marginalized by
promoting and protecting livelihood and employment, protecting against hazards and
sudden loss of income, and improving people’s capacity to manage risk, as well as all
other interventions that support communities, households, and individuals, both
women and men, and realizing their rights as citizens through their full participation
in decision-making affecting their access to and control over resources necessary to
maintain and sustain a decent and secured life;
        ​
        (k) Social protection floor refers to a set of guarantees ensuring access to
essential health services, income support for the education of children, health and
nutrition, income assistance for economically active groups who do not have
sufficient earnings in case of emergencies, and financial support for the elderly
population and women informal workers in difficult and vulnerable circumstances
like single mothers, and women survivors of abuse and violence;
        ​
        (l) Social and solidarity economy units refer to enterprises and organizations
including cooperatives as defined under Republic Act (RA) No. 9520, otherwise
known as the “Philippine Cooperative Code of 2008”, mutual benefit societies,
associations, foundations, and social enterprises which produce goods, services, and
knowledge that meet the needs of the community they serve, through the pursuit of
specific social and environmental objectives and the fostering of solidarity, including
labor market interventions, social insurance, social welfare, safety nets, disaster risk
reduction and management mechanisms, adaptive measures in the context of climate
change, affordable housing, and other provisions necessary for marginalized and
vulnerable groups to adequately address and manage risks, vulnerabilities, and threats
to workers’ livelihood activities and overall quality of life;
        ​
        (m) Volunteer refers to workers in government instrumentalities who do not
occupy plantilla positions, who receive salaries but not benefits, who are contracted
out for specific periods of time, and who receive allowances or honoraria, including
project staff, barangay health workers, barangay tanod, barangay nutrition scholars,
barangay daycare workers, barangay volunteers, and other vulnerable workers for
disasters, among others;
        ​       ​
        (n) Workers in the informal economy refer to a group of persons who have no
employer-employee relationship in order to earn a living such as small farmers,
tenants, sharecroppers, or laborers of rural and agricultural lands, small fisherfolk,
fisherfolk without fishing equipment, on-call fish workers, porters and batillos,
home-based workers who are independent producers of goods or services, industrial

homeworkers, self-employed who are engaged in subcontracting arrangements,

markets, sari-sari stores owners and workers, land and sea transportation drivers,

economic opportunities, and institutional representation. A typology of formalization

decent work, and inclusive economic growth as a comprehensive process involving
legal, economic, social, labor, and institutional dimensions, consistent with ILO
Recommendation No. 204 and the ILO MNE Declaration.

                         Chapter II
 RIGHTS AND BENEFITS THROUGH PROGRESSIVE FORMALIZATION

      SEC. 5. Workers in the Informal Economy. – Workers in the informal
economy (WIE) shall have the same basic rights accorded to all other workers, as


                                                                                         8
enshrined in the Constitution, Presidential Decree No. 442, as amended, otherwise
known as the “Labor Code of the Philippines”, and relevant international human
rights instruments and international labor standards. Accordingly, the State shall
guarantee all WIE the following rights:

              (a)​    Security of and at workplaces;

              (b)​    Freely chosen or accepted work, and availment of technical and
       vocational guidance and training programs;

               (c)​    Just and favorable conditions of work, including provision for
       work-life balance such as child care and other facilities to reduce and
       redistribute family and domestic responsibilities;

              (d)​   A living wage and equal remuneration for work of equal value
       without distinction of any kind, in particular for women who shall be
       guaranteed equal wages for work similar or equivalent to those done by men;

              (e)​    Equal opportunity for promotion, subject to no other
       considerations than seniority and competence;

              (f)​    Safe and healthy working conditions safeguarding general,
       occupational, and reproductive health;

               (g)​    Basic services such as affordable medical care, reproductive
       and other health services including modern family planning, low-cost housing,
       water, sanitation, electricity, and transport;

              (h)​   Rest, leisure and reasonable limitation of working hours and
       periodic holidays with pay, as well as remuneration for public holidays
       whenever applicable;

              (i)​    Maternity and paternity benefits, as provided for by law and
       providing cash maternity benefits for at least three (3) months after birth,
       which are tax based for women in the informal sector who cannot afford SSS
       contributions;

               (j)​   Equal access to education, skills training, and economic
       resources to develop self-reliance, especially of children and young persons,
       without any discrimination, including organizing support groups for women
       and transportation allowance while they are on trainings such as inTESDA;

               (k)​     Self-organization to collectively negotiate with government and
       other entities in the promotion of their welfare and advancement of their
       interests, free from any political interference;

              (l)​   An adequate standard of living for workers and their families,
       adequate food, clothing, and shelter, and the continuous improvement of such
       standard;



                                                                                     9
               (m)​ Freedom from any form of discrimination, violence,
       exploitation including sexual exploitation, harassment, abuse, and any form of
       inhumane treatment which debases, degrades, or demeans the intrinsic worth
       and dignity of the worker as a human being;

              (n)​    Accessible, affordable, and appropriate social protection as
       defined in Section 3(k) of this Act;

              (o)​    Equal treatment before the law;

               (p)​    Substantial participation in policy and decision-making
       processes through their organizations and the use of social dialogue, including
       access to information and other resources relevant to the promotion and
       protection of their rights and welfare; and

              (q)​    Access to public procurement, including advice and reserving
       quotas for informal economic units.


       SEC. 6. Own-Account Workers and Informal Economic Units. –
Own-account workers as well as informal economic units, being the working poor’s
primary instruments to address and overcome poverty, shall be accorded the following
preferential rights by the State:

   (a)​ Infrastructure support such as farm to market roads, common, affordable, and
        secure workplaces and facilities, merchandising centers, farmers’ markets or
        bagsakan with proper storage facilities, and inventory bulk-buying centers;

   (b)​Policy support to promote and protect local and domestically-produced
       products and services, and support in enhancing their participation in the value
       chain process;

   (c)​ Ease and facilitation of access to markets, including capacity-building to
        access institutional market and maximize e-marketing facilities;

   (d)​Access to affordable, appropriate and adequate financial services, including,
       among others, collateral-free and gender-responsive credit at low interest;

   (e)​ Access to appropriate and adequate machinery, equipment, and other
        technologies, with the end in view of increasing productivity, enhancing the
        quality of products and services, and growth;

   (f)​ Protection from unjust dislocation from places where economic activities are
        conducted with the provision of relocation before demolition;

   (g)​Measures against racketeering, extortion, and harassment, by both State and
       non-State elements;




                                                                                    10
   (h)​Common workplaces, standard technology facilities, adequate and affordable
       marketing facilities such as economic freedom parks, merchandising centers,
       and inventory bulk-buying centers;

   (i)​ Fiscal relief, including among others exemption from taxes, fees and other
        assessments, to be put into place by concerned National Government agencies
        and local government units;

   (j)​ Affordable and customized social security and insurance programs;

   (k)​Promotion of Circular Economy from household and industrial wastes for the
       waste pickers and recognizing them as active labor market; and

   (l)​ Consider all WIE or working poor as active labor market

        SEC. 7. Legitimate Organizations of Workers in the Informal Economy. –
Organizations of workers in informal employment, including unions, cooperatives,
social and solidarity economic units or organizations, and mutual benefit associations,
shall have the right to the following:

               (a)​   Freely function and act as the representatives of their members
       in policy and decision-making processes, collective negotiations, and other
       similar bodies and processes in the context of tripartism and multi-sector;

               (b)​  Establish, join or affiliate with local unions, national
       federations or confederations, and international trade union organizations
       which shall be duly recognized, protected and supported by the National
       Government;

               (c)​   Access information from concerned government institutions
       and other parties that are pertinent to the protection and promotion of the
       rights and welfare of their members;

              (d)​    Own property, real or personal, for the use and benefit of their
       organizations and members;

               (e)​   Sue and be sued under their registered names; and

               (f)​    Undertake all other activities, not contrary to law, designed to
       benefit their organizations and members.

       SEC. 8. Informal Economy Workers’ Organization. – Workers in the
Informal Economy (WIE) shall be encouraged and supported to organize themselves
into self-help organizations, cooperatives, associations, federations, and
confederations for their mutual benefit, protection, representation, and other
legitimate purposes. Special attention shall be given to the organization and
empowerment of women and other vulnerable groups of WIE, including the
following:




                                                                                    11
        (a) Rural Workers Associations may be expanded into or complemented by
informal economy associations in order to recognize and cover workers and economic
units outside rural areas;

        (b) Informal economy enterprises or economic units/operators shall be
encouraged to register with cooperatives, local government unit (LGU) of residence,
the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), or the Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI), consistent with the proposed regulatory and promotional framework

       (c) Associations of informal economy workers or the working poor shall

(RWAs).

                               Chapter III
                  SECURITY OF AND AT THE WORKPLACE

       SEC. 9. Designation of Workplaces. – Local government units (LGUs) shall,
in coordination with organizations of WIE within their jurisdiction, affected
communities, and other relevant groups, endeavor to identify, designate, and design a
system of assignment on the following:

        Productivity and merchandizing centers as viable workplaces for informal
workers which may include markets and vacant areas near markets, vacant public
spaces and spaces which may be a private property that the LGU may acquire, lease
or negotiate with legitimate organizations of WIE for lease under a memorandum of
agreement; and routes, terminals, and specific lanes for small transport workers in
land and water. The process of identifying and designating such workplaces shall be
determined in the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) of this Act, and the
process shall have the substantial participation of relevant WIE organizations and
stakeholders. Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the local
government units (LGUs) shall enact the necessary ordinances designating
appropriate workplaces, routes, terminals, and specific lanes. For areas with existing
routes and ordinances, the LGUs shall undertake a review and rationalization process
in consultation with small land and water transport stakeholders to ensure the
inclusion and integration of e-trikes and other electric vehicles in accordance with the
implementation of the Electric Vehicle Industry Development Act (EVIDA).

       SEC. 10. Eviction and Demolition. – Workers in the informal economy or
employment shall not be evicted from their homes and workplaces without legal
ground as provided by RA 7279, otherwise known as the “Urban Development and
Housing Act of 1992,” and provision of livelihood opportunities, adequate availability
of water and electricity, as well as decent conditions of living in accordance with the
law. The logistical viability for the displaced workers shall be given priority in
determining resettlement areas. There shall be no demolition without ensuring
appropriate resettlement.

       The LGU shall provide designated areas with viability for the ambulant and/or
sidewalk vendors before demolition. The parks and playground, a portion of the area


                                                                                     12
will be for organized vendors with regulations on safety and health, waste disposal,
lactation station and clean toilets.

       SEC. 11. Confiscation of Materials and Impounding of Vehicles. – In cases
where demolition or eviction is warranted, any person, government agency, or their
agents who conduct the same shall issue a duly signed itemized receipt of all
products, goods, and other materials seized from vendors and other affected WIE.

        Tricycles, pedicabs, and other modes of transportation shall not be impounded
for violations of license, registration, or traffic regulations unless the said vehicles
were utilized in the conduct of criminal activities. In cases of violation of traffic
regulations, the issuance of a traffic violation ticket shall suffice.

       SEC. 12. Relocation of Vending Sites. – Before any public market is closed,
sold, or demolished, all market vendors shall first be relocated by the LGU to a
temporary or new public market. Notice of the intention to close, sell, or demolish any
public market shall be made to all concerned vendors at least sixty (60) days before
the actual transfer or relocation to another market site. Within the sixty (60)-day
period after issuance of the notice, the LGU shall conduct consultations with affected
vendors on selecting the relocation site and implementing the relocation.

        Vendors, ambulant or otherwise, occupying public places not previously
designated as vending sites shall be provided with viable temporary sites by the LGU.
Notice of temporary transfer shall be given to the vendors at least fifteen (15) days
before the actual transfer. This shall likewise apply to vendors granted with permits
but whose workplaces are withdrawn from the list of allowable vending sites. Any
change in the list of allowable vending sites shall only be done after consultations
with affected vendors.

        If a new public market is constructed in place of an old one, market vendors
with stalls displaced from their workplaces shall be assured of an affordable stall
assignment in the new market. Displaced ambulant vendors shall, as much as
practicable, also be given appropriate selling sites.

       SEC. 13. Relocation of Terminals. – Designated terminals for tricycles,
pedicabs and other similar modes of transportation shall not be arbitrarily relocated
without prior notice and consultation with small transport groups, legitimate
organizations of workers in the informal economy, affected community and other
relevant sectors. Relocation of terminals can only be effected through an ordinance:
Provided, That after enacting such ordinance, a notice of intention to relocate
terminals shall be made to affected transport groups and community at least sixty (60)
days before the actual relocation.

         SEC. 14. Right to Self-organization and Collective Bargaining. – Informal
workers that are registered and are part of the formal economy shall have the right to
form, join or assist in the formation of a labor organization of their own choosing for
purposes of collective bargaining, for mutual aid benefit, and to engage in concerted
activities which are not contrary to law.




                                                                                     13
       All those operators of the informal sector must be identified as informal
employers if they have more than one unit in the case of small transport, jeepneys,
public market stalls, and others of the same type in order to make the informal
employer accountable for registration to contributory social protection.

                                   Chapter IV
                              SOCIAL PROTECTION

        SEC. 15. Institutionalization of the Social Protection Floor. – The State
shall support, sustain, strengthen, and institutionalize social protection initiatives for
Workers in the Informal Economy (WIE) within the three-sector framework of: (i) the
Informal Sector, (ii) the Formal Sector, and (iii) the Household Own-Use Production
and Community Sector, through the convergence and harmonization of resources and
programs of relevant government agencies. Such initiatives shall ensure the
continuous provision of social security and health insurance subsidies for WIE, while
also supporting programs for the unemployed, children, women in difficult and
vulnerable circumstances, and older persons, consistent with applicable laws and
evolving social protection standards.

       SEC. 16. Social Welfare Efforts. – The Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD) shall consolidate its strategic social welfare efforts to address
the needs of the WIE, including direct assistance, policy development and community
engagement for the workers and treat as active labor market those livelihood
beneficiaries.

         SEC. 17. Role of the Department of Labor and Employment. – The
Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall undertake labor market
interventions that provide adequate protection and promote decent work for Workers
in the Informal Economy (WIE). It shall likewise ensure timely and effective action
on labor concerns, including security of tenure, job generation, workers’ rights, and
other related issues affecting WIE. The DOLE shall lead the advocacy and
institutional adoption of the new conceptual and operational definitions of the
informal economy among government agencies, legislators, and other relevant
stakeholders. In coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), the Department of Finance (DOF), and other livelihood and
enterprise development agencies, the DOLE shall develop a framework and typology
for the formalization of informal economic units. Such framework shall include
measures on simplified and affordable registration systems, reduced business permit
costs, improved regulatory compliance, and expanded access to labor standards
protection, social security, and other government support programs, among others.

        SEC. 18. Role of the Department of Trade and Industry. – The Department
of Trade and Industry (DTI) shall ensure that all agencies implementing livelihood
programs shall ensure that financial literacy is mainstreamed into capacity building
especially of the start-ups and/or budding entrepreneurs. The DTI shall also ensure the
transfer of technology on production and access to the institutional market.

       SEC. 19. Role of the Department of Finance. - The Department of Trade
and Industry (DTI) shall ensure that access to credit is available and continuity of
“Basic Deposit Account” for financial inclusion of those who were underserved and


                                                                                       14
unserved by the banks. It shall also develop Toolkit to Guide the LGU towards an
incentivized LGU taxation especially the business permit costs.

        SEC. 20. Social Safety Nets. – The State shall ensure that the social safety
nets provided by the DOLE, DSWD, Department of Agriculture (DA) and other
government agencies shall be easily accessible to the WIE and their organizations.
These include conditional cash transfers, unconditional cash transfers, school feeding
programs, unconditional in-kind transfers, public works, cash-for-work, emergency
employment schemes, and fee waivers. The DSWD shall provide maternity cash
assistance equivalent to three (3) months for women workers in the informal sector
who are unable to afford the required monthly contributions to the Social Security
System (SSS) and PhilHealth.

        SEC. 21. Social Welfare Benefits. – The government shall ensure that social
welfare programs such as Social Security System (SSS), Home Development Mutual
Fund (Pag-IBIG Fund), Social Housing Finance Corporation (SHFC), Department of
Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD) housing program for the poor,
PhilHealth, and Government Service Insurance System (GSIS) shall be fully
available, accessible and affordable to WIE, and that the government agencies
governing them shall formulate policies and mechanisms for their full utilization and
benefit, in consultation with the representatives of WIE.

       SEC. 22. Disaster Risk Reduction and Management and Climate Change.
– The State, particularly at the community level, shall engage WIE and their
organizations in disaster risk reduction and management efforts from preparedness,
response, rehabilitation and recovery. Responding to climate change in terms of
minimizing contributions to greenhouse gas emissions and adapting to its long-term
impact through resiliency-building programs shall also involve WIE and their
organizations.

                          Chapter V
       LABOR STANDARDS AND ENFORCEMENT OF LABOR LAWS

       SEC. 23. Applicability of the Labor Code. – The provisions of Presidential
Decree No. 442, as amended, otherwise known as the “Labor Code of the Philippines”
governing the employer-employee relationship, particularly on general labor
standards and occupational safety and health standards, shall apply to all forms of
work arrangements in the informal economy.

For workers in the informal sector engaged in profit- or fee-based activities, clear
guidelines shall be established to determine the existence of employer-employee
relationships between operators and workers. Operators shall be recognized as
informal employers, and the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall
develop appropriate guidelines and capacity-building programs to assist them in
transitioning into formal employers in compliance with labor and social protection
standards. To support the transition of informal economic units into the formal
economy, the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Science and
Technology (DOST), government financial institutions (GFIs), Department of Budget
and Management (DBM), Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD),
Department of Agriculture (DA), Department of Agrarian Reform (DAR),


                                                                                   15
Cooperative Development Authority (CDA), Department of Finance (DOF), and
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall jointly develop a
typology and framework for formalization. Such framework shall address the key
drivers of informality, including policy incoherence, regulatory burdens, lack of
access to finance and technology, and other structural barriers to formalization.

         The State shall adopt a developmental perspective in dealing with informal
economic units. The DOLE shall, in coordination with the Informal Economy
Development Council (IEDC), assess the capacity of these enterprises to comply with
general labor standards. Informal economic units incapable of meeting the standards
shall be assisted in upgrading their capacity to improve their businesses, as well as the
ability to comply with relevant labor and social standards.

        SEC. 24. Visitorial and Enforcement Power of the Secretary of Labor and
Employment. – Employment contracts or work arrangements in the informal
economy shall be subject to the visitorial and enforcement powers of the Secretary of
Labor and Employment pursuant to Article 128 of the Labor Code of the Philippines.
In the transition toward formalization and full compliance with labor standards,
existing approaches for engaging community-based enterprises, including Technical
Advisory Visits (TAV), may continue to be implemented. Such strategies shall
likewise extend to industrial homeworkers and other workers engaged in supply chain
and value-adding arrangements to promote compliance, capacity-building, and access
to labor and social protection measures.

                            TITLE II
             INFORMAL ECONOMY DEVELOPMENT COUNCIL
                  COMPOSITION AND ORGANIZATION
                            Chapter I
             INFORMAL ECONOMY DEVELOPMENT COUNCIL

        SEC. 25. Informal Economy Development Council. – There is hereby
created an Informal Economy Development Council (IEDC) which shall be an
attached agency of the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development
(DEPDev). The IEDC shall institute appropriate reforms within its structure,
programming and other processes, adjusting its human resource capabilities to ensure
that WIE-focused programs, with reference to definitions based on PSA Reso No 18,
series of 2025, are efficiently and adequately implemented to address the needs of
these workers. An inter-agency committee shall be formed to coordinate policies and
programs of various government agencies towards the transition of informal economic
units and workers to a formal economy.

       The IEDC shall be constituted within thirty (30) days after the approval of the
IRR of this Act.

      SEC. 26. Powers and Functions. – The IEDC shall exercise the following
powers and functions:

      (a) Develop and ensure implementation of annual, long-term and
medium-term plans for the informal economy units and workers which should form
part of the Medium-Term Philippine Development Plans (MTPDP) towards


                                                                                      16
institutionalizing comprehensive, needs and rights-based, gender-responsive, just
transitioning plan in all workplaces, industries and communities, and child-friendly
programs and policies for the WIE;

        (b) Develop policies to progressively transition WIE and informal economic
units progressively to the formal economy and according to standards provided for in
this Act and related laws, treaties or statutes;

        (c) Develop and implement training programs, and certify workers for jobs in
industries that produce green goods and render services for the benefit of the WIE;

       (d) Develop and ensure implementation of gender-based monitoring and
evaluation mechanisms, efficient and effective programs and policies, and
gender-responsive interventions toward harnessing the full potentials of women WIE;

        (e) Ensure that concerned government agencies shall allocate in their
respective Special Allocation for Development Initiatives adequate resources for
programs that benefit WIE, such as Social and Solidarity Economy Enterprises,
Leadrepreneurship Projects;

       (f) Develop and ensure implementation of a simple system of registration of
WIE and accreditation of informal livelihood activities enterprises, and organizations
in accordance with the standards and provisions of this Act;

        (g) Provide guidelines for the implementation by LGUs of a fair and credible
system of evaluation, accreditation, review and assessment, merit promotion,
rendering of grant and incentive awards, and other policies relative to the effective
and efficient implementation of this Act;

       (h) Ensure the effective implementation of labor standards covering WIE as
provided for by this Act and its corresponding IRR;

        (i) Coordinate with LGUs for the development and implementation of periodic
evaluation of all accredited WIE members, enterprises, and organizations for use as
bases for accreditation, grant of awards and incentives, and training and retraining;

        (j) Ensure effective participation of WIE and their organizations through the
establishment of regular consultative mechanisms and processes;

        (k) Establish and develop a centralized and sex-disaggregated database system
to effectively guide policy formulation and implementation relative to the WIE which
must be available for public use while strictly complying with laws on privacy and
confidentiality including other pertinent information specified in the IRR of this Act;

       (l) Develop and implement a communication plan and campaign targeting
WIE, their enterprises, and organizations towards a better understanding and
appreciation of the benefits of this Act;

      (m) Consistent with Section 2 (d) of this Act, to develop and ensure
implementation of a comprehensive plan to eliminate child labor;

      (n) Assess all means to ensure the transition of informal economic units and
the WIE from their position in the informal economy to the formal economy;

       (o) Coordinate and harmonize all informal economy-related policies,
programs, projects, and activities of various government agencies, including the
regional IEDC officers, towards greater efficiency and effectiveness;

       (p) Monitor the LGU-generated incomes from informal employment;

       (q) Conduct annual reviews of government programs for the WIE and submit

       (r) Develop and ensure implementation of accessible just conflict resolution

        (s) Call on the participation of relevant government agencies in its

       (t) Ensure that the Department of Finance–Bureau of Local Government

access support;

        (x) Require PhilHealth to review the classification of workers in the informal

restoration of an individual-paying scheme consistent with Republic Act No. 9178, or

       (y) Review existing laws, rules, and regulations affecting workers and

and Drug Administration (FDA) permits, the Local Government Code, taxation,

        SEC. 27. Composition. – The IEDC is co-chaired by the Director General of
Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), Secretary of
Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), and Secretary of Department of
Trade and Industry (DTI). IEDC members may elect from among themselves a
vice-chairperson to preside over meetings in the absence of the chairperson. The
members are the relevant government instrumentalities to be determined by the IRR
of this Act: Provided, That at least thirty percent (30%) of the total membership of
IEDC shall come from organizations of WIE accredited with the SEC, DOLE or DA:
Provided, further, That at least thirty percent (30%) are women.

       Representatives from the social partners, private sector, NGOs and WIEO
(Workers in the Informal Economy and Operators) shall serve as IEDC members for a
term of three (3) years without re-election and shall be subject to terms and conditions
provided for by the IRR.

        SEC. 28. IEDC Secretariat. – The IEDC secretariat shall be jointly organized
by the DEPDev, DTI, and the DOLE whose functions shall be determined by the IRR
of this Act and integrated with the DOLE secretariat functions.

        SEC. 29. Regional and Provincial IEDC Officers. – There shall be regional
and provincial IEDC officers who shall be appointed by the IEDC and shall have
functions that include: (a) monitoring and coordinating of IEDC initiatives in the
regions and provinces, evaluation of policies programs for WIE; (b) providing
technical assistance in the development of local plans and programs; and (c) providing
other administrative functions necessary for the success of IEDC initiatives in their
jurisdiction.

      IEDC regional officers shall have automatic seats in all Regional Development
Councils (RDCs).

    WORKERS AND OPERATORS IN THE INFORMAL EMPLOYMENT
               LOCAL DEVELOPMENT OFFICE

       SEC. 30. Workers and Operators in the Informal Economy Local
Development Office. – The Workers and Operators in Informal Economy Local
Development Office (WIELDO) shall, in consultation with the WIE and their
organizations, be established in every city and municipality to perform the following
functions:


                                                                                     19
   (a)​ Prepare an overall development plan and work program that address the needs
        of the WIE incorporating the barangay development plans;

   (b)​Encourage the organization of informal economic units and WIE;

   (c)​ Develop and maintain a comprehensive, age, and sex-disaggregated database
        of all WIE and economic units within the jurisdiction of the LGU that shall
        include all relevant information as contained in the IRR of this Act. Provided,
        That laws on privacy and confidentiality shall be fully complied with.
        Provided, further, That the database shall include information on payments

   (d)​Establish an informal economy one-stop shop center which handles all
       transactions and processing of business permit applications within their

   (e)​ Support micro small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) informal enterprise
        development towards transitioning to formality;

   (f)​ Perform other similar functions and undertakings pursuant to the provisions of
        this Act; and

   (g)​Coordinate with the LGU for the establishment of database of local informal
       sector and workers in the informal economy and establish local registry
       system.

       In the performance of WIEOLDO’s functions, the principle of preferential
treatment to the poorest and most vulnerable shall be observed.

        SEC. 31. Composition. – The local chief executives (LCEs) are the primary
overseers of WIELDO operations. The IRR of this Act shall provide for WIELDO’s
composition. The informal workers’ representatives shall form at least fifty percent
(50%) of WIEOLDO’s membership: Provided, That WIE representatives are not less
than five (5) and at least thirty percent (30%) of WIEOLDO’s members are women.
Other relevant guidelines on WIEOLDO’s operations shall be included in the IRR of
this Act.

       SEC. 32. Local Government Unit. Informal Economy Development Fund.
– A local government unit - informal economy development fund (LGU-IEDF) shall
be established in every municipality and city. Such fund shall be supervised and
managed by the concerned LGU through the WIEOLDO and subject to audit by the
Commission on Audit (COA).

        For the initial operations of WIEOLDO, each LGU shall allocate a portion
from its internal revenue allotment or IRA.

       Thereafter, the LGU-IEDF shall be funded with the following:




                                                                                    20
   (a)​ Registration fees and annual dues paid by individual workers in the informal
        economy, and accreditation fees of their livelihood activities, organizations,
        and enterprises; and

   (b)​Annual allocations from LGUs’ development fund the percentage of which
       shall be determined by LGUs.

       Following the principle of merit and equity, LGUs that fulfill such allocations
may receive additional assistance from the national government as recommended by
IEDC: Provided, That the LGUs have sufficiently complied with all requirements by
the IEDC.

        SEC. 33. Registration of WIE and Accreditation of Economic Units and
Organizations. – Pursuant to its functions under the Local Government Code, the
Local Development Council (LDC), in coordination with the WIEOLDO, shall
establish a standard system of registration of WIE, and accreditation of informal
livelihood activities, organizations, and economic units: Provided, That the system
shall be simple, efficient, affordable, uniform, and accessible to all.

        Such registration or accreditation shall signify eligibility of the WIE, their
livelihood activities, organizations, or enterprises to avail of development programs:
Provided, That the poorest and most vulnerable shall be given priority. The
registration or accreditation may be renewed subject to requirements set forth by the
LDC in coordination with the WIEOLDO.

       SEC. 34. Annual Dues. – WIE and their organizations shall pay annual dues
to be determined by the concerned LGU, in consultation with their WIEOLDO. Said
annual dues shall not exceed more than 30% of the legally prescribed daily minimum
wage in their respective regions or provinces.

        The annual dues shall accrue to the concerned LGU and shall be used for
programs to benefit WIE, their organizations, business activities, and enterprises
based on recommendations by the WIEOLDO and the local informal economy
constituents.

        SEC. 35. Registration and Non-Registration. – In no case shall registration
be construed as a basis for rights and entitlements under this Act. Non-registration
shall not deprive WIE of their rights stated in this Act. Registration as a WIEO in the
LGU is voluntary and shall not be considered a prerequisite to work in any private
enterprise or government unit. However, registration shall guarantee prioritization and
immediate recognition of the rights and benefits due to the workers in the informal
economy.

                                   TITLE III
                               FINAL PROVISIONS

       SEC. 36. Prohibited Acts. – The following are prohibited acts:

              (a)​   Non-compliance with minimum labor standards and
       occupational health and safety standards. – The employment contract must be


                                                                                    21
in writing, with specified terms and conditions of engagement, and which
must not be lower than the minimum standards provided by law. However, the
absence of a written agreement does not mean the absence of an employment
relationship. The contract shall be explained to the worker and to be attested
by an elected barangay official in the barangay where the worker is designated
to work.

        (b)​  Charging of recruitment or finders’ fees. – Regardless of
whether the worker was sourced either through an employment agency or a
third party, WIE shall neither be charged nor levied a recruitment fee or
finders’ fee.

       (c)​   Hazardous working conditions. – Workers shall not be engaged
to do hazardous work, activity or undertaking, or be exposed to hazardous
working conditions in accordance with existing laws.

      (d)​   Interference and coercion. – Any person is prohibited from
committing any of the following acts of interference or coercion:

   (1) Preventing any worker from upholding or exercising one’s rights;

   (2) Preventing any worker from joining or assisting an organization for
purposes not contrary to law;

   (3) Preventing any worker from carrying out one’s duties or functions in an
organization, or penalizing the same for any lawful action performed in that
capacity;

    (4) Harassing, threatening, coercing, or intimidating any worker that result
in preventing the worker from performing one’s duties and functions;

   (5) Transferring, penalizing, or terminating the services of a worker
without valid or legal ground; and

   (6) Other acts that threaten to diminish the independence and freedom of a
workers’ organization to direct its own affairs.

   (7) Those in the subcontracting, seasonal workers, in the value chain and
supply chain workers, non-compliance to policies on industrial homework,
pursuant to DOLE Order No. 5 on industrial homework is also prohibited.

        (e)​ Non-compliance with RA 7610, as amended, otherwise known
as the “Special Protection of Children Against Child Abuse, Exploitation and
Discrimination Act,” in the employment of minors. – In cases where minors
are contracted or hired to render work or services, the age must be at least
fifteen (15) years old, and there [is] must be a parental or legal guardian’s
consent in the employment contract, which is presented and attested to by any
representative of the LGU or duly elected barangay official where the work is
to be done, and that the employers of these children must provide them with



                                                                             22
       access to at least elementary or secondary education, either through traditional
       schooling or alternative learning systems.

        SEC. 37. Penalties. – (a) Any person who fails to comply with the minimum
labor standards and occupational health and safety standards; charge or levy
recruitment or finders’ fees regardless of whether the worker was sourced either
through an employment agency or a third party; or caused the worker to do hazardous
work, activity, or undertaking or be exposed to hazardous working conditions, in
violation of Section 37 (a), (b) and (c) of this Act, shall, upon conviction, be punished
by a fine of not less than Two hundred thousand pesos (P200,000.00), but not more
than Three hundred thousand pesos (P300,000.00), or imprisonment of one (1) month
and one (1) day to six (6) months, or both at the discretion of the court.

        All private-sector employers of informal economy workers, including
industrial homeworkers, shall be covered by this Act. Informal economy units
transitioning to formality shall initially be subject to Technical Advisory Visits and
compliance assistance to foster adherence to Occupational Safety and Health (OSH)
standards. Administrative penalties for violations shall be imposed only after
reasonable opportunity for compliance has been provided and shall be calibrated
according to the size, capitalization, and economic capacity of the enterprise.

        (b) Any person who willfully interferes with, restrain or coerce a worker in the
exercise of the worker’s rights under Section 37 (d) of this Act, shall, upon
conviction, be punished by a fine of not less than Three hundred thousand pesos
(P300,000.00), but not more than Five hundred thousand pesos (P500,000.00), or
imprisonment of six (6) months and one (1) day to six (6) years, or both at the
discretion of the court.

       (c) Any person who fails to comply with RA 7610 in the employment of
minors under Section 37 (e) of this Act shall, in addition to the penalty of
imprisonment imposed by RA 7610, be punished by a fine of not less than Three
hundred thousand pesos (P300,000.00), but not more than Five hundred thousand
pesos (P500,000.00).

        (d) Any person who violates the provision on policy on confiscation of
materials, and impounding of vehicles under Section 11 of this Act, shall be punished
with a fine of not less than One hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00), but not more
than Two hundred thousand pesos (P200,000.00). Nothing herein shall prohibit the
aggrieved WIE from initiating a criminal or civil action against the responsible person
or officer.

        (e) Any official or employee who fails to implement the provision on the
relocation of vending sites and policy on relocation of terminals under Sections 12
and 13 of this Act shall be administratively liable pursuant to RA 7160 and other
applicable laws without prejudice to any civil or criminal case that may be filed
against the erring official or employee. If the act is committed by a private person or
private juridical entity, its business license shall be suspended or revoked at the
discretion of the court.




                                                                                      23
        If the offender is a public official or employee, the court may impose the
penalty of disqualification to hold public office, in addition to the penalties provided
in the preceding paragraph.

       If the offender is a juridical entity, the person criminally liable shall be its
board of directors, officers or employees who participated in the illegal acts.

       SEC. 38. Transitory Clause. – Within five (5) years from the effectivity of
this Act, all workers in the informal economy are entitled to the same labor standards
as workers in the formal economy, in accordance with Section 22 of this Act.

        SEC. 39. Appropriations. – The amount of One hundred million pesos
(P100,000,000.00) from the presidential discretionary fund is hereby appropriated for
the initial implementation of this Act. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for
its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.

        SEC. 40. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Labor and Employment shall, in
consultation with the Director-General of DEPDev, concerned government agencies,
and WIE organizations, promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 41. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the provisions not affected shall continue to be in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 42. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations
or other issuances inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 43. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
