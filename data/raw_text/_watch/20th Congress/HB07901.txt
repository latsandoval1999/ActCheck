                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Children are the fundamental bearers of progress and growth of any society.
In the Philippines, Article 15 Section 3(2) of the 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates
that the State shall defend the right of children to assistance, including proper care
and nutrition, and special protection from all forms of neglect, abuse, cruelty,
exploitation, and other conditions prejudicial to their development – ensuring the
torch-bearers of our nation of the protection and security from any harm and danger.
In line with this mandate, the Philippines has made significant strides in combating
crimes against minors online, most notably through the enactment of Republic Act No.
11930, otherwise known as the Anti-Online Sexual Abuse or Exploitation of Children
(OSAEC) and Anti-Child Sexual Abuse or Exploitation Materials (CSAEM) Act.

       The National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC) reported that
over 1.7 million internet-using children in the Philippines aged 12 to 17 had
experienced OSAEC in 2024. While this is an evident and a significant decrease in the
number of affected children since Republic Act No. 11930 was enacted, present gaps
in the law must be addressed in order to further eliminate and combat OSAEC and
CSAEM in our country.

      Republic Act No. 11930 provided the legal framework to protect children from
such dangers online. Indeed, RA 11930 is a landmark step in criminalizing and
penalizing such heinous offenses. However, crucial legal, operational, and
enforcement gaps that are not able to conform to rapid societal evolution necessitate
amendments to our laws that govern the Philippine society.

       This proposed measure seeks to amend RA 11930 to expand its scope and
introduce a whole-of-nation approach in combatting OSAEC and CSAEM. This measure
provides the legal framework to combat the rising dangers of Artificial Intelligence (AI)
and its utilization in exploiting and sexually abusing children online, addressing
emerging digital threats, closes regulatory gaps, and safeguards children in an
increasingly technology-driven environment.

        Further, specific acts and offenses against children are also defined and
classified by this amendment, ensuring that harmful conduct is no longer obscured by
ambiguity but clearly identified, penalized, and addressed through a stronger
framework of accountability and child protection.

        By strengthening enforcement mechanisms, clarifying legal definitions, and
integrating technology-responsive safeguards, this proposed measure aims to make
the State’s response to OSAEC and CSAEM more just, judicious, and forward-looking.
It aligns domestic policy with evolving international standards, ensuring adaptability
in an increasingly borderless digital environment. In doing so, it ushers in a proactive
governmental approach to the escalating dangers faced by millions of Filipino children,
while positioning the Philippines as a potential global benchmark in combating online
child exploitation and abuse.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
