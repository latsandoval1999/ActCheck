                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      In recent years, the Philippines has uncovered the presence of
transnational organized crimes within its borders, ranging from illegal
gambling and large-scale scams to cybercrime and human trafficking. These
operations persisted for years despite existing enforcement structures,
underscoring gaps in detection, intelligence-sharing, and investigative
capacity.


       While the Philippine National Police is primarily responsible for
maintaining peace and order at the local level, the National Bureau of
Investigation is mandated to investigate complex, transnational, and high-
profile crimes, including graft and corruption, cybercrime, and human
trafficking. However, the rapidly evolving nature of these threats—driven by
digital platforms, encrypted communications, and cross-border syndicates—
demands a corresponding upgrade in the NBI’s technological, forensic, and
intelligence capabilities.


      At present, constraints in funding, equipment, and personnel limit the
NBI’s ability to fully perform its mandate. Many of its investigative processes
remain constrained by outdated tools, insufficient forensic infrastructure, and
limited access to advanced cybercrime technologies. Moreover, the increasing

volume and sophistication of cases require highly specialized training, inter-
agency coordination, and sustained investment in modern investigative
systems.


      This bill seeks to address these gaps by providing dedicated
modernization funding for the NBI and strengthening its institutional
structure. It aims to support the acquisition of advanced forensic equipment,
enhancement of cybercrime and intelligence capabilities, development of
modern digital investigative systems, and expansion of specialized services
within the Bureau. It likewise promotes improved personnel welfare and
professional development, as well as a more sustainable funding mechanism
through authorized retention of service-related revenues, ensuring the long-
term operational efficiency of the agency.


      By investing in the institutional strength of the NBI, the State affirms its
commitment to upholding the rule of law, protecting national security, and
ensuring that justice is swift, effective, and responsive to emerging threats.


      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

 3             SECTION 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the National Bureau of
 4      Investigation Modernization Act.

 5             SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
 6      to ensure the development of an effective, modern, professional, and highly trained
 7      investigative institution that is nationally integrated and responsive to emerging and
 8      complex forms of criminality.
 9             Towards this end, the State shall strengthen the National Bureau of
10      Investigation (NBI) through institutional modernization, expanded capability,
11      enhanced manpower, and upgraded technological and forensic resources to ensure
12      efficient law enforcement and the effective administration of justice.

 7         SEC. 7. Retention of Fees and Self-Funded Modernization. – The NBI is
 8   authorized to retain and utilize up to forty percent (40%) of its annual collections
 9   from clearance and related service fees for modernization, capacity building, and
10   operational expenses, including capital outlay, training, and professional
11   development:
12         Provided, That total retention shall not exceed Three Hundred Million Pesos
13   (₱300,000,000.00) annually, unless otherwise authorized by law.
14         SEC. 8. Funding Source. – The funds required for the implementation of
15   this Act, including employee benefits, shall be taken from:
16         a) Collections from NBI clearance and certification fees;
17         b) Incidental income from trainings, tests, and the like;
18         c) Proceeds from the sale of confiscated/forfeited articles after public
19             auction from the cases investigated by the NBI upon order of the court;
20         d) Other fees which may be determined and imposed by the Director; and
21         e) The General Appropriations Act.
