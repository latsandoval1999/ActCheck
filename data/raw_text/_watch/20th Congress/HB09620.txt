                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The right of every Filipino to quality education is not merely a political
aspiration but a solemn constitutional obligation. Section 1, Article XIV of the
Constitution declares that “the State shall protect and promote the right of all citizens to
quality education at all levels and shall take appropriate steps to make such education accessible
to all.” Section 5(2) of the same Article guarantees academic freedom in all institutions
of higher learning, while Section 5(5) affirms that the State shall assign the highest
budgetary priority to education. These provisions are not decorative clauses but are
enforceable obligations binding upon every branch and instrumentality of government.
When the State fails to equip its public universities with the governance structures, the
resources, and the institutional autonomy they need to fulfill their mission, it does not
merely fall short of a policy goal but violates a constitutional duty owed to every
Filipino child who cannot afford to go anywhere else.

       It is in the light of this constitutional imperative that the modernization of the
Charter of the Polytechnic University of the Philippines (PUP) becomes not only
warranted but urgent. The PUP traces its origins to the Manila Business School,
established in 1904, and has since grown into one of the largest and most consequential
public universities in the nation, with campuses spanning the National Capital Region
and multiple provinces serving students from the most underserved communities in the
country. Its current foundational charter, Presidential Decree (PD) No. 1341 enacted on
April 1, 1978, has, for nearly five decades, served as the legal foundation of an
institution that has produced principled, competent, and socially committed graduates

                                                                                        Page 1 of 24
who now serve in every government agency, in every vital industry, and in countless
communities across the archipelago and beyond. Surveys on the most preferred
graduates by employers in the Philippines consistently place PUP alumni at the very
top of the list, a testament not to privilege or resources but to the resilience and
excellence of a university that has done more with less than almost any comparable
institution in the country.

       Yet a decree conceived in 1978 cannot govern a university that must compete in
the 21st century and beyond. PD 1341 was crafted in an era that could not have
anticipated the global dominance of artificial intelligence, the imperative of
computational literacy across all sectors of the economy, the depth of digital
transformation reshaping labor markets, or the scale of demand for inclusive, flexible,
and technology-enabled higher education. The result is an institutional framework that,
however admirable at its inception, has become structurally inadequate. PUP's branches
and campuses operate under chronic underfunding, with a documented funding gap of
over ₱9 billion in 2026 alone. Several campuses face the very real threat of closure due to
the absence of sustained national government support. Faculty positions remain
unfilled. Infrastructure remains incomplete. Research capacity remains constrained.
These are not administrative inconveniences but are failures of the State to honor its
constitutional promise to the working families whose children fill PUP's classrooms.

        Legislative efforts to correct this injustice have spanned successive Congresses. In
2019, President Rodrigo Duterte vetoed a bill that sought to designate PUP as the
National Polytechnic University. In 2025, President Ferdinand R. Marcos, Jr. vetoed a
similar measure, once again deferring what had become an overdue legislative
commitment. These repeated vetoes have not extinguished the imperative for reform,
but rather sharpened it. Every day that PUP operates as an underfunded institution
under its old charter is another day its students learn in deteriorating facilities, its
faculty serve without adequate support, and its campuses in the provinces remain
vulnerable to the political vagaries of annual appropriations. The passage of this bill is,
therefore, not a matter of institutional prestige but a matter of constitutional fidelity and
social justice. The State must not allow yet another generation of Filipinos to bear the
cost of inaction.

       This measure seeks to repeal Presidential Decree No. 1341 and enact the Revised
Polytechnic University of the Philippines Charter, thereby strengthening PUP as the
National Polytechnic University and providing it with the governance architecture,
institutional autonomy, fiscal security, and expanded mandate that its students, its
faculty, and the nation's working class have long deserved. The reforms introduced by


                                                                                    Page 2 of 24
this bill are substantive, structural, and long overdue. They address, in a direct and
concrete manner, the deficiencies that decades of delay have allowed to accumulate.

        Before describing those reforms, however, it is necessary to address the
misinformation that has too often distorted public discourse on measures of this kind,
and that has been weaponized, whether through ignorance or bad faith, to obstruct
progress for the University. To begin with, this bill does not rename the PUP, since the
designation of National Polytechnic University confers an elevated mandate and
nothing more, functioning in precisely the same manner as the national university
status of the University of the Philippines and the national engineering university
designation of Batangas State University. PUP will remain PUP. Equally without basis
is the claim that this bill abolishes social science programs, humanities, or any existing
academic offering, given that the proposed charter's enumeration of disciplines is
deliberately broader than the one it replaces, contains no enrollment ratio requirements,
and imposes no prohibition on non-technical programs. More flagrant is the assertion
that this bill eliminates free tuition, since RA 10931 or the “Universal Access to Quality
Tertiary Education Act” is law this charter revision does not override, and in fact, the
proposed charter itself expressly requires that any fee-setting authority be exercised in
conformity with that law. Most egregiously false is the claim that this bill privatizes the
university, a charge that fundamentally misreads both the measure and the legal
framework within which it operates. The provisions on joint ventures and the
outsourcing of non-academic services are not novelties introduced by this charter but
restatements of authorities that have governed all State Universities and Colleges
(SUCs) for nearly three decades. In fact, the proposed charter strengthens the
surrounding safeguards by expressly prohibiting the sale of existing university real
property, requiring supermajority Board approval and competitive public bidding for
significant asset transactions, mandating that all joint venture proceeds be used
exclusively for the development and strengthening of PUP, and restricting outsourcing
strictly to non-academic functions while requiring the university to continuously build
its own capacity to eventually perform those services directly. Those who spread these
falsehoods do a disservice not only to factual discourse but to the students and workers
whose futures depend on this university being strengthened, not paralyzed by
manufactured controversy.

       The reforms this bill introduces are grounded in institutional necessity and the
demands of a rapidly changing economy, and they address, in concrete and structural
terms, the deficiencies that decades of legislative delay have allowed to accumulate. The
proposed charter restructures PUP's Board of Regents by elevating the President of PUP
to Co-Chairperson and replacing regional agency directors with cabinet-level
Secretaries, ensuring that PUP's governance is directly connected to the agencies most
                                                                                  Page 3 of 24
consequential to the labor market and the technological future, while the inclusion of a
representative for non-teaching personnel further democratizes the Board by extending
meaningful institutional voice to a constituency that has too long been invisible in
governance decisions.

        Equally consequential are the fiscal reforms this bill introduces, which address
PUP's chronic material crisis with the seriousness it demands. The proposed charter
appropriates substantial initial funding from the national treasury, supplemented by
dedicated annual allocations for campus development and infrastructure
modernization, provisions that are not symbolic gestures but a direct legislative
response to the documented funding gaps, unfinished classrooms, and deteriorating
facilities that have for years undermined the quality of education available to hundreds
of thousands of students from working-class and marginalized communities. Beyond
these appropriations, the charter guarantees that PUP's annual budget shall not be
reduced below the amount appropriated for the previous year and shall be
automatically and regularly released, insulating the university from the budgetary
volatility and political discretion that have historically constrained its operations and
that represent, more than anything else, the difference between a university that merely
exists and one that can genuinely fulfill its mandate.

       On the frontiers of learning, the proposed charter establishes a dedicated
institutional center for artificial intelligence and emerging technologies, positioning
PUP to build national competencies in the disciplines that will define the global
economy of the coming decades, not for an elite, but for the broader Filipino workforce,
and signaling unambiguously that polytechnic education is not a second-tier alternative
to traditional academic instruction but a dynamic, forward-looking pathway to
professional excellence and national development.

       The proposed charter likewise strengthens the foundations of inclusive access
that have always defined PUP's identity, affirming that no student shall be denied
admission on the basis of any form of discrimination, that scholarship programs and
affirmative action mechanisms shall continue to serve poor but deserving students, that
democratic student representation is protected and guaranteed across every campus
and constituent unit, and that the autonomy of student publications shall at all times be
upheld. These provisions are not peripheral details but are the institutional backbone of
a university whose fundamental purpose is to serve those for whom higher education
would otherwise remain permanently out of reach.




                                                                                Page 4 of 24
        PUP has endured two presidential vetoes. It has endured decades of
underfunding, outdated governance, and the compounding inequities visited upon an
institution whose students are disproportionately drawn from the households that can
least afford for it to fail. Through all of it, PUP has endured, and its graduates have
gone on to serve, lead, and build this nation. That resilience, however, is not a
justification for continued neglect. It is a demonstration of what PUP and its community
are capable of when given even inadequate support, and a measure of what they could
accomplish with the support this charter provides. The modernization of PUP's charter
is not a gift to an institution but the fulfillment of a promise made to the Filipino
working class in the text of the Constitution itself, a promise that every citizen,
regardless of origin, income, or circumstance, shall have access to quality education.
This Congress and this administration has the power and the duty to keep that promise.
As the PUP administration aptly put following the veto of the National Polytechnic
University bill, “every day that this legislation remains unsigned is another day education is
treated as a privilege instead of a right. We are losing time and losing futures.”

       In solidarity with the working class, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

    AN ACT STRENGTHENING THE POLYTECHNIC UNIVERSITY OF THE
  PHILIPPINES AS THE NATIONAL POLYTECHNIC UNIVERSITY, REPEALING
 FOR THE PURPOSE PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 1341, AND APPROPRIATING
                          FUNDS THEREFOR

   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Revised Polytechnic
University of the Philippines Charter.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote,
foster, nurture, and protect the right of all citizens to accessible quality education; and
establish, maintain, and support a complete, adequate, and integrated system of
education that is relevant to the needs of the people and society.

        Towards this end, the Polytechnic University of the Philippines (PUP) shall be
strengthened as the National Polytechnic University to enhance its capacity to provide
accessible and inclusive quality education and research tailored to the present and
future needs of Filipinos; to develop the competencies and specialization of its
constituent units; to adopt conventional or alternative instructional methodologies; to
foster a unique and distinctive academic leadership in the development and delivery of
higher education services in the Philippines, including capacity building in artiﬁcial
intelligence, computational thinking, and key emerging technologies; and to serve as
guide and resource to other polytechnic state universities and colleges in the

                                                                                              Page 6 of 24
development and delivery of professional and technical programs, thereby raising the
bar for polytechnic education nationwide.

SEC. 3. The University System. – The Polytechnic University of the Philippines is a
university system and shall be composed of constituent units established solely by its
Board of Regents upon the recommendation of the President of the University. It shall
comprise of its main campus and existing branches and campuses located in the Cities
of Manila, Caloocan, Parañaque, Quezon, San Juan, and Taguig in the National Capital
Region; Municipalities of Pulilan and Sta. Maria in the Province of Bulacan;
Municipality of Mariveles in the Province of Bataan; Municipality of Cabiao in the
Province of Nueva Ecija; Municipalities of Alfonso and Maragondon in the Province of
Cavite; City of Sto. Tomas in the Province of Batangas; Cities of Biñan, San Pedro, and
Sta. Rosa, and Municipality of Calauan in the Province of Laguna; Municipalities of
General Luna, Lopez, Mulanay, and Unisan in the Province of Quezon; Municipality of
Bansud in the Province of Oriental Mindoro; Municipality of Sablayan in the Province
of Occidental Mindoro; Municipality of Ragay in the Province of Camarines Sur;
Municipality of Leyte in the Province of Leyte, and other constituent units, regular and
extension campuses, and academic or non-academic units that may be established in the
future to improve access to quality education, promote innovation and progress in the
academe, and advance its research capabilities.

SEC. 4. General Mandate. – As the National Polytechnic University, PUP shall adopt a
polytechnic program of education designed to provide individuals with employable
skills and managerial knowledge to bolster their creativity, productivity, and
self-reliance. The PUP shall also provide advanced education, higher technological,
professional courses, and training programs in accountancy, engineering, architecture,
applied and social sciences, law, education, interior design, psychology, business, and
management, among other disciplines, and promote applied research, advanced
studies, and progressive leadership in the stated fields.

SEC. 5. Curricular Offering. – The PUP shall offer undergraduate and graduate courses,
advanced professional and technical instruction, and training within its areas of
specialization in the fields of accountancy, engineering, architecture, applied and social
sciences, law, education, interior design, psychology, business. management, and in
other polytechnic areas.

       The PUP shall continue to operate and maintain a night school and to collect
tuition and other fees in such amounts as may be fixed by the Board.

SEC. 9. Meetings of the Board. – The Board shall regularly convene once every three (3)
months. The Chairperson of the Board or the President of PUP may, however, call a
special meeting whenever necessary: Provided, That members are notified in writing at
least three (3) days prior to the conduct of said meeting.

       A quorum of the Board shall consist of a majority of all its members holding
office at the time of the meeting: Provided, That the Chairperson of the Board or the
President of PUP is among those present in the meeting.

        In case the Chairperson of CHED cannot attend a meeting of the Board, a CHED
Commissioner duly designated by the Chairperson of CHED as representative to the
meeting shall have all the rights and responsibilities of a regular member: Provided, That
during the meeting, the President of the PUP, as Co-Chairperson, shall be the presiding
officer.

       All ex-officio members of the Board may designate their respective
representatives who shall have the same rights and responsibilities as regular members
of the Board: Provided, That all members of the Board may exercise their right to vote
through remote communication and in absentia, as provided for in Section 49 of
Republic Act No. 11232.

       In case of vacancy in the Board, except for the President of the PUP, such vacancy
shall be filled in the same manner as provided for the predecessor, and such appointee
shall hold office for the unexpired portion of the term. In case of a sectoral regent,
membership in the Board ceases upon suspension, separation, or recall.

       The members of the Board shall not receive any salary, but shall be entitled to
reimbursements for actual and necessary expenses incurred, for their attendance to the
meetings of the Board or in connection with other official business authorized by a
resolution of the Board, subject to existing laws and regulations.

SEC. 10. The University President. – The President of PUP shall be its chief academic
officer, head of the university faculty, and chief executive officer. As such, the President
shall exercise the powers specifically provided for in this Act, those determined by the
Board, those which pertain to the Office of the President, and those which are related or
necessary to its functions.

       The PUP President shall be appointed by the Board subject to its guidelines,
qualifications, and standards, and based on the recommendation of a search committee
duly constituted for the purpose of selecting the President.

        The President shall render full-time service and have a term of four (4) years and
may be reappointed to another term: Provided, That in order to ensure the attainment of
its distinctive and purposeful mandate as such, as declared and provided in this Act,
the incumbent President of the PUP shall serve a new term as the first president of the
PUP as the National Polytechnic University and shall not be eligible for reappointment.

       Within six (6) months before the expiration of the term of office of the PUP
President, the Board shall constitute the Search Committee for the Presidency, and shall
ensure the appointment or reappointment of a President on or before the expiration of
the term.

      The Board shall determine the compensation of the PUP President in accordance
with Republic Act No. 11466 or the “Salary Standardization Law of 2019”, and such other
laws amendatory thereto, which may hereinafter be enacted.

        In case of a vacancy in the office of the president by reason of death, compulsory
retirement, resignation, removal for cause, or incapacity to perform the functions of the
office, the Board shall designate an Officer-in-Charge (OIC) of the PUP, pending the
appointment of a new President: Provided, That the OIC shall serve only during the
unexpired portion of the term.

       The PUP President shall be assisted by the following officials: Executive Vice
President, the Vice President for Administration, the Vice President for Academic
Affairs, the Vice President for Student Affairs, the Vice President for Research and
Extension, the Vice President for Planning and Development, the Vice President for
Finance, the Vice President for External Affairs, the Secretary of the University, and

may be determined by the Board. They shall all be appointed by the Board upon the

SEC. 11. The University Secretary. – The Board shall appoint a Secretary who shall serve
as such for both the Board and PUP and keep all records and proceedings of meetings
of the Board. The Secretary of PUP shall serve upon each member of the Board the
appropriate notice of board meetings.

SEC. 12. The University Treasurer. – The Treasurer of the Philippines shall be the
ex-officio Treasurer of PUP.

SEC. 13. The Administrative Council. – There shall be an Administrative Council
consisting of the President of PUP as Chairperson, and the vice presidents, deans,
directors, and other officials of equal rank, as members. The Administrative Council
shall review and recommend to the Board the policies that shall govern the
administration, management, and development planning of the PUP.

SEC. 14. The Academic Council. – There shall be an Academic Council, which shall
formulate academic policies. It shall be composed of the PUP President, who shall act as
Chairperson, and all academic staff with the rank of at least an assistant professor, as
members. The Academic Council shall have the power to review and recommend the
curricular offerings and rules on student discipline, subject to the approval of the Board.
It shall fix the requirements for the admission of students, as well as for their
graduation and conferment of degrees, subject to the review and approval of the Board
through the PUP President.

       It shall exercise disciplinary power over the students of PUP and shall formulate
rules and regulations on student discipline, subject to the approval of the Board.

SEC. 15. Heads of University Campuses. – The constituent campuses of PUP shall each be
headed by a Campus Director who shall serve as head or administrator of each of the
campuses and render full-time service for a term of two (2) years and may be
reappointed to another term. The Campus Director shall be appointed or designated by
the Board, upon the recommendation of the Search Committee and the PUP President,
subject to the guidelines, qualifications and standards set by the Board: Provided, That
upon the effectivity of this Act, the incumbent Campus Directors of the existing
campuses of PUP shall continue to serve as such.

SEC. 16. The Faculty. – The teaching staff of each college shall constitute its faculty. The
College Faculty shall be presided over by a Dean. The Dean shall be appointed by the
Board, subject to the guidelines, qualifications and standards set by the Board, and


                                                                                  Page 16 of 24
upon the recommendation of the President of PUP, following a process of consultation
with the constituents of the college concerned.

       The powers and duties of the Dean of the College, in addition to those
specifically provided in this Act, shall include those usually pertaining to the Office of
the Dean of similar state universities and colleges and those delegated by the PUP
President.

       In case of vacancy in the Office of the Dean by reason of death, compulsory
retirement, resignation, removal for cause, or incapacity of the Dean to perform the
functions of the Office, the Board shall, upon the recommendation of the PUP President,
designate an Officer-in-Charge of the College, pending the appointment of a new Dean.

       No political belief, sex, gender, cultural or community affiliation, ethnic origin,
religious opinion or affiliation shall be a matter of inquiry in the appointment of faculty
members of PUP: Provided, That said appointment shall be subject to the guidelines,
qualifications and standards set by the Board.

SEC. 17. Faculty Sabbatical Leave. – The PUP may grant sabbatical leave to deserving and
qualified faculty for purposes of research or community engagement ranging from
three (3) months to one (1) year, subject to the recommendation of the PUP President
and upon approval of the Board.

       Faculty on long-term sabbatical leave shall be temporarily relieved from their
teaching duties to conduct research, write, and engage in scholarly or professional
activity and required to submit a research output published in a reputable journal.

SEC. 18. Student Affairs and Democratic Representation. – There shall be established in the
PUP a democratic system of student representation composed of duly constituted
student councils in every college, institute, campus, and constituent unit of the National
Polytechnic University, as well as a federation or general assembly of student councils
representing the entire University System.

       The student councils shall be composed of students elected by their respective
constituencies in accordance with their constitutions, bylaws, and democratic
procedures. Each officer or representative shall serve for a term of one (1) year, without
prejudice to reelection in accordance with the rules of the student council concerned.

      The student councils shall serve as the primary representative bodies advancing
and defending the rights, welfare, democratic interests, and aspirations of the students.
                                                                                 Page 17 of 24
They shall enjoy autonomy in the formulation of their internal policies, programs, and
activities, consistent with this Act and other applicable laws and regulations.

       The University shall likewise recognize the right of students to organize
associations, alliances, unions, publications, and other similar formations in pursuit of
academic freedom, student rights, and democratic participation in university
governance.

       Subject to due and comprehensive consultation with the students concerned,
there shall be established and maintained independent student publications in every
constituent campus, college, or institute, to be funded by student publication fees.
Editorial and fiscal autonomy, including the selection of editors, staff, and editorial
policies, shall at all times be guaranteed and protected.

SEC. 19. Academic Freedom. – The National Polytechnic University shall enjoy academic
freedom pursuant to Article XIV, Section 5(5) of the Constitution.

SEC. 20. Institutional Autonomy as the National Polytechnic University. – As the National
Polytechnic University, the PUP shall enjoy institutional autonomy and fiscal
autonomy. It shall be treated in a manner consistent with its institutional requirements
by the service-wide agencies in the exercise of their respective jurisdiction. Taking into
account national goals and priorities, it shall exclusively determine its teaching,
research and extensions thrust, plans, policies, programs and standards, consistent with
the policies standards and guidelines promulgated by the Commission on Higher
Education. On the basis of such determination, the National Polytechnic University
shall recommend its annual budget to the President of the Republic of the Philippines
and Congress. Appropriations for the University shall not be reduced by the legislature
below the amount appropriated for the previous year and after approval shall be
automatically and regularly released.

technology, and innovation, the PUP Center for Artificial Intelligence, Computational
Thinking, And Key Emerging Technologies, hereinafter referred to as the Center, is
hereby established.

      The Center shall have:

   (a)​Suitable physical facilities for study and laboratory research of graduate and post
       graduate level in artificial intelligence, computational thinking, and key
                                                                                Page 18 of 24

   (b)​A regular staff of scientists, technicians, academicians, and administrative
       personnel, to be augmented, as circumstances require, by guest researchers,

       The Center shall perform the following functions:

   (a)​Undertake fundamental studies and research in artificial intelligence,

   (c)​ Provide assistance, upon request and when feasible, to other public and private

   (d)​Collaborate, upon request and when feasible, with other schools, organizations

SEC. 22. Scholarship Programs and Admission. – Consistent with and in addition to the
benefits and programs provided under Republic Act No. 10931, otherwise known as the
“Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act,” the PUP shall provide scholarship
programs, subsidies, grants, and other affirmative action programs to assist poor but
deserving students who qualify for admission to PUP.

       No student shall be denied admission to PUP by reason of sex, gender, religion,
cultural community affiliation, ethnic origin, socioeconomic status, disability, or any
other form of discrimination prohibited by law.

SEC. 23. Loan or Transfer of Supplies, Apparatuses, or Equipment, or Detail of Personnel. –

the Board through the President of PUP, loan or transfer supplies, apparatuses, or

       The personnel to be detailed to PUP shall perform such duties as may be deemed

part of the detailed personnel’s regular service.

SEC. 24. Assets, Liabilities, and Personnel. – All assets, both real and personal, personnel
and records of PUP, as well as its liabilities or obligations, shall remain under the name
of PUP: Provided, That the positions, rights, and security of tenure of faculty members
and personnel therein employed under existing laws shall be respected: Provided,
further, That the incumbents occupying the positions shall remain in the same status
until otherwise provided by the Board: Provided, finally, That PUP shall also accordingly
upgrade or increase its human resource component as may be deemed appropriate by
the Board.

SEC. 29. Reportorial Requirements. – On or before the fifteenth (15th) day of the second
(2nd) month after the opening of regular classes each year, the Board shall file a detailed
report on the progress, condition, and needs of PUP with the Office of the President of
the Philippines and both Houses of Congress.

SEC. 30. Suppletory Application. – The provisions of Republic Act No. 8292, otherwise
known as the “Higher Education Modernization Act of 1997,” shall have suppletory
application to this Act insofar as they are not inconsistent with the provisions, purposes,
institutional autonomy, and distinctive mandate of the National Polytechnic University
under this Charter.



                                                                                 Page 23 of 24
SEC. 31. Rules of Construction. - No statutory or other issuances shall diminish the
powers, rights, privileges and benefits accorded to the PUP under this Act, or those
enjoyed at present by virtue of other issuances not otherwise modified or repealed
under this Act, unless subsequent legislation expressly provides for their repeal,
amendment or modification. Any case of doubt in the interpretation of any of the
provisions of this Charter shall be resolved in favor of the academic freedom and fiscal
autonomy of the National Polytechnic University.

SEC. 31. Appropriations. - In addition to the amount already appropriated to PUP for the
current fiscal year, there is hereby appropriated an initial sum of Nine billion pesos
(₱9,000,000,000.00) from any amount available in the Treasury of the Philippines for the
purposes of carrying out the initial implementation of this Act. Thereafter, the amount
necessary to strengthen PUP and its capacity as the National Polytechnic University
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).

         Further, an additional sum of One hundred million pesos (₱100,000,000.00) shall
be appropriated separate and above the said initial funding for each fiscal year for the
first five (5) years of the implementation of this Act, which shall likewise be included in
the annual GAA.

SEC. 32. Repealing Clause. — Presidential Decree No. 1341, entitled “Converting the
Philippine College of Commerce into a Polytechnic University, Defining its Objectives,
Organizational Structure and Functions, and Expanding Its Curricular Offerings” is hereby
repealed. All other laws, decrees, executive orders, implementing rules and regulations,
issuances, or any part thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are deemed
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 33. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional,
the remaining parts or provisions hereof not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

SEC. 34. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication, in
English and Filipino, in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
