                          AN ACT
   EXEMPTING THE UNDERPRIVILEGED AND HOMELESS FROM THE,
REQUIRED PAYMENT OF SUPERSEDEAS BOND TO PERFECT AN APPEAL IN
      RELATION TO EJECTMENT CASES FILED AGAINST THEM,
   AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7279 OR THE
          "URBAN DEVELOPMENT HOUSING ACT OF 1992"

                                            EXPLANATORY NOTE


       More than three decades after the enactment of Republic Act (R.A.) No. 7279,
or the Urban Development and Housing Act of 1992, housing insecurity remains one
of the pressing issues that the government seeks to address and end. Related to this,
the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) reported that 238,747
informal settler families, of whom only 57,134, or 23.93 percent, had been relocated
as of August 2025, leaving more than 181,000 identified families still awaiting
resettlement.1

       The vulnerability of these families is aggravated when housing disputes and
cases are filed before the courts. In May 2025, for instance, residents of a Tondo
community resisted the implementation of a court-ordered demolition, with affected
families expressing that they had nowhere else to go. The dispute had already
undergone approximately four years of litigation before the final demolition order was
issued.2 Similar disputes underscore the realities for indigent families that ejectment
cases are not simply a question of possession but determines whether a family
continues to have a roof over its head.

      Under Rule 70 of the Rules of Court, judgments against defendants in
ejectment cases are immediately executory. To stay or put on hold the execution
pending appeal, the defendant must, among others, file a sufficient supersedeas bond
and continue making the required deposits for the use and occupation of the property.

The Supreme Court has consistently held that failure to comply with these
requirements may result in the immediate execution of the judgment. For
underprivileged and homeless citizens, however, the very financial incapacity that
places them at risk of homelessness may also prevent them from securing the
temporary protection necessary to meaningfully pursue their appeal.

       This measure seeks to amend Section 28 of R.A. No. 7279 by clarifying the
treatment of court-ordered eviction and demolition and by exempting qualified
underprivileged and homeless citizens from the supersedeas bond requirement,
subject to appropriate proof of indigency. It does not extinguish the property rights of
lawful owners, nor does it prevent courts from ultimately enforcing valid judgments.
Rather, it ensures that the ability to seek appellate relief and temporarily stay
displacement will not depend solely on one's ability to pay.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure, which is a
counterpart measure of Senate Bill no. 70, is earnestly sought.
