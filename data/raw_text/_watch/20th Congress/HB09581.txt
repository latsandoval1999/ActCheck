                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution recognizes the duty of the State
to protect and promote the right to health of the people and to instill health
consciousness among Filipinos. In support of this constitutional mandate, Republic Act
No. 11223, or the “Universal Health Care Act,” emphasizes the need for an integrated
and efficient health information system that will strengthen the delivery of accessible,
responsive, and patient-centered healthcare services throughout the country.

       While efforts toward healthcare digitalization continue to progress, the
country’s health information systems remain largely fragmented and disconnected.
The absence of a unified and interoperable electronic health record infrastructure has
resulted in delays in patient care, duplication of medical procedures, inefficiencies in
health service delivery, and limitations in public health monitoring and planning. These
challenges are especially evident in underserved and geographically isolated
communities where access to healthcare information and digital infrastructure remains
limited.
       This proposed measure seeks to establish a National Interoperable Electronic
Health Information System (NIEHIS) that will integrate public and accredited private
healthcare institutions into a secure, reliable, and patient-centered digital health
network. Through the institutionalization of a National Electronic Health Record (EHR)
System, the bill aims to ensure interoperability among healthcare providers,
strengthen data privacy and cybersecurity protections, improve continuity and quality
of care, and support evidence-based health governance and disease surveillance. It
likewise provides mechanisms for offline accessibility in rural and remote areas and
establishes a clear governance and implementation framework for the modernization
of the country’s healthcare information systems.

      Moreover, the measure upholds the rights of patients over their personal health
information by ensuring informed consent, data protection, and secure access to
medical records, while enabling authorized healthcare professionals’ timely access to
accurate patient information necessary for efficient healthcare delivery.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

 4          SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “National
 5   Interoperable Electronic Health Information System Act.
 6
 7          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
 8   State to protect and promote the right to health of every Filipino by ensuring
 9   accessible, efficient, responsive, and continuous healthcare services through digital
10   transformation. Towards this end, the State shall institutionalize a secure,
11   interoperable, and universally accessible electronic health information system
12   nationwide, while upholding patient privacy, confidentiality, and ownership of health
13   information consistent with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data
14   Privacy Act of 2012.” The State shall likewise promote evidence-based policymaking,
15   disease surveillance, and effective health system planning through integrated health
16   data systems, and ensure equitable access to digital healthcare services, especially in
17   geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.
18
19         SECTION 3. Definition of terms. - For purposes of this Act:
 1
 2   (a) “Electronic Health Record (EHR)” refers to a digital repository of patient health
 3   information containing medical history, diagnoses, medications, laboratory results,
 4   treatment plans, immunization records, and related health data;
 5
 6   (b) “Interoperability” refers to the capability of different health information systems
 7   and applications to exchange, interpret, and utilize data seamlessly;
 8
 9   (c) “Health Information Exchange (HIE)” refers to the electronic sharing of patient
10   health information among authorized healthcare institutions and professionals;
11
12   (d) “National Electronic Health Record System (NEHRS)” refers to the centralized and
13   interoperable national platform established under this Act;
14
15   (e) “Digital Health ID” refers to the unique identifier assigned to every Filipino for
16   healthcare record access and integration;
17
18   (f) “Healthcare Provider” refers to any hospital, clinic, laboratory, rural health unit,
19   health center, or licensed medical professional participating in the NEHRS;
20
21   (g) “Patient Consent” refers to the freely given, informed, and specific agreement by
22   a patient permitting access to or sharing of health information.
23
24           SECTION 4. Establishment of the National Electronic Health Record
25   System. - There is hereby established a National Electronic Health Record System
26   (NEHRS) under the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Department
27   of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), PhilHealth, the Philippine
28   Statistics Authority (PSA), and the National Privacy Commission (NPC).
29           The NEHRS shall:
30           (a) Serve as the centralized and interoperable repository of electronic health
31           records nationwide;
32
33          (b) Ensure secure real-time access to patient records by authorized healthcare
34          providers and patients;
35
36          (c) Integrate with PhilHealth systems, Universal Health Care databases, LGU
37          health systems, and the Philippine Identification System (PhilSys);
38
39          (d) Provide offline access functionality for geographically isolated and
40          disadvantaged areas;
41          (e) Utilize internationally recognized interoperability and cybersecurity
42          standards; and
43
44          (f) Support disease surveillance, public health monitoring, disaster response,
45          and national health planning.
46
 1            SECTION 5. Digital Health ID System. - Every Filipino shall be assigned a
 2   Digital Health ID linked with the Philippine Identification System whenever practicable,
 3   which shall be recognized and usable across all public and accredited private health
 4   facilities nationwide. The Digital Health ID shall be protected through multi-factor
 5   authentication and appropriate cybersecurity safeguards, and shall remain confidential
 6   and subject to the provisions of Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data
 7   Privacy Act of 2012.”
 8
 9          SECTION 6. Mandatory Adoption and Integration. - Within three (3)
10   years from the effectivity of this Act, the following shall adopt and integrate into the
11   NEHRS:
12
13          (a) All DOH-retained hospitals;
14
15          (b) Provincial, city, and municipal hospitals;
16
17          (c) Rural health units (RHUs), barangay health stations, and local health offices;
18
19          (d) State universities and colleges with health-related programs; and
20
21          (e) Government-operated laboratories and specialty centers.
22
23           Private healthcare facilities shall be encouraged to participate within the first
24   two (2) years and shall thereafter comply with interoperability standards as a
25   requirement for PhilHealth accreditation, reimbursements, and coordinated care
26   participation.
27
28          SECTION 7. Patient Rights and Ownership of Health Information. -
29   Patients shall retain ownership and access rights over their electronic health records.
30   No health information shall be accessed, disclosed, or shared without the informed
31   consent of the patient, except when required by law, upon lawful court order, during
32   public health emergencies, or for lawful public health surveillance purposes consistent
33   with existing laws. Patients shall likewise have the right to access their records, request
34   corrections of inaccurate information, monitor access logs relating to their records,
35   and receive notice of data breaches affecting their personal information.
36
37            SECTION 8. Data Privacy, Cybersecurity, and Ethical Use. - The
38   Department of Health (DOH), the Department of Information and Communications
39   Technology (DICT), and the National Privacy Commission (NPC) shall establish
40   mandatory cybersecurity and data governance standards for all participating entities.
41   Such standards shall include end-to-end encryption of health data, secure cloud and
42   on-premise storage systems, multi-factor authentication, periodic cybersecurity audits
43   and penetration testing, incident response and breach notification mechanisms, and
44   ethical standards governing the use of health data, including research access and
45   artificial intelligence applications. Unauthorized access, misuse, tampering, sale,
 1   disclosure, or negligent handling of health information shall be subject to penalties
 2   under existing laws and this Act.

 3          SECTION 9. National Health Information Governance Council. - There
 4   is hereby created a National Health Information Governance Council composed of:

 5   (a) Secretary of Health – Chairperson;

 6   (b) Secretary of the DICT – Vice Chairperson;

 7   (c) Chairperson of the NPC;

 8   (d) President and Chief Executive Officer of PhilHealth;

 9   (e) Representative from the PSA;

10   (f) Representative from the Department of Science and Technology (DOST);

11   (g) One (1) representative each from:

12      1. Private hospitals;

13      2. Government hospitals;

14      3. Medical professional organizations;

15      4. Information technology and cybersecurity experts; and

16      5. Patient advocacy organizations.

17   The Council shall formulate interoperability standards, governance policies,
18   implementation protocols, cybersecurity guidelines, and compliance mechanisms.

19          SECTION 10. Capacity Building and Technical Assistance. - The DOH,
20   in coordination with the DICT, DOST, and TESDA, shall conduct nationwide training
21   programs for healthcare workers, administrators, and information technology
22   personnel.

23          Priority assistance shall be given to geographically isolated and disadvantaged
24   areas, including indigenous communities and underserved local government units.

25            SECTION 11. Incentives for Early Compliance. - Private healthcare
26   facilities and local government units that adopt and fully integrate into the NEHRS
27   within the voluntary adoption period shall be entitled to incentives, which may
28   include:

29         (a) Priority access to government grants and subsidies;

30         (b) Technical assistance and equipment support;
 1         (c) PhilHealth processing incentives; and

 2          (d) Tax deductions for qualified digital health infrastructure investments,
 3   subject to existing laws.

 4           SECTION 12. Penalties. - Any person or entity found guilty of unauthorized
 5   access, disclosure, misuse, tampering, or commercial exploitation of electronic health
 6   records shall, without prejudice to liabilities under Republic Act No. 10173 and other
 7   applicable laws, suffer the following penalties:
 8           (a) Imprisonment of six (6) months to six (6) years;
 9           (b) A fine ranging from Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php500,000.00) to Five
10   Million Pesos (Php5,000,000.00); and
11           (c) Revocation of licenses, accreditation, or authority to operate, when
12   applicable.
13           Public officials who knowingly fail to implement the provisions of this Act
14   without justifiable cause shall be administratively liable.
15

21          The government may likewise receive grants, donations, and technical
