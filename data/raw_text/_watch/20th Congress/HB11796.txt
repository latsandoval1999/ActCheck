EXPLANATORY NOTE

Finding a job is already a challenge for many Filipinos. For some, that challenge becomes
even greater when job requirements, application procedures, and employment-related information
are communicated in a language they do not fully understand. A qualified applicant may have the
skills and willingness to work, yet still struggle to simply understand the qualifications for a
vacancy, the documents needed to apply, or the steps required to access government employment
assistance. Language should not become another obstacle for Filipinos who are simply seeking an
opportunity to earn a decent living and provide for their families.

Under Republic Act No. 8759, as amended by Republic Act No. 10691, otherwise known
as the Public Employment Service Office Act of 1999, Public Employment Service Offices
(PESOs) across the country provide essential services such as job matching, employment referrals,
career guidance, pre-employment orientation, and labor market information. While many PESOs
already communicate with jobseekers in Filipino or languages commonly spoken in their
communities, there is no express and uniform statutory requirement to provide reasonable
language assistance when a jobseeker encounters difficulty understanding essential employment
information. This may place certain jobseekers, particularly those more comfortable
communicating in their regional or indigenous languages, at a disadvantage in accessing available
employment opportunities.

This measure thus seeks to address this gap by making language assistance an express
function of PESOs within the existing public employment service system. It does not seek to
establish a separate employment program or duplicate the functions already performed by PESOs.
Rather, it aims to strengthen the delivery of existing services by ensuring that jobseekers may
request assistance in understanding job qualifications, application procedures, employment
referrals, pre-employment orientation, and other essential employment information.

The proposal likewise recognizes the different capacities and circumstances of local
government units. It does not require PESOs to hire dedicated interpreters, maintain personnel
proficient in every Philippine language, or impose additional translation obligations on private
employers. Instead, PESOs may utilize existing personnel, coordinate with other government
offices, access standardized language resources, or employ appropriate remote and technology-
assisted means to provide reasonable assistance according to local needs and available resources.

To support implementation, the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), in
coordination with the Komisyon sa Wikang Filipino (KWF), the National Commission on
Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), and other relevant agencies, shall develop or facilitate access to
practical language resources for commonly delivered employment services. These resources are
intended to help PESOs explain essential employment terms and procedures more clearly, without
requiring the translation of every job posting, employment contract, or government document into
multiple languages.

For a Filipino looking for work, understanding a job opportunity should not depend on
how well they speak English or another language unfamiliar to them. What matters is that they are
able to understand the requirements, make informed decisions, and pursue employment
opportunities for which they may be qualified. By making existing public employment services
more accessible and responsive to the languages spoken by Filipino communities, this measure
seeks to remove an avoidable barrier between jobseekers and the opportunities that can help
improve their lives.

AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING LANGUAGE ACCESSIBILITY IN PUBLIC EMPLOYMENT
SERVICES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8759, AS
AMENDED BY REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10691, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE PUBLIC
EMPLOYMENT SERVICE OFFICE ACT OF 1999, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Language Access
in Public Employment Services Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State recognizes the right of every Filipino to
equal and meaningful access to public employment services. Consistent with the recognition and
promotion of Filipino and Philippine regional and indigenous languages, the State shal! ensure
that language does not become an unreasonable barrier to a jobseeker's access to employment
information, job matching, employment referrals, counseling, pre-employment orientation, and
other services delivered through the Public Employment Service Office system.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
be defined as follows:

(a) “Jobseeker” refers to any person seeking employment information, referral, placement,
counseling, orientation, or other employment-related assistance from a Public Employment
Service Office;

(b) “Language Assistance” refers to oral, written, in-person, remote, or other appropriate
assistance that enables a jobseeker to understand and meaningfully access public
employment services in Filipino or in a Philippine regional or indigenous language
understood by the jobseeker, without necessarily requiring the full translation of
employment documents;

(c) “Philippine Regional or Indigenous Language” refers to a language native to or
traditionally spoken by a community in the Philippines, including languages used by
indigenous cultural communities or indigenous peoples; and

(d) “Public Employment Service Office (PESO)” refers to a Public Employment Service
Office established and operating pursuant to Republic Act No. 8759, as amended by
Republic Act No. 10691.

No. 8759, as amended by Republic Act No. 10691, is hereby further amended by inserting a new
paragraph (i) immediately after paragraph (h) and renumbering the existing paragraph (i) as
paragraph (j), to read as follows:

“(i) Provide language assistance, upon request of a jobseeker, in the delivery of public
employment services, including the explanation of:

SECTION 5. Language Assistance Mechanism. — Each PESO shall establish a
mechanism for carrying out the language assistance required under the preceding section.
Assistance may be provided through existing personnel, coordination with other offices or
personnel of the local government unit, interpretation or referral arrangements, remote or

technology-assisted support, standardized language resources, or other means prescribed by the
DOLE.

Nothing in this Act shall require a PESO to employ a dedicated interpreter or maintain
personnel proficient in every Philippine regional or indigenous language. The mechanism adopted
shall take into account the languages commonly used in the locality, actual service needs, and
available government resources.

Language assistance shall, to the extent practicable, be provided in Filipino or in a
Philippine regional or indigenous language understood by the jobseeker, taking into account the
request and the circumstances of the transaction. Where assistance in the requested language
cannot be provided immediately, the PESO shall arrange an alternative means of communicating
the essential information within a reasonable period, taking into account any applicable
employment application or referral deadline.

Language assistance under this Act shall be limited to facilitating the understanding of
essential employment information and procedures. It shall not require PESOs to provide certified
translations, legal advice, or complete translations of employment contracts and other documents,
nor shall it impose additional translation obligations on private employers. Technology-assisted
translation may be utilized where appropriate, provided that reasonable measures are taken to
ensure the accuracy of essential information communicated to the jobseeker and the protection of
personal information.

No jobseeker shall be denied access to PESO services or subjected to discriminatory
treatment solely on account of the language used or a request for language assistance.

SECTION 6. Standardized Employment Language Resources. — The DOLE, in
coordination with the KWF, the NCIP, when indigenous languages are concerned, local
government units, and other relevant agencies, shall develop or facilitate access to standardized
language resources for commonly delivered PESO services, including employment-service
glossaries, guides, orientation materials, and other resources that may be adapted to languages
commonly used in particular localities.

In developing such resources, the DOLE shall, to the extent practicable, utilize and adapt
existing government materials, translation resources, and information systems to avoid duplication
of programs and unnecessary expenditure.

as amended by Republic Act No. 10691, is hereby further amended by adding a new paragraph
(h) under the responsibilities of the Department of Labor and Employment, to read as follows:

“(h) Establish language-accessibility standards for PESO services and incorporate
compliance with such standards into the monitoring, assessment, and evaluation of PESO

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOLE, in coordination with the KWF, the NCIP, Department of the
Interior and Local Government (DILG), and other relevant agencies, shall promulgate the rules
and regulations necessary for its implementation, including minimum standards for language
assistance, reasonable response periods, appropriate use of technology-assisted translation, and
integration into existing PESO reporting and monitoring mechanisms.

SECTION 9. Appropriations. — The funding requirements for the implementation of this
Act by Public Employment Service Offices established, operated, and maintained by local
government units shall be included in the respective budgets of the local government units
concerned, subject to applicable budgeting laws, rules, and regulations.

The funding requirements of the Department of Labor and Employment and other national
government agencies for the performance of their respective functions under this Act shall be
charged against their existing appropriations. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in their respective annual appropriations.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
and effect.

SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
