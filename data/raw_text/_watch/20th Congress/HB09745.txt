22               DISMISSAL, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

25                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
26
27          The past several years have witnessed the proliferation of a special kind of
28   harassment suit called Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPP) as a
29   powerful mechanism for stifling the cognate rights of freedom of speech, expression,
30   or of the press, and the right of the people peaceably to assemble and petition the
31   government for redress of grievances.
32
33          SLAPP defies simple definition. They are initiated by influential entities like
34   corporations, government officials, politicians, landlords, or employers against
35   community residents, indigenous peoples, environmentalists, members of the press,
36   peasants, workers, students, or even against ordinary citizens to prevent them from fully
37   exercising their right to participate in matters of public concern.
38
39          The term SLAPP was coined in the 1980s by University of Denver professors
40   Penelope Canan and George W. Pring. It was originally defined as “a lawsuit involving
41   communications made to petition for a governmental action or outcome, which resulted
42   in a baseless suit filed against individuals or non-governmental organizations, on a
43   substantive issue of some public interest or social significance.” It has since been
44   defined more broadly to include suits arising from speech on any public issue.
45




                                                                                                           1
 1          Essentially, SLAPPs are designed and intended to intimidate and silence certain
 2   public constituencies by burdening them with the cost of a legal defense until they
 3   abandon their advocacies and concerns. Winning the lawsuit is not necessarily the
 4   intent of the plaintiff or complainant. Their goals are accomplished if the defendant,
 5   respondent or accused succumbs to fear, intimidation, mounting legal costs or simple
 6   exhaustion and thereby abandons the public advocacy.
 7
 8         According to New York Supreme Court Judge J. Nicholas Colabella, “Short of
 9   a gun to the head, a greater threat to First Amendment expression can scarcely be
10   imagined.”
11
12          SLAPPs are cloaked as claims or accusations for multi-million libel suits,
13   slander, grave coercion, nuisance, illegal assembly, direct assault, resistance and
14   disobedience, and injunction, to name a few.
15
16          In the Philippines, for instance, a notable SLAPP case involves the charges
17   against fifteen (15) individuals in connection with the evacuation of some 700 Lumads
18   to a church sanctuary in Davao City. The Lumads left their homes because of alleged
19   human rights violations and militarization in their communities. The Haran 15, as they
20   are collectively called, are composed of human rights workers, church people,
21   prominent members of indigenous peoples and socio-civic groups, who were charged
22   with kidnapping and illegal detention. The case was eventually dismissed by the
23   Department of Justice after finding no probable cause for the commission of the accused
24   crime.
25
26          For all the diversity of SLAPPs, however, their unifying features make them a
27   dangerous force: they are brought not in pursuit of justice but rather to ensnare their
28   targets in costly litigation that distracts them from the controversy at hand, and to deter
29   them and others from engaging in their rights of speech and petition on issues of public
30   concern.
31
32          Hence, the poor and the marginalized sectors, who have become the usual
33   victims of SLAPPs, find it difficult to continue their work or pursue their livelihood,
34   engage in social and participative actions, and assert their civil, political, economic,
35   social, and cultural rights as they are being burdened, restrained, threatened, or
36   distressed by SLAPPs.
37
38          The inequality of wealth and resources between the rich and the poor, the abuse
39   of power and processes, the lack of independence and impartiality of prosecutors and
40   judges, among other reasons, bring about these SLAPPs.
41
42          In other jurisdictions, particularly in Canada, Europe, and twenty-six (26) States
43   and one (1) territory of the United States, to name a few, the disturbing increase in
44   SLAPPs has led said jurisdictions to either enact anti-SLAPP legislations or promulgate
45   rules against SLAPPs.

 2           Hence, this proposed measure is being filed as a concrete step to address the
 3   societal problems caused by SLAPPs in the Philippines, particularly the achievement
 4   of the following purposes:
 5
 6      a. Promote and protect the constitutional rights of freedom of speech, expression,
 7         and of the press, and the right of the people peaceably to assembly, and petition
 8         the government for redress of grievances;
 9      b. Encourage and strengthen the participation of individuals in matters of public
10         concern;
11      c. Prevent any abuse of the judicial process that shall curtail such participation;
12      d. Prohibit the filing of SLAPPs;
13      e. Establish the speedy dismissal of SLAPPs;
14      f. Award damages, litigation costs, attorney’s fees, and other relief to the
15         defendant, respondent, or accused, as the case may be, upon the final and
16         executory dismissal of SLAPPs; and
17      g. Provide the defendant, respondent, or accused, as the case may be, the right to
18         recover separate damages, litigations costs, attorney’s fees, and other relief by
19         filing a SLAPPBack action against the complainant of a SLAPP upon the final
20         and executory dismissal of such SLAPP.
21
22          This Bill was first filed in February 2009, authored by Bayan Muna Party-List
23   Reps. Satur Ocampo and Teodoro Casiño, aiming to prohibit the filing of harassment
24   cases against persons involved in “public interest advocacies.” It was refiled by Bayan
25   Muna Partylist representatives Neri Colmenares and Carlos Isagani Zarate in the 16th
26   Congress in recognition of the need for the protection against legal harassment of people
27   in the environment, human rights and other socio-civic advocacies. It was refiled by
28   the Makabayan Bloc in the succeeding Congresses.
29
30          It is in this light that the approval of this proposed measure is earnestly sought.

20   DEFINING STRATEGIC LAWSUITS AGAINST PUBLIC PARTICIPATION,
21   PROHIBITING THE FILING THEREOF, PROVIDING MEASURES FOR ITS
22               DISMISSAL, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

25   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
26   Congress assembled:
27
28   SECTION 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Anti-Strategic Lawsuits Against
29   Public Participation Act” or the “Anti-SLAPP Act.”
30
31   SEC. 2. Statement of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to promote and
32   protect the freedom of speech, expression, and of the press, and the right of the people
33   peaceably to assemble, and petition the government for redress of grievances. The State
34   hereby finds and declares that it is in the public interest for people to participate in
35   matters of public concern and provide information to the public on issues that affect
36   them, and that this participation should not be impeded through abuse of the judicial
37   process. Thus, lawsuits that are intended to harass, vex, or silence critics or individuals
38   or groups, or exert undue pressure on them, or deplete their resources, thereby deterring
39   them from fully exercising such constitutional rights shall not be allowed. To this end,
40   this Act shall be construed broadly in favor of the exercise of such constitutional rights.
41
42   SEC. 3. Purposes. – The purposes of this Act shall be the following:
43
44       1. Promote and protect the constitutional rights of freedom of speech, expression,
45           and of the press, and the right of the people peaceably to assemble, and petition
46           the government for redress of grievances;


                                                                                                           4
 1      2. Encourage and strengthen the participation of individuals in matters of public
 2         concern;
 3      3. Prevent any abuse of the judicial process that shall curtail such participation;
 4      4. Prohibit the filing of SLAPPs;
 5      5. Establish the speedy dismissal of SLAPPs;
 6      6. Award damages, litigation costs, attorney’s fees, and other relief to the
 7         defendant, respondent, or accused, as the case may be, upon the final and
 8         executory dismissal of SLAPPs; and
 9      7. Provide the defendant, respondent, or accused, as the case may be, the right to
10         recover separate damages, litigations costs, attorney’s fees, and other relief by
11         filing a SLAPPBack action against the complainant of a SLAPP upon the final
12         and executory dismissal of such SLAPP.
13
14   SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
15   defined as follows:
16
17      1. “Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation” or SLAPP includes any
18         civil complaint, counterclaim, cross-claim, third (fourth, etc.)-party complaint,
19         or complaint-in-intervention, criminal complaint or information, or
20         administrative complaint filed against individual or individuals, groups, labor
21         unions, entities or associations, community residents, or the like by reason or
22         arising out of their exercise of freedom of speech, expression, or of the press, or
23         of the right of the people peaceably to assemble, or petition the government for
24         redress of grievances in matters of public concern, and is intended to harass, vex,
25         or silence individual or individuals, groups, labor unions, entities or associations,
26         community residents, or the like, or exert undue pressure on them, or deplete
27         their resources.
28
29      2. “Public Concern” means anything that involves matters of public, social, or
30         community issue, significance, interest, importance, or welfare.
31
32      3. “Claim” means any civil complaint, counterclaim, cross-claim, third (fourth,
33         etc.)-party complaint, or complaint-in-intervention.
34
35      4. “Plaintiff” refers to the claiming party, the counter-claimant, the cross-claimant, or
36         the third (fourth, etc.)-party plaintiff.
37
38      5. “Defendant” refers to the original defending party, the defendant in
39         counterclaim, the cross-defendant, or the third (fourth, etc.)-party defendant.
40
41      6. “SLAPPBack” means an action for damages filed by the defendant, respondent,
42         or accused, as the case may be, of a SLAPP against the plaintiff or complainant
43         thereof arising from the final and executory dismissal of the said SLAPP.
44
45   SEC. 5. Prohibition on Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation.– It is hereby
46   prohibited to file a Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation (SLAPP) as defined


                                                                                              5
 1   in this Act. The courts, prosecutors and public or private officers, as the case may be,
 2   with the duty to act and decide on civil, criminal or administrative complaints or
 3   information, as the case may be, shall immediately order the dismissal of suits
 4   determined by them to be SLAPPs.
 5
 6   SEC. 6. Duty of the court.– Upon receipt of any complaint or pleading asserting a claim
 7   or any complaint or information, the court shall have the duty to immediately make a
 8   determination whether the same is a SLAPP.
 9
10          If the court finds the same to be a SLAPP, it shall order its dismissal and award
11   to the defendant or accused, as the case may be, damages, litigation costs, attorney’s
12   fees, and other relief as warranted by the circumstances of the case.
13
14          Subject to the right of appeal, an order dismissing the complaint or pleading
15   asserting a claim determined to be a SLAPP shall bar the refiling of the same action or
16   claim, and an order dismissing the complaint or information determined to be a SLAPP
17   shall bar another prosecution for the same offense.
18
19      (a) Motion to Dismiss. – In civil cases, the duty of the court to make such a
20          determination does not preclude the defendant from filing a motion to dismiss
21          on the ground that the complaint or pleading asserting a claim is a SLAPP. Such
22          ground shall not be deemed waived even if not included in a motion to dismiss.
23
24      (b) Motion for Judicial Determination. – In criminal cases, the duty of the court to
25          make such determination does not preclude the accused from filing a motion for
26          judicial determination of the complaint or information as a SLAPP within a
27          reasonable period of time upon the receipt of the complaint or information by the
28          court.
29
30   SEC. 7. Duty of the prosecutor. – Upon receipt of any complaint, or in the conduct of
31   inquest proceedings of a person validly arrested without a warrant, the investigating or
32   inquest prosecutor, as the case may be, shall have the duty to immediately make a
33   determination whether the complaint is a SLAPP.
34
35          If the investigating or inquest prosecutor, as the case may be, finds the same to
36   be a SLAPP, he shall order its dismissal. Subject to the right of appeal, an order
37   dismissing the complaint determined to be a SLAPP shall bar another prosecution for
38   the same offense.
39
40      (a) Motion for Determination. – The duty of the investigating prosecutor to make
41          such determination does not preclude the respondent from filing a motion for
42          determination of the complaint as a SLAPP prior to the submission of his
43          counter-affidavit and that of his witnesses and other supporting documents relied
44          upon for his defense.
45




                                                                                           6
 1      (b) Release for Further Investigation. – If the inquest prosecutor is not certain
 2          whether the complaint is a SLAPP but that there is a possibility that the same
 3          may be a SLAPP as reasonably shown during the inquest proceedings by the
 4          person arrested, the inquest prosecutor shall order the further investigation of the
 5          same, and the immediate release of the person arrested.
 6
 7   The duties of the investigating prosecutor and the rights of the respondent as stated in
 8   this section shall be performed and observed within the conduct of such further
 9   investigation.
10
11   SEC. 8. Administrative cases. – In every administrative case, whether in the public or
12   private sector, the public or private officer, as the case may be, having the duty to decide
13   the complaint shall have the duty to immediately make a determination whether the
14   same is a SLAPP.
15
16          In case of a public officer, if he finds the same to be a SLAPP, he shall order its
17   dismissal and award to the respondent damages, litigation costs, attorney’s fees, and
18   other relief as warranted by the circumstances of the case.
19
20         In case of a private officer, if he finds the same to be a SLAPP, he shall order its
21   dismissal.
22   Subject to the right of appeal, an order dismissing the complaint determined to be a
23   SLAPP shall bar the refiling of the same complaint.
24
25         The duty of the public or private officer, as the case may be, to make such
26   determination does not preclude the respondent from filing a motion to dismiss on the
27   ground that the complaint is a SLAPP.
28
29   SEC. 9. Non-applicability to action involving public interest. – The right to file such
30   motion to dismiss or motion for determination, as the case may be, shall not be
31   applicable against any action brought solely and certainly in the public interest or
32   involving public concern.
33
34   SEC. 10. Right to file legal action and recover separate damages, litigation costs,
35   attorney’s fees, and other reliefs. – Upon the final and executory dismissal of a SLAPP,
36   the defendant, respondent, or accused thereof, as the case may be, shall have the right
37   to file a SLAPPBack, as defined in Section 4 (f) hereof, for damages against the plaintiff
38   or complainant, as the case may be, of the SLAPP, and recover separate damages,
39   litigation costs, attorney’s fees, and other relief as warranted by the circumstances of
40   the case.
41
42           The court shall consider only the fact of the final and executory dismissal of the
43   SLAPP in granting the right to file legal action: Provided, That the court shall consider
44   the circumstances of the case in the determination of the amount of damages, litigation
45   costs, attorney’s costs, and other relief that shall be awarded to the plaintiff thereof.
46

 1   SEC. 11. Motion to dismiss SLAPPBack. – The defendant in a SLAPPBack action may
 2   file a motion to dismiss a SLAPPBack action on the ground that the dismissal of the
 3   SLAPP from which the former arises is not final and executory, among other grounds
 4   that may be available upon the defendant under the Rules of Court: Provided, That if
 5   the motion to dismiss a SLAPPBack action on any ground is found to be frivolous or
 6   solely intended for delay,the court shall deny the motion and award to the plaintiff
 7   damages, litigation costs, attorney’s fees, and other relief as warranted by the
 8   circumstances of the filing and denial of the motion: Provided further, That the same
 9   are separate from the damages, litigation costs, attorney’s fees, and other relief that shall
10   be awarded to the plaintiff in the SLAPPBack action upon the favorable judgment
11   therein becoming final and executory.
12
13   SEC. 12. Prohibition of Appeal. – The denial of (a) a motion to dismiss on the ground
14   that the complaint or pleading asserting a claim is a SLAPP, (b) a motion for
15   determination of the complaint or information as a SLAPP, or (c) a motion to dismiss a
16   SLAPPBack action on the ground that the dismissal of the SLAPP is not final and
17   executory, is not appealable: Provided, That the movant may file an appropriate special
18   civil action under Rule 65 of the Rules of Court.
19
20   SEC. 13. Rules and Regulations. – The Supreme Court shall promulgate the rules and
21   regulations, administrative orders, and circulars for this purpose.
22
23   SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, acts, decrees, executive orders, issuances, and
24   rules and regulations or parts thereof which are contrary to and inconsistent with any
25   provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.
26
27   SEC. 15. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is subsequently declared
28   unconstitutional, the validity of the remaining provisions hereof shall remain in full
29   force and effect.
30
31   SEC. 16. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
32   complete publication either in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
33   circulation in the Philippines.
