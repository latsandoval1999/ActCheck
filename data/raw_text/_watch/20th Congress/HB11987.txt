                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Over the decades, Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) have served as the
undeniable lifeblood of the Philippine economy. Beyond their staggering individual
contributions, foreign remittances represent a critical macroeconomic pillar—bolstering
the country's foreign exchange reserves, stabilizing the national currency, driving
household consumption, and cushioning the domestic economy against external financial
shocks.

        Every single year, billions of dollars flow into the Philippines through the sweat,
grit, and sacrifice of millions of Filipinos working abroad. These financial inflows directly
translate into human capital development, fueling investments in education, healthcare,
small businesses, and long-term savings that uplift entire communities and stimulate
regional growth.

       Despite their monumental contribution to national development, OFWs and their
beneficiaries face a persistent structural barrier: the exorbitant cost of sending money
home. Hard-earned earnings are routinely eroded by excessive transfer fees, hidden
foreign exchange rate markups, opaque intermediary charges, and predatory financial
schemes. When a substantial percentage of remittance money is siphoned off by high
transaction costs and unfair financial practices, the intended macroeconomic impact,
such as poverty reduction, family empowerment, and localized economic stimulation, is
severely undermined.

      To comprehensively address these systemic vulnerabilities and shield our migrant
workers from bad-faith practices, this measure institutes clear-cut statutory safeguards
and enforcement mechanisms:

   1. Mandating Remittance Fee Caps: Placing strict, reasonable ceilings on money
      transfer fees through the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) to ensure that hard-
      earned money goes directly to families rather than excessive corporate charges.
   2. Enforcing Absolute Exchange Rate Transparency: Requiring remittance
      transfer service providers (RTSPs) to fully disclose interbank exchange rates,
      actual spreads, hidden fees, and net payouts prior to transaction completion under
      a binding rate guarantee.
   3. Granting Permanent Tax Relief: Relieving migrant workers from unnecessary
      financial burdens by explicitly exempting OFW remittances from Documentary
      Stamp Tax (DST), local transaction taxes, and Value-Added Tax (VAT) on
      processing fees via a non-obstante clause.
   4. Combating Fraud, Unlicensed Operators, and Offshore Loopholes:
      Mandating strict registration and local pairing for foreign-based digital wallets and
      remittance platforms, backed by joint and several liability for local partners and
      stringent anti-fraud protocols.
   5. Establishing a Consumer Redress Fund & 24/7 Digital Grievance Desk:
      Requiring a mandatory escrow security deposit with the BSP for immediate
      restitution of losses, alongside an integrated 24/7 assistance desk for rapid dispute
      resolution.
   6. Empowering Whistleblowers and Legal Advocates: Providing absolute legal
      immunity and financial rewards for internal corporate whistleblowers, while
      expanding legal standing for OFW migrant organizations and labor groups to file
      class suits on behalf of affected workers.
   7. Promoting Financial Literacy and Inclusion: Embedding mandatory financial
      education into pre-departure and post-arrival orientation seminars to empower
      OFWs and their families to maximize their investments, savings, and digital
      financial tools.

Safeguarding OFW remittances is not merely a matter of consumer protection; it is an
urgent economic imperative. By protecting every peso earned abroad, the State ensures
that the immense sacrifices of our modern-day heroes translate into sustainable, secure,
and dignified futures for their families and the nation as a whole.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE PROTECTION OF OVERSEAS FILIPINO WORKERS (OFW)
REMITTANCES, MANDATING FEE CAPS AND TRANSPARENCY IN TRANSFER
COSTS, PENALIZING UNAUTHORIZED AND PREDATORY REMITTANCE
PRACTICES, ESTABLISHING CONSUMER REDRESS AND WHISTLEBLOWER
SAFEGUARDS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "OFW Remittance Protection
Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to afford full protection to
labor, local and overseas, and to recognize the vital role of Overseas Filipino Workers
(OFWs) in national development and economic stabilization. The State recognizes that
foreign remittances sent by OFWs represent the product of their hard work, personal
sacrifice, and critical macroeconomic inflows. Towards this end, the State shall
implement stringent measures to protect OFW remittances from unfair transaction
fees, opaque exchange rate markups, predatory financial practices, and illegal
remittance schemes, while promoting accessible, secure, and affordable financial
channels.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
defined as follows:
   a) Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) refers to a person who is to be engaged, is
      engaged, or has been engaged in a remunerated activity in a state of which he or
      she is not a citizen, or on board a vessel navigating foreign seas.
   b) Remittance refers to foreign exchange earnings or money transfers sent by an
      OFW from abroad to a beneficiary in the Philippines through authorized financial
      channels.
   c) Remittance Transfer Service Provider (RTSP) refers to any bank, non-bank
      financial institution, money transfer operator (MTO), electronic money issuer (EMI),
      microfinance institution, digital fintech platform, or authorized agent offering money
      transfer services to OFWs, regardless of whether their principal operations are
      domestic or extraterritorial.
   d) Exchange Rate Spread refers to the difference between the prevailing interbank
      exchange rate and the exchange rate offered by the RTSP to the sender or
      beneficiary.
   e) Baseline Interbank Transfer Index refers to the quarterly benchmark rate published
      by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas for transfer fee evaluations.

SEC. 4. Cap on Remittance Fees and Anti-Predatory Pricing Rule. — The following
shall govern the remittances of OFWs:

   a) No bank, non-bank financial institution, or licensed RTSP operating or servicing
      transactions in the Philippines shall charge remittance transfer fees exceeding fifty
      percent (50%) of the prevailing standard market rate for money transfers, or a
      maximum ceiling of two percent (2%) of the total amount transferred, whichever is
      lower.
   b) The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) shall periodically review, benchmark, and
      publish a definitive, standardized "Baseline Interbank Transfer Index" quarterly to
      ensure affordability and enforceability.
   c) Non-Diminution and Anti-Circumvention Clause: No RTSP may fragment transfer
      costs into ancillary processing tiers, handling fees, or unbundled network charges
      to circumvent the 2% hard ceiling.

SEC. 5. Full Disclosure and Exchange Rate Transparency. — All RTSPs operating or
partnering in the Philippines shall strictly adhere to transparency standards in all
remittance transactions. Prior to executing a transaction, the RTSP shall deliver Real-
Time Pre-Commitment Disclosures via digital prompts or physical receipts stating:

             a) The exact transfer fee charged;
             b) The prevailing interbank exchange rate and the actual exchange rate
             applied to the transaction;
             c) The total exchange rate margin or spread collected by the RTSP;
             d) The net amount in Philippine Pesos (PHP) to be received by the
             beneficiary; and
             e) The estimated date and time of availability of the funds.

      The exchange rate and fee structure disclosed must remain locked or guaranteed
and binding for the transaction. Any discrepancy or un-itemized markup added post-
transaction renders the transaction voidable at the option of the OFW sender, with a
mandatory full refund of fees plus statutory interest. No hidden charges, undisclosed
processing fees, or dynamic currency conversion markups shall be collected beyond what
was explicitly disclosed prior to transaction confirmation.

SEC. 6. Tax Exemption on Remittance Services. —Notwithstanding any provision of
the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC) of 1997, as amended, to the contrary:
           a) All money transfers sent by OFWs to their legitimate family members in the
              Philippines shall be exempt from Documentary Stamp Tax and local
              government transaction taxes; and
           b) Service fees charged by domestic financial institutions and payment
              channels for processing OFW remittances shall be exempt from Value-
              Added Tax.

       These tax reliefs shall remain permanent and unhindered by administrative sunset
provisions short of explicit legislative amendment.

SEC. 7. Protection Against Fraud, Unlicensed Operators, and Extraterritorial
Compliance. — To protect better OFW remittances from fraudulent transactions and
unregistered RTSPs, the following shall govern RSTPs’ operations:

   a) Mandatory Registration and Extraterritorial Reach: Only licensed RTSPs duly
        registered with the BSP and accredited by the Department of Migrant Workers
        (DMW) shall be authorized to handle OFW remittance services.
   b)   Mandatory Local Pairing and Extraterritorial Jurisdiction: Any foreign-based
        remittance operator, digital wallet, or financial platform servicing OFWs abroad
        must maintain a registered legal entity, a designated compliance officer, and a
        formal partnership agreement with a BSP-supervised financial institution within the
        Philippines. Failure to subject themselves to the concurrent regulatory jurisdiction
        of the BSP and DMW shall result in an automatic blocking of their digital
        applications, websites, and payout channels across Philippine telecommunications
        and internet networks, in coordination with the National Telecommunications
        Commission.
   c)   Anti-Fraud Protocols: All RTSPs shall implement robust cybersecurity measures,
        multi-factor authentication, and consumer dispute resolution mechanisms to
        protect transactions against fraud, phishing, and unauthorized deductions.
   d)   Joint and Several Liability Principle: Domestic partner banks, telecommunication
        companies, or local payment gateways that facilitate transactions for unregistered
        or unlicensed foreign RTSPs shall be held jointly and severally liable for any
        financial losses suffered by the OFW beneficiary.
   e)   Prohibition of Illegal Remittance Practices: The operation of unlicensed money
        transfer networks, informal hawala-style unregistered channels targeting OFWs,
        and predatory lending tied to remittance receipts is hereby declared illegal.

SEC. 8. OFW Remittance Consumer Redress Fund. — All licensed RTSPs operating
in the Philippines shall maintain a mandatory security deposit or bond, equivalent to a
percentage of their total average monthly remittance volume, placed in an escrow account
with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas. This fund shall be utilized exclusively for the
immediate, expedited restitution and indemnification of OFWs and beneficiaries who
suffer financial losses due to unauthorized deductions, system outages, cyber-breaches,
or predatory pricing by the RTSP.
SEC. 9. Inter-Agency Oversight Council on OFW Remittance Protection. — An Inter-
Agency Coordinating Council is hereby established to monitor compliance, investigate
abuses, and streamline remittance services for OFWs, composed of:

   a) Secretary of the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) – Chairperson
   b) Governor of the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) – Co-Chairperson
   c) Secretary of the Department of Finance (DOF) – Member
   d) Secretary of the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) – Member
   e) Secretary of the Department of Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDev) –
      Member
   f) Two (2) representatives from accredited OFW non-governmental organizations –
      Members

SEC. 10. 24/7 Remittance Assistance and Digital Grievance Desk. — The DMW, in
coordination with the Overseas Workers Welfare Administration (OWWA) and the BSP,
shall establish a dedicated 24/7 digital grievance desk and integrated tracking system
specifically for remittance-related disputes. RTSPs shall be mandated to resolve or
respond to logged complaints within forty-eight (48) hours. Failure to resolve valid
complaints within the prescribed window shall incur automatic daily administrative fines.

SEC. 11. Financial Literacy and Digital Inclusion. — The DMW, in coordination with
the BSP, OWWA, and accredited banks, shall integrate mandatory financial literacy
training into the Pre-Departure Orientation Seminars (PDOS) and Post-Arrival Orientation
Seminars (PAOS) for OFWs. The program shall cover savings, investment, financial
planning, digital remittance tools, and protection against financial scams.

SEC. 12. Whistleblower Protection and Rewards. — Any employee, officer, or agent
of an RTSP who reports, discloses, or testifies regarding violations of this Act shall be
granted absolute immunity from civil and criminal liability for their involvement in the
reported violation, provided they did not mastermind the illegal activity. Furthermore,
whistleblowers shall be entitled to a ten percent (10%) share of any administrative fines
collected from the violating institution as a reward. Strict confidentiality and protection
against workplace retaliation or termination shall be enforced by the DMW.

SEC. 13. Class Actions and Legal Standing. — Accredited OFW migrant organizations,
labor unions, and non-governmental organizations dedicated to migrant welfare are
hereby granted legal standing (locus standi) to file class suits or administrative complaints
on behalf of affected OFWs before the BSP, DMW, or courts of law. In cases where a
class suit is successful, the court or administrative body shall award reasonable attorney's
fees to the representing legal aid organization, to be paid separately by the offending
RTSP.

SEC. 14. Penal Provisions and Restitution. — Any RTSP, financial institution,
corporate officer, or individual found violating the provisions of this Act shall be subject to
the following administrative and criminal penalties:
   a) First Offense: A fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00)
       but not exceeding One Million Pesos (₱1,000,000.00), and a formal reprimand;
   b) Second Offense: A fine of not less than One Million Pesos (₱1,000,000.00) but not
      exceeding Two Million Pesos (₱2,000,000.00), and a temporary suspension of
      business operating license for six (6) months;
   c) Third and Subsequent Offenses: A fine of not less than Three Million Pesos
      (₱3,000,000.00) but not exceeding Five Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00), permanent
      revocation of the operating license, and imprisonment of responsible corporate
      officers (specifically including the president, general manager, chief financial
      officer, and members of the board of directors who knowingly authorize, tolerate,
      or fail to act upon systemic violations) for a period of not less than six (6) years nor
      more than twelve (12) years.

    The BSP and DMW are empowered to issue freeze orders on the operational
accounts of violating RTSPs and compel the complete restitution or disgorgement of
illegally collected fees directly to affected OFWs prior to the exhaustion of administrative
appeals.

SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the BSP, in consultation with the DMW, DOF, DTI, DEPDev, OWWA,
and relevant private sector stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 16. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, rules, and regulations, or parts
thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended
accordingly.

SEC. 18. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
