                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The worsening housing gap in lower- and middle-income countries is largely
attributed to rapid urbanization and population growth outpacing the housing
supply (Lee and Singh, 2022). In 2018, the Philippine housing backlog reached 5.7
million units and absent any significant intervention, this housing backlog is
projected to reach 22 million by 2040 (Padojinog and Yap, 2020). Furthermore,
with 4.1 million individuals displaced due to government infrastructure projects in
2019 alone (Bermudez et al., 2020) and an estimated 3.7 million Informal Settler
Families (ISFs) residing in hazard-prone areas (UN-Habitat, 2023), addressing the
housing backlog remains a priority concern of the government.

       Despite ongoing government housing initiatives targeting the housing deficit,
beneficiaries of socialized housing moved in to housing units without basic services
such as electricity (Ebdane, 2024). Compounding this issue, plant outages and
rising consumer electricity demand consistently triggered yellow and red alerts in
the transmission grid, resulting in power interruptions and prompting several
legislative inquiries (Baligad, 2024). While socialized housing programs are
addressing the need for shelter, many resettlement communities remain unserved
or underserved in terms of reliable, affordable, and sustainable energy access.

        The Philippines' reliance on imported fuel makes the country highly
vulnerable to external energy shocks, contributing to rising food and energy costs
(De Leon, 2026). Estimated electricity rates in the country are among the highest in
Asia, ranking fourth overall (Dela Peña, 2026). These conditions expose Filipino
households to high energy costs, unstable power supply, or limited access to grid
electricity, with greater impact to households in resettlement sites, undermining
the long-term livability and sustainability of housing interventions. Coupled with
the increasing frequency and intensity of climate-related hazards, the vulnerability
of traditional energy systems underscores the need for resilient, decentralized, and
renewable energy solutions.

       Article XIII, Section 9 of the 1987 Constitution mandates the State to
undertake a continuing program of urban land reform and housing which will
make available at affordable cost decent housing and basic services to
underprivileged and homeless citizens. Legislations, including Republic Act No.
9513 or the Renewable Energy Act of 2008, affirm the national policy to accelerate
the utilization of renewable energy resources. However, despite existing policy
frameworks, the integration of renewable energy systems, particularly solar home
systems (SHS), into socialized housing developments remains largely dependent on
project-level initiatives rather than a standardized national framework.
       This bill seeks to address the policy and implementation gap by mandating
the integration of SHS in all socialized housing projects, whether on-grid, off-grid,
or hybrid, and by establishing a dedicated financing mechanism to ensure its
feasibility and affordability. By institutionalizing SHS as a standard component of
housing design and delivery, this measure ensures that energy access is no longer
treated as an auxiliary concern but as an essential element of housing adequacy. It
likewise integrates the SHS Financing Program into housing amortization schemes
with repayment terms of up to twenty-five (25) years, while employing a mix of
subsidies, grants, and concessional financing to minimize the burden on
beneficiary households. The financing structure is designed to ensure that total
electricity-related expenditures remain comparable to, if not lower than,
conventional grid-based electricity costs over time.

        The measure also establishes the technical standards, quality assurance
mechanisms, and mandatory maintenance requirements to ensure system
reliability and sustainability. It institutionalizes coordination among key agencies,
including the Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development,
Department of Energy, National Housing Authority, and Social Housing Finance
Corporation, as well as Local Government Units, to ensure alignment in planning
and execution. In addition, it provides for beneficiary orientation programs and a
monitoring and evaluation system to track system performance, household energy
savings, and broader socioeconomic and environmental impacts.

       Beyond ensuring energy adequacy, this measure promotes electricity
security in socialized housing communities without adding strain to the national
power grid. Additionally, where systems are grid-connected and eligible for net
metering, these socialized housing communities may even contribute to the overall
energy stability by serving as solar farms, supplying excess power back to the grid.

       The integration of solar energy systems in socialized housing represents a
strategic shift toward building a climate-resilient, energy-secure, and sustainable
communities, consistent with the State's commitment to inclusive development and
environmental stewardship.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
MANDATING THE INTEGRATION OF SOLAR HOME SYSTEMS IN ALL
SOCIALIZED HOUSING PROJECTS, ESTABLISHING A SOLAR HOME SYSTEM
FINANCING PROGRAM, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in the Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Solar Home
Integration and Financing for Transformation (SHIFT) in Socialized Housing Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to protect and promote the right of people to decent housing with access to
affordable, reliable, and sustainable basic services, including electricity. The State
recognizes that Informal Settler Families (ISF) residing in danger zones and those
displaced by government infrastructure projects and calamities have the
fundamental right to habitable, climate-resilient, and energy-secure resettlement
communities.
       Toward this end, the State shall promote the adoption of renewable energy
systems, particularly solar energy, as a cost-efficient, reliable, and environmentally
sustainable energy solution for household electrification. The State shall ensure
that socialized housing projects are provided with appropriate solar energy
solutions, whether off-grid, on-grid, or hybrid systems, to enhance energy access
and stability, reduce long-term household expenditures, and contribute to climate
change mitigation and adaptation.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
      (a) Hybrid Solar Systems refer to electrical systems combining solar
          photovoltaic (PV) with a battery energy storage system (BESS) and/or
          grid connection;
      (b) Net Metering refers to a system, appropriate for distributed generation, in
          which a distribution grid user has a two-way connection to the grid and
          is only charged for his net electricity consumption and is credited for any
          overall contribution to the electricity grid;
      (c) Off-Grid Systems refer to electrical systems not connected to the wires
          and related facilities of the On-Grid Systems of the Philippines;
      (d) On-Grid Systems refers to electrical systems composed of interconnected
          transmission lines, distribution lines, substations, and related facilities
          for the purpose of conveyance of bulk power on the grid of the
          Philippines;
      (e) Renewable Energy Systems refer to energy systems which convert
          Renewable Energy resources into useful energy forms, like electrical,
          mechanical, etc.;
      (f) Socialized Housing Projects refer to housing programs and projects
          undertaken by the National Government, Local Government Units
          (LGUs), Government-Owned and Controlled Corporations (GOCCs), or the
          private sector intended for the underprivileged and homeless citizens;
      (g) Solar Energy refers to the energy derived from solar radiation that can be
          converted into useful thermal or electric energy;
      (h) Solar Energy System refers to energy systems which convert solar energy
          into thermal or electrical energy; and
      (i) Solar Home System (SHS) refers to a decentralized, stand-alone solar PV
          energy system supplying amenity power for lighting and household
          appliances providing affordable and reliable alternative to traditional
          electrical grids. Depending on viability, SHS may be on-grid, off-grid, or
          hybrid solar system.

       SEC. 4. Mandatory Integration of Solar Home Systems. – All existing and
future socialized housing projects shall integrate Solar Home Systems (SHS) as a
standard component of housing design and delivery, whether such project is
located in on-grid or off-grid areas, subject to technical feasibility, cost-efficiency
standards, and suitability to the housing configuration, as determined by the
Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD) and the
Department of Energy (DOE).
       For socialized housing typologies, SHS integration shall be implemented as
follows:
       (a) Horizontal housing developments such as row houses, duplexes, and
           single-detached units shall utilize individual SHS installation; and
       (b) Vertical or multi-storey housing developments with shared roofing may
           utilize shared, centralized, or cluster-based SHS configuration with
           appropriate system design. The DHSUD and DOE shall establish a
           mechanism to guarantee equitable access, prevent overconsumption, and
           responsible use of electricity which may include installation of load
           management systems, including sub-metering systems and load limiters
           or equivalent controls to prevent system overloading and ensure fair
           distribution of available energy.

       SEC. 5. Solar Home System Configuration. – Socialized housing SHS
installation shall be configured according to service type as follows:
       (a) For socialized housing projects in on-grid areas, SHS may be installed as
           grid-tied or hybrid systems, with eligibility for net metering in accordance
           with Republic Act 9513, otherwise known as the Renewable Energy Act of
           2008, and its subsequent amendments, rules, and regulations
           promulgated thereunder; and
       (b) For socialized housing projects in off-grid areas, SHS shall serve as the
           primary source of electricity. To ensure continuous power supply during
           periods of low or no solar production, the SHS may be installed as a
           hybrid system with BESS integration.

      SEC. 6. Technical Standards, Quality Assurance, and Maintenance. –
The DOE, in coordination with the DHSUD, shall develop and enforce a minimum
technical standard for SHS, including:
      (a) System capacity and performance requirement;
      (b) Equipment certification and quality standards;
      (c) Installation, safety, and maintenance protocols; and
      (d) Accreditation of suppliers and service providers.

        All SHS installed under this Act shall have a mandatory warranty provision,
after-sales service and maintenance support, and sustainable mechanism for
lifecycle costs, including battery replacement and system upkeep.

       SEC. 7. Solar Home Systems Financing Program. – An SHS Financing
Program is hereby established to support the integration of SHS in all current and
future socialized housing financing projects in both in-city and off-city resettlement
sites.
       All government housing financing mechanisms, including but not limited to
those administered by the National Housing Authority (NHA), Social Housing
Finance Corporation (SHFC), Home Development Mutual Fund (Pag-IBIG Fund),
Government Financial Institutions (GFIs), and LGUs, shall incorporate SHS
financing as an integral component of housing finance packages.
       For this purpose, the cost of SHS shall be mandatorily included in the total
project cost and shall form part of the loanable amount or amortization base of the
housing unit, subject to affordability standards. The program shall utilize a mix of
capital subsidies, grants, concessional financing, or other appropriate financial
instruments to defray the upfront cost of SHS. Beneficiaries shall amortize the SHS
cost together with the housing unit under concessional and affordable terms of up
to twenty-five (25) years and shall be structured such that the total electricity-
related expenditures of beneficiary households shall not exceed the estimated cost
of conventional grid-based electricity over a reasonable period.
       The annual target for SHS provision shall be based on the combined annual
socialized housing targets of the LGUs, NHA, and SHFC. A corresponding per-unit
allocation shall be established based on standardized benchmarks and ceilings to
be determined by the DOE in coordination with the DHSUD.

        SEC. 8. Solar Home Systems Orientation. – The NHA, SHFC, and LGUs
shall, in coordination with the DOE, orient ISF beneficiaries of socialized housing
projects on the use and benefits of SHS as an affordable, viable, accessible, and
clean energy source and the integration of SHS financing program in socialized
housing projects.

       SEC. 9. Monitoring and Reporting. – The DHSUD shall, in coordination
with concerned agencies, establish a monitoring and evaluation system and submit
an annual report to Congress by March 31 of the succeeding year, including:
       (a) Number of housing units installed with SHS;
       (b) System functionality and performance rates;
       (c) Household energy cost savings; and
       (d) Other relevant socioeconomic and environmental indicators.

       SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s
appropriations of the concerned agencies. Thereafter, such sums shall be included
in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DHSUD, NHA, and the SHFC, in coordination
with the DOE, Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and other
government agencies concerned, shall promulgate the necessary implementing
rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.
       SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, letters of instruction, administrative rules and regulations or parts thereof,
which are contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
