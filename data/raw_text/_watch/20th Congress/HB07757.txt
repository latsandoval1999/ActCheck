                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       No less than the Constitution protects every Filipino’s right to travel.
Section 6, Article III of the 1987 Constitution provides that “neither shall the right
to travel be impaired except in the interest of national security, public safety, or
public health, as may be provided by law.”

       Enacted on 3 October 1977, Presidential Decree no. 1183, as amended, was
issued to facilitate foreign travel and make the same more beneficial for both the
State and the People. However, the imposition of Travel Taxes, and its continued
collection every single instance a Filipino has to travel abroad – regardless if the
departure is for official, educational, corporate, or leisurely purpose – is
becoming a burden. Currently, travelers pay travel taxes ranging from P300 to
P2,700 per departure, depending on flight class and eligibility for discounts.

       Tourism Secretary Christina Frasco said the travel tax “provides benefits
to the Filipino people,” noting that 50 percent of the collections are allocated to
tourism infrastructure, 40 percent support tourism education, while the remaining
10 percent are allocated to heritage agencies.

       The justification that travel tax funds several programs of the Department
of Tourism is akin to stating travelers shall forever carry the burden of funding
these state programs. That is the job of the Government. As legislators, we have
to ensure that the laws work for the people and make life more livable for them.
Our policies shall ensure not only that our programs are well-funded, but more so
that our people are not constantly and unreasonably carrying the burden of paying
various onerous taxes for all our funding needs.
       Abolishing travel taxes is also not unheard of. The ASEAN Tourism
Agreement of 2002 provides that Member States have committed to facilitate
travel within the region, including the removal or phasing out of travel levies and
travel taxes on nationals of ASEAN Member States traveling to other ASEAN
Member States. This obligation under ASEAN Agreement is yet to be fulfilled.

        Furthermore, as of February 2026, the Philippines remains the only country
in Southeast Asia that imposes a separate, mandatory travel tax specifically on its
own citizens. While a similar charge is also collected by other countries like Japan
(Sayonara Tax) and Australia (Passenger Movement Charge), these countries
incorporate those fees into their airlines fares and collect against all travelers, not
just its citizens.

      Thus, this measure seeks to abolish in total the imposition of travel tax
under Presidential Decree 1183 and its subsequent amendments.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                     AN ACT ABOLISHING THE TRAVEL TAX

     Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
     in Congress assembled:

 1         SECTION 1. Presidential Decree No. 1183, as amended, laws, executive
 2   orders, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, modified,
 3   or amended accordingly.
 4
 5          Further, Republic Act No. 1478, as amended, Republic Act No. 7722,
 6   Republic Act No. 9593, and all provisions of laws relating to the authority to
 7   collect, appropriate, or receive funding from the collection of travel tax are hereby
 8   repealed.
 9
10         Travel tax shall therefore no longer be imposed on travellers leaving the
11   Philippines for every international departure.
12
13

15   collections and for the implementation of this Act shall be charged against the
16   respective fund sources under the appropriations provided in the current General
17   Appropriations Act (GAA) of the Department of Tourism for the Tourism
18   Infrastructure and Enterprise Zone Authority (TIEZA), the Commission on
19   Higher Education (CHED) for the Higher Education Development Fund, and the
20   National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA) for the National
21   Endowment Fund for Culture and the Arts. Thereafter, such sums as may be
22   necessary shall be included in the annual GAA.
