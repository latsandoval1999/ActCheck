                             AN ACT
PROHIBITING THE USE OF MOBILE DIGITAL WALLETS, ELECTRONIC MONEY,
PAYMENT GATEWAYS, ONLINE BANKING TRANSFERS, AND OTHER ELECTRONIC
PAYMENT SYSTEMS AS PAYMENT CHANNELS FOR ALL FORMS OF ONLINE
GAMBLING AND ELECTRONIC WAGERING, PROVIDING COMPLIANCE DUTIES,
ENFORCEMENT MECHANISMS, PENALTIES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

Online gambling has become easier to access and harder to control because people
can now place bets anytime using mobile wallets, online banking, and other digital
payment apps. Unlike casinos or betting shops that have clear limits, online gambling
is always just one click away. This constant access increases the risk of addiction, debt,
fraud, and inancial distress, especially among the youth and other vulnerable groups,
because money can be deposited, gambled, and withdrawn instantly using everyday
payment tools.

Online gambling also poses serious risks to the country’s inancial system. Gaming
platforms can be used to move and hide illegal money quickly, making enforcement
more dif icult. While the government already has laws to ight money laundering,
these laws are designed to act after illegal activity is detected and require strict legal
processes. As a result, they are not enough on their own to stop the widespread and
daily use of digital payments as fuel for online gambling.

This bill offers a clear and practical solution: it cuts off electronic payment channels
as a means to fund online gambling and electronic wagering, except for limited
purposes such as refunds or court-ordered transactions. By stopping payments at the

and families, and helps safeguard the integrity of the inancial system. The approach

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Online Gambling Payment
Channel Prohibition Act of 2026.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
safeguard public morals, protect consumers and vulnerable sectors, and preserve the
integrity of the inancial system by cutting off electronic payment rails used to
fund online gambling and electronic wagering, regardless of whether such
gambling activity is claimed to be licensed, authorized, or regulated, unless expressly
exempted by this Act.

For this purpose, the State shall require payment intermediaries to adopt effective
controls against online gambling payments, consistent with due process and existing
 inancial and anti-money laundering laws.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
de ined:

   (a) “Electronic Payment Channel” refers to any facility, instrument, platform, or
       service that enables the transfer of monetary value electronically, including
       but not limited to: electronic money (e-money), mobile wallets, online banking
       transfers, InstaPay/PESONet-type transfers, payment gateways, merchant
       acquiring, card-not-present facilities, QR payments, stored value facilities, and
       other analogous channels.

   (b) “Payment Service Provider (PSP)” refers to any person or entity that
       provides or operates an Electronic Payment Channel to users or merchants,
       including electronic money issuers, wallet operators, payment gateways,
       merchant acquirers, and other intermediaries, whether bank or non-bank, that
       are subject to appropriate supervision and regulation.

   (c) “Online Gambling or Electronic Wagering” refers to any gambling, betting,
       wagering, staking, or gaming activity conducted through the internet or any
       digital network, where money or anything of value is paid, staked, wagered,
       won, lost, credited, or withdrawn through electronic means.

   (d) “Online Gambling Payment Transaction” refers to any transaction that
       funds, stakes, reloads, buys credits for, cashes out from, settles, or otherwise
       monetizes Online Gambling or Electronic Wagering, including transactions
       made to or from a gambling operator, its agent, its payment proxy, or a
       merchant identi ier associated with such activity.

   (e) “Covered Online Gambling Operators” includes, for avoidance of doubt,
       offshore gaming operations and their service providers that may be
       regulated/accredited by PAGCOR or any agency, but are nonetheless covered
       by the prohibition under this Act.

SECTION 4. General Prohibition. — Notwithstanding any license, accreditation,
permit, or authority granted by any government of ice or instrumentality, it
shall be unlawful for any person or entity to use, and for any PSP to process, any
Online Gambling Payment Transaction through an Electronic Payment Channel,
except as expressly allowed under Section 5 (Permitted Transactions).

SECTION 5. Permitted Transactions (narrow exceptions). — The following shall
not be considered violations of this Act, subject to conditions in the IRR:

   (a) Refunds and reversals initiated by PSPs or banks to correct erroneous,
       duplicate, or unauthorized debits, provided that such refunds do not enable
       further gambling play;

   (b) Chargeback processing and dispute resolution under consumer protection
       and payment network rules;

   (c) Regulatory compliance transactions, including preservation/transfer of
       funds pursuant to a lawful court order, AMLC/competent authority directives
       consistent with AMLA, or compliance with lawful processes; and

   (d) Other narrow exceptions that may be necessary to implement this Act without
       undermining its policy, as may be de ined in the IRR, subject to congressional
       oversight through reporting.

SECTION 6. Prohibited Acts. — It shall be unlawful for:

   (a) PSPs to process, facilitate, accept, route, settle, or enable any Online Gambling
       Payment Transaction, directly or indirectly, including through merchant
       acquiring, QR codes, payment links, proxies, agents, aggregators, or
       “merchant-of-record” arrangements;

   (b) Any person to initiate, attempt to initiate, or conspire to initiate an Online
       Gambling Payment Transaction through an Electronic Payment Channel;

   (c) Online gambling operators, agents, af iliates, payment proxies, and service
       providers to receive, transmit, or cause the receipt or transmission of funds
       through prohibited Electronic Payment Channels; and

   (d) PSPs to willfully evade or circumvent controls under this Act by disguising
       merchant descriptors, misclassifying merchants, using layering/structuring,
       or similar schemes.

SECTION 7. Compliance Duties of PSPs. — All PSPs shall:

   (a) Implement risk-based KYC and merchant onboarding controls reasonably
       designed to prevent Online Gambling Payment Transactions;

                                                                            5|Page
   (b) Establish automated and manual monitoring capable of detecting and
       interdiction of Online Gambling Payment Transactions using risk indicators to
       be issued under the IRR;

   (c) Decline, block, or suspend Online Gambling Payment Transactions and
       associated instruments or merchant access, consistent with due process
       standards in BSP supervisory rules and the IRR;

   (d) Maintain records and audit trails of interdicted transactions and actions taken;

   (e) Submit compliance reports to the BSP (and to AMLC where applicable) in the
       form and frequency to be prescribed.

SECTION 8. Enforcement and Coordination. —

   (a) Primary regulator for PSP compliance. The BSP shall have primary
       supervisory authority over BSP-supervised PSPs to ensure compliance with
       this Act and may impose supervisory enforcement actions authorized under
       existing laws and regulations.

   (b) AMLC role. The AMLC shall exercise its powers under AMLA, as amended,
       including investigation and application for freeze orders and bank inquiry
       orders, consistent with existing standards.

   (c) Referrals and coordination. The BSP, AMLC, DICT, NTC, PNP/NBI, and other
       agencies shall establish mechanisms for information-sharing, referrals, and
       evidence preservation.

   (d) Blocking of access (telecom/digital measures). DICT/NTC coordination for
       blocking of access to illegal online gambling sites may be undertaken subject
       to existing laws, jurisdictional mandates, and due process, and shall be
       addressed in the IRR to avoid unconstitutional or ultra vires blocking.

SECTION 9. Freezing of Funds and Bank Inquiry; Standards and Due Process. —

   (a) Any freezing of monetary instruments or property related to violations of
       AMLA shall be pursued through the Court of Appeals upon AMLC application
       as provided by law.

SECTION 10. Penalties. —

   (a) Individuals (users/initiators). Imprisonment of one (1) year to ive (5)
       years and/or a ine of PHP 200,000.00 to PHP 5,000,000.00.

   (c) PSPs/juridical    entities. Fine   of PHP     5,000,000.00      to    PHP

   (d) Responsible of icers. Directors, of icers, compliance of icers, and employees

       criminally liable and may be perpetually disquali ied from employment in BSP-

       sanctions.

SECTION 11. Non-Exemption from Other Liabilities. — Prosecution under this Act
shall be without prejudice to liability under AMLA, cybercrime and other penal laws,
and relevant regulatory issuances.

SECTION 12. Transitory Provision. — Within ninety (90) days from effectivity,
PSPs shall: (i) implement controls to block Online Gambling Payment Transactions;
(ii) terminate prohibited merchant arrangements; and (iii) submit a sworn
compliance certi ication and implementation plan to the BSP in the form prescribed
by regulation.
