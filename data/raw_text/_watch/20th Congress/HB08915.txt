                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The rapid growth in the use of internet cafes and computer shops in the
Philippines has created both opportunities and challenges for ensuring safe
digital spaces, especially for the youth and the general public. While these
establishments provide affordable internet access to many Filipinos, reports
and existing local ordinances highlight significant risks related to unregulated
access to adult content and lewd websites, which can contribute to moral and
social harm.

      A 2024 assessment by Plan International Pilipinas revealed that Filipino
children and youth are still significantly exposed to unsafe online content and
behaviors even within supervised spaces. Moreover, the Foundation for Media
Alternatives documented an alarming rise in cases of online sexual abuse and
exploitation of children (OSAEC). Despite protective legislation such as the
Anti-Child Pornography Act, minors remain exposed to harmful online content
and abuse.

      In response to these challenges, the City of Naga enacted Ordinance No.
2013-005, which mandates the installation of filtering software to block access
to adult content, satanic, and violent websites in internet cafes in its
jurisdiction and requires transparent partitions to prevent illicit use1 However,
enforcement and implementation remain inconsistent at the national level.

      More recently, the COVID-19 pandemic spurred operational guidelines
for internet cafes by local governments such as Manila, emphasizing

       The proposed “Internet Safe Youth Spaces Act” seeks to establish a
uniform, enforceable framework nationwide requiring internet cafes and
similar establishments to install effective blocking software against adult
content and lewd sites, impose penalties for non-compliance, and empower
citizens to report violations confidentially to authorities. In doing so, the State
aims to protect the youth and promote community welfare, while preserving
public digital spaces.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Internet Safe Youth
Spaces Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect
the moral, physical, and mental well-being of its citizens, especially the youth,
from all forms of harm, including adult contents and lewd content accessible
online. The State shall ensure that computer shops and similar
establishments do not serve as venues for the distribution or viewing of such
materials.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the term:

     a)   “Computer Shop/Internet Cafe” means any establishment providing
          public access to computers and the internet for a fee;

     b)    “Adult Contents and Lewd Sites” shall refer to websites containing
          prurient interests, depicting sexual acts, nudity, or lewd conduct
          that violate laws against obscenity as defined under Philippine law
          and regulations;

     c)   “Blocking Software/Technology” any industry-recognized and
          effective filter, software, or technological means capable of
          preventing access to adult content or lewd content.
SECTION 4. Mandatory Blocking of Adult Content and Lewd Sites. All
operators, owners, and managers of computer shops, internet cafes, and
establishments offering public computer access are required to install,
operate, and maintain effective blocking software or technologies on all
terminals and systems to prevent access to adult content and lewd internet
sites.

SECTION 5. Oversight and Supervision. The Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), in collaboration with the Department of
the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and the Department of Education
(DepEd), shall issue the guidelines for approved blocking software or similar
technologies on all terminals and systems to prevent access to adult content
and lewd internet sites.

SECTION 6. Penalties. Any establishment that fails to comply with this Act
shall, after due notice and hearing, be subject to the following:

      First Offense: Fine of not less than twenty thousand pesos (PHP 20,000)

      Second Offense: Suspension of Business of up to 6 months and a fine
      of not less than fifty thousand pesos (PHP 50, 000)

      Third and Subsequent Offenses: Revocation of the business permit and
      permanent closure of the establishment. Furthermore, the owner,
      operator, or person responsible shall be barred from registering,
      applying for, or owning any computer shop, internet café, or similar
      establishment offering public computer access for a period of ten (10)
      years from the date of the revocation.

SECTION 7. Duty to Report. Operators and employees of such
establishments are required to report attempts to bypass or disable blocking
mechanisms. Knowingly allowing access or assisting in the disabling of such
controls shall result in corresponding administrative and criminal sanctions.

Moreover, any citizen or member of the public who has knowledge of or
reasonable suspicion of violation of this Act by any establishment may report
such information to the DICT, DILG, and the Local Government Units (LGUs).
The authorities are mandated to provide accessible and confidential channels
for such reports and to act promptly on verified complaints.

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of
Health, within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act, shall prescribe
the necessary rules and regulations to carry out and enforce the provisions of
this Act.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the same shall not affect the validity and effectivity of the
other provisions hereof.
SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, and issuances,
or portions thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 11. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
