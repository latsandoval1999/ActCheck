                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) has quietly escalated into a massive national public health crisis
and currently ranks among the top ten leading causes of death in the Philippines, with National
Kidney and Transplant Institute (NKTI) data revealing that one Filipino develops kidney failure
every hour. Recent health figures indicate that approximately 11.2% of the population, or an
estimated 12.94 million Filipinos, live with some form of kidney damage. Crucially, data from the
Philippine Renal Disease Registry (PRDR) highlights that this crisis disproportionately impacts
the national workforce, as 57.44% of diagnosed patients are between the ages of 20 and 59,
alongside an equally critical upward trend in kidney cases among young children.

For years, the government's approach has been to step in only when the kidneys have already
failed. While expanding PhilHealth subsidies for dialysis and kidney transplants has been a
lifesaver for thousands, this approach is financially unsustainable. Dialysis and long-term
treatments push Filipino families into severe financial distress and place a heavy, long-term drain
on our public health funds.

The tragedy is that kidney disease can be stopped if caught early. The main causes of kidney
failure in the country are high blood pressure (33.07% of cases), diabetes complications
(30.04%), and chronic kidney inflammation (12.20%). If we catch these issues in their early
stages, simple lifestyle shifts and proper medical care can stop the kidneys from getting worse.
Since early kidney disease does not cause pain, millions of our citizens have no idea their health
is at risk until it is too late.

To confront this challenge, health awareness out of the hospitals shall be brought directly to
where Filipinos spend their daily lives. The behavioral risk factors driving chronic kidney disease,
including high-sodium diets, excessive sugar intake, and the misuse of nephrotoxic painkillers, are
rooted in daily life at home, work, and local markets. Consequently, health promotion campaigns
must transition out of clinical environments and meet citizens within their daily environments

Maximizing public awareness through a practical and everyday approach, the proposed measure
mandates the strategic deployment of clear health reminders directly within key public and
commercial spaces. Specifically, it integrates simple signage at supermarket shelves and checkout
lines to guide consumers toward low-sodium choices, utilizes culturally sensitive posters in public
wet markets (palengkes) to educate shoppers on hidden salts in local food preparation, and
leverages daily commutes by displaying accessible health tips inside public utility vehicles (PUVs)
and major transit terminals to reach millions of working-class Filipinos.

                           AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A COMPREHENSIVE NATIONAL PROGRAM FOR THE
PREVENTION, EARLY DETECTION, AND MANAGEMENT OF CHRONIC
KIDNEY DISEASE (CKD), MANDATING PRIMARY CARE SCREENING,
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Chronic Kidney Disease
Prevention and Early Detection Act of 2026."

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and promote the
right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them. Recognizing that
Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is a preventable, high-burden illness that compromises the
national workforce and drains public resources, the State shall adopt a comprehensive, proactive,
and preventive approach to kidney health. The State shall prioritize primary healthcare
interventions, early diagnostic screening, and public education to reduce CKD incidence and halt
its progression to end-stage renal disease.

         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act, the term:

             a. Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) refers to a condition characterized by the
                gradual loss of kidney function over a period of months or years, categorized into
                Stages 1 through 5 based on the Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR).

             b. Primary Care Provider refers to out-patient clinics, Rural Health Units (RHUs),
                Barangay Health Stations (BHS), and urban health centers that serve as the initial
                point of contact for patients in the healthcare system.

             c. High-Risk Individuals refer to persons diagnosed with diabetes, hypertension,
                cardiovascular disease, obesity, a family history of renal illness, or those aged 60
                and above.

             d. Renal Disease Control Program (REDCOP) refers to the mandatory preventive

               health program of the Department of Health (DOH) executed through the
               National Kidney and Transplant Institute (NKTI).

        SEC. 4. The National CKD Preventative Framework. – The Department of Health
(DOH), in coordination with the National Kidney and Transplant Institute (NKTI) and the
Philippine Society of Nephrology (PSN), shall formulate and implement a National CKD
Preventative Framework. This framework shall be integrated into the implementation of the
Universal Health Care (UHC) Act and shall focus on:

           a.​ Primary prevention through lifestyle modification, diet control, and smoking
               cessation campaigns;
           b.​ Mandatory early detection protocols for high-risk populations;
           c.​ Standardized clinical pathways for stalling the progression of early-stage CKD
               (Stages 1 to 3); and
           d.​ Capacity building for primary care physicians and community health workers.

       SEC. 5. Mandatory Annual Screening for High-Risk Individuals. – All primary care
networks, Rural Health Units (RHUs), and government hospitals shall provide mandatory, free
annual kidney function screenings for all high-risk individuals as defined under Section 3 of this
Act. The screening package shall include, at the minimum:

           a.​ Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (UACR) or microalbuminuria test;
           b.​ Serum Creatinine test with Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)
               calculation; and
           c.​ Routine blood pressure and blood glucose measurement.

The Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) shall fully cover these diagnostic
procedures under its primary care benefit packages (Konsulta Package).

       SEC. 6. Capacity Building of Barangay Health Workers (BHWs). – The DOH,
through REDCOP, shall conduct nationwide training modules for Barangay Health Workers
(BHWs) and community nutritionists. BHWs shall be trained to:

           a.​ Identify high-risk individuals within their respective jurisdictions;
           b.​ Educate the community on the risk factors of CKD, including high sodium
               intake, excessive sugar consumption, and self-medication with nephrotoxic
               substances (e.g., NSAIDs/painkillers); and
           c.​ Facilitate referrals to primary care physicians for individuals showing early
               indicators of renal stress.

       SEC 7. School and Workplace Kidney Health Promotion. –

           a.​ Educational Institutions

               The Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on Higher
               Education (CHED), in coordination with the DOH, shall integrate kidney health
               awareness, proper hydration habits, and nutritional guides into the physical


                                                                                                2
               education and health curricula of basic and higher education.

           b.​ Workplaces

               The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall mandate employers
               to incorporate kidney health awareness and basic metabolic screenings into
               annual employee physical examinations, recognizing the high prevalence of CKD
               among the working-age population.

           c.​ Supermarkets and Hypermarkets

               The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination with the DOH,
               shall require all supermarkets and hypermarkets to display prominent, visible
               point-of-sale educational signage detailing the dangers of high-sodium and
               high-sugar diets. Dedicated "Kidney-Healthy Choices" shelves or markers shall
               be encouraged to steer consumers toward low-sodium food alternatives.

           d.​ Public Wet Markets (Palengke)

               Local Government Units (LGUs), through their market administrators, shall
               place permanent, culturally sensitive educational posters, tarpaulins, or digital
               infographics at prominent entry points, common areas, and retail sections of
               public and private wet markets. These materials shall focus on food preparation,
               hidden salts, and kidney health.

           e.​ Public Utility Vehicles (PUVs) and Transit Terminals

               The Department of Transportation (DOTr), through the Land Transportation
               Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) and the Maritime Industry Authority
               (MARINA), shall coordinate with transport operators and cooperatives to install
               kidney health infographics, commuter advisories, or multi-media public service
               announcements inside public utility buses (PUBs), modern and traditional public
               utility jeepneys (PUJs), passenger vessels, and major mass transit terminals.

       SEC 8. Public Information and Front-of-Pack Nutrition Labeling. – To address the
primary behavioral causes of hypertension and diabetes leading to CKD, the Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) shall enforce strict, easy-to-understand front-of-pack labeling rules for
processed foods, explicitly highlighting high sodium and high sugar content. The DOH shall also
launch a sustained, multi-media national public awareness campaign every June, aligned with
National Kidney Month.

        SEC 9. Strengthening the Philippine Renal Disease Registry (PRDR). – All public and
private healthcare facilities, diagnostic laboratories, and primary care centers shall mandatorily
report new cases of CKD (from Stage 3 onwards) to the PRDR managed by the DOH. This
data shall be utilized strictly for epidemiological mapping, resource allocation, and program
improvement, maintaining absolute patient confidentiality.

        SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the Department of Health.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA). A fixed percentage of the revenues collected
from sin taxes (tobacco and alcohol) and sweetened beverage taxes shall be earmarked
specifically for the procurement of primary care kidney testing kits, community-level REDCOP
operations, and the production of multi-sectoral public informational materials.

        SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with the NKTI, PhilHealth, DTI, DOTr,
DOLE, DILG, and the Philippine Society of Nephrology (PSN), shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for its effective implementation.

       SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional,
the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force and effect.

        SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations or parts
thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
