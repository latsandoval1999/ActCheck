                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Republic Act No. 9136, otherwise known as the Electric Power Industry
Reform Act of 2001 (EPIRA), restructured the Philippine electric power industry
by promoting competition, private sector participation, and independent
regulation. Pursuant to Section 43(f) thereof, the Energy Regulatory Commission
(ERC) is authorized to determine the recoverable system loss of distribution
utilities.

       To implement this authority, the ERC adopted Resolution No. 20, Series
of 2017, establishing utility-specific Distribution System Loss (DSL) Caps based
on engineering, operational, and geographic considerations. During the
deliberations of previous legislative proposals, the Technical Working Group of
the House Committee on Energy likewise recognized that recoverable system
losses should continue to be determined through performance-based and utility-
specific standards rather than rigid statutory percentages.

      Notwithstanding these reforms, consumers continue to question the
recovery of system losses arising from electricity pilferage, illegal connections,
meter tampering, fraud, administrative inefficiency, negligence, and other
avoidable causes beyond their control.

      This measure strengthens the existing regulatory framework by clarifying
the statutory principles governing the recoverability of technical and non-
technical system losses, reaffirming the authority of the Energy Regulatory
Commission to establish utility-specific performance benchmarks, and
enhancing transparency and accountability in the recovery of system losses.

       The bill does not dismantle the regulatory framework established under
EPIRA. Rather, it builds upon existing law and regulatory policy by ensuring that
consumers are charged only for efficient technical losses determined by the ERC
while encouraging distribution utilities to continuously improve operational
efficiency.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

SECTION 43(F) OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
THE "ELECTRIC POWER INDUSTRY REFORM ACT OF 2001," AND
SECTION 10 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7832, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES”

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Electricity
Consumer Protection in System Loss Recovery Act."

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of
the State to protect electricity consumers by ensuring that only prudent,
reasonable, and efficiently incurred technical system losses, as determined by
the Energy Regulatory Commission in accordance with law, may be recovered
from consumers, while encouraging distribution utilities to continuously
improve operational efficiency and reduce avoidable losses through sound
engineering practices, prudent investments, and effective management.

       SECTION 3. Definitions - For purposes of this Act:

       a) Technical System Losses - refer to the inherent and unavoidable loss
           of electrical energy occurring during the transmission and distribution
           of electricity due to the physical characteristics of electrical equipment
           and systems.

       b) Non-Technical System Losses - refer to losses resulting from
           electricity pilferage, illegal connections, meter tampering, fraud, billing
           irregularities, administrative inefficiency, or other causes not
           attributable to the normal operation of the electrical system.

       c) System Loss Benchmark - refers to the maximum level of recoverable
           technical system losses approved by the ERC.

           SEC. 10. Recoverable System Losses. — Only prudent,

     as determined and approved by the Energy Regulatory

          The Energy Regulatory Commission shall establish and

           The Commission shall, through rules and regulations,

     cause, and recoverability, taking into consideration prevailing

     from electricity pilferage, illegal connections, meter

      SECTION 6. Transparency and Consumer Information. - Every
Distribution Utility shall annually publish and make available to the public:

      (a) its ERC-approved recoverable technical system loss benchmark;
      (b) its actual technical system losses;
      (c) its actual non-technical system losses;
      (d) the amount recovered through system loss charges; and
      (e) the measures undertaken to reduce system losses.

      The ERC shall prescribe the form, frequency, and manner of disclosure,
including publication through the distribution utility's official website and such
other accessible platforms as the Commission may require.

      SECTION 6-A. Refund of Unauthorized Recoveries. - Amounts collected
in excess of the recoverable system loss benchmark or otherwise determined by
the ERC to be unauthorized shall be refunded to affected consumers in
accordance with rules prescribed by the Commission, without prejudice to the
imposition of administrative sanctions.

      SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the ERC, after public consultation and in
coordination with the Department of Energy, the National Electrification
Administration, distribution utilities, electric cooperatives and consumer
representatives, shall promulgate the Implementing Rules and Regulations
necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act. The Commission shall likewise
review and, where necessary, amend, its existing rules, including ERC
Resolution No. 20, Series of 2017, to conform with this Act.

      SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 9. Saving Clause. - Except as expressly modified by this Act, all
powers, duties, and functions of the ERC under Republic Act No. 9136 and other
applicable laws shall remain in full force and effect.
     SECTION 10. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 11. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
