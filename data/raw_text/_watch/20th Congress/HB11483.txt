                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Schools must always remain places of learning, formation, and safety. Recent
successive incidents and threats involving bullying and cyberbullying, bomb scares,
deadly weapons, online threats, and other acts of violence have haunted our places
of learning and peace. They have also exposed the evolving risks faced by learners,
teachers, and school personnel.

       In response, the Department of Education has strengthened its policies on
weapons and bomb threats and introduced school safety audits, active-threat
preparedness exercises, confidential reporting mechanisms, and mental-health
interventions. Existing laws address particular aspects of school safety, including
bullying, mental health, and disaster risk reduction. Nevertheless, there remains a
need for a unified and permanent framework covering the prevention, preparedness,
response, and recovery measures necessary to address both natural hazards and
intentional acts of violence across educational institutions.

      This bill therefore establishes a National Comprehensive School Safety
Program. It mandates school-specific safety and vulnerability assessments,
emergency plans and drills, reasonable security infrastructure, weapons-control
measures, violence-prevention and mental-health programs, confidential reporting
and threat assessment mechanisms, inter-agency coordination, and post-incident
recovery and continuity-of-learning measures.

 1        SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “National
 2   Comprehensive School Safety Act”.
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State
 5   to protect and promote the right of every learner, teacher, and school personnel to
 6   study and work in a safe, secure, and resilient educational environment.
 7
 8           The State shall protect children and youth from violence, criminal acts,
 9   terrorism, armed attacks, disasters, emergencies, and other threats that may disrupt
10   education or endanger life, health, and safety. Towards this end, the State shall
11   institutionalize a comprehensive framework for the prevention of, preparedness for,
12   response to, and recovery from school safety and security incidents and emergencies.
13
14          SEC. 3. National Comprehensive School Safety Program. The National
15   School Security Program is hereby established to be jointly implemented by the
16   Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on Higher Education (CHED),
17   in coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
18   Department of Health (DOH), Philippine National Police (PNP), Bureau of Fire
19   Protection (BFP), National Intelligence Coordinating Agency (NICA), National Bureau
20   of Investigation (NBI), local government units (LGUs), and such other government
21   agencies and instrumentalities as may be necessary for its effective implementation.
22
23          SEC. 4. School Security Risk Assessments. Every educational institution
24   shall periodically conduct a site-specific and evidence-based assessment of existing

 1   and emerging threats, hazards, and vulnerabilities within and in the immediate vicinity
 2   of its premises. The assessment shall include, but shall not be limited to, the following:

 4          SEC. 7. Emergency Drills. All schools shall conduct at least two (2) active-
 5   threat, active assailant or armed-intrusion preparedness exercises per school year, in
 6   addition to fire, earthquake, and other emergency drills required under existing laws
 7   and regulations. Additional exercises may be conducted when warranted by the school
 8   safety and security assessment. Such exercises shall be age-appropriate, trauma-
 9   informed, accessible to learners with disabilities, and designed to prevent unnecessary
10   fear or psychological harm.
11
12          Learners, school personnel, and parents or guardians shall be given appropriate
13   prior notice. The use of actual or imitation firearms, simulated gunfire, masked
14   assailants, fake blood or injuries, and other realistic depictions of violence during
15   learner-participated exercises shall be prohibited. Every exercise shall be followed by
16   a debriefing and evaluation to identify operational deficiencies and provide
17   psychosocial support when necessary.
18
19         SEC. 8. School Security Infrastructure. Subject to the availability of funds
20   and the results of the school safety and security assessment, schools shall
21   progressively establish:
22
23         a) Controlled entry points;
24         b) Emergency alarm systems;
25         c) Security cameras in appropriate common areas, subject to Republic Act No.
26            10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and applicable
27            rules of the National Privacy Commission;
28         d) Emergency communications equipment;
29         e) Adequate exterior lighting;
30         f) Accessible evacuation routes, emergency exits, and designated assembly
31            and reunification areas; and
32         g) Other reasonable security measures.
33
34          SEC. 9. Firearms and Weapons Prohibition. No firearms, explosive, deadly
35   weapon, or similar instrument shall be brought into school premises except by duly
36   authorized law enforcement personnel acting within the scope of official duties.
37
38            SEC. 10. Mental Health and Violence Prevention Programs. Schools
39   shall implement programs promoting mental health, conflict resolution, anti-bullying
40   initiatives, violence prevention, and early intervention for at-risk individuals.
41
42          SEC. 11. Local School Safety Coordination Committee. A School Safety
43   Coordination Committee shall be constituted under the appropriate Local Disaster Risk
44   Reduction and Management Council, in coordination with the Local Council for the
45   Protection of Children. The Committee shall include representatives from the DepEd,
46   CHED, TESDA, DOH, DSWD, LGU, PNP, BFP, public and private schools, parent
47   associations, youth organizations, and such other agencies or sectors as may be
48   necessary. It shall coordinate local threat assessments, emergency response plans,

 4          SEC. 12. School Threat Assessment, Reporting, Incident Response,
 5   and Information System. Every school shall establish a multidisciplinary School
 6   Safety and Threat Assessment Team or designate an existing school body capable of
 7   performing its functions. The Team shall be composed, as appropriate, of the school
 8   head, school safety or disaster risk reduction focal person, guidance counselor or
 9   mental-health professional, child protection representative, school health personnel,
10   and other qualified personnel.
11   The Team shall:
12          a) Receive, assess, and act upon reports of violence, self-harm, bullying,
13             hazing, abuse, harassment, prohibited weapons, bomb threats, suspicious
14             packages, online threats, and other serious safety concerns;
15          b) Determine whether a reported threat is credible, imminent, or requires
16             safety, referral, psychosocial, disciplinary, or law-enforcement intervention;
17          c) Coordinate, when necessary, with parents or guardians, social welfare
18             officers, mental-health professionals, law-enforcement authorities,
19             emergency responders, and other concerned agencies; and
20          d) Maintain confidential records in accordance with applicable child-protection
21             and data-privacy laws.
22
23           Every school shall establish accessible, confidential, and anonymous reporting
24   mechanisms, including hotlines, websites, mobile applications, or other appropriate
25   means, through which learners, teachers and other school personnel, parents or
26   guardians, and members of the community may report violence, threats, self-harm,
27   bullying, hazing, abuse, harassment, prohibited weapons, bomb threats, suspicious
28   packages, online threats, and other serious safety concerns. Reports involving an
29   imminent threat shall be immediately referred to the appropriate law-enforcement or
30   emergency authority. Persons who make reports in good faith shall be protected from
31   retaliation, intimidation, discrimination, or disciplinary action. False or malicious
32   reports shall be dealt with in accordance with applicable laws and school regulations,
33   subject to due process and child-sensitive intervention.
34
35           Threat assessment shall be based on observable conduct, credible information,
36   and relevant circumstances and shall not, by itself, constitute criminal profiling or
37   disciplinary punishment.
