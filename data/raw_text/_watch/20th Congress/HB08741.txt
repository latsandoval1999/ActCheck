                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE


        The Service Incentive Leave (SIL) is one of the basic benefits granted to employees
under the Labor Code. At present, however, workers are entitled to only five (5) days of service
incentive leave annually. This has long been regarded as insufficient, especially given the
increasing demands of modern work, the need for work-life balance, and the growing
recognition of the importance of rest, health, and family time in sustaining productivity.

        Under existing labor standards, the Philippines provides only the minimum five (5)
days SIL after one year of service, which may be converted to cash if unused. Compared to
international benchmarks, this falls short. Studies and labor reports indicate that many countries
provide at least twelve (12) to fourteen (14) days of paid leave annually. Within the ASEAN
region, the average paid leave benefit already exceeds the current Philippine standard,
highlighting the need to update existing policies in line with global labor practices.

        Moreover, research from labor and health institutions, including reports associated with
the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), the International Labour Organization
(ILO), and the World Health Organization (WHO), show that adequate rest periods
significantly improve worker productivity and well-being. Employees who are able to take
sufficient leave experience up to a 20–30% increase in productivity, while studies also reveal
that around 60% of workers experience work-related stress or burnout due to insufficient rest
and prolonged working hours.

       This measure seeks to increase the service incentive leave from five (5) days to ten (10)
days with pay. At the same time, it introduces a fair balance by allowing employees to convert
only up to five (5) unused days into its monetary equivalent. In this way, the law encourages
employees to actually use their leave days for rest and personal well-being, while also ensuring
that employers are not unduly burdened.

        The proposed amendment strengthens the protection of labor as mandated by the
Constitution. It recognizes the dignity of work, values the welfare of employees, and fosters a
healthier and more productive workforce. It is likewise consistent with global labor standards
that acknowledge adequate rest periods as essential to sustainable development and inclusive
economic growth.

      AN ACT INCREASING THE SERVICE INCENTIVE LEAVE OF EMPLOYEES,
     AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE ARTICLE 95 OF PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO.
       442, AS AMENDED, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE LABOR CODE OF THE
                               PHILIPPINES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

1           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Service Incentive Leave Act
2    of 2026.”

3           SEC. 2. Amendment to Article 95 of the Labor Code. – Article 95 of Presidential Decree
4    No. 442, as amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:

5           “Article 95. Right to service incentive leave –

 6           a) Every employee who has rendered at least one year of service shall be entitled to a
 7              yearly service incentive leave of [five] TEN days with pay, PROVIDED THAT,
 8              THE EMPLOYEE SHALL BE ENTITLED TO CONVERT ONLY FIVE DAYS
 9              OF LEAVE TO ITS MONEY EQUIVALENT IF NOT USED OR EXHAUSTED
10              AT THE END OF THE YEAR.
11           b) This provision shall not apply to those who are already enjoying the benefit herein
12              provided, those enjoying vacation leave with pay of at least [five] TEN days and those
13              employed in establishments regularly employing less than ten employees or in
14              establishments exempted from granting this benefit by the Secretary of Labor and
15              Employment after considering the viability of financial condition of such
16              establishment.
17           c) The grant of benefit in excess of that provided herein shall not be made a subject of
18              arbitration or any court or administrative action.”

19          SEC. 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
20   approval of this Act, the Department of Labor and Employment, in consultation with relevant
21   stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.
