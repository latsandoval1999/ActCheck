20                                              EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   While the Philippines has a high basic literacy rate, functional literacy remains an area of worry.
23   Functional literacy refers to the ability to read, write, compute, and understand and apply
24   information in everyday life. The 2024 FLEMMS reported that 93.1% of Filipinos aged 10 to 64
25   had basic literacy, but only 70.8% had functional literacy. In other words, many Filipinos who
26   can read, write, and compute still have difficulty understanding and applying information.
27
28   This concern is even more relevant today, when people are constantly exposed to fake news,
29   misleading information, and other unreliable content. In today’s world, literacy must therefore
30   go beyond the ability to read and write.
31
32   Hence, the proposed measure therefore seeks to strengthen the Literacy Coordinating Council
33   as the country’s lead coordinating and advisory body on literacy. The Council will be given more
34   specific functions to identify literacy needs, support marginalized and vulnerable groups,
35   promote research and evidence-based policies, engage the private sector and other
36   stakeholders, and strengthen coordination with the Bureau of Alternative Education (BAE) and
37   the LGUs. It also establishes Local Literacy Councils to bring literacy efforts closer to
38   communities and provides support for effective literacy programs and outstanding literacy
39   workers and ALS mobile teachers.
40
41   Through these provisions and updates, the measure aims to ensure that literacy programs do
42   not merely teach people to read and write, but help them understand, evaluate, and use
43   information effectively in everyday life.
44
45   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

20                              AN ACT
21     RENAMING THE LITERACY COORDINATING COUNCIL TO NATIONAL
22    LITERACY COUNCIL, STRENGTHENING ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS,
23   EXPANDING ITS MEMBERSHIP STRUCTURE AND SUPPORT MECHANISMS,
24   AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, REPEALING FOR THE PURPOSE
25                 REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7165, AS AMENDED
26
27   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
28   Congress assembled:
29
30         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Literacy
31   Council Act”.
32
33           SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – Pursuant to Article II, Section 17 of the
34   Constitution, it is the declared policy of the State to give priority to education to foster
35   patriotism and nationalism, accelerate social progress, and promote total human
36   liberation and development.
37
38           Article XIV, Section 2(4) of the Constitution likewise mandates the State to
39   encourage non-formal, informal, and indigenous learning systems, as well as self-
40   learning, independent, and out-of-school study programs particularly those that respond
41   to community needs. To this end, the State shall give priority to the adoption of measures
42   for the universalization of literacy.
43
44           SECTION 3. Renaming of the Literacy Coordinating Council. – The
45   nomenclature of the Literacy Coordinating Council, established pursuant to Republic
46   Act No. 7165, as amended, entitled “An Act Creating the Literacy Coordinating
47   Council, Defining Its Powers and Functions, Appropriating Funds Therefor, and for
48   Other Purposes”, is hereby changed to National Literacy Council, hereinafter referred
49   to in this Act as the “Council”.
50
51          The Council shall serve as the lead inter-agency coordinating and advisory body
52   to the various agencies of the national government, the local government units
53   (LGUs), and the private sector, on matters pertaining to the formulation of policies
 1   and the adoption and implementation of measures for the universalization of literacy.
 2   The Council shall be attached solely for budgetary coordination and related purposes
 3   to the Department of Education (DepEd).
 4
 5       SECTION 4. Composition. – The Council shall be composed of the following
 6   members:
 7
 8         a)   Secretary of the DepEd, as Chairperson, who may designate an
 9         undersecretary as permanent representative;
10
11         b) Secretary of the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
12         as co-Chairperson, who may designate an undersecretary as permanent
13         representative;
14
15         c) Secretary of the National Economic and Development Authority, or a
16         duly designated permanent representative;
17
18         d) Director General of the Technical Education and Skills Development
19         Authority, or a duly designated permanent representative;
20
21         e) Chairperson of the Commission on Higher Education, or a duly
22         designated permanent representative;
23
24         f) Director General of the Philippine Information Agency, or a duly designated
25         permanent representative;
26
27         g) President of the Philippine Normal University, or a duly designated
28         permanent representative;
29
30         h) National Statistician and Civil Registrar General of the Philippine
31         Statistics Authority (PSA), or a duly designated permanent representative;
32
33         i) Director of the Bureau of Alternative Education (BAE) of the DepEd;
34
35         j) a representative from the non-government organizations (NGOs), who is a
36         citizen of the Philippines with a demonstrated personal or professional
37         commitment and advocacy in the propagation of literacy; and
38
39         k) a representative from the associations of private educational institutions, who
40         is a citizen of the Philippines with a demonstrated personal or professional
41         commitment and advocacy in the propagation of literacy. The Council shall
42         approve and confirm the appointment of the representative from the NGOs and
43         the representative from the associations of private educational institutions
44         through the adoption of a resolution. Such representatives shall serve for a term
45         of three (3) years, and may be reappointed only once for another three (3) years
46         on the basis of merit and performance.
47
48         SECTION 5. Meetings. – The Council shall conduct its regular meetings
49   quarterly at any agreed location within the Philippines and may hold special meetings to
50   consider urgent matters upon the call of the chairperson or a majority of its members.
51   A majority of all its incumbent members shall constitute a quorum, the presence of which
 1   is necessary to transact business. A meeting may be conducted virtually only in
 2   circumstances when meeting in person is impossible or impractical and all the Council
 3   members are able to communicate adequately during the meeting.
 4
 5           Members of the Council shall serve without compensation, but shall be entitled
 6   to appropriate honoraria for attendance in meetings as well as appropriate
 7   allowances and reimbursement of expenses related to their participation in the official
 8   activities of the Council, in accordance with pertinent laws, rules and regulations.
 9
10          The duly designated representatives to the Council shall be afforded equal
11   opportunity to participate in the meetings of the Council, and shall be allowed to vote on
12   any matter in the agenda of the Council. Likewise, they shall be afforded, whenever
13   necessary, reimbursement of any actual expense incurred during Council-related
14   functions and duties, subject to the provisions of existing laws, rules and regulations.
15
16           SECTION 6. Powers and Functions. – The Council shall be vested with the
17   following powers and functions:
18
19          a) Act as the lead advisory and coordinating body of the government and
20          initiate, promote and recommend the adoption of policy and program directions
21          for all literacy endeavors in the country;
22
23          b) Evaluate the literacy situation in the country, propose ways and means
24          for expanding educational opportunities to citizens of varied socio-economic
25          origins, and adopt modern communications and educational technology and
26          other innovative modes of transmitting knowledge to support literacy endeavors
27          at the national and local levels;
28
29          c) Support and assist the BAE on matters concerning community mapping
30          activities for the maintenance of a centralized database for out-of-school children
31          and adults, including persons with disabilities, indigenous peoples, and other
32          marginalized and vulnerable members of society, who may not have availed of
33          or completed their basic education and who may need intervention and support;
34
35          d) Strengthen support structures identified as literacy advocates of LGUs in the
36          city, municipal, and barangay levels, by adopting strategies that maximize private
37          sector support for, cooperation, and involvement in literacy development
38          programs, projects, and related activities;
39
40          e) Promote and support initiatives for the conduct of research studies and
41          similar undertakings aimed at enhancing and strengthening the crafting of
42          relevant and timely policy recommendations, and for the effective
43          implementation of literacy-related programs, projects, and other activities;
44
45          f) Organize periodic assemblies, such as literacy conferences and giving of
46          awards to increase awareness on and help strengthen good literacy practices,
47          and engage close partnerships with literacy advocates throughout the country;
48
49          g) Establish the organizational structure of the Council secretariat and
50          determine the secretariat’s staffing pattern, qualifications, duties and
51          responsibilities, and other administrative policies including, among others,
 1         those pertaining to hiring and promotion, subject to the guidelines set forth by
 2         the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and the Civil Service
 3         Commission;
 4
 5         h) Prepare an annual budget proposal and ensure that the allotted annual
 6         appropriation shall be spent in accordance with the purposes as specified
 7         in the General Appropriations Act;
 8
 9         i) Formulate necessary guidelines for business continuity, disaster recovery
10         and backup capabilities and facilities to ensure continued operation and
11         delivery of services in times of public health emergency, occurrence of calamity,
12         and any other threat that requires public safety and protection; and 2
13
14         j) Perform such other functions as may be necessary in furtherance of the
15         purposes of this Act.
16
17          SECTION 7. The Secretariat. – The secretariat shall be headed by an
18   Executive Director, who shall assist the Council in the performance of its functions. The
19   salaries of the Executive Director and other officers and employees of the Council
20   secretariat shall be in accordance with the Revised Compensation and Position
21   Classification System and other existing laws.
22
23          The existing organizational structure and staffing pattern of the secretariat
24   shall be revised so as to integrate appropriately the provisions of this Act subject to
25   the review and approval by the DBM: Provided, That no officer or employee of the
26   existing secretariat shall be removed from the service except for a valid cause, in
27   accordance with Republic Act No. 6656, entitled “An Act to Protect the Security of
28   Tenure of Civil Service Officers and Employees in the Implementation of Government
29   Reorganization”.
30
31         SECTION 8. Duties and Responsibilities of the Secretariat. – In the
32   performance of its duties and responsibilities, the Council secretariat shall:
33
34         a) Provide administrative and technical support and assistance to the
35         Council, including the preparation of policy recommendations, plans and
36         programs, and other Council priorities and measures necessary for the
37         advancement of basic and functional literacy, and other basic capabilities of out-
38         of-school children and adults, including persons with disabilities, indigenous
39         peoples, and other marginalized and vulnerable members of society, who may
40         not have availed of or completed their basic education and who may need
41         intervention and support;
42
43         b) Consult and coordinate with national government agencies, LGUs,
44         NGOs, academic institutions, and other stakeholders on matters relating to the
45         functions of the Council and on other matters pertaining to the propagation
46         of literacy endeavors;
47
48         c) Provide technical assistance to the implementers of the programs and
49         projects on literacy;
50
51         d) Initiate, organize, and promote the conduct of research activities,
 1          workshops, conferences, national literacy awards and the like, subject to the
 2          approval of the Council;
 3
 4          e) Set up, maintain, and update a management information system on
 5          literacy programs and projects, policies, best practices, research studies,
 6          statistical data and other relevant information relative to the literacy situation in
 7          the country;
 8
 9          f) Coordinate with the PSA in the conduct of the Functional Literacy, Education
10          and Mass Media Survey (FLEMMS) for purposes of providing a
11          quantitative framework that shall serve as one of the bases in the formulation
12          of policies and programs on the improvement of literacy and education status in
13          the country;
14
15          g) Recommend programs, projects, and other activities to be included for funding
16          in the annual budget proposal of the Council, including support services in
17          the preparation of the Council’s annual budget proposal; and
18
19          h) Perform other tasks as may be directed by the Council.
20
21          SECTION 9. Collaboration and Linkages with the BAE. – The Council
22   shall closely coordinate with the BAE concerning the management of Alternative
23   Learning System (ALS) programs, projects and other activities, especially those
24   that are geared towards the universalization of literacy, particularly among out-of-
25   school children and adults, including persons with disabilities, indigenous peoples, and
26   other marginalized and vulnerable members of society. It shall also collaborate with the
27   BAE on matters pertaining to literacy concerns for policy formulation, knowledge
28   management, advocacy, social mobilization, and partnerships, among others.
29
30          SECTION 10. Establishment of Local Literacy Councils. – The Council shall
31   promote the establishment of Local Literacy Councils (LLCs) as support structures at
32   the city, municipal, and barangay levels, which shall serve as the coordinating and
33   advisory body at the local level on matters pertaining to the adoption and
34   implementation of community-based measures for the propagation of literacy.
35
36          The LLCs shall work with the LGUs in the implementation of literacy programs
37   and endeavors at the local level, including the identification of priority literacy programs,
38   provision of technical assistance to literacy volunteer workers, program monitoring and
39   evaluation, and coordination with government and non-government partners for
40   program support activities. The LLCs shall closely coordinate, support and assist
41   the BAE to ensure the implementation of community literacy mapping activities within
42   the local level. The Council shall, upon consultation with the DILG and DepEd, issue
43   the general guidelines for the establishment, implementation, monitoring and
44   evaluation of LLCs. An LGU may, motu proprio or upon the recommendation of the
45   Council, establish its LLC within its jurisdiction in accordance with the guidelines set
46   forth by the Council.
47
48         SECTION 11. National Literacy Awards. – The Council shall organize the
49   National Literacy Awards for the purpose of recognizing, commending, and awarding
50   deserving LGUs and NGOs for implementing outstanding literacy programs in their
51   respective jurisdictions within the country. The National Literacy Awards shall be held
 1   once every two (2) years and shall encourage the participation of all literacy
 2   advocates and stakeholders nationwide. The event shall serve as the venue to exhibit,
 3   discover, share, and help strengthen good literacy practices and thus, engage
 4   partnerships with various literacy advocates and stakeholders.
 5
 6          The Council shall formulate the guidelines on the conduct of the National
 7   Literacy Awards, including the criteria for the creation of a board of judges per
 8   category and the selection of winners. The board of judges for each category shall be
 9   chosen from a panel of experts in the field of literacy and education.
10
11           SECTION 12. Subsidy for Qualified Outstanding Literacy Programs;
12   Outstanding Individual Awards. – The Council shall award appropriate financial
13   subsidies to the top awardees of the Search for Outstanding Literacy Programs for
14   both LGU and NGO categories to fund their chosen literacy programs in their respective
15   localities:   Provided, That the NGO awardees are non-stock, non-profit, and
16   established for educational purposes. The Council shall formulate and issue
17   guidelines on the release of this financial subsidy including the amount of the award,
18   period and frequency of release of such amounts to the awardees, and conditions or
19   qualifications for entitlement thereto. The amounts of the financial subsidy to qualified
20   awardees may be subject to periodic adjustments, as deemed necessary by the Council.
21
22          The Council shall also designate awardees for the Outstanding Individual
23   Literacy Workers and Outstanding ALS Mobile Teachers whose qualifications for
24   entitlement to a financial award and the amount thereof shall be included in the
25   guidelines.
26
27          SECTION 13. Assistance from Special Education Fund. – Notwithstanding the
28   provisions of Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the “Local Government
29   Code of 1991”, as amended, the LGUs may be authorized to use a portion of the
30   Special Education Fund to augment the funds appropriated by the LGUs for the purpose
31   of implementing literacy-related programs and activities at the local level.
32
33            SECTION 14. Annual Report. – The Council shall submit to the Senate
34   Committee on Basic Education, Arts and Culture and the House of Representatives’
35   Committee on Basic Education and Culture a detailed annual report which shall include
36   its accomplishments, programs and projects, and policy recommendations and, if
37   necessary, propose legislation for the universalization and continuing improvement
38   of literacy rate and numeracy skills of learners.
39
40          SECTION 15. Appropriations. – The initial amount for the implementation of this
41   Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the DepEd. Thereafter,
42   the funding of which shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
43
44          SECTION 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred
45   twenty (120) days after the effectivity of this Act, the Chairperson of the Council shall, in
46   consultation with the Council members and other concerned stakeholders, promulgate
47   the rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.
48
49           SECTION 17. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held
50   invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not so declared shall remain in force and
51   effect.

 8
 9          All other laws, decrees, orders, and rules and regulations contrary to or

11
12          SECTION 19. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
13   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
