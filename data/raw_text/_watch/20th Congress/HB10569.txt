     CONTRACT OF SERVICE WORKERS OF THE GOVERNMENT WHO HAVE

                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippine Government remains one of the largest employers in the
country. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), total employment
in the public administration and defense sector alone reached approximately 2.1
million workers as of 2023, reflecting the continued expansion of the public
workforce. Meanwhile, data from the Department of Budget and Management
(DBM) indicate that funded positions in the national government have steadily
increased, exceeding 1.9 million authorized positions in recent years.1

      Despite this, a significant portion of the government workforce remains
under non-regular employment arrangements. The Civil Service Commission
(CSC) has consistently reported that hundreds of thousands of workers in
government are engaged under Job Order (JO) and Contract of Service (COS)
arrangements. Based on DBM and CSC estimates, JO and COS workers in
government agencies have ranged from approximately 600,000 to 700,000 in

       This persistent reliance on contractual and short-term workers has led to
the characterization of the government as one of the largest “endo employers,”
mirroring the very labor practices it seeks to regulate in the private sector. While
efforts to address contractualization have been initiated in previous
administrations—including policy pronouncements and executive issuances
promoting security of tenure—the absence of a comprehensive legislative
solution continues to leave many government workers in precarious employment
conditions.

      The 1987 Constitution explicitly guarantees full protection to labor,
promotes full employment, and ensures equal work opportunities regardless of
status. It mandates the State to uphold the rights of workers to security of
tenure, humane conditions of work, and a living wage. These constitutional
guarantees must extend equally to all workers in government, regardless of
employment classification.

       Under the current framework, JO and COS workers are engaged for short-
term periods, typically not exceeding six months per contract, and are excluded
from the coverage of the Civil Service Law, rules, and regulations. They are not
entitled to standard benefits such as leave credits, retirement benefits, and other
social protections enjoyed by regular government employees. Moreover, their
service is not considered government service for purposes of tenure and career
progression, and no employer-employee relationship is recognized.

      This bill seeks to address these longstanding inequities by promoting the
regularization of qualified Job Order and Contract of Service workers in
government. It aims to uphold the constitutional mandate of security of tenure,
ensure fair and humane working conditions, and set an example for the private
sector by institutionalizing just labor practices within the public sector.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Security of Tenure
Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State shall harness its human
resources to cope with the rapid economic development and population growth.
Government workers, being very important components of the State's human
resources, shall be given equal opportunity to quality education, justice, and
security of tenure.

SECTION 3. Coverage. - Subject to the provisions of the Constitution and
applicable civil service laws, rules and regulations, it is hereby mandated that
the all incumbent job order and contract of service government employees who
have rendered at least five (5) years of continuous service in the case of national
government agencies or a total of ten (10) years of continuous service in the case
of local government units as of the date of the approval of this Act shall be
entitled to security of tenure. Provided, That the said requirement of continuous
service may be waived if the service of the official/employee concerned prior to
the completion of the said years, was interrupted by not more than three (3)
years, taken cumulatively, due to either the abolition of his position,
reorganization of the office wherein he was phased out, or reduction in force:
Provided further, That in the case of officials/employees who have been
previously employed in the same government agency in any capacity, the said
requirement of continuous service may likewise be waived if the interruption of
their service is not more than eighteen (18) months, taken cumulatively, so long
as the minimum required period is met.

SECTION 4. Status of Covered Employees and Affected Positions. - All of the
positions affected by this Act currently held by the covered employees shall be
deemed necessary and/or desirable for the efficient operation of the government
and shall be marked as co-terminus with the incumbent. All covered employees
may not be operated or terminated from the said positions except for just or
lawful cause and with due process of law, nor can their positions be abolished
except when the same are vacated by their incumbents.

SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The Civil Service
Commission, in consultation with the Department of Budget and Management,
shall issue the rules and regulations necessary to implement the provisions of
this Act. Said Implementing Rules and Regulations shall be promulgated within
ninety (90) days after the approval of this Act.

SECTION 6. Penal Provisions. - Any government employee who shall apply for
entitlement to security of tenure under the provisions of this Act on the basis of
false claims and/or documents as well as any government officer or employee
who Will make or issue false certifications, attestations, endorsements and/or
spurious documents in relation to any such application shall suffer the penalty
of imprisonment of one (1) to three (3) years or a fine in amount up to one
hundred thousand pesos (Php 100,000.00) or both, at the discretion of the judge,
in addition to perpetual disqualification from public office. Said employees may
also be administratively charged under civil service laws, rules and regulations.

SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations or parts thereof contrary to, or inconsistent with, this Act are hereby
modified or repealed accordingly.

SECTION 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions of this Act not otherwise affected
shall remain valid and subsisting.

SECTION 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following
its complete publication in any national newspaper of general circulation or in
the Official Gazette.
