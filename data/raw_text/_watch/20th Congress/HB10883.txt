                        AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL MATERNAL MENTAL HEALTH
PROGRAM, PROVIDING ACCESS TO MENTAL HEALTH SERVICES
FOR PREGNANT AND POSTPARTUM WOMEN, INTEGRATING
MATERNAL MENTAL HEALTH INTO PUBLIC HEALTH CARE
SYSTEMS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Pregnancy and the postpartum period are significant stages in a woman’s life
that involve profound physical, emotional, psychological, and social changes. While
motherhood is often associated with joy and fulfillment, many women experience
mental health challenges during this period, including depression, anxiety disorders,
trauma-related conditions, and other mental health concerns. If left undetected and
untreated, these conditions may adversely affect the health, safety, and quality of life
of mothers, their children, and their families.

       Maternal mental health conditions remain an underrecognized public health
concern due to various barriers, including stigma, lack of awareness, limited access
to mental health professionals, inadequate screening and referral systems, and
financial constraints. Many women experiencing symptoms do not seek care because
they fear judgment, discrimination, or misunderstanding of their condition. As a
result, opportunities for early detection, intervention, and recovery are often missed.
                                                                                           1
        Thus, this bill seeks to establish a National Maternal Mental Health Program
 that integrates maternal mental health services into existing maternal, newborn,
 child, and primary healthcare systems. It provides for routine and evidence-based
 screening during pregnancy and the postpartum period, standardized referral
 pathways, community-based support services, emergency intervention mechanisms,
 and continuity of care for women requiring further assessment and treatment.

        Further, the measure strengthens the capacity of healthcare providers and
 frontline workers through competency-based training and continuing education on
 maternal mental health detection, intervention, referral, and stigma-sensitive care. It
 promotes workplace and educational support systems, community awareness
 programs, and the responsible use of tele-mental health services to expand access,
 particularly in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

        By recognizing maternal mental health as an essential component of women’s
 health, family well-being, and public health, this bill seeks to build a healthcare
 system that supports mothers not only in surviving pregnancy and childbirth but also
 in achieving mental, emotional, and social well-being throughout the postpartum
 period.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                        AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL MATERNAL MENTAL HEALTH

FOR PREGNANT AND POSTPARTUM WOMEN, INTEGRATING

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Maternal Mental Health
and Postpartum Care Act.”


SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to protect and promote the physical, mental, and emotional well-being of women
during pregnancy and the postpartum period by ensuring equitable access to


                                                                               1
affordable, accessible, gender-responsive, evidence-based, and stigma-free maternal
mental health services. Consistent with the constitutional duty of the State to protect
and promote the right to health, the State shall integrate maternal mental health care
into the national public health system, promote early detection, timely intervention,
treatment, and recovery for maternal mental health conditions, strengthen
community, family, and institutional support systems for mothers and their families,
and reduce the stigma associated with maternal mental health through education,
awareness, and coordinated public health programs.


SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:


(a) "Maternal mental health condition" refers to any mental, emotional, behavioral,
or psychological condition that affects a woman during pregnancy or within two (2)
years after childbirth, including but not limited to postpartum depression, anxiety
disorders, postpartum psychosis, bipolar disorders, trauma-related disorders,
substance use disorders, and other clinically recognized perinatal mental health
conditions.


(b) "Postpartum period" refers to the period beginning immediately after childbirth
and extending up to two (2) years after delivery for purposes of this Act.


(c) "Maternal mental health services" refer to preventive, promotive, diagnostic,
therapeutic, rehabilitative, and recovery-oriented services designed to support
maternal mental well-being, including mental health screening, psychological
assessment, counseling, psychiatric consultation, psychosocial support, crisis




                                                                                     2
intervention, referral, treatment, follow-up care, and other appropriate mental health
interventions.


(d) "Mental health screening" refers to the use of standardized, evidence-based, and
culturally appropriate screening tools to identify individuals who may be
experiencing maternal mental health conditions and who may require further
assessment or intervention. Screening shall not, by itself, constitute a clinical
diagnosis.


(e) "Evidence-based screening tools" refer to scientifically validated and culturally
appropriate instruments approved or recognized by the Department of Health (DOH)
for the screening of maternal mental health conditions.


(f) "Psychosocial support" refers to services and interventions that promote the
psychological, emotional, and social well-being of mothers through counseling, peer
support, family engagement, community-based services, and other appropriate
support mechanisms.


(g) "Tele-mental health services" refer to the delivery of maternal mental health care
through information and communication technologies, including telemedicine,
teleconsultation, and other remote mental health services in accordance with
applicable laws, rules, and regulations.


SECTION 4. National Maternal Mental Health Program. - The Department of
Health (DOH), in coordination with the Philippine Commission on Women (PCW),
the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), the Philippine Health


                                                                                    3
Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), local government units (LGUs), public and
private health care providers, and other concerned government agencies and
stakeholders, shall establish, implement, and regularly evaluate a National Maternal
Mental Health Program to ensure the delivery of accessible, equitable, evidence-
based, and integrated maternal mental health services throughout pregnancy and the
postpartum period.


The Program shall be integrated into existing maternal, newborn, child, and primary
health care services and shall include, but not be limited to, the following:


(a) Universal maternal mental health screening during prenatal, childbirth, and
postpartum care using evidence-based screening tools approved by the DOH;


(b) Standardized clinical guidelines, screening protocols, and referral pathways for
the prevention, identification, assessment, treatment, and management of maternal
mental health conditions;


(c) Community-based counseling, psychosocial support, peer support services, and
family-centered interventions;


(d) Timely referral and access to appropriate mental health professionals, specialized
care, and emergency psychiatric services for severe or high-risk cases;


(e) Public education and awareness campaigns to promote maternal mental health
literacy, reduce stigma, and encourage early help-seeking among mothers, families,
and communities;


                                                                                    4
(f) Capacity-building and continuing education programs for health care workers
and other service providers involved in maternal and child health;


(g) The establishment of mechanisms to ensure continuity of care, follow-up
services, and coordination among health care facilities, local government units, and
relevant service providers; and


(h) Monitoring, evaluation, and quality assurance mechanisms to assess program
implementation, service delivery, and health outcomes, and to support evidence-
based policy development and continuous program improvement.


SECTION 5. Mandatory Screening and Assessment. - All public hospitals, rural
health units, barangay health stations, government-accredited maternal care
facilities, and other health care providers offering prenatal, childbirth, and
postpartum services shall conduct maternal mental health screening for pregnant and
postpartum women using standardized, evidence-based, and culturally appropriate
screening tools approved by the Department of Health (DOH).


Screening shall, at a minimum, be conducted during the following periods:


(a) The initial prenatal consultation;


(b) At least one (1) prenatal consultation during the third trimester of pregnancy;




                                                                                      5
(c) Prior to discharge following childbirth or before transfer from the health care
facility, when clinically appropriate;


(d) The routine postpartum follow-up visit, including the six (6)-week postpartum
checkup or its equivalent; and


(e) Such subsequent postpartum or pediatric well-child visits, within two (2) years
after childbirth, as may be determined appropriate under the implementing rules and
regulations, taking into consideration the mother's clinical condition and risk factors.


Mental health screening conducted pursuant to this Act shall be confidential,
voluntary, culturally sensitive, non-discriminatory, and performed in accordance
with applicable laws on data privacy and patient confidentiality. A positive screening
result shall not, by itself, constitute a clinical diagnosis, but shall serve as the basis
for further assessment, counseling, referral, or appropriate intervention by qualified
health care professionals in accordance with DOH guidelines.


The DOH shall prescribe the standards, screening tools, referral protocols,
documentation requirements, and quality assurance measures necessary for the
effective implementation of this Section.


SECTION 6. Rights of Mothers Receiving Maternal Mental Health Services. -
Every pregnant and postpartum woman receiving services under this Act shall be
entitled to the following rights:




                                                                                        6
(a) To receive maternal mental health services that are accessible, equitable, gender-
responsive, culturally sensitive, trauma-informed, and free from stigma and
discrimination;


(b) To be treated with dignity, respect, compassion, and courtesy at all times;


(c) To receive complete, accurate, and understandable information regarding the
nature, purpose, benefits, and possible risks of any screening, assessment, treatment,
or intervention, and to provide informed consent in accordance with applicable laws;


(d) To the confidentiality and privacy of personal information, medical records, and
all communications relating to maternal mental health services, subject only to
exceptions provided by law;


(e) To participate in decisions concerning her care and treatment and, where
appropriate, to involve her spouse, partner, family member, or chosen support
person, consistent with her preferences and applicable laws;


(f) To be referred promptly to appropriate health care providers or facilities when
specialized assessment, treatment, or emergency intervention is necessary;


(g) To receive continuity of care through appropriate follow-up services and
coordinated referral mechanisms; and




                                                                                    7
(h) To be protected against discrimination, coercion, harassment, or denial of health
care services on the basis of a maternal mental health condition or the seeking or
receipt of maternal mental health services.


Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish or impair any rights guaranteed
under existing laws relating to mental health, patients' rights, privacy, or the
protection of women and children.


SECTION 7. Emergency Referral and Crisis Intervention. - All public hospitals,
rural health units, barangay health stations, government-accredited maternal care
facilities, and other health care providers providing maternal health services shall
establish protocols for the timely identification, management, and referral of
pregnant and postpartum women experiencing severe or urgent maternal mental
health conditions.


Immediate assessment and appropriate intervention shall be provided to women
presenting with, including but not limited to:


(a) Suicidal thoughts, plans, attempts, or other indications of risk of self-harm;
(b) Symptoms of postpartum psychosis or other severe psychiatric conditions
requiring urgent evaluation;
(c) Severe depression, anxiety, trauma-related symptoms, or other maternal mental
health conditions that significantly impair daily functioning or present a risk to the
mother, infant, or others; and
(d) Other mental health emergencies as determined by qualified health care
professionals in accordance with Department of Health (DOH) guidelines.


                                                                                     8
Health care facilities and service providers shall ensure the availability of
appropriate referral pathways to qualified mental health professionals, emergency
services, hospitals, and other specialized facilities capable of providing timely and
appropriate care.


Emergency referral and crisis intervention services under this Section shall be
provided in a manner that respects the dignity, safety, privacy, and rights of the
mother, and shall prioritize the use of supportive, least restrictive, and clinically
appropriate interventions consistent with existing mental health laws and standards
of care.


The DOH shall develop national guidelines for maternal mental health crisis
response, including referral protocols, coordination mechanisms among health
facilities, emergency response procedures, and training requirements for frontline
health care workers.


SECTION 8. Establishment of Maternal Mental Health Support Services. -
Every province and highly urbanized city shall establish or designate accessible
maternal mental health support services within their respective local health systems
to ensure the timely provision of preventive, promotive, curative, rehabilitative, and
recovery-oriented mental health care for pregnant and postpartum women.


Such services may be provided through government hospitals, rural health units,
barangay health stations, community health centers, or other accredited public or
private health care facilities, and shall, where practicable, include:


                                                                                    9
(a) Individual, group, and family counseling services;


(b) Peer support groups and community-based psychosocial support programs;


(c) Community-based mental health hotlines and crisis response services;


(d) Tele-mental health services, including teleconsultation, remote counseling, and
other technology-assisted mental health services, particularly for geographically
isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs);


(e) Home visitation services for pregnant and postpartum women who are at risk,
have limited mobility, or have difficulty accessing health care facilities;


(f) Mobile mental health outreach services for underserved and remote communities;


(g) Referral networks linking primary care facilities with mental health specialists,
hospitals, and emergency psychiatric services to ensure continuity of care; and


(h) Other support services that the Department of Health (DOH) may determine
necessary to improve access to quality maternal mental health care.


The DOH shall issue standards and guidelines governing the establishment,
operation, coordination, and quality assurance of maternal mental health support
services under this Act. Local government units are encouraged to partner with
accredited private health care providers, civil society organizations, academic


                                                                                  10
institutions, and community-based organizations in expanding the availability and
accessibility of such services.


SECTION 9. Tele-Mental Health Services. - The Department of Health (DOH),
in coordination with local government units (LGUs), the Philippine Health Insurance
Corporation (PhilHealth), accredited health care providers, and other concerned
stakeholders, shall promote and support the use of tele-mental health services to
improve access to maternal mental health care, particularly for pregnant and
postpartum women in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs),
underserved communities, and areas with limited availability of mental health
professionals. Tele-mental health services under this Act may include:


(a) Remote mental health screening and assessment;
(b) Teleconsultation with qualified mental health professionals;
(c) Remote counseling, psychosocial support, and follow-up care;
(d) Referral coordination and case management;
(e) Mental health education and support services for mothers and families; and
(f) Other technology-assisted maternal mental health interventions as may be
determined appropriate by the DOH.


The provision of tele-mental health services shall comply with applicable laws,
rules, and regulations on patient confidentiality, data privacy, informed consent,
professional standards, and the secure use of health information technologies.


The DOH shall establish standards and guidelines for the implementation,
accreditation, quality assurance, and integration of tele-mental health services into


                                                                                  11
existing maternal health programs, including protocols for determining when in-
person assessment or emergency referral is necessary.


SECTION 10. Capacity Building and Training. - The Department of Health
(DOH), in coordination with the Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD), the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), the Commission on
Higher Education (CHED), concerned professional organizations, and other relevant
government agencies and stakeholders, shall develop and implement competency-
based education and continuing training programs on maternal mental health for
health care providers and other frontline personnel.


The training programs shall be designed to strengthen the capacity of service
providers in the prevention, early identification, screening, assessment,
management, referral, and follow-up care of maternal mental health conditions,
while promoting gender-responsive, culturally sensitive, trauma-informed, and
stigma-free care.


Participants in such training programs shall include, but not be limited to:


(a) Physicians;
(b) Psychiatrists;
(c) Psychologists;
(d) Nurses;
(e) Midwives;
(f) Social workers;
(g) Barangay health workers;


                                                                               12
(h) Community health workers and other frontline health personnel; and
(i) Other professionals and service providers involved in maternal and child health
care, as may be determined by the DOH.


The DOH shall establish minimum competency standards and training guidelines
and shall ensure that continuing education and refresher training programs are
conducted periodically to keep health care providers updated on current evidence-
based practices and national clinical guidelines on maternal mental health.


SECTION 11. Workplace and Educational Support. - All government agencies,
government-owned or -controlled corporations, and state universities and colleges
employing women shall adopt and implement maternal mental wellness policies and
programs to support the mental health and well-being of pregnant and postpartum
employees.


Private employers are encouraged to adopt similar workplace policies, taking into
consideration the size of the enterprise, the nature of its operations, and existing
labor laws and regulations.


Such workplace policies and programs may include, but shall not be limited to:


(a) Reasonable workplace accommodations and flexible work arrangements, where
practicable and consistent with operational requirements;
(b) Access to information on available maternal mental health services and
appropriate referral mechanisms;




                                                                                 13
(c) Mental health awareness, education, and stigma reduction programs for
employees and supervisors;
(d) Reintegration and return-to-work support for employees following maternity
leave or mental health-related leave associated with pregnancy or the postpartum
period;
(e) Confidential access to counseling services, employee assistance programs, or
mental health support services, where available; and
(f) Measures to foster a supportive, respectful, and non-discriminatory work
environment for pregnant and postpartum employees.


No employee shall be subjected to discrimination, harassment, or any adverse
employment action solely on the basis of seeking, receiving, or being referred for
maternal mental health services, consistent with existing labor laws and applicable
civil service rules.


The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), the Civil Service Commission
(CSC), and the Department of Health (DOH) shall jointly develop guidelines to
assist employers in the implementation of workplace maternal mental wellness
policies under this Act.


The Commission on Higher Education (CHED), in coordination with the DOH and
concerned professional regulatory bodies, shall ensure that maternal mental health
education is appropriately integrated into the curricula of medicine, nursing,
midwifery, psychology, social work, and other allied health professions, consistent
with national educational standards.




                                                                                14
SECTION 12. Community-Based Awareness and Education Programs. - The
Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Philippine Commission on
Women (PCW), the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), local
government units (LGUs), and other concerned government agencies and
stakeholders, shall develop and implement sustained community-based awareness
and education programs to promote maternal mental health and increase public
understanding of maternal mental health conditions.


Such programs shall include, but not be limited to:


(a) Public education on maternal mental health, including postpartum depression,
anxiety disorders, postpartum psychosis, and other maternal mental health
conditions;
(b) Information on the signs and symptoms of maternal mental health conditions,
available treatment options, and the importance of early detection and timely
intervention;
(c) Promotion of mental health literacy and stigma reduction through culturally
appropriate and evidence-based information campaigns;
(d) Education and engagement of spouses, partners, family members, caregivers,
employers, and community leaders to strengthen support systems for pregnant and
postpartum women;
(e) Dissemination of information on government programs, available maternal
mental health services, referral pathways, and emergency mental health resources;
(f) Community outreach activities in geographically isolated and disadvantaged
areas (GIDAs), indigenous cultural communities, and other underserved populations
to improve awareness and access to maternal mental health services; and


                                                                                15
(g) Observance of relevant national and international mental health awareness
activities, as may be determined by the DOH, to promote public education and
advocacy on maternal mental health.


The DOH shall encourage partnerships with civil society organizations, professional
associations, academic institutions, faith-based organizations, and community-based
organizations in the development and implementation of awareness and education
programs under this Act.


Information, education, and communication (IEC) materials developed pursuant to
this Act shall be evidence-based, culturally sensitive, gender-responsive, accessible
to persons with disabilities where practicable, and made available in Filipino,
English, and other local languages, as appropriate.


SECTION 13. Data Collection, Monitoring, and Research. - The Department of
Health (DOH) shall establish and maintain a national maternal mental health data
collection, monitoring, and evaluation system to support evidence-based
policymaking, improve service delivery, and assess the effectiveness of programs
implemented under this Act.


The system shall, subject to applicable laws on data privacy and confidentiality,
collect and analyze data on:


(a) The prevalence and incidence of maternal mental health conditions;
(b) The utilization and accessibility of maternal mental health services;
(c) Screening, referral, treatment, and follow-up outcomes;


                                                                                  16
(d) Workforce capacity, service availability, and regional disparities in access to
care;
(e) Program implementation, quality of service delivery, and health outcomes; and
(f) Other indicators as may be determined by the DOH for the effective
implementation and evaluation of this Act.


The DOH shall regularly monitor and evaluate the implementation of the National
Maternal Mental Health Program and may collaborate with academic institutions,
research organizations, professional associations, local government units, and other
relevant stakeholders in the conduct of research and program evaluation.


The DOH shall submit an annual report to the President of the Philippines and to
Congress on the implementation of this Act. The report shall include, among others,
program accomplishments, utilization of maternal mental health services,
prevalence trends, implementation challenges, expenditures, identified service gaps,
and recommendations for legislative, policy, or administrative action.


All personal information and medical records collected pursuant to this Act shall be
processed and protected in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and regulations
on data privacy, confidentiality, and the protection of patient information. The
publication of reports and research findings under this Act shall not disclose
information that would identify any individual patient.


SECTION 14. Monitoring and Evaluation of Program Implementation. - The
Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Philippine Commission on
Women (PCW), the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), local


                                                                                 17
government units (LGUs), the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
(PhilHealth), and other concerned agencies and stakeholders, shall establish a
monitoring and evaluation framework to assess the implementation, effectiveness,
accessibility, and sustainability of programs and services established under this Act.


The monitoring and evaluation framework shall include, but shall not be limited to,
the assessment of:


(a) The availability, accessibility, and utilization of maternal mental health services
across regions and local government units;
(b) The implementation and compliance of health care facilities with screening,
referral, and service delivery standards;
(c) The effectiveness of training programs and the adequacy of the maternal mental
health workforce;
(d) The effectiveness of public awareness programs in improving maternal mental
health literacy and reducing stigma;
(e) The outcomes of maternal mental health interventions, including referral
completion, continuity of care, and patient satisfaction;
(f) The adequacy and utilization of financial mechanisms supporting maternal
mental health services, including PhilHealth benefits and government funding; and
(g) Other indicators necessary to determine the effectiveness and impact of this Act.


The DOH shall conduct periodic evaluations of the National Maternal Mental Health
Program and shall submit recommendations for policy improvement, program
enhancement, and resource allocation based on evaluation findings.




                                                                                    18
The DOH shall make available appropriate summary reports on program
implementation while ensuring that all personal and sensitive health information
remains protected in accordance with applicable data privacy and confidentiality
laws.


SECTION 15. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


Local government units are encouraged to allocate funds from their Gender and
Development (GAD) budgets and health funds for maternal mental health programs.


SECTION 16. PhilHealth Coverage for Maternal Mental Health Services. - The
Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), in coordination with the
Department of Health (DOH), shall develop, enhance, and implement benefit
packages or mechanisms to ensure financial access to maternal mental health
services for pregnant and postpartum women.


Such coverage shall include, subject to applicable PhilHealth policies and benefit
standards:


(a) Maternal mental health screening and assessment;
(b) Psychological counseling, psychosocial support, and other appropriate non-
pharmacological interventions;
(c) Psychiatric consultation, diagnosis, treatment, and follow-up care for women
requiring specialized mental health services;
(d) Crisis intervention and emergency mental health services for high-risk cases;


                                                                                    19
(e) Essential medicines and other clinically indicated treatments in accordance with
approved clinical guidelines; and
(f) Other maternal mental health services as may be determined necessary by
PhilHealth and the DOH.


PhilHealth shall ensure that benefit mechanisms under this Section are accessible
through accredited health care providers, including primary care facilities, hospitals,
and other qualified service providers, and shall establish appropriate referral and
coordination mechanisms to support continuity of care.


The DOH and PhilHealth shall periodically review the utilization, effectiveness, and
adequacy of maternal mental health benefits and shall make appropriate
recommendations to improve coverage, affordability, and accessibility of services
under this Act.


SECTION 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with PCW, DSWD,
Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), Department of Education (DepEd),
Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and other concerned agencies, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of
this Act.


SECTION 18. Administrative Accountability. - All government agencies, local
government units, public health care facilities, and government-accredited maternal
care providers responsible for the implementation of this Act shall comply with the




                                                                                    20
standards, protocols, and requirements established herein and under the
implementing rules and regulations issued pursuant to this Act.


Failure to comply with the mandatory provisions of this Act, including the required
establishment of maternal mental health services, screening procedures, referral
mechanisms, reporting requirements, and other obligations imposed upon covered
government entities and facilities, shall be subject to appropriate administrative
remedies in accordance with existing civil service laws, health regulations, and other
applicable laws, rules, and regulations.


No health care worker shall be held administratively liable solely due to the absence
of available resources, lack of access to appropriate referral facilities, or other
circumstances beyond their reasonable control, provided that the health care worker
acted in good faith, followed applicable protocols, and made reasonable efforts to
provide or facilitate appropriate care.


The Department of Health (DOH) shall establish mechanisms for monitoring
compliance, receiving reports of non-compliance, providing technical assistance,
and supporting corrective actions to ensure the effective implementation of this Act.


SECTION 19. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


SECTION 20. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


                                                                                   21
SECTION 21. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
