                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

The barangay is the first place people turn to when they need help from government.
It handles everyday concerns like settling disputes, keeping peace and order,
responding to emergencies, linking families to health and social services, and
planning small but important community projects. Despite this heavy responsibility,
many barangay officials work with low and uneven pay, limited benefits, and little
protection. These gaps weaken their ability to serve the public well and put
unnecessary strain on those who are closest to the people.

This bill proposes a Magna Carta for barangays and barangay officials to fix these
long-standing problems. It brings together clear and fair rules on compensation,
benefits, and protection, and sets minimum standards of support so barangays can
stand on their own and function effectively. By strengthening barangays as self-
reliant communities, the measure gives real meaning to local autonomy and ensures
that when duties are given to barangays, the resources and support they need follow.

In simple terms, the bill aims to improve the lives and working conditions of barangay
officials, standardize compensation to reduce unfair disparities, protect and
professionalize barangay tanods, and ensure basic services and facilities reach every
community. It also promotes steady and direct funding for barangays and encourages
local development through jobs, scholarships, and small enterprises. Overall, this
measure invests in stronger barangays, because when barangays are supported,
public service becomes faster, fairer, and closer to the people.

                             AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR A MAGNA CARTA OF BARANGAYS AND BARANGAY OFFICIALS

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta for
Barangays and Barangay Officials.”


SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote and protect the welfare of barangays and barangay officials, recognizing
their indispensable role as front-liners in local governance, peace and order, and
community development. The State shall ensure that barangay officials are afforded
adequate compensation, benefits, capacity support, and protection to enable them to
perform their duties effectively, uphold their dignity, and encourage dedicated public
service at the grassroots level.

SECTION 3. General Principles. — The following shall guide the implementation of
this Act:

   (a) The National Government shall give due attention to the development of
       barangays as the country’s basic political units;
   (b) Every barangay shall be strengthened with authority and capability to address
       local concerns at its level, consistent with local autonomy; and
   (c) Self-help and people participation shall be the guiding principles in barangay
       development initiatives.

SECTION 4. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) “Barangay officials” refer to the Punong Barangay, members of the
       Sangguniang Barangay, Sangguniang Kabataan Chairperson, Barangay
       Secretary, and Barangay Treasurer.

   (b) “Barangay tanods” refer to barangay-based community safety personnel
       appointed pursuant to Section 6 of this Act and performing peace and order
       functions within the barangay.


SECTION 5. Compensation and Benefits of Barangay Officials. —

   (a) Without prejudice to existing laws on the compensation of elective local
       officials, barangay officials shall receive compensation and benefits
       commensurate to their functions, including but not limited to salaries or
       honoraria, allowances, and social protection benefits as may be provided by
       law and applicable guidelines.

   (b) Barangay officials shall be entitled to benefits analogous to those received by
       government personnel, as may be allowed by existing laws and rules, including
       but not limited to: representation and transportation allowance (RATA),
       personnel economic relief allowance (PERA), productivity-based incentives,
       clothing or uniform allowance, mid-year and year-end benefits, and
       mandatory insurance coverage, subject to availability of funds and applicable
       accounting and auditing rules.

SECTION 6. Standardized Salary Rates of Barangay Officials. — Subject to the
enactment of the necessary appropriation ordinances and the identification of lawful
funding sources by the city or municipality concerned in coordination with the
barangay, and in no case later than six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act, the
following standardized rates shall apply:

   (a) Punong Barangay — an amount equivalent to the salary rate of a Sangguniang
       Bayan member of the municipality or city concerned;

   (b) Members of the Sangguniang Barangay — an amount equivalent to eighty
       percent (80%) of the salary rate of a Sangguniang Bayan member of the
       municipality or city concerned; and




                                                                                4 | Page
  (c) Sangguniang Kabataan Chairperson, Barangay Secretary, and Barangay
      Treasurer — an amount equivalent to seventy-five percent (75%) of the salary
      rate of a Sangguniang Bayan member of the municipality or city concerned.

SECTION 7. Barangay Tanods: Organization, Appointment, Tenure, and
Benefits. —

  (a) Organization. — There shall be barangay tanods in every barangay to assist in
      the maintenance of peace and order and in the enforcement of barangay
      ordinances and other applicable laws within the barangay. The Sangguniang
      Barangay shall, by resolution, determine the number of barangay tanods
      needed, subject to available funds and pertinent guidelines.

  (b) Appointment and Qualifications. — Barangay tanods shall be appointed by the
      Punong Barangay with the concurrence of the majority of all the members of
      the Sangguniang Barangay. No person shall be appointed barangay tanod
      unless he or she is:

     (1) a citizen of the Philippines;
     (2) a resident of the barangay concerned;
     (3) of good moral character; and
     (4) of sound mind. Patronage or partisan considerations shall not be a basis
         for selection and appointment.

  (c) Tenure. — Barangay tanods shall have a term co-terminus with the appointing
      Punong Barangay and Sangguniang Barangay, unless earlier removed for just
      cause and after due process.

  (d) Benefits and Privileges. — Barangay tanods shall receive the following, subject
      to funding and applicable rules:

     (1) Honoraria, allowances, and other emoluments as may be authorized by law
         or by barangay, municipal, or city ordinance, but in no case less than Six
         hundred pesos (P600.00) per month;

     (2) A Christmas bonus of at least One thousand pesos (P1,000.00) each,
         chargeable against the barangay general fund or such other funds as may
         be provided by the National Government for the purpose;




                                                                             5 | Page
       (3) Insurance coverage including, but not limited to, temporary and
           permanent disability, double indemnity, accident insurance, death, and
           burial benefits;

       (4) Free medical care in any government hospital or institution, including
           necessary subsistence, medicines, and medical attendance, and including
           surgery, medicines, X-rays, laboratory fees, and other hospital expenses;
           and

       (5) Educational assistance, during incumbency, in the form of exemption from
           tuition and matriculation fees for legitimate dependent children in state
           universities and colleges (SUCs), subject to applicable SUC admission and
           residency policies. The barangay tanod may likewise avail of such
           educational benefit in an SUC within the province or city to which the
           barangay belongs.

SECTION 8. Minimum Access to Safe Drinking Water. — It is the policy of the State
that every barangay should have regular access to clean and potable water. For this
purpose, every city or municipality, as the case may be, shall include in its local
development and investment plans the construction, rehabilitation, or maintenance
of drinking-water facilities reasonably sufficient to serve barangay residents, subject
to national standards and available funds.

SECTION 9. Minimum Access to Public Transportation. — Every city or
municipality, as the case may be, shall, in coordination with appropriate agencies,
endeavor to ensure the availability of public transportation servicing each barangay
at least once a day, subject to route feasibility, safety, and applicable regulatory
standards.

SECTION 10. Basic Facilities: Schools, Health Center, and Barangay Hall. — As
far as practicable and consistent with national standards and available funds:

   (a) Every barangay shall be provided with at least one (1) kindergarten and at
       least one (1) elementary school; and

   (b) Each barangay shall be entitled to one (1) health center and one (1) barangay
       hall. For secondary education, the National Government and local government
       units shall plan access so that a high school is reasonably accessible to
       barangay residents, consistent with the education sector plan.

SECTION 11. Direct and Automatic Release of Barangay Share from national
Taxes. — The share of barangays from national taxes, as may be provided under
existing laws, shall be released directly to the barangay treasurer in accordance with
the applicable budgeting, cash programming, accounting, and auditing rules.

SECTION 12. Transfer of Funds for Barangay Roads and Bridges. — Public funds
appropriated from the National Treasury for the maintenance of barangay roads and
bridges and similar works intended for barangay-level implementation shall be
transferred or remitted in accordance with applicable laws and regulations, and shall
be subject to auditing rules and regulations.

SECTION 13. Share of Barangays in Certain Local Collections. —

   (a) A portion of real property taxes collected on real property located within the
       barangay shall accrue to the barangay, in the percentages and manner
       provided by existing laws.

   (b) The barangay shall likewise receive a share in other local taxes, fees, and
       charges collected within its territorial jurisdiction, as may be provided by
       existing laws and ordinances.


SECTION 14. Equitable Share from Proceeds of Utilization and Development of
National Wealth. — For the exploitation, utilization, and development of natural
resources within its territorial jurisdiction, every barangay shall be entitled to an
equitable share in the proceeds derived therefrom, in accordance with existing laws.
The city or municipality concerned shall conduct consultations with affected
barangays and relevant stakeholders to determine the equitable allocation, taking
into account exposure to pollution, flood risks, and ecological impacts, subject to
applicable laws and regulations.

SECTION 15. Barangay Scholarship Grant. — Every barangay shall endeavor to
sponsor at least one (1) college scholarship grant every year, subject to availability of
funds. The scholarship shall be awarded by a committee of five (5) persons
designated by the Sangguniang Barangay with the approval of the Punong Barangay,
through a transparent and competitive process. Priority shall be given to bona fide
barangay residents. The grant may include tuition and other school fees, and
reasonable allowances for books, supplies, transportation, and other expenses, as
may be determined by the barangay.

SECTION 16. Priority in Employment for Barangay Residents. — Residents of the
barangay shall be given priority, consistent with applicable laws and civil service
rules, in the hiring of workers and laborers needed for government-funded
construction or development projects within the barangay.

SECTION 17. Cooperative Enterprises. — The establishment and organization of
cooperative enterprises in the barangay shall be encouraged. Barangays shall extend
assistance, within their powers and resources, to strengthen cooperative
undertakings as part of local economic development.

SECTION 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — The Department of
the Interior and Local Government (DILG), in coordination with concerned agencies
and stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations (IRR)
necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act. The IRR shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in a newspaper of general circulation.

SECTION 19. Appropriations — The amount necessary to implement the provisions
of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act and, where
applicable, in the annual budgets of local government units, subject to existing
budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws and rules.

SECTION 20. Penal Provision. — Any person who shall willfully interfere with,
restrain, or coerce any barangay official or barangay resident in the exercise of the
rights guaranteed by this Act, or who shall in any manner commit any act to defeat
any of the provisions of this Act, shall, upon conviction, be punished by a fine of not
less than Five hundred pesos (P500.00) nor more than Two thousand pesos
(P2,000.00), or by imprisonment of not less than one (1) month nor more than six (6)
months, or both, at the discretion of the court.

SECTION 15. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, rules, regulations or
parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby amended, repealed, or modified
accordingly.

SECTION 17. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
