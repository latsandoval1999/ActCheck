                            AN ACT
GRANTING A MATERNITY BENEFIT TO WOMEN WORKERS IN THE INFORMAL
ECONOMY, AMENDING FOR THIS PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11210,
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article XIII, Section 14 of the 1987 Constitution provides that, “[t]he State shall
protect working women by providing safe and healthful working conditions, taking into
account their maternal functions, and such facilities and opportunities that will enhance
their welfare and enable them to realize their full potential in the service of the nation.”
Republic Act No. 11210, otherwise known as the “105-Day Expanded Maternity Leave
Law,” is among the landmark laws affirming the State’s commitment to strengthen the
protection of working mothers and their children.

        Republic Act No. 11210 significantly expanded the maternity benefits among
Filipino working mothers. The law provides them with ample time to recover their
health and overall well-being, while allowing them to focus on childcare before
returning to work. However, the existing law fails to cover women working in the
informal economy who are non-members of the Social Security System (SSS).

       According to 2021 data from the Philippine Legislators' Committee on
Population and Development (PLCPD), “more than 70% of Filipino workers were
informally employed, with women disproportionately represented in this sector.”1 They
include “street vendors, domestic helpers, and micro-entrepreneurs,” among others,
who often don’t have access to fair wages, benefits, and job security, including SSS
membership.2

       This bill seeks to expand the coverage of maternity benefits to women workers
in the informal economy by providing a one-time direct maternity cash benefit per
delivery equivalent to the prevailing daily minimum wage rate in the mother’s region of
residence multiplied by forty-four (44) days. The cash benefit distribution will be
administered by the Department of Social Work and Development (DSWD). This bill
therefore, seeks to further strengthen the State’s Constitutional mandate to ensure
that the rights to health and decent working conditions of all working women in the
country are upheld and promoted.

      In the 20th Congress, similar bills were filed by Representatives Brian Yamsuan,
Ferdinand Martin Romualdez, Yedda Romualdez, Andrew Romualdez, Dadah Ismula,
Jose Manuel Diokno, Percival Cendaña, and Kaka Bag-ao in the House of
Representatives, and Senator Risa Hontiveros in the Senate. This bill fully adopts
Senator Hontiveros’s Senate Bill No. 1597.

      Early approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

 1         SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Maternity Benefit
 2   for Women in the Informal Economy Act.”
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to protect
 5   the rights and welfare of women workers in the informal economy, to ensure optimal
 6   maternal health consistent with the Sustainable Development Goals, and to encourage
 7   and support the practice of breastfeeding in furtherance of its obligation to ensure the
 8   survival and development of Filipino children.
 9
10         SEC. 3. Maternity Benefit for Women Workers in the Informal Economy. –
11   Section 11 of Republic Act No. 11210 is hereby deleted and replaced with a new
12   Section 11, which will read as follows:
13
14          SECTION 11. MATERNITY BENEFITS FOR FEMALE WORKERS WHO ARE
15          NON-MEMBERS OF THE SSS. – FEMALE WORKERS WHO ARE NEITHER
16          VOLUNTARY NOR REGULAR MEMBERS OF THE SSS SHALL RECEIVE,
17          THROUGH THE DEPARTMENT OF SOCIAL WELFARE AND
18          DEVELOPMENT (DSWD), A ONE-TIME DIRECT MATERNITY CASH
19          BENEFIT PER DELIVERY EQUIVALENT TO THE PREVAILING DAILY
20          MINIMUM WAGE RATE IN HER REGION OF RESIDENCE MULTIPLIED BY
21          FORTY FOUR (44) DAYS.
