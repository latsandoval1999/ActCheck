                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Early detection and timely intervention are critical in improving the long-term
development, learning capacity, and quality of life of children with developmental
concerns. Delayed diagnosis often results in missed opportunities for early care and
support, which may lead to more severe developmental challenges later in life.
However, many Filipino families continue to face difficulties in accessing affordable
screening, assessment, therapy, and intervention services, particularly in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

Every child deserves the opportunity to grow, learn, and reach their full potential
regardless of physical, developmental, or social challenges. There is a need to
establish a comprehensive and inclusive framework that ensures accessible and
affordable early detection and intervention services for children with special needs.
Through strengthened coordination among government agencies, healthcare
providers, educational institutions, and local government units, more children can
receive timely support and appropriate interventions during their formative years.

Therefore, this bill seeks to institutionalize a National Program for Early Detection and
Intervention that will strengthen developmental screening and assessment services,
improve access to therapeutic and educational interventions, enhance public
awareness, and promote the training of qualified professionals in child development
and special needs care.

Through this bill, we aim to ensure that every Filipino child is given the opportunity to
reach their full potential through accessible, responsive, and inclusive early childhood
intervention services.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT PROVIDING A COMPREHENSIVE AND AFFORDABLE FRAMEWORK
  FOR EARLY DETECTION AND INTERVENTION SERVICES FOR CHILDREN
                      WITH SPECIAL NEEDS

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in the
Congress assembled:


SEC. 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Early Detection and Intervention
Services for Children with Special Needs Act”.
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and promote the
rights of children with special needs to equal opportunities in education, healthcare,
and social integration. The State shall establish a comprehensive framework for early
detection, assessment, and intervention, ensuring that no child is left behind,
regardless of geographic, economic, and social barriers. Towards this end, the State
shall support and empower children with special needs and their families by giving
them better lifelong outcomes through accessible and affordable early detection and
intervention services.
SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act seeks to promote the rights and developmental
potential of all Filipino children, particularly those with or at risk of developmental
delays and disabilities, by establishing a comprehensive and inclusive framework for
early detection and intervention. Specifically, it aims to:
   a. Establish a unified and integrated National Program for the early screening,
      identification, diagnosis, detection, and monitoring of developmental delays,
      disabilities, and disorders in children. The National Program shall be led by the
      Department of Health (DOH), in close coordination with the Department of
      Education (DepEd), the Department of Social Welfare and Development
      (DSWD), and Local Government Units (LGUs);
   b. Ensure the provision of free or subsidized intervention services, including but
      not limited to occupational, physical, speech-language, psychological, and
      educational therapies, accessible across all regions, including geographically
      isolated and disadvantaged areas;
   c. Build and sustain a competent and adequately trained workforce in the fields of
      health, education, and social services through continuing professional
      development programs focused on early childhood disability identification and
      intervention;
   d. Promote public awareness and advocacy to empower parents, caregivers, and
      communities with knowledge on child development, early warning signs,
      available services, and to reduce stigma against children with disabilities;
   e. Provide scholarship grants to deserving Filipino students pursuing degree
      programs in professions relevant to early childhood development, disability
      identification, and intervention, with a corresponding return service obligation in
      public or accredited facilities; and
   f. Strengthen inter-agency coordination and governance mechanisms among the
      DOH, DepEd, DSWD, LGUs, and other relevant entities to ensure the effective,
      inclusive, and rights-based delivery of services to children with developmental
      needs.
SEC. 4. Coverage. – All infants and children up to eight (8) years of age shall be
entitled to early childhood developmental screening, assessment, and intervention
services under this Act. Priority shall be given to children who are suspected of, or
diagnosed with, developmental delays, disabilities, or neurodevelopmental disorders,
including, but not limited to, the following:
   a. Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) – A developmental disorder typically
      appearing before age three, characterized by challenges in communication,
      social interaction, and repetitive behaviors, often co-occurring with intellectual
      disability or epilepsy;
   b. Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) – A neurodevelopmental
      disorder marked by persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and
      impulsivity inappropriate for the child’s age;
   c. Behavioral Disorders – Emotional or behavioral issues that disrupt learning,
      social functioning, or classroom environments, requiring behavioral support or
      intervention;
   d. Cerebral Palsy (CP) – A group of non-progressive neurological disorders
      affecting movement, posture, and coordination, often caused by brain damage
      before, during, or shortly after birth
   e. Down Syndrome – A genetic condition caused by the presence of an extra
      chromosome 21, leading to developmental delays, intellectual disability, and
      characteristic physical features;
   f. Global Developmental Delay (GDD) – A condition diagnosed in children under
      five years old who show significant delays in two or more developmental
      domains: motor skills, speech and language, cognitive development, social
      interaction, or activities of daily living;
   g. Intellectual Disability – A condition marked by significant limitations in
      intellectual functioning and adaptive behavior, affecting conceptual, social, and
      practical skills;
   h. Learning Disabilities – Specific developmental disorders that affect the ability
      to read, write, or perform mathematical calculations, such as dyslexia or
      dyscalculia, are usually identified during school age, but with early warning
      signs in preschool years;
   i. Visual Impairment – Includes both blindness and low vision. Blindness refers to
      a visual acuity of 20/200 or less in the better eye with maximum correction,
      while low vision involves limited vision that allows reading only under special
      conditions;
   j. Hearing Impairment – Includes deafness (non-functional hearing) and hard-of-
      hearing conditions (partially functional hearing, possibly with hearing aids);
   k. Orthopedic or Physical Disabilities – Conditions affecting the musculoskeletal
      system that hinder mobility or motor functioning, whether temporary or
      permanent;
   l. Learning Disabilities – Disorders in processing information, resulting in
      difficulties with listening, speaking, reading, writing, spelling, or mathematics,
      despite having normal intelligence. Includes dyslexia, brain injury, and
      developmental aphasia;
   m. Speech or Communication Disorders – Conditions that impair the ability to
      communicate, including stuttering, articulation issues, language impairment, or
      voice disorders that negatively impact learning;
   n. Multiple Disabilities – The presence of two or more disabling conditions (e.g.,
      deaf-blindness, autism with intellectual disability) requiring combined support
      services;
   o. Specific Learning Disability – A disorder in one or more of the basic
      psychological processes related to understanding or using language,
      manifesting as difficulties in listening, thinking, speaking, reading, writing, or
      mathematical calculations; and
   p. Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) – An acquired brain injury caused by external
      physical force, resulting in cognitive, behavioral, or physical impairments that
      adversely affect learning and functioning. Excludes congenital or degenerative
      brain conditions and birth-related trauma.
This Act shall also grant priority to those who reside in disaster-prone areas and GIDA,
such as areas that are isolated due to distance, inaccessibility to transportation, and
weather conditions, underserved and unserved communities and other areas
identified to have high incidences of poverty, those persons belonging to the
vulnerable sector, communities in or recovering from situation of crisis or armed
conflict and recognized as such by a government body.
SEC. 5. National Program for Early Detection and Intervention. – The Department of
Health (DOH), in coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd), the
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) and other relevant agencies,
shall jointly develop, establish, and implement a comprehensive and integrated
National Program for Early Detection and Intervention. The Program shall aim to
identify, support, and monitor Filipino children with developmental concerns. It shall
ensure the following key components:
   a. Early and Accessible Screening, Diagnosis, and Intervention Services – A
      standardized system shall be developed and deployed nationwide to provide
      routine developmental screening at barangay health centers, daycare centers,
      and other community-based settings, with appropriate referral pathways for
      diagnostic and intervention services. Services shall be culturally sensitive,
      family-centered, and financially accessible;
   b. Trained and Competent Workforce in Early Intervention and Child Development
      – The Program shall support the capacity building of healthcare and education
      professionals, including but not limited to special education teachers,
      developmental pediatricians, therapists, social workers, allied health personnel,
      and barangay health workers. Training shall focus on early childhood
      development, disability inclusion, and evidence-based intervention strategies;
   c. Public Awareness and Advocacy Campaign. – A nationwide information and
      advocacy campaign shall be implemented to educate parents, caregivers, and
      communities on early childhood development, the importance of timely
      screening and intervention, and to reduce stigma and discrimination against
      children with disabilities;
   d. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Data Management. – A robust monitoring and
      evaluation framework shall be established to track the implementation and
      outcomes of the Program. This shall include the collection and analysis of age-
      disaggregated and disability-specific data, the establishment of benchmarks,
      and the use of performance indicators to assess impact and inform policy
      adjustments.
The implementing agencies shall issue joint guidelines within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act to operationalize the National Program and ensure its
harmonization with existing government initiatives on child development, disability
rights, and inclusive education.
SEC. 6. Mandatory Early Childhood Developmental Screening and Assessment. – All
government health centers and public hospitals shall be mandated to conduct regular
developmental screening and assessments for infants and children from birth up to
eight (8) years of age as part of their basic health services.
Such screening shall follow evidence-based clinical guidelines to be issued by the
DOH, and shall be performed by trained health professionals or barangay health
workers (BHWs) under supervision.
The mandatory early childhood developmental screening and assessment shall be
free of charge.
SEC. 7. Mandatory PhilHealth Coverage. – The Philippine Health Insurance
Corporation (PhilHealth) shall provide mandatory coverage for medically necessary
occupational therapy, physical therapy, speech therapy, and other related therapeutic
interventions for children with special needs. Such coverage shall include, but not be
limited to, assessment, diagnosis, treatment, and rehabilitation services rendered by
duly licensed and accredited professionals. PhilHealth is hereby directed to formulate
and implement appropriate benefit packages and reimbursement schemes to ensure
accessible, affordable, and quality therapeutic care for children with special needs.
The Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with PhilHealth and other relevant
agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to effectively
implement this provision.
SEC. 8. Scholarship Grants in Early Childhood Disability Identification and
Intervention. – To ensure the sustained availability of qualified professionals in the
field of early childhood disability identification and intervention, scholarship grants shall
hereby be provided to deserving Filipino students who wish to pursue degree
programs in medicine, psychology, occupational therapy, physical therapy, speech-
language pathology, special education, social work, and other allied health or social
science disciplines relevant to the early detection and management of childhood
disabilities, subject to guidelines to be issued by the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), in coordination with the Department of Health (DOH), the Department of
Education (DepEd), and other concerned agencies.
Scholars shall be required to render return service in government institutions or
accredited facilities providing services to children with special needs, for a period
equivalent to the number of years they benefited from the scholarship, under terms to
be defined in the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.
SEC. 9. Establishment of Early Childhood Disability Identification and Intervention
Service Facilities in Government Hospitals. – To ensure the accessibility and
availability of early childhood disability identification and intervention services across
all regions of the country, the following shall be mandated:
   a. Level 3 Government Hospitals. – All Level 3 government hospitals under the
      supervision of the Department of Health (DOH) shall establish, operate, and
      maintain an Early Childhood Development and Inclusion Center dedicated to
      the comprehensive evaluation, diagnosis, and intervention of developmental
      delays, disabilities, and disorders in children. These Centers shall be
      multidisciplinary in nature and staffed by qualified professionals, including but
      not limited to developmental pediatricians, psychologists, speech-language
      pathologists, occupational therapists, physical therapists, and social workers.
      Services shall include developmental screening, comprehensive assessments,
      individualized therapy programs, caregiver support, and coordination with
      community-based services; and
   b. Level 1 and Level 2 Government Hospitals. – All Level 1 and Level 2
      government hospitals shall establish at least a Special Needs Unit or Office
      within their premises to provide initial developmental screening and referral
      services for children suspected of having developmental delays or disabilities.
      These Units or Offices shall be manned by appropriately trained healthcare
      personnel and equipped to conduct preliminary assessments and facilitate
      timely referrals to higher-level hospitals or specialized centers, as may be
      necessary.
The DOH shall issue the necessary standards, protocols, and capacity-building
programs to ensure the effective implementation of this provision and shall prioritize
the allocation of resources to geographically isolated, disadvantaged, and remote
areas.
SEC. 10. Enhancement and Improvement of Existing Special Education (SPED)
Centers. – The Department of Education (DepEd) shall ensure the enhancement,
upgrading, and continuous improvement of all existing Special Education (SPED)
Centers in public schools and institutions. This shall include, but not be limited to, the
following:
   a. Upgrading of facilities and learning environments to meet inclusive and child-
      friendly standards, with appropriate assistive technologies, instructional
      materials, and accessible infrastructure;
   b. Provision of adequate and qualified SPED teachers, para-teachers, and allied
      professionals through targeted recruitment, training, and capacity-building
      programs;
   c. Allocation of sufficient budget and resources for the maintenance and
      operational requirements of SPED Centers, including the procurement of
      developmental tools, therapy aids, and learning equipment tailored for children
      with disabilities;
   d. Integration of inclusive and culturally responsive pedagogical approaches and
      individualized education programs (IEPs) in SPED curricula; and
   e. Strengthening of partnerships with local government units, non-governmental
      organizations, and the private sector to support the delivery of specialized
      services and community-based interventions.
DepEd shall conduct an annual assessment of all public SPED Centers to monitor
compliance, identify resource gaps, and ensure the effective implementation of this
provision.
SEC. 11. Lead Implementing Agencies. – The Department of Health (DOH), in
collaboration with the Department of Education (DepEd), the Department of Social
Welfare and Development (DSWD), and the National Council on Disability Affairs
(NCDA), shall jointly implement this program through Regional and Local Health Units
and Special Education (SPED) Centers.
SEC. 12. Local Government Involvement. – The LGUs shall integrate the program into
their health and social welfare services, ensuring barangay-level implementation, and
designate focal persons for monitoring and reporting.
SEC. 13. Appropriations. – The amount deemed necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be charged against appropriations of the concerned agencies under the
current General Appropriations Act (GAA). Thereafter, such amounts as may be
necessary shall be included in the annual GAA.
SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOH, DepEd, and DSWD, in consultation with relevant
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary IRR.
SEC. 15. Separability Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, and rules
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with or contrary to the
provisions of this Act are repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
SEC. 17. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or at least two national newspapers of general
circulation.
