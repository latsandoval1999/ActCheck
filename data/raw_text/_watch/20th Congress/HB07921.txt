                              EXPLANATORY NOTE


       Part of the objectives of enacting Republic Act No. 7279 or the Urban
Development and Housing Act of 1992 (UDHA) was the provision of secure, safe,
adequate, decent, and affordable housing for the urban poor, especially for informal
settler families (ISFs). Although the UDHA intended to limit the displacement of the
urban poor and provide safeguards in cases of resettlement, experiences of ISFs over
the years show a distortion in the implementation of the existing framework to
circumvent the requirements under the law.


       In 2008, the United Nations Committee on Economic, Social, and Cultural Rights
(UN CESCR) expressed its deep concern for the large-scale forced evictions of urban
families:
          “The Committee regrets that most of its previous recommendations
          relating to the prevention of illegal forced evictions (E/C.12/1995/7,
          paras. 31 and 32) have not been acted upon by the State party, and
          remains deeply concerned about the large- scale forced eviction of urban
          families carried out for the purpose of urban renewal and beautification,
          which has reportedly affected over 1.2 million people in the period
          between 1995 and 2008. It is also concerned about the lack of effective
          consultation of, and legal redress for, persons affected by forced
          evictions,     and     the     inadequate       measures        to    provide     sufficient
          compensation or adequate relocation sites to families who have been
          forcibly removed from their properties.”1


        Eight years later, the UN CESCR urged the Philippines to amend the UDHA and
adopt a legal framework “to be followed in the case of evictions in line with
international standards[.]”2


        Over three decades have passed since the enactment of the UDHA, yet there
is still no legal framework for housing and resettlement that adequately addresses the
needs of ISFs and the urban poor that is consistent with our constitutional mandates
and core international instruments.


        The proposed bill seeks to address the gaps in the UDHA by: a) strictly
prohibiting unjust evictions of ISFs and demolitions of the structures they live in; b)
imposing additional requirements before, during, and after any eviction or demolition
of ISFs, such as social preparation, adequate resettlement, spaces for children to play
and interact in relocation sites, and adequate and genuine consultation; and c)

       The ISFs and the urban poor have experienced many of the worst injustices in
our country. At the center of these injustices are children who are forcibly displaced
from their homes and suffer abusive and inhumane practices in the conduct of eviction
and demolition. No less than Section 10 of Article III of the Constitution mandates
that “[u]rban or rural poor dwellers shall not be evicted nor their dwelling demolished,
except in accordance with law and in a just and humane manner.”


       In light of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

 1      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Just and Humane
 2   Resettlement Act.”
 3
 4       Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Pursuant to Section 9, Article XIII of the
 5   Constitution, the State shall ensure that underprivileged and homeless citizens have
 6   access to adequate, safe, secure, habitable, sustainable, resilient and affordable
 7   housing.
 8
 9        Pursuant to Section 10, Article XIII and Section 10, Article II of the Constitution,
10   the State shall neither evict nor demolish informal settler families, except in
11   accordance with law and in a just and humane manner, and shall promote social
12   justice in all phases of national development.
13
14       The State shall, by law and for the common good, undertake, in cooperation with
15   the private sector, a continuing program of housing and urban development which
 1   shall make available at affordable cost, decent housing and basic services to
 2   underprivileged and homeless citizens in urban centers and resettlement areas.
 3
 4       In addition, the State shall encourage on-site development in the implementation
 5   of housing programs and shall promote the creation of new settlements and
 6   development of sustainable urban renewal programs while guaranteeing the
 7   preservation of agricultural lands necessary for food security.
 8
 9        The State shall encourage more effective people’s participation in the urban
10   development process through a mechanism of adequate and genuine consultation in
11   all stages of the relocation and resettlement process with the affected communities,
12   which should include a space for children, women and other marginalized groups.
13
14       Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms are
15   defined as follows:
16       (a) Adequate and Genuine Consultation refers to the constitutionally mandated
17           process whereby the public, on their own or through people's organizations,
18           is provided an opportunity to be heard and to participate in the decision
19           making process on matters involving the protection and promotion of its
20           legitimate collective interest, which shall include the following:
21              i. Effective dissemination and full access, to the public, of relevant
22                 information, plans and documents, including but not limited to land
23                 records, housing budgets, the proposed plan or project, alternative
24                 housing options, technical studies, and comprehensive resettlement
25                 plans;
26             ii. Reasonable time, which shall not be less than thirty (30) days, for the
27                 public to review, comment and object to the plans and studies;
28            iii. Provision by the government or non-government organizations of legal,
29                 technical, and other appropriate advice to affected persons, households,
30                 and/or organizations on their rights and option;
31            iv. Separate sessions of open and public consultations with affected
32                 households in general and affected subsectors in particular, including but
33                 not limited to women, children, youth, senior citizens, persons with
34                 disabilities (PWDs), workers, farmers, fisherfolk, and lesbians, gays,
35                 bisexuals, and transgender persons (LGBT);
36             v. Public hearings that provide affected informal settler families (ISFs) and
37                 their advocates with opportunities to challenge the eviction decision or
38                 present alternative proposals and to articulate their demands and
39                 development priorities; and
40            vi. The conclusion of any mediation, arbitration, or adjudication proceedings
41                 by an independent body vested with constitutional authority such as a
 1            court of law, when appropriate and availed of, in case no agreement is
 2            reached on the proposals of the concerned parties.
 3
 4   (b) Danger Area refers to areas which, when occupied for residential purposes,
 5       actually pose a danger to the life and safety and property of either the
 6       concerned residents or of the general community. The danger is due to an
 7       unavoidable source of probable harm to human life or well-being.
 8
 9   (c) Demolition refers to the dismantling by the local government unit (LGU), or
10       any legally authorized agency of government, of all structures within the
11       premises subject for clearing.
12
13   (d) Eviction refers to the removal of persons and their belongings from a subject
14       building, structure, or area, or both, in accordance with law.
15
16   (e) High Risk Area refers to an area which poses a high level of threat to the
17       public welfare and safety that cannot be addressed through scientific, physical
18       and engineering methods and, thereby, is unsuitable for settlement and
19       permanent structures.
20
21   (f) Informal Settler Families (ISFs) refers to individuals or households living in
22       any of the following places:
23       (i) Lots, buildings, dwelling units, or other structures without the consent
24             of the property owner;
25       (ii) Danger areas;
26       (iii) Areas for government infrastructure projects;
27       (iv) Protected or forest areas, except for indigenous peoples;
28       (v) Areas for priority development as declared under Proclamation No. 1967,
29             series of 1980, if applicable;
30       (vi) Government or public lands or facilities not intended for human
31             habitation; or
32       (vii) Any other place, after being displaced due to natural or man-made
33             hazards.
34
35   (g) People’s Plan refers to the plan formulated and initiated by the beneficiary
36       associations, with the assistance of the concerned government agencies, in
37       cooperation with the Presidential Commission for the Urban Poor (PCUP), with
38       or without the support of civil society organizations (CSOs), which shall
39       contain a site development plan that conforms to standards of adequate
40       housing and to the comprehensive land use plan of the LGU under whose
41       jurisdiction the project site is proposed to be located, including architecture
42       and engineering house design, community health, sanitation and security
 1           plans, as well as nonphysical development components such as self-help
 2           development, capability building, and a system of allocation of socialized
 3           housing units. The formulation and the resulting plan should promote and
 4           protect the welfare of women, children, persons with disabilities, and senior
 5           citizens.
 6
 7       (h) Social Preparation refers to the series of activities designed to prepare the
 8           affected ISFs to cope with the changes brought about by government
 9           infrastructure or development projects and to encourage them to actively
10           participate and prepare their societal, community and personal responsibilities
11           in their quest for tenurial security, human development and basic services,
12           employment and livelihood and other government programs for the ISFs.
13
14       Sec. 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply in all cases of demolition or eviction,
15   whether undertaken by the government or private individuals or entities, and whether
16   ordered by the courts or otherwise.
17
18       Sec. 5. Policy on Eviction and Demolition. – As a general rule, any eviction
19   of ISFs or demolition of the structures they live in or any appurtenance thereto shall
20   be prohibited.
21
22       Sec. 6. Exceptions. – Eviction of ISFs or demolition of the structures they live in
23   or any appurtenance thereto shall be allowed only in the following exceptional cases:
24
25       (a) When ISFs occupy a danger area, such as esteros, railroad tracks, garbage
26           dumps, riverbanks, shorelines, waterways, and other public places such as
27           sidewalks, roads, parks, and playgrounds, or a high risk area which poses a
28           high level of threat to public welfare and safety that cannot be addressed
29           through or remedied by scientific, physical and engineering methods and,
30           thereby, is unsuitable for settlement and permanent structures: Provided, that
31           an area can only be declared as a danger area or high risk area through an
32           appropriate technical study, adequate and genuine public consultation with
33           the affected persons or entities, and certification by the appropriate
34           government agency. It shall be the duty of the province and the city or
35           municipality, after one hundred twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this
36           Act, to conduct the technical studies and public consultations necessary to
37           identify all danger and high risk areas within their respective jurisdictions.
38
39       (b) When government infrastructure projects with available funding are about to
40           be implemented: Provided, that evictions or displacements of population due
41           to any development project or business venture involving the government
42           should be avoided or minimized: Provided, further, that if eviction is
 1          unavoidable under the circumstances, the government shall conduct a
 2          comprehensive and holistic impact assessment, before the project is
 3          implemented, to determine whether the project will serve the public interest
 4          and to explore alternatives and strategies to minimize harm and further
 5          violation of human rights of the affected communities, particularly children,
 6          women and other marginalized groups: Provided, finally, that the agency
 7          which seeks to implement the eviction or demolition shall establish the budget
 8          pertaining to it upon its application to the PCUP for an eviction or demolition
 9          certificate of compliance; and
10
11      (c) When there is a court order for eviction and demolition: Provided, however,
12          that no execution of eviction or demolition order shall proceed and be carried
13          out during the pendency of an appeal, notwithstanding the failure to file a
14          supersedeas bond to stay the execution of the order if the appellant is an
15          underprivileged and homeless citizen.
16
17      Sec. 7. Mandatory Requirements Before, During, and After the Conduct
18   of Eviction or Demolition. – No eviction or demolition involving ISFs shall be
19   executed without full compliance with the following mandatory requirements to uphold
20   human dignity and prevent any violation of human rights:
21
22      (a) Before Eviction or Demolition –
23
24          (i)    Conduct of social preparation activities related to asset reform, human
25                 development an basic services, employment and livelihood, and other
26                 programs of the government for both the affected underprivileged and
27                 homeless citizens and the affected families of the place where the former
28                 will be relocated. Provided, that, separate social preparation activities for
29                 children of both communities on matters relating to the promotion and
30                 protection of their right to development, survival, protection and
31                 participation shall be conducted;
32
33          (ii)   Notice upon the affected persons or entities at least thirty (30) days prior
34                 to the date of eviction or demolition;
35
36          (iii) Adequate and genuine consultations on the matter of resettlement with
37                the duly designated representatives of the families to be resettled and
38                the affected communities in the areas where they are to be relocated:
39                Provided, that a consultation in the form of an inter-agency pre-eviction
40                or pre-demolition conference with the affected underprivileged and
41                homeless citizens shall be convened by the national government agency
 1   or LGU authorized to evict or demolish and the same shall be complied
 2   with under the following conditions:
 3
 4   1)   The local Philippine National Police (PNP) personnel, whose
 5        function is to provide law enforcement and civil disturbance control
 6        but not to participate in the physical dismantling of any structure,
 7        shall actively participate in the consultation process and their role
 8        shall be clearly set forth;
 9
10   2)   The following agencies shall be represented during the inter-
11        agency pre-eviction or pre-demolition conference with the affected
12        underprivileged and homeless citizens:
13        A. Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);
14        B. Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD);
15        C. Department of Education (DepEd);
16        D. Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH);
17        E. Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development
18            (DHSUD);
19        F.  National Housing Authority (NHA);
20        G. Department of Health (DOH);
21        H. PCUP;
22        I.  Commission on Human Rights (CHR);
23        J.  Philippine Commission on Women (PCW);
24        K. Council for the Welfare of Children (CWC);
25        L.  Appropriate Key Shelter Agencies;
26        M. Representatives from non-government organizations and/or
27            people’s organizations working for the interest of the ISFs;
28            and
29        N. The concerned LGUs.
30
31        If the conference is convened by the LGU, the PCW and CWC shall
32        be replaced by the Local GAD Focal Point System and the Local
33        Council for the Protection of Children representatives, respectively,
34        of the concerned LGUs.
35
36   3)   The inter-agency pre-eviction or pre-demolition conference with
37        the affected underprivileged and homeless citizens shall be
38        presided by the PCUP, which may call on the assistance of other
39        agencies whenever necessary;
40
41   4)   A plan for eviction or demolition shall be formulated during the
42        inter-agency pre-eviction or pre-demolition conference, setting
 1                 forth therein clear procedures that will be implemented during the
 2                 eviction or demolition to prevent any human rights violation;
 3
 4            5)   The details of the inter-agency pre-eviction or pre-demolition
 5                 conference with the affected underprivileged and homeless citizens
 6                 shall be documented or recorded, and a summary of the points of
 7                 the agreements arrived at as well as the disagreements shall be
 8                 considered and given weight by the inter-agency body in coming
 9                 up with a decision on matters relating to the conduct of eviction or
10                 demolition: Provided, that copies of the documentation and
11                 summary shall be provided to all the stakeholders present three (3)
12                 days after the pre-eviction or pre-demolition conference;
13
14            6)   The inter-agency pre-eviction or pre-demolition conference with
15                 the affected underprivileged and homeless citizens shall be
16                 conducted after seven (7) days from service of notice of eviction or
17                 demolition upon the affected persons and entities; and
18
19            7)   Any request for PNP assistance shall be accompanied by a
20                 certification from the PCUP that an inter-agency pre-eviction or pre-
21                 demolition conference with the affected underprivileged and
22                 homeless citizens was conducted.
23
24      (iv) Submission to the Barangay Officials, before any eviction or demolition,
25           of the following:
26           1) Certificate of Compliance secured from the PCUP;
27           2) Names of all personnel, staff, and crew taking part in the eviction
28                 or demolition activity, including private security, and the names of
29                 the PNP personnel assigned to maintain law and order;
30           3) Notice of the date of demolition which shall be at least five (5)
31                 calendar days from such notice; and
32           4) List of officers of the agencies forming part of the inter-agency
33                 body convened to extend support and assistance.
34
35   (b) During Eviction and Demolition –
36
37      (i)   Reading of the plan of eviction or demolition agreed upon during the
38            inter-agency pre-eviction or pre-demolition conference;
39      (ii) Presence of local government officials or their representatives during
40            eviction or demolition;
41      (iii) Proper identification of all persons taking part in the demolition;
 1       (iv) Execution of eviction or demolition only during regular office hours from
 2              Mondays to Fridays and during good weather, unless the affected
 3              families consent otherwise;
 4       (v) No use of heavy equipment for demolition except for structures that are
 5              permanent and made of concrete materials;
 6       (vi) Proper uniforms for members of the PNP who shall occupy the first line
 7              of law enforcement and observe proper disturbance control procedures:
 8              Provided, that the PNP, in coordination with the DSWD, shall ensure the
 9              safety and security of the affected children, pregnant and lactating
10              mothers, senior citizens, persons with disabilities and other groups or
11              marginalized sectors in the areas of eviction or demolition;
12       (vii) Observance of a minimum standard of conduct based on rules of
13              engagement that apply maximum tolerance in order to prevent the
14              outbreak of violence or the escalation thereof: Provided, that any legal
15              use of force must respect the principles of necessity and proportionality:
16              Provided, further, that the child protection policy in the conduct of
17              eviction or demolition shall be implemented;
18       (viii) Measures to protect the evictees from threats or harassment from the
19              government or private persons or entities, including gender-based
20              violence: Provided, that legal assistance to any complaining party shall
21              be available; and
22       (ix) Transparency in all stages of the eviction or demolition process, allowing
23              the media to cover the whole event.
24
25   (c) After Eviction or Demolition –
26
27       (i)    Adequate housing and relocation, whether temporary or permanent:
28              Provided, that in cases of eviction and demolition pursuant to a court
29              order involving underprivileged and homeless citizens, relocation shall
30              be undertaken by the LGU concerned and the National Housing Authority
31              with the assistance of other government agencies within forty-five (45)
32              days from the service of notice of final judgement by the court, after
33              which period the said order shall be executed: Provided, however, that
34              should relocation not be possible within the said period, financial
35              assistance in the amount equivalent to the prevailing and applicable
36              minimum daily wage for non-agricultural workers, multiplied by sixty
37              (60) days, shall be extended to the affected families by the LGU
38              concerned: Provided, further, that the financial assistance shall not
39              replace the requirement of adequate relocation; and
40     (ii)    Immediately after the eviction, all relief measures, medical facilities,
41             psychosocial services, documentation of evictees to prevent displacement
 1               or family separation and similar assistance shall be put in place by the
 2               LGUs concerned in partnership with relevant government agencies.
 3
 4       Observance of the above requirements shall be mandatory in all cases involving
 5   the eviction and demolition of underprivileged and homeless citizens and ISFs,
 6   regardless of whether or not their dwellings or residential structures were constructed
 7   before or after the effectivity of this Act.
 8
 9       The DILG and the DHSUD shall jointly promulgate the necessary rules and

12       Sec. 8. Prohibition on Fencing After a Disaster or Calamity. – It shall be
13   prohibited for any person to secure or build a perimeter fence on an area or property
14   inhabited by underprivileged and homeless citizens after the same is razed to the
15   ground by a fire or ruined by a natural calamity: Provided, that the fencing of the of
16   the property shall be allowed if the person causing the fencing can show a duly-signed
17   court order allowing the same.
18
19       Sec. 9. PCUP as Central Coordinating Body for Eviction, Demolition, and
20   Resettlement. – The PCUP is hereby designated as the Central Coordinating Body
21   for the conduct of eviction and demolition activities involving underprivileged and
22   homeless citizens.

17          Sec. 11. Penalty for Violation. – The penalty of not less than six (6) months
18   but not more than six (6) years of imprisonment, or a fine of not less than Twenty-
19   five thousand Pesos (Php 25,000.00) but not more than One hundred thousand Pesos
20   (Php 100,000.00), or both, at the discretion of the court, shall be imposed on any
21   person who violates Sections 6 or 7 of this Act.

23           If the offender is a corporation, partnership, association, government-owned or
24   controlled corporation, or other juridical entity, the penalty shall be imposed on the
25   officer or officers of said corporation, partnership, association, government-owned or
26   controlled corporation, or juridical entity who caused the violation.
