                       AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL ORGAN TRANSPLANT REGISTRY,
PROVIDING FINANCIAL AND MEDICAL SUPPORT FOR INDIGENT
ORGAN TRANSPLANT RECIPIENT, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Organ transplantation remains one of the most critical life-saving medical
interventions for patients suffering from end-stage organ failure. While the
Philippines has long recognized the importance of organ donation through Republic
Act No. 7170, otherwise known as the ‘Organ Donation Act of 1991’, gaps persist
in the implementation of a centralized, transparent, and efficient system for organ
allocation and transplant coordination. The absence of an integrated national
registry, coupled with the high cost of transplantation and post-operative care,
continues to limit access—especially for indigent patients.

       Thousands of Filipinos suffer from chronic kidney disease, liver failure, and
other organ-related conditions that could be treated through transplantation. Despite
advancements in medical science, many patients are unable to receive timely and
adequate care due to financial constraints and fragmented information systems for
organ donation and recipient matching.


                                                                                     1
        At present, the absence of a comprehensive national transplant registry
 hampers the efficient allocation of donated organs, resulting in delays, inequities,
 and preventable loss of life. Moreover, indigent patients often face insurmountable
 financial barriers, including costs related to surgery, hospitalization, post-operative
 care, and lifelong immunosuppressive therapy.

        This measure seeks to establish a National Organ Transplant Registry that
 integrates donor and recipient data, ensures equitable allocation of organs based on
 medical need and ethical standards, and strengthens government oversight. It
 likewise institutionalizes financial and medical support mechanisms for indigent
 patients, thereby promoting equitable access to transplantation services.
 In strengthening existing laws and addressing systemic gaps, this bill aims to save
 lives, uphold human dignity, and advance universal health care in the Philippines.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Organ
Transplant Support and Registry Act.”


      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right to health by ensuring equitable, ethical, and
accessible organ donation and transplantation services. The State shall establish a



                                                                                  1
centralized and transparent system for organ allocation, strengthen institutional
mechanisms for organ donation, and provide comprehensive financial risk protection
for transplant patients, particularly those who are indigent.


      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:


      (a) Organ Transplantation refers to surgical procedure of transferring human
      organs from a donor to a recipient for the purpose of medical treatment;
      (b) Donor refers to a living or deceased individual who donates an organ in
      accordance with law;
      (c) Recipient refers to a patient receiving an organ transplant;
      (d) Indigent Patient refers to a patient certified by the Department of Social
      Welfare and Development (DSWD) or a local social welfare office as
      financially incapable of defraying medical costs;
      (e) Registry refers to the National Organ Transplant Registry (NOTR);
      (f) Transplant Center refers to a hospital duly licensed and accredited by the
      Department of Health (DOH) to perform transplantation procedures;
      (g) Organ Procurement refers to the process of identifying, retrieving, and
      preserving organs for transplantation.


      SECTION 4. Establishment of the National Organ Transplant Registry. —
There is hereby established, under the Department of Health (DOH), a National
Organ Transplant Registry (NOTR), which shall serve as a centralized, secure, and
real-time system for the registration, matching, allocation, and monitoring of all
organ donation and transplantation activities in the country.




                                                                                  2
      The NOTR shall:


      (a) Maintain a national waiting list ranked according to objective and
      standardized criteria including medical urgency, compatibility, and waiting
      time;
      (b) Operate a 24-hour national coordination system for organ allocation and
      transport;
      (c) Establish a National Donor Registry for voluntary donor consent;
      (d) Ensure full traceability of organs from donor to recipient;
      (e) Integrate with relevant government databases, subject to applicable laws;
      (f) Develop regional clustering mechanisms; and
      (g) Generate data analytics for policy and planning


      SECTION 5. Mandatory Reporting, Compliance, and Audit – All transplant
centers, hospitals, organ procurement units, and related facilities shall submit
complete and accurate data to the NOTR within twenty-four (24) hours from
identification of a potential donor, completion of organ retrieval, or performance of
a transplant procedure, and within seven (7) calendar days for full clinical outcome
reporting. Each facility shall designate a full-time Transplant Registry Officer,
trained and certified by the DOH within six (6) months from effectivity of this Act,
responsible for compliance and data integrity. All facilities shall adopt interoperable
electronic systems compliant with DOH standards within one (1) year.


      The DOH shall conduct annual audits and at least one (1) unannounced
inspection per year for each accredited facility, supported by automated data
validation tools. A compliance scorecard shall be published annually. Non-


                                                                                     3
compliance shall result in graduated sanctions: (a) fine of not less than ₱100,000 for
first offense; (b) suspension of accreditation for second offense; and (c) revocation
of license for repeated or willful violations, without prejudice to civil or criminal
liability.


       SECTION 6. Comprehensive Financial and Medical Support for Indigent
Patients. – The State shall guarantee full continuum care for indigent transplant
patients, including pre-transplant evaluation, transplant surgery, and post-transplant
management. PhilHealth shall provide a comprehensive transplant benefit package
covering at least ninety percent (90%) of total hospitalization and surgical costs,
inclusive of professional fees. The DOH shall subsidize the remaining costs through
the National Transplant Assistance Fund, ensuring zero or minimal out-of-pocket
expense not exceeding ten percent (10%) of total cost.


       Post-transplant care shall include provision of lifelong immunosuppressive
therapy, with annual coverage not less than ₱250,000 per patient, subject to periodic
adjustment by the DOH. Transportation and accommodation assistance for indigent
patients from geographically isolated areas shall be provided up to ₱50,000 per case.
Claims processing shall be completed within thirty (30) days, and automatic
enrollment of qualified patients into PhilHealth and assistance programs shall be
mandatory.


       SECTION 7. Institutional Roles and National Transplant Network. – The
DOH shall establish a National Transplant Network within one (1) year from
effectivity of this Act, composed of accredited transplant centers, regional referral
hospitals, and organ procurement units. Each province and highly urbanized city


                                                                                    4
shall designate at least one (1) Level III or Level II government hospital as a Referral
and Donor Identification Unit within six (6) months, with trained personnel for
donor detection and referral.


      LGUs shall allocate at least one percent (1%) of their local health budget,
where feasible, to support transplant-related services, including donor awareness and
logistics. A National Transplant Advisory Council shall convene at least quarterly
and submit policy recommendations to the DOH. The DOH shall issue national
referral and transport protocols, including maximum allowable transport times per
organ type, within six (6) months.


      SECTION 8. Ethical Standards and Safeguards. — All organ donation shall
require documented informed consent and shall strictly adhere to non-
commercialization principles. Living donor transplantation shall require approval by
an independent ethics committee within seventy-two (72) hours from submission of
complete documentation, including medical, psychosocial, and financial assessment.
A mandatory seven (7)-day cooling-off period shall be observed prior to final
consent.


      The DOH shall establish a national reporting hotline and digital platform for
suspected organ trafficking within six (6) months. Confirmed violations involving
sale or purchase of organs shall be punishable by imprisonment and fines not less
than ₱1,000,000, without prejudice to prosecution under other laws. Healthcare
professionals found complicit shall face immediate suspension pending
investigation.




                                                                                      5
      SECTION 9. Data Privacy, Security and Patient Rights. – The NOTR shall
implement end-to-end encryption, multi-factor authentication, and real-time access
logs, compliant with national and international health data security standards. All
data breaches shall be reported to the National Privacy Commission within seventy-
two (72) hours and affected individuals notified within five (5) days.


      Patients shall have the right to access their records within fifteen (15) working
days from request and to request corrections within thirty (30) days. Unauthorized
disclosure or misuse of data shall be punishable by fines and/or imprisonment, in
addition to penalties under existing data privacy laws.


      SECTION 10. Public Awareness and Donor Promotion. — The DOH, in
coordination with LGUs, educational institutions, civil society organizations, and
relevant stakeholders, shall implement sustained and culturally sensitive public
awareness programs to promote voluntary organ donation. Such programs shall
include integration of organ donation education in school curricula, community
health campaigns, and partnerships with religious and community leaders to address
ethical and cultural concerns.


      A National Organ Donation Awareness Program shall be established,
including annual observance activities and the development of accessible donor
registration platforms. Recognition programs for organ donors and their families
shall be instituted to honor their contribution, provided that such recognition shall
not involve monetary or material compensation that may be construed as
commercialization.




                                                                                     6
      SECTION 11. Funding. – The amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. Funding shall
likewise be sourced from PhilHealth allocations, earmarked revenues from excise
taxes on tobacco, alcohol, and other health-related products, as well as grants,
donations, and official development assistance.


      SECTION 12. Monitoring, Evaluation and Transparency. – The DOH shall
establish a comprehensive monitoring and evaluation system to assess the
implementation, effectiveness, and impact of this Act. It shall develop measurable
performance indicators, including transplant rates, donor rates, waiting times,
geographic equity, and patient outcomes, and shall regularly review system
performance.


      An annual report shall be submitted to Congress detailing program
implementation, fund utilization, and outcomes. The DOH shall also maintain a
publicly accessible platform containing anonymized and aggregated data to promote
transparency, accountability, and public trust in the organ transplantation system.


      SECTION 13. Penalties. – Any violation of this Act shall be subject to the
following penalties:


      (a) fines ranging from ₱100,000 to ₱5,000,000, depending on gravity;
      (b) suspension or revocation of licenses and accreditation; and
      (c) imprisonment of not less than six (6) years for serious offenses, including
      organ trafficking, coercion, or commercialization.




                                                                                      7
      Where the offender is a public official or healthcare professional, the penalty
shall include perpetual disqualification from public office or practice of profession,
in addition to criminal liability. Institutions found liable shall be subject to closure
and blacklisting from government programs.


      All complaints shall be acted upon promptly, fairly, and in accordance with
due process, with strict observance of confidentiality and data privacy laws. The
mechanism shall ensure full protection against retaliation or reprisal, whether direct
or indirect, against complainants or witnesses, and shall be supported by designated
child protection focal persons responsible for assisting in the reporting and
resolution process.


      SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulation (IRR) – Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), as the
lead implementing agency, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for
its effective implementation, in coordination with the Philippine Health Insurance
Corporation (PhilHealth), Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD), Department of Budget and Management (DBM), National Privacy
Commission (NPC), Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
Commission on Audit (COA), and other relevant government agencies and
stakeholders as may be necessary.


      SECTION 15. Separability Clause. If, for any reason, any part or provision
of this Act shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, other parts of provisions thereof
not affected shall thereby continue to be in full force and effect.

      SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. - Any law, presidential decree or issuance,
executive order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation contrary
to, or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed, modified, or
amended accordingly.


      SECTION 17. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days
following its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of
general circulation.
