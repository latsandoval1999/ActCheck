                                   Explanatory Note


       For over a century, Central Luzon State University (CLSU) has been the
forefront of quality Tertiary education within Central Luzon while greatly contributing
to the uplifting of the agricultural sector within the region.


       The University started as a farm school under the name of Central Luzon
Agricultural School (CLAS) way back in 1907 under Executive Order 10. The school’s
main activities back then involved skills training on better farming methods,
agricultural mechanics and homemaking arts. Later, the activities evolved into a model
vocational-agricultural teaching and learning program.


       On 1950, CLAS was converted into Central Luzon Agricultural College
(CLAC) by virtue of EO 393 becoming the first state institution in the country to offer
a four-year curriculum for training teachers of vocational agriculture. One of the unique
features under their program was its practicum requirement. This requirement involved
underlying concerns like “learning-by-doing” and acquisition of practical skills and
expertise. This made CLAC known as “the mother of vocational agricultural schools”
in the country.


       Fourteen years later, CLAC then became Central Luzon State University
(CLSU) by virtue of RA 4067. Currently, the University is composed of nine colleges
namely: College of Agriculture, College of Arts and Social Science, College of

Business and Accountancy, College of Education, College of Engineering, College of
Fisheries, College of Home Science and Industry, College of Veterinary Science and
Medicine, College of Science, and an Open University offering alternative, non-
residential, and ICT-mediated teaching-learning delivery system. It also boasts six
Centers of Excellence (COE) designated by the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED) namely for: Agriculture, Agricultural Engineering, Biology, Fisheries,
Teacher Education, and Veterinary Medicine.


       As of 2025, the University has also earned various awards and recognition.
Notably, it was held as The Second-Best State University in the Philippines while
earning the 153rd rank in Southeast Asia according to the QS World University
Ranking. Additionally, the University has also contributed to uplifting the agricultural
sector through its various research and technology.


       Indeed, for more than a decade, CLSU has greatly contributed to both Quality
Education and our Agricultural Sector. Hence, this bill aims to further support the
school by expanding its general mandate. The expansion would cover a wider spectrum
of fields. Additionally, it also aims to grant government funding support and priority in
the establishment of specialized laboratories and smart classrooms to further its
capacity for both research and education.


       Thus, approval of this Bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE CENTRAL LUZON STATE UNIVERSITY
                       CHARTER OF 2026


Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Central Luzon State University Charter
of 2026”.

Section 2. General Mandate. — The Central Luzon State University, hereinafter referred to as
CLSU, shall primarily provide advanced instruction, professional and technical training in the fields
of agriculture, business and accountancy, criminal justice, engineering and architecture, health,
humanities, digital and information technology, law, science and mathematics, social sciences,
teacher education and other disciplines as provided by law. It shall also deliver research, extension
and production services, and nurture and promote holistic, developmental, visionary and
transformational leadership in its areas of specialization.

Section 3. Curricular Offerings. — CLSU shall offer certificate, undergraduate, graduate and post-
graduate programs on the fields stated in Section 2 hereof to attain its objectives and to respond to
the human resource development needs of the communities it serves.

The existing laboratory schools for kindergarten, elementary education, and secondary education shall
continue to operate to address the required experiential learning courses of the students.

Section 4. Governing Board. - The governing board of CLSU shall be the Board of Regents.

Section 5. Composition of the Governing Board. - Except as provided for by law, the governing
board of CLSU shall be composed of the following:

         a. The Chairperson of CHED as Chairperson. The CHED Chairperson is authorized to
            designate a CHED Commissioner as the regular Chairperson of the Governing Board;
         b. The President of CLSU as Vice-Chairperson;
         c. The Chairperson of the Committee on Higher, Technical and Vocational Education of
            the Senate of the Philippines as Member;
         d. The Chairperson of the Committee on Higher and Technical Education of the House of
            Representatives as Member;

Section 6. Rights and Responsibilities of Governing Board Members. - The governing board
Chairperson, Vice Chairperson, and government officials who are governing board members in an ex-
officio capacity shall perform their functions and duties upon their assumption to office.

The membership of the faculty, alumni and student representatives to the governing board shall
commence immediately upon assumption to office and shall remain effective during the period of their
incumbency as heads of their respective sectoral associations.

Section 7. Representatives of Governing Board Members Coming from the Government. - If the
Chairperson of the Senate Committee on Higher, Technical and Vocational Education, and the
Chairperson of the House Committee on Higher and Technical Education cannot personally attend
any meeting of the governing board, they may designate in writing any of the following to attend in
their stead:

     a.   The Vice-Chairperson of the Committee;
     b.   A Senator or Member of the House of Representatives who is a member of the Committee;
     c.   Any Senator or Member of the House of Representatives;
     d.   The Secretary or Assistant Secretary of the Committee; or
     e.   A member of the legislative staff of the Chair of either chamber.

Such written authority should be presented to the Board Secretary before the start of the meeting. After
such submission, the representative shall be entitled to the rights and powers of any regular member
of the governing board, unless the principal provides otherwise in writing. In case of a special session
for the election of CLSU President, the written authority should specify the authority to attend and
vote on their behalf. As the official representative of the Senate/House Committee Chair, the decision
of his/her representative shall be considered to be the decision of the principal.

Section 8. Search, Qualification, and Selection of the Two (2) Private Sector Representatives
(PSR). – The search for private sector representatives shall be governed by the existing guidelines
prescribed by law.

Section 9. Filing of Report by the University President. – On or before the fifteenth day of the
second month after the opening of regular classes of every academic year, the CLSU President shall
file with the Office of the President of the Philippines, through the Office of the CHED Chairperson
or the CHED Commissioner designated as the Chairperson of the Governing Board, and with the
respective Committee Chairpersons of the Senate and the House of Representatives, a detailed report
on the progress, conditions, and needs of the University.

Section 10. Terms of Office. - The terms of office of the faculty, alumni and student regents in the
governing board of CLSU shall be coterminous with their respective terms of office as
President/Chairperson of their respective sectoral associations.

In case of graduation, resignation, retirement, transfer, expulsion, removal for cause, permanent
physical or mental incapacity, death or other analogous causes, whichever is applicable to the student,
faculty, or alumni regent, they shall be represented by their respective duly-elected next-in-rank
officer. The next-in-rank officer shall sit as a regular member of the governing board and shall serve
only the unexpired term of his/her predecessor.

Section 11. Meetings. - The governing board of CLSU may hold regular meetings or special meetings.

  11.1. Regular Meetings. - The governing board of CLSU must regularly convene at least once
        every quarter.

  11.2. Special meetings. - Special sessions of the governing board of CLSU may only be held twice
         per year, upon the call of its Chairperson, provided that all the members of the Board shall
         be duly notified in writing of a special meeting three (3) days before it is held. A governing
         board member may propose for the conduct of a special meeting through a written request
         to the CLSU governing board Chairperson.

  11.3. Frequency, Date, and Venue of Regular and/or Special Meetings. - The frequency over
         and above the minimum stated in Section 11.1 and 11.2, and the date and venue of regular
         meetings of the governing board shall be determined by the governing board itself, provided
         that the members shall receive the authorized allowances in only four (4) regular meetings
         and two (2) special meetings per year.

Section 12. Quorum. - A majority of all the members of the governing board of CLSU holding office
at the time of its regular meeting or special meeting shall constitute a quorum.

No regular meeting or special session of the governing board of CLSU, however, shall be validly held
without the presence of the CLSU governing board Chairperson. The governing board Chairperson
may, however, waive his/her participation in writing as may be warranted by the situation. In such
case, the CLSU President, who is the governing board Vice-Chairperson, shall preside over the regular
meeting or special meeting.

Section 13. Presiding Officer. - The CHED Chairperson shall be the presiding officer of the regular
meetings and special meetings of the governing board as its Chairperson, except as otherwise provided
herein.

The CHED Chairperson may designate in writing a CHED Commissioner to act as the regular
Chairperson of the governing board of CLSU, until revoked or modified. In such an event, the CHED
Commissioner shall assume all the powers of CHED Chairperson in CLSU, and shall be referred to
as the CLSU governing board Chairperson.

In the absence of the governing board Chairperson and upon his/her authority, the governing board
Vice-Chairperson shall preside over the regular meetings and special meetings of the governing board,
except as hereinafter provided.

Section 14. Representative of the CHED Chairperson. - In case the CHED Chairperson is unable

The representative of the CHED Chairperson so assigned shall not preside over the regular meeting

Section 15. No Compensation for the Chairperson, Vice Chairperson, and Members of the
Governing Board. The Governing Board shall serve without compensation, but they shall be
reimbursed for necessary expenses such as, but not limited to, airfare, accommodation, swab testing,
the amount of which will be based on existing government issuances incurred in their attendance in
the governing board meetings or in connection with the conduct of official business authorized by the
resolution of the governing board and per diem, the amount of which will be as follows, unless
changed or modified by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) Circular: Board
Chairperson are entitled to an amount equivalent to 25% of the monthly representation and
transportation allowance of the CLSU President, for every meeting actually attended, but not to exceed
four (4) paid meetings per month. Members of governing boards are entitled to an amount equivalent
to 25% of the monthly representation and transportation allowance of the CLSU Vice President, for
every meeting actually attended but not to exceed four (4) paid meetings per month.

Section 17. Promulgation and Implementation of Policies by the Governing Board. - The
governing board of CLSU may promulgate and implement policies, provided these are consistent
with the declared state policies on education and other pertinent provisions of the Constitution of the
Philippines on education, science and technology, arts, culture and sports, and with the policies,
standards and thrusts of the CHED.

appoint their Secretary who shall serve as such for the Board.

The Secretary so appointed shall, among others, keep all the records and the minutes of the proceedings
of the governing board and shall communicate to the governing board Chairperson, Vice Chairperson,

 Section 19. Treasurer of the Philippines. - The Treasurer of the Philippines shall be the ex-officio
treasurer of the University.

Section 20. Audit. - All accounts and expenses of CLSU shall be audited by the COA or its duly
authorized representative.

Section 21. Administrative Council. - There shall be an Administrative Council consisting of the
President of CLSU as Chairperson, and its Vice Presidents, Deans, Directors, and other officials of
equal rank, including the faculty and student regents, as members.

Section 22. Duties of the Administrative Council. - The Administrative Council of CLSU so formed
and constituted shall review and recommend to the governing board policies governing the
administration, management, and development planning of the University.

Section 23. Academic Council. - There shall be an Academic Council consisting of the President of
CLSU as Chairperson, and all personnel with academic rank of not lower than Assistant Professor, as
Members.

Section 24. Powers of the Academic Council. - The Academic Council so formed and constituted
shall have the following powers, in accordance with the Implementing Rules and Regulations of
Republic Act No. 8292 to wit:

   a. Determine, review, and recommend for the approval of its governing board, the course
      offerings of the University; and

   b. Draft, review, and recommend for the approval of the governing board, rules on student
      discipline of CLSU. It shall fix the requirements for the admission of students as well as for
      their graduation and conferment of degrees, and submit such rules to the governing board for
      review and approval in accordance with Republic Act No. 8292 and its Implementing Rules
      and Regulations.

Section 25. The University President. - CLSU shall be headed by a President who shall be
elected by the governing board subject to its guidelines on qualifications and standards, and based
on the recommendation of a search committee duly constituted for the purpose of selecting the
President. The CLSU President shall render full-time service, serve a term of four (4) years, and may
be reappointed to another term subject to the policies and guidelines implemented by the CHED.

Within six (6) months before the expiration of the term of office of the incumbent President of
CLSU, the governing board shall constitute the Search Committee for Presidency.

The powers and duties of the President of CLSU, in addition to those specifically provided in this
Act, shall be those usually pertaining to the Office of the President of universities and those delegated
by the Board.

The salary of the President of CLSU shall be in accordance with the law.

In case of a vacancy in the Office of the President by reason of death, compulsory retirement,
resignation, removal for cause, or incapacity of the President to perform the functions of the
office, the governing board shall designate an Officer-in-Charge pending the appointment of a
new President: Provided, that the Officer-in-Charge shall serve only during the unexpired portion
of the term.

Section 26. The Faculty Members. - No political belief, gender preference, cultural or
community affiliation or ethnic origin, nor religious opinion or affiliation shall be a matter of
inquiry in the appointment of faculty members of CLSU; Provided, that said appointment shall
be subject to the guidelines, qualifications, and standards set by the government: Provided further,
t h a t no member of the faculty shall teach for or against any particular church or religious sect.

Section 27. Academic Freedom and Institutional Autonomy. - CLSU shall enjoy academic freedom
and institutional autonomy pursuant to Paragraph 2, Section 5 of Article XIV of the Constitution.

Section 28. Scholarship Programs/Admission - CLSU shall provide scholarship and other
affirmative action programs to assist poor but deserving students who qualify for admission in the
University.

No student shall be denied admission in CLSU by reason of gender, religion, cultural or community
affiliation, physical disability or ethnic origin.

Section 29. Establishment of Specialized Laboratories and Smart Classrooms. - The government
shall prioritize the establishment of specialized laboratories in furtherance of the mandate of the
specialized centers, and construction of smart classrooms to support the instruction function of the
University, thereby effecting an effective mode of transfer of knowledge products to students and

Section 30. Authority to Loan or Transfer Apparatus, Equipment, Supplies and Detail of
Personnel. - Upon the request of the President of CLSU, the heads of the bureaus and offices of
the national government may loan or transfer to CLSU such apparatus, equipment or supplies as
may be needed by it, and detail employees for duty therein when, in the judgment of the head of
bureau or office, the apparatus, equipment, supplies or service of such employees can be spared
without serious detriment to public service.

The employees so detailed shall perform such duties as required of them by the President of CLSU
and the time thus served shall be counted as part of their regular service.

Section 31. Tax Exemption on Importation and Donations. - The importation by the CLSU
of technical, educational and cultural materials including apparatus and laboratory equipment which
are for technical, vocational, educational, philosophical, historical or cultural purposes shall, upon
certification by the appropriate authority, be exempt from customs duties in accordance with the
provisions of relevant laws.

All grants, bequests, endowments, donations and contributions made to and used actually, directly
and exclusively by the CLSU, shall be exempt from donor's tax and the same shall be considered
as allowable deduction from the gross income of the donor in accordance with the provisions of the
law.

Section 33. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

Section 34. Repealing Clause. – All legal issuances contrary to or inconsistent with this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

Section 35. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect upon its approval.
