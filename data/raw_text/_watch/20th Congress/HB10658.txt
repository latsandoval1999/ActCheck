13                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE
14
15   Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution declares that “the State shall protect and
16   promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among
17   them.” Consistent with this constitutional mandate, Republic Act No. 11223,
18   otherwise known as the “Universal Health Care Act,” seeks to ensure that every
19   Filipino has access to a comprehensive range of quality and cost-effective promotive,
20   preventive, curative, rehabilitative, and palliative health services without suffering
21   financial hardship, with particular attention to those who are least able to afford the
22   cost of medical care.
23
24   Despite continuing reforms in health financing, increased PhilHealth expenditures,
25   and the availability of other sources of medical assistance, many Filipinos still face
26   serious financial barriers when illness strikes. For indigent and financially
27   incapacitated patients, even clinically necessary treatment may remain beyond reach
28   when hospital charges, medicines, diagnostic procedures, medical supplies, and other
29   prescribed interventions exceed the amounts covered by PhilHealth benefit packages,
30   case rates, or other available funding sources.
31
32   The Medical Assistance to Indigent and Financially Incapacitated Patients (MAIFIP)
33   Program has become an important means of addressing this gap. It provides
34   assistance for clinically indicated needs prescribed by a physician or other qualified
35   health professional for both in-patients and out-patients who are unable to fully
36   shoulder the cost of their medical care. In practical terms, the program serves as an
37   essential final layer of financial protection for patients whose remaining hospital and
38   medical expenses cannot otherwise be met. At present, however, the continued
39   operation of such assistance should not rest solely on administrative arrangements,
40   annual budgetary provisions, or changing implementation practices. A program upon
41   which vulnerable patients increasingly depend deserves a clear, stable, and
42   permanent legal framework.
43




                                                   1
 1   This measure therefore seeks to institutionalize the MAIFIP Program by establishing
 2   in law its purpose, coverage, basic processes, and standards for implementation.
 3   Institutionalization will provide continuity in the delivery of assistance, greater
 4   predictability for patients and health facilities, and clearer accountability among the
 5   agencies and institutions tasked with administering the program.
 6
 7   More importantly, placing the program on a statutory footing will help ensure that
 8   access to medical assistance is governed by an orderly and transparent process rather
 9   than by uncertainty as to whether assistance will be available, what expenses may be
10   covered, or how applications will be processed. A patient already burdened by illness
11   should not also have to navigate an unclear or inconsistent system merely to secure
12   assistance for medically necessary care.
13
14   The measure is intended to complement, rather than replace, PhilHealth and other
15   existing sources of health financing. MAIFIP assistance shall apply to clinically
16   indicated expenses that remain after the application of applicable PhilHealth
17   benefits, case rates, and other available funding sources. In this manner, the program
18   serves as a necessary safety net within the broader framework of Universal Health
19   Care.
20
21   Institutionalizing MAIFIP is ultimately about ensuring continuity, consistency, and
22   dignity in the delivery of medical assistance. It affirms that when public policy
23   recognizes the need to assist indigent and financially incapacitated patients, the
24   process for extending that assistance should likewise be dependable, accessible, and
25   firmly established in law.
26
27   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

14                            AN ACT
15   INSTITUTIONALIZING THE MEDICAL ASSISTANCE TO INDIGENT AND
16   FINANCIALLY INCAPACITATED PATIENTS (MAIFIP) PROGRAM,
17   APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
18
19   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
20   Congress assembled:
21
22   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Medical Assistance to
23   Indigent and Financially Incapacitated Patients (MAIP) Act."
24
25   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to protect
26   and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among
27   them. The State shall also improve the adequate delivery of health care services to the
28   people and ensure its availability and accessibility.
29
30   SECTION 3. Medical Assistance to Indigent Patients (MAIFIP) Program. – The
31   Department of Health (DOH) shall establish a “Medical Assistance to Indigent and
32   Financially Incapacitated Patients (MAIFIP) Program” which shall provide medical
33   assistance to support clinically-indicated needs prescribed by a physician or health
34   professional for indigent and financially incapacitated in-patients and out-patients.
35   This assistance applies to expenses exceeding the applicable PhilHealth benefit
36   packages, case rates, or other available funding sources.
37
38   The MAIFIP Program shall continue to be available and accessible in all DOH central
39   and regional offices, BARMM Ministry of Health, specialty hospitals, DOH hospitals
40   and treatment and rehabilitation centers, including hospitals under the Department
41   of National Defense (DND) and the Department of Interior and Local Government
42   (DILG), State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) hospitals and Department of Justice
43   (DOJ) infirmaries, local government unit (LGU) hospitals, and other partner
44   government and privately-owned hospitals and health facilities.
45




                                                 3
 1   SECTION 4. Qualified Beneficiaries. – The qualified beneficiaries of the MAIFIP
 2   Program shall refer to indigent and financially incapacitated patients who shall
 3   demonstrate clear inability to pay or spend for necessary expenditures for one’s
 4   medical treatment, such as, but not limited to patients with catastrophic illness or any
 5   illness, which is life or limb-threatening and requires prolonged hospitalization,
 6   extremely expensive therapies or other special but essential care that would deplete
 7   one’s financial resources, as assessed and certified by the Medical Social Worker
 8   (MSW).
 9
10   Provided that, patients availing of medical assistance or their authorized
11   representatives through the MAIFIP Program in DOH hospitals, other government
12   hospitals and privately-owned hospitals and health facilities shall present documents
13   and undergo proper assessment and evaluation by the MSW or their designated
14   officer. Referrals may also be issued by the DOH Central Office and/or DOH Centers
15   for Health Development (CHDs), subject to the standard screening and evaluation
16   process conducted by the health facility's MSW.
17
18   The registered MSW of the health facility shall assess the eligibility of patients
19   applying for medical assistance based on their existing assessment standards. Health
20   facilities shall judiciously enforce a thorough screening of beneficiaries to ensure the
21   efficient and rational use of funds, diligent documentation, and data encoding
22   through the information system designated by the DOH.
23
24   SECTION 5. Coverage. – The MAIFIP Program shall cover medicines, services, and
25   other medical products as prescribed by a licensed physician or health professional,
26   such as, but not limited to, the following:
27
28   (a) Drugs and medicines, as approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA);
29
30   (b) Laboratory, imaging, radiological, and other diagnostic procedures, including
31   assessment/readers' fees;
32
33   (c) Blood and other related blood screening/products;
34
35   (d) Clinically indicated medical-surgical cases, obstetrics-gynecological cases
36   considered as high-risk: cases requiring implants, medical devices, and supplies, and
37   other relevant procedures;
38
39   (e) Clinically indicated dental cases, including routine preventive oral care, subject to
40   the guidelines issued by the DOH;
41
42   (f) Dialysis sessions exceeding the coverage and package rates provided by
43   PhilHealth;
44
45   (g) Prescribed post-hospitalization, rehabilitation services, aftercare program, and
46   appropriate mental and psychosocial support;
47



                                                  4
 1   (h) All hospital bills or charges after all applicable and available deductions; and
 2
 3   (i) Professional fees (PF), subject to the guidelines issued by the DOH.
 4
 5   SECTION 6. Program Management. – The DOH shall be responsible for the overall
 6   management and administration of the MAIFIP Program.
 7
 8   SECTION 7. Order of Charging. – The DOH shall establish an order of charging for
 9   available government and private assistance. Hospitals and other partner health
10   facilities shall ensure that double charging of medical assistance funds is strictly
11   prohibited.
12
13   SECTION 8. Downloading of Funds. – The DOH shall ensure that the MAIFIP
14   Program funds appropriated under the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA)
15   shall be available in all DOH hospitals and specialty hospitals at the beginning of
16   every fiscal year and replenish such funds, as necessary, to ensure continuous and
17   uninterrupted assistance to patients through the MAIFIP Program.
18
19   The DOH, through its CHDs, shall enter into a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA)
20   with LGU hospitals, and other health facilities, both private and public, such as, but
21   not limited to, other hospitals not operated by the DOH, clinical laboratories and
22   primary care facilities, dental clinics, and free-standing dialysis clinics for the
23   availability and transfer of funds of the MAIFIP Program in the health facility.
24
25   SECTION 9. Payment Mechanism. – All hospitals and other health facilities under
26   the MAIFIP Program shall receive and accept letters of guarantee from the DOH as
27   payment for the medical expenses of the beneficiaries. Provided that, the DOH shall
28   remit payment to the health facility to cover the amount of the issued letter of
29   guarantee not later than fifteen (15) days from the availment of medicines and
30   services or upon discharge.
31
32   SECTION 10. Monitoring and Reporting. – All health facilities under the MAIFIP
33   Program shall submit their respective monthly Fund Utilization Reports (FURs) to
34   the DOH. The DOH shall submit to the Senate Committee on Health and
35   Demography, and the House of Representatives Committee on Health, on or before
36   the end of December of every year, or upon the request of any of the aforesaid offices,
37   a report giving a detailed account of the status of the implementation of this Act.
38
39   SECTION 11. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement the provisions
40   of this Act shall be included in the budget of the DOH under the assistance to patients
41   through the MAIFIP Program.
42
43   SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DOH shall promulgate
44   the necessary implementing rules and regulations within ninety (90) days from the
45   effectivity of this Act.
46

 2   unconstitutional, the provisions not otherwise affected shall remain valid and
 3   subsisting.
 4
 5   SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, rules or regulations,
