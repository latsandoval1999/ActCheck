                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Public school teachers play a vital role in educating the Filipino youth and
advancing national development. Many teachers, however, continue to face difficulties in
securing decent, safe, affordable, and accessible housing, particularly those assigned to
remote, underserved, and geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

       This measure seeks to establish a Public School Teachers’ Housing Program that
will provide qualified teachers with access to government-built and near-site housing,
subsidized housing loans, rent-to-own arrangements, down-payment and rental
assistance, housing construction financing, and other appropriate housing assistance.

      The bill also creates a Public School Teachers’ Housing Fund and a Public School
Teachers’ Housing Program Council to ensure coordinated implementation, sustainable
financing, transparent beneficiary selection, and proper monitoring of the Program.

       By providing teachers with affordable and secure housing, the State can
strengthen their economic security and welfare while helping ensure that they remain
able to serve effectively in schools and communities throughout the country.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                            “AN ACT
      INSTITUTING A COMPREHENSIVE HOUSING PROGRAM FOR
      PUBLIC SCHOOL TEACHERS, PROVIDING FOR GOVERNMENT-
      BUILT HOUSING, SUBSIDIZED HOUSING FINANCING, RENT-
      TO-OWN AND OTHER HOUSING ASSISTANCE PROGRAMS,
      CREATING A PUBLIC SCHOOL TEACHERS' HOUSING FUND,
      PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Public
School Teachers’ Housing Program Act.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to
recognize and promote the vital role of public school teachers in national
development and to protect and enhance their welfare, dignity, economic
security, and quality of life.

      Towards this end, the State shall establish a comprehensive,
sustainable, affordable, and accessible housing program for qualified public
school teachers, particularly those who do not own adequate housing, those
assigned in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, remote or
underserved communities, and those who experience significant housing
affordability constraints.

      The Program shall be implemented consistent with Republic Act No.
11201, or the “Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development
Act,” Republic Act No. 12216, or the “National Housing Authority Act,”
Republic Act No. 7279, as amended, including by Republic Act No. 10884,
and Republic Act No. 9679, or the “Home Development Mutual Fund Law of
2009,” and other applicable housing, land use, budgeting, procurement,
taxation, auditing, and public financial management laws, rules, and
regulations.
      SECTION 3. Establishment of the Public School Teachers’
Housing Program. – There is hereby established a Public School Teachers’
Housing Program, hereinafter referred to as the “Program”, which shall
provide qualified public school teachers with access to decent, safe,
affordable, and adequately serviced housing.

      The Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development
(DHSUD) shall serve as the lead government agency for housing policy,
planning, coordination, regulation, and monitoring of the Program,
consistent with Republic Act No. 11201.

       The Department of Education (DepEd) shall identify and validate
qualified teacher-beneficiaries and assist in determining their housing
needs.

      The National Housing Authority (NHA) shall undertake housing
development and other housing operations under the Program consistent
with Republic Act No. 12216 and the policies and standards of the DHSUD.

     The Home Development Mutual Fund (Pag-IBIG Fund) shall provide
and administer housing financing under the Program consistent with
Republic Act No. 9679, its charter, and applicable rules and regulations.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to qualified public
school teachers employed in public elementary, junior high school, and
senior high school institutions under the jurisdiction of the Department of
Education (DepEd)s.

       For purposes of this Act, a qualified public school teacher shall refer
to a teacher who:

      (a) is a Filipino citizen;
      (b) holds a permanent or regular appointment as a public school
          teacher under the Department of Education;
      (c) meets the applicable income, housing, financing, and other
          eligibility requirements prescribed under this Act and its
          implementing rules and regulations; and
      (d) has not been determined to have committed fraud or
          misrepresentation in connection with any government housing
          program.



       The implementing rules and regulations may provide appropriate
eligibility requirements for newly appointed permanent teachers and other
teacher categories when warranted by the availability of funds and housing
units.

      SECTION 5. Priority Beneficiaries. - In the allocation of housing
units, financing assistance, subsidies, and other benefits under the
Program, priority shall be determined primarily on the basis of
demonstrated housing need, with particular consideration given to qualified
teachers who:

      (a) do not own a house and lot or other adequate residential property
          suitable for their family’s needs;
      (b) are assigned to geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas
          (GIDAs), remote, underserved, or hard-to-reach communities;
      (c) are assigned to schools where adequate rental or housing facilities
          are not reasonably available;
      (d) belong to low-income or economically vulnerable households;
      (e) are paying rent that constitutes a substantial portion of household
          income;
      (f) have served the public school system for a longer period, which
          may be considered as a secondary prioritization factor where
          applicants have substantially similar housing needs;
      (g) have dependents or family members whose housing needs are not
          otherwise adequately provided for; or
      (h) are otherwise determined by the Program Council to have
          demonstrated substantial housing need.

      No qualified beneficiary shall be denied access solely on the ground
that the teacher is assigned outside his or her place of permanent residence,
provided that all other eligibility requirements are satisfied.

      SECTION 6. Components of the Program. - The Program shall
include, but shall not be limited to, the following:

      (a) Government-Built Housing Projects. - The NHA, in coordination
          with DHSUD and DepEd, shall identify, acquire, develop, or utilize
          suitable government-owned or otherwise legally available land for
          the construction of housing units, subdivisions, medium-rise
          buildings, condominium-type developments, or other appropriate
          residential projects for qualified teacher-beneficiaries.

          Priority shall be given to sites that are reasonably accessible to
          public schools, employment centers, transportation, health
          facilities, markets, water supply, electricity, sanitation, and other
          essential public services.
      (b) Near-Site and Strategic Teacher Housing. - Where appropriate and
          legally feasible, housing projects may be established near public
        schools or within reasonable commuting distance thereof,
        particularly in areas where teachers face significant transportation
        or housing difficulties.

     (c) Subsidized Housing Loans. - The Pag-IBIG Fund shall establish or
         maintain a dedicated housing financing window for qualified public
         school teachers, subject to its charter, applicable laws, actuarial
         requirements, and implementing regulations.

      Subject to the availability of appropriated funds, the Government may
provide an interest-rate subsidy or other financing support to qualified
beneficiaries to enable them to obtain housing loans at a preferential
interest rate of not more than three percent (3%) per annum, subject to
applicable loan ceilings, repayment capacity, and program guidelines.

     (d) Housing Construction and Improvement Loans. - Qualified
         beneficiaries who already own a legally acceptable residential lot
         may be provided access to subsidized financing for the
         construction, completion, repair, or substantial improvement of a
         dwelling.

     (e) Rent-to-Own Housing. - Government-built housing units may be
         offered under a rent-to-own arrangement for qualified beneficiaries
         who cannot immediately meet the required equity or down
         payment.

      Under such arrangement, a portion of rental payments may, as
determined under the implementing rules and regulations, be credited
toward the acquisition price of the housing unit.

     (f) Down-Payment and Equity Assistance. - Subject to the availability
         of appropriations, qualified low-income beneficiaries may be
         provided assistance for the required equity, down payment, or
         other initial housing costs.

     (g) Rental Assistance. – Subject to available funds and implementing
         guidelines, temporary rental assistance may be provided to
         qualified teachers assigned to GIDAs, remote areas, high-cost
         areas, or locations where adequate affordable housing is not
         reasonably available.

     (h) Acquisition of Existing Housing Units. - The Program may provide
         financing assistance for the acquisition of existing residential units
         that comply with applicable standards and requirements.
      (i) Public-Private Partnerships. - The Government may enter into
          appropriate public-private partnerships with qualified developers,
          housing cooperatives, financial institutions, and other private
          entities for the development, financing, construction, or
          management of housing projects under the Program.

      (j) Other Housing Assistance. - Other appropriate housing assistance
          mechanisms authorized by law may be established by the Program
          Council to respond to local housing conditions and the particular
          needs of qualified teacher-beneficiaries.

      SECTION 7. Government Land for the Program. - The DHSUD, in
coordination with the NHA, DepEd, and other concerned government
agencies, shall conduct an inventory and assessment of government-owned
lands that may legally and suitably be utilized for the Program.

      Government land may be utilized for the Program only when:

      (a) it is legally available or may lawfully be made available for housing
          purposes;
      (b) it is not required for essential government operations or public
          purposes;
      (c) its use is consistent with applicable land use, zoning,
          environmental, safety, and building regulations;

      (d) it is suitable for residential development or can reasonably be
          made suitable; and

      (e) the necessary authority, transfer, lease, usufruct, or other legal
          arrangement for its use has been secured.

       The utilization, transfer, lease, usufruct, disposition, or development
of government-owned lands under this Section shall remain subject to
existing laws, rules, regulations, and constitutional requirements governing
government property.

      SECTION 8. Teacher Housing Projects and Standards. - All housing
projects established under this Act shall, to the extent practicable, provide:

      (a) safe and structurally sound housing units;
      (b) access to potable water and adequate sanitation;
      (c) electricity and appropriate drainage;
      (d) roads and transportation access;
      (e) adequate open and community spaces;
      (f) access to schools, health facilities, markets, and other essential
          services; and
      (g) resilience measures appropriate to the area's exposure to natural
          hazards, including flooding, earthquakes, typhoons, landslides,
          and other disasters.

     Housing units shall comply with applicable national and local
housing, building, environmental, fire safety, and accessibility standards.

      SECTION 9. Preferential Housing Financing and Interest Subsidy.
- To make housing financing affordable to qualified public school teachers,
the Government shall provide an interest-rate subsidy to qualified loans
granted under the Program.

       The subsidy shall be designed to enable qualified beneficiaries to
obtain financing at a preferential rate of not more than three percent (3%)
per annum, subject to the availability of appropriations and such loan
ceilings, income limits, repayment periods, and other conditions as may be
prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations.

      The interest-rate subsidy shall cover only the amount necessary to
reduce the applicable interest rate to the preferential rate prescribed under
this Section and shall not constitute a waiver of principal, fees, insurance
premiums, taxes, or other lawful charges unless expressly authorized.

      The subsidy shall be funded from appropriations specifically
authorized for the purpose and shall be administered in a manner that does
not impair the financial sustainability, actuarial soundness, or statutory
investment requirements of the Pag-IBIG Fund.

      The subsidy shall be administered in a transparent manner and shall
not impair the financial sustainability of the Pag-IBIG Fund.

       The maximum repayment period for qualified housing loans under the
Program may be extended to thirty (30) years, subject to the applicable rules
of the Pag-IBIG Fund and the borrower's capacity to pay.

      SECTION 10. Rent-to-Own Program. - The NHA, in coordination
with DHSUD, DepEd, and Pag-IBIG Fund, shall establish a rent-to-own
mechanism for qualified teacher-beneficiaries occupying housing units
developed under the Program.

      The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe:

      (a) the rental rate;
      (b) the purchase price and valuation of the housing unit;
      (c) the portion of rental payments that may be credited toward the
          purchase price;
      (d) the maximum rental period before exercise of the purchase option;
      (e) maintenance and utility responsibilities;
      (f) consequences of default;
      (g) succession or transfer rules in cases of death or permanent
          incapacity; and
      (h) other terms necessary to protect both the Government and the
          beneficiary.

      SECTION 11. Private Sector Participation and Incentives. -
Qualified private developers, contractors, financial institutions, housing
cooperatives, and other private entities may participate in the Program
through public-private partnership arrangements, joint ventures, housing
development agreements, financing arrangements, or other lawful
mechanisms.

       Housing projects that qualify as socialized housing under Republic
Act No. 7279, as amended by Republic Act No. 10884, may avail of the
incentives and benefits provided under such laws, subject to the applicable
qualification, certification, development, and compliance requirements.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed as granting a tax exemption or
incentive broader than that authorized under existing tax and housing laws.

      SECTION 12. Public School Teachers’ Housing Fund. - There is
hereby created a Public School Teachers’ Housing Fund, hereinafter referred
to as the “Housing Fund,” which shall be used exclusively to support the
implementation of this Act.

      The Housing Fund shall be maintained as a special account in the
General Fund, unless otherwise authorized by law, and shall be
administered in accordance with existing budgeting, accounting, auditing,
procurement, and public financial management laws, rules, and
regulations.

      The Housing Fund may be used for:

      (a) site development and housing construction;
      (b) interest-rate subsidies;
      (c) down-payment and equity assistance;
      (d) rent-to-own subsidies;
      (e) rental assistance for qualified beneficiaries;
      (f) housing-related infrastructure;
      (g) feasibility studies, planning, design, and technical assistance;
      (h) monitoring and evaluation;
      (i) assistance for housing cooperatives and community-based housing
          initiatives; and
      (j) other expenses directly necessary for the implementation of the
          Program.

     The Housing Fund shall be administered in accordance with existing
budgeting, accounting, auditing, procurement, and public financial
management laws, rules, and regulations.

       SECTION 13. Sources of the Housing Fund. - The Housing Fund
shall consist of:

      (a) appropriations authorized under the General Appropriations Act;
      (b) appropriations specifically authorized by subsequent laws;
      (c) grants, donations, and contributions from domestic or foreign
          sources, subject to existing laws and government accounting and
          auditing rules;
      (d) income or proceeds from Program activities, to the extent
          authorized by law and subject to existing budgeting, accounting,
          and auditing laws, rules, and regulations;
      (e) contributions from participating government agencies, subject to
          existing laws; and
      (f) such other sources as may be authorized by law.

       SECTION 14. Public School Teachers’ Housing Program Council. –
There is hereby created a Public School Teachers’ Housing Program Council,
hereinafter referred to as the “Program Council,” which shall provide policy
direction and inter-agency coordination for the implementation of this Act.

      The Program Council shall be composed of:

      (a) the Secretary of Human Settlements and Urban Development, as
          Chairperson;

      (b) the Secretary of Education, as Vice Chairperson;

      (c)   the General Manager of the National Housing Authority;

      (d) the President of the Home Development Mutual Fund or his or
          her authorized representative;

      (e)   the Secretary of Budget and Management or his or her authorized
            representative;

      (f)   the Secretary of Finance or his or her authorized representative;

      (g)   one (1) representative from the Department of Public Works and
            Highways;
      (h) one (1) representative from the local government sector; and

      (i)   two (2) representatives of recognized national organizations of
            public school teachers, who shall be designated by the Secretary
            of Education in consultation with such organizations.

       The Program Council may invite representatives of other government
agencies, local government units, housing cooperatives, financial
institutions, private developers, and other stakeholders when necessary.

      Members of the Program Council shall serve without additional
compensation, except for reasonable actual and necessary expenses
incurred in the performance of their official functions, subject to existing
accounting and auditing rules.

      SECTION 15. Powers and Functions of the Program Council. - The
Program Council shall:

      (a) formulate the national policy and strategic framework for the
          Program;

      (b) establish uniform eligibility and prioritization standards;

      (c) determine annual housing targets and geographic priorities;

      (d) coordinate the identification and development of suitable housing
          sites;

      (e) establish mechanisms for the efficient administration of the
          Housing Fund;

      (f) coordinate housing financing and interest subsidies with the Pag-
          IBIG Fund;

      (g) develop standards for rent-to-own, rental assistance, and other
          housing programs;

      (h) facilitate public-private partnerships;

      (i) establish a centralized monitoring and beneficiary information
          system, subject to applicable data privacy laws;

      (j) prevent duplication of housing assistance and ensure equitable
          allocation of benefits;
         (k) monitor program implementation and utilization of funds; and

         (l) perform such other functions as may be necessary to accomplish
             the objectives of this Act.

     SECTION 16. Role of the Department of Education. - The
Department of Education shall:

         (a) establish and maintain a database of qualified            teacher-
             beneficiaries, subject to applicable data privacy laws;

         (b) validate the employment status and service records of applicants;

         (c) assist in determining the housing needs of public school teachers;

         (d) identify schools and areas where teachers experience significant
             housing shortages or difficulties;

         (e) coordinate with DHSUD, NHA, Pag-IBIG Fund,              and    local
             government units in implementing the Program; and

         (f) perform such other functions as may be necessary under this Act
             and its implementing rules and regulations.

     SECTION 17. Role of the Department of Human Settlements and
Urban Development. - The DHSUD shall:

         (a) lead national housing policy planning, coordination, regulation,
             and monitoring coordination for the Program;
         (b) coordinate the identification of suitable housing sites;
         (c) establish appropriate housing and urban development standards;
         (d) facilitate coordination with local government units;
         (e) monitor compliance of housing projects with applicable housing
             laws and standards; and
         (f) perform such other functions as may be necessary for the
             implementation of this Act.

         SECTION 18. Role of the National Housing Authority. - The NHA
shall:

         (a) undertake or participate in the planning, development,
             construction, disposition, and management of government housing
             projects under the Program, consistent with Republic Act No.
             12216 and policies and standards prescribed by the DHSUD;

         (b) assist in the development of suitable government-owned land;
      (c) implement approved housing        projects   in   accordance   with
          applicable laws and standards;

      (d) coordinate with local government units            concerning   site
          development and community facilities; and

      (e) perform such other functions as may be necessary under this Act.

      SECTION 19. Role of the Pag-IBIG Fund. - The Pag-IBIG Fund shall:

      (a) establish a dedicated financing window for qualified teacher-
          beneficiaries, consistent with Republic Act No. 9679;

      (b) process and administer qualified housing loans under the
          Program;

      (c) coordinate   with   the   Government    concerning    interest-rate
          subsidies;

      (d) provide financing for the acquisition, construction, completion,
          repair, or improvement of qualified housing;

      (e) establish appropriate loan products for rent-to-own and other
          Program components, subject to its charter and applicable laws;
          and

      (f) perform such other functions as may be authorized by law and
          necessary to implement the financing provisions of this Act.

      SECTION 20. Local Government Unit Participation. - Local
government units may participate in the Program through:

      (a) identification and provision of suitable land, subject to law;
      (b) assistance in securing necessary permits and clearances;
      (c) provision or facilitation of basic infrastructure and utilities;
      (d) provision of local housing and community development support;
      (e) coordination with national government housing programs; and
      (f) other appropriate forms of assistance authorized by law.

      Local government units may enter into agreements with the national
government and other Program partners for the implementation of teacher
housing projects.

       SECTION 21. Beneficiary Selection and Transparency. - The
selection of beneficiaries shall be based on transparent, objective, and non-
discriminatory criteria established under this Act and its implementing
rules and regulations.

     The Program Council shall establish mechanisms to ensure that:

     (a) applicants are informed of eligibility requirements and selection
         criteria;

     (b) applications are processed within reasonable periods;

     (c) beneficiary lists are subject to appropriate verification;

     (d) fraudulent applications and multiple availment of substantially
         similar government housing benefits are prevented; and

     (e) qualified applicants may file an appeal or request            for
         reconsideration in accordance with prescribed procedures.

       SECTION 22. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Annual Report. - The
Program Council shall establish a system for the continuous monitoring and
evaluation of the Program. Within ninety (90) days after the end of every
fiscal year, the Program Council shall submit to the President of the
Philippines, the Senate, and the House of Representatives an annual report
containing, among others:

     (a) the number and location of housing units completed and occupied;
     (b) the number of teacher-beneficiaries served;
     (c) the number and amount of housing loans approved and released;
     (d) the amount of interest subsidies and other assistance provided;
     (e) the number of rent-to-own beneficiaries;
     (f) the number of teachers benefiting from rental or down-payment
         assistance;
     (g) the utilization of the Housing Fund;
     (h) the geographic distribution of beneficiaries;
     (i) the remaining estimated housing needs of public school teachers;
         and
     (j) recommendations for improving the Program.

      SECTION 23. Protection Against Displacement and Unjustified
Eviction. - A beneficiary occupying a government-built housing unit under
the Program shall not be unjustly or arbitrarily deprived of possession.

     No beneficiary shall be evicted except for lawful grounds and
pursuant to due process and the terms and conditions of the applicable
housing agreement.
      SECTION 24. Non-Transferability and Resale Restrictions. -
Housing units acquired through substantial government subsidy under the
Program shall be subject to restrictions on sale, transfer, assignment, lease,
or encumbrance for such period as may be prescribed by the implementing
rules and regulations, without prejudice to stricter restrictions provided
under Republic Act No. 7279, as amended, and other applicable laws.

      The implementing rules and regulations shall provide appropriate
exceptions in cases of death, permanent disability, retirement, compulsory
reassignment, or other circumstances that justify transfer or disposition,
subject to the protection of public funds and the interests of the beneficiary.

       SECTION 25. Coordination with Existing Housing Programs. - The
Program shall be harmonized, to the extent practicable, with existing
national government housing programs, financing facilities, socialized and
economic housing programs, and other applicable housing laws and
policies, including Republic Act No. 11201, Republic Act No. 12216,
Republic Act No. 7279, as amended, and Republic Act No. 9679.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed as repealing or diminishing
benefits available to public school teachers under existing laws, unless
expressly provided herein.

      A qualified teacher may avail of the Program and other government
housing benefits only to the extent permitted by applicable laws and
regulations and subject to rules against double or multiple subsidization for
the same housing purpose.

      SECTION 26. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DHSUD, in coordination with
the DepEd, NHA, Pag-IBIG Fund, DBM, DOF, LGUs, recognized national
organizations of public school teachers, housing cooperatives, and other
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary implementing rules
and regulations.

      The implementing rules and regulations shall include, among others:

      (a) beneficiary eligibility and prioritization;

      (b) housing design and affordability standards;

      (c) loan ceilings and repayment terms;

      (d) interest-rate subsidy mechanisms, including the manner of
          computation, funding, release, monitoring, and termination of
          subsidies;
      (e) rent-to-own arrangements;

      (f) down-payment assistance;

      (g) rental assistance;

      (h) use and development of government land;

      (i) public-private partnership arrangements;

      (j) beneficiary selection and appeals;

      (k) monitoring and reporting;

      (l) restrictions on transfer and resale; and

      (m) such other matters necessary for the effective implementation of
         this Act.

     SECTION 27. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the appropriations of
the DHSUD, DepEd, NHA, and other concerned agencies under the current
General Appropriations Act, subject to existing budgeting, accounting,
procurement, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.

     Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

      The inclusion of appropriations for the Program shall be consistent
with the Medium-Term Fiscal Framework, national expenditure program,
and other applicable fiscal and budgetary policies of the Government.

       SECTION 28. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 29. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
presidential issuances, rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent
with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

       SECTION 30. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers
of general circulation.
Approved.
