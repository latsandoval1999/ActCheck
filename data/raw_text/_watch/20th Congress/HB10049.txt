                              EXPLANATORY NOTE


The internet has become an indispensable tool for education, communication,
and daily life. However, the digital landscape also exposes young learners to
severe risks, including cyberbullying, online scams, identity theft, exposure to
age-inappropriate content, and online child sexual exploitation and abuse.

While students are highly adept at navigating digital interfaces, they often lack
the critical thinking skills and psychological readiness to identify digital threats,
protect their personal data, and practice responsible digital citizenship.
Leaving digital literacy entirely to parental supervision is no longer sufficient, as
many parents struggle to keep pace with rapid technological advancements. A
structured, proactive approach is necessary.

This bill seeks to institutionalize a Safe Internet Education Program within the
mandatory curriculum of all public and private elementary and secondary
schools. By integrating age-appropriate lessons on digital safety, data privacy,
and online ethics into existing subjects, we empower the next generation to
navigate the digital world safely, responsibly, and productively.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING A SAFE INTERNET EDUCATION PROGRAM IN THE
  CURRICULUM OF ALL ELEMENTARY AND SECONDARY SCHOOLS AND
                      APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREOF
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Safe Internet
Education Act."

      Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect the
physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being of the youth.
Recognizing the vital role of information and communications technology in
nation-building, the State shall ensure that children are provided with the
necessary knowledge and skills to shield themselves from online hazards and
become responsible digital citizens.

      Sec. 3. Establishment of the Safe Internet Education Program. – There is
hereby established a Safe Internet Education Program (SIEP), which shall be
integrated into the mandatory curriculum of all public and private elementary
and secondary schools in the country.

      Sec. 4. Core Content of the Program.- The Department of Education
(DepEd), in coordination with the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), shall develop an age-appropriate
curriculum that covers, but is not limited to, the following core areas:

   •   Digital Citizenship and Netiquette: Responsible online behavior, respectful
       communication, and understanding the permanent nature of the "digital
       footprint."

   •   Online Privacy and Security: The importance of strong passwords,
       protecting personal identifiable information (PII), and recognizing phishing,
       malware, and social engineering tactics.
   •   Cyberbullying Prevention: Identifying online harassment, reporting
       mechanisms, mental health support, and promoting empathy online.

   •   Critical Thinking and Media Literacy: Recognizing fake news, evaluating
       online sources, and understanding the risks of online misinformation.

   •   Protection Against Online Exploitation: Awareness of the tactics used by
       online predators, grooming signs, and immediate reporting procedures for
       child exploitation.


       Sec. 5. Integration into Existing Subjects.– To prevent curriculum overload,
the SIEP shall be integrated into relevant existing subjects, such as Edukasyon
sa Pagpapakatao (EsP), Media and Information Literacy (MIL), Technology and
Livelihood Education (TLE), or their equivalent counterpart subjects in the revised
basic education curriculum.

      Sec. 6. Teacher Training and Capacity Building. — The DepEd, through the
National Educators Academy of the Philippines (NEAP) and in partnership with
the DICT, shall conduct mandatory training programs for teachers to equip them
with the necessary knowledge and pedagogical skills to effectively teach the
internet safety curriculum.

      Sec. 7. Parental and Community Involvement.— Schools shall conduct
annual or semi-annual seminars or workshops on internet safety for parents,
guardians, and local community leaders to foster a collaborative environment in
protecting children online.

       Sec. 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement the
provisions of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations
of the Department of Education. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for
its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA).

       Sec. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, in consultation with the DICT, the National
Privacy Commission (NPC), and relevant non-governmental organizations, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation
of this Act.

       Sec. 10. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
      Sec. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
presidential issuances, and other administrative rules and regulations or parts
thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

      Sec. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
