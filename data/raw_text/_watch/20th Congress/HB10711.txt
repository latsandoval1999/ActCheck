                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        A person's name is a fundamental aspect of identity, dignity, and individuality. The
principles of individual liberty and self-expression are among the hallmarks of our great vibrant
democracy. Every person is afforded the freedom to define and express one’s identity, so long as
the exercise of such freedom does not prejudice the rights of others or the public interest. When we
think of our first names, it is more than a mere entry in the civil registry; it is the name by which a
person is identified in everything that matters. In school, employment, financial transactions,
government records, and everyday social interactions, we are known by our first names. It is our
very mark in society. Over the course of one's life, an individual may come to identify more closely
with a different first name that better reflects one's identity rather than the name by which one has
long been known.

         Consistent with these principles, Republic Act No. 9048 authorized the administrative
change of first name under specified circumstances, sparing thousands of Filipinos from costly and
time-consuming judicial proceedings. Nevertheless, the law remains narrowly drawn, as it limits the
administrative change of a first name to only three specific statutory grounds: the first name is
embarrassing or impractical; the petitioner has long and consistently used another first name; or the
change will prevent confusion. As a result, individuals who seek to adopt a first name solely as an
exercise of personal autonomy remain without an appropriate administrative remedy. This limitation
fails to reflect the evolving recognition that personal identity encompasses an individual's reasonable
freedom to determine how one wishes to be known and identified as.

        As society becomes more dynamic and individuals place greater importance on self-
identification, the law should likewise evolve to accommodate legitimate requests that do not
prejudice public order or the rights of third persons. This bill therefore proposes to amend Section 4
of Republic Act No. 9048 by recognizing the exercise of personal autonomy as an additional ground
for the administrative change of a first name. To safeguard the integrity of the civil registry, the
proposed ground is subject to clear limitations: the change may be availed of only once and only
when it is not sought for any fraudulent, malicious, or unlawful purpose. These safeguards preserve
the State's interest in preventing identity fraud while affording individuals reasonable latitude in
determining the name by which they wish to be known.

        Recognizing personal autonomy in matters of one's first name is consistent with the
constitutional values of human dignity, liberty, and the free development of personality. Identity is
not merely an administrative concern; it is a fundamental expression of personhood that deserves
reasonable legal recognition. Or simply because for a lot of people: to be known matters.
