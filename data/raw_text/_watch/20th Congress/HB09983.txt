                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates that the State shall afford full protection to
labor, promote full employment, and ensure equality of employment opportunities for all.
However, a digital loophole has emerged where thousands of TNVS drivers and delivery
riders are considered "Independent Contractors" on paper, but are treated like employees
in practice. This is the "Gig Economy Gap."

Platform providers often use "non-exclusivity" clauses as a legal shield to avoid
providing benefits. Yet, through Algorithmic Control, these platforms impose delivery
quotas so high that workers have no choice but to remain exclusive to one application.
When they fail to meet these quotas, they are "de-platformed" by a faceless
algorithm—without a hearing, without a human explanation, and without due process.

This Act seeks to align our labor laws with the smartphone age by bridging the gap
between technological advancement and worker protection. It defines "Algorithmic
Control" to recognize that tracking movements and imposing strict quotas constitute
modern employer supervision.

Furthemore, this Act seeks to empower platform workers by granting full-time workers
legal recognition, providing due process against unfair suspensions, and mandating
transparent algorithmic management to ensure that automated decisions are fair and
subject to human oversight.

In essence, this bill aims to build a sustainable digital economy where technological
progress does not sacrifice labor rights. It ensures that amid rapid innovation, every
Filipino worker remains protected and included.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT ESTABLISHING EQUITABLE LABOR STANDARDS FOR GIG
  ECONOMY WORKERS, REGULATING ALGORITHMIC MANAGEMENT,
       AND PROTECTING AGAINST UNFAIR DE-PLATFORMING

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Gig Economy Equitable
Standards Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to afford full
protection to labor, promote decent work, and ensure that technological innovation and
digital platforms operate in a manner consistent with social justice and fundamental labor
rights. The State recognizes that workers engaged through online platforms contribute
significantly to the national economy and shall protect such workers from unfair labor
practices, arbitrary deprivation of livelihood, economic coercion, and opaque digital
management systems that substantially affect their earnings and working conditions. The
State shall likewise ensure transparency, accountability, and meaningful human oversight
in decisions affecting a platform worker’s access to earning opportunities.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
be defined as follows:

   a. Algorithmic Control – refers to the use of mathematical algorithms and
      software-based systems to remotely manage, monitor, supervise, and evaluate
      workers, including the imposition of high-volume delivery quotas or
      performance-based incentives that functionally require a worker to dedicate
      full-time hours to a single platform, thereby rendering "non-exclusivity" clauses
      illusory.
   b. De-platforming – refers to the act of an Online Platform Provider in restricting,
      suspending, or permanently terminating a worker’s access to the platform’s digital
      interface, effectively ending their ability to earn income, whether such action is
      automated or manually initiated.
   c. Equipment Depreciation – refers to the measurable decline in the value and utility
      of a worker’s personal assets (e.g., motorcycles, cars, smartphones) used in the
      performance of gig work, which must be factored into the minimum
      compensation to ensure a living wage.
   d.​ Material Adverse Action – refers to any suspension, deactivation, reduction of
       visibility, withholding of incentives, restriction of access to bookings, or other
       action that significantly reduces a worker's earning opportunities.
   e.​ Online Platform Provider – refers to any person, corporation, partnership, or
       entity that operates a digital platform, application, or technological system that
       connects consumers with Platform Workers for the provision of services.
   f.​ Platform Worker – refers to any natural person who provides transportation,
       delivery, logistics, courier, or other income-generating services through an online
       platform or digital application in exchange for compensation.
   g.​ Shadow-banning – also known as "algorithmic retaliation," refers to the practice
       of reducing a worker’s visibility or task-priority within the platform’s algorithm
       without official notice, leading to a sudden, unexplained decrease in assigned
       tasks as a disciplinary or retaliatory measure.

SECTION 4. Rebuttable Presumption of Employment Relationship. — A rebuttable
presumption of an employer-employee relationship shall arise where an Online Platform
Provider:

   a.​ determines, directly or indirectly, the rates or compensation payable to Platform
       Workers;
   b.​ imposes acceptance rates, completion rates, performance metrics, quotas, ratings,
       or similar requirements as a condition for continued access to earning
       opportunities;
   c.​ exercises the power to suspend, deactivate, restrict, or otherwise limit a worker’s
       access to the platform;
   d.​ continuously monitors the worker’s performance through digital, technological, or
       algorithmic means; or
   e.​ substantially controls the worker’s access to customers, bookings, assignments, or
       earning opportunities.

The burden of proving the absence of an employer-employee relationship shall rest upon
the Online Platform Provider.

SECTION 5. Due Process in Suspension, Deactivation, and Other Material Adverse
Actions. — No Platform Worker shall be suspended, deactivated, restricted from
accessing the platform, denied assignments, subjected to a reduction in visibility or
earning opportunities, or otherwise subjected to any Material Adverse Action without due
process.

Prior to the imposition of any Material Adverse Action, the Online Platform Provider
shall:

   a.​ provide the worker with written notice stating the specific facts, acts, omissions,
       complaints, or circumstances forming the basis of the proposed action;
   b.​ disclose, upon request, the records, reports, ratings, complaints, trip data, digital
       records, or other information materially relied upon in making the determination,
       subject only to limitations necessary to protect personal data, trade secrets, or
       public safety;
   c.​ provide the worker a reasonable opportunity to explain, contest, or rebut the
       allegations through accessible electronic or physical means; and
   d.​ ensure that the final determination is reviewed and approved by a duly authorized
       human decision-maker who has the authority to affirm, modify, or reverse the
       proposed action after considering the worker’s response.

No Material Adverse Action shall be imposed solely on the basis of an automated
decision-making system, algorithmic assessment, customer rating, or artificial
intelligence tool without meaningful human evaluation of the relevant facts and
circumstances. Any suspension, deactivation, or restriction imposed without compliance
with this Section shall be presumed unjustified and shall be immediately lifted upon order
of the Department of Labor and Employment or other competent authority, without
prejudice to the worker’s right to damages and other remedies provided by law. The
burden of proving compliance with the requirements of this Section shall rest upon the
Online Platform Provider.

SECTION 6. Social Protection and Multi-Platform Contributions.

   a.​ Every Platform Worker shall be registered with the Social Security System (SSS),
       Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), and the Home
       Development Mutual Fund (Pag-IBIG), and shall be entitled to the benefits and
       protections provided under the respective laws governing such institutions.
   b.​ Registration under this Act shall be attached to the Platform Worker and shall not
       require separate enrollment for each Online Platform Provider. A Platform Worker
       shall maintain a single membership record with each social protection institution
       regardless of the number of platforms through which the worker earns income.
   c.​ Every Online Platform Provider that pays compensation to a Platform Worker
       shall remit the corresponding contributions, premiums, or dues based on the
       compensation paid through its platform, in accordance with applicable laws,
       contribution schedules, and implementing regulations issued by the concerned
       government agencies.
   d.​ Where a Platform Worker receives compensation from two or more Online
       Platform Providers during the same contribution period, each Online Platform
       Provider shall be responsible only for its proportional share of contributions
       corresponding to the compensation it paid to the worker, subject to statutory
       ceilings and limitations prescribed by law.

   e.​ The Social Security System (SSS), Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
       (PhilHealth), Home Development Mutual Fund (Pag-IBIG), and the Department
       of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall jointly establish and maintain a
       centralized reporting, reconciliation, and compliance mechanism to:

           1.​ prevent duplicate enrollment of Platform Workers;
           2.​ prevent overpayment of contributions beyond statutory ceilings;
           3.​ allocate contribution obligations among multiple Online Platform
               Providers;
           4.​ ensure accurate and timely crediting of contributions to the Platform
               Worker;
           5.​ facilitate compliance monitoring, auditing, and enforcement; and
           6.​ streamline reporting requirements and administrative procedures for
               workers and Online Platform Providers.
       f. Online Platform Providers shall submit such reports, records, and information
       as may be required by the SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG Fund, and DOLE for the
       implementation of this Section, subject to applicable laws on data privacy and
       confidentiality.

       g. No Online Platform Provider shall evade contribution obligations by
       classifying a Platform Worker as an independent contractor, partner, merchant,
       service provider, or by any other designation inconsistent with the actual nature of
       the working arrangement.

       h. Compliance with this Section shall not, by itself, be construed as conclusive
       proof of the existence or absence of an employer-employee relationship for
       purposes of labor standards, labor relations, taxation, social legislation, or any
       other law. The determination of employment status shall remain subject to
       applicable laws and the facts of each case.

       i. The Department of Labor and Employment, in coordination with the SSS,
       PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG Fund, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
       regulations for the effective implementation of this Section within ninety (90)
       days from the effectivity of this Act.

SECTION 7. Unlawful Practices. — It shall be unlawful for any platform to:

   a.​ Use "non-exclusivity" as a defense for independent contractor status if the
       platform's internal algorithms enforce high-volume quotas that make such
       freedom impossible.
   b.​ Retaliate against workers for filing complaints or organizing.
   c.​ Use "non-exclusivity" or "independent contractor" clauses as a legal defense to
       evade employer-employee obligations if the platform's internal algorithms,
       quotas, or incentive structures functionally require full-time dedication, thereby
       rendering such freedom impossible;
   d.​ Retaliate against, bias the algorithm against, or "shadow-ban" workers,
       characterized by a sudden, unexplained decrease in assigned tasks—for filing
       complaints, organizing, or participating in collective actions;
   e.​ Impose "penalties" or deduct from a worker’s earnings for declining tasks that
       would exceed safe driving hours or take the worker outside of their designated
       service area;
   f.​ Restrict, suspend, or terminate a worker’s access to the platform based on
       automated metrics (such as acceptance rates) without providing a specific,
       evidence-based written justification reviewed by a human officer;
   g.​ Withhold essential trip data—including the full destination, estimated duration,
       and net pay after fees—before a worker accepts a task, effectively forcing "blind"
       acceptance to meet quotas;
   h.​ Mandate that workers remain "online" or active within specific geographic zones
       during "surge" periods without providing a guaranteed base compensation for that
       standby time;
   i.​ Systematically cap a worker’s hours or tasks specifically to prevent them from
       reaching thresholds that would trigger mandatory social protection contributions
       or benefits.
   j.​ Engaging in algorithmic retaliation, including the reduction of visibility,
       assignments, incentives, or earning opportunities in response to a worker's filing
       of complaints, participation in lawful organizations, or exercise of rights under
       this Act;
   k.​ Deliberately manipulating assignments, work opportunities, or platform access to
       prevent workers from qualifying for statutory benefits, incentives, or protections;
   l.​ Issuing generic, vague, or automated explanations that fail to substantially
       disclose the factual basis of disciplinary actions.

SECTION 8. Civil Penalties. — Any platform found in violation of these standards shall
be subject to a fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) but not more than
Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00) per violation.

SECTION 9. Presumption of Algorithmic Retaliation. — A sudden and unexplained
reduction of at least fifty percent (50%) in a Platform Worker’s average assignments,
bookings, visibility, or earnings occurring within thirty (30) days after the worker files a
complaint, participates in collective activities, or exercises rights under this Act shall
create a rebuttable presumption of algorithmic retaliation. The burden of proving a
legitimate and non-retaliatory reason shall rest upon the Online Platform Provider.

SECTION 10. Platform Work Oversight. — The Department of Labor and Employment
shall exercise regulatory and visitorial powers to ensure compliance with this Act and
may require Online Platform Providers to submit records relating to compensation
systems, disciplinary actions, suspensions, deactivations, and worker complaints.

The Department may conduct inspections, investigations, and compliance audits
whenever warranted.

       Approved.
