                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Province of Albay, where the world famous Mayon Volcano
majestically sits, is the second most populous province in the Bicol region.
The total population of the province stands at 1,379,398 distributed across
277,127 households (PSA Population Census 2024). Albay is accessible to
tourists and other travelers via land, air or sea. A developing road network
connects tourist destinations to urban centers and the countryside through
public utility vehicles, i.e., buses, jeepneys, tricycles, padyaks (pedicabs)
and privately owned motorcycles, cars and other conveyances. Expectedly,
the rising vehicle density greatly contributes to traffic congestion – one of
the critically urgent challenges to land transportation management.

       Albay is divided into three (3) Congressional Districts composed of
fifteen (15) municipalities and three (3) cities. One of these three is Tabaco
City, which together with the five (5) municipalities of Bacacay, Malilipot,
Malinao, Sto. Domingo and Tiwi constitute the First District covering a
population of approximately 433,349 (PSA Population Census 2024).

      The Land Transportation Office (LTO) Tabaco District Office hobbled by
a meager workforce of only eight (8) plantilla positions as it is under the
Class “D” category, provides the necessary major services. These include
Licensing, Registration, Adjudication and Law Enforcement to a fast-growing
population of road users.

   Despite human resource constraint, the LTO Tabaco District Office,
although not without difficulty, has exhibited a commendable operational
performance particularly for the period 2021-2025. An evidence-based
analysis of the LTO Tabaco District Office’s overall performance for this five-
year period reveals robust accomplishments in the delivery of the following
services:

   •   Motor Vehicle (MV) Registration: A total of 417,890 MV transactions
       were handled over the five-year period with an annual average of
       83,578 transactions. Transactions increased significantly from 2021 to

      reduction may be attributed to the 2023 LTO policy extending the
      validity of initial registration for motorcycles to three (3) years,

      succeeding years.

  •   License and Permits: The office processed a total of 176,277
      transactions, with an annual average of 35,255. The number of
      licenses and permits handled steadily rose from 2021 to 2024, before
      declining in 2025. The decrease may be partly due to the long-
      standing effect of the 10-year validity of driver’s licenses, which
      reduces the frequency of renewal activities, although overall figures
      remain higher compared to earlier years.

  •   Apprehension Cases: A total of 4,367 apprehension cases were
      recorded over the five-year period, with an average of 873 cases per
      year. Cases gradually declined from 2021 to 2023, reflecting improved
      compliance with land transportation regulations. In 2024,
      apprehensions rose due to intensified enforcement operations;
      however, in 2025, the number decreased as motorists continued to
      adhere to traffic rules, demonstrating the positive impact of sustained
      enforcement and compliance efforts.

  •   Revenue collection: The LTO Tabaco District Office generated a total
      revenue of P307,381,319.74, with an average annual collection of
      approximately P61.48 million. Despite the decline in some transaction
      volumes, revenue collection consistently increased from 2021 to 2023
      and remained steady at a high level in 2024 and 2025, demonstrating
      sustained financial performance and efficiency in revenue-generating
      services. (Records of the Operations Division, LTO V)

   Needless to stress, the upgrade of the LTO Tabaco District Office from
Class “D” to Class “B” is imperative. Hence, this proposed measure that
seeks to implement the upgrade hopefully forthwith. The recommended
upgrade shall necessarily increase the plantilla positions of the LTO Tabaco
District Office to twenty (20), thereby creating an additional twelve (12)
positions to its current eight (8) personnel complement.

   The change in classification and the consequent accompanying addition of
personnel will indubitably benefit the patrons and clients from Tabaco City
and nearby municipalities primarily through enhanced services and
expedited transactions. These timely and much-needed reforms will trigger
and promote higher revenue collection which in turn is expected to further
expand and optimize provision of transport and allied public welfare services.

  In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT UPGRADING THE CLASSIFICATION OF TABACO DISTRICT
 OFFICE OF THE LAND TRANSPORTATION OFFICE (LTO) IN TABACO
CITY, PROVINCE OF ALBAY FROM CLASS “D” TO CLASS “B” DISTRICT
         OFFICE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

    SECTION 1. Creation. – The Tabaco District Office of the Land
Transportation Office in the City of Tabaco, Province of Albay is hereby
upgraded from Class “D” to Class “B”.

    SEC. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Assistant
Secretary of the Land Transportation Office, in consultation with the
Secretary of the Department of Transportation (DOTr), shall formulate the
necessary rules and regulations to implement this Act.

     SEC. 3. Appropriations. – The Secretary of the DOTr shall immediately
include in the Department’s program the operationalization of the LTO
Tabaco District Office in the City of Tabaco, Province of Albay, the funding of
which shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

     SEC. 3. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in any newspaper of general
circulation.
