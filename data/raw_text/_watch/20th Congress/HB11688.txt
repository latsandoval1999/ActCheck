EXPLANATORY NOTE

Oral disease remains one of the most widespread yet chronically neglected
public health crises in the Philippines. Baseline data from the Department of Health
(DOH) underscore an alarming reality: the 2018 National Survey on Oral Health
revealed that roughly 72% to 73% of Filipinos suffer from dental caries, while up to
50% are afflicted by gum disease. More recent figures from the DOH National
Monitoring and Epidemiological Dental Survey (NMEDS) point to an even graver
escalation, indicating that tooth decay affects up to 92.4% of the population, and
78% suffer from periodontal (gum) diseases.

These figures place the Philippines among the countries with the highest
levels of dental decay globally. The systemic toll is profound: 12-year-old Filipino
children manifest an average Decayed, Missing, and Filled Teeth (DMFT) score of
4.48, significantly worse than the World Health Organization (WHO) standard
benchmark of 3.0 or lower. Furthermore, the cumulative neglect leaves older
Filipinos aged 70 to 79 with an average of only seven (7) natural teeth, with roughly
a third experiencing total edentulism (complete tooth loss). Beyond physical pain
and social stigma, this burden is clinically linked to systemic complications,
including malnutrition, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and cognitive decline.

Much of this suffering is entirely preventable. Yet, public dental delivery has
frequently been reduced to a reactive cycle of “bunot” (extractions) followed by
makeshift “pustiso" (dentures), treating the symptoms of a failing public health
architecture rather than its root causes. This systemic challenge is compounded
by severe geographic inequity. While dental professionals and advanced clinics
are heavily concentrated in urban centers like the National Capital Region (NCR),
vast populations in Mindanao, the Cordillera Administrative Region (CAR), Eastern
Visayas, and the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM)
face extreme practitioner shortages, with national dentist-to-population ratios
stretching far beyond safe thresholds.

===== [page 2] =====
While dental outreach missions are designed to bridge this accessibility gap,
they have historically operated in a regulatory vacuum. Improvised in gymnasiums,
covered courts, and multi-purpose halls, many missions lack basic infection
prevention and control (IPC) protocols, operating without dependable clean
running water, proper instrument sterilization, physical separation of clean and
contaminated zones, or safe biomedical waste disposal. Consequently, health
missions meant to heal run the risk of inadvertently transmitting infections, while
an over-concentration on extractions entrenches the very cycle of tooth loss they
seek to resolve.

Aligned with Republic Act No. 11223 (Universal Health Care Act) and the
national “Ngiting 70-20" roadmap, this measure strategically reorients community-
based dental outreach toward minimally invasive and preventive care. It
establishes mandatory standards for infection control, accessible clean water, and
strict physical zoning plans for every outreach venue, regardless of how temporary.

By transforming dental missions into safe, standardized, and prevention-
driven platforms, this bill ensures that healthcare outreach preserves, rather than
compromises, the dignity and well-being of underserved Filipinos.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT PROMOTING THE SAFE, NON-INVASIVE, AND STANDARDIZED
CONDUCT OF DENTAL OUTREACH, PRESCRIBING INFECTION,
PREVENTION AND CONTROL AND MINIMUM DESIGNATED-AREA
REQUIREMENTS FOR THE DELIVERY OF COMMUNITY-BASED ORAL
HEALTH SERVICES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Titie. - This Act shall be known as the "Safe Dental
Outreach Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. \t is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health of the people. Recognizing that oral health is integral to
general health and that access to oral health services remains limited in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged and other underserved areas, the State
shall ensure that dental outreach, as a means of reaching these communities, is
conducted safely, humanely, and in accordance with current, evidence-based
standards of care and infection prevention and
control.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. As used in this Act:

(a) Clinical oral health services refer to dental or oral health services that only
a licensed dental professional can provide, including mouth examination or
oral screening, oral prophylaxis, tooth restoration, sealant application,
fluoride varnish application, and surgical procedures, including simple tooth
extraction;

(b) Dental outreach refers to activities, whether denominated as dental
missions, health caravans, medical missions, or other outreach activities,

that provide or include oral health services in geographically isolated and
disadvantaged and other underserved and unserved areas;

(c) Non-invasive oral health services refer to oral health procedures that do not
involve the cutting of hard or soft tissue, such as silver diamine fluoride
application, atraumatic restorative treatment, root surface protection, and pit
and fissure sealant application; and

(d) Infection prevention and control refers to the discipline composed of the
measures, practices, protocols, and procedures towards preventing and
controlling the development of infections acquired in healthcare and
community-based service settings.

SECTION 4. Coverage. This Act shall apply to all dental outreach conducted
by the Department, government-owned hospitals, local government units, national
government agencies, non-government and civil society organizations, academic
institutions, professional organizations, and private entities providing oral health
services. In the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao, this Act
shall be adopted in accordance with the applicable provisions of Republic Act No.
11054.

SECTION 5. Non-invasive Oral Health Services as the Rule. - Oral health
services provided during dental outreach shall, as a rule, be non-invasive. Every
patient shall first undergo oral health screening and risk assessment, and, where
applicable, non-invasive oral prophylaxis, fluoride varnish application, and
counseling, before any other service is rendered.

Tooth extraction and other invasive clinical oral health services shall not be
the default or primary service of a dental outreach. They may be performed only
upon proper indication, in an area isolated from or separate from the main
treatment area, and in accordance with standard infection prevention and control
protocols. Complex oral and periodontal surgeries, denture and obturator
fabrication, restorations using rotary handpieces, and similar procedures shall be
conducted inside a health facility, unless the area is geographically isolated and
disadvantaged or underserved and accessibility is restricted. Patients seventeen
(17) years of age and below shall present the consent of a parent or guardian, or
of the head of the institution in the case of closed-door facilities, before availing of
treatment.

SECTION 6. Designated Areas and Minimum Area Plan. Every dental
outreach, whether conducted in a health facility or in a non-facility or makeshift
venue such as a gymnasium, covered court, barangay hall, school, or similar
community space, shall establish and observe a minimum area plan. The area plan
shall delineate the following designated areas, arranged to permit an orderly, one-
directional flow of patients and instruments and to keep clean areas separate from
contaminated ones:

(a) a pre-screening and registration area, for the master listing, registration,
screening, and medical-history taking of patients prior to treatment;

(b) a patient's waiting area;

===== [page 5] =====
(c) arecord management area;
(d) an examination and treatment area;

(e) an invasive treatment area, where applicable, which shall be isolated from
or separate from the main treatment area;

(f) areceiving area for used or contaminated instruments;
(g) acleaning and decontamination area;

(h) asterilization and equipment dispensing area, from which only cleaned and
sterilized instruments shall be released; and

(i) apharmacy.

Patients shall enter through a single entrance, proceed from the pre-
screening area to the treatment area and then to the pharmacy, and leave through
a designated exit. Used instruments shall move from the treatment area to the
receiving area for contaminated instruments, then to the cleaning and
decontamination area, then to sterilization and packing, after which only sterilized
instruments shall be released back to the treatment area. In no case shail the flow
of contaminated instruments cross or intersect the flow of clean or sterilized
instruments or of patients.

SECTION 7. Sterilization, Water, and Prevention of Contamination. - Every
dental outreach shall adopt and strictly observe an infection prevention and control
protocol, and shall designate an infection prevention and control officer to ensure
compliance. The protocol shall provide, at the minimum, for the following:

(a) the use of an autoclave or portable autoclave as the sterilization equipment
of choice; where electricity is unavailable, the pressure- cooker method of
sterilization may be used as a substitute in accordance with recognized
infection-control guidelines, and where neither is feasible, pre-sterilized
instruments shall be prepared beforehand;

(b) the availability of clean and, where feasible, running water dedicated to
instrument cleaning for the entire duration of the activity; where clean water
is not otherwise available, the organizer shall provide a sufficient supply;
and

(c) a waste management plan providing for the segregation, proper color-
coding, containment in appropriate sharps and waste receptacles, and
disposal of biomedical, sharps, and other hazardous wastes, duly
coordinated with the concerned health office and local government unit.

SECTION 8. Proposals and Approval. - Every organizer that is not the local
government unit of the target area shall submit a proposal, together with a letter of
intent addressed to the local chief executive and furnished to the municipal or city
health officer. The proposal shall include the area plan and the infection prevention
and control, and waste management plans required under this Act. All proposals
shall be reviewed by the municipal or city health officer and endorsed to the local

chief executive for approval. Where the activity involves patients under the
responsibility of a school, residential care center, closed-door facility, military
camp, or emergency shelter, the approval of the head of the concerned institution
shall likewise be secured.

SECTION 9. Monitoring and Evaluation. - A signed post-activity report shall
be submitted to the rural health unit or city health office of the area, together with
the patient records and referrals to higher levels of care. The concerned health
offices and the Centers for Health Development shall consolidate all dental
outreach data for planning and evaluation and for reporting to the National Oral
Health Program of the Department.

SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department, in coordination with the Department
of the Interior and Local Government, the concerned professional regulatory
bodies, and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations
necessary to implement this Act.

SECTION. 11. Separability Clause. - \f any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause.- All laws, decrees, orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
