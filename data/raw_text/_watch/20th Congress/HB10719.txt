                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The transition from educational institutions to the workforce is a critical
milestone for fresh graduates and young adults. However, immediate access to
employment opportunities is rarely guaranteed upon graduation. Beyond a
competitive job market, fresh graduates face immediate financial hurdles, such as
the incidental and necessary expenses incurred while searching for work, including
transportation, communication, printing, and attire. These are costs that many
graduates simply cannot afford.

       Under Article XIII, Section 3 of the 1987 Constitution, the State is mandated to
promote full employment and equality of employment opportunities. Article II,
Section 13 recognizes the vital role of youth in nation-building. This mandates the
State to enact policies that will protect their interest and welfare while fostering their
potential.

       While Republic Act No. 11261 or the “First Time Jobseekers Assistance Act”
exempts first-time jobseekers from paying fees and charges for government-issued
pre-employment documents, there are still financial burdens for job seeking fresh
graduates during employment search.
       According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), in June 2026, the
unemployment rate in the country increased at 4.9%, with an estimated 2.59 millions
unemployed Filipinos.1 Among the 592,000 youth aged 15-24 seeking jobs for the
first time, only 310,000 landed gainful employment, leaving 282,000 young job
seekers without job.

       To address the same, this bill seeks to establish a one-time grant of five
thousand pesos (PHP 5,000) to qualified Fresh Filipino graduates. The grant aims to
provide assistance for the necessary and incidental expenses of the job seeking fresh
graduates, complementing the existing benefits under RA 11261.

       By easing the financial strain on our job seeking fresh graduates, this
legislative measure provide a fair starting point for young filipinos as they transition
into productive members of the workforce.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

            AN ACT PROVIDING FRESH FILIPINO GRADUATES
   OF PHILIPPINE TERTIARY EDUCATION INSTITUTIONS, UNIVERSITIES,
  COLLEGES AND OTHER TRAINING INSTITUTIONS, OF A ONE-TIME CASH
              GRANT OF FIVE THOUSAND PESOS (PHP 5,000)

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      Section 1. Shor t Title. – This Act shall be known as “Fr esh Gr aduates One-Time
Cash Gr ant Act of 2026”.

        Section 2. Declar ation of Policy. – It is the declared policy that the State recognizes
the vital role of the youth in nation-building. It is further mandated that the State promote and
protect the interest of the youth, by leveling the access to employment opportunities.

       To this end, the State shall create policies that will directly address the financial
burden that fresh filipino graduates encounter while seeking gainful employment.

        Section. 3. Cover age of Cash Gr ant to Fr esh Gr aduates. – All Fresh Filipino
Graduates of Philippine Tertiary Institutions, Universities, Colleges, and Training Institutions
shall receive the amount of five thousand pesos (PHP 5,000.00) as productivity cash grant to
cover the initial expenses of his or her employment application, and other pre-employment or
job settling expenses.

        Section. 4. Pr oof of Gr aduation – To avail of the one-time cash grant under this Act,
the Fresh Filipino Graduate must submit proof of her successful completion of his/her course,
which includes a copy of diploma, certification, or other school documents from which he or
she has graduated, issued to that effect, to the concerned government agency or local
government unit.
        The diploma, certification or school documents shall clearly state the date of
graduation or completion, the degree earned, or course or training completed, and must be
duly signed by the head or authorized representaties of institutions, universities or colleges.

      Section. 5. Cr eation of Inter -Agency Monitor ing Committee – An Inter-Agency
Monitoring Committee (IAMC) is hereby created to be headed by the Chairperson of the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED).

       The IAMC shall be tasked to formulate the implementing rules and regulations to
supplement the implementation of this Act, and to monitor compliance by the concerned
government agencies and instrumentalities.

       Section. 6. Appr opr iations – The amount needed to implement this Act shall be
included in the General Appropriations Act, coursed through the Commission on Higher
Education.

       Section. 7. Separ ability Clause. – If any section or provision of this Act is held
unconstitutional or invalid, the other sections or provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

        Section. 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, proclamations, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

        Section. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following
its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
