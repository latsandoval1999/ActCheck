       Notwithstanding this legal framework, there remains a need to translate
system-level requirements into a direct, uniform, and convenient means by which
patients may access their own health information and authorized health facilities
may retrieve updated records when necessary for treatment. When records remain
dispersed among different facilities or cannot be promptly retrieved, healthcare
providers may be deprived of information necessary for timely diagnosis, referral,
and treatment. PhilHealth identifies improved access to complete health records,
better care coordination, and reduced costs from repeated laboratory examinations
among the intended benefits of a national health data system.2 The World Health
Organization likewise recognizes electronic health records as an important
mechanism for supporting continuity of care.3

      This bill therefore establishes a secure, accessible, interoperable, centralized,
and universal Electronic Health Record. It mandates all health facilities to transfer
patient records to an Electronic Health Record Website and promptly update them
following every patient encounter. It likewise grants patients and duly authorized
representatives of health facilities controlled access to such records while requiring
appropriate safeguards against unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, loss, or
destruction. These protections are consistent with the rights of access and data
portability and the security requirements established under Republic Act No. 10173,

      Section 4. Establishment of an Electronic Health Record. ± The Department
of Health, in coordination with the Department of Information and Communications
Technology and other relevant agencies, shall establish and maintain a secure,
accessible, interoperable, centralized, and universal Electronic Health Record. It
shall allow patients to access their health records and shall permit duly authorized
representatives of health facilities to access, update, and transmit such records for
the provision and continuity of healthcare, subject to patient consent and applicable
data privacy and confidentiality laws.

      Section 5. Mandatory Digitization and Updating of Health Records. Every

Electronic Health Record Site. Such records shall be updated promptly after every
patient encounter and shall contain, as applicable:

   a. Patient identification and demographic information;
   b. Relevant medical history, including allergies and existing medical conditions;
   c. Diagnoses, clinical findings, and treatment plans;
   d. Prescriptions, medications administered, and immunization records;
   e. Laboratory, diagnostic, imaging, and other examination results;
   f. Medical procedures, interventions, and services provided;
   g. Referral, admission, transfer, and discharge records; and
   h. Such other information necessary for the continuity, safety, and quality of
      patient care, as may be prescribed by the Department of Health.

      All entries and subsequent corrections shall identify the responsible healthcare
professional and indicate the date and time of entry or modification.

      Section 6. Electronic Health Records Digitalization Assistance Program.
The Department of Health and Philippine Health Insurance Corporation, in
coordination with the Department of Information and Communications Technology
and local government units, shall implement a Health Records Digitalization
Assistance Program for health facilities that lack adequate digital capacity.

        Assistance may include:

           a. Computers, scanners, servers, connectivity, and other necessary
              infrastructure;
           b. Digitization and migration of active and medically relevant records;
           c. Training of healthcare, records, and information technology personnel;
           d. Technical maintenance, cybersecurity, and help-desk support; and
           e. Backup, recovery, offline, and system-continuity services.

        Priority shall be given to government hospitals, primary care facilities, rural
health units, geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, and facilities serving
indigent and underserved patients.

from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health, in coordination with the
Department of Information and Communications Technology, the National Privacy

promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its effective implementation.

      Section 10. Separability Clause.      If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

      Section 11. Repealing Clause.        All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, presidential proclamations, rules and regulations or parts thereof contrary to
or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      Section 12. Effectivity.   This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
