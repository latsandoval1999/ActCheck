                                AN ACT

    ASSIGNED OR DEPLOYED IN THE WEST PHILIPPINE SEA, APPROPRIATING
               FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

The 1987 Philippine Constitution asserts our sovereignty over the archipelago's waters
around, between, and connecting the islands of the archipelago as stated in Article I,
while it mandates the state to protect its archipelagic waters, territorial sea, and exclusive
economic zone (EEZ) for exclusive enjoyment of Filipinos as stated in Article XII,
Section 2.

Meanwhile, the West Philippine Sea has become a critical area for the protection of the
Philippines’ maritime sovereignty, national security, and economic resources. Various
government agencies deploy not only uniformed personnel but also civilian staff to
support maritime patrols, logistics operations, scientific research, fisheries monitoring,
and technical missions in the area.

The importance of maintaining an active Philippine presence in the West Philippine Sea
was further affirmed by the landmark ruling in Philippines v. China Arbitration, decided
by the Permanent Court of Arbitration on 12 July 20161. The tribunal ruled in favor of
the Philippines, declaring that China’s expansive “nine-dash line” claim has no legal basis
under international law and affirming the Philippines’ sovereign rights within its Exclusive
Economic Zone under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea.

In the aftermath of this ruling, the Philippine government has intensified maritime
patrols, fisheries protection, scientific research, and logistical operations in the West

Civilian government personnel—such as researchers, technical staff, fisheries monitors,
and logistics specialists—play a crucial role in these operations alongside uniformed
personnel from agencies such as the Armed Forces of the Philippines and the Philippine
Coast Guard.

However, civilian government personnel assigned to the West Philippine Sea often face
hazardous working conditions, including severe weather, extended deployments at sea,
and exposure to security threats due to heightened geopolitical tensions in the region34.
Despite the risks associated with their duties, many civilian workers supporting these
operations do not receive adequate compensation recognizing the hazardous nature of
their assignments.

Recognizing the strategic importance of the West Philippine Sea and the invaluable
contributions of civilian personnel to the protection of the nation’s maritime rights, it is
imperative that the State provide appropriate compensation and welfare support for
those who serve in this challenging operational environment.

This measure seeks to grant hazard pay and related benefits to civilian government
personnel assigned to the West Philippine Sea in order to promote fairness, enhance
morale, and ensure the continued effectiveness of government operations in the area.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “West Philippine Sea Civilian
 2   Employees Hazard Pay Act.”
 3
 4   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   recognize the risks and hazards faced by civilian government personnel assigned or
 6   deployed in the West Philippine Sea and to provide them with adequate compensation,
 7   benefits, and protection commensurate with the dangers inherent in their assignments.
 8
 9   SECTION 3. Coverage. This Act shall apply to all civilian government personnel who are
10   assigned, detailed, or deployed to perform duties in the West Philippine Sea, including
11   personnel from the following agencies:
12      a) Department of National Defense
13      b) Armed Forces of the Philippines (civilian employees)
14      c) Philippine Coast Guard
15      d) Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources
16      e) Department of Science and Technology
17      f) Other government agencies whose personnel are directly involved in maritime
18         operations, research, monitoring, or logistical support in the West Philippine Sea.
19
20   This coverage shall include permanent, temporary, contractual, and job-order personnel
21   performing duties in the area.
22
 1   SECTION 4. Grant of Hazard Pay. Civilian government personnel covered under this Act
 2   who are physically deployed in maritime operations in the West Philippine Sea shall be
 3   entitled to a hazard pay, the amount or rate of which shall be set by the government
 4   agencies mentioned in Section 3 of this Act.
 5
 6   The hazard pay to be granted to civilian employees under this Act shall:
 7      a) Be in addition to the basic salary, subsistence allowance, and other benefits
 8         provided under existing laws;
 9      b) Be computed based on the number of days of actual exposure to hazardous duty;
10         and
11      c) Not be subject to reduction of existing allowances or benefits.
12
13   The Department of Budget and Management, in coordination with the Department of
14   National Defense, the Philippine Coast Guard, the Civil Service Commission, and other
15   government agencies concerned, may periodically review and recommend adjustments
16   to the hazard pay rate to account for inflation, operational risk, and other relevant factors.
17
18   SECTION 5. Eligibility. Civilian personnel shall be eligible for hazard pay if they:
19     a) Are physically deployed to vessels, maritime stations, or facilities operating within
20        the West Philippine Sea;
21     b) Perform duties related to maritime patrol support, monitoring, fisheries
22        enforcement, logistics, scientific research, or technical operations; or
23     c) Are exposed to hazardous maritime conditions or security threats in the
24        performance of their official duties.
25
26   SECTION 6. Additional Benefits. In addition to hazard pay, covered personnel may be
27   entitled to:
28      a) Insurance coverage for injury, disability, or death incurred during deployment;
29      b) Medical and psychological services;
30      c) Emergency evacuation and transportation support; and
31      d) Training on maritime safety, emergency preparedness, and crisis response.
32
33   SECTION 7. Appropriations. The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
34   charged against the appropriations of the concerned agencies in the current fiscal year.
35   Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be
36   included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
37
38   SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days from the
39   effectivity of this Act, the Department of National Defense, in coordination with the
40   Department of Budget and Management, the Civil Service Commission, and other
41   concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
42   implementation of this Act.
43
44   SECTION 9. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
45   or invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.
46
