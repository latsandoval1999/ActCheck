                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

The widespread use of mobile devices and electronic gadgets among students has
created both opportunities and challenges in the classroom. While these tools can support
learning, their unregulated use during class hours has led to distractions, reduced
academic performance, and even health concerns such as poor posture, sleep disorders,
and vision problems.

According to the 2022 Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA), 41% of
Filipino students are distracted by their own device use and 37% by other students using
devices during math classes. Globally, countries such as France, Spain, China, and
Australia have already adopted restrictions on smartphone use in schools, balancing
technology with structured learning environments.

Locally, the Department of Education (DepEd) has issued guidelines discouraging
cellphone use in classrooms, but enforcement has been inconsistent. This bill
institutionalizes a clear and enforceable policy across all public and private schools from
Kindergarten to Senior High School, ensuring that classrooms remain focused and
conducive to holistic development.

The measure also provides for monitoring and compliance to ensure that schools adhere
to the prohibition and that enforcement is consistent nationwide.

By passing this Act, Congress affirms its duty to protect the physical, intellectual, and
social well-being of Filipino learners. Limiting gadget use during class hours will foster
discipline, improve focus, and promote healthier learning environments for the youth.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT PROHIBITING THE USE OF MOBILE DEVICES AND OTHER ELECTRONIC
 GADGETS DURING CLASS HOURS FROM KINDERGARTEN TO SENIOR HIGH
     SCHOOL IN ALL PUBLIC AND PRIVATE EDUCATION INSTITUTIONS

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Electronic Gadget-Free
Schools Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote and protect the
physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being of our youth and learners.
Towards this end, policies must be crafted to safeguard their well-being and enhance
their potentials and skills through eliminating distractions caused by mobile devices and
other electronic gadgets and protecting them from the unwarranted effects of excessive
online and gaming use.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall mean:
a. Mobile Device refers to any form of portable electronic devices such as but not limited
to smartphones, cellular phones, tablet computers, pocket personal computer (PC),
palmtops, smartwatches, virtual reality (VR) headsets, smart glasses, wearable cameras,
and other similar devices which can store, send, receive, and move data through the
internet or wireless connection. b. Electronic Gadget refers to any portable electronic
device that can enable its user to make calls, capture photos, stream video, and play
games such as but not limited to laptops, video game consoles, virtual reality headsets,
smart glasses, wearable cameras, and other similar contraptions.
SEC. 4. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to all public and private education institutions
offering any level of the basic education curriculum.

SEC. 5. Prohibition on the Use of Mobile Devices and Other Electronic Gadgets by
Learners. — The Department of Education (DepEd) shall promulgate guidelines on the
prohibition of the use of mobile devices and other electronic gadgets by learners from
Kindergarten to Senior High School within the school premises during class hours except
as provided under Section 7 of this Act.

The DepEd shall likewise ensure that private education institutions formulate guidelines
in accordance with this Act and include the same in their respective handbooks and
school policies.

School premises shall include classrooms, laboratories, bathrooms, gymnasiums,
canteens or cafeterias, designated school club rooms, swimming pools, teachers'
lounges, and school offices.

SEC. 6. Prohibition on the Use of Mobile Devices and Other Electronic Gadgets by
Teachers and Faculty Members. — Teachers and faculty members are likewise
prohibited from using mobile devices and other electronic gadgets during their designated
teaching hours in classrooms, except as provided under Section 7 of this Act.

SEC. 7. Exceptions. — Mobile devices and other electronic gadgets shall not be used
within the school premises during class hours except in the following circumstances: a.
Learning-related exceptions, such as classroom presentations or class-based learning
activities, requiring the use of such technologies, or for learners for whom a reasonable
adjustment to a learning program is needed due to learning disability or difficulty; b. Health
and well-being-related exceptions, such as learners with specific health conditions that
may require mobile devices and other electronic gadgets, as certified by a medical doctor
and duly noted by the School Head; c. Exceptions related to managing risks, such as in
case of emergency, in response to a perceived threat or danger, or in case of field trips
and other school-related activities outside the school premises; d. Other analogous
circumstances where the use of mobile devices and other electronic gadgets are essential
to support the learning, education, health and protection of students, teachers, and other
non-teaching personnel within the school premises.

SEC. 8. Sanctions for Non-compliance. — All covered public and private education
institutions who fail to comply with the provisions of this Act shall be subject to sanctions
that may be imposed by the DepEd. Learners shall likewise be subject to appropriate
sanctions provided in the school policies set forth by the DepEd, for violation of Section
5 hereof.

SEC. 9. Monitoring and Compliance. — The Department of Education (DepEd) shall
monitor the implementation of this Act and ensure compliance by all public and private
schools. DepEd shall require schools to submit annual reports and may conduct
inspections to verify adherence.
SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The DepEd, in consultation with the
Department of Health, representatives from the Parent-Teacher Associations, relevant
government agencies, and other private stakeholders shall promulgate the rules and
regulations within ninety (90) days after the effectivity of this Act.

SEC. 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. — All other laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
administrative orders, rules and regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with
or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

SEC. 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
