REGULATING DEBT COLLECTION PRACTICES TO PROTECT DEBTORS FROM
ABUSES, HARASSMENT, UNFAIR TREATMENT, AND MISREPRESENTATION

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


        Article XII, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution envisions a national economy
that promotes a more equitable distribution of opportunities, income, and wealth, with
the ultimate goal of improving the quality of life of all Filipinos, especially the
underprivileged. Consistent with this principle, access to financial services must be
accompanied by adequate safeguards that protect consumers from unfair and abusive
practices.
        With the growing use of digital financial services and online lending
applications, borrowing money has become more accessible to Filipinos. However, this
has also led to increasing concerns over abusive and unfair debt collection practices,
including harassment, threats, misleading representations, and unauthorized
disclosure of borrowers' personal information. Despite existing safeguards under SEC
Memorandum Circular No. 18, s. 2019, NPC Circular No. 2020-01, and RA No. 11765,
or the Financial Products and Services Consumer Protection Act, abusive debt
collection practices continue to be reported and experienced by borrowers. These
practices not only violate their rights to privacy and fair treatment but may also cause
financial distress, emotional stress, and reputational harm. While creditors have the
right to collect legitimate debts, such collection must be carried out through lawful,
fair, and reasonable means. The obligation to repay a loan should never justify
harassment, intimidation, or public humiliation.

       This bill seeks to strengthen our consumer protection laws by prohibiting unfair
and abusive debt collection practices, thereby establishing minimum standards for
lawful debt collection and ensuring confidentiality and proper conduct in debt-related


                                           1
transactions. It likewise aims to balance the rights of creditors to recover legitimate
obligations with the rights of borrowers to fair and dignified treatment.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
urged.

4           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State
5    to prohibit and eliminate the use of abusive, deceptive, and unfair debt collection
6    practices to protect debtors against abuse and harassment from unscrupulous lenders
7    and debt collectors. Further, consistent with Republic Act No. 11765, also known as
8    the Financial Products and Services Consumer Protection Act, it is the policy of the
9    State to uphold the principles of fair treatment, transparency, and accountability in
10   debt collection.

22          SEC. 6. Prohibition Against Harassment or Abuse. Financing companies,
23   lending companies, and debt collectors shall not engage in any conduct the natural
24   consequence of which is to harass, oppress, or abuse any debtor in connection with
25   the collection of a debt. The following acts shall be considered a violation of this
26   section:
27                 (a)    The use, or threat of use, of violence or other criminal means to
28                        harm the physical person, reputation, or property of the debtor
29                        or their family;
30                 (b)    The use of insults, obscene or profane language or language the
31                        natural consequence of which is to abuse or shame the debtor or
32                        their family, or which amounts to a criminal act or offense under
33                        applicable laws;
34                 (c)    The disclosure, publication, or posting of the names and other
35                        personal information of the debtor who allegedly refuses to pay
36                        debts, except as may be allowed under Section 11 of this Act;
37                 (d)   The advertisement for sale of any debt to coerce payment of the
38                       debt;
39                 (e)   Causing the telephone or mobile phone of the debtor, their
40                       family, or any person other than the debtor in relation to Section
41                       4 of this Act, to ring repeatedly or continuously, or engaging any
42                       such person in telephone or mobile phone conversation

27             SEC. 8. Unfair Collection Practices. Creditors and debt collectors shall
28   not use unfair or unconscionable means to collect or attempt to collect any debt. The
29   following acts shall be considered a violation of this section:
30                 (a)     The collection of any amount Other than the principal obligation,
31                         including any interest, fee, charge, or incidental expense, unless
32                         Such amount is expressly authorized by the agreement creating
33                         the debt or permitted by law;
34                 (b)     The acceptance by a debt collector from any person of a check

36                         days, unless such person is notified in writing of the debt
37                         collector's intent to deposit such check or instrument not less
38                         than three (3) business days prior to such deposit;
39                 (c)     Depositing or threatening to deposit any postdated check or other
40                         postdated instrument prior to the date on such check or
41                         instrument;

 1                (d)    Causing charges to be made to any person for communications
 2                       by concealment of the true purpose of the communication,
 3                       including but not limited to collect telephone calls and telegram
 4                       fees;
 5                (e)    Taking or threatening to take any non-judicial action to effect
 6                       dispossession or disablement of property if -
 7                       (i)    There is no present right to possession of the property
 8                              claimed as collateral through an enforceable security
 9                              interest;
10                       (ii)   There is no present right to take possession of the

18              SEC. 10. Prohibited Agreements. Any agreement entered into by the

 6           SEC. 12. Issuance of Receipts. Every debt collector shall issue an
 7   acknowledgment receipt for all cash transactions, payments made in person, or
 8   payments made by the debtor. Such acknowledgment receipt shall contain the
 9   following information:
10          (a) The date the amount is collected or received;
11          (b) The name of the debtor;
12          (c) The name of the person from whom the debt collector acts on behalf of;
13          (d) The amount received from the debtor; and
14          (e) The signature of the debt collector.

16           SEC. 13. Settlement Agreement. Once a debt collector reaches a
17   settlement agreement with a debtor and the amount set in the settlement agreement
18   has been paid in full, the debt collector shall issue to the debtor a receipt containing
19   the following information:
20                 (a)    The amount paid;
21                 (b)    The name of the creditor or creditors;
22                 (c)    A statement that the amount received is in final settlement of the
23                        debt or debts owing; and
24                 (d)    The signature of the debt collector.

26                  SEC. 14. Request for Accounting. A debt collector must provide the
27   debtor, upon request, with a complete accounting of all the details of the debt:
28   Provided, That if such accounting is not in the possession of the debt collector, the
29   debt collector must request the creditor to provide the same. Provided, further, That
30   if within thirty (30) days after receiving a request for an accounting from the debt
31   collector, the creditor has not provided a complete accounting of the debt, the debt
32   collector must:
33                  (a)    Inform the debtor in writing that it cannot provide the accounting
34                         and the reasons for the same; and
35                  (b)    Cease all collection activity for that account and not resume
36                         collection activity until the accounting is provided.
37           A debtor may request a complete accounting only once every six (6) months,
38   unless the debt collector fails to provide the complete accounting as requested.
39   Provided, That in the case of short-term loans offered by creditors, the Securities and
40   Exchange Commission (SEC) may prescribe the appropriate periods for the provision
41   of the accounting and the frequency of such requests by the debtor.

28          SEC. 18. Solidary Liability. A creditor shall be responsible for ensuring that
29   its debt collectors, authorized representatives, or third-party service providers
30   engaged in debt collection comply with this Act and all applicable laws, rules and
31   regulations. The creditor shall be solidarily liable with such agents or service providers
32   for any violation of this Act committed in the course of debt collection.

34           SEC. 19. Debtor Complaints and Redress Mechanism. Financing and
35   lending companies shall establish and maintain a customer service department who
36   shall be responsible for promptly addressing questions and concerns of debtors and
37   which shall serve as an internal complaint-handling and redress mechanism that is
38   accessible, transparent, and free of charge. The SEC shall oversee compliance with
39   this requirement and provide an external redress mechanism for escalation of
40   unresolved complaints, including an accessible online platform for reporting violations
41   of this Act.
