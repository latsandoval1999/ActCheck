                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Republic Act No. 9165 or the “Comprehensive Dangerous Drugs Act of 2002,” has long
served as the legal framework for the country’s campaign against illegal drugs. However, more
than two decades since its enactment, the illegal drugs landscape in the Philippines has
substantially evolved. The volume of confiscated illegal drugs, valued at billions of pesos,
continues to rise, while drug syndicates have become more sophisticated in their methods. Law
enforcers must therefore keep pace through improved intelligence-gathering, upgraded
investigative tools, and enhanced operational capacity, all of which require substantial funding
support.

        Over the years, the dynamic nature of the illegal drug trade has exposed several procedural
and operational gaps in the implementation of Republic Act No. 9165. Certain provisions of the
law have become less responsive to the present realities, thereby giving rise to interpretative
challenges, operational inefficiencies, and difficulties in prosecution. In view of this
circumstances, this proposed measure seeks to amend Sections 20, 21, 83, and 84 of Republic Act
No. 9165 in order to make the law more adaptive to current demands. The amendments aim to
strengthen procedural standards, improve operational efficiency, minimize leakages in anti-drug
operations, and reinforce institutional leadership.

        Specifically, the proposed bill provides for the following: (1) The confiscation and
forfeiture of proceeds or instruments of unlawful acts in favor of the agency conducting the
operating unit; (2) The exemption of media practitioners from serving as (insulating) witnesses in
the inventory of seized items during anti-illegal drug operations; (3) The establishment of PDEA’s
own destruction facility for the proper and timely disposition of dangerous drugs, controlled
precursors, and essential chemicals; (4) The creation of additional PDEA offices and services,
including the establishment of Country Anti-Drug Attaché posts abroad to enhance international
coordination; and (5) The grant of parity in salaries, allowances, benefits, and privileges to PDEA
personnel, equivalent to those received by the uniformed and non-uniformed personnel of the
Philippine National Police (PNP).

      Through these amendments, the measure intends to strengthen the institutional framework
of PDEA, modernize its operations, and ensure that the country’s anti-drug campaign remains
effective, transparent, and aligned with current enforcement realities.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

             “COMPREHENSIVE DANGEROUS DRUGS ACT OF 2002”

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Section 20 is hereby amended to read as follows:

    Section 20. Confiscation and Forfeiture of the Proceeds or Instruments of the Unlawful
    Act, Including the Properties or Proceeds Derived from the Illegal Trafficking of
    Dangerous Drugs and/or Precursors and Essential Chemicals. – Every penalty imposed
    for the unlawful importation, sale, trading, administration, dispensation, delivery,
    distribution, transportation or manufacture of any dangerous drug and/or controlled
    precursor and essential chemical, the cultivation or culture of plants which are sources of
    dangerous drugs, and the possession of any equipment, instrument, apparatus and other
    paraphernalia for dangerous drugs including other laboratory equipment, shall carry with
    it the confiscation and forfeiture IN FAVOR OF THE AGENCY OF THE
    OPERATING UNITS TO AUGMENT ITS RESOURCES IN THE CAMPAIGN
    AGAINST ILLEGAL DRUGS, of all the proceeds and properties derived from the
    unlawful act, including, but not limited to, money and other assets obtained thereby, and
    the instruments or tools with which the particular unlawful act was committed, unless they
    are the property of a third person not liable for the unlawful act, but those which are not of
    lawful commerce shall be ordered destroyed without delay pursuant to the provisions of
    Section 21 of this Act.

    IN CASE OF CONVICTION, the Regional Trial Court SHALL INCLUDE IN ITS
    DECISION the confiscation and forfeiture of all the proceeds AND INSTRUMENTS
    IN THE COMMISSION of the offense IN FAVOR OF THE AGENCY OF THE
    OPERATING UNITS. MOREOVER, PROPERTIES AND ASSETS THAT ARE
    MANIFESTLY OUT OF PROPORTION TO THE LAWFUL INCOME OF THE
    ACCUSED MAY           BE THE SUBJECT OF SEPARATE ACTION                              OR
    PROCEEDING.

    During the pendency of the case in the Regional Trial Court, no property, or income
    derived therefrom, which may be confiscated and forfeited, shall be disposed, alienated
    or transferred and the same shall be in custodia legis and no bond shall be admitted for
    the release of the same.

    FURTHER, IN THE MAINTENANCE AND CUSTODY OF NON-DRUG
    EVIDENCE, SEIZED OR CONFISCATED FROM ANTI-ILLEGAL DRUG
    OPERATIONS, THE AGENCY OF THE OPERATING UNIT CONCERNED
    SHALL ESTABLISH ITS OWN NDEV FACILITY.

SECTION 5. Separability Clause. – If for any reason any provision of this Act or portion hereof
shall be declared unconstitutional, such declaration shall not invalidate the other parts which shall
remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 6. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or part thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

SECTION 7. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following its publication in
at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
