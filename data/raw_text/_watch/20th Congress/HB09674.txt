                           AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A RECOVERY MECHANISM FOR ERRONEOUS ELECTRONIC
     FUND TRANSFERS, PROVIDING REMEDIES AGAINST UNJUST
   ENRICHMENT, DEFINING THE RESPONSIBILITIES OF FINANCIAL
 INSTITUTIONS AND PAYMENT SERVICE PROVIDERS, AND FOR OTHER
                         PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        In the age of instant digital payments, a single wrong digit can mean the loss
of hard-earned money. While the law already rejects unjust enrichment, ordinary
consumers who mistakenly transfer funds to the wrong account or mobile number are
often left without a clear, prompt, and effective means of recovery. This premise is
connected to the rapid growth of digital payments, mobile wallets, online banking,
and instant fund transfers has made financial transactions more convenient for millions
of Filipinos. With a few taps on a mobile phone, money may now be sent instantly to
another person through a bank account, e-wallet, mobile number, QR code, or other
digital payment channel.

        In many cases, the online sender discovers the mistake only after the transfer
has already been completed. The sender is then left to rely on customer service
escalation, which at times is very tedious, and the cooperation of the unintended
recipient, or costly and time-consuming legal remedies. Under existing principles of
civil law, no person should unjustly enrich himself or herself at the expense of another.
Where a person receives money without legal right and by reason of mistake, the
obligation to return arises. Yet, while the substantive right to recover exists, there
remains no clear, fast, uniform, and accessible statutory mechanism for ordinary
consumers to recover funds erroneously transferred through digital payment systems.
This gap disproportionately affects workers, small entrepreneurs, farmers, fisherfolk,
students, overseas Filipino families, public transport workers, and ordinary consumers
who rely heavily on digital wallets and electronic fund transfers for daily transactions.
Even a modest amount sent to the wrong account may be significant to a Filipino
household.



                                           1
        This bill seeks to establish a fair and practical recovery and redress mechanism
for erroneous electronic fund transfers. It requires covered financial institutions and
payment service providers to adopt preventive safeguards, including clear transaction
confirmation prompts. It also provides for a timely reporting mechanism, provisional
holding of disputed funds when still available, notice to the unintended recipient,
consent-based return, and a structured process for contested claims. The measure
carefully balances the rights of both the sender and the recipient. It does not authorize
arbitrary or automatic reversal of transactions. Instead, it provides a clear mechanism
to verify the claim, protect the funds where appropriate, and return the money when
the mistake is admitted, uncontested, or duly established. It also protects good-faith
recipients while imposing liability on persons who knowingly retain, conceal, withdraw,
transfer, or spend money that they received by mistake and without lawful right. By
establishing a practical legal framework for erroneous fund transfers, this measure
strengthens consumer protection, promotes trust in digital financial services, prevents
unjust enrichment, and ensures that the benefits of financial technology are not
undermined by the absence of accessible remedies.

       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

 2         SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Erroneous Transfer
 3   Recovery Act.”
 4
 5          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State
 6   to protect consumers, promote trust and confidence in digital financial services,
 7   strengthen the integrity of electronic payment systems, and ensure that no person
 8   shall unjustly enrich himself or herself at the expense of another.
 9
10          Towards this end, the State shall establish a fair, prompt, accessible, and due-
11   process-based mechanism for the recovery of funds erroneously transferred through
12   electronic fund transfer systems, digital wallets, online banking platforms, mobile
13   payment applications, and other similar financial channels.
14
15          The State shall likewise ensure that the mechanism established under this Act
16   protects both the sender and the recipient, prevents abuse, respects data privacy, and
17   preserves the stability and finality of legitimate financial transactions.
18
19          SEC. 3. Coverage. This Act shall apply to erroneous electronic fund transfers
20   involving banks, non-bank financial institutions, electronic money issuers, operators
21   of payment systems, payment service providers, remittance and transfer companies,
22   and other entities supervised or regulated by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas that
23   enable the electronic transfer of funds.

 1          This Act shall cover fund transfers made through, but not limited to, the
 2   following:
 3       a) Bank-to-bank electronic transfers;
 4       b) E-wallet-to-e-wallet transfers;

13      SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act:
14            a. Bangko Sentral refers to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP).
15            b. Covered Institution refers to any bank, non-bank financial institution,
16               electronic money issuer, operator of payment system, payment service
17               provider, remittance and transfer company, or other entity supervised
18               or regulated by the Bangko Sentral that enables or facilitates electronic
19               fund transfers.
20            c. Electronic Fund Transfer refers to any transfer of funds initiated through
21               electronic, digital, online, mobile, or other similar channels.
22            d. Erroneous Fund Transfer refers to an electronic fund transfer made by
23               mistake to an unintended recipient, wrong account, wrong mobile
24               number, wrong wallet, wrong QR code, or in an incorrect amount due
25               to the sender’s error, system error, or other analogous circumstance,
26               where the recipient has no lawful right to receive or retain the funds.
27               This does not apply to transfers or payments voluntarily made pursuant
28               to a sale, purchase, loan, investment, or other commercial transaction.
29            e. Originating Institution refers to the covered institution from which the
30               funds were sent or through which the sender initiated the erroneous
31               fund transfer.
32            f. Receiving Institution refers to the covered institution that received or
33               credited the funds to the account, wallet, or payment instrument of the
34               unintended recipient.
35            g. Sender refers to the person who initiated or caused the electronic fund
36               transfer.
37            h. Unintended Recipient refers to the person or account holder who
38               received funds through an erroneous fund transfer and who has no
39               lawful right to retain the same.
40            i. Provisional Hold refers to the temporary restriction imposed on the
41               amount subject of an erroneous fund transfer claim, without freezing
42               the entire account or wallet of the recipient, unless otherwise authorized
43               by law.
44            j. Disputed Funds refer to the amount claimed to have been erroneously
45               transferred, including any remaining portion thereof that is still available
46               in the recipient’s account, wallet, or payment instrument.

 1          SEC. 5. Pre-Transaction Safeguards. Covered institutions shall adopt
 2   reasonable safeguards to prevent erroneous fund transfers, including clear,
 3   conspicuous, and user-friendly confirmation prompts before a transfer is completed.
 4   The confirmation prompt shall display, whenever technically feasible and consistent
 5   with data privacy laws, the following:
 6             a. Amount to be transferred;
 7             b. Masked name or verified account name of the intended recipient;
 8             c. account number, mobile number, wallet number, or other recipient
 9                 identifier, with appropriate masking;
10             d. Transaction fees, if any; and
11             e. A clear warning that the sender must verify the recipient details before
12                 confirming the transaction.
13          Covered institutions shall provide an additional warning when the transfer is
14   made to a new or unsaved recipient, or where the recipient name cannot be verified
15   or displayed.
16
17          SEC. 6. Right to Report an Erroneous Fund Transfer. A sender who claims
18   to have made an erroneous fund transfer may file an Erroneous Fund Transfer
19   Recovery Request with the originating institution through any available branch,
20   hotline, mobile application, online platform, electronic mail, or other accessible
21   reporting channel.
22          Covered institutions shall maintain a twenty-four-seven (24/7) reporting
23   channel for erroneous fund transfer complaints and shall provide the sender with an
24   acknowledgment, reference number, and list of documentary requirements.
25   The request shall contain, at minimum:
26             a) Name and contact details of the sender;
27             b) Date and time of the transaction;
28             c) Amount transferred;
29             d) Transaction reference number;
30             e) Account, wallet, mobile number, QR code, or other identifier used;
31             f) Reason why the transfer is claimed to be erroneous; and
32             g) Proof of the transaction and such other information as may be required
33                under the implementing rules and regulations.

34          The sender or claimant shall certify, through written, electronic, recorded, or
35   other verifiable means, that the information and documents submitted in support of
36   the Erroneous Fund Transfer Recovery Request are true and correct to the best of his
37   or her knowledge. The certification shall include a notice that the filing of a false,
38   fraudulent, malicious, or materially misleading claim shall subject the claimant to civil,
39   administrative, and criminal liability under this Act and other applicable laws.

40         SEC. 7. Duties of the Originating Institution. Upon receipt of a sufficient
41   Erroneous Fund Transfer Recovery Request, the originating institution shall:

42             a. Acknowledge the complaint and issue a reference number;
43             b. Conduct a preliminary verification of the transaction based on available
44                records;


                                                 3
 1             c. Notify the receiving institution not later than the next banking day from
 2                receipt of sufficient information;
 3             d. Assist the sender in completing the required documentation;
 4             e. Provide periodic updates to the sender; and
 5             f. Coordinate with the receiving institution for the verification, provisional
 6                holding, and possible return of the disputed funds.
 7                The originating institution shall not dismiss a request solely on the
 8                ground that the transfer was completed, where the sender has
 9                presented sufficient information to identify and verify the transaction.
10
11          SEC. 8. Duties of the Receiving Institution. Upon notice from the
12   originating institution, the receiving institution shall:
13              a) Verify whether the disputed funds, or any portion thereof, remain
14                  available in the account, wallet, or payment instrument of the
15                  unintended recipient;
16              b) When warranted by the records and when funds remain available,
17                  impose a provisional hold only on the disputed amount or remaining
18                  available portion thereof;
19              c) Notify the unintended recipient of the claim and the provisional hold, if
20                  any;
21              d) Provide the unintended recipient with an opportunity to consent to the
22                  return or to contest the claim;
23              e) Coordinate with the originating institution for the resolution of the
24                  request; and
25              f) Act within the periods prescribed under this Act and its implementing
26                  rules and regulations.
27
28        The receiving institution shall not impose a hold exceeding the disputed
29   amount, unless otherwise authorized by law, court order, or competent authority.
30
31          SEC. 9. Provisional Hold on Disputed Funds. Upon receipt of a verified
32   report of an alleged erroneous fund transfer, the receiving covered institution shall
33   impose a provisional hold on the disputed funds, or any available portion thereof,
34   when all of the following conditions are present:
35             a) Transaction can be identified through the records of the covered
36                 institutions;
37             b) The disputed funds, or any portion thereof, remain available in the
38                 account, wallet, payment instrument, or other financial account of the
39                 unintended recipient;
40             c) The claim is supported by prima facie evidence, as may be defined by
41                 the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas; and
42             d) The imposition of the provisional hold is technically and legally possible.
43                 The provisional hold shall be limited only to the amount subject of the
44                 alleged erroneous fund transfer and shall not extend to unrelated funds,
45                 accounts, wallets, or transactions of the unintended recipient, unless
46                 otherwise authorized by law, court order, or competent authority.
47

 1           The provisional hold shall remain effective for a period not exceeding fifteen
 2   (15) banking days from notice to the unintended recipient, unless extended by consent
 3   of the parties, order of a court, directive of a competent authority, or such other
 4   circumstances as may be provided in the implementing rules and regulations. The
 5   imposition of a provisional hold shall not be deemed a determination of ownership,
 6   fault, fraud, or criminal liability, but shall serve only as a temporary protective measure
 7   pending verification, consent-based return, dispute resolution, or lawful determination
 8   of the claim.
 9
10          SEC. 10. Notice to the Unintended Recipient. Upon receipt of a verified
11   claim involving an alleged erroneous fund transfer, the receiving covered institution
12   shall promptly notify the unintended recipient that a claim has been made over funds
13   credited to his or her account, wallet, payment instrument, or other financial account.

14          The notice shall state the amount claimed, the date and time of the transaction,
15   the transaction reference number, where available, and the fact that the funds are
16   alleged to have been transferred by mistake. It shall likewise inform the unintended
17   recipient of his or her right to consent to the return of the disputed funds or to contest
18   the claim within the period prescribed by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas. The notice
19   shall also clearly state the possible consequences of bad-faith withdrawal,
20   concealment, transfer, use, conversion, or refusal to return funds received without
21   lawful right, without prejudice to the rights and remedies of the parties under existing
22   laws.

23          Notice under this Section shall be made through the registered communication
24   channels of the unintended recipient, including in-app notification, text message,
25   electronic mail, registered mobile number, or such other secure and verifiable means
26   as may be allowed by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas.

27          For purposes of this Act, notice shall be deemed sufficient upon proof of
28   successful electronic transmission to the registered communication channel of the
29   unintended recipient, or upon documented notification attempts through the available
30   registered communication channels, as confirmed by the records of the receiving
31   covered institution and in accordance with the standards prescribed by the Bangko
32   Sentral ng Pilipinas.

33
34          SEC. 11. Consent-Based Return of Funds. If the unintended recipient
35   consents to the return of the disputed funds, the receiving institution shall return the
36   funds to the sender through the originating institution within the period prescribed by
37   the Bangko Sentral.
38
39          The consent may be given electronically, in writing, through an in-app
40   confirmation, recorded customer service channel, or other verifiable means.
41   No fee shall be charged to the sender or the unintended recipient for the return of
42   funds under this Section, except such reasonable charges as may be approved or
43   authorized by the Bangko Sentral.
44

 1          SEC. 12. Uncontested Claims. If the unintended recipient fails to respond
 2   within seven (7) banking days from receipt of notice, and the available records and
 3   submitted documents sufficiently establish that the transfer was erroneous, the
 4   receiving institution may, in coordination with the originating institution, return the
 5   disputed funds to the sender.
 6          The Bangko Sentral shall prescribe safeguards to ensure that the unintended
 7   recipient is given adequate notice and opportunity to contest the claim before any
 8   return is made under this Section.
 9
10          SEC. 13. Contested Claims. If the unintended recipient contests the claim,
11   the covered institutions shall not make a final determination of ownership over the
12   disputed funds, unless expressly authorized by law, court order, directive of a
13   competent authority, or the implementing rules and regulations issued pursuant to
14   this Act.
15          In such cases, the originating institution and the receiving institution shall
16   promptly inform both the sender and the unintended recipient that the claim has been
17   contested. The covered institutions shall assist the parties in the voluntary resolution
18   or mediation of the dispute, consistent with the procedures prescribed by the Bangko
19   Sentral ng Pilipinas. Subject to the Data Privacy Act of 2012, bank secrecy laws, and
20   other applicable confidentiality rules, each party shall be furnished or allowed access
21   to the necessary transaction records pertaining to his or her own account, wallet,
22   payment instrument, or transaction, including such records as may be necessary to
23   establish the fact, amount, date, time, and reference number of the disputed transfer.
24          Where available, the covered institutions shall refer the parties to the
25   appropriate consumer assistance, mediation, or dispute resolution mechanism under
26   the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas or other competent authority. The sender shall likewise
27   be informed of the right to pursue civil, small claims, criminal, administrative, or other
28   remedies available under existing laws.
29          The provisional hold, if any, shall be governed by the period and conditions
30   provided under this Act and its implementing rules and regulations, unless extended
31   by consent of the parties, order of a court, or directive of a competent authority.
32
33          SEC. 14. Criminal Liability. Any person who, after receipt of valid notice
34   and with intent to gain or to permanently deprive the sender of the funds, knowingly
35   withdraws, transfers, conceals, converts, or uses funds received through an erroneous
36   fund transfer and without lawful right shall, upon conviction, be punished by a fine of
37   not less than the amount wrongfully retained or converted but not more than three
38   (3) times such amount, or imprisonment of arresto mayor, or both, at the discretion
39   of the court.
40          No criminal liability shall arise from mere receipt of an erroneous fund transfer,
41   good-faith delay, inability to immediately respond, or a genuine dispute as to
42   entitlement to the funds.
43          This Section shall be without prejudice to prosecution under the Revised Penal
44   Code, the Cybercrime Prevention Act, the Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act, and
45   other applicable laws when the acts complained of constitute offenses under said laws.

 1          SEC. 15. Protection of Good-Faith Recipients. An unintended recipient
 2   who promptly reports, discloses, preserves, or returns funds received through an
 3   erroneous fund transfer shall not be subject to criminal, civil, or administrative liability
 4   solely by reason of having received the funds by mistake.
 5          Covered institutions shall provide simple and accessible channels through which
 6   recipients may report and return funds that they believe were erroneously credited to
 7   their accounts, wallets, or payment instruments.
 8
 9           SEC. 16. Duties and Liability of Covered Institutions. Covered
10   institutions shall adopt policies, systems, and procedures necessary to implement this
11   Act, including:
12               a) Clear transaction confirmation safeguards;
13               b) Accessible reporting channels;
14               c) Timely acknowledgment and tracking of complaints;
15               d) Inter-institution coordination;
16               e) Provisional holding procedures;
17               f) Notice and response mechanisms;
18               g) Consumer assistance and dispute resolution procedures; and
19               h) Data protection and recordkeeping standards. A covered institution shall
20                    not be liable for the sender’s own encoding error when it has complied
21                    with the requirements of this Act and its implementing rules and
22                    regulations.
23           However, a covered institution may be held administratively liable for
24   unjustified failure to act on a sufficient report, failure to coordinate with the concerned
25   institution, failure to notify the unintended recipient, or failure to impose a provisional
26   hold when the conditions under Section 9 of this Act are clearly present, the disputed
27   funds or any portion thereof remain available, and the imposition of such hold is
28   technically and legally possible.
29
30           No liability shall attach when the disputed funds are no longer available at the
31   time the receiving covered institution receives sufficient notice, when the transaction
32   cannot be reasonably identified from available records, when the claim is not
33   supported by prima facie evidence, or when the provisional hold cannot be imposed
34   due to technical, legal, regulatory, or operational limitations not attributable to bad
35   faith, gross negligence, or willful misconduct of the covered institution.
36
37           SEC. 17. Limited Immunity for Good-Faith Compliance. A covered
38   institution, including its directors, officers, and employees, shall not be held liable for
39   imposing a provisional hold or returning disputed funds when such action is done in
40   good faith, in accordance with this Act, its implementing rules and regulations, a court
41   order, or a directive of a competent authority.
42   This immunity shall not apply in cases of gross negligence, bad faith, fraud, willful
43   misconduct, or violation of data privacy and bank secrecy laws.
44
45          SEC. 18. Data Privacy and Confidentiality. All proceedings, notices,
46   disclosures, and exchanges of information under this Act shall comply with the Data
47   Privacy Act of 2012, bank secrecy laws, and other applicable confidentiality rules.

 8          SEC. 19. Transaction Records as Prima Facie Evidence. Certified
 9   transaction records, electronic confirmations, notices, reference numbers, account
10   statements, and reports issued by covered institutions in connection with an erroneous
11   fund transfer shall be admissible as prima facie evidence in civil, criminal,
12   administrative, small claims, or other proceedings, subject to the Rules on Electronic

14
15          SEC. 20. Small Claims and Judicial Remedies. Nothing in this Act shall
16   prevent the sender from pursuing recovery through the appropriate court, including
17   the small claims procedure where applicable.
18          Courts shall give due consideration to the summary nature of erroneous fund
19   transfer claims and may direct the production of relevant transaction records from
20   covered institutions, subject to applicable laws on confidentiality, data privacy, and
21   bank secrecy.
22
23           SEC. 21. Consumer Education. The Bangko Sentral, in coordination with
24   covered institutions and relevant government agencies, shall conduct public
25   information campaigns on safe digital fund transfers, proper verification of recipient
26   details, reporting of erroneous transfers, and the legal duty to return funds received
27   by mistake.
28   Covered institutions shall regularly remind users to verify the recipient’s account name,
29   mobile number, QR code, or other payment identifier before confirming a transaction.
30
31           SEC. 22. Administrative Sanctions. The Bangko Sentral may impose
32   administrative sanctions, fines, or penalties upon covered institutions, their directors,
33   officers, or responsible employees for violations of this Act, its implementing rules and
34   regulations, or lawful directives issued pursuant thereto, in accordance with existing
35   banking, payment systems, and financial consumer protection laws.
36   The Bangko Sentral may likewise require covered institutions to adopt corrective
37   measures, improve systems, refund improper charges, submit compliance reports, or
38   undertake consumer redress measures.
