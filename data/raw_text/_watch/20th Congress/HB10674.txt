                              EXPLANATORY NOTE


Article II, Section 5 of the Philippine Constitution enshrines the principle that the
maintenance of a just and dynamic social order shall guarantee the prosperity
and independence of the nation and free the people from poverty through policies
that provide adequate social services and promote a rising standard of living.

Over the years, the continuous escalation of the cost of living—driven by
inflationary pressures on basic commodities, utility fees, housing, and healthcare
expenses—has significantly eroded the purchasing power of the Filipino
workforce. While the enactment of Republic Act No. 10963 (otherwise known as
the TRAIN Law) provided commendable relief by restructuring personal income
taxes, the economic landscape has drastically shifted. The current tax-exemption
ceiling of Two Hundred Fifty Thousand Pesos (P250,000) annually, established
years ago, no longer reflects the economic realities faced by ordinary wage earners
and middle-class families today.

An obvious mismatch currently exists between fixed personal exemption
thresholds and contemporary living costs, forcing low- and middle-income
earners to shoulder heavy tax burdens that eat deeply into funds required for
basic survival and family welfare.

Raising the tax exemption ceiling to Four Hundred Eighty Thousand Pesos
(P480,000) annually corrects this imbalance. It ensures that the working class is
granted genuine fiscal breathing room, directly injecting much-needed liquidity
into households, stimulating domestic consumption, and fostering a fairer, more
equitable progressive tax system.

Furthermore, this adjustment aligns the 8% preferential tax option for self-
employed individuals and professionals under Section 24(A)(2)(b) with the
updated baseline, protecting small-scale entrepreneurs and independent
practitioners from premature taxation.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

complete publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of
