                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines continues to face persistent challenges in unemployment,
underemployment, and informal employment, particularly at the community level
where many workers remain outside formal labor market systems. Despite
existing employment and livelihood programs, the absence of a standardized and
updated barangay-level employment database limits the government’s ability to
efficiently identify jobseekers, match workers with opportunities, and deliver
targeted interventions.

        Barangays, as the government units closest to the people, are in the best
position to gather accurate and timely information on the employment status,
skills, and livelihood needs of residents. Establishing a Barangay Employment
Registry (BER) will strengthen grassroots labor market information systems and
improve coordination among local government units (LGUs), Public
Employment Service Offices (PESOs), and national government agencies.

      This bill seeks to institutionalize a standardized Barangay Employment
Registry nationwide to facilitate employment matching, skills training, livelihood
  support, and evidence-based labor planning. It likewise promotes inter-agency
  coordination, digital integration with existing employment systems, and
  compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 to ensure responsible handling of
  personal information.

        By creating a reliable community-based employment registry system, this
  measure aims to expand access to jobs and economic opportunities while
  strengthening inclusive and responsive local employment governance.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Barangay
Employment Registry Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Pursuant to Article II, Section 18 and Article
XIII of the 1987 Constitution, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote full, productive, and decent employment opportunities for all Filipinos
and to ensure that employment facilitation, skills development, and livelihood
interventions effectively reach communities and individuals in need.
      Toward this end, the State shall institutionalize a community-based
employment targeting and labor market information system through the
establishment of a regularly updated Barangay Employment Registry that shall
serve as the primary local database of employment profiles, skills, qualifications,
and livelihood interests of residents.


      The State likewise recognizes the role of local government units (LGUs),
Public Employment Service Offices (PESOs), and community stakeholders in
promoting inclusive economic participation and evidence-based labor planning.


SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
hereby defined:
   a) “Barangay Employment Registry (BER)” refers to the official barangay-
      level database containing employment, skills, educational, and livelihood
      information of residents;
   b) “Underemployed” refers to employed persons who express the desire for
      additional hours of work in their present job, an additional job, or a new
      job with longer working hours;
   c) “Unemployed” refers to persons who are without work, currently available
      for work, and actively seeking work;
   d) “Informal Worker” refers to self-employed individuals, unpaid family
      workers, or workers employed in enterprises not formally registered or
      regulated under existing laws;
   e) “Public Employment Service Office (PESO)” refers to the non-fee
      charging multi-employment service facility established or maintained
      pursuant to Republic Act No. 10691;




                                                                                  2
   f) “Labor Market Information” refers to quantitative and qualitative data and
      analyses     related   to employment, unemployment, skills       demand,
      occupations, wages, and labor trends;
   g) “Barangay Employment Focal Person” refers to the designated barangay
      personnel or official responsible for the implementation and maintenance
      of the BER.


SEC. 4. Objectives. – This Act aims to:
   a) Establish a standardized and integrated employment registry system in
      every barangay;
   b) Identify and classify unemployed, underemployed, self-employed, and
      informally employed residents for purposes of employment facilitation and
      policy planning;
   c) Facilitate   efficient job matching, skills training, entrepreneurship
      development, and livelihood support;
   d) Strengthen coordination among LGUs, PESOs, and national government
      agencies involved in labor and employment services;
   e) Generate reliable community-level labor market information to support
      evidence-based planning and policymaking; and
   f) Enhance access of residents to government employment, training, and
      livelihood programs.


SEC. 5. Establishment of the Barangay Employment Registry (BER). – Every
barangay nationwide shall establish, maintain, and regularly update a Barangay
Employment Registry (BER), which shall contain relevant employment and labor
market information on residents, including but not limited to:
   a) Basic personal and demographic information;
   b) Educational attainment;

                                                                               3
   c) Technical skills, certifications, and work experience;
   d) Employment       status,   including   whether   employed,     unemployed,
      underemployed, self-employed, or engaged in informal work;
   e) Preferred occupation, industry, or training program;
   f) Availability for local employment, remote work, overseas employment, or
      relocation;
   g) Interest in livelihood, entrepreneurship, or enterprise development
      programs; and
   h) Such other information as may be determined necessary by the Department
      of Labor and Employment (DOLE), subject to existing laws and
      regulations.


   Participation in the BER shall be voluntary and free of charge.


   The BER shall be updated at least twice every year and may be updated more
frequently as determined by the concerned LGU.


   The BER shall, whenever practicable, be digitally maintained and integrated
with the databases and information systems of the Public Employment Service
Office (PESO), PhilJobNet, and other relevant government labor market
information systems.


   The DOLE shall ensure interoperability of the BER with existing national
government databases and digital systems, consistent with applicable laws and
regulations on data privacy, cybersecurity, and e-governance.


SEC. 6. Implementing Agencies and Functions. – The Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE), in coordination with the Department of the Interior and

                                                                               4
Local Government (DILG), the National Economic and Development Authority
(NEDA), the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA),
the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), the Department of Social Welfare
and Development (DSWD), and concerned LGUs, shall serve as the lead
implementing agency of this Act. For this purpose, the DOLE shall:
   a) Develop standardized forms, data collection tools, digital platforms, and
      operational protocols for the implementation of the BER;
   b) Formulate guidelines for data gathering, validation, updating, sharing, and
      utilization;
   c) Provide capacity-building, technical assistance, and training to barangay
      personnel, PESO staff, and LGU implementers;
   d) Monitor and evaluate compliance, data accuracy, and operational
      effectiveness of the BER system;
   e) Ensure interoperability and integration of BER data with national
      employment and labor market information systems; and
   f) Establish a centralized monitoring and reporting system for BER
      implementation nationwide.


SEC. 7. Responsibilities of Barangays and Local Government Units. – Every
Barangay Council shall:
   a) Designate a Barangay Employment Focal Person from among existing
      barangay personnel or officials;
   b) Conduct regular registration drives, community outreach activities, and
      information dissemination campaigns to encourage resident participation
      in the BER;
   c) Coordinate with the city or municipal PESO in the validation,
      management, and utilization of registry data;



                                                                                5
   d) Assist residents in accessing employment opportunities, training
      programs, and livelihood interventions; and
   e) Submit periodic reports to the concerned city or municipal government and
      DOLE, in accordance with the implementing rules and regulations.


   City and municipal governments shall provide technical and administrative
support to barangays in the implementation of the BER.


   Provincial governments shall monitor and coordinate BER implementation
within their respective jurisdictions and may provide supplemental funding and
technical assistance.


SEC. 8. Use of Registry Data. – Data gathered through the BER shall be used
exclusively for legitimate governmental and public interest purposes, including:
   a) Employment facilitation and job matching;
   b) Skills training, scholarship, and competency development programs;
   c) Livelihood and enterprise development initiatives;
   d) Labor market research, planning, and policy formulation; and
   e) Targeting and implementation of social protection and economic inclusion
      programs.


   No data collected under this Act shall be sold, transferred, disclosed, or used
for commercial, partisan political, election, or unauthorized profiling activities.


SEC. 9. Data Privacy and Confidentiality. – The collection, processing, storage,
sharing, and use of all personal information under this Act shall be subject to the
provisions of Data Privacy Act of 2012 and other applicable laws, rules, and
regulations.

                                                                                      6
       Appropriate safeguards shall be adopted to ensure the confidentiality,
integrity, accessibility, and security of all registry data.


       Only authorized personnel and agencies shall have access to personally
identifiable information contained in the BER.


SEC. 10. Incentives and Program Convergence. – Barangays and LGUs that
maintain functional, updated, and compliant Barangay Employment Registries
shall be prioritized, subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations, in the
allocation and implementation of employment, training, and livelihood programs,
including but not limited to:
   a) DOLE employment facilitation and livelihood assistance programs;
   b) TESDA scholarship and skills training programs;
   c) Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) livelihood and enterprise
       development assistance;
   d) Social protection and economic inclusion programs of the Department of
       Social Welfare and Development (DSWD); and
   e) Other similar national government programs.


SEC. 11. Annual Reporting to Congress. – The DOLE shall submit an annual
report to Congress on the implementation of this Act, which shall include:
   a) The status of BER establishment and functionality nationwide;
   b) Statistics on registered residents and employment classifications;
   c) Utilization of BER data for employment and livelihood interventions;
   d) Challenges encountered and recommendations for policy improvement;
       and



                                                                              7
   e) Such other information as may be necessary to evaluate the effectiveness
      of the BER system.


SEC. 12. Funding. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOLE and DILG.
Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary for the continued implementation
of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


      LGUs may appropriate additional funds from their respective local budgets
for the implementation and enhancement of the BER.


SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOLE, in coordination with the DILG, NEDA,
TESDA, DTI, DSWD, the National Privacy Commission, representatives from
LGUs, PESOs, and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary
rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.


SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.


SEC. 16. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
