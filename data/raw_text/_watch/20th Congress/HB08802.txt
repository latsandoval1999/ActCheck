                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to guarantee free essential medical and dental services for
indigent children, in recognition of the State’s duty to protect and promote the right
to health of every Filipino child.

       The reality of poverty creates a formidable barrier between impoverished
families and essential clinical care. Data from the United Nations Children’s Fund
(UNICEF) reveals that one (1) in four (4) children globally live in severe child food
poverty, and there are approximately two million (2,000,000) children in the
Philippines who experience severe child food poverty. The risks attributed to this
manifest in preventable illnesses, untreated conditions, and poor oral health
outcomes, especially among indigent children who lack regular access to healthcare
services.

       Regular medical and dental care plays an important role in early detection,
and children who receive consistent healthcare support are more likely to grow
healthy and have an improved educational performance. However, indigent families
often prioritize immediate subsistence over preventive care, which results in delayed
treatment and higher long-term health costs.

       This proposed measure ensures that essential healthcare becomes accessible at
the point of need and reinforces the State’s commitment in the health and nutritional
security of indigent children.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
PROVIDING FREE MEDICAL AND DENTAL SERVICES TO INDIGENT
CHILDREN, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Indigent Children Free
Medical and Dental Service Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of children, and to ensure their access to
essential healthcare services. Towards this end, the State shall prioritize indigent
children in the delivery of accessible, affordable, and quality medical and dental
services.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

          a. Indigent children refers to persons below eighteen (18) years of age
       who have no visible means of support, or whose parents or guardians have
       no means of providing for their immediate medical and dental needs. They
       shall be certified as indigent by the Department of Social Welfare and
       Development (DSWD);

          b. Medical Services refer to preventive, curative, and rehabilitative health
       services, including but not limited to consultations, diagnostic services,
       medicines, and hospitalization; and

           c. Dental Services refer to preventive and basic curative dental care,
       including but not limited to oral examination, prophylaxis, fluoride
       treatment, tooth extraction.

      SEC. 4. Free Medical Service. — All hospitals, medical centers, clinics and other
health institutions that provide frontline medical services throughout the



                                           1
Philippines are hereby mandated under this Act to provide free medical treatment to
indigent children.

       SEC. 5. Free Dental Service. — All dental clinics, medical centers and other
health institutions that provide frontline dental healthcare throughout the country
are mandated under this Act to provide free dental healthcare to indigent children.

       SEC. 6. DSWD and DOH Function. — The Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD), in coordination with the Department of Health (DOH), shall
be the lead agencies in the implementation of this Act. The DSWD shall determine if
the child is indeed an indigent while the DOH shall coordinate with the
implementation of the free medical and dental services to indigent children.

       SEC. 7. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of
this Act shall be included in the General Appropriations Act of the year following its
enactment into law and thereafter.

      SEC. 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder of this Act or any provisions not affected
thereby shall remain in force and effect.

       SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. — Any law, presidential decrees or issuance,
executive orders, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule, or regulation
contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
modified or amended accordingly.

        SEC.10. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days from the date
of its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
