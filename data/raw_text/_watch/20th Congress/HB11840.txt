EXPLANATORY NOTE

For millions of Filipino microentrepreneurs, a small business is more than
a source of income—it is the foundation of a family’s livelihood. A sari-sari store,
a small eatery, a home-based enterprise, a tricycle, a fishing boat, a set of
livelihood tools, or a farmer’s crop inventory may represent years of savings,
sacrifice, and hard work. These productive assets allow families to earn, provide
for their needs, employ others, serve their communities, and build a more
secure future.

Yet the very livelihood that sustains a family can be placed at risk by a
single unexpected event.

A typhoon can destroy crops or business inventory. A flood can damage
equipment and facilities. A fire can wipe out a small store in a matter of hours. A
disease outbreak, accident, livestock loss, or other emergency can deprive a
family of its ability to earn. Sudden disruptions in supply, markets, or other
economic conditions can likewise interrupt business operations and erode
already limited working capital.

For a large enterprise, such losses may be absorbed through financial
reserves, diversified assets, or conventional insurance. For a microenterprise,
however, the same loss can mean the loss of the family’s principal source of
income. Without adequate protection, recovery may require borrowing, selling
productive assets, reducing household necessities, or abandoning the enterprise
altogether. What took years to build can be lost in a single event—and rebuilding
can push a family deeper into debt.

This is why supporting our microentrepreneurs must go beyond helping
them start a business. We must also help them protect what they have built,
keep their enterprises operating, and recover when unexpected shocks occur.
Enterprise development must therefore include not only access to capital,
markets, skills, and technology, but also practical and affordable mechanisms for
managing the risks that can threaten a livelihood.


===== [page 2] =====
The bill seeks to address this gap by promoting greater access to
appropriate non-life microinsurance and other risk-protection mechanisms for
microentrepreneurs. It recognizes that risk protection should be understandable,
affordable, accessible, and responsive to the actual circumstances of micro and
small enterprises and the communities they serve.

To achieve this, the bill strengthens the role of the Department of Trade
and Industry (DTI), particularly through its Negosyo Centers and other
appropriate enterprise-support mechanisms, in providing information on
available risk-protection options and facilitating referrals to appropriate
organizations and providers. The measure enables Negosyo Centers to serve as
accessible points of information and referral so that microentrepreneurs are
better equipped to make informed decisions about protecting their livelihoods.

The bill likewise recognizes Microinsurance Mutual Benefit Associations
(Mi-MBAs) as important partners in promoting business continuity, enterprise
resilience, and livelihood protection. By strengthening their sustainability,
governance, operational capacity, and ability to provide appropriate
microinsurance and other risk-protection products and services, the measure
seeks to ensure that these community-based institutions are better positioned to
serve the people who depend upon them.

The measure also promotes stronger coordination among government
agencies, microfinance and enterprise-support institutions, local stakeholders,
and risk-protection providers. Through greater awareness, appropriate product
development, digital and accessible delivery mechanisms, and_ stronger
consumer and member protection, the bill seeks to make risk protection a more
practical component of enterprise development rather than a resource available
only to those who can afford conventional insurance.

Ultimately, a resilient microenterprise is a more secure livelihood, and a
protected livelihood gives a family a better chance to recover without losing
everything it has worked to build. By helping microentrepreneurs understand
and access appropriate risk protection, and by strengthening the institutions that
provide and facilitate such protection, this measure seeks to reduce the
economic consequences of unexpected shocks, support faster recovery, and help
ensure that temporary setbacks do not become permanent losses of livelihood.

In protecting the productive assets of our microentrepreneurs, we are not
merely protecting businesses. We are protecting incomes, families, communities,
and the opportunity for ordinary Filipinos to build a better future through their
own work and enterprise.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Microenterprise and
Livelihood Risk Protection Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote social justice, financial inclusion, community-based risk protection, and
affordable access to microinsurance, mutual benefit, and other inclusive risk-
protection services, particularly for low-income, vulnerable, underserved, and
informal-sector members.

The State recognizes that access to appropriate risk protection is an
important component of enterprise development, business continuity, enterprise
resilience, and livelihood recovery.

Towards this end, the State shall promote and support access to appropriate
mechanisms and services relating to business continuity, livelihood protection,
and microinsurance and other lawful risk-protection products.

The State further recognizes duly licensed Microinsurance Mutual Benefit
Associations (Mi-MBA) as member-based and community-based mechanisms for
delivering affordable risk protection to microentrepreneurs, individuals engaged
in livelihood activities, and other underserved economic actors.

The State shall therefore promote the sustainability, financial soundness,
good governance, transparency, responsible growth, operational capacity, and
digital transformation of Mi-MBAs.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:
(a) Benefit Fund refers to any fund established, maintained, or administered

by an Mi-MBA for the payment of benefits, claims, equity value, refunds, or

(c) Business Continuity Protection refers to approved non-life microinsurance
or other risk-protection coverage intended to mitigate losses affecting business
inventory, livelihood assets, productive assets, tools, equipment, property,

resumption, or continued operation of a covered microenterprise, livelihood

(f) Guaranty Fund refers to the fund required under the Insurance Code and

mutual benefit, microinsurance, Micro-Product, or similar member-protection

(j) Member Equity Value or MEV refers to the refundable equity value or

(k) Member Protection Mechanism refers to the system of governance,

Sec. 4. Promotion of Microenterprise Business Continuity and Risk Protection.
-The DTI, in coordination with the Insurance Commission, shall promote
awareness among microenterprises and microentrepreneurs of business
continuity, enterprise resilience, and appropriate risk-protection mechanisms.
For this purpose, the DTI may utilize its Negosyo Centers and other appropriate
enterprise-support programs and offices to facilitate access to information and
appropriate referrals relating to business continuity, livelihood protection, and
available microinsurance and other lawful risk-protection products.

Sec. 5. Negosyo Centers as Microenterprise Risk Protection Referral and
Support Hubs. - Negosyo Centers may serve as referral and support hubs to help
microenterprises and microentrepreneurs access information and connect with
appropriate organizations and services relating to business continuity, livelihood
protection, and risk management.

For this purpose, Negosyo Centers may:

(a) Provide or facilitate access to basic information on business continuity,
risk awareness, and available microinsurance and other lawful risk-protection
mechanisms;

(b) Facilitate referrals, upon the request of microenterprises and
microentrepreneurs, to duly licensed or authorized Mi-MBAs, insurance
providers, microfinance institutions, cooperatives, and other appropriate
organizations that may provide or facilitate access to relevant services; and

(c) Coordinate with the Insurance Commission and relevant public and
private organizations in disseminating accessible information on available risk-
protection products and services.

Nothing in this section shall authorize the DTI, any Negosyo Center, or any of
its officers or personnel to underwrite, issue, sell, solicit, market, negotiate,
adjust, approve, or adjudicate any insurance or microinsurance policy or claim,
or to act as an insurer, insurance intermediary, Mi-MBA, or insurance provider,
unless duly licensed or otherwise authorized under applicable law.

===== [page 7] =====
Any referral or support provided under this section shall not constitute an
endorsement, recommendation, guarantee, or certification by the DTI or any
Negosyo Center of any particular provider, product, or _ risk-protection
arrangement.

SEC. 6. Recognition of Mi-MBAs as Business Continuity, Livelihood Protection,
and Community-Based Risk Protection Partners. - Duly licensed Mi-MBAs are
hereby recognized as key community-based partners of the Government,
particularly in expanding access to affordable insurance and other risk-protection
mechanisms, and in promoting business continuity, enterprise resilience,
livelihood protection, disaster resilience, and financial inclusion among their
members.

Given their member-based structure, community presence, and capacity to
provide or facilitate mutual protection arrangements, Mi-MBAs_ shail be
recognized as preferred partners, where appropriate, for the delivery and
facilitation of insurance and other risk-protection programs for eligible
microenterprises and microentrepreneurs.

The DTI and IC shall facilitate the participation of Mi-MBAs in programs and
partnerships established under this Act, subject to applicable laws, rules,
regulations, and the regulatory authority of the Insurance Commission.  Mi-
MBAs may participate in the promotion, distribution, administration, servicing,
and facilitation of access to approved insurance, microinsurance, and other risk-
protection products, as well as business continuity programs, through
mechanisms established under this Act.

Duly licensed Mi-MBAs shall be eligible for the recognition, benefits,
authorities, and privileges specifically provided under this Act. Regular Mutual
Benefit Associations that are not licensed or authorized by the Insurance
Commission as Mi-MBAs shail not be entitled to such benefits, authorities, or
privileges unless they subsequently qualify and are duly authorized as Mi-MBAs
in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

SEC. 7. Authority of Mi-MBAs to Provide Non-Life Microinsurance. - A duly
licensed Mi-MBA may, upon prior approval of the Insurance Commission, provide
non-life microinsurance products to its members. Consistent with the nature and
limits of microinsurance, priority may be given to products intended to protect
the productive capacity, economic activity, and continuity of microenterprises,
livelihood activities, and other small businesses of members.

Non-life microinsurance products that may be approved under this Act
include, but are not limited to:

(a) Business inventory protection;
(b) Livelihood asset protection;

(c) Business equipment, tools, assets, and small machinery protection;

===== [page 8] =====
(d) Small business property protection; e) Microenterprise interruption or
livelihood disruption protection;

(f) Fire microinsurance, including fire affecting business premises, productive
assets, or inventory;

(g) Calamity, typhoon, flood, earthquake, and disaster microinsurance;

(h) Residential house microinsurance, particularly where the residence is also
used for livelihood, home-based enterprise, or microenterprise activities;

(i) Crop and agricultural microinsurance; j) Livestock microinsurance; k)
Equipment, tool, tricycle, motorcycle, banca, or other smail livelihood vehicle
protection;

(1) Other small property protection;

(m) Personal accident microinsurance where appropriate to the livelihood or
economic activity of the member;

(n) Funeral, emergency, and disaster-related protection products, subject to
applicable regulation; and

(o) Other non-life microinsurance or business continuity protection products
that may be approved by the Insurance Commission.

The Insurance Commission shall encourage the development of simple and
affordable product designs appropriate to the scale, risks, cash flows, and
recovery needs of microenterprises, microentrepreneurs, and_ livelihood
operators, without compromising actuarial soundness, solvency, member
protection, or prudential standards.

The authority granted under this section shall be limited to approved
microinsurance products issued to members of the Mi-MBA and shall not be
construed as a general authority to engage in commercial non-life insurance
business.

SEC. 8. Authority of Mi-MBAs to Facilitate Micro-Risk Protection Products. - A
duly licensed Mi-MBA may, subject to the approval, rules, or supervision of the
Insurance Commission and other concerned regulators, facilitate access by its
members to Micro-Products or micro risk protection products issued by duly
licensed Partner Risk-Protection Providers.

Such facilitated products may include life insurance, non-life insurance, Micro
Takaful, health protection, pre-need, personal accident, calamity, property,
business inventory, productive asset, livelihood, agricultural, business
interruption, funeral, emergency, or other member-protection products that the
Mi-MBA is not authorized or able to develop or issue on its own.

The Mi-MBA may receive reasonable service fees, administrative fees, or
other lawful compensation for facilitating, administering, collecting, servicing, or
assisting in the delivery of such products, provided that:

===== [page 9] =====
(a) The product is issued by a duly licensed or authorized provider;

(b) The Mi-MBA does not misrepresent itself as the issuer or underwriter of
the product, unless legally authorized;

(c) The roles, fees, obligations, and liabilities of the Mi-MBA and the partner
provider are clearly disclosed to members;

(d) The product is suitable to the needs and capacity of the members;

(e) The arrangement does not result in unfair, excessive, hidden, or
unauthorized charges to members;

(f) Net income received by the Mi-MBA is used exclusively for member benefit
purposes; and

(g) The arrangement complies with the rules of the Insurance Commission
and other concerned regulators.

SEC. 9. Conditions for Approval of Non-Life Microinsurance Products. - The
Insurance Commission shall approve non-life microinsurance products of Mi-
MBAs only upon compliance with prudential, actuarial, governance, and
member-protection requirements, including but not limited to:

(a) Actuarial study or certification;

(b) Product design suitable for microinsurance and responsive to the needs
and risks of low-income members, microentrepreneurs, and livelihood operators;

(c) Clear benefit schedule;

(d) Affordable premiums, contributions, dues, or assessments;

(e) Simple and proportionate documentation requirements;

(f) Prompt, accessible, and uncomplicated claims processing;

(g) Adequate reserves;

(h) Separate accounting for life and non-life funds;

(i) Separate actuarial valuation, where required;

(j) Reinsurance, catastrophe protection, stop-loss arrangement, risk-pooling
arrangement, or other risk-transfer mechanism, when required by the Insurance
Commission;

(k) Risk management plan;

(1} Claims management plan;

===== [page 10] =====
(m) Member education plan;
(n) Disaster or catastrophe exposure assessment, where applicable;

(0) Assessment of business continuity or livelihood recovery needs, where
appropriate to the product;

(p) Financial capacity and solvency assessment; and

(q) Such other requirements as may be imposed by the Insurance
Commission.

In prescribing requirements under this section, the Insurance Commission
shall take into account the low-value and simplified nature of microinsurance
products and shall seek to avoid unnecessarily burdensome requirements that
could render appropriate products inaccessible or unaffordable, without
compromising actuarial soundness, financial stability, or member protection.

SEC. 10. Separate Accounting, Financial Reporting, and Protection of Funds. -
Mi-MBAs_ shall maintain separate books, accounts, and records for life
microinsurance, non-life microinsurance, Micro Takaful, facilitated micro-risk
protection products, guaranty funds, benefit funds, reserves, member equity
value, claims expense, investment income, service fees, administrative fees,
grants, donations, and revenue-generating activities.

Mi-MBAs_ shall observe the applicable financial reporting framework,
accounting standards, and standard chart of accounts prescribed or recognized
by the Insurance Commission and other competent regulatory authorities.

For this purpose, Mi-MBAs shall maintain accurate, complete, timely, and
verifiable books of accounts, financial records, product records, claims records,
member equity value records, and other regulatory records necessary to ensure
transparency, accountability, proper fund segregation, and member protection.

No fund intended for the payment of member benefits, claims expense,
reserves, guaranty fund obligations, member equity value, refunds, business
continuity protection benefits, or other member entitlements shall be diverted,
commingled, transferred, or used for any purpose not authorized by law,
regulation, the approved product, membership certificate, or the Insurance
Commission.

SEC. 11. Investments by Mi-MBAs. - A duly licensed Mi-MBA may invest
funds not immediately needed for claims, benefits, reserves, member equity
value, and lawful obligations, subject to the rules, limitations, and approval
requirements of the Insurance Commission.

Allowable investments may include deposits, government securities,
approved debt instruments, quoted and unquoted equity securities, investment
properties, approved collective investment schemes, and other safe, liquid,
prudent, and lawful investments authorized under the Insurance Code, rules of
the Insurance Commission, and other applicable jaws.

===== [page 11] =====
In all cases, the investment policy of an Mi-MBA shail observe the principles
of safety, liquidity, diversification, prudence, transparency, accountability, and
protection of members and shall take into account the need to maintain
sufficient liquidity for timely settlement of claims, including claims arising from
calamities, business interruption, and livelihood losses.

SEC. 12. Revenue-Generating Activities. - A duly licensed Mi-MBA may
engage in revenue-generating activities, subject to such approval, regulation, or
reporting requirements as may be prescribed by the Insurance Commission,
provided that such activities are incidental, complementary, or supportive of its
mutual benefit, microinsurance, business continuity, livelihood protection,
financial inclusion, or member-service purposes.

Prior approval of the Insurance Commission shall be required only for such
revenue-generating activities, transactions, or arrangements that the Insurance
Commission may specifically identify as requiring prior approval, taking into
account their nature, scale, risk, impact on members, use of member funds, and
consistency with the purposes of the Mi-MBA.

Sec. 13. Use of Income from Revenue-Generating Activities. - Net income
from revenue-generating activities shall be used exclusively for the benefit of
members and for purposes consistent with the nature of a Mi-MBA, including:

(a) Payment or enhancement of benefits and claims;

(b) Development, expansion, or improvement of non-life microinsurance
products covering business inventory, livelihood assets, tools, equipment,
productive property, agricultural assets, business interruption, and other
business continuity risks;

(c) Reduction or stabilization of member contributions;

(d) Strengthening of guaranty funds, benefit funds, reserves, and member
equity value funds;

(e) Improvement and acceleration of claims processing and member
services;

(f) Digitalization and technology upgrades;
(g) Efficient collection and payment systems;

(h) Research and development relating to product innovation,
microenterprise and livelihood risks, market development, actuarial soundness,
and financial reporting standards;

(i) Financial literacy, insurance literacy, business continuity education, and
member education;

(j) Disaster mitigation, disaster preparedness, climate-risk education, and
enterprise risk-reduction programs;


===== [page 12] =====
(k) Disaster response, calamity assistance, emergency relief, livelihood
recovery, and enterprise recovery programs;

(1) Expansion of approved microinsurance, business continuity protection, and
member-protection programs;

(m) Non-financial member benefits, including educational scholarships, civil
documentation assistance, community-based welfare support, and_ similar
member-benefit programs approved by the Insurance Commission;

(n) Costs of maintaining, administering, and operating approved revenue-
generating activities; and

(o) Other purposes approved by the Insurance Commission.

No part of the net income or assets of a Mi-MBA shall inure to the benefit of
any trustee, officer, employee, incorporator, organizer, partner institution,
service provider, or private individual, except as reasonable compensation for
services actually rendered, reimbursement of duly documented expenses, lawful
payments under approved contracts, or lawful benefits due to members.

Cost-sharing arrangements between a Mi-MBA and a partner microfinance
institution, cooperative, rural bank, association, or other partner institution for
joint activities involving the same client-members shall be allowed, provided that
such arrangements are fair, reasonable, documented in writing, properly
accounted for, approved or reported to the Insurance Commission when

required, and not used to divert funds, conceal private benefit, or unduly burden
members.

SEC. 14. Digitalization of Mi-MBA Operations. - Mi-MBAs are encouraged to
adopt secured, safe, accessible, and member-friendly digital systems for:

(a) Member onboarding and registration;
(b) Identity verification and lawful Know-Your-Customer procedures;
(c) Collection of contributions, premiums, dues, and assessments;

(d) Issuance of membership certificates, microinsurance certificates, Micro-
Product certificates, notices, and other documents;

(e) Efficient collection and claims settlement;
(f) Claims filing, claims tracking, and claims payment;

(g) Expedited processing of claims involving business inventory, productive
assets, livelihood assets, and microenterprise interruption;

(h) Member engagement, education, and communication;

(i) Regulatory and supervisory compliance reports;

===== [page 13] =====
(j) Financial and operational reports to members, the board, management,
partners, regulators, and stakeholders;

(k) Capturing, storing, and updating KYC information of members and their
dependents;

(1) Capturing, storing, and reporting member payments, refundable member
equity value, benefits, and claims;

(m) Capturing, storing, and reporting Mi-MBA products and product
performance;

(n) Recordkeeping, audit, and risk management; and
(o) Other lawful purposes approved by the Insurance Commission.

The use of digital systems shail comply with applicable laws on data privacy,
cybersecurity, consumer protection, electronic transactions, financial regulation,
and anti-money laundering, where applicable.

SEC. 15. Tax Treatrnent of Member Contributions, Premiums, Dues, and
Assessments. - Member contributions, premiums, dues, assessments, and
similar amounts collected by a duly licensed Mi-MBA in connection with approved
life and non-life mutual benefit, microinsurance, Micro-Product, business
continuity protection, or other member-protection products shall be exempt from
regular corporate income tax, provided that such amounts are held, reserved,
invested, or applied exclusively for the payment of benefits, claims, claims
expenses, reserves, member equity value, refunds, member services,
reasonable and necessary operating expenses directly connected with the
carrying out of its approved business, or other lawful purposes directly
connected with the protection and welfare of its members.

For purposes of this section, the tax treatment herein provided shall apply to
both life and non-life products, whether directly provided by the Mi-MBA within
the scope of its authority or otherwise lawfully offered, administered, or
facilitated pursuant to this Act and applicable rules and regulations.

The tax exemptions provided under this section shall apply only to duly
licensed Mi-MBAs with the Insurance Commission and only with respect to
products, benefits, or programs approved by the Insurance Commission or other
appropriate regulator.

SEC. 16. Preferential Premium Tax on Non-Life Microinsurance. - Premiums,
contributions, dues, assessments, and similar payments collected by a duly
licensed Mi-MBA for approved non-life microinsurance products issued to its
members shall be subject to a preferential premium tax rate of two percent
(2%) of gross premiums, contributions, dues, or assessments collected.

The same preferential premium tax rate may apply to approved non-life
microinsurance products facilitated by a duly licensed Mi-MBA for its members
through a duly licensed non-life insurance company or other authorized partner
risk-protection provider, subject to the rules and conditions to be jointly

===== [page 14] =====
prescribed by the Department of Finance, Bureau of Internal Revenue, and
Insurance Commission.

The tax imposed under this section shall be in lieu of value-added tax,
percentage tax, and other national business taxes imposed on such premiums,
contributions, dues, or assessments.

Service fees, administrative fees, commissions, or other facilitation income
received by a Mi-MBA in connection with facilitated micro-risk protection
products shall be separately accounted for and shall be subject to the tax
treatment prescribed by the Department of Finance, Bureau of Internal Revenue,
and Insurance Commission, taking into account the non-stock, non-profit, and
member-benefit character of Mi-MBAs.

The Department of Finance, Bureau of Internal Revenue, and Insurance
Commission shall issue the necessary rules to implement this section, including
the manner of payment, reporting, remittance, audit, verification, classification
of covered products, and safeguards against abuse.

SEC. 17. Local Tax Treatment. - Local government units may extend local
tax, fee, or charge incentives to duly licensed Mi-MBAs, consistent with the Local
Government Code and other applicable laws, in recognition of their role in
supporting community-based risk protection, enterprise resilience, livelihood
protection, and business continuity.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed as automatically exempting Mi-MBAs
from local taxes, fees, charges, permits, or regulatory requirements unless
expressly provided by law or ordinance.

SEC. 18. Documentary Stamp Tax Treatment. - Membership certificates,
microinsurance certificates, Micro-Product certificates, business continuity
protection certificates, renewals, endorsements, riders, notices, proofs of
coverage, and similar documents issued by a duly licensed Mi-MBA in connection
with approved mutual benefit, microinsurance, Micro-Product, business
continuity protection, or member-protection products shall be exempt from
documentary stamp tax, subject to such conditions, thresholds, reporting
requirements, and anti-abuse safeguards as may be jointly prescribed by the
Department of Finance, Bureau of Internal Revenue, and Insurance Commission.

The exemption under this section shall apply only to documents directly
connected with approved mutual benefit, microinsurance, Micro-Product,
business continuity protection, or member-protection products issued to
members of duly licensed Mi-MBAs.

SEC. 19. Tax Treatment of Claims Expense, Benefit Funds, Guaranty Funds,
Reserves, Member Equity Value, and Investment Income. - Claims expense,
benefit funds, guaranty funds, reserve funds, member equity value funds, and
other funds required by law, regulation, actuarial determination, or membership
certificate to be maintained by a Mi-MBA shall not be treated as taxable income
when such funds are held, maintained, accumulated, credited, or applied
exclusively for the benefit of members or to comply with prudential, reserve,
solvency, equity value, or member-protection requirements.


===== [page 15] =====
Investment income derived from the lawful investment of such funds shall
likewise be exempt from income tax, provided that the same is used exclusively
for any of the following purposes:

(a) Payment of benefits, claims, or claims expense;

(b) Strengthening of reserves, guaranty funds, benefit funds, or member
equity value funds;

(c) Crediting of interest or earnings to refundable member equity value,
where required by law, regulation, actuarial determination, or rules of the
Insurance Commission;

(d) Enhancement of member benefits;
(e) Reduction or stabilization of member contributions;

(f) Development or expansion of affordable non-life microinsurance products
supporting business continuity, enterprise resilience, or livelihood protection and
recovery;

(g) Improvement of claims processing, digital systems, and member
services;

(h) Member education, financial literacy, insurance literacy, business
continuity education, and disaster preparedness programs;

(i) Disaster-response, emergency assistance, calamity-related member
support, livelihood recovery, or enterprise recovery programs;

(j) Research and development related to product innovation, market
development, actuarial studies, financial reporting standards, business and
livelihood risk protection, and member protection; or

(k) Such other member-benefit purposes as may be approved by the
Insurance Commission.

SEC. 20. Partnerships for Enterprise, Livelihood, and Community-Based Risk
Protection. - Mi-MBAs may enter into partnerships with the DTI, local
government units, cooperatives, microfinance institutions, rural banks, business
organizations, people's organizations, workers' associations, farmers' and
fisherfolk organizations, transport groups, homeowners' associations, and other
qualified community-based or enterprise-support organizations for the promotion
and facilitation of business continuity, livelihood protection, disaster resilience,
financial inclusion, and access to approved insurance, microinsurance, and other
risk-protection programs.

Such partnerships may include information and education, referrals, risk
assessment, business continuity planning, insurance literacy, claims assistance,
disaster-response coordination, and participation in group or pooled risk-


===== [page 16] =====
protection arrangements, subject to the rules and regulations of the Insurance
Commission and other applicable laws.

Nothing in this section shall authorize any partner institution to engage in an
insurance, microinsurance, Micro-Product, takaful, or other regulated activity
without the license, authority, accreditation, or approval required under
applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

SEC. 21. Member Protection Standards and Bill of Rights of Policyholders. -
Mi-MBAs shall adopt and implement member protection standards consistent
with the Bill of Rights of Policyholders and other applicable issuances of the
Insurance Commission. These standards shall include:

(a) The right to clear, accurate, and understandable information on benefits,
exclusions, limitations, contributions, dues, assessments, fees, charges, and
claims requirements;

(b) The right to receive a membership certificate, microinsurance certificate,
Micro-Product certificate, proof of coverage, or similar document evidencing
coverage or entitlement;

(c) The right to understand, where applicable, the property, asset, inventory,
income-disruption, or other business or livelihood risks covered by the product;

(d) The right to know the actual issuer, underwriter, facilitator, administrator,
or servicing entity for facilitated micro-risk protection products; e) The right to
timely processing and payment of valid claims;

(f) The right to receive a written explanation for denial of claims;

(g) The right to internal reconsideration, appeal, or grievance mechanisms;
(h) The right to fair, respectful, and non-discriminatory treatment;

(i) The right to data privacy and confidentiality;

(j) The right to annual or periodic reporting on member benefits, funds, and
relevant association matters;

(k) The right to member education, financial literacy, insurance literacy, and
appropriate information on risk protection; and

(1) Such other rights and protection standards as may be prescribed by the
Insurance Commission.

SEC. 22. Claims Processing. - Mi-MBAs shall establish simple, accessible, and
timely claims processing procedures appropriate to the nature of mutual benefit,
microinsurance, Micro-Product, and business continuity protection arrangements.
Such procedures may include the filing, submission, verification, tracking,
approval, denial, and payment of claims through electronic, digital, mobile,
online, or other technology-enabled means, subject to applicable laws on


===== [page 17] =====
electronic transactions, data privacy, cybersecurity, consumer protection, and
regulations of the Insurance Commission.

All claims shall be processed, approved, denied, or settled within five (5)
working days from notice of claim. For approved claims, payment shall be made
within the same period, unless a shorter period is prescribed by the Insurance
Commission.

In prescribing claims processing rules, the Insurance Commission shall take
into account the simplified, low-value, urgent, and technology-enabled nature of
microinsurance and Micro-Products.

The Insurance Commission shall also give due consideration to the economic
consequences of delayed claims payment, particularly for claims involving
business inventory, livelihood assets, productive equipment, agricultural assets,
and microenterprise or livelihood interruption, where delayed recovery may
impair the continued operation or resumption of the insured enterprise or
livelihood activity. The Insurance Commission may prescribe simplified or
expedited claims procedures for calamities, disasters, and other events affecting
substantial numbers of microentrepreneurs or livelihood operators, subject to
appropriate safeguards against fraud and abuse. Any denial of claim shall be in
writing, which may be in electronic form where allowed by law and regulation,
and shall clearly state the factual and legal basis for such denial.

SEC. 23. Governance Standards. - Mi-MBAs shall comply with governance
standards prescribed by the Insurance Commission, including:

(a) Fit and proper standards for trustees, directors, officers, and key
management personnel;

(b) Duties of loyalty, integrity, stewardship diligence, prudence, good faith,
transparency, and accountability;

(c) Prohibition against self-dealing and conflict-of-interest transactions,
except under conditions approved by the Insurance Commission;

(d) Regular meetings and reporting to members;
(e) Independent audit;

(f) Actuarial review, when required;

(g) Risk management and internal control systems;

(h) Protection of benefit funds, guaranty funds, reserves, and member equity
value funds;

(i) Transparent reporting of investments, revenue-generating activities,
service fees, grants, donations, and cost-sharing arrangements; and

(j) Other governance requirements necessary to protect members.

SEC. 25. Penalties and Sanctions. - Any person, association, corporation,
trustee, officer, employee, agent, representative, service provider, or partner
institution that violates this Act shall be subject to administrative, civil, and
criminal penalties under the Insurance Code, National Internal Revenue Code,
Revised Penal Code, and other applicable laws.

Without prejudice to the imposition of higher penaities under existing laws,
the Insurance Commission may impose any or all of the following administrative
sanctions:

(a) Warning or reprimand;

(b) Administrative fine;

(c) Restitution or refund to affected members;


===== [page 19] =====
(d) Suspension of product approval;

(e) Suspension or revocation of license or authority;

(f) Disqualification of trustees, directors, officers, or responsible persons;
(g) Cease and desist order;

(h) Disallowance of tax privileges or preferential treatment, in coordination
with the Department of Finance and Bureau of Internal Revenue;

(i) Appointment of conservator, receiver, or other authorized regulatory
intervention, when warranted by law; and

(j) Such other sanctions as may be authorized by law.

In determining the appropriate sanction, good faith, degree of participation,
nature of the violation, damage to members, repetition, conceaiment,
restitution, cooperation, and other mitigating or aggravating circumstances may
be considered.

SEC. 26. Coordination Among the Insurance Commission, Department of
Finance, and Bureau of Internal Revenue. - The Insurance Commission,
Department of Finance, and Bureau of Internal Revenue shall jointly issue rules
and regulations to implement the tax provisions of this Act, including rules on:

(a) Tax treatment of member contributions, premiums, dues, and
assessments;

(b) Preferential two percent (2%) premium tax on non-life microinsurance;

(c) Preferential tax treatment of collection fees, service fees, administrative
fees, or similar compensation paid to duly authorized distribution channels,
partner institutions, or delivery partners for the distribution, collection,

administration, servicing, or delivery of approved microinsurance, non-life
microinsurance, Micro Takaful, or Micro-Product arrangements of Mi-MBAs;

(d) Documentary stamp tax exemption or treatment;

(e) Tax treatment of benefit funds, guaranty funds, reserves, member equity
value, claims expense, and investment income;

(f) Tax treatment of revenue-generating activities, facilitated micro-risk
protection products, grants, donations, service fees, administrative fees, and
cost-sharing arrangements;

(g) Reporting, withholding, audit, verification, and certification requirements;

(h) Prevention of abuse, tax avoidance, unauthorized insurance activity, or
private inurement; and

(i) Such other matters necessary to implement this Act.

SEC. 28. Safeguards Against Abuse. - The tax treatment, incentives,
authorities, and privileges granted under this Act shall not apply to any
transaction, product, fund, investment, activity, partnership, or arrangement
that is designed or used primarily for tax avoidance, private benefit, profit
distribution, unauthorized insurance business, circumvention of regulatory
requirements, or violation of insurance, tax, banking, securities, cooperative,
data privacy, anti-money laundering, consumer protection, local government, or
other applicable laws.

The Insurance Commission, Department of Finance, and Bureau of Internal
Revenue may disallow the tax treatment, incentives, authorities, or privileges
granted under this Act upon finding that the Mi-MBA, partner institution, service
provider, trustee, officer, or responsible person has violated this Act or its
implementing rules and regulations.

SEC. 29. Gender-Responsive and Inclusive Risk Protection. — In
implementing this Act, the Insurance Commission, Department of Trade and
Industry, and concerned agencies shall consider the distinct risk exposure,
economic circumstances, and access barriers of women, persons with

===== [page 21] =====
disabilities, older persons, indigenous peoples, farmers, fisherfolk, informal
workers, and other vulnerable or underserved microentrepreneurs and livelihood
operators, consistent with applicable laws and without discrimination.

Programs and products developed pursuant to this section shall remain
subject to actuarial soundness, affordability, prudential requirements, and
member protection standards.

SEC. 30. Harmonization with Existing Laws. - This Act shall be interpreted in
harmony with the Insurance Code, National Internal Revenue Code, Local
Government Code, Data Privacy Act, Electronic Commerce Act, cooperative laws
and regulations, banking and financial regulations, anti-money laundering laws,
consumer protection laws, and other applicable statutes and regulations.
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish the regulatory, supervisory,
visitorial, examination, enforcement, or rule-making powers of the Insurance
Commission over Mi-MBAs, insurance products, microinsurance products, Micro-
Products, and related activities.

SEC. 31. Transition Period. - Existing Mi-MBAs shall be given a reasonable
transition period, not exceeding three (3) years from the effectivity of the
implementing rules and regulations, to comply with new reportorial, accounting,
product approval, digitalization, governance, and tax-related requirements under
this Act, unless a shorter period is required by the Insurance Commission for
member protection or public interest.

SEC. 32. Congressional and Regulatory Review. - Within three (3) years from
the effectivity of this Act, and every three (3) years thereafter, the Insurance
Commission, in coordination with the Department of Finance and Bureau of
Internal Revenue, shall submit to Congress a report on the implementation of
this Act. The report shall include, among others:

(a) Number of licensed Mi-MBAs;

(b) Number of members covered;

(c) Products approved;

(d) Claims paid;

(e) Non-life microinsurance products issued or facilitated;

(f) Micro-Products and Micro Takaful issued or facilitated, where applicable;

(g) Tax incentives or preferential treatment availed of;

(h) Investment and revenue-generating activities undertaken;

(i) Member protection issues and complaints;

(j) Regulatory actions taken; and

(k) Recommendations for legislative or regulatory improvement.

SEC. 33. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Rules and regulations
necessary for the effective enforcement of the provisions of this Act shall be
formulated by the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and the Insurance
Commission, in coordination with the Department of Finance, Bureau of Internal
Revenue, Bureau of Local Government Finance, and other relevant government
agencies and non-government stakeholders, shall be implemented within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act.

SEC. 34. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the _ initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the existing appropriations of
the Insurance Commission and other concerned agencies. Thereafter, such sums
as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 35. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not so declared shall remain in force and
effect.

SEC. 36. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders and rules and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or amended accordingly.

SEC. 37. Effectivity. - Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the implementing
rules and regulations, this Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
