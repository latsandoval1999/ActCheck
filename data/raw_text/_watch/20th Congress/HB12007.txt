                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Philippines has an opportunity to develop advanced industrial capabilities in
semiconductors, critical minerals, artificial intelligence infrastructure, electronics, and
associated supply chains. The Pax Silica initiative provides an infrastructure for such
advancement. Industrial modernization, however, must yield employment, stronger
Filipino skills, and demonstrable environmental gains rather than technology investment
alone.

        Republic Act No. 10771, or the Philippine Green Jobs Act of 2016, recognizes
employment that contributes to environmental preservation or restoration while meeting
decent-work standards. Participation in Pax Silica does not, by itself, confer green job
status. A statutory program is therefore warranted to translate eligible investments into
verified occupations, apprenticeships, technical training, safer workplaces, and accessible
employment for Filipino workers.

        This measure establishes a focused program under the leadership of DOLE for
workforce planning, employment facilitation, decent-work compliance, and labor-market
monitoring. Under the proposed legislation herein, the Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority (TESDA) retains responsibility for technical-vocational standards
and training and the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) for
environmental regulation. The law will also require the Department of Trade and Industry
(DTI) to have the obligation over industry and investment coordination relating to Pax
Silica. The Climate Change Commission and the other agencies tasked under this
proposed law will continue to exercise their functions based on existing laws, including
those related to green jobs certification and incentives.
      It should be highlighted that the proposed law herein does not grant blanket
exemptions, automatic tax privileges, or exclusive rights to any foreign or domestic
enterprise. When the bill herein becomes law, it will link public support to measurable
outcomes and accountable implementation.

       This representation is championing the passage of making the DOLE the lead
agency in the participation of the country in the Pax Silica initiative because it is the labor
sector which will greatly benefit from such activity. Such matter was taken-up during the
ASEAN Green Jobs Forum held in Manila last October 19-20, 2026 when this
representation was able to talk to officials of DOLE.

       For the Filipino people, approval of the proposed law is earnestly being sought.

  AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE PAX SILICA GREEN JOBS AND WORKFORCE
   DEVELOPMENT PROGRAM, DESIGNATING THE DEPARTMENT OF LABOR
     AND EMPLOYMENT AS LEAD IMPLEMENTING AGENCY, PROVIDING
  FOR INTER-AGENCY COORDINATION, WORKER PROTECTION, FUNDING,
                    AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippine Congress
Assembled:

Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Pax Silica Green Jobs and
Workforce Development Act.”

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is policy of the State to promote full, productive,
freely chosen, safe, and decent employment; strengthen Filipino participation in advanced
industries; develop skills for a competitive and resilient economy; and support
environmentally sustainable industrialization. The State shall ensure that industrial
cooperation and supply-chain initiatives advance labor rights, occupational safety and
health, social protection, and measurable environmental improvements.

Section 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to Philippine-based projects, enterprises,
workers and training activities identified under the Program established in Section 5 of
this Act. This Act shall likewise apply to projects associated with Pax Silica and comparable
advanced-technology supply-chain initiatives as may be determined under the
implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

Section 4. Definition of Terms. – The terms below shall have the following meaning
under this Act:
   a. Green jobs – shall refer to jobs as defined by Republic Act No. 10771.
   b. Pax Silica – shall refer to the global multilateral tech supply chain initiative that the
      Philippines signed. The participation of the Philippines is currently centered on the
      building of facilities and infrastructure for artificial intelligence and semiconductor
      manufacturing.
   c. DOLE – shall refer to the Department of Labor and Employment.
   d. Decent work – shall refer to productive work with fair renumeration, respect of
      labor rights, safe conditions, social protection, and social dialogue.
   e. Eligible Project – shall refer to a project admitted to the Program, as established
      in Section 5 of this Act, based on transparent criteria, provided that, admission
      shall not constitute green job certification or regulatory approval.

Section 5. Establishment of the Program. There is hereby established the Pax Silica
Green Jobs and Workforce Development Program under this Act which shall have the
following purposes:

   a. Identify emerging occupations and skills gaps.
   b. Develop green skills pathways and industry partnerships.
   c. Facilitate employment and lawful apprenticeships.
   d. Promote occupational safety and health and decent work compliance.
   e. Support workers affected by technological change.
   f. Monitor verifiable environmental and employment outcomes.

   Priority fields may include resource efficient semiconductor and electronics production,
renewable energy systems, circular economy services, responsible material recovery,
energy efficient digital infrastructure and other qualified activities.

Section 6. Lead Implementing Agency. – The DOLE shall be the lead implementing
agency of the Program and shall chair the interagency steering committee under this Act.
The DOLE shall formulate the national workforce plan, coordinate labor market
assessments, set program-level decent-work safeguards consistent with existing laws,
facilitate placement and worker transitions, consolidate performance reporting, and issue
necessary labor-related guidelines. This leadership shall not diminish the powers of other
agencies over environmental permitting, investment regulation, education, taxation, or
certification.

Section 7. Interagency Steering Committee. An Interagency Steering Committee is
hereby created under this Act. The Interagency Steering Committee shall be chaired by
the Secretary of DOLE or his duly designated representative. The Interagency Steering
Committee shall be composed of the following:

   a. Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE)
   b. Department of Trade and Industry (DTI)
   c. Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR)
   d. Department of Energy (DOE)
   e. Department of Finance (DOF)
   f. Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT)
   g. Department of Economy, Planning and Development (DepDev)
   h. Climate Change Commission (CCC)
   i. Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA)
   j. Commission on Higher Education (CHED)

Section 8. Skills Development and Certification. TESDA, in coordination with DOLE,
CHED, industry, workers’ organizations, and relevant professional bodies, shall assess and
develop or update competency standards, training regulations, assessment systems, and
qualifications for covered occupations. The Program may support scholarships, upskilling,
recognition of prior learning, training of trainers, and industry-based training, subject to
existing laws and available appropriations. Training shall be accessible to women, youth,
persons with disabilities, displaced workers, and communities near project sites.

Section 9. Green Jobs Verification and Environmental Integrity. No occupation,
enterprise, or project shall be represented as a certified green job or green jobs enterprise
solely by reason of participation in Pax Silica or the Program. Green job determinations
and enterprise certifications for purposes of Republic Act No. 10771 and its incentives
shall be made by the authorities designated under applicable law and rules. DENR and
other competent regulators shall independently administer environmental impact
assessment, permits, pollution control, waste management, and other environmental
obligations. Program monitoring shall track credible indicators of energy, water, materials,
emissions, waste and occupational outcomes.

Section 10. Investment Facilitation and Incentives. DTI and concerned investment-
promotion agencies may coordinate with DOLE to inform investors of workforce
requirements and applicable programs. Any fiscal incentive shall be granted only upon
satisfaction of the requirements and applicable programs. Any fiscal incentive shall be
granted only upon satisfaction of the requirements of existing tax, customs, investment,
and green job laws, and the approval of competent authorities. Nothing in this Act shall
be construed as an automatic tax exemption, preferential award, or exemption from
procurement, competition, nationality, or environmental laws.

Section 11. Local Employment and Just Transition. DOLE, in coordination with local
government units, public employment service offices, TESDA, and participating
employers, shall encourage transparent recruitment, local workforce development, and
lawful employment opportunities for qualified Filipinos. Where technological change or
industrial restructuring threatens jobs, the Program shall provide employment facilitation,
reskilling referrals, and transition support subject to law and available resources.

Section 12. Program Registry, Transparency and Annual Report. DOLE shall maintain a
program registry of participating projects and aggregate workforce outcomes, subject to
the Data Privacy Act and legitimate confidentiality protections. Within ninety (90) days
after each calendar year, DOLE shall submit to Congress an annual report on jobs
generated, worker training and placement, safety and labor compliance, inclusion,
environmental-performance indicators supplied by competent agencies, public
expenditures, and recommendations. Reported green jobs shall be distinguished from
other technology-related jobs.

Section 13. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation shall
be charged against available appropriations of the concerned agencies, subject to
budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. Government partnerships,
grants and industry contributions may be accepted only in accordance with applicable
laws and without compromising regulatory independence.

Section 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within one hundred eighty (100)
days from the effectivity of this Act, DOLE, in coordination and consultation with the other
agencies in this Act, workers organizations and employers’ associations, and other
stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations implementing the provisions of
this Act. The rules shall also prescribe the admission criteria, measurable indicators,
safeguards, interagency coordination, and grievance referral procedures, without
modifying existing legal mandates.

Section 15. Separability Clause. If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall
remain in force and effect.

Sections 16. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly, provided that,
existing worker protections and environmental safeguards shall not be deemed
diminished by implication.

Section 17. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
either in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation in the Philippines.
