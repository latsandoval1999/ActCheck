25                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
26
27           Paternity leave is the period that a father takes off work before, during, and
28   after the delivery in order to support his wife or partner during the earliest stages of
29   childrearing and the mother’s recovery. Research suggests that fathers who take
30   paternity leave, especially those taking two weeks or more immediately after
31   childbirth, are more likely to be involved with their young children.1 Giving new
32   fathers time off work helps to evenly distribute childcare duties and allows women to
33   take care of their health and maintain their careers. Beyond having positive effects for
34   gender equality in the home and at work, paternity leave also greatly improves
35   maternal and infant health, and its multiple advantages echo into the child’s
36   adulthood.2
37
38          No international standard concerning paternity leave exists. However, the
39   International Labor Organization in 2009 called for governments to develop adequate
40   policies for a better balance of work and family responsibilities, to include paternity
41   and/or parental leave, with incentives for men to use them.3

 2           Republic Act 8187 of the Paternity Leave Act was enacted in 1996, granting
 3   seven days leave with full pay to all married male employees in the private and public
 4   sectors. Though beneficial, this law limits the vital bond between the expecting
 5   parents and their infants, and the period is not long enough for the husband to perform
 6   all his obligations.
 7
 8          This Bill seeks to grant to male employees a longer paternity leave to give them
 9   a longer time to assist their wives and partners and to care for their newborns. Thirty
10   days is about one-third of the total maternity leave of 105 days.
11
12          This Bill also removes the limit of four deliveries in availing the benefit and
13   extends the benefit to all male employees, regardless of employment status.
14   Furthermore, in recognition of current social realities in which about four million of
15   the population are in “live-in” or common-law relationships,4 the bill proposes to
16   make the benefit available to a male employee who is not married to the woman who
17   gave birth or suffered an abortion or miscarriage, provided that they have no legal
18   impediment to marry each other during their cohabitation and have been living
19   exclusively as husband and wife for at least one year at the time of childbirth, abortion,
20   or miscarriage.
21
22             For these reasons, passage of this Bill is earnestly sought.

28   SECTION 1. The title of Republic Act 8187 otherwise known as The Paternity
29   Leave Act of 1996 is hereby amended to read as follows:
30
31                                   AN ACT
32            GRANTING PATERNITY LEAVE OF [SEVEN (7) DAYS] THIRTY (30)
33              DAYS WITH FULL PAY TO ALL [MARRIED] QUALIFIED MALE
34             EMPLOYEES IN THE PRIVATE AND PUBLIC SECTORS FOR [THE
35             FIRST FOUR (4)] ALL DELIVERIES OF THE LEGITIMATE SPOUSE
36            WITH WHOM HE IS COHABITING OR THE WOMAN WITH WHOM
37            HE IS EXCLUSIVELY COHABITING WITHOUT THE BENEFIT OF
38                      MARRIAGE AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
39
40   SEC. 2. Section 2 of the same law is hereby further amended to read as follows:
41
42           “Section 2. PATERNITY LEAVE. – Notwithstanding any law, rules and
43           regulations to the contrary, every [married] male employee, REGARDLESS
44           OF EMPLOYMENT STATUS, MARRIED OR IN A COMMON-LAW
45           RELATIONSHIP, in the private and public sectors shall be entitled to a
46           paternity leave of [seven (7)] THIRTY (30) WORKING days with full pay

 9   SEC. 3. Section 3 is hereby amended to read as follows:
10
11          “Section 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act,
12
13               a. Delivery shall include childbirth, abortion, or any miscarriage.
14
15               b. Male employee is in a common-law relationship if he and his spouse
16                  have been living together as husband and wife without the benefit of
17                  marriage for at least two years at the time of childbirth, abortion, or
18                  miscarriage: Provided, however, that the male employee and his partner
19                  have been exclusively cohabiting for at least one (1) year, having no
20                  legal impediment to marry each other during their common-law
21                  relationship.”
22
23               c. Paternity leave refers to the benefits granted to a male employee,
24                  regardless of employment status, married or in a common-law
25                  relationship, allowing him not to report for work for thirty (30) working
26                  days but continues to earn the compensation therefor, on the condition
27                  that his legitimate spouse or the woman with whom he is exclusively
28                  cohabiting without the benefit of marriage has delivered a child,
29                  undergo abortion or suffered a miscarriage for purposes of enabling him
30                  to effectively lend support to his wife in her period of recovery and/or
31                  nursing of the newly-born child.”
32
33   SEC. 4. Separability Clause. If for any reason any section or provision of this Act is
34   declared unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby shall
35   continue to be in full force and effect.
36
37   SEC. 5. Repealing Clause. - Any Laws, decrees, or laws, decrees, or rules and
38   regulations which are inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are
39   hereby amended or repealed.
40
41   SEC. 6. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days from the publication in
42   the Official Gazette or in any newspaper of general circulation.
