                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE


This bill seeks to reduce and standardize the excessive paperwork currently required when
ordinary Filipinos apply for government financial aid. These include cash transfers, calamity
assistance, medical subsidies, scholarships, emergency loans, 4Ps grants, and other social
welfare programs under DSWD, DOH, PCSO, Philhealth and LGUs.

Every day, millions of low-income Filipinos struggle with the same frustrating reality. To get
help, they must gather a long list of documents: PSA birth certificates, barangay clearances,
income tax returns or certificates of indigency, multiple IDs, proof of residency, marriage
contracts, school records, and more. Each document costs money and time. Many applicants
spend days lining up at different offices, pay for transportation they cannot afford, lose wages
from missed work, and still get rejected for missing “one more requirement.”

The poor, senior citizens, persons with disabilities, single parents, and those in far-flung
provinces suffer the most. During emergencies — typhoons, serious illness, or sudden job loss
— these delays mean families go hungry or cannot pay hospital bills while waiting weeks or
months for approval. Many simply give up and never receive the help the law promises them.

This bill cuts the red tape by allowing self-certification for basic information, accepting digital
IDs and one government ID as sufficient proof, creating a standardized checklist across all
agencies, and setting up one-stop processing centers. The goal is simple: make government
assistance truly accessible so that aid reaches those who need it — quickly, fairly, and without
unnecessary obstacles.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
        SIMPLIFYING THE DOCUMENTARY FOR ACCESSING ASSISTANCE
      REQUIREMENTS GOVERNMENT FINANCIAL AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Bilis Tulong Act of 2026”.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State shall promote the general welfare of the Filipino people by ensuring
that individuals and families in difficult situations are provided with timely and accessible financial assistance.
To this end, the government shall adopt measures to remove excessive documentary requirements and
streamline procedures for availing aid from government agencies.

SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all national and local government agencies and
instrumentalities that provide medical, burial, and similar forms of financial assistance, including the
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Department of Health (DOH), Philippine
Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO), and Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth).

SEC. 4. Standardized Requirements. - To avail of immediate financial assistance, beneficiaries shall
be required to submit only the following documents:

    a) Valid government-issued identification (ID);
    b) Billing statement from the hospital, clinic, funeral parlor, or related entities; and
    c) Original or certified true copy of a medical certificate or medical abstract with the date of
       issuance, complete name, signature, and license number of the attending physician, or death
       certificate issued by the hospital or the city/municipal health office, whichever is applicable.

        Hospitals, clinics, and other authorized medical facilities issuing such medical certificates or
        medical abstracts shall, in coordination with the DOH, ensure that the foregoing information is
        clearly indicated therein.

        Where the request for assistance is made through a representative, such representative shall
        present an authorization letter duly signed by the beneficiary, or, where the beneficiary is
        unable to sign due to age, illness, disability, or illiteracy, such other reasonable proof of
        authorization as may be prescribed by the implementing rules and regulations, along with a
        valid government issued ID of both the beneficiary and the representative. The authorized
       representative must be a relative of the beneficiary within the fourth degree of consanguinity
       or affinity, or a legal guardian: Provided, that no individual shall act as a representative for
       more than two (2) beneficiaries.

SEC. 5. Digital Requests for Government Aid. - The Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT) shall be primarily tasked to develop and use the eGov PH Super App or other
appropriate digital systems or platforms as may be prescribed in the implementing rules and
regulations, as a mechanism for Filipinos to avail aid from government agencies. The online or mobile
application shall be utilized to facilitate requests for medical, burial, and similar forms of financial
assistance.

SEC. 6. Streamlined Processing. - All concerned government agencies shall process requests for
assistance within the day, subject to availability of funds: Provided, that complete documentary
requirements are submitted during official working hours. Requests submitted on weekends, holidays,
or outside official working hours shall be processed on the next working day.

SEC. 7. Coordination with the Anti-Red Tape Authority. - The DSWD and all implementing agencies
shall coordinate with the Anti-Red Tape Authority (ARTA) to ensure full compliance with Republic
Act No. 9485, as amended by 8 Republic Act No. 11032 or the "Ease of Doing Business and Efficient
Government Service Delivery Act of 2018". They shall likewise work with ARTA to address
complaints involving undue delays, excessive documentary requirements, or other forms of non-
compliance with the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act,
the DSWD, in coordination with ARTA, DOH, PCSO, PhilHealth, and the Department of the Interior
and Local Government, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations.

SEC. 9. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the
other provisions and parts hereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, proclamations, rules and regulations, or parts
thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 11. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its publication in the
Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
