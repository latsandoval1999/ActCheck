EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Great and Little Santa Cruz Islands, which are located in Zamboanga City, have made
a significant contribution to the city's identity, its tourism industry, and its environmental heritage.
Because of their distinctive natural beauty and ecological value, these islands have helped establish
Zamboanga City as a prominent tourist destination and have at the same time provided vital
habitats for both wildlife and marine life. The mangrove ecosystems on the islands serve to protect
the coastlines, support fisheries, store carbon, enhance the city's resilience to climate change, and
help maintain the ecological balance of the adjacent coastal waters.

It is essential to protect, restore, and innovate these mangroves if the city’s natural resources
are to be preserved for both current and future generations, and at the same time responsible
ecotourism can help to promote environmental education, scientific research, technological
innovation, and sustainable ways of earning a living for local communities without in any way
compromising the integrity of these ecosystems.

The islands are under the jurisdiction of Presidential Proclamation No. 271 of 23 April
2000, which created the Great and Little Sta. Cruz Islands Protected Landscape and Seascape, and
their management is regulated by Republic Act No. 7586, as amended by Republic Act No. 11038,
as well as by other relevant environmental laws and protected-area regulations.

Even though such measures already exist, it is necessary to take stronger action in order to
keep an eye on mangrove conditions, stop illegal felling and habitat deterioration, restore areas
that have been damaged, promote scientific research, and improve coastal management in a way
that is resilient to climate change. Moreover, a clear framework for sustainable ecotourism should
be set up, involving ecological assessments, limits on the number of visitors, suitable zoning, and
the acquisition of the necessary permits and clearances.

The measure in question aims at setting up the Great and Little Santa Cruz Islands
Mangrove Protection, Conservation, Innovation Development, and Sustainable Ecotourism Use
Program in order to encourage the long-term protection, restoration, scientific study, and
responsible use of the mangrove ecosystems on the islands. The Program is to promote biodiversity
conservation, environmental education, technological innovation, climate resilience, and

community participation.

Allowed ecotourism activities can involve guided tours of the mangroves, specific nature
trails, approved boardwalks and places for observation, birdwatching, watching wildlife,
educational trips, citizen science projects, and other nature-based experiences which have a low


===== [page 2] =====
impact. All activities and facilities must adhere to ecological assessments, the zoning rules of
protected areas, visitor quotas, and relevant laws, without leading to the destruction or to
unauthorized changes of the mangroves and other sensitive habitats.

The Program should also encourage qualified local residents, fisherfolk, women, youth,
and community-based organizations to take part in training, guiding, environmental interpretation,
authorized tourism services, and other livelihood activities which are compatible with
conservation, provided that the relevant permits, accreditation, and safety requirements are met.

The measure is in accordance with Republic Act No. 7586, as amended by Republic Act
No. 11038; Republic Act No. 8550, as amended by Republic Act No. 10654; Republic Act No.
9593; Republic Act No. 7160; and other relevant environmental and tourism laws, under the
provisions of Article II, Section 16 of the 1987 Constitution. It acknowledges the responsibilities
of the DENR, PAMB, PASu, the City Government of Zamboanga, the DOT, the DOST, the DA-
BFAR, and other concerned agencies, without establishing a separate protected-area authority or
undermining the current safeguards.

In order to ensure continuity, the Program must include a five-year Program Innovation
Development and Management Plan, arrangements for inter-agency coordination, annual work and
financial plans, measurable environmental and socioeconomic indicators, public reporting, and
government audit. For the first year of the implementation, Fifty Million Pesos (P50,000,000.00)
is suggested, on condition that the relevant budgeting and fund-release procedures are followed,
and further funding will be obtained from annual appropriations and other legitimate sources.

By taking this action, Zamboanga City will be able to continue to safeguard the Great and
Little Santa Cruz Islands as symbols of its natural heritage and at the same time promote science-
based conservation, responsible ecotourism, climate resilience, and inclusive local development
for the advantage of both current and future generations.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Great and Little Santa Cruz
Islands Mangrove Ecosystems Protection, Conservation, Innovation Development, and

Sustainable Ecotourism Use Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect,
conserve, restore, and sustainably manage the mangrove ecosystems, biodiversity, coastal and
marine resources, and natural heritage of the Great and Little Santa Cruz Islands in Zamboanga
City while promoting innovation development, climate resilience, scientific research,

environmental education, responsible ecotourism, and inclusive local economic development.

The State recognizes the ecological, economic, scientific, cultural, and protective functions
of mangroves, including their contributions to coastal protection, carbon sequestration, fisheries
productivity, biodiversity conservation, disaster risk reduction, and the sustainable livelihoods of

coastal communities.

tidal channels, mudflats, or other sensitive habitats.

SEC. 7. Designation and Sustainable Ecotourism Use of Mangrove Sites. — The DENR,
in coordination with the PAMB, PASu, City Government of Zamboanga, and other competent
authorities, shall identify and recommend mangrove sites suitable for ecotourism based on

scientific assessment and the applicable protected-area management plan.
The designation of a mangrove site for ecotourism shall consider, among other factors:

(a) Ecological condition, biodiversity value, habitat sensitivity, and the presence of

threatened or protected species;

(b) Tidal patterns, hydrology, soil stability, erosion risks, and vulnerability to storms,

flooding, and other coastal hazards;

(c) The presence of breeding, feeding, nesting, and nursery habitats requiring restricted

access or seasonal protection;

(d) The site's ecological carrying capacity and the potential cumulative effects of visitor
activities;

(e) Accessibility, visitor safety, emergency response requirements, and the availability of

appropriate waste and sanitation facilities;

(f) The compatibility of the proposed use with the approved protected-area management

plan, applicable zoning, and existing community and cultural rights; and

(g) The potential for environmental education, conservation financing, local employment,

innovation development, and other legitimate community benefits.

Only sites determined to be ecologically suitable and legally available for the proposed
activity may be opened or developed for ecotourism. Designation under this section shall not, by
itself, constitute a development permit, construction clearance, tourism accreditation, or

authorization to occupy public land or use natural resources.

The competent authorities shall establish appropriate visitor routes, access points,

operating schedules, group sizes, supervision requirements, and other site-specific controls. Areas

SEC. 9. Carrying Capacity, Visitor Management, and Environmental Safeguards. —
Before the commencement, expansion, or material alteration of an ecotourism activity, the
implementing agencies, in coordination with the PAMB and other competent authorities, shall

undertake the necessary ecological and visitor-management assessments.

The assessments shall establish appropriate visitor carrying capacities, group sizes, routes,
operating hours, seasonal restrictions, access controls, monitoring procedures, and other
safeguards. Carrying capacities shall be based on the best available scientific evidence and shall

be periodically reviewed as ecological conditions and visitor impacts change.

Visitor management measures may include advance booking, visitor registration,
mandatory orientation, guided access, designated entry and exit points, temporary closures,

restrictions on noise and lighting, limits on equipment, and other reasonable controls necessary to

protect the site.

SEC. 13. Program Financing and Financial Administration. — Subject to existing
budgeting, accounting, treasury, procurement, and auditing laws and regulations, funds

appropriated for the Program shall be used exclusively for authorized activities under this Act.

The DENR and the City Government of Zamboanga may receive and administer funds

Any special account, trust fund, or other earmarked financial mechanism shall be

(a) Annual appropriations under the General Appropriations Act;

(b) Lawful appropriations and counterpart contributions of the City Government of

Zamboanga;

(d) Lawfully collected user fees, ecotourism charges, and other authorized revenues,

rules; and
(e) Other lawful sources of funding.

allocated, and utilized in accordance with applicable law. No fee, charge, revenue retention, or
expenditure shall be authorized under this Act where separate statutory authority or approval is

(d) Visitor numbers, compliance with carrying-capacity limits, and reported environmental

(e) Community participation, employment, training, livelihood, and enterprise outcomes;

(f) Research, innovation development, environmental education, and public awareness

outputs;

(g) Funds received, obligated, disbursed, and remaining, including applicable ecotourism-

(h) Environmental compliance inspections, enforcement actions, and restoration measures;

(i) Recommended improvements to the Program, including changes to visitor limits, site

The reports shall be made publicly accessible, subject to applicable laws on data privacy,

confidentiality, national security, and the protection of sensitive ecological information.

All Program expenditures shall be subject to government accounting and auditing laws,

rules, and regulations.

SEC. 16. Environmental Compliance and Enforcement. — Ail activities under the
Program shall comply with applicable environmental laws, including Republic Act No. 7586, as
amended by Republic Act No. 11038; Republic Act No. 8550, as amended by Republic Act No.
10654; Republic Act No. 9147, otherwise known as the Wildlife Resources Conservation and
Protection Act; Republic Act No. 9275, otherwise known as the Philippine Clean Water Act of
2004; Republic Act No. 9003, otherwise known as the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act
of 2000; Presidential Decree No. 1586, establishing the Philippine Environmental Impact

Statement System; and other relevant laws, rules, and regulations.

Violations shall be investigated and prosecuted by the competent authorities in accordance
with existing law. Nothing in this Act shall create a new criminal offense or penalty unless

expressly provided in a subsequent statutory provision.

The DENR, PAMB, PASu, City Government of Zamboanga, and other authorized
enforcement agencies shall coordinate within their respective legal mandates to ensure compliance

and facilitate lawful restoration or remediation of environmental damage.

Tourism operators, accredited guides, community organizations, facility operators,
researchers, and other participants in the Program shall comply with the conditions of their permits,
authorizations, and agreements. Appropriate administrative measures, including suspension or
revocation of program-specific accreditation or authorization where legally permissible, may be

undertaken in accordance with due process and applicable law.

SEC. 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DENR, in coordination with the City Government of Zamboanga,
PAMB, PASu, DOT, DOST, and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the rules and

regulations necessary for the implementation of this Act.

The implementing rules and regulations shall provide standards for mangrove site
designation, ecotourism operations, visitor carrying-capacity assessments, environmental
monitoring, facility approval, guide training and accreditation, innovation development,
community participation, financial administration, reporting, and other matters necessary to

implement the Program.

The implementing rules and regulations shall respect the powers and responsibilities of

existing protected-area authorities and shail not authorize any activity prohibited by law.

SEC. 18. Separability Clause. — \f any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional

or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 19. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and regulations,

or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or

modified accordingly.
