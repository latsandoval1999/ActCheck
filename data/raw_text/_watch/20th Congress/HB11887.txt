                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Internet has evolved from a luxury to an absolute necessity. In the modern
era, it is the backbone of commerce, education, public service, and daily
communication. Despite the exponential growth of the digital economy, the
quality of internet service in the country has consistently lagged behind global
standards, leaving subscribers dealing with intermittent connections, unmet
advertised speeds, and unresponsive customer service.

Currently, internet service providers (ISPs) often market their services based on
"up to" maximum speeds, with no legally binding obligation to maintain a strict
minimum threshold of quality or reliability. This leaves consumers paying
premium rates for subpar services, with little to no recourse for prolonged outages
or throttled connections.

This bill seeks to address these systemic issues by establishing mandatory
minimum internet connection speeds and strict Quality of Service (QoS)
standards for all ISPs and Public Telecommunications Entities (PTEs).
Furthermore, it codifies a Bill of Rights for Internet Service Subscribers, ensuring
fair treatment, transparent billing, and mandatory rebates for service
interruptions.

By holding service providers accountable and empowering consumers, this
measure aims to accelerate the country’s digital transformation and ensure that
every citizen has access to fast, reliable, and affordable internet.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT ESTABLISHING MINIMUM INTERNET CONNECTION SPEEDS AND
     QUALITY OF SERVICE STANDARDS, PROTECTING THE RIGHTS OF
      INTERNET SERVICE SUBSCRIBERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Internet Quality of
Service and Subscriber Protection Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
recognize access to the internet as a basic necessity for national development.
The State shall ensure the provision of reliable, accessible, and fast internet
services, protect consumers against deceptive and unfair trade practices, and
promote accountability among Internet Service Providers (ISPs) and Public
Telecommunications Entities (PTEs).

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   1. Internet Service Provider (ISP) refers to any entity, including PTEs,
      providing internet access to the public for a fee.
   2. Quality of Service (QoS) refers to the overall performance of a network,
      including parameters such as download/upload speed, latency, packet
      loss, and network availability.
   3. Advertised Speed refers to the internet connection speed marketed or
      offered by the ISP to the subscriber in their service contract.
   4. Service Outage refers to a complete loss of internet connectivity lasting for
      at least one (1) continuous hour.
SEC. 4. Minimum Internet Connection Speeds. — All ISPs and PTEs shall provide
a minimum base internet connection speed that shall not be lower than:

   •   Fifty (50) Megabits per second (Mbps) for fixed broadband access in urban
       and highly urbanized areas.
   •   Twenty-Five (25) Mbps for fixed broadband access in rural areas.
   •   Ten (10) Mbps for mobile broadband access nationwide.

The National Telecommunications Commission (NTC), in coordination with the
Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), shall review
and upgrade these minimum speed standards every two (2) years to align with
global technological advancements.

SECT. 5. Quality of Service (QoS) Standards. — ISPs shall strictly comply with the
following QoS parameters:

   1. Speed Reliability: Subscribers must experience a connection speed of not
      less than eighty percent (80%) of the subscribed Advertised Speed at least
      eighty percent (80%) of the time during a billing cycle.
   2. Network Availability: ISPs must maintain a network uptime of at least
      ninety-nine percent (99%) per month, excluding scheduled maintenance.
      Scheduled maintenance must be announced to subscribers at least forty-
      eight (48) hours in advance.
   3. Latency and Packet Loss: The NTC shall prescribe strict thresholds for
      maximum allowable latency and packet loss to ensure VoIP, video
      conferencing, and other real-time applications function smoothly.

SEC. 6. Rights of Internet Service Subscribers. — To protect consumers from
unfair practices, an Internet Subscriber Bill of Rights is hereby established:

   1. Right to Pro-Rated Rebates: Subscribers are entitled to an automatic, pro-
      rated rebate on their monthly bill for total service outages lasting twenty-
      four (24) cumulative hours or more within a single billing cycle, without the
      need for the subscriber to file a formal request.
   2. Right to Contract Termination: A subscriber may terminate their contract
      without incurring pre-termination fees or penalties if the ISP fails to deliver
      the guaranteed 80% minimum advertised speed for two (2) consecutive
      months.
   3. Right to Transparent Billing: ISPs must provide clear, itemized billing
      statements that explicitly state the advertised speed, actual data usage (if
      capped), and any applied rebates.
   4. Right to Prompt Customer Service: ISPs must resolve reported technical
      issues or service interruptions within forty-eight (48) hours of the initial
      complaint.
SEC. 7. Penalties. — Any ISP or PTE that fails to comply with the minimum
speeds, QoS standards, or the subscriber rights provisions outlined in this Act
shall be subject to the following penalties:

   •   First Offense: A fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos
       (P500,000.00) but not exceeding One Million Pesos (P1,000,000.00).
   •   Second Offense: A fine of not less than One Million Pesos (P1,000,000.00)
       but not exceeding Two Million Pesos (P2,000,000.00).
   •   Third Offense: A fine of not less than Two Million Pesos (P2,000,000.00) but
       not exceeding Five Million Pesos (P5,000,000.00) and the revocation of their
       legislative franchise or certificate of public convenience and necessity
       (CPCN).

SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the NTC, DICT, and the Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI), in consultation with consumer groups and industry stakeholders,
shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

SEC. 9. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
