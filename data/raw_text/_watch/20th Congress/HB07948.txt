                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The process of securing government clearances in the Philippines remains
fragmented, repetitive, and burdensome for citizens. Job applicants, overseas
Filipino workers, and other members of the transacting public are commonly
required to present multiple government-issued IDs and secure separate
clearances from the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), the Philippine
National Police (PNP), and their barangay of residence, among others.

       Each clearance-issuing agency maintains its own portal, procedures, and
fees, often requiring applicants to appear in person for photo or biometric capture.
Some institutions even demand additional clearances from the Sandiganbayan,
the Judiciary, or the Office of the Ombudsman. This lack of coordination among
agencies reflects the fragmented and non-interoperable nature of government
systems, which burdens the public and delays the delivery of credible and timely
information to law enforcement and judicial institutions.
         Moreover, the absence of a unified and authoritative source of clearance
  data results in redundant information storage and inconsistent verification
  standards. This fragmentation undermines data protection and erodes public trust,
  as sensitive personal information is dispersed across multiple databases without
  harmonized security protocols. Further, the financial cost of obtaining clearances,
  which is estimated at over 32% of an average household’s daily income,
  excluding transportation and opportunity costs, adds an unnecessary burden,
  particularly for low-income earners and those residing far from urban centers.

         While the government has taken significant steps toward digitalization
  through initiatives such as the eGov PH app of the Department of Information
  and Communications Technology (DICT), and the eLGU and eBOSS programs
  of the Anti-Red Tape Authority (ARTA), most systems remain siloed and
  incompatible. These efforts highlight the progress made but also underscore the
  need for a unified, interoperable platform to streamline clearance issuance and
  verification.

         To ease the transactions of the country’s workforce with national and local
  clearance-issuing agencies, an enabling law to build and sustain a service
  platform that extends the usability and spurs the adoption of the National ID,
  among others, and consolidates all clearance-related transactions into a highly
  available and federated service, securely accessible to every citizen or permanent
  resident, regardless of their location, is paramount.

         To these effects, the National Integrated Clearance System or NICS, shall
  provide a single window system for clearances that shall be accessible to
  individual applicants, employers, government agencies, and other authorized
  institutions at all times. It will harmonize government policies and streamline
  procedures on the issuance of regulatory, judiciary, or law enforcement
  clearances, thus, providing a persistent service that is available and accessible to
  individuals and permissioned institutions.

        This measure was also filed during the 19th Congress.

        In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                   TITLE I
                           PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National
Integrated Clearance System Act” or the “NICS Act.”


      Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to establish a single clearance system referred to as the “National Integrated
Clearance System,” or “NICS,” which shall provide “single window
functionalities” centralizing all transacting public requests for clearances,
responses, and subsequent issuances of clearance-issuing law enforcement or
judiciary agencies of government into a cohesive, non-intrusive, secure,
enterprise-grade, policy-based, rules-driven, and workflow-enabled system to
safeguard propriety, privacy, and proportionate use of sensitive private
information into a fully auditable service platform.


      It is in the interest of the State to achieve a paperless, seamless, and
interoperable environment as a foundation for government agencies to improve
service efficiency, transparency, auditability, traceability, and accountability; an
environment that enhances standards of governance and protocols to mitigate
deficiencies that may be exploited by threat actors; a system to curtail
bureaucratic red tape; and, a system to continually improve quality and ease in
the conduct of business between government agencies and the public.


      Towards this end, and specific to any public service at the national and
local levels involving the application and issuances of clearances, commensurate
with its intent to promote a paperless environment for processing, issuing, and
administering clearances, a secure national integrated clearance platform shall be
developed, implemented, and deployed, to serve as the main interface between
the transacting public and all clearance-issuing government institutions,
including, but not limited to, law enforcement, revenue, and judiciary institutions.
This platform shall be accessible to users and administrators using established
technologies that enforce strong security, encryption, and best-in-class identity
and privacy protection protocols, to ensure that propriety, privacy, and the
proportionate use of sensitive personal data are at all times upheld and protected.


      Section 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms
are defined as follows:

a) Applicant refers to the individual making a request for a clearance.
b) Consumer refers to the representative of a government or private entity
   requiring a clearance, authorized by the data owner, to use the

c) Clearance refers to the digitally signed document, exclusively
   presented in an abstracted and machine-readable-only form generated
   by a government agency, containing pertinent and data-delimited
   information.
d) Distributed Ledgers refers to digital registries containing encrypted
   copies of data stored across different locations and devices within a
   private-permissioned blockchain network.
e) Metadata refers to data which describes other data.
f) Blockchain Nodes refers to devices which maintain copies of the
   distributed ledgers.
g) Non-repudiability refers to a description of data or information which
   has proof of origin and authenticity that such data have not been
   tampered.
h) Portal refers to the online accessible system from which authorized
   users, using established security protocols and permission levels, can
   access authorized information.
i) Record refers to a uniquely identifiable data set of a transaction that is
   indexed and stored in a retrievable electronic format.
j) Security refers to the physical and virtual protection of interconnected
   systems, such as data, hardware, and software from internal or external
   threats.
k) Single Window refers to a system which allows persons and entities to
   transact using a centralized or single access point.

      Section 4. Scope. – This Act shall apply to the funding, acquisition,
development, integration, implementation, operationalization, and continual
future systems improvement, inclusive of technological, functional, and
procedural improvements of the National Integrated Clearance System, to service
all clearance-issuing national and local government agencies. Its scope shall be
limited to the following functions:


      (a) Serve as the single window for applicants to complete digital clearance
         requests to any clearance-issuing agency of government, national or
         local, determined by the type of clearance requested.
      (b) Serve as the single window for applicant-authorized consumers of
         clearances, government or private, to securely access data-delimited
         clearance issuances.
      (c) Simultaneously and digitally route all requests or application for
         clearances submitted by applicants to concerned agency or agencies, for
         proper dispensation, disposition, and issuance as required by the type
         of clearance requested.
      (d) Monitor the duration and status of all applications or requests baselined
         against the last recorded action.
      (e) Generate an immutable machine-readable-only reference code,
         barcode, or quick response (QR) code that is permanently coupled to
         the date and version of the issued clearance.
      (f) Maintain a network of context-specific and encrypted distributed ledger
         nodes to store non-repudiable and immutable, metadata-only, record of

         user access, submitted transactions, dispositions, issuances, and
         references to authorized changes.
      (g) Provide standard multi-protocol web service endpoints that shall be
         made accessible to all authorized and permissioned consumers.
      (h) Provide local and international airlines, public utility buses, and
         terrestrial and marine interisland passenger vessels, as a service, a
         standard web service endpoint facility for such, to check as a function
         of ticketing, against any outstanding restrictions, prohibitions, or
         warrants specifically preventing any passenger, whether a citizen, legal
         resident, or foreign persons from boarding any public transport vehicle,
         aircraft, or vessel.
      (i) Establish, publish, and maintain a national authoritative data
         interoperability standard encapsulating detailed specifications on
         required data structures, data harmonization, information sharing, data
         exchanges, data protection, security protocols, and privacy protection
         protocols affecting clearances issued by national or local government
         entities.
      (j) Provide continual data quality management and integration services to
         all national and local government agencies to transform and harmonize
         all data sources into a cohesive national interoperability standard as will
         be defined under the Implementing Rules and Regulations of this Act.

      Section 6. Limitations. – The NICS shall not be used by any government
agency, entity, or person to:


      (1) Store any data or information in substantive form and beyond that
          which is classified as metadata, that is generated and sourced from
          respective government agencies. The system shall strictly capture and
          store only transactional metadata, shall serve the purpose of aggregating
          the requests, routing requests to the appropriate agency, and
          consolidating the responses from issuing agencies using a system-
          generated machine-readable-only reference code in the form of a
          barcode or QR code. The contents or substance of each clearance shall
          remain under the governance and control of the issuing agency and shall
          not be stored in any of the system’s repositories.


      (2) Disclose, collect, record, convey, disseminate, publish, or use any
          information from the NICS, give access thereto, or give copies thereof
          to third parties or entities, including law enforcement agencies, national
          security agencies, or units of the Armed Forces of the Philippines
          (AFP), beyond those allowed under the Data Privacy Act, except in the
          following circumstances:
             (a) When the registered person has given his or her consent, specific
                to the purpose prior to collection and processing.
             (b) When the compelling interest of national security and public
                safety is at stake, relevant information may be disclosed upon
                order of a competent court, without prejudice to the principle of
                proportionate use as specified under the Republic Act No. 10173
                or the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and other laws and international
                treaties, as may be applicable.

      Section 7. National Integrated Clearance System Council. – The National
Integrated Clearance System Council, hereinafter referred to as the Council, is
hereby established to provide clearly defined directions, policies, and guidelines
to ensure the effective, proportionate, and secure implementation, coordination,
and use of the NICS.


      The Council shall be composed of the following:
             1. Secretary, National Economic Development Authority;
             2. National Statistician and Civil Registrar, Philippine Statistics
                Authority;
             3. Undersecretary, Department of Interior and Local Government;
             4. Undersecretary, Department of Justice;
             5. Chairperson, Commission on Human Rights; and,
             6. Chairman, National Privacy Commission.


      The Secretary of the National Economic Development Authority shall be
the Chairperson of the Council while the National Statistician and Civil Registrar
of the Philippine Statistics Authority shall be the Vice Chairperson.


      Section 8. Role of the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA). – The
Philippine Statistics Authority, together with the Council, shall carry out the
implementation of this Act in such manner that the operations of relevant
government agencies shall not be impeded in order not to unduly delay delivery
of public service: Provided, that the PSA shall create a transition plan, which shall
include pilot implementation, to gradually interface with all existing government
digital clearance systems. The transition plan shall set up the timelines and
deliverables and shall be crafted in coordination with agencies concerned and the

                                                                                    8
Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), based on
the guidelines issued by the Council and subject to the provisions of the Data
Privacy Act of 2012 and other applicable laws and regulations.


      Section 9. Interoperability and Harmonization. – Government agencies
identified by the Council in the implementing rules and regulations issued
pursuant to this Act shall work with the PSA to streamline and harmonize
interagency procedures and to integrate, connect, or establish automated digital
communications between the NICS and their respective digital systems:
Provided, That the government agencies have already established an acceptable
level of accuracy of their records.


      The Council, together with the PSA, shall regularly update the list of
government agencies which shall integrate with, connect to, or establish digital
communications with the NICS and shall regularly check the level of accuracy of
their records.


                                        TITLE III
                                    FINAL PROVISIONS

      Section 10. Administrative, Criminal or Civil Liability. – Violation of any
provision of this Act and falsification of records by any person, natural or
juridical, shall subject the person involved to administrative, criminal, or civil
liability, under applicable laws.


      Section 11. Presidential and Congressional Oversight. – For the effective
implementation of this Act, there shall be a Congressional Oversight Committee,
hereinafter referred to as the NICS Oversight Committee, which shall include two
(2) members from the Senate and twenty (20) members from the House of
                                                                                 9
Representatives. The PSA, in coordination with the Council, shall submit an
annual report to the NICS Oversight Committee and the President of the
Philippines not later than the first quarter of each year.


      Section 12. Appropriations and Funding. – The amount necessary for the
effective implementation of the provisions of this Act shall be taken from the
current year's appropriation of the Philippine Statistics Authority. Thereafter,
such amount as may be needed for the continued implementation of this Act shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


      Section 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one-hundred
eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the PSA, shall promulgate, with
the approval of the Council, the rules, and regulations to effectively implement
the provisions of this Act.


      Section 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be held
unconstitutional or invalid, all other provisions not affected thereby shall in full
force and effect.


      Section 15. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
