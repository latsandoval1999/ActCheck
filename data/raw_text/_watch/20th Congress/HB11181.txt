                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines has strong potential to become a competitive destination for international higher
education. Its widespread use of English, cultural diversity, and relatively affordable tuition make
its higher education sector attractive to foreign students and academic partners. Yet, despite these
advantages, the country remains a modest participant in global academic mobility. Foreign student
enrollment increased from 14,132 in 2017 to 22,247 in 2022, but still trails behind neighboring
countries such as Malaysia, Singapore, and Thailand.

This gap reflects persistent institutional barriers, including complex regulatory processes, limited
opportunities for foreign faculty, inadequate funding, weak international partnerships, and
insufficient campus facilities. While existing laws, including Republic Act No. 7722 and Republic
Act No. 11448, provide a foundation for higher education development and transnational education,
they do not establish a comprehensive and sustained national strategy for internationalization.

This measure seeks to address these gaps by establishing a National Internationalization Framework
for Higher Education, supported by institutional internationalization plans and an
Internationalization Support Fund. The framework will support student and faculty mobility,
international research partnerships, joint academic programs, and Internationalization at Home,
allowing students to gain global competencies even without studying abroad.The measure also
strengthens coordination among CHED, the Department of Trade and Industry, and the Department
of Foreign Affairs to promote Philippine higher education internationally.

Internationalization should not be pursued solely as a means of competing with other countries. It
should strengthen the quality of Philippine higher education, expand opportunities for Filipino
learners, and connect institutions across the country to the global academic community. Through a
coordinated and inclusive national policy, the Philippines can move from having the potential to
internationalize to actually becoming an active participant in global higher education.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Philippine Higher Education
 2   Internationalization Act".
 3   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote the
 4   internationalization of Philippine higher education as a strategic driver of national development,
 5   global competitiveness, and social equity. Consistent with the constitutional mandate to promote the
 6   right of all citizens to quality education, the State affirms that the systematic integration of
 7   international, intercultural, and global dimensions into higher education advances academic
 8   excellence, expands opportunity, and strengthens the capacity of Filipino learners and institutions
 9   to contribute to and benefit from a rapidly changing global environment.
10          Towards this end, the State shall pursue the following goals:
11      (a) Embed internationalization as a core and enduring function of Philippine higher education,
12          integrated into institutional governance, academic programming, quality assurance, research,
13          and student development, in a manner consistent with academic freedom, institutional
14          autonomy, and national interest;
15      (b) Ensure equitable and inclusive access to the benefits of internationalization for all learners,
16          faculty, and institutions, regardless of geographic location, institutional type, or resource
17          level, through structured public investment, capacity-building, and differentiated support;
18      (c) Strengthen the global engagement capacity of Philippine higher education institutions
19          through international partnerships, academic mobility, collaborative research, and
20          transnational education, resulting in measurable improvements in teaching quality, research
21          productivity, and institutional capability;
22      (d) Develop Philippine higher education as a competitive and credible education destination for
23          international learners, scholars, and researchers, contributing to foreign exchange earnings,
24          expanding global academic networks, and enhancing national reputation, while
25          strengthening the contribution of higher education internationalization to the country's
26          innovation ecosystem, workforce development agenda, and long-term economic resilience;
27          and
28      (e) Advance internationalization at home as a quality-oriented, access-equalizing approach that
29          ensures all learners benefit from global and intercultural learning within domestic
30          educational environments.
31   SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. -As used in this Act:
32      (a) Academic Mobility refers to the movement of students, faculty members, researchers, or
33          academic professionals across national borders for purposes of study, teaching, research,
34          training, or academic collaboration;
35      (b) Collaborative Online International Learning (COIL) refers to digitally enabled, faculty-led,
36          and curriculum-integrated teaching and learning activities conducted jointly by partner
37          institutions in different countries, without requiring physical travel;
38      (c) Dual or Double Degree Program refers to an academic program jointly delivered by partner
39          institutions in different countries, resulting in the conferment of two separate but coordinated
40          qualifications, each awarded by the respective partner institution;
41      (d) Education Export refers to the provision, marketing, and delivery of Philippine-based higher
42          education services to international learners, institutions, governments, and organizations
43          through any of the following modes: cross-border supply; consumption abroad; commercial
44          presence; and presence of natural persons, encompassing student mobility, transnational
45          higher education arrangements, joint and dual degree programs, collaborative research, and
46          digital or virtual learning;
47      (e) Internationalization at Home refers to deliberate institutional policies and practices that
48          embed international and intercultural learning outcomes into domestic curricula, pedagogy,
49          co-curricular activities, research, and campus environments, ensuring inclusive global
50          exposure regardless of physical mobility;
51      (f) Internationalization of Higher Education refers to the intentional, systematic, and sustained
52          integration of international, intercultural, transnational, and global dimensions into the
53          purpose, governance, core functions, delivery modalities, and outcomes of higher education,
54          encompassing instruction, research, extension, student services, quality assurance, and
55          institutional management;
56      (g) Joint Degree Program refers to an academic program jointly designed, delivered, and quality-
57          assured by two or more higher education institutions in different countries, leading to the
58          conferment of a single qualification awarded jointly by the partner institutions; and
59      (h) Transnational Higher Education (TNHE) refers to all modes of delivery of higher education
60          programs, courses, or educational services, including distance education, branch campuses,
61          franchise arrangements, and study-abroad programs, in which the learners are located in a
62          country different from the one where the awarding institution is based, or where the program
63          involves the education systems of a country other than where the program provider operates
64   SEC. 4. National Internationalization Framework for Higher Education. - The Commission on
65   Higher Education (CHED) shall establish, implement, and maintain a National Internationalization
66   Framework for Higher Education, hereinafter referred to as the Framework, which shall serve as the
67   central policy, planning, coordination, and governance instrument for all internationalization and
68   education export efforts across the Philippine higher education sector, and shall guide public and
69   private higher education institutions (HEIs) in integrating international, intercultural, and global
70   dimensions into their core institutional functions, consistent with academic freedom, institutional
71   autonomy, and national interest.
72          The Framework shall:
73      (a) Define strategic national objectives for higher education internationalization and education
74          export, aligned with national development priorities, workforce strategies, research and
75          innovation agendas, and the country's economic, social, and diplomatic goals;
76      (b) Identify priority geographic markets, partner countries, disciplines, and fields of study with
77          strong international demand, strategic national importance, or high potential for global
78          collaboration and education export, without compromising domestic access, equity, or
79          quality;
80      (c) Establish outcome-based performance indicators and benchmarks to assess institutional
81          readiness, program effectiveness, and sector-wide impact, including metrics on international
82          student enrollment, academic mobility, joint and dual degree offerings, international
83          research collaboration, faculty global exposure, and education export outcomes, designed to
84          promote continuous improvement rather than punitive regulation;
85      (d) Prescribe differentiated and inclusive pathways for internationalization, recognizing
86          variations in institutional mission, typology, geographic context, disciplinary strengths, and
87          resource capacity, and enabling all categories of HEIs, including state universities and
88          colleges (SUCs), local universities and colleges (LUCs), and private institutions, to pursue
89          strategies appropriate to their mandates;
90      (e) Set governance standards and institutional arrangements for internationalization at the sector
91          level, including guidance on leadership roles internal coordination structures,
 92          professionalization of international offices, partnership development and management, and
 93          transparent decision-making processes;
 94      (f) Articulate risk management principles and safeguards for cross-border education and
 95          education export, including consumer protection, academic integrity, data privacy, financial
 96          sustainability, reputational risk management, and compliance with applicable law;
 97      (g) Ensure coherence with quality assurance and accreditation systems, including outcomes-
 98          based education frameworks, program accreditation, and institutional quality assurance
 99          mechanisms, so that internationalization activities directly contribute to quality enhancement
100          and learner outcomes; and
101      (h) Establish mechanisms for periodic review and structured stakeholder consultation, involving
102          HEIs, faculty, students, industry, relevant government agencies, and international education
103          experts, to ensure the Framework remains evidence-based, responsive, and aligned with
104          national and global development CHED shall review and update the Framework at least once
105          every five (5) years, or earlier as warranted by significant changes in the national or global
106          higher education environment. The results of each review shall be publicly disclosed.
107   SEC. 5. Internationalization at Home. - CHED shall promote and support Internationalization at
108   Home as a core, inclusive, and sustainable pillar of higher education internationalization.
109   Recognizing that meaningful global and intercultural learning should not be contingent on physical
110   mobility, CHED shall ensure that all learners, regardless of capacity or opportunity for travel abroad,
111   benefit from internationally oriented learning experiences within domestic educational
112   environments.
113          For this purpose, Internationalization at Home initiatives shall include:
114      (a) Internationalization of Curriculum - This includes the integration of international,
115          comparative, and intercultural perspectives into program outcomes, course content, teaching
116          methods, assessment, and learning materials, consistent with quality assurance standards;
117      (b) Digital and Technological Engagement - This includes establishing a Collaborative Online
118          International Learning (COIL) system, as well as other forms of virtual or digital
119          international exchange with partner institutions abroad, enabling cross-border learning
120          without requiring physical travel;
121      (c) Degree Classification and Development - This refers to determining the expanse of degree
122          programs to be offered, including joint, dual, and collaborative degree programs
123          implemented within the Philippines in partnership with foreign higher education institutions,
124          subject to applicable quality assurance, accreditation, and regulatory requirements;
125      (d) Personnel Recruitment and Management - This includes engagement of international
126          scholars, visiting professors, researchers, and practitioners in teaching, research, mentoring,
127          curriculum development, and academic discourse, in accordance with applicable
128          immigration, labor, and professional regulatory laws;
129      (e) Campus Development - This refers to establishing a foundation of developing multicultural
130          and intercultural campus communities, including globally oriented student organizations,
131          cross-cultural programs, international student integration and support services, and campus
132          activities that foster mutual understanding and global citizenship; and
133      (f) Personnel Development Programs - This refers to faculty and staff capacity development for
134          internationalized teaching, learning, and institutional management, including training on
135          intercultural pedagogy, global curriculum design, digital collaboration, and international
136          partnership management.
137          CHED shall ensure that Internationalization at Home initiatives are inclusive, academically
138   rigorous, quality-driven, and complementary to, and not a substitute for, physical academic mobility
139   and other forms of international engagement.
140   SEC. 6. Institutional Internationalization Plans. - Each HEI shall develop, adopt, and implement
141   an Institutional Internationalization Plan (IIP) as an integral component of its institutional
142   development planning and quality assurance processes. The IIP shall articulate the institution's
143   vision, goals, and priorities for internationalization; identify the resources, governance
144   arrangements, and staffing necessary for implementation; define measurable targets and
145   accountability mechanisms; and include provisions for annual self-assessment, stakeholder
146   participation, and alignment with the HEI's quality assurance and accreditation obligations. CHED
147   shall issue guidelines prescribing minimum standards for IIPs, calibrated to institutional typology,
148   size, and mission. CHED shall provide technical assistance, particularly to SUCs and LUCs, in the
149   development and implementation of their respective Plans. Nothing in this section shall be construed
150   as limiting institutional autonomy or academic freedom in determining the substance and strategic
151   direction of an institution's internationalization priorities.
152   SEC. 7. Quality Assurance and Local Professional Protection. - CHED shall integrate
153   internationalization performance as an explicit component of its quality assurance, accreditation,
154   and institutional monitoring systems. CHED shall also prescribe quality standards for joint, dual,
155   transnational, and other cross-border academic programs offered by Philippine HEIs, consistent with
156   international best practices, applicable Philippine law, and relevant regional and global quality
157   assurance frameworks.
158          Minimum standards prescribed by CHED shall address:
159      (a) Program design, academic content, learning outcomes, and credit transfer and recognition
160          arrangements;
161      (b) Student protection, including transparent disclosure of fees, program terms, graduation
162          requirements, and applicable laws and regulations, as well as minimum standards for
163          insurance and health coverage for outbound students participating in academic mobility
164          programs;
165      (c) Partner institution due diligence, institutional accountability, and I and cross-border
166          governance requirements for transnational arrangements;
167      (d) Data privacy and cross-border data protection, governing the transfer of student and
168          institutional data to foreign partner institutions and platforms; and
169      (e) Compliance with immigration, labor, data privacy, consumer protection, and other
170          applicable laws.
171          CHED shall coordinate with the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) to ensure that
172   academic programs offered through joint, dual, and transnationalarrangements are aligned with
173   applicable professional licensure examinationprerequisites, and that Filipino graduates of such
174   programs are not prejudiced in their access to professional practice.

176   Service (IAS) of CHED is hereby designated as the primary office responsible for the formulation,

178   education internationalization and education export under this Act. Provided, That such designation
179   shall be without prejudice to the authority of the Commission en banc over higher education policy
180   and regulation.
181          The IAS shall:
182      (a) Lead strategic policy development and planning for higher education internationalization
183          and education export, and recommend such policies and plans to the Commission en banc
184          for approval;
185      (b) Coordinate, oversee, and support the implementation of internationalization programs,
186          projects, and activities of CHED and HEIs, including those funded under the
187          Internationalization Support Fund;
188      (c) Provide technical assistance and capacity-building support to HEIs in developing and
189          implementing Institutional Internationalization Plans, managing international partnerships,
190          establishing transnational higher education arrangements, and operationalizing academic
191          mobility programs;
192      (d) Coordinate with other CHED offices and services to integrate internationalization
193          considerations into quality assurance, policy planning, research, student services, and data
194          management systems;
195      (e) Serve as the primary inter-agency focal point for higher education internationalization and
196          education export, maintaining operational coordination with the Department of Trade and
197          Industry, the Department of Foreign Affairs, the Professional Regulation Commission, the
198          Bureau of Immigration, the Department of Labor and Employment, foreign diplomatic
199          missions, international organizations, and bilateral or multilateral education partners; and
200      (f) Develop, maintain, and manage data systems, analytics, and reporting mechanisms on
201          internationalization and education export outcomes, and submit annual reports to the
202          Commission en banc and to Congress as required under this Act.
203   Sec. 9. Internationalization Support Fund. - There is hereby created an Internationalization
204   Support Fund, hereinafter referred to as the Fund, which shall form part of the Higher Education
205   Fund and be administered by CHED. The Fund shall serve as a dedicated financing mechanism for
206   programs, projects, and activities that advance higher education internationalization and education
207   export development, guided by the principles of equity, quality, accountability, transparency, and
208   strategic impact.
209          Provided, That the Fund shall be sourced from at least ten percent (10%) of the Higher
210   Education Fund established under Republic Act No. 7722, otherwise known as the Higher Education
211   Act of 1994, which shall be earmarked and automatically allocated to the Fund.
212   Sec. 10. Use of the Fund. - The Fund shall be used to provide grants-in-aid, institutional support,
213   technical assistance, and capacity-building for programs, projects, and activities that advance higher
214   education internationalization and education export, including:
215       (a) Leadership development and governance capacity-building for HEI presidents, deans,
216           international relations officers, and academic leaders, including training on
217           internationalization strategy, transnational higher education management, global partnership
218           development, and education export governance;
219       (b) Academic mobility programs, including inbound and outbound student, faculty, researcher,
220           and academic professional mobility, short-term exchanges, visiting scholar appointments,
221           fellowships, joint supervision arrangements, and international internships;
222       (c) Development, accreditation, and implementation of joint, dual, and transnational degree
223           programs, including curriculum co-design, credit transfer and articulation mechanisms,
224           mutual recognition arrangements, and quality assurance alignment;
225       (d) International research collaboration and knowledge exchange, including participation in
226           international research consortia, co-funded research projects, joint publications, and
227           engagement in regional and global innovation and research networks;
228       (e) Internationalization at Home initiatives, including curriculum internationalization, virtual
229           exchange programs, engagement of international scholars and practitioners, intercultural
230           campus programs, and faculty and staff development;
231       (f) Institutional systems development, including the establishment or enhancement of
232           international offices, partnership and mobility managementsystems, digital learning and
233           transnational delivery platforms, student and faculty support services, and compliance
234           management systems;
235       (g) Strategic promotion and international positioning of Philippine HEIs and programs,
236           including participation in international education fairs, academic missions, and global
237           academic networks, in coordination with relevant government agencies; and
238       (h) Monitoring, evaluation, and policy research, including databases, performance indicator
239           systems, tracer studies, impact assessments, and technical studies supporting evidence-
240           based policymaking and continuous improvement.
241           In allocating and utilizing the Fund, CHED shall give priority to initiatives that demonstrably
242   contribute capacity-building, inclusive participation, and alignment with national education export
243   objectives, without compromising domestic higher education access, equity, or quality.
244   SEC. 11. Fund Allocation Framework. - CHED shall adopt and publicly disclose a prioritization
245   and scoring framework for the allocation of resources froM the Fund. The framework shall be guided
246   by the principle that allocation shall serve quality enhancement, institutional capacity-building, and
247   inclusive participation and shall not favor institutions solely on the basis of size, existing reputation,
248   or prior international exposure.
249           The framework shall include, at minimum, the following criteria:
250       (a) Alignment with the National Internationalization Framework and national development
251           priorities;
252       (b) Contribution to academic quality, teaching and learning improvement, and to academic
253           student competencies;
254       (c) Potential to strengthen institutional capacity, with priority consideration for emerging or
255           resource-constrained institutions;
256       (d) Inclusivity and equity, including the participation of SUCs, LUCs, regional and island-based
257           institutions, and underserved academic disciplines;
258       (e) Education export potential and impact on global engagement;
259       (f) Sustainability, replicability, and potential for institutionalization beyond initial grant
260           support;
261       (g) Cost-effectiveness, availability of counterpart funding, and efficient use of public resources;
262           and
263       (h) Adequacy of implementation plans, performance indicators, monitoring mechanisms, and
264           risk mitigation measures.
265            CHED is authorized to assign relative weights to the foregoing criteria, prescribe minimum
266   eligibility thresholds, and adopt differentiated scoring methodologies based on institutional typology
267   or stage of development. The framework shall be uniformly applied and subject to periodic review.

270   Sec. 12. Inter-Agency Coordination on Education Export. - CHED shall coordinate with the
271   Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) to advance
272   higher education internationalization, strengthen the country's education export sector, and promote
273   the global engagement of Philippine HEIs, consistent with the respective mandates, authorities, and
274   institutional competencies of the concerned agencies.
275          Such coordination shall include:
276      (a) Policy and Strategic Alignment. CHED, DTI, and DFA shall align higher education
277          internationalization initiatives with national services export strategies, trade and investment
278          promotion policies, and foreign policy objectives, including the identification of priority
279          markets, regions, and modalities for education export and academic cooperation;
280      (b) International Promotion and Market Development. DTI, in coordination with CHED, shall
281          support the integration of higher education services into national export promotion programs,
282          including the provision of market intelligence, participation in international education fairs,
283          and facilitation of linkages with foreign partners;
284      (c) Diplomatic and International Engagement. DFA, through Philippine foreign service posts
285          and diplomatic channels, shall assist in promoting Philippine higher education institutions
286          and programs abroad, facilitating academic government-to-government education
287          cooperation initiatives; and
288      (d) Facilitation of Academic Mobility and Partnerships. The concerned agencies shall, within
289          their respective mandates, support the mobility of students, faculty, researchers, and
290          academic professionals, and facilitate institutional partnerships, joint programs, and
291          collaborative research, subject to applicable immigration, labor, and regulatory
292          requirements.
293   CHED shall execute a formal inter-agency agreement with DTI and DFA, within one hundred eighty
294   (180) days from the effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations, defining respective agency
295   roles, deliverables, coordination mechanisms, data-sharing arrangements, and performance targets
296   for education export promotion.
297   CHED may establish technical working groups, focal points, or other consultative mechanisms as
298   necessary to ensure policy coherence, regular information exchange, and effective joint
299   implementation. Nothing in this section shall be construed as creating a new inter-agency body,
300   mandating automatic funding obligations, or diminishing the primary regulatory authority of any
301   concerned agency.
302   SEC. 13. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Annual Reporting. - CHED shall establish a comprehensive
303   monitoring and evaluation framework for the implementation of this Act, covering the National
304   Internationalization Framework, the Internationalization Support Fund, and the internationalization
305   performance of the higher education sector as a whole.
306          The monitoring and evaluation framework shall:
307      (a) Define outcome-based performance indicators aligned with the National Internationalization
308          Framework and consistent with this Act;
309      (b) Require annual data collection and reporting from HEIs on internationalization performance,
310          covering international student enrollment, academic mobility, joint and dual degree
311          programs, research collaboration, and education export outcomes; and
312      (c) Develop and maintain data systems, including performance databases, trace study
313          mechanisms, and policy analytics tools, to support evidence-based policymaking, program
314          evaluation, and continuous improvement; and provide for independent periodic evaluation
315          of program effectiveness, Fund utilization, and institutional compliance.
316   CHED shall submit to the Senate, House of Representatives, and the President of the Philippines,
317   within sixty (60) days after the end of each calendar year, an annual report on the state of higher
318   education internationalization in the Philippines. The annual report shall cover the implementation
319   of the National Internationalization Framework, the utilization and outcomes of the
320   Internationalization Support Fund sector-wide progress on internationalization and education
321   export, and and assessment of institutional compliance with this Act. The annual report shall be
322   made publicly available on the CHED website.
