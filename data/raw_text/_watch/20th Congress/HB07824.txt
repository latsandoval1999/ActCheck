                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Article II, Section 9 of the 1987 Constitution mandates the State to
promote a just and dynamic social order that shall ensure the prosperity and
independence of the nation and free the people from poverty through policies
that provide adequate social services and promote full employment, rising
standards of living, and an improved quality of life for all.

      In operationalizing this constitutional directive within the context of
disasters and public emergencies, Republic Act No. 10121, otherwise known
as the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010,
establishes the national framework for disaster risk reduction and
management (DRRM). Section 2 thereof declares it the policy of the State to,
among others:
      (a) Uphold the people's constitutional rights to life and property by addressing
      the root causes of vulnerabilities to disasters, strengthening the country's
      institutional capacity for disaster risk reduction and management and
      building the resilience of local communities to disasters including climate
      change impacts;
      xxx
      (d) Adopt a disaster risk reduction and management approach that is holistic,
      comprehensive, integrated, and proactive in lessening the socioeconomic and
      environmental impacts of disasters including climate change, and promote
      the involvement and participation of all sectors and all stakeholders
      concerned, at all levels, especially the local community;
      xxx
      (h) Institutionalize the policies, structures, coordination mechanisms and
      programs with continuing budget appropriation on disaster risk reduction
      from national down to local levels towards building a disaster-resilient nation
      and communities;
      xxx
      (l) Recognize and strengthen the capacities of LGUs and communities in
      mitigating and preparing for, responding to, and recovering from the impact
      of disaster's;
      x x x (n) Develop and strengthen the capacities of vulnerable and marginalized
      groups to mitigate, prepare for, respond to, and recover from the effects of
      disasters;
      xxx

      These policy declarations underscore the State’s obligation not merely
to respond to disasters, but to do so in a manner that is timely, coordinated,
and responsive to the immediate needs of affected populations.

       In practice, however, the delivery of assistance, particularly cash-
based aid, has frequently been hampered by administrative bottlenecks,
verification delays, coordination gaps between national agencies and LGUs,
and the absence of standardized timelines for release. While relief in kind
may be mobilized relatively swiftly, cash assistance, which is often more
flexible and responsive to household-specific needs, tends to suffer from
procedural lag.

      In situations where a locality is placed under a state of calamity or
emergency, affected families experience sudden income disruption,
displacement, and heightened vulnerability. In such circumstances, delayed
assistance undermines both the protective and recovery functions envisioned
under Republic Act No. 10121. Timely financial support is not merely
humanitarian relief, it is a mechanism for preventing deeper poverty,
indebtedness, and long-term socioeconomic decline.

      There is, therefore, a compelling need to institutionalize clear
standards, timelines, and mechanisms for the prompt and automatic release
of cash assistance to families affected by declared disasters or emergencies.
The proposed measure seeks to ensure the timely distribution of monthly
cash assistance for the duration of the declared emergency or calamity,
subject to defined parameters and oversight safeguards. By doing so, it
strengthens the implementation of existing disaster response policies and
reinforces the State’s constitutional duty to protect vulnerable communities
in times of crisis.

It is for the foregoing premises that the approval of this Bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
    PROVIDING FOR THE AUTOMATIC RELEASE OF MONTHLY CASH
        ASSISTANCE TO PROVINCES, UPON OCCURRENCE OF
                 DISASTERS OR EMERGENCIES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Automatic
Emergency or Disaster Relief Act.”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State acknowledges the
importance of ensuring that emergency or disaster response, specifically aid
or relief distribution, is made more responsive to the immediate needs of
Filipinos affected by emergencies or disasters. The State recognizes that this
goes beyond extending the right or appropriate kind of aid to beneficiaries,
encompassing even the very promptness of distribution.

       Thus, it shall be State policy to immediately, or without delay, give cash
assistance to affected localities, to capacitate each family to freely attend to
or satisfy their unique needs, which cannot be simply resolved by the
distribution of traditional or usual relief goods.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – The following terms, as used in this
Act, shall be understood thusly:

      A. Emergency. – Any unforeseen or sudden occurrence, especially
         danger, demanding immediate action, which causes, prompts, or
         results in a declaration of a state of emergency, in accordance with
         the provisions of the 1987 Constitution.

         This shall encompass public health emergencies.

      B. Disaster. – A serious disruption of the functioning of a community
         or a society involving widespread human, material, economic, or
         environmental losses and impacts, which the affected community or
         society cannot feasibly cope with using only its own resources.

         It also refers to any happening which causes, prompts, or results in
         a declaration of a state of calamity, pursuant to the provisions of
         Republic Act. No. 10121 (“Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and
         Management Act of 2010”).

      C. Automatic Relief. – Automatic cash assistance to provinces upon
         occurrence of disasters or emergencies.

       SECTION 4. Automatic Emergency or Disaster Relief. – Upon the
declaration of a state of emergency, or of calamity, the Regional Offices (ROs)
of the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) shall
automatically begin releasing cash assistance to families in the affected
localities.

       The release shall be done per family and by province. Each family living
in a province that has been placed under a state of emergency, or of calamity,
shall receive Five thousand to Eight thousand pesos (PhP5,000.00 to
PhP8,000.00), depending on the minimum wage rate in their respective
region, or any other amount as determined by the Congress upon the
recommendation of the relevant government agencies, per month, until the
issuance of the declaration officially lifting the state of emergency, or of
calamity.

       SECTION 5. Participation of Local Government Units (LGUs). – The funds
for the monthly cash assistance to affected families shall be devolved by the
DSWD to its ROs, which will take charge of distribution to beneficiary families,
and shall not be given to the LGUs. LGUs’ participation shall be limited to the
following:

      1) Preparing, validating, and submitting to the DSWD’s involved ROs a
         list of beneficiary families, based on their records of families actually
         residing within their jurisdiction upon the declaration of the state of
         emergency, or of calamity; and

      2) Providing the necessary support to the DSWD’s involved ROs’ cash
         assistance distribution, including in terms of logistics and security.

      SECTION 6. Appropriations. – Such an amount as may be necessary to
implement the provisions of this Act is hereby authorized to be appropriated
from the National Treasury. Congress shall enact a special appropriation
within 15 days from the occurrence of an emergency or disaster.


      SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days after this Act takes effect, the DSWD, in consultation and coordination
with appropriate government agencies, representatives from the private
sector, and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary implementing
rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

       SECTION 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 10. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
