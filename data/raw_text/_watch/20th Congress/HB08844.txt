13                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE
14
15   The COVID-19 pandemic fundamentally changed the way people work and showed
16   that telecommuting is not merely an emergency response: but a reliable, sustainable,
17   and practical work arrangement. For many workers, remote work reduced time lost
18   in traffic and added more energy in productivity, lowered transportation costs, and
19   improved work-life balance, while allowing employers to maintain efficiency and
20   continuity of operations.
21
22   Although Republic Act No. 11165, or the “Telecommuting Act,” already recognizes
23   telecommuting as an alternative work arrangement and was enacted to promote
24   flexibility using telecommunications and technological advancements in computers,
25   it has also underscored the need to revisit and strengthen the existing law so it can
26   better respond to present-day realities and provide clearer protection for workers.
27   While the current framework provides the basis for telecommuting in the private
28   sector, it remains limited in scope and does not sufficiently address the needs and
29   welfare of employees engaged in such arrangements.
30
31   This proposed measure seeks to expand the coverage of Republic Act No. 11165 by
32   expressly including employees in the public sector and by recognizing telecommuting
33   as an output-oriented work arrangement applicable where physical presence in the
34   workplace is not essential to the performance of duties.
35
36   The bill further provides for the voluntary adoption of telecommuting programs,
37   subject to mutually agreed terms and conditions between employers and employees,
38   while ensuring that all minimum labor standards are fully observed. It requires
39   employers to give employees adequate written information on the terms and
40   conditions of the telecommuting program – including working hours, overtime, rest
41   days, and leave benefits, so that their rights and responsibilities are clearly defined.
42
43   Moreover, the measure introduces a system of incentives for telecommuting
44   employees through the grant of a non-taxable monthly allowance to help defray
45   additional expenses incurred in remote work, such as internet connectivity and
46   electricity costs. Correspondingly, the bill allows such allowances granted by private
47   employers to be treated as deductible expenses for income tax purposes, thereby
48   encouraging employer compliance and support, as well as a wider adoption of
49   telecommuting arrangements.
50

 1   The amendments likewise strengthen mechanisms for administration and redress by
 2   providing for recourse and clarifying the role of appropriate government agencies,
 3   including the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) and the Civil Service
 4   Commission (CSC), as may be applicable, when grievance mechanisms are absent or
 5   inadequate in resolving disputes arising from telecommuting arrangements.
 6
 7   A more responsive and comprehensive legal framework such as this bill ensures that
 8   telecommuting arrangements are fair, transparent, and beneficial to both employers
 9   and employees.
10
11   By institutionalizing stronger protections and incentives for telecommuting, the aim
12   is to promote a more inclusive, efficient, and responsive work environment that
13   adapts to the changing demands of workforce and its evolving workplace practices –
14   especially today in the digital age of modernized labor – while safeguarding the rights
15   and welfare of professional workers.
16
17   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

13   AN ACT EXPANDING THE COVERAGE OF THE TELECOMMUTING ACT
14    AND PROVIDING INCENTIVES FOR TELECOMMUTING EMPLOYEES,
15        AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11165,
16     OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "TELECOMMUTING ACT" AND FOR
17                       OTHER PURPOSES
18
19
20   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
21   Congress assembled:
22
23   SECTION 1. Section 3 of Republic Act No. 11165 or the "Telecommuting Act" is
24   hereby amended to read as follows:
25
26                “Section 3. Telecommuting Defined. — As used in this Act, the term
27         "telecommuting” refers to [a] AN OUTPUT-ORIENTED work arrangement
28         that allows an employee in the private OR PUBLIC sector to work from an
29         alternative workplace with the use of telecommunication and/or computer
30         technologies.”
31
32   SECTION 2. Section 4 of Republic Act No. 11165 or the "Telecommuting Act" is
33   hereby amended to read as follows:
34
35                “Section 4. Telecommuting Program. — An employer in the private OR
36         PUBLIC sector may offer a telecommuting program to its employees on a
37         voluntary basis, WHOSE PHYSICAL PRESENCE IN THE
38         WORKPLACE IS NOT NECESSARY FOR THE PERFORMANCE OR
39         COMPLETION OF THEIR JOB, and upon such terms and conditions as
40         they may mutually agree upon: Provided, That such terms and conditions shall
41         not be less than the minimum labor standards set by law, and shall include
42         compensable work hours, minimum number of work hours, overtime, rest
43         days, and entitlement to leave benefits. In all cases, the employer shall provide
44         the telecommuting employee with relevant written information to adequately
45         apprise the individual of the terms and conditions of the telecommuting
46         program, and the responsibilities of the employee.”
47
48   SECTION 3. Section 7 (b) of Republic Act No. 11165 or the "Telecommuting Act" is
49   hereby amended to read as follows:

11   SECTION 4. A new section is hereby inserted after Section 8 of Republic Act No.
12   11165 or the “Telecommuting Act” which shall read as follows:
13
14   “SECTION 8-A. INCENTIVES FOR TELECOMMUTING EMPLOYEES. –
15   ALL EMPLOYEES WORKING UNDER A TELECOMMUTING PROGRAM
16   SHALL BE ENTITLED TO A NON-TAXABLE TELECOMMUTING
17   ALLOWANCE OF AT LEAST ONE THOUSAND PESOS (P1,000.00) FOR
18   EVERY MONTH SERVED UNDER SUCH AGREEMENT: PROVIDED,
19   THAT NOTHING IN THIS PROVISION SHALL BE CONSTRUED TO
20   ELIPIINATE OR IN ANY WAY DIMINISH SUPPLEMENTS,
21   ALLOWANCES, OR OTHER EMPLOYEE BENEFITS SERVING A SIMILAR
22   PURPOSE BEING ENJOYED AT THE TIME OF THE IMPLEMENTATION
23   OF THIS LAW: PROVIDED, FURTHER, THAT NOTHING IN THIS
24   PROVISION SHALL BE INTERPRETED TO PROHIBIT THE EMPLOYERS
25   OF THE COLLECTIVE BARGAINING AGREEMENT FROM PROVIDING
26   TERMS HIGHER OR MORE FAVORABLE THAN THE ABOVE-
27   MENTIONED RATE: PROVIDED, FINALLY, THAT ALLOWANCES,
28   GRANTED BY PRIVATE EMPLOYERS UNDER THIS PROVISION SHALL
29   BE DEDUCTIBLE FOR INCOME TAX PURPOSES.”
30
31   SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of
32   Labor and Employment (DOLE), the Civil Service Commission (CSC), the
33   Department of Finance (DOF), and the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), shall
34   promulgate the rules and regulations to implement this Act within ninety (90) days
35   from the effectivity of this Act.
36
37   SECTION 6. Separability Clause. – If any part, section or provision of this Act
38   is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any other
39   provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in full force and effect.
40
41   SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, acts, decrees, executive orders, rules,
42   and regulations, letters of instructions, proclamations, or other issuances or parts
43   thereof which are contrary to and inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
44   hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.
45
46   SECTION 8. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
47   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
