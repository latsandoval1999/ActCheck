                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) remain among the country’s
strongest economic pillars, with personal remittances reaching USD 36.14
billion in 2024, accounting for a significant share of the Philippines’ gross
domestic product and serving as a lifeline for millions of Filipino households.1
Despite this substantial contribution, many OFWs continue to face high
remittance fees, non-transparent exchange rate practices, and exposure to
abusive or exploitative financial schemes that reduce the value of their hard-
earned income.
        This bill seeks to establish a comprehensive framework for the protection
of OFW remittances by regulating fees imposed by financial institutions,
ensuring transparency in exchange rates, penalizing fraudulent and abusive
practices, and strengthening financial literacy among OFWs and their families.
It also promotes greater accountability among service providers and reinforces
the role of government agencies in safeguarding remittance flows.
      By securing the integrity and maximizing the value of remittances, this
measure aims to protect the financial welfare of OFWs and sustain their critical
contribution to national economic stability.

         AN ACT PROVIDING FOR PROTECTION OF THE REMITTANCES
                    OF OVERSEAS FILIPINO WORKERS

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
           Congress assembled:


 1            SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Overseas
 2      Filipino Workers (OFWs) Remittance Protection Act.”
 3            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The Constitution affirms that labor is the
 4      primary socioeconomic force and mandates the State to protect the rights of the
 5      workers and promote their welfare.
 6            Recognizing the significant contribution of OFWs to the national economy
 7      through foreign exchange remittances, the State shall adopt measures to protect the
 8      hard-earned money they remit home against usurious interest rates and exorbitant
 9      fees charged by financial institutions that deplete the value of their remittances and
10      provide them and their families adequate education and training on financial
11      literacy, such as financial planning and management of finances or savings, to help
12      ensure a source of livelihood even after their overseas employment.


13            SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

 1         (a) Overseas Filipino Worker refers to a person who is to be engaged, is
 2             engaged or has been engaged in a remunerated activity in a state of which
 3             the person is not a citizen or on board a vessel navigating the foreign seas

12         SEC. 4. Applicability of this Act. – The provisions of this Act shall be
13   applicable to all OFW remittances, whether voluntary or mandated by law, orders,
14   issuances, or rules and regulations.
15         SEC. 5. Discount on Remittance Fees and Tax Deduction Granted to
16   Establishments. – Fees imposed by banks and non-bank financial intermediaries on
17   OFW remittances to immediate family members shall be subject to a fifty percent
18   (50%) discount.
19         Bank and non-bank financial intermediaries providing discounts on
20   remittance fees may claim the discounts granted as tax deduction based on the cost
21   of services rendered to OFWs to be treated as ordinary and necessary expense
22   deductible from the gross income of the intermediary falling under the category of
23   itemized deductions: Provided, That the Secretary of Finance shall, upon the
24   recommendation of the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, issue the pertinent
25   revenue regulation for the purpose.
26         SEC. 6. Requirement of Posting of the Peso Equivalent of the Currency to
27   be Exchanged. – All bank and non-bank financial intermediaries offering remittance
28   services to OFWs shall be required to post in a conspicuous place within the




                                                                                         2
 1   establishment's premises the Philippine peso equivalent rate of the foreign
 2   currencies being transacted. The Philippine peso equivalent of the amount as
 3   remitted shall be the same amount that shall be received by the beneficiary of the
 4   remittance.
 5         SEC. 7. Prohibition from Raising Remittance Fees. – All banks and non-
 6   bank financial intermediaries offering remittance services to OFWs are prohibited
 7   from raising their current remittance fees without prior consultation with the
 8   Department of Finance (DOF), Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), and the
 9   Department of Migrant Workers (DMW).
10         SEC. 8. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts are also prohibited:
11         (a) Misappropriation or conversion, to the prejudice of the OFW or
12             beneficiary, of foreign exchange remittances received in trust, or on
13             commission, or for administration, or under any other obligation
14             involving the duty to make delivery of, or to return the same, or by
15             denying having received such foreign exchange remittance;
16         (b) Taking of foreign exchange remittances without the consent of the OFW
17             or beneficiary;
18         (c) Imposition of remittance fees in excess of those prescribed under Section
19             5 of this Act;
20         (d) Failure to post in a conspicuous place of the establishment the Philippine
21             Peso rate of the foreign currency being transacted; and
22         (e) Failure to conduct consultation with the DOF, BSP, and the DMW before
23             raising remittance fees.
24         SEC. 9. Penalties. – Any person who is found guilty of any of the acts
25   described in Section 8 hereof shall be punished by an imprisonment of six (6)
26   months and one (1) day to six (6) years and a fine of Fifty thousand pesos (PHP
27   50,000.00) but not exceeding Seven hundred fifty thousand pesos (PHP
28   750,000.00).

10          SEC. 10. Free Mandatory Financial Education for OFWs and their
11   Families. – The DMW as the lead agency, in coordination with the Overseas
12   Workers Welfare Administration (OWWA), the DOF, and the BSP, shall ensure that
13   a mandatory financial education program – which shall be served free-of-charge –
14   shall be provided to OFWs and their families. These agencies shall likewise educate
15   the OFWs and their families on the following financial subjects, issues or programs:
16   Consumer Protection, Protection on Mortgaged or Collateralized Properties,
17   Avoidance of Exorbitant Interests on Loans or Debts, and Credit Information on
18   Micro- and Small-Scale Enterprises to prospective lenders.
19          Financial education shall also include information on Obligations and
20   Contracts, Credit Transactions, Interests, Pledges, Mortgages, Guarantees, and
21   knowledge on Financial Products, such as stocks, bonds, insurance, and mutual
22   funds, which are available in the market.
23          As such, all OFWs shall undergo mandatory and continuously updated
24   financial education (literacy training) seminars which shall become an integral part
25   of the Pre-Departure Orientation Seminar (PDOS) of departing OFWs. Moreover,
26   Post-Arrival Training Seminars (PATS) on financial education or literacy shall
27   likewise be required of OFWs within a reasonable time upon their arrival in their
28   respective countries of destination on their first employment contract, or those who

13          The DMW shall regularly coordinate with the Department of Information and
14   Communications Technology and social media companies to disallow posts and

17   scams, pyramiding schemes, and the like.
18          As the lead agency, the DMW is tasked to formulate training modules on
19   financial education or literacy; initiate and conduct training programs on financial
20   management and responsibility; apprise OFWs on financial opportunities and
21   pitfalls, among others; ensure the accessibility of qualified online financial and
22   economic advisors and/or counselors; and disseminate knowledge-expanding
23   activities that promote financial education (literacy) among OFWs and their
24   dependents.
25          SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, issuances,
26   rules and regulations which may be inconsistent with any of the provisions of this
27   Act are hereby deemed repealed, amended or modified accordingly.
