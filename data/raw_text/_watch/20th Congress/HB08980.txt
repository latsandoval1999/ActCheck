                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines stands at a critical moment in its pursuit of sustainable
economic growth and resilience. While the country has historically relied on
fossil-based energy sources to drive development, the escalating threats of
climate change warrant a substantial change in our national energy policy.
This bill seeks to institutionalize a clean and just energy transition framework
that balances the urgent need for decarbonization with the essential
requirements of energy security and economic equity.

       Current global energy trends and international commitments under the
Paris Agreement demand a concrete move toward renewable and clean energy
systems. By setting a moratorium on new fossil fuel power plants and
establishing a clear roadmap for the phase-out of existing systems, this
measure ensures that the Philippines remains competitive in a global market
that is rapidly prioritizing "green" investments. Furthermore, the inclusion of
a Carbon Tax mechanism creates a necessary fiscal deterrent against high-
emissions activities, while the prohibition on passing these costs to end-
consumers ensures that the burden of transition remains with the industrial
emitters rather than the Filipino households.

       An important anchor of this legislation is the principle of a "Just
Transition." Recognizing that a shift away from fossil fuels could disrupt the
livelihoods of thousands of workers in the coal and transport sectors, the bill
mandates comprehensive labor protection and retraining programs. By
involving agencies such as DOLE and TESDA, the state guarantees that the
workforce is equipped with the skills necessary to thrive in the emerging
energy industry.

       Finally, this bill provides the necessary institutional mechanisms by
empowering the Climate Change Commission to lead a multi-sectoral
technical working group. This "whole-of-government" approach harmonizes
existing law, including the Renewable Energy Act and the Electric Vehicle
Industry Development Act, into a single, cohesive Energy Transition Plan. By
providing clear national targets and a stable regulatory environment, this Act
will attract sustainable investments, lower greenhouse gas emissions, and
secure a cleaner, more resilient future for all Filipinos.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
     PROVIDING FOR A NATIONAL ENERGY POLICY AND FRAMEWORK FOR
       A CLEAN AND JUST ENERGY TRANSITION IN THE COUNTRY, AND
                   APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Republic
     of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

1          SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Energy
2    Transition Act".

3          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
4    State to adopt a clean, just, and comprehensive energy transition framework
5    which shall:

6       a. Enable the country to meet its international obligations to lower
7          greenhouse gas emissions by reducing usage of fossil-based systems of
8          energy production and consumption;

9       b. Provide protection to the environment by accelerating the exploration,
10         development, and utilization of clean energy sources to include, among
11         others, biomass, solar, wind, geothermal, ocean energy, hydropower,
12         green hydrogen, and biofuels;
1       c. Ensure energy security in the country as it transitions into clean
2          energy;

3       d. Warrant energy equity in the country during the energy transition; and

4       e. Afford protection to labor and investments while the country shifts from
5          fossil-based systems of energy production and consumption to clean
6          energy sources.

7          SEC. 3. Scope. - This Act shall establish a framework for introducing
8    and institutionalizing fundamental policies on energy transition, including
9    the promotion of clean energy sources, decrease in the utilization of fossil-
10   based sources of energy, and the delineation of responsibilities among various
11   government agencies in the energy sector.

12         SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act, the following terms
13   shall be defined as:

14      a. "Carbon offset" refers to a certification attesting to a reduction in
15         greenhouse gas (GHG) by a voluntary carbon market provider. It can be
16         used for voluntary carbon reduction commitments or for compliance
17         with cap-and-trade programs, if applicable;

18      b. "Carbon tax" refers to the tax charged on the total amount of reckonable
19         GHG emissions of a taxable facility;

20      c. "Clean Energy Sources" refers to technologies, such as electric vehicles
21         and fuel cells, and energy sources, such as renewable energy, that when
22         utilized emits minimal or no GHG;

23      d. "Climate Risk" refers to both physical and transition risk arising from
24         climate change;

25      e. "Emissions Threshold" refers to the maximum amount of GHG that
26         various sources can emit without being subjected to the carbon tax as
27         determined by Climate Change Commission pursuant to Section 7 of
28         this Act;

29      f. "Energy Sector" refers to the sector engaged in exploration and
30         production, refining and marketing, storage and transportation, and
31         utilization of energy sources such as fossil fuels and renewable energy,
32         whichever is applicable;

33      g. "Energy Transition" refers to the global energy sector's shift from fossil-
34         based systems of energy production and consumption to clean energy
35         sources;
1    h. "Hybrid Systems" refer to any power or energy generation facility which
2       makes use of two or more types of technologies utilizing both
3       conventional and/or renewable fuel sources, such as, but not limited
4       to, integrated solar/wind systems, biomass/fossil fuel systems,
5       hydro/fossil fuel systems, integrated solar/biomass systems,
6       integrated wind/fossil fuel systems, with a minimum of ten (10)
7       megawatts or ten percent (10%) of the annual energy output provided
8       by the RE component;

9    i. "Just Transition" refers to globally recognized principles that should
10      guide the transition to environmentally sustainable economies and
11      societies;

12   j. "Net zero emissions" refers to the state of the atmosphere where the
13      GHG emitted are balanced by its removal;

14   k. “Physical risk" refers to potential loss or damage to tangible assets
15      arising from climate change or other weather-related conditions such
16      as floods, typhoons, droughts, earthquakes, extreme weather
17      variability, and rising sea levels;

18   l. "Reckonable emissions" refers to GHG emissions of a taxable facility in
19      circumstances specified by the schedule to be prepared by the Climate
20      Change Commission pursuant to Section 7 of this Act;

21   m. "Renewable Energy" refers to energy resources that do not have an
22      upper limit on the total quantity to be used include, among others,
23      biomass, solar, wind, geothermal, ocean energy, and hydropower
24      pursuant to the provision of Republic Act No. 9513;

25   n. "Taxable facility" refers to a juridical person that emits GHG above the
26      emissions threshold;

27   o. "Transition risk" refers to the potential economic adjustment cost
28      resulting from policy, legal, technology, and market changes to meet
29      climate change mitigation and adaptation requirements;

30   p. "Voluntary Carbon Market (VCM)" refers to a digital platform where
31      natural or juridical persons can buy and sell carbon offsets;

32   q. "VCM consumers" refers to natural or juridical persons who buy carbon
33      offsets in the VCM; and

34   r. "VCM providers" refers to natural or juridical persons who sell carbon
35      offsets in the VCM.
1          SEC. 5. National Targets. - The country, through the national
2    government agencies specified in this Act, shall:

3       a. Implement a moratorium on the following:

4            i.   Establishment of new fossil fuel power plants upon the effectivity
5                 of this Act;

6           ii.   Sale of new internal combustion engine vehicles within the
7                 timeframe to be determined by the Energy Transition Plan;

8       b. Implement a phase out of the following:

9            i.   Existing fossil fuel power plants within the timeframe to be
10                determined by the Energy Transition Plan;

11          ii.   Existing internal combustion engine vehicles within           the
12                timeframe to be determined by the Energy Transition Plan;

13      c. Increase the share of clean energy sources in the energy mix of the
14         country while taking into account the moratorium of new fossil fuel
15         power plants and phase out of existing fossil fuel power plants;

16      d. Reduce GHG emissions in accordance with the country's National
17         Determined Contribution (NDC) and achieve net zero emissions in the
18         energy sector by 2050; and

19      e. Establish a preferential policy for the upgrading of all off-grid power
20         plants to hybrid systems.

21         SEC. 6. Energy Transition Plan. - There shall be an Energy Transition
22   Plan (ETP) which shall provide for a comprehensive framework in the
23   exploration, development, utilization, and distribution of energy sources as
24   well as resilience and upgrading of existing energy infrastructure in support
25   of the country's shift from fossil-based systems of energy production and
26   consumption to clean energy sources guided by the principle of just transition
27   as defined under Section 12 of this Act. The ETP shall be prepared by the
28   Climate Change Commission (CCC), in coordination with the technical
29   working group created under Section 7 of his Act.

30         The ETP shall:

31      a. Include the following:
32           i. The national targets under Section 5 of this Act,
1          ii.    The annual targeted reduction in GHG emissions for the energy
2                 sector,

3          iii.   Mechanisms to determine compliance with the targets by the
4                 energy sector which shall be the monitored by the CCC, and

5          iv.    Programs to be implemented by the Department of Energy,
6                 Department of Transportation, Department of Trade and
7                 Industry, Department of Environment and Natural Resources,
8                 and Department of Agriculture, and other relevant agencies to
9                 reduce GHG emissions to reach the annual targets;

11        including Republic Act No. 9136, Republic Act No. 9513, Republic Act
12        No. 11285 otherwise known as the Energy Efficiency and Conservation
13        Act, Republic Act No. 11572 otherwise known as the Philippine Energy
14        Research and Policy Institute Act, Republic Act No. 11646 otherwise
15        known as the Microgrid Systems Act, and Republic Act No. 11697
16        otherwise known as the Electric Vehicle Industry Development Act;

17     c. Be harmonized with the existing plans which shall include the National
18        Determined Contribution, Philippine Energy Plan, National Transport
19        Policy, Philippine Development Plan and Health and Pollution Action
20        Plan; and

21     d. Be updated and submitted to the Office of the President and Congress

23        Energy Transition Committee.

18          SEC. 8. Carbon Tax. - A carbon tax shall be levied, assessed, and
19   collected on a taxable facility amounting to the following:

20      a. Php 100 per ton of carbon dioxide equivalent (tCO2e): three (3) years
21         after the effectivity of this Act, and

22      b. Php 250 per tCO2e: five (5) years after the effectivity of this Act.

23       The amount levied shall accrue to the Energy Transition Fund under
24   Section 15 of this Act. The carbon tax shall not be passed on to end consumers
25   of fossil fuels and fossil fuel power plants.

26          SEC. 9. Voluntary Carbon Market (VCMs). - VCMs shall be allowed to
27   be established and operate in the country. The CCC and the SEC shall, upon
28   consultation with public and private stakeholders and taking into
29   consideration internationally recognized best practices of VCMs, establish
30   and implement streamlined and simplified guidelines and regulations for the
31   establishment and operations of VCMs in the country within one (1) year from
32   the effectivity of this Act. The DOE and SEC shall regularly review and update,
33   if necessary, these guidelines and regulations.
1            SEC. 10. Mandatory Disclosure. - The SEC shall make it mandatory
2    for listed companies to disclose climate risks as the country shift from fossil
3    fuels to clean energy sources.

4           SEC. 11. Just Transition. - Under the principle of just transition, the
5    TWG, in approving the ETP and its implementation, shall ensure a fair and
6    inclusive energy transition by minimizing its negative impacts to communities
7    that are reliant on fossil-based system of production and utilization. This shall
8    include labor programs which includes conduct of training for employees that
9    are under fossil-based system of production and utilization industries prior
10   the implementation of this Act to ensure their continuous employment, if
11   practicable, and to protect their rights and benefits in accordance with the
12   existing labor laws and rules and regulations.

13         SEC. 12. Application of Incentives under RA 9513 or Renewable
14   Energy Act of 2008 and RA 11285 or the Energy Efficiency and
15   Conservation Act. - Projects for the implementation of the ETP under this
16   Act shall be entitled to the incentives under RA 9513 and RA 11285.

17         SEC. 13. Prohibited Acts. - The following acts are prohibited:
