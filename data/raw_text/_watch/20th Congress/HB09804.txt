                               AN ACT
ESTABLISHING AT SOUTHERN LEYTE STATE UNIVERSITY – HINUNANGAN
CAMPUS A HIGHLY-SPECIALIZED RESEARCH INSTITUTE TO BE KNOWN AS THE
DISASTER RESILIENCE FOR ECO-EFFICIENT AGRICULTURE MANAGEMENT
INSTITUTE (DREAM INSTITUTE), DEFINING ITS MANDATE, POWERS, FUNCTIONS,
ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Southern Leyte possesses immense ecological, agricultural, and tourism-linked assets
that place it in a strategic position to pursue an integrated Tourism-Environment-
Agriculture (TEA) Nexus of development. As an agriculture-based province with
important upland, watershed, spring, riverine, coastal, and biodiversity-rich
ecosystems, Southern Leyte requires a specialized institutional mechanism capable
of generating baseline scienti ic data, translating such knowledge into resilient
production and livelihood systems, protecting biodiversity and ecosystem integrity,
and supporting disaster- and climate-responsive decision-making.

Southern Leyte State University – Hinunangan Campus has already established three
Board-approved research centers that respond to portions of this challenge: the Soil
and Water Research Institute (SWaRI), the Smallholder Horticulture and Agroforestry
Research & Extension (SHARE) Center, and the Biodiversity Conservation and
Management Institute (BioCon). However, these centers presently operate with a
limited personnel complement and a fragmented institutional arrangement, making it
dif icult to fully respond to the increasing demands of climate adaptation,
environmental protection, agricultural resilience, biodiversity conservation,
community-based development, and policy support in the Province of Southern Leyte.

                                                                       1|Page
To address this need, this bill seeks to establish a uni ied and highly specialized
institute to be known as the Disaster Resilience for Eco-ef icient Agriculture
Management Institute (DREAM Institute) at Southern Leyte State University –
Hinunangan Campus. The proposed DREAM Institute shall consolidate and elevate
the functions of SWaRI, SHARE, and BioCon into one integrated institutional
framework, with SWaRI serving as the Foundation Room, SHARE as the Production
and Livelihood Room, and BioCon as the Shield and coordinating lead.

Under this framework, the Institute shall adopt the following key systems and
platforms: WADE (Wilderness Areas Diversity Enhancement) as the ecological
restoration framework institutionalizing the principle “One Man, One Tree Can Make
a Forest” through native tree stewardship, habitat restoration, ecological buffering,
and integration of Indigenous Knowledge Systems (IKS); the League of Wildlife
Conservancy (LWS) as the Institute’s social engine for volunteerism, stewardship,
citizen science, and community mobilization; Das-ay Link Platform as the nervous
system of the Institute, serving as the central data, validation, and decision-support
platform for baseline data from SWaRI, government institutions, validated and
veri iable IKS, and future operational outputs from DREAM LABS, DREAM FARMS,
DREAM BIOSECURE, and DREAM WATERSHIELDS; ACCESS (Adaptive Climate Change
Education Support Services) as the trend-based adaptive learning and support stream
arising from Das-ay Link; and RADAR (Resilience Against Disaster and Agricultural
Risks) as the shock-based resilience and response stream likewise arising from Das-
ay Link.

The creation of the DREAM Institute through congressional legislation will provide
Southern Leyte State University – Hinunangan Campus with a stronger, more
coherent, and more functionally relevant platform for soil and water science,
biodiversity conservation, ecological restoration, climate adaptation, risk reduction,
resilient agriculture, community extension, and sustainability governance in
Southern Leyte and the broader region.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                               AN ACT
ESTABLISHING AT SOUTHERN LEYTE STATE UNIVERSITY – HINUNANGAN

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “DREAM Institute Act of
2026.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote ecological integrity, climate resilience, scienti ic and technological
advancement, biodiversity conservation, soil and water security, sustainable
agricultural development, disaster preparedness, community participation, and
culturally responsive innovation as foundations of national and local development.

Towards this end, the State shall support the establishment of specialized institutions
that integrate research, extension, education, invocation, data systems, and
community-based action to address the interconnected challenges of environmental
degradation, biodiversity loss, agricultural vulnerability, climate change, and disaster
risk.




                                                                           3|Page
SECTION 3. Creation of the DREAM Institute. — There is hereby established at
Southern Leyte State University – Hinunangan Campus a highly-specialized research
institute to be known as the Disaster Resilience in Eco-ef icient Agriculture
Management Institute (DREAM Institute), hereinafter referred to as the Institute.

The Institute shall serve as a premier and integrated center for research, innovation,
extension, training, policy support, data systems development, and ield-based action
on biodiversity conservation, soil and water systems, ecological restoration, climate
adaptation, disaster resilience, sustainable agriculture, and related development
concerns within the Province of Southern Leyte and such other areas as may be
appropriate.

SECTION 4. Mandate. —

   (a) To generate, integrate, and apply scienti ic, technical, local, and validated
       Indigenous Knowledge Systems toward resilient and eco-ef icient
       development;

   (b) To strengthen soil and water science, biodiversity conservation, ecological
       restoration, watershed and spring protection, climate adaptation, and disaster
       risk resilience;

   (c) To support sustainable and enterprise-oriented agriculture development
       consistent with the Tourism-Environment-Agriculture (TEA) Nexus and the
       Negosyohanong Panguma development context;

   (d) To provide extension, training, ield demonstration, policy support, and
       knowledge services to local government units, communities, schools, people’s
       organizations, farmers, isherfolk, partner agencies, and other stakeholders;
       and

   (e) To function as a specialized institutional platform linking research,
       restoration, production, sustainability, and decision intelligence for public
       bene it.

SECTION 5. Institutional Framework of the DREAM Institute. —

   (a) SWaRI — the Soil and Water Research Institute, which shall serve as the
       baseline creator and Foundation Room;


                                                                          4|Page
   (b) SHARE — the Smallholder Horticultural and Agroforestry Research &
       Extension Center, which shall serve as the action driver and Production and
       Livelihood Room; and

   (c) BioCon — the Biodiversity Conservation and Management Institute, which
       shall serve as the Shield, Protection and Sustainability Room, and the lead
       coordinating unit of the Institute.

The Technical Trinity shall constitute the core operational structure of the Institute.

SECTION 6. Functions of SWaRI. —

   (a) Generate baseline data on soil health, land capability, hydrology, water quality,
       watershed systems, spring systems, and related environmental conditions;

   (b) Conduct soil, water, and environmental diagnostics, assessment, and
       monitoring;

   (c) Support research on climate, land, water and watershed resilience;

   (d) Provide scienti ic inputs for restoration, planning, climate adaptation, and
       disaster resilience; and

   (e) Perform such other related functions as may be necessary to carry out the
       purposed of this Act.

SECTION 7. Functions of SHARE. —

   (a) Translate research and technical indings into sustainable agricultural
       production, agroforestry, horticulture, propagation, and livelihood systems;

   (b) Promote climate-responsive, smallholder-oriented, and enterprise-linked
       production models;

   (c) Develop and maintain demonstration farms, nurseries,                  ield-based
       propagation systems, and extension support mechanisms;

   (d) Strengthen agricultural innovation and value-chain development under a
       Negosyohanong Panguma framework; and


                                                                            5|Page
   (e) Perform such other related functions as may be necessary to carry out the
       purposes of this Act.

SECTION 8. Functions of BioCon. —

   (a) Lead biodiversity inventory and monitoring of lora, fauna, aquatic
       ecosystems, wetlands, caves, springs, watersheds, and related ecological
       systems;

   (b) Coordinate habitat and watershed conservation, ecological buffering, living
       collections, seed and seedling conservation, and biodiversity-supportive iled
       laboratories;

   (c) Oversee and coordinate ecological restoration and sustainability measures
       under the Institute;

   (d) Integrate biodiversity protection and ecosystem-based resilience principles
       across the Institute’s programs and operations; and

   (e) Coordinate the implementation of WADE, ACCESS, and RADAR under this Act.

SECTION 9. WADE Framework. —

   (a) Guide the rehabilitation, restoration, enhancement, and protection of
       degraded and vulnerable ecosystems, including forests, watersheds, wetlands,
       riparian areas, springs, caves, and biodiversity corridors;

   (b) Institutionalize the principle “One Man, One Tree Can Make a Forest” through
       science-based and long-term native tree stewardship, assisted natural
       regeneration, habitat enhancement, and ecological buffering;

   (c) Prioritize indigenous and site-appropriate native species, survival monitoring,
       maintenance, and ecological functionality over one-time planting activities;
       and

   (d) Integrate Indigenous Knowledge Systems (IKS), local ecological memory, and
       community-based stewardship practices in restoration planning, species
       selection, monitoring, and long-term management.



                                                                          6|Page
SECTION 10. Integration of Indigenous Knowledge Systems. — The DREAM
Institute shall adopt a science-based and culturally responsive approach in the
conduct of its functions. To this end, the Institute shall recognize, document, validate,
and where appropriate integrate veri iable Indigenous Knowledgeable Systems (IKS),
local ecological knowledge, and traditional stewardship practices relevant to soil and
water systems, biodiversity conservation, ecological restoration, production
resilience, and disaster adaptation.

Validation protocols for IKS shall be developed to ensure scienti ic integrity, cultural
sensitivity, and community consent.

SECTION 11. League of Wildlife Conservancy. — The League of Wildlife
Conservancy (LWS) is hereby recognized as the social engine of the DREAM Institute.

The LWS shall serve as the Institute’s organized stewardship, volunteer, advocacy, and
participatory action mechanism and shall:

   (a) Mobilize students, faculty, local communities, youth groups, partner
       institutions, and environmental advocates;

   (b) Support biodiversity conservation, ecological restoration, habitat stewardship,
       and environmental awareness programs;

   (c) Participate in citizen science, monitoring, ield education, and restoration
       support activities; and

   (d) Promote shared responsibility and social ownership of the Institute’s
       ecological and resilience goals.

SECTION 12. Das-ay Link Platform. — The Institute shall establish and maintain the
Das-ay Link Platform as its central data, validation, knowledge integration, and
decision-support platform, hereinafter referred to as the Platform.

The Platform shall serve as the nervous system of the Institute and shall:

   (a) Receive, store, organize, process, and update baseline data generated by
       SWaRI;




                                                                             7|Page
   (b) Integrate relevant data from government institutions, partner agencies,
       validated Indigenous Knowledge Systems, and local ecological observations
       subject to proper veri ication;

   (c) Serve as the institutional database, monitoring backbone, and knowledge
       repository of the Institute;

   (d) Be enhanced over time through the outputs and datasets generated by DREAM
       LABS, DREAM FARMS, DREAM BIOSECURE, and DREAM WATERSHIELDS;

   (e) Generate analytical outputs to support adaptation, resilience, restoration,
       extension, planning, and policy decisions; and

   (f) Perform such other related functions as may be necessary to support the
       operation of the Institute.

SECTION 13. Operational Platforms Under Das-ay Link. — To progressively
strengthen the Institute’s data, ield, and applied operational capacities, the following
platforms may be established and developed under the DREAM Institute:

   (a) DREAM LABS, for laboratory, diagnostics, experimentation, tissue culture,
       validation, and related scienti ic work;

   (b) DREAM FARMS, for adaptive agriculture, agroforestry, demonstration,
       smallholder innovation, and livelihood-linked iled application;

   (c) DREAM BIOSECURE, for environmental, agricultural, ecological, and
       biosecurity observation, monitoring, and response support; and

   (d) DREAM WATERSHIELDS, for watershed, spring,                  riparian, wetland,
       hydrological, and ecological buffering initiatives.

The outputs of these platforms shall be integrated into Das-ay Link as they become
available.

SECTION 14. ACCESS AND RADAR. — The analytical and decision-support outputs
of Das-ay Link shall include the following streams:

   (a) ACCESS (Adaptive Climate Change Education Support Services), which shall
       serve as the Institute’s trend-based adaptive learning, education, and support

                                                                            8|Page
      stream, focusing on long-term changes, patterns, emerging needs, and
      evidence-based learning for climate adaptation and sustainability; and

   (b) RADAR (Resilience Against Disaster and Agricultural Risks), which shall serve
       as the Institute’s shock-based resilience, preparedness, and response stream,
       focusing on disturbances, risks, vulnerabilities, hazard signals, and timely
       decision support for disaster and agricultural resilience.

      ACCESS and RADAR shall be used to guide institute operations, extension
      activities, restoration planning, stakeholder engagement, and policy support.

SECTION 15. Powers and Functions of the DREAM Institute. —

   (a) Formulate and implement research, extension, training, restoration, data. And
       innovation programs aligned with its mandate;

   (b) Undertake scienti ic studies,      ield assessments, laboratory analyses,
       monitoring, and evaluations;

   (c) Develop and maintain databases, ield laboratories, demonstration areas,
       living collections, nurseries, and related facilities;

   (d) Provide technical assistance, advisory services, capacity building, and policy
       support to public and private stakeholders;

   (e) Enter into partnerships, linkages, memoranda of agreement, and collaborative
       undertakings with government agencies, local government units, state
       universities and colleges, civil society organizations, people’s organizations,
       Indigenous Peoples, private entities, and international institutions, subject to
       existing laws, rules, and regulations;

   (f) Receive grants, donations, endowments, bequests, and other lawful assistance
       from local and foreign sources, subject to applicable laws, auditing, accounting,
       and government regulations;

   (g) Produce and disseminate publications, policy papers, extension materials, and
       knowledge products;

   (h) Establish systems for monitoring, validation, and quality assurance of data,
       research outputs, and ield operations; and

   (i) Exercise such other powers as may be necessary and incidental to effectively
       carry out the objectives of this Act.

SECTION 16. Organizational Structure. — The DREAM Institute shall be headed by
an Executive Director, who shall be appointed in accordance with existing laws, rules,
and university policies, and shall be supported by such deputy directors, division
heads, technical personnel, administrative staff, and other employees as may be
necessary for the effective operation of the Institute.

The organizational structure of the Institute shall include, but not limited to:

   (a) An Executive and Shared Services Of ice;
   (b) SWaRI as the Foundation Room;
   (c) SHARE as the Production and Livelihood Room;
   (d) BioCon as the Shield and lead coordinating unit; and
   (e) An Extension, TEA Nexus, and Community Interface unit.

SECTION 18. Personnel Absorption and Transition. — Quali ied existing
personnel, staff, and workers from the current research centers and extension units
of Southern Leyte State University – Hinunangan Campus may be absorbed,
reclassi ied, redesignated, or integrated into the DREAM Institute, subject to
quali ication standards, staf ing needs, and applicable civil service and budgeting
laws, rules, and regulations.




                                                                       11 | P a g e
No incumbent personnel shall be removed solely by reason of the reorganization
under this Act, except for lawful cause and due process.

SECTION 19. Extension, Training, and Community Engagement. — The Institute
shall actively develop and implement extension, training, technical assistance, and
community engagement programs in support of farmers, isherfolk, youth, women,
local government units, people’s organizations, Indigenous cultural communities,
tourism stakeholders, academic institutions, and other sectors relevant to the TEA
Nexus and the Institute’s mandate.

SECTION 20. Research, Data, and Knowledge Governance. — The Institute shall
develop and maintain appropriate policies and protocols on data governance,
validation, ethical use, knowledge management, research quality assurance,
environmental and cultural safeguards, intellectual property where applicable, and
access or sharing of information consistent with existing laws and regulations.

Special protocols shall be established for the documentation and use of Indigenous
Knowledge Systems to ensure respect for customary rights, community participation,
informed consent, and cultural integrity.

SECTION 21. Coordination with National and Local Government Agencies. — The
Institute shall coordinate with the Department of Agriculture, Department of
Environment and Natural Resources, Department of Science and Technology, Climate
Change Commission, National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council,
Department of Tourism, National Commission on Indigenous Peoples, local
government units, and other relevant agencies and institutions in the implementation
of this Act.

SECTION 22. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
Southern Leyte State University to the extent available. Thereafter, such sums as may
be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.

The initial annual operating requirement of the Institute shall include appropriate
Personnel Services, Capital Outlay, and a realistic Maintenance and Other Operating
Expenses (MOOE) level suf icient to support laboratory operations, ield monitoring,
restoration work, extension activities, data systems, training, stakeholder
engagement, and other functions of the Institute.

SECTION 23. Initial MOOE Planning Requirement. — For planning and budget
preparation purposes, the Institute shall develop an initial annual MOOE program
adequate for its operational needs, including but not limited to:

   (a) Laboratory reagents, chemicals, supplies, ield kits, and protective equipment;
   (b) Field research, biodiversity monitoring, watershed and restoration
       operations;
   (c) Travel, transport, fuel, and ield logistics;
   (d) Nursery, seed bank, restoration, ecological buffering, and demonstration
       operations;
   (e) Training, extension, consultations, stakeholder engagement, and information
       services;
   (f) Utilities, communications, internet, database systems, software, and digital
       services;
   (g) Repairs and maintenance of laboratories, ield equipment, nursery facilities,
       and support infrastructure;
   (h) Publications, documentation, knowledge products, and policy outputs; and
   (i) Contract of service, job order, or temporary technical support, subject to
       applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

SECTION 24. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred twenty
(120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Board of Regents of Southern Leyte
State University, in coordination with the appropriate government agencies, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of
this Act.

SECTION 25. Congressional Oversight and Reporting. — The DREAM Institute
shall submit an annual report to the Board of Regents of Southern Leyte State
University and furnish copies to the appropriate committees of the House of
Representatives and the Senate, containing its programs, accomplishments, staf ing,
budget utilization, partnerships, and recommendations for policy support.

SECTION 26. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

SECTION 27. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, memorandum circulars, and other issuances or parts thereof
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modi ied accordingly.
