                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Sa araw-araw, milyon-milyong mga Pilipino ang nililisan ang kanilang
tahanan bago magbukang-liwayway, iniiwan ang kanilang pamilya at kadalasan
maging ang ating bansa upang mamasukan at mag-alaga ng hindi nila anak at ng
ibang pamilya.

       Likas na mapagmahal ang mga Pilipino. Madali nating makapalagayan ng
loob ang mga taong nakasasalamuha natin, lalo na kung palagi natin silang kapiling.
Ngunit masakit para sa isang magulang na higit na maalagaan ang mga anak na
hindi niya kadugo kaysa sa sarili niyang anak.

        Filipino kasambahay prepare another family’s meals, comfort another
family’s children when they cry, and stay awake when someone else’s child
is sick, while their own children wait for them, often under the care of a
grandmother, an older sibling, or a neighbor, for months or even years at a
time. This is the quiet burden carried by the Filipino kasambahay: those who
give the best of their care to households that are not their own, often at the cost of the
time and presence they wish they could give their own children.

       Based on a recent joint survey of the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE) and the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), there are
approximately 1.4 million kasambahay in the Philippines1. Of this number,
the majority are on a live-out arrangement, leaving their own households
each day to care for another family's home and children. The same survey
found that 83 per cent of kasambahay have no social security coverage at all,
leaving them and their families exposed in the event of illness, disability, or

       For those who leave the country to work as domestic helpers, the
burden is even heavier. The PSA reported that the number of overseas
Filipino workers (OFWs) rose to 2.19 million in 2024, from 2.16 million in
2023, with women comprising 57.2 per cent, or 1.25 million, of this
workforce3. Approximately four (4) out of every ten (10) OFWs are in
elementary occupations, which include domestic and household work.
Moreover, Filipina household service workers are heavily concentrated in
Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, and Hong Kong4. Estimates
from the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) place domestic workers at
20 to 30 per cent of all OFWs deployed each year. These figures demonstrate
that household service work is one of the most common forms of overseas
employment for Filipino women.

       Hindi lamang ito basta mga numero… sa likod ng bawat datos na ito
ay ang mga anak na naiiwan ng kanilang mga magulang na
naghahanapbuhay. Researchers estimate that at least 1.5 million Filipino
children are growing up with one or both parents working overseas. A study
published in The Lancet Regional Health – Western Pacific linked prolonged
parental separation to loneliness, emotional strain, and health and
developmental concerns among left-behind children, even as the remittances
sent by their parents help support their education5.

       This is the irony this bill seeks to address: the very people entrusted
with the care of other people's children often cannot afford the time, presence,
or financial security to properly care for their own. Albeit existing laws such
as Republic Act (RA) No. 10361, otherwise known as “Batas Kasambahay
Act” and RA No. 8042, as amended by RA No. 10022 or the “Migrant Workers
and Overseas Filipinos Act”, protect the kasambahay as a worker, there
remains no law that directly assists them in their role as parents.

      The Kasambahay Parents Support Act aims to fill this gap. It provides
monthly cash assistance to Kasambahay Parents, whether locally employed
or deployed overseas, for the benefit of their qualified children left at home.
This measure also addresses the concern that 83 per cent of kasambahay
currently have no social security coverage.

   AN ACT PROVIDING CASH ASSISTANCE TO KASAMBAHAY
   PARENTS, INCLUDING OVERSEAS FILIPINO WORKER (OFW)
    HOUSEHOLD SERVICE WORKERS, WITH THE OPTION TO
  CONVERT SUCH ASSISTANCE INTO SOCIAL SECURITY SYSTEM
      (SSS) CONTRIBUTIONS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


       SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Kasambahay
Parents Support Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and strengthen the family as a basic autonomous social
institution and to promote the welfare of Filipino children, whether their
parents work within the Philippines or abroad. Consistent with Republic Act
(RA) No. 10361, or the “Batas Kasambahay,” and RA No. 8042, as amended
by RA No. 10022, or the “Migrant Workers and Overseas Filipinos Act,” the
State recognizes that Kasambahay who are parents, whether employed
locally or deployed overseas as household service workers, face a distinct
burden in balancing their duty to their employers’ households with their duty
to their own children left at home. Towards this end, the State shall extend
cash assistance to qualified Kasambahay parents to help ensure the welfare
of their minor children, shall allow such assistance to instead be applied
towards the beneficiary’s long-term social security protection at the
beneficiary’s option, and shall ensure that such assistance complements, and
does not duplicate, existing welfare programs for overseas Filipino workers.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:
       a) Kasambahay or domestic worker refers to any person engaged in
          domestic work within an employment relationship, as defined
          under RA No. 10361;
       b) OFW Kasambahay refers to a Filipino domestic worker or household
          service worker deployed for overseas employment under a
                                                                                  3
          contract processed and verified by the Department of Migrant
          Workers (DMW);
      c) Kasambahay Parent refers to a Kasambahay or an OFW Kasambahay
          who has one or more Qualified Children, regardless of civil status;
      d) Qualified Child refers to a child of a Kasambahay Parent who is below
         eighteen (18) years of age and who is left in the child’s home in the
         Philippines, or in the care of another person other than the
         Kasambahay Parent, for the greater part of the day or, in the case
         of a live-in Kasambahay or an OFW Kasambahay, for the duration
         of the Kasambahay Parent’s live-in employment or overseas
         deployment;
      e) Local Guardian refers to the relative or other person residing in the
         Philippines whom an OFW Kasambahay has designated, in a
         manner prescribed by the Department of Social Welfare and
         Development (DSWD), as the person in whose care the Qualified
         Child is left, and through whom the benefits under this Act shall be
         coursed on behalf of the Qualified Child;
      f) Cash Assistance refers to the monthly financial assistance granted
          under this Act to a qualified Kasambahay Parent; and
      g) SSS Contribution Option refers to the option of a qualified
         Kasambahay Parent to have the amount of the Cash Assistance
         applied instead as a contribution to their mandatory coverage
         under the Social Security System (SSS), in accordance with Section
         6 hereof.

    SEC. 4. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to: (a) all Kasambahay, regardless
of civil status, who are employed under a valid employment arrangement
recognized under RA No. 10361; and (b) all OFW Kasambahay, regardless of
civil status, who are deployed under a valid overseas employment contract
processed and verified by the DMW, in either case who have at least one (1)
Qualified Child left at home while the Kasambahay Parent is at work:
Provided, That where both parents of a Qualified Child are themselves
Kasambahay Parents, whether one or both are deployed overseas, only one
(1) of them shall be qualified to avail of the Cash Assistance or SSS
Contribution Option under this Act with respect to that child: Provided,
further, That a Kasambahay Parent may avail of the benefits under this Act for
not more than three (3) Qualified Children at any given time.

       SEC. 5. Cash Assistance. — Cash assistance equivalent to One
Thousand Pesos (Php1,000.00) per month shall be given to Kasambahay
Parents who are covered under this Act, for every Qualified Child, subject to
the limits set in Section 4 hereof.

       In the case of an OFW Kasambahay, the Cash Assistance shall be
released to the Local Guardian for the benefit of the Qualified Child, in a
manner to be prescribed by the DSWD, without prejudice to the OFW
Kasambahay's right to exercise the option under Section 6.

                                                                             4
       To account for inflation and align the assistance with legislated wage
increases, the amount of cash assistance shall be reviewed by Congress every
three (3) years, in consultation with the DSWD, the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE), and the DMW.

    SEC. 6. Option to Convert Assistance into SSS Contribution. - A qualified
Kasambahay Parent shall have the option to either: (a) receive the full amount
of the Cash Assistance under Section 5; or (b) have the equivalent amount
applied as an additional contribution to their mandatory coverage under the
Social Security System (SSS), whether such mandatory coverage arises under
the Batas Kasambahay for a locally-employed Kasambahay, or under RA No.
11199, otherwise known as the “Social Security Act of 2018,” for an OFW
Kasambahay, to be remitted directly by the DSWD to the SSS on behalf of the
beneficiary.
    The choice between the two options under this Section shall be made by
the Kasambahay Parent upon registration as a beneficiary, and may be
changed once every twelve (12) months through a written request filed with
the DSWD, the concerned local Social Welfare and Development Office
(LSWDO), or, in the case of an OFW Kasambahay, through the Migrant
Workers Office (MWO) or Philippine Embassy or Consulate having
jurisdiction over the OFW Kasambahay's place of deployment.

   SEC. 7. Coordination with the Department of Migrant Workers and the
Overseas Workers Welfare Administration. - The Cash Assistance and SSS
Contribution Option under this Act are granted on account of the welfare of
the Qualified Child, and are separate and distinct from the member-directed
benefits provided by the Overseas Workers Welfare Administration
(OWWA) to OFWs under existing law. Availment of the benefits under this
Act shall not diminish, and shall not be a ground for the denial of, any benefit
to which an OFW Kasambahay or their dependents are otherwise entitled
under OWWA membership or any other program for OFWs.
    The DSWD shall enter into a data-sharing agreement with the DMW and
OWWA for purposes of verifying the deployment status and OWWA
membership of an OFW Kasambahay applying for benefits under this Act,
and to ensure that no Qualified Child is the subject of duplicate availment of
any substantially similar cash or in-kind benefit for the same purpose under
this Act and under any program administered by the DMW or OWWA.

   SEC. 8. Age Limit and Termination of Benefit. - The Cash Assistance or
SSS Contribution Option granted under this Act shall be extended only for as
long as the Qualified Child concerned is below eighteen (18) years of age.
Upon the Qualified Child reaching eighteen (18) years of age, the
corresponding Cash Assistance or SSS Contribution Option for that child
shall automatically cease, without prejudice to the continuation of benefits for
any other Qualified Child who remains eligible under this Act.

   SEC. 9. Conditionalities. - The Cash Assistance or SSS Contribution
Option shall be released by the DSWD, with the support of the concerned
local government units (LGUs) and, for OFW Kasambahay, the concerned
                                                                              5
MWO or Philippine Embassy or Consulate, subject to the following
conditions:
       a) The Qualified Child or Qualified Children is/are enrolled in school,
          whether public or private, with at least 85 percent ( 85 %)
          attendance, unless below school age;
       b) The Kasambahay Parent remains employed as a Kasambahay under
          a valid employment arrangement recognized under RA No. 10361,
          or, in the case of an OFW Kasambahay, remains deployed under a
          valid overseas employment contract processed and verified by the
          DMW;
       c) The Kasambahay Parent submits, at least once a year, proof of the
          child’s living arrangement and caregiving arrangement while the
          Kasambahay Parent is at work, in a manner to be prescribed by the
          DSWD;
       d) Where both parents of a Qualified Child are Kasambahay Parents,
          the parents shall jointly execute a written declaration identifying
          which of them shall avail of the benefits under this Act with respect
          to that child, in a manner to be prescribed by the DSWD; and
       e)     An OFW Kasambahay shall submit the name and proof of
            relationship of the Local Guardian designated to receive the Cash
            Assistance on behalf of the Qualified Child, and shall notify the
            DSWD of any change in such designation.
       SEC. 10. Non-Duplication of Benefits Under Other Social Protection
Programs. — A Qualified Child who is already a listed beneficiary of the
Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) or any other social protection,
cash transfer, or subsidy program administered by the national government
or any LGU granting substantially similar cash or in-kind assistance for the
same purpose shall not be qualified to receive the Cash Assistance or SSS
Contribution Option under this Act, unless such other program's cash grant
has been suspended, terminated, or the household has been delisted
therefrom.

       The DSWD shall cross-match the registry of applicants under this Act
against the Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS) and the 4Ps
beneficiary registry, and against the registries of other national government
social protection programs, prior to approval of any application, and on at
least an annual basis thereafter, to prevent duplicate availment.

       Where a Qualified Child is found to be a beneficiary of another
program under this Section after the Kasambahay Parent has already been
receiving benefits under this Act, the DSWD shall suspend further release of
the Cash Assistance or SSS Contribution Option for that child until such time
as the duplication is resolved, without prejudice to the recovery of any benefit
shown to have been fraudulently obtained.

  SEC. 11. Implementing Agency. - The DSWD, in coordination with the
DOLE, the SSS, the DMW, OWWA, and the concerned local government
                                                                              6
(LGUs), shall implement the provisions of this Act. The DSWD shall maintain
a registry of Kasambahay Parent beneficiaries, drawing from the registry of
Kasambahay maintained under Batas Kasambay and, for OFW Kasambahay,
from the deployment and membership records of the DMW and OWWA, and
shall put in place a mechanism to prevent duplicate availment under Section
4 where both parents of a Qualified Child are Kasambahay Parents.

    SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). - Within ninety (90)
days from the approval of this Act, the Secretary of the DSWD, in consultation
with the Secretary of Labor and Employment, the Secretary of Migrant
Workers, and the President and Chief Executive Officer of the SSS, in
consultation with stakeholders, shall promulgate the IRR necessary for the
effective implementation of this Act. This includes the targeting, screening,
and registration of beneficiaries, the designation and verification of Local
Guardians, and the procedure for exercising the option under Section 6 of this
Act.

    SEC. 13. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the appropriations of the DSWD, DOLE,
DMW, and LGUs. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the General
Appropriations Act (GAA).

   SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders and
proclamations, rules and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this
Act, are hereby amended or modified accordingly.

    SEC. 15. Separability Clause. -If any provision of this Act or the
application of such provision to any instrumentalities or entities or
circumstances is held invalid or unconstitutional for any reason or reasons,
the remainder of this Act or the application of such other provisions shall not
be affected thereby.

   SEC. 16. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
