13                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE
14
15   The power to tax is indispensable to government, but taxation must remain fair,
16   progressive, and responsive to the actual capacity of citizens and enterprises to pay.
17   Government requires revenue to provide public services, build infrastructure,
18   maintain peace and order, educate the young, protect public health, and support those
19   who need assistance. At the same time, every peso collected in taxes is a peso first
20   earned by a Filipino worker, professional, entrepreneur, or business.
21
22   Tax policy must therefore strike a sensible balance. Government should collect what
23   is reasonably necessary to perform its functions while allowing Filipinos to retain a
24   greater share of the value of their work.
25
26   The individual income tax system presently exempts the first Two Hundred Fifty
27   Thousand Pesos (₱250,000.00) of taxable income. While this represented substantial
28   relief when the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion Act was enacted, the value
29   of that exemption has steadily been eroded by inflation. Food, electricity,
30   transportation, housing, education, healthcare, and other necessities cost
31   considerably more today. A worker may receive salary adjustments merely to keep
32   pace with these increases and yet become liable for greater income taxes even though
33   his or her real purchasing power has barely improved.
34
35   This phenomenon, commonly referred to as bracket creep, effectively allows inflation
36   to increase the tax burden without Congress imposing a new tax. A tax system intended
37   to be progressive should not treat a nominal increase in income as an increase in
38   economic capacity when that increase merely compensates for a higher cost of living.
39
40   This measure therefore raises the annual tax-exempt income threshold to Four
41   Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱400,000.00), recalibrates the succeeding income
42   brackets, and reduces the applicable marginal income tax rates. The lowest taxable
43   rate is reduced to eight percent (8%), while the succeeding rates are likewise lowered.
44   The highest marginal rate is reduced to thirty percent (30%). The purpose is
45   straightforward: Filipino taxpayers should be allowed to retain more of the income
46   they earn.




                                                 1
 1   The measure also addresses the recurring erosion of tax relief by providing for
 2   automatic adjustment of the exemption threshold and income brackets based on
 3   inflation. Once Congress determines the amount of income that should reasonably
 4   remain untaxed, inflation should not silently diminish that protection year after year.
 5   Self-employed individuals, professionals, freelancers, and sole proprietors deserve
 6   comparable relief. The optional eight percent (8%) income tax applicable to qualified
 7   self-employed individuals and professionals is therefore reduced to six percent (6%),
 8   with the first Four Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱400,000.00) likewise excluded from
 9   its application where allowed under the National Internal Revenue Code.
10
11   The same principle applies to enterprises. Micro and small businesses create
12   employment, sustain communities, and provide much of the economic activity outside
13   the country's largest corporations. Yet the minimum corporate income tax may require
14   a corporation to pay tax based on gross income even when its actual taxable profit is
15   minimal. A minimum tax designed as an anti-avoidance safeguard should not become
16   an additional burden upon legitimate small enterprises struggling to remain viable.
17
18   Accordingly, qualified small domestic corporations are exempted from the minimum
19   corporate income tax. The preferential regular corporate income tax applicable to
20   qualified small domestic corporations is likewise reduced from twenty percent (20%)
21   to fifteen percent (15%), giving these enterprises additional capital for wages,
22   expansion, equipment, and employment.
23
24   This bill also recognizes that a tax system must provide a practical means for taxpayers
25   with unresolved liabilities to return to compliance. Outstanding tax liabilities that
26   remain disputed, uncollected, or buried under accumulated interest and penalties do
27   not necessarily translate into actual government revenue. A properly structured tax
28   amnesty can convert these dormant liabilities into immediate collections, reduce
29   litigation and administrative costs, improve taxpayer records, and bring taxpayers
30   back into the formal system.
31
32   A one-time general tax amnesty is therefore provided for qualified national internal
33   revenue tax liabilities for taxable year 2025 and prior years. The rates are deliberately
34   reasonable to encourage genuine participation, while safeguards exclude unlawfully
35   acquired wealth, taxes collected from third persons but deliberately not remitted to
36   government, money laundering, and other unlawful activities.
37
38   Separate and simplified mechanisms are provided for delinquent accounts, disputed
39   assessments, and estate taxes. Once a taxpayer has truthfully complied with the
40   requirements and fully paid the applicable amnesty tax, the Government must likewise
41   honor its side of the bargain. The amnesty must provide genuine finality rather than
42   merely opening another avenue for subsequent assessment or investigation.
43




                                                  2
 1   Ultimately, meaningful tax relief need not always take the form of another government
 2   subsidy or assistance program. There are circumstances when the simplest and most
 3   immediate form of economic relief is for government to collect less and allow citizens
 4   to keep more of what they have earned. Money retained by taxpayers supports
 5   household consumption, savings, investment, entrepreneurship, and economic
 6   activity without requiring an application, qualification process, appropriation, or
 7   administrative distribution mechanism.
 8
 9   The Sustaining Economic Relief through Broader Income-tax Savings and Yield
10   Opportunities (SERBISYO) Act seeks to place this principle at the center of tax policy:
11   raise necessary revenues fairly, protect the value of work, give small enterprises room
12   to grow, and make compliance easier for taxpayers willing to fulfill their obligations.
13
14   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

30   SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Sustaining Economic Relief
31   through Broader Income-tax Savings and Yield Opportunities (SERBISYO) Act.”
32
33   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
34   maintain a fair, progressive, reasonable, and sustainable system of taxation that raises
35   revenues necessary for government while recognizing the right of taxpayers to retain
36   a reasonable and meaningful share of the income generated by their labor, profession,
37   enterprise, and investment.
38
39   The State recognizes that income retained by taxpayers contributes directly to
40   household welfare, consumption, savings, investment, entrepreneurship,
41   employment, and economic growth. Tax policy shall therefore protect the purchasing
42   power of Filipino workers and families and prevent inflation from increasing their
43   effective tax burden solely through nominal increases in income.
44

 1   The State further recognizes the indispensable contribution of micro and small
 2   enterprises to employment and national development. Taxation of such enterprises
 3   shall take into consideration their capacity to pay and shall not unnecessarily impair
 4   their capitalization, survival, expansion, or ability to generate employment.
 5
 6   Towards these ends, the State shall provide meaningful and continuing individual
 7   income tax relief, protect taxpayers against bracket creep caused by inflation, reduce
 8   the tax burden on qualified small enterprises, simplify tax compliance, and provide
 9   qualified taxpayers a reasonable one-time opportunity to settle prior national internal
10   revenue tax liabilities and return to full compliance.

22   SECTION 9. Coverage. — There is hereby authorized and granted a one-time General
23   Tax Amnesty covering all unpaid national internal revenue taxes for taxable year 2025
24   and prior years, whether or not a return was filed, and whether or not an assessment
25   has been issued, including:
26
27         (a) income tax;
28         (b) capital gains tax;
29         (c) donor's tax;
30         (d) value-added tax;
31         (e) percentage taxes;
32         (f) excise taxes;
33         (g) documentary stamp taxes; and
34         (h) other national internal revenue taxes collected by the Bureau of Internal
35         Revenue.
36
37   Estate taxes shall be governed by Title V of this Act. Delinquent accounts and
38   assessments shall be governed by Title IV.
39
40   SECTION 10. General Amnesty Tax. — A taxpayer availing of the General Tax Amnesty
41   shall file a General Tax Amnesty Return and may elect to pay:
42
43         (a) one percent (1%) of total assets as of December 31, 2025; or
44         (b) three percent (3%) of net worth as of December 31, 2025.
45
46   The taxpayer's election shall become irrevocable upon filing of the General Tax
47   Amnesty Return and payment of the corresponding amnesty tax.

15   SECTION 16. Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies. — Taxpayers with delinquent national
16   internal revenue tax liabilities for taxable year 2025 and prior years may avail of tax
17   amnesty by paying:
18
19          (a) twenty percent (20%) of the basic tax assessed, in cases of delinquent
20          accounts and assessments which have become final and executory;
21          (b) ten percent (10%) of the basic tax assessed, in tax cases subject of pending

23          Department of Justice, Court of Tax Appeals, or other court of competent

26          executory judgment has been rendered by a court.

28          compromise penalties, and other additions to tax shall be deemed fully waived,
29          and the tax liability covered by the payment shall be considered fully settled.
30

32   Upon valid availment of the amnesty under this Title, any pending administrative or
33   judicial proceeding relating solely to the tax liability covered by the amnesty shall,
34   upon proper manifestation by the taxpayer and proof of payment, be terminated
35   without further liability for the taxes and periods covered.

14         (a) reduce an exemption or income threshold established under this Act;

16         (c) narrow an income bracket established by this Act;
17         (d) shorten a period expressly granted to taxpayers;
18         (e) impose a substantive qualification for relief not authorized by this Act; or
19         (f) otherwise diminish a right, exemption, privilege, or relief expressly granted
20         herein.

27   SECTION 26. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
28   the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance, upon recommendation of the
29   Commissioner of Internal Revenue and after consultation with affected taxpayer and
30   business sectors, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its
31   implementation.
32
33   The failure to promulgate implementing rules and regulations within the period
34   prescribed shall not suspend the effectivity or implementation of the tax rates,
35   exemptions, thresholds, and other substantive rights expressly granted under this Act.

37   SECTION 27. Congressional Oversight and Revenue Report. — The Department of
38   Finance and Bureau of Internal Revenue shall submit to the appropriate committees
39   of the Senate and House of Representatives, not later than one (1) year after the
40   implementation of this Act and annually thereafter for three (3) years, a report
41   containing:

44         (b) the number of qualified small domestic corporations benefiting from the
45         corporate tax relief;
