                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


       The first days following the birth of a child are among the most critical for
both the newborn and the mother. This period demands not only medical attention
but also emotional support, shared responsibility, and the active presence of both
parents. Fathers play a vital role in helping mothers recover, managing household
responsibilities, and ensuring that the child receives proper care and attention during
this delicate stage of life.

       Extending paternity leave acknowledges that fathers are not merely providers
but equal partners in caregiving. Allowing them more time at home enables stronger
bonding with their child, meaningful support for the mother’s recovery, and the
creation of a healthier family environment. It reduces stress, promotes balanced
parenting, and strengthens relationships within the household.

       Across the world, many societies have already expanded paternity leave to
reflect modern values of family life and gender equality. Longer leave for fathers
encourages shared parenting responsibilities, supports child development, and
fosters stronger family ties. It also signals that we value fatherhood and recognize
the importance of both parents in raising children.

       This measure is not simply about granting additional leave days. It is about
supporting families, promoting equal caregiving, and ensuring that Filipino children
grow up in nurturing homes from the very beginning of life. By extending paternity
leave, we invest in stronger marriages, healthier households, and a workforce that
feels supported in balancing family and career. Ultimately, this is a step toward
building a society that values not only economic productivity but also the well-being
of families -- the very foundation of our nation.

      Hence, approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
FURTHER PROMOTING AND VALUING THE ROLE OF LEGITIMATE
HUSBANDS AND FATHERS DURING THE EARLY DAYS OF THEIR CHILD’S
BIRTH, AMENDING, FOR THIS PURPOSE, SECTION 2 OF REPUBLIC ACT
NO. 8187, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE PATERNITY LEAVE ACT OF 1993

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     Section 1. Section 2 of Republic Act No. 8187, otherwise known as the Paternity
Leave Act of 1993, is hereby amended to read as follows:


             Section 2. Notwithstanding any law, rules and regulations to the
             contrary, every married male employee in the private and public
             sectors shall be entitled to a paternity leave of [seven (7)] TWENTY
             (20) days with full pay [for the first four (4)] IN EVERY INSTANCE
             OF deliveriesY of the legitimate spouse with whom he is cohabiting
             WITH AN OPTION TO EXTEND FOR AN ADDITIONAL TEN (10)
             DAYS WITHOUT PAY. The male employee applying for paternity
             leave shall notify his employer of the pregnancy of his legitimate
             spouse and the expected date of such delivery.


             For purposes of this Act, delivery shall include childbirth, [or any]
             miscarriage, OR EMERGENCY TERMINATION OF PREGNANCY:
             PROVIDED, THAT IN CASE OF MISCARRIAGE OR EMERGENCY



                                         2
             TERMINATION OF PREGNANCY, A PATERNITY LEAVE OF TEN
             (10) DAYS WITH FULL PAY SHALL BE GRANTED.


     Sec. 2. Rule-Making Authority – The Secretary of Labor and Employment, the
Chairman of the Civil Service Commission, and the Secretary of Health shall, within
thirty (30) days from the effectivity of this Act, issue such rules and regulations
necessary for the proper implementation of the provisions thereof.


     Sec. 3. Repealing Clause – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations inconsistent with any provision of this Act are hereby repealed, modified,
superseded, or amended accordingly.


      Sec. 4. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act or the application of
such provision to any person or circumstance is held invalid for any reason, the
remainder of this Act or the application of such provision to the other persons or
circumstances shall not be affected thereby.


      Sec. 5. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following
its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) national
newspaper of general circulation, whichever comes first.
