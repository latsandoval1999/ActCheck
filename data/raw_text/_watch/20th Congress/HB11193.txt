                          AN ACT
      EXPANDING THE COVERAGE AND STRENGTHENING THE
ENFORCEMENT OF THE PROHIBITION AGAINST THE “NO PERMIT, NO
EXAM” POLICY IN ALL EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS, AMENDING FOR
THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11984, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
    “NO PERMIT, NO EXAM PROHIBITION ACT,” AND FOR OTHER
                         PURPOSES

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Section 1, Article XIV of the 1987 Constitution provides that "The
State shall protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at
all levels, and shall take appropriate steps to make such education accessible
to all." A student who is turned away from an examination hall because of
an unpaid balance has been denied that access at the very moment it
matters most.

       The country took an important first step with the enactment of
Republic Act No. 11984, the “No Permit, No Exam Prohibition Act,” on 11
March 2024. That law mandates public and private educational institutions
to allow a student to sit the scheduled periodic and final examinations
without a permit despite unpaid tuition and other school fees. Its passage
settled the principle. What remains is to make the remedy reach the
students who need it.

       Republic Act No. 11984, as presently written, protects only a
“Disadvantaged Student,” and conditions that protection on a certification
issued by a local or regional social welfare and development officer on the
ground of calamities, emergencies, force majeure, and other valid or
justifiable reasons.

      This bill amends Republic Act No. 11984 to close the gaps within RA
11984. It makes the right to sit an examination universal and
unconditional, so that no certification of any kind is required to claim it. It
prohibits the examination permit requirement outright. It extends coverage
to every technical-vocational program regardless of duration. It caps the
down payment that may be required upon enrollment at thirty percent (30%)
of total fees. It prohibits the public disclosure or posting of a student’s
unpaid account.

      It replaces the denial of re-enrollment with a structured payment
agreement. It guarantees the release of records and credentials where these
are needed for employment, for a licensure or civil service examination, or
for transfer. And it provides graduated administrative sanctions together
with a complaint mechanism that carries fixed periods for action, including
provisional relief allowing a student to sit the examination while a complaint
is pending.

      The legitimate interest of educational institutions in collecting what is
due them is fully preserved, and in one respect improved. Schools may still
require a promissory note or a structured payment agreement, still withhold
the release of grades, records, and credentials subject to the narrow
exceptions this Act provides, and still pursue every legal and administrative
remedy for collection. What they may no longer do is interrupt a student’s
education as a collection device.

      Students who are refused admission to their examinations are
exposed to public humiliation before their classmates and are placed at
markedly higher risk of dropping out altogether. The burden falls hardest on
those least able to bear it. This is a harm the State can end at no cost to the
public treasury and at no real cost to the schools.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
      EXPANDING THE COVERAGE AND STRENGTHENING THE
ENFORCEMENT OF THE PROHIBITION AGAINST THE “NO PERMIT, NO
EXAM” POLICY IN ALL EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS, AMENDING FOR
THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11984, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
    “NO PERMIT, NO EXAM PROHIBITION ACT,” AND FOR OTHER
                         PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Expanded
Anti-No Permit, No Exam Prohibition Act".

     SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - Section 2 of Republic Act No.
11984 is hereby amended to read as follows:

     “SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to protect
     and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels
     and to take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to
     all, regardless of economic status.

     “Towards this end, the State declares that the right of a student to
     take an examination shall not be made to depend upon the settlement
     of financial obligations to the educational institution, and that this
     right shall be enjoyed by every student without the need for any
     certification, declaration, or proof of indigency or disadvantaged
     status. No student shall be subjected to public disclosure,
     identification, segregation, or humiliation on account of an unpaid
     account.

     “The State likewise recognizes the legitimate right of educational
     institutions to collect the tuition and other school fees due them, and
     shall ensure that this right is exercised through remedies that do not
     interrupt or terminate the education of the learner.”
    SECTION 3. Coverage. - Section 3 of Republic Act No. 11984 is hereby
amended to read as follows:

     “SEC. 3. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all public and private
     educational institutions, including basic education institutions
     offering Kindergarten to Grade 12 under Republic Act No. 10533,
     higher education institutions, and technical-vocational institutions
     and other training providers registered with the Technical Education
     and Skills Development Authority, regardless of the length or duration
     of the program, course, or qualification offered.”

     SECTION 4. Right of Students to Take Examinations. - Section 4 of
Republic Act No. 11984 is hereby amended to read as follows:

     “SEC. 4. Right of Students to Take Examinations. - All students shall
     be allowed to take every scheduled examination, including
     preliminary, midterm, periodic, and final examinations, and any other
     test, assessment, or requirement forming part of the computation of
     grades, without the need to secure a permit or clearance and
     notwithstanding the existence of unpaid tuition and other school fees.

     “No certification, affidavit, declaration, or other proof of indigency or
     disadvantaged status shall be required as a condition for the exercise
     of the right granted under this Section.

     “An educational institution may require a student who has an unpaid
     account to execute a promissory note or a structured payment
     agreement under Section 4-B hereof; provided, that the refusal or
     failure of the student to execute the same shall not be a ground to
     deny the student admission to any examination.

     “Subject to Sections 4-A, 4-B, and 4-C hereof, an educational
     institution may withhold the release of grades, records, and
     credentials until the account is settled, and may pursue all legal and
     administrative remedies available to it for the collection of the
     amounts due.”

     SECTION 5. Prohibited Acts. - A new Section 4-A is hereby inserted in
Republic Act No. 11984 to read as follows:

     “SECTION. 4-A. Prohibited Acts. - It shall be unlawful for any
     educational institution, or for any of its officers, employees, or agents,
     to commit any of the following acts:

           “(a) Requiring any student to secure an examination permit,
           clearance, or similar document as a condition for taking any
           examination;
           “(b) Disallowing, excluding, or removing a student from any
           examination, or invalidating or refusing to record the result of
           any examination taken, on account of unpaid tuition and other
           school fees;
           “(c) Conditioning the exercise of the right under Section 4 upon
           the presentation of a certification of disadvantaged status or any
           equivalent document;
           “(d) Publicly disclosing, posting, announcing, reading aloud, or
           otherwise identifying a student as having an unpaid account,
           whether in a class, in any school facility, in any publication, or
           through any electronic, digital, or online means; or segregating,
           marking, or separately seating students on that basis;
           “(e) Requiring, upon enrollment, a down payment or first
           installment exceeding thirty percent (30%) of the total tuition
           and other school fees for the semester, term, or duration of the
           course or program;
           “(f) Denying admission or re-enrollment for the succeeding
           semester, term, or school year on the ground of an unpaid
           account, where the student has executed and is complying with
           a structured payment agreement under Section 4-B hereof; and
           “(g) Imposing interest, surcharges, or penalty charges upon an
           account for any period during which the student is complying
           with a structured payment agreement under Section 4-B
           hereof.”

     SECTION 6. Structured Payment Agreement. - A new Section 4-B is
hereby inserted in Republic Act No. 11984 to read as follows:

     “SEC. 4-B. Structured Payment Agreement. - An educational
     institution shall offer to any student with an unpaid account the
     option to execute a structured payment agreement providing for the
     settlement of the account in reasonable periodic installments.

     “For as long as the student is complying with the agreement, the
     student shall not be denied admission or re-enrollment on the ground
     of the account, shall not be charged interest, surcharges, or penalty
     charges thereon, and shall continue to enjoy all rights under this Act.

     “The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe the minimum
     terms of a structured payment agreement, including the maximum
     installment that may be required relative to the outstanding balance,
     the minimum period for settlement, and the cure period before
     default.”

      SECTION 7. Release of Records and Credentials. - A new Section 4-C
is hereby inserted in Republic Act No. 11984 to read as follows:

     “SEC. 4-C. Release of Records and Credentials. - Notwithstanding any
     unpaid account, an educational institution shall release the transcript
     of records, diploma, certificate of completion or graduation, certificate
     of training, or other academic record or credential of a student upon a
     showing that the same is required for any of the following purposes:

           “(a) Employment, or an application, offer, or requirement of
           employment;
           “(b) Admission to, or the taking of, any licensure, board, bar, or
           civil service examination;
           “(c) Transfer to another educational institution;
           “(d) Application for a scholarship, grant, or financial assistance;
           or
           “(e) Compliance with any requirement of law or of any
           government agency.

     “In no case shall an educational institution withhold the permanent
     record of a learner in basic education where the same is required for
     transfer to another school.

     “The release of any record or credential under this Section shall not
     extinguish, novate, or diminish the obligation of the student. The
     educational institution shall retain in full its right to collect the
     amounts due through all legal and administrative remedies available
     to it, and may require the execution of a structured payment
     agreement under Section 4-B prior to release.”

     SECTION 8. Sanctions. - Section 5 of Republic Act No. 11984 is
hereby amended to read as follows:

     “SEC. 5. Administrative Sanctions. - An educational institution that
     commits any of the acts prohibited under Section 4-A hereof shall,
     after notice and hearing, be subject to the following graduated
     administrative sanctions to be imposed by the Department of
     Education, the Commission on Higher Education, or the Technical
     Education and Skills Development Authority, as the case may be:

           “(a) For the first offense, a written reprimand, together with a
           directive to admit the affected student to the examination or to
           release the record or credential concerned within a non-
           extendible period, and to refund any amount collected in
           violation of this Act;
           “(b) For the second offense, a fine in the amount to be
           prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations, imposed
           for each affected student, in addition to the directives under the
           preceding paragraph; and
           “(c) For the third and every subsequent offense committed
           within a period of three (3) years, a fine in an increased amount
           to be prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations, and
           the suspension of the permit, recognition, or program
           registration covering the course or program concerned, or the
           non-renewal thereof.
     “No sanction under this Section shall be imposed or executed in a
     manner that interrupts the schooling of enrolled students. Where a
     permit, recognition, or program registration is suspended or not
     renewed, the agency concerned shall ensure the orderly transfer of
     affected students to other institutions without loss of academic credit.

     “The head, president, dean, director, or other responsible officer of the
     educational institution who ordered, authorized, or knowingly
     tolerated the commission of any prohibited act shall be personally
     accountable therefor, and in the case of a public educational
     institution, shall be liable under the applicable civil service laws,
     rules, and regulations.

     “Nothing in this Section shall preclude liability under any other law.”

      SECTION 9. Complaint Mechanism. - A new Section 5-A is hereby
inserted in Republic Act No. 11984 to read as follows:

     “SEC. 5-A. Complaint Mechanism. - A student, or a parent, guardian,
     duly recognized student organization, or accredited civil society
     organization acting on the student’s behalf, may file a complaint for
     any violation of this Act with the regional office of the Department of
     Education, the Commission on Higher Education, or the Technical
     Education and Skills Development Authority having jurisdiction over
     the educational institution concerned.

     “Complaints may be filed in person, in writing, or through a telephone
     hotline or online portal to be established for the purpose, and may be
     filed anonymously.

     “Within five (5) working days from receipt of a complaint, the agency
     concerned shall issue a provisional order directing the educational
     institution to admit the student to the examination or to release the
     record or credential concerned, pending resolution of the complaint.
     The complaint shall be resolved within thirty (30) days from receipt.

     “No student shall be subjected to reprisal, disciplinary action, denial
     of re-enrollment, or any form of retaliation for the filing of a complaint
     or for participating in any proceeding under this Act.”

     SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Section 6 of
Republic Act No. 11984 is hereby amended to read as follows:

     “SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90)
     days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Education, the
     Commission on Higher Education, and the Technical Education and
     Skills Development Authority shall jointly promulgate the rules and
     regulations necessary for the implementation of this Act, in
     coordination with the Department of Social Welfare and Development
      and in consultation with duly recognized national student
      organizations, teachers’ organizations, parent-teacher associations,
      associations of private educational institutions, and accredited civil
      society organizations working on the right to education.

      “The certification of disadvantaged status previously required under
      this Act shall no longer be a condition for the exercise of any right
      granted herein, and shall be retained only for the purpose of
      qualifying a student for such additional assistance as may be provided
      under other laws, programs, or issuances.”

       SECTION 11. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part thereof is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the other sections or provisions thereof not
affected thereby shall remain in force and effect.

      SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations and issuances or parts thereof which
are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 13. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days following its publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers
of general circulation.
