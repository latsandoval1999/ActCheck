                            AN ACT
     EXEMPTING MICRO AND SMALL ENTERPRISES FROM MINIMUM

  OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

Micro and small-sized enterprises (MSEs) are part of the backbone of our country’s
economy.1 They generate employment opportunities, encourage local growth and
development, and contribute to poverty reduction and the country’s gross domestic
product. In 2023, there were more than 1.2 million MSE establishments in the country,
making up over 99% of the total number of establishments. Moreover, these
enterprises generated over 5.4 million jobs, accounting for over 60% of the country’s
total employment.2

However, MSEs remain vulnerable to economic and environmental circumstances.
Difficulties in accessing affordable financing options and the burden of taxation often
push MSEs into precarious financial positions.

Under the current National Internal Revenue Code, domestic corporations, including
MSEs, are taxed at either (a) 20% or 25% of their taxable income (i.e. gross income
less allowable deductions); or (b) the minimum corporate income tax (MCIT) of 2% of
their gross income beginning with its fourth taxable year, whichever is higher. In
practice, MCIT creates an economic burden for struggling small businesses, because

Under this proposed measure, MSEs will be exempt from MCIT, thus easing their
financial burden. Therefore, if the MSE is struggling and has zero taxable income for
a certain year, it will no longer be subjected to corporate income tax. This measure
aims to provide the necessary relief to MSEs, help them get the most out of their
revenue, which they can use to improve their daily operations, hire more employees,
and expand their business, and ultimately achieve long-term stability.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

SEC. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance shall, upon the recommendation of the
Commissioner of Internal Revenue, promulgate the necessary rules and regulations
to implement the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 3. Separability Clause.- If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional,
the remainder hereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 4. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, regulations,
and other issuances or parts thereof which are contrary to or inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 5. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
