APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Millions of Filipino minimum wage earners continue to grapple with the
rising cost of basic goods, while micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs)
which account for 99.5% of all business establishments and employ the majority
of the Filipino workforce — struggle with thin margins and limited consumer
demand.

       This measure addresses both problems with a single intervention: a direct,
regular, and digitally delivered subsidy to minimum wage earners and their
families that can only be spent in registered local MSMEs. Unlike traditional cash
assistance programs that are one-time, leakage-prone, and often captured by
intermediaries, this bill guarantees that (1) assistance reaches families directly
through their digital wallets, (2) the benefit is spread throughout the year through
monthly disbursements and a daily spending limit, encouraging use for daily
essentials rather than lump-sum consumption, and (3) every peso of public
assistance circulates within local communities, stimulating neighborhood
economies rather than large retail chains.

       This measure is designed to complement, not duplicate, existing social
protection programs. Families already receiving assistance under the Pantawid
Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) or its successor conditional cash transfer
program remain covered by that program and are excluded from this Act,
ensuring that public funds reach a distinct population: minimum wage earners
who work full-time yet remain financially strained, and who are typically
ineligible for 4Ps but still without a dedicated income supplement.

       The measure likewise accelerates financial inclusion and digitalization
among both households and small businesses, consistent with the Bangko Sentral
ng Pilipinas' Digital Payments Transformation Roadmap.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Pambansang
Agapay sa Pamilya at Maliliit na Negosyo (PAMANA) Act” or the “Family
Subsidy Through Local Enterprises Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote a just
and dynamic social order that will ensure the prosperity and independence of the
nation and free the people from poverty through policies that provide adequate
social services and an improved quality of life for all. The State recognizes that
minimum wage earners, while gainfully employed, continue to face financial
strain from the rising cost of living and are largely excluded from existing
conditional cash transfer programs intended for indigent and unemployed
households. Toward this end, the State shall provide direct financial assistance to
minimum wage earners and their families, delivered efficiently through digital
means, and channeled toward local micro, small, and medium enterprises to
stimulate community-level economic activity.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

(a) Minimum Wage Earner refers to a worker in the private sector paid the
statutory minimum wage rate fixed by the appropriate Regional Tripartite Wages
and Productivity Board (RTWPB) pursuant to Republic Act No. 6727, as
amended, or a worker in the public sector receiving compensation not exceeding
the statutory minimum wage, consistent with the definition under Republic Act
No. 9504;

(b) Qualified Family refers to a family whose Head of Family is a Minimum
Wage Earner, duly registered under the Philippine Identification System
(PhilSys), and which is not a current household-beneficiary of the Pantawid
Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) or its successor conditional cash transfer
program;

(c) Digital Wallet refers to an electronic money account issued by an electronic
money issuer (EMI) duly licensed and regulated by the Bangko Sentral ng
Pilipinas (BSP);

(d) Local MSME refers to a micro, small, or medium enterprise as defined under
Republic Act No. 9501, otherwise known as the “Magna Carta for Micro, Small
and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs),” registered with the Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI) or the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), holding a
                                                                                 2
valid business permit from the city or municipality where it operates, and
accredited as a program merchant under this Act;

(e) Program Subsidy refers to the financial assistance granted to Qualified
Families under this Act; and

(f) Head of Family refers to the Minimum Wage Earner in whose name the digital
wallet shall be registered, or, in case of a family with more than one Minimum
Wage Earner, the member so designated by the family for purposes of this Act.

SEC. 4. The PAMANA Program. — There is hereby established the Pambansang
Agapay sa Pamilya at Maliliit na Negosyo (PAMANA) Program, to be
administered by the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD),
in coordination with the DTI, the BSP, the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), and the PSA.

SEC. 5. Amount and Manner of Disbursement. — Every Qualified Family shall
receive an annual Program Subsidy of Twelve Thousand Pesos (P12,000.00),
disbursed in equal monthly tranches of One Thousand Pesos (P1,000.00), credited
directly to the digital wallet of the Head of Family not later than the fifth (5th)
day of each month.

SEC. 6. Daily Spending Limit. — Subsidy funds may be spent up to a maximum
of Two Hundred Pesos (P200.00) per day per family wallet. Unspent daily
allowances shall remain in the wallet and may be spent on succeeding days,
subject to the same daily limit. Subsidy funds shall not be convertible to cash,
transferable to other accounts, or usable for online gambling, alcohol, tobacco, or
other goods and services excluded under the implementing rules and regulations
of this Act.



                                                                                 3
SEC. 7. Exclusive Use in Local MSMEs. — The Program Subsidy may be spent
only through accredited Local MSMEs. The DTI, in coordination with local
government units (LGUs), shall establish a simplified accreditation and
onboarding system for MSME merchants, including sari-sari stores, public
market vendors, carinderias, and other community-based enterprises, and shall
ensure that accredited merchants are equipped to accept digital wallet payments
at no cost to the merchant. Settlement of merchant payments shall be made within
twenty-four (24) hours from the transaction.

SEC. 8. Digital Wallet Onboarding and Financial Inclusion. — The DSWD,
the BSP, and the DICT shall ensure that every Head of Family is provided a free,
PhilSys-linked digital wallet. For beneficiaries in areas with limited connectivity
or without smartphones, the implementing agencies shall provide alternative
access mechanisms, including QR-enabled cash cards or offline payment
solutions, at no cost to the beneficiary.

SEC. 9. Identification of Beneficiaries. — The Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE), in coordination with the Social Security System (SSS), the
Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), and employers, shall maintain a registry of
Minimum Wage Earners based on payroll, SSS contribution, and other
employment records, subject to Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the
“Data Privacy Act of 2012.”

      The DSWD shall cross-match the DOLE registry against the Listahanan,
the 4Ps registry, or their successor registries, and shall exclude from the list of
Qualified Families any household that is a current 4Ps beneficiary. Self-employed
and informal sector workers earning at or below the applicable regional minimum
wage may apply for inclusion through their city or municipal social welfare and
development office, subject to verification.


                                                                                  4
         The list of Qualified Families shall be updated quarterly to reflect changes
in employment status, regional wage orders, and 4Ps beneficiary status. Any
person aggrieved by exclusion from the list, or by removal therefrom, may file an
appeal with the DSWD under the procedures provided in the implementing rules
and regulations of this Act.

SEC. 10. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. — The following acts shall be
punishable by a fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) nor more
than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00), or imprisonment of not less
than six (6) months nor more than six (6) years, or both, at the discretion of the
court:

(a) Fraudulent registration or misrepresentation to qualify for the Program
Subsidy;

(b) Conversion of subsidy funds to cash, or any scheme to circumvent the
merchant restriction or daily spending limit, whether by a beneficiary, merchant,
or third party;

(c) Diversion, retention, or misuse of program funds by any public official or
employee, without prejudice to prosecution under Republic Act No. 3019,
otherwise known as the “Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act,” and other
applicable laws; and

(d) Imposition by any merchant of a surcharge or price differential on subsidy
transactions.

SEC. 11. Transparency and Oversight. — The DSWD shall maintain a publicly
accessible dashboard reporting, on a monthly basis, aggregate disbursements, the
number of beneficiary families, and accredited merchants per province, city, and
municipality, subject to Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data

Privacy Act of 2012.” A Joint Congressional Oversight Committee is hereby
created to review the implementation of this Act.

SEC. 12. The PAMANA Trust Fund; Appropriations. — There is hereby
created a special fund, to be known as the PAMANA Trust Fund, to be maintained
by the Bureau of the Treasury and administered by the DSWD, which shall be
used exclusively for the payment of Program Subsidies and the costs of
implementing this Act. The Fund shall be sourced from the following:

(a) Fifty percent (50%) of the incremental revenues from the value-added tax on
digital services under Republic Act No. 12023;

(b) Fifty percent (50%) of the national government share in royalties, windfall
profits taxes, and other revenues collected under the fiscal regime for the mining
industry;

(c) The incremental revenues from any law imposing excise taxes on sweetened
beverages and pre-packaged food products lacking nutritional value, upon the
effectivity thereof;

(d) Dividends remitted by government-owned or -controlled corporations in
excess of the minimum remittance required under Republic Act No. 7656;

(e) Proceeds from the privatization of idle and non-performing government assets
as may be identified by the Privatization Council; and

(f) Such amounts as may be appropriated under the annual General
Appropriations Act.

      Any deficiency in the Fund shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act. Amounts accruing to the Fund shall not revert to the General
Fund at the end of the fiscal year and shall be carried over to the succeeding year

SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, in coordination with the DTI, the BSP, the
DICT, the PSA, and the Department of Budget and Management, shall
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation
of this Act.

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue
in full force and effect.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations,
or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
