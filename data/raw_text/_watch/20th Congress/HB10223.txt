                            EXPLANATORY NOTE


Under Republic Act No. 9003, otherwise known as the "Ecological Solid Waste
Management Act of 2000," local government units (LGUs)—specifically the
barangays—are tasked with primary responsibility for decentralized waste
segregation, recycling, and composting. Central to this mandate is Section 32,
which requires the establishment of a Materials Recovery Facility (MRF) in every
barangay or cluster of barangays.

However, more than two decades after the passage of RA 9003, full compliance
remains elusive. A substantial number of barangay MRFs across the country are
non-operational, serve merely as designated "open drop points" without actual
processing capabilities, or lack the fundamental infrastructure needed to sort,
compost, and store recyclable and organic waste safely. The primary bottlenecks
to sustained operational performance are:

   1. Vague Standards: Current legislation outlines the requirement for MRFs
      but lacks clear, enforceable minimum infrastructure, equipment, and
      occupational safety standards.

   2. Lack of Ring-Fenced Funding: Most barangays lack sufficient fiscal
      resources to cover capital expenditures for standard equipment or
      recurring operational costs like honoraria for waste handlers and utility
      bills.

This bill addresses these critical gaps by amending Section 32 of RA 9003 to
establish explicit Minimum Operational and Technical Standards for all Barangay
MRFs (covering structural enclosure, equipment, health/safety safeguards, and
recordkeeping).
Funding will be guaranteed through a National Barangay MRF Support Fund (NB-
MRFSF) alongside a mandatory appropriation of not less than five percent (5%) of
each LGU's National Tax Allotment (NTA) local solid waste management allocation
dedicated strictly to MRF operations.

Professionalizing and incentivizing local waste management personnel through
standardized training and safety protocols shall also be effected. By strengthening
local resource recovery, this measure will significantly increase national waste
diversion rates, reduce pressure on municipal landfills, and foster a localized
circular economy.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT ENHANCING BARANGAY MATERIALS RECOVERY FACILITIES BY
 SETTING MINIMUM OPERATIONAL AND INFRASTRUCTURE STANDARDS,
  PROVIDING DEDICATED FUNDING MECHANISMS, AMENDING FOR THE
    PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9003, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Barangay
Materials Recovery Facility (MRF) Standardization and Funding Act."

     SEC. 2. Amendment of Section 32 of Republic Act No. 9003. — Section 32 of
Republic Act No. 9003 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 32. Establishment and Minimum Operational Standards of LGU
      Materials Recovery Facility. — There shall be established a Materials
      Recovery Facility (MRF) in every barangay or cluster of barangays. The
      facility shall be established on barangay-owned or leased land, or any
      suitable open space determined by the barangay through its Sanggunian.

      (A) MINIMUM OPERATIONAL AND INFRASTRUCTURE STANDARDS. —
          EVERY BARANGAY MRF MUST BE FULLY OPERATIONAL AND
          SATISFY THE FOLLOWING MINIMUM TECHNICAL STANDARDS
          PRESCRIBED BY THE DEPARTMENT OF ENVIRONMENT AND
          NATURAL RESOURCES (DENR) THROUGH THE ENVIRONMENTAL
          MANAGEMENT BUREAU (EMB):

   1. STRUCTURAL SPECIFICATIONS: A COVERED, WELL-VENTILATED, AND
      ENCLOSED STRUCTURE WITH AN IMPERMEABLE (CONCRETE) FLOOR
      TO  PREVENT   LEACHATE     CONTAMINATION   OF   SOIL   AND
      GROUNDWATER.
  2. FUNCTIONAL ZONING: CLEARLY DESIGNATED DEDICATED AREAS FOR:

        o RECEIVING AND INITIAL SORTING OF RECYCLABLES AND
          RESIDUALS;

        o DRY STORAGE FOR SORTED RECYCLABLE                       MATERIALS
          (PLASTICS, PAPER, METALS, GLASS);

        o A COMPOSTING SITE OR ORGANIC WASTE PROCESSING ZONE
          COMPLIANT WITH DENR-EMB SANITARY GUIDELINES; AND

        o A TEMPORARY HOLDING AREA FOR SPECIAL AND HAZARDOUS
          HOUSEHOLD WASTES (E.G., BUSTED BULBS, E-WASTE,
          BATTERIES) PRIOR TO MUNICIPAL COLLECTION.

  3. ESSENTIAL EQUIPMENT: PROVISION OF BASIC PROCESSING
     EQUIPMENT PROPORTIONATE TO THE BARANGAY'S GENERATED
     WASTE VOLUME, INCLUDING BUT NOT LIMITED TO WEIGHING SCALES,
     SHREDDERS, COMPOST ROTARY DRUMS/BINS, BALING PRESSES
     (WHERE FEASIBLE), AND STANDARD SEGREGATION CONTAINERS.

  4. OCCUPATIONAL HEALTH AND SAFETY: PROVISION OF PERSONAL
     PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT (PPE) TO ALL WASTE HANDLERS, ACCESS TO
     HANDWASHING STATIONS AND SANITATION FACILITIES, MANDATORY
     ANNUAL IMMUNIZATIONS, AND COMPLIANCE WITH LOCAL LABOR
     STANDARDS.

  5. DIGITAL OR PHYSICAL LOGBOOK: MANDATORY MAINTENANCE OF
     REAL-TIME LOGS TRACKING DAILY INCOMING WASTE VOLUMES,
     DIVERTED RECYCLABLE AND ORGANIC VOLUMES, AND RESIDUAL
     WASTE RETURNED TO MUNICIPAL COLLECTION.

     (B) CLUSTERING OF BARANGAYS. — MUNICIPALITIES OR CITIES MAY
AUTHORIZE THE CLUSTERING OF BARANGAYS TO ESTABLISH A JOINT MRF;
PROVIDED, THAT THE COMBINED FACILITY HAS INCREASED CAPACITY,
EQUIPMENT, AND PERSONNEL DIRECTLY PROPORTIONATE TO THE
AGGREGATE WASTE VOLUME OF ALL PARTICIPATING BARANGAYS."

      SEC. 3. Provision of Dedicated Funding. – Chapter VI of Republic Act No.
9003 is hereby amended by adding a new section to read as follows:

     "SEC. 45-A. FUNDING SUPPORT FOR BARANGAY MATERIALS RECOVERY
     FACILITIES:

     (A) NATIONAL BARANGAY MRF SUPPORT FUND (NB-MRFSF). — THERE
         IS HEREBY CREATED A SPECIAL COMPONENT UNDER THE SOLID
         WASTE MANAGEMENT FUND MANAGED BY THE NATIONAL SOLID
          WASTE MANAGEMENT COMMISSION (NSWMC). AN INITIAL
          ALLOCATION OF TWO BILLION PHILIPPINE PESOS (PHP
          2,000,000,000.00) SHALL BE APPROPRIATED UNDER THE ANNUAL
          BUDGET OF THE DENR TO ASSIST 4TH, 5TH, AND 6TH CLASS
          MUNICIPALITIES AND LOW-INCOME BARANGAYS IN CAPITAL
          OUTLAYS FOR MRF CONSTRUCTION AND EQUIPMENT ACQUISITION.

      (B) LOCAL GOVERNMENT APPROPRIATIONS. — CITY AND MUNICIPAL
          GOVERNMENT UNITS SHALL ALLOCATE NOT LESS THAN FIVE
          PERCENT (5%) OF THEIR ANNUAL ECOLOGICAL SOLID WASTE
          MANAGEMENT BUDGET DERIVED FROM THEIR NATIONAL TAX
          ALLOTMENT (NTA) TO SUPPORT THE OPERATIONS, MAINTENANCE,
          AND PERSONNEL HONORARIA OF BARANGAY MRFS WITHIN THEIR
          JURISDICTION.

      (C) REVENUE RETENTION. — ALL INCOMES GENERATED BY THE
          BARANGAY MRF FROM THE SALE OF RECYCLABLES, COMPOST
          PRODUCTS,   AND   TIPPING   FEES     FROM    COMMERCIAL
          ESTABLISHMENTS SHALL BE RETAINED IN FULL BY THE BARANGAY
          AS A REVOLVING TRUST FUND DIRECTLY DEDICATED TO
          MAINTENANCE, OPERATIONAL IMPROVEMENTS, AND INCENTIVES
          FOR BARANGAY WASTE HANDLERS."


       SEC. 4. Capacity Building and Accreditation.- The Technical Education and
Skills Development Authority (TESDA), in coordination with the DENR and the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), shall develop a
standardized certification program for Barangay Waste Management Officers and
MRF Operators to ensure technical competency in material separation, safety,
and composting techniques.

      SEC. 5. Penalties and Non-Compliance.– Unjustified failure or refusal of
Local Chief Executives or Barangay Chairpersons to comply with the minimum
operational standards prescribed in Section 32 hereof within eighteen (18)
months from the effectivity of this Act shall constitute dereliction of duty, subject
to administrative sanctions provided under Republic Act No. 7160 (Local
Government Code of 1991).

       SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DENR, through the EMB and in consultation with
the DILG, the League of Barangays, and non-governmental environmental
organizations, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.
       SEC. 7. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, presidential
issuances, and other administrative rules and regulations or parts thereof which
are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

      SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
