                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive tertiary education
scholarship and grant program for the children of professional Filipino athletes who
have represented the Philippines in the international arena.

        The 1987 Constitution, under Article XIV, Section 19, states that “The State
shall promote physical education and encourage sports programs, league competitions, and
amateur sports, including training for international competitions, to foster self-discipline,
teamwork, and excellence for the development of a healthy and alert citizenry.” Our
professional athletes are not just mere competitors, but are also ambassadors of the
Philippine spirit. Every time a Filipino athlete steps onto the international stage,
they carry a sense of “Pinoy Pride” that boosts our national image and inspires the
next generation of Filipinos.

        Today, we are witnessing a golden age of Philippine sports. From the historic
double-gold performance of Carlos Yulo in the 2024 Paris Olympics1 to the
consistent world-class excellence of pole vaulter EJ Obiena2, and the breakthrough
rise of tennis player Alex Eala into the global Top 503, our athletes continue to prove
that Filipino talent and grit is world-tier.

       However, the career of a professional athlete is notoriously brief and
physically taxing. Unlike their counterparts in other countries who may benefit
from extensive state-sponsored lifelines, many Filipino professional athletes must
divide their focus between rigorous training, and the need to provide for their
families. While an athlete’s peak years bring immediate glory to the country, those
same years are often spent in financial anxiety regarding the long-term security of

       This proposed measure recognizes that the most meaningful way to honor an
athlete’s service is to ensure that their children are not left behind. Granting full
college scholarships and educational allowances to the children of our professional
athletes provides a lifeline of opportunity that outlasts any trophy or medal. It
transforms the fleeting honor of a sporting victory into the permanent legacy of a
college education for the athlete’s children.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                         AN ACT
GRANTING FULL COLLEGE SCHOLARSHIP TO CHILDREN OF
PROFESSIONAL   FILIPINO  ATHLETES   WHO     COMPETED     IN
INTERNATIONAL PROFESSIONAL SPORTS COMPETITIONS, OR IN OTHER
EQUALLY PRESTIGIOUS INTERNATIONAL GAMES, AND PROVIDING
FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Tertiary Scholarship
Grant to Children of Professional Filipino Athletes Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The 1987 Constitution provides that the State
shall give priority to sports in order for the citizens to foster patriotism and
nationalism, accelerate social progress, and promote total human liberation and
development. Thus, the State shall provide necessary benefits, which include but
may not be limited to, health, retirement, death, and educational benefits, to
professional Filipino athletes who competed or shall compete in international
professional sports competitions or in other equally prestigious world championship
games, in both individual or team events, in honor of the pride and glory they have
brought to the country.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

          a. ﻿Board refers to the Games and Amusement Board (GAB);

         b. ﻿Filipino athlete refers to a professional athlete who is a citizen of the
      Philippines, except those enjoying dual citizenship by virtue of Republic Act
      (RA) No. 9225, otherwise known as the “Citizenship Retention and Reacquisition
      Act of 2003”;

         c. ﻿International professional sports competitions refer to sanctioned
      athletic events, tournaments, or matches involving professional athletes from
      multiple nations, which are organized or sanctioned by an International

       Sporting Organization recognized, accredited, and have a mutual working
       relationship, agreement, or memorandum of understanding with the Board;

          d.​ ﻿International sporting organizations refer to international or regional
       governing bodies that establish rules, promote, and oversee specific sports
       disciplines across multiple countries, and which are formally recognized,
       accredited, and have a mutual working relationship, agreement, or
       memorandum of understanding with the Board;

           e.​ Other equally prestigious international games refer to other
       international professional sports competitions or events which, while not
       explicitly labeled as “competitions” under Section (3)(c), are determined by
       the Board to confer the same rank, stature, and international recognition as
       those defined therein;

           f.​ ﻿Primary beneficiaries refer to the legitimate spouse and legitimate, or
       illegitimate children of the professional athlete;

           g.​ ﻿Professional athlete refers to any natural person who is paid a sum of
       money or other equivalent compensation as a salary or prize money for
       participating in a game, bout, tournament or contest of professional sport,
       either as an individual contestant or a member of a team, under a contract
       entered into with a professional sports promoter, operator, or team owner,
       and is licensed by the Board;

          h.​ ﻿Professional sports refer to individual or team sports, games, contest,
       bouts, tournaments or competitions whereby the participating athlete is paid
       sums of money or other forms of compensation as regular salary or prize for
       participation, which are accredited and recognized by the Board; and

          i.​ ﻿Tertiary education refers to a level of education pursued beyond high
       school considered as undergraduate credential.

       SEC. 4. Educational Benefits to Primary Beneficiaries or Children. – Professional
Filipino Athletes, as defined under this Act, who competed in international
professional sports competitions, and other equally prestigious international games
shall be qualified to receive the following educational benefits for their chosen
primary beneficiaries or children:

          a.​ ﻿Free tuition and exemption from other fees upon admission to state
       universities and colleges (SUCs) and local universities and colleges (LUCs) in
       the Philippines pursuant to RA No. 10931, otherwise known as the “Universal
       Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act”; and

           b.​ educational allowance amounting to Twenty-Five Thousand Pesos
       (P25,000.00) per academic year for the whole duration of the beneficiary or
       child's Tertiary education: Provided, That the primary beneficiary or child shall
       be officially enrolled in any of the country’s SUCs or LUCs: Provided, further,

       That the Professional Filipino Athlete has not received, is not receiving, or
       will not receive any financial incentive from the government which
       cumulative amount is more than Ten Million Pesos (P10,000,000.00) for a
       period five (5) years prior the enrollment of the primary beneficiary or child
       in Tertiary education, has no declared liabilities, and has not been convicted
       of any crimes involving moral turpitude.

       SEC. 5. Claiming of Educational Benefits Upon the Death of Professional Filipino
Athletes. – Upon the death of a Professional Filipino Athlete before the primary
beneficiaries or children become qualified for Tertiary Education, the primary
beneficiaries or children shall still be entitled to the educational benefits or
scholarship grant upon their official enrollment in Tertiary Education programs of
any of the country's SUCs or LUCs, as stipulated in this Act.

       SEC. 6. Appropriations. – The Chairman of the Board shall immediately
include in the Board's program the implementation of the benefits under this Act,
the funding of which shall be included in the Annual General Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Board within ninety (90)
days, after the effectivity of this Act, through a technical working committee, and in
consultation with the sectors concerned, shall promulgate and issue the
implementing rules and regulations for the full implementation of this Act: Provided,
however, That copies of the formulated rules and regulations shall be submitted to
the Committee on Games and Amusement of both Houses of Congress. Such rules
and regulations shall take effect upon publication in a newspaper of general
circulation.

      SEC. 8. Reportorial Requirements. – The Board shall submit an annual report of
the implementation of this Act to the relevant committees of both Houses of
Congress.

       SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
subsequently declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which are
not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof that are inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 11. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette and at least two (2) newspapers of national
circulation.
