                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to strengthen early intervention, guidance and counseling,
anti-bullying programs, and restorative approaches to student misconduct. It seeks
to promote a school environment that protects learners and provides timely support
to prevent behavioral misconduct from escalating into more serious incidents.

       Schools have a responsibility to respond promptly to bullying, threats,
violence, and other disciplinary incidents while addressing the circumstances that
may contribute to a learner’s behavior. Ideally, schools are also expected to provide
accessible counseling services with adequately trained personnel, coordinated
interventions, and with clear procedures for identifying risks and responding
immediately to potential threats.

       This proposed measure establishes a coordinated framework to strengthen
guidance and counseling services, behavioral risk assessments, and safety
assessments in support of Republic Act No. 12080, otherwise known as the “Basic
Education Mental Health and Well-Being Promotion Act”, and other existing
anti-bullying policies.

       Every learner deserves a safe school environment where education can take
place free from bullying, violence, and fear. Learners who experience bullying or
violence must receive timely protection and support, while those who exhibit
concerning behavior should have access to interventions that address the root causes
of these misdemeanors. Protecting learners requires not only a firm response to
harmful conduct but also a collective commitment to prevent bullying, to provide
timely assistance, and to advance the welfare of every child.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE SCHOOL DISCIPLINE AND VIOLENCE
PREVENTION SYSTEM, PROMOTING RESTORATIVE APPROACHES TO
STUDENT MISCONDUCT, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “School Discipline and
Early Intervention Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect children and youth from violence, abuse, bullying, neglect, discrimination,
and other circumstances that may impede their healthy development and
well-being. The State recognizes that schools play a vital role not only in providing
education but also in promoting the safety, welfare, and holistic development of
learners.

       Towards this end, the State shall strengthen school-based mechanisms for
early identification, intervention, counseling, guidance, and support for children
who may be at risk of behavioral, emotional, psychological, social, or academic
difficulties. It shall promote safe, inclusive, and supportive learning environments,
encourage the use of restorative and rehabilitative approaches to student discipline,
and prioritize preventive measures that address the root causes of misconduct
before they escalate into delinquency, violence, or criminal behavior.

       The State shall likewise collaborate with educational institutions, parents,
local government units (LGUs), social workers, mental health professionals, law
enforcement agencies, and other stakeholders in ensuring the welfare of children
and maintaining school safety.

      Consistent with the principles embodied in Republic Act No. 9344 otherwise
known as the “Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006”, as amended, all interventions
under this Act shall be guided by the best interests of the child, the promotion of

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

         a.​ Early Intervention refers to a coordinated system of identifying,
      assessing, and addressing behavioral, emotional, psychological, social,
      academic, or environmental factors that may place a learner at risk of
      delinquency, violence, victimization, self-harm, or conflict with the law;

         b.​ Restorative School Discipline refers to a child-centered approach to
      discipline that emphasizes accountability, reconciliation, behavioral
      correction, rehabilitation and reintegration, rather than purely punitive
      sanctions;

          c.​ School Discipline Policy Committee refers to the multidisciplinary
      body established under this Act to formulate and oversee school discipline,
      intervention, counseling, anti-bullying, and violence prevention policies;

         d.​ School Threat Assessment Team refers to a multidisciplinary body
      responsible for evaluating reports of violence, threats, possession of weapons,
      severe bullying, self-harm, or other conduct indicating a potential risk to
      school safety; and

          e.​ Student Support Services refer to counseling, guidance, mentoring,
      referral, intervention, rehabilitation, and other programs designed to address
      the developmental and behavioral needs of learners.

       SEC. 4. Creation of the School Discipline Policy Committee. – There is hereby
established a School Discipline Policy Committee, hereinafter referred to as the
Committee, in all public and private educational institutions which shall be
composed of the following:

          a.​ The School Head or Principal as Chairperson;

          b.​ Guidance Counselor or Guidance Advocate;

          c.​ Representative from the teaching personnel;

          d.​ A representative from the parents’ association;

          e.​ A student representative, where appropriate; and

        f.​ Such other members as may be determined by the Department of
      Education (DepEd).

      The Committee shall formulate, review, and oversee the implementation of
school discipline policies, intervention programs, anti-bullying initiatives,
counseling services, and violence prevention strategies.

       SEC. 5. Early Identification and Intervention Program. – All schools shall
establish systems for the early identification of learners exhibiting behavioral,
emotional, psychological, social, academic, or environmental indicators associated
with elevated risks of violence, self-harm, bullying, victimization, substance abuse,
delinquency, or conflict with the law.

      Upon identification, schools shall implement appropriate intervention
measures, including counseling, mentoring, family engagement, referral services,
behavioral support plans, and other evidence-based programs.

       SEC. 6. Strengthening Counseling and Guidance Programs. – All educational
institutions shall strengthen counseling and guidance services through:

          a.​ Mental health and wellness assessments;

          b.​ Crisis intervention services;

          c.​ Peer support and monitoring initiatives;

          d.​ Family engagement initiatives;

         e.​ Referral mechanisms to social workers, psychologists, and mental
      health professionals; and

         f.​ Continuous training and professional development for guidance
      personnel.

       The DepEd shall develop standards to improve the accessibility and quality
of guidance and counseling services in schools.

        SEC. 7. School Anti-Bullying and Violence Prevention Programs. – All schools
shall implement comprehensive anti-bullying and violence prevention programs,
which shall include but shall not be limited to:

          a.​ Anonymous and confidential reporting mechanisms;

          b.​ Peer mediation and conflict resolution initiatives;

          c.​ Awareness and education campaigns;

          d.​ Immediate response protocols for threats and acts of violence; and

          e.​ Protection and support measures for victims and witnesses.




                                              3
       SEC. 8. Restorative and Non-Punitive Interventions. – Schools shall prioritize
restorative and rehabilitative approaches in addressing student misconduct
whenever appropriate. Such interventions may include counseling, mediation,
behavioral improvement plans, family conferencing, community service, mentorship
programs, and other developmental measures designed to promote accountability
and behavioral change. Suspension, expulsion, referral to law enforcement, or other
punitive sanctions shall be imposed only when necessary to protect public safety or
when less restrictive measures have proven ineffective.

       SEC. 9. School Threat Assessment Teams. – All public and private educational
institutions shall establish a School Threat Assessment Team which shall be
composed of the School Head or Principal, Guidance Counselor or Guidance
Advocate, a Teacher Representative, a Parent Representative, and such other
personnel as may be designated by the school. Where available, the Team may
include a School Psychologist, School Social Worker, School Nurse, or other qualified
professionals.

       The Team shall evaluate reports involving threats of violence, possession of
weapons, severe bullying, self-harm, suicidal ideation, violent behavior, or other
circumstances indicating a potential risk to school safety and shall recommend
appropriate intervention and support measures. All assessments conducted under
this section shall remain confidential and shall be used solely for prevention,
intervention, and child protection purposes.

       SEC. 10. Student Behavioral Risk Assessment System. – All schools shall establish
a Student Behavioral Risk Assessment System designed to identify learners who
may be experiencing behavioral, emotional, psychological, social, academic, or
environmental difficulties that increase their risk of violence, victimization,
self-harm, delinquency, or conflict with the law.

       The system shall utilize evidence-based assessment tools and intervention
measures. No learner shall be subjected to discrimination, stigmatization, exclusion,
or disciplinary action solely on the basis of an assessment conducted under this
section.

        SEC. 11. School Climate and Safety Assessment. – All public and private
educational institutions shall conduct an annual School Climate and Safety
Assessment to evaluate the overall learning environment and identify factors
affecting student well-being, school safety, and the effectiveness of discipline and
intervention programs.

      The assessment may include anonymous surveys, consultations, focus group
discussions, and other evidence-based methods and shall be used solely for policy
development, program improvement, violence prevention, and student support.

      SEC. 12. Mandatory Parent Engagement Conferences. – Prior to the imposition of
any major disciplinary sanction, including long-term suspension, exclusion,
expulsion, or referral to law enforcement authorities, schools shall conduct a Parent

Engagement Conference involving the learner, the learner’s parent/s or guardian/s,
relevant school personnel, and guidance personnel. The conference shall seek to
identify the causes of the learner’s behavior, develop appropriate corrective
measures, and encourage cooperation among all parties in promoting the learner’s
welfare and accountability.

       SEC. 13. School Safety Planning. – All educational institutions shall formulate
and periodically update a School Safety and Violence Prevention Plan containing
policies and procedures on:

          a.​ Prevention of school violence and bullying;

          b.​ Threat reporting and response mechanisms;

          c.​ Crisis intervention and emergency response;

          d.​ Coordination with local government units and emergency responders;

          e.​ Student mental health and psychosocial support services; and

          f.​ Post-incident recovery and reintegration measures.

       SEC. 14. School Violence Prevention Fund. – There is hereby established a School
Violence Prevention Fund to be administered by the DepEd. The Fund shall be used
exclusively for:

         a.​ Hiring and      training   guidance    counselors and       mental health
      professionals;

          b.​ Capacity-building programs for teachers and school personnel;

          c.​ Anti-bullying and violence prevention initiatives;

          d.​ Counseling and intervention services;

          e.​ School safety and threat assessment programs;

          f.​ Research, monitoring, and evaluation activities; and

          g.​ Other activities necessary to carry out the objectives of this Act.

      SEC. 15. Data Collection and Research. – The DepEd, in coordination with the
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Department of Health
(DOH), Juvenile Justice and Welfare Council (JJWC), Commission on Higher
Education (CHED), Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA), and other concerned agencies, shall establish and maintain a national
database on school discipline incidents, bullying, school violence, intervention
programs, student support services, and related matters.

       All data collection and processing activities shall comply with applicable laws

       SEC. 16. Annual Congressional Reporting. – The DepEd shall submit to the
Senate and the House of Representatives, not later than March 31 of every year, a
comprehensive report on the implementation of this Act. The report shall include,
but shall not be limited to the statistics on school violence, bullying, disciplinary
incidents, intervention measures, utilization of the School Violence Prevention Fund,
implementation challenges, and recommendations for legislative and policy
improvements.

       SEC. 17. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, in coordination with the DSWD, DOH, JJWC,
CHED, TESDA, and other concerned stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary
rules and regulations for the implementation of this Act.

       SEC. 19. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

       SEC. 20. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 21. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
