                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Shortly after being sworn in, President Ferdinand R. Marcos, Jr. declared
that food security is the key to having a vibrant future for the country and
emphasized that his administration will focus on agriculture to build a strong
economy and to assure adequate food supply even in emergencies.1

       Moreover, during the 35th United Nations Food and Agriculture
Organization (FAO) Regional Conference for Asia and the Pacific held in
November 2020, the Philippines Department of Agriculture (DA) said that it
would push for the digitalization of the country's agriculture sector under the
“new normal” to achieve its goals of reducing poverty and attaining food
security in the country. DA related that this can be achieved through teaching
farm families and other vulnerable agriculture groups to capitalize on digital
agriculture to increase their productivity and income, by providing them the
needed technical, marketing and financial support. Furthermore, the DA is "fast-
tracking" the development of a digital road map for the agriculture sector to
promote and integrate precision agriculture and digital technology in local
farming practices to assimilate digital technologies in the food value chain and
logistics, benefiting both producers and consumers.2

       In support of this policy direction on digitalization of the DA, and in line
with the President's thrust to upgrade our agriculture sector, this bill seeks to
establish an Agriculture Information System (AIS), managed by the DA, which
shall serve as a dynamic online computer database where information on the

       The concept of AIS is likewise based on the Municipal Agriculture
Information System (MAIS) developed by the Local Government Unit of Mina,
Iloilo which collects, sorts, evaluates and utilizes agricultural data on farmers,
their family, their activities, land, crop and livestock production in real time. It
provides information to decision makers, market players and other key
government and private sector interest groups based on data gathered by
barangay-level volunteers and relayed by SMS or stored in flash drives and are
received at the municipal level and entered in the central data bank where they
are digitally collated, sorted and analyzed. Accurate information on any
commodity produced in the municipality of Mina are inputs to helping farmers
market their products outside, giving them assurance on better prices and
volumes they can sell which directly redound to improved and stable income
for them.3

           With all these considerations, support for the passage is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
     ESTABLISHING THE AGRICULTURE INFORMATION SYSTEM IN
     ALL  PROVINCES,   CITIES  AND  MUNICIPALITIES, AND
     APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

1          Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Agriculture
2    Information System Act.”
3
4           Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to uplift the
5    socioeconomic conditions of farmers and farming communities nationwide in
6    order to promote their welfare and empower their sector in the overall
7    development and progress of the country. Pursuant to this, an integrated,
8    computerized, web-based agricultural commodity supply inventory and registry
9    system linking farmers and organized communities all over the country needs to
10   be established to provide an easy-to-access local and global real time information
11   for agricultural commodities, with an end view of creating a wider range of
12   market linkages among farmers and agricultural buyers. Furthermore, through a
13   synchronized agriculture information system, the National Government shall
14   have access to real-time agricultural information which can rationalize the
15   objectives and policy directives in the agricultural sector.
16
17         Sec. 3. Objectives. – This Act aims to attain the following objectives:
18
1       a) Provide an up-to-date database of agricultural and fisheries products in a
2          locality;
3       b) Foster information sharing between farmers and potential buyers of
4          agricultural and fisheries products;
5       c) (c) Secure a market for agricultural and fisheries products and ensure
6          appropriate return of investment for farmers and fisheries stakeholders;
7       d) (d) Allow local government units to manage a secure and independent
8          platform that creates linkages for farmers and fisheries stakeholders;
9       e) (e) Provide data on specific commodities that are readily available in a
10         particular locality for purposes of production monitoring in order to meet
11         the demands of the market;
12      f) (f) Provide a secure, transparent, and independent modern web application
13         that may be accessed anytime by the public through a multi-platform
14         support or cross-platform support devices which can be linked to existing
15         systems in the Department of Agriculture (DA);
16      g) (g) Streamline training and seminars in the management of the operations;
17      h) (h) Monitor the use of inputs given by producers, buyers, and other
18         segments of the value-chain including information during post-harvest and
19         processing; and
20      (i) Ensure that all forms of assistance given to local government units (LGUs),
21          registered cooperatives and other qualified individuals and organizations
22          are monitored.
23
24          Sec. 4. Agriculture Information System. – All cities and municipalities are
25   hereby mandated to establish an Agriculture Information System (AIS). The AIS
26   shall serve as a dynamic online computer database where information on the
27   demand for specific agricultural and fisheries commodities is gathered and
28   uploaded simultaneously with the production data from farmers in every
29   barangay. The AIS shall merge and synchronize agricultural data from different
30   sources into a cohesive database designed to facilitate linkages from place of
31   origin or the farm to local, and ultimately, global markets. The AIS shall also be
32   integrated with the existing systems of the DA.
33
34         The AIS shall have the following features and contents:
1         a) Municipal and city-wide identification of individual farmers, fish
2            breeders, and fisherfolk as well as qualified organizations which produce
3            specific farm and fisheries products, regardless of quantity;
4         b) Crop harvest, volume, and type forecasting;
5         c) Inventory of specific classification of crop varieties planted, livestock
6            and type of fish raised in a particular LGU;
7         d) Farmers' and fisherfolk demographics, including age group, gender,
8            ethnic group, income, seminars and trainings attended, and assistance
9            received;
10        e) Selected reports presented in both tabular and graphical form;
11        f) Registry of farmers' contact details and farm location to facilitate market
12           linkages;
13        g) Search engine which allows farmers, fish breeders, and fisherfolk to look
14           for potential and actual buyers and for such buyers to search for farms,
15           farmers, and available agricultural and fisheries products; and
16        h) Customizable dashboard which is accessible only to registered farmers
17           and fisherfolk clients and contains the farmers' and fisherfolk's profile
18           such as the area of the farm or fishpond, crops planted, livestock and
19           type of fish raised.
20
21          Sec. 5. Registration. – All cities and municipalities shall encourage and
22   facilitate the free registration of all farmers, fisherfolk, as well as buyers
23   transacting within their respective areas in the AIS.
24
25        Sec. 6. Functions and Responsibilities of Cities and Municipalities. – The
26   LGU shall exercise the following functions and responsibilities:

28           Agrarian Reform Office, shall gather, encode, and consolidate all
29           pertinent data on the profile and demographics of farmers, fisherfolk as
30           well as buyers;
31        b) Monitor and control farm inputs including machineries and equipment;
32        c) For municipalities and component cities, prepare and submit real-time
33           data under (a) and (b) to the provincial agricultural office and to the
1            respective regional offices of the DA;
2         d) For highly urbanized cities, submit real-time data to the respective
3            regional offices of the DA;
4         e) Establish farmer and buyer linkages and act as a gatekeeper at the city
5            or municipal level in the nationwide marketing of agricultural products;
6            and
7         f) Serve as the web administrator at the municipal or city level and be
8            responsible for integrating farm and fisheries locations into the
9            geographic integration system map.
10

11         Sec. 7. Functions and Responsibilities of the Province. – The provincial
12   government shall have the following functions and responsibilities:
13        a. The Provincial Planning and Development Office (PPDO) is the web
14           administrator at the provincial level and is responsible for integrating
15           farm and fisheries data into the geographic integration system maps for
16           agriculture and fisheries;
17        b. The Local Economic and Investment Promotion Office (LEIPO) acts as
18           the lead administrator of the AIS in the provincial level and ensures the
19           balance between supply and demand;
20        c. The Provincial Agricultural Office monitors the submission of reports
21           from municipalities and component cities, and ensures the completeness,
22           accuracy, and validity of data submitted by the Municipal Agricultural
23           Offices and the Municipal Agrarian Reform Offices; and
24        d. Except for highly urbanized cities, prepare and submit such data to the
25           regional office of the DA.
26

29   responsibilities:
30

31        a. Integrate the data provided by the provinces and highly urbanized cities

4           Sec. 10. Duties of the Secretary. – The Secretary of the DA, in coordination
