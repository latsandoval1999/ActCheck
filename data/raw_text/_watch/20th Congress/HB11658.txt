                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

Republic Act No. 7923, approved in 1995, consolidated three public postsecondary institutions
in Camiguin into the Camiguin Polytechnic State College and gave the College a mandate
centered on the educational and development conditions prevailing at that time. More than
three decades later, CPSC serves a substantially changed province and higher-education
environment. New professions, technologies, regulatory standards, modes of instruction,
research needs, and workforce requirements have emerged, while the College has developed
academic institutes, programs, extension activities, and institutional responsibilities that were
not contemplated in detail by the original charter.

CPSC remains the principal public higher-education institution based in the island province.
Its present offerings provide important pathways in teacher education, engineering and
computer studies, industrial technology, agriculture, business and public administration,
tourism, marine biology, and graduate education. These programs have enabled generations of
students to obtain an affordable public education close to their families. The statutory authority
of the College, however, has not kept pace with the breadth of present and anticipated demand.

Many qualified students seek programs that CPSC does not presently offer, particularly in
nursing and allied health sciences, medicine and public health, emerging technologies,
specialized engineering, environmental and marine-resource management, and other fields
required by the province and the region. When a needed program is unavailable locally, a
student may be compelled to leave Camiguin, assume additional transportation and living
expenses, postpone higher education, or pursue a course that does not match the student’s
aptitude or intended profession. These burdens fall most heavily on low-income families and
may prevent the province from developing and retaining the professionals it needs.

The proposed amendment is therefore not a conversion of CPSC into a university and does not
alter the composition of its Board of Trustees. It strengthens the existing State College by
modernizing its academic mandate and authorizing it to retain its current programs and, when
justified, establish additional technical-vocational, undergraduate, graduate, postgraduate,
professional, continuing-education, and flexible-learning programs within its competence and
areas of specialization.

The authority to offer additional programs is not automatic or unlimited. A program may open
only after demonstrated local or regional need, inclusion in the institutional development plan,
availability of funds, recommendation through the appropriate academic process, and full


                                                1
compliance with the policies, standards, and requirements of the Commission on Higher
Education, the Professional Regulation Commission, and other competent authorities.

Programs requiring licensure, laboratories, clinical training, internships, affiliations,
accreditation, or specialized facilities may not admit students until the necessary faculty,
curriculum, facilities, equipment, learning resources, and regulatory authority are in place.

Academic authority must be matched by institutional capacity. The bill accordingly provides
a legal basis for the phased construction, repair, renovation, upgrading, and maintenance of
classrooms, laboratories, simulation and clinical-training facilities, workshops, libraries,
research and innovation centers, digital systems, student-support facilities, accessibility
features, safety systems, and disaster-resilient infrastructure. It also supports the acquisition
and maintenance of books, databases, software, medical and laboratory equipment,
instructional technologies, and other learning resources. Implementation will follow
institutional priorities and available appropriations and will remain subject to procurement,
property, budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws.

The measure also recognizes that facilities alone cannot sustain quality education. CPSC must
be able to maintain a sufficient complement of qualified faculty members, researchers,
specialists, librarians, laboratory and clinical personnel, information-technology personnel,
student-support personnel, and administrative personnel. The College must likewise support
graduate study, professional certification, licensure, clinical or industry exposure, research
training, and other forms of faculty and personnel development. These investments are
essential to regulatory compliance, program accreditation, instructional quality, research,
extension, and student welfare.

To support orderly implementation, the bill requires an updated institutional development plan,
phased program development, and coordination with CHED, the Department of Budget and
Management, and other concerned agencies. It modernizes selected powers and procedures of
the Board, facilitates institutional partnerships and lawful resource sharing, and updates the
appropriations provision so that additional academic programs, facilities, equipment, plantilla
requirements, faculty development, research, scholarships, student support, accreditation, and
regulatory compliance may be properly considered in the annual national budget.

Amending the charter will enable CPSC to respond to student aspirations and the human-
resource requirements of Camiguin and Northern Mindanao without compromising academic
quality or fiscal responsibility. It will give the College the legal and institutional framework to
expand carefully, accommodate more qualified students as capacity permits, and deliver
programs supported by the personnel, facilities, equipment, and public funding necessary for
their success.

In view of the foregoing, immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.
