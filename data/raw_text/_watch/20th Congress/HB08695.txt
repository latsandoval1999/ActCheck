                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Mobile devices and other electronic gadgets have increasingly become
valuable tools in education, enhancing both teaching and learning. They
enable flexible course delivery, allow access to online platforms and resources,
and facilitate digital interaction. 1However, despite these benefits, rapid
technological advancement has also introduced distractions that can
negatively affect learning, particularly among students in basic education.

       Studies conducted in other countries indicate that excessive mobile
device use undermines student performance in two ways. 2 First, there is a
direct impact, as frequent phone use while studying is associated with lower
grades or scores. Second, there is an indirect impact, as overuse can impair the
cognitive skills and abilities necessary for academic success.

      Data from the Philippines, based on the Programme for International
Student Assessment (PISA) 2022, show that 8 out of 10 Filipino learners aged
15 reported being distracted in class due to their own smartphone use, while a
similar proportion reported distraction caused by classmates using
smartphones. Further analysis found that such distractions are linked to

      Beyond academic performance, access to mobile devices may also
increase the risk of involvement in cyberbullying.3 For this reason, regulating
the use of mobile devices and electronic gadgets particularly during class
hours iss necessary to strike a balance between leveraging technology and
maintaining an effective learning environment.

       Recognizing these concerns, the 2023 Global Education Monitoring
Report of the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural
Organization (UNESCO) recommends stricter regulation of mobile phone use
in classrooms. 4The report notes that only 13% of countries have implemented
laws that partially or fully ban mobile phone use in schools, while 14% have
adopted policies or guidelines for similar purposes.5

To promote responsible technology use and improve student engagement and
academic outcomes, the following measures are proposed:

    • Prohibit the use of mobile devices and electronic gadgets by learners
      from Kindergarten to Senior High School within school premises
      during class hours, as well as by teachers and faculty during their
      assigned teaching periods, except in cases involving learning needs,
      health and well-being, or risk management;
    • Direct the Department of Education to develop and implement
      guidelines on this prohibition, with private educational institutions
      required to adopt corresponding measures; and
    • Impose appropriate sanctions on public and private educational
      institutions that fail to comply with the provisions of this Act, with

                              AN ACT
     PROHIBITING THE USE OF MOBILE DEVICES AND OTHER
     ELECTRONIC   GADGETS     DURING CLASS  HOURS    FROM
     KINDERGARTEN TO SENIOR HIGH SCHOOL IN ALL PUBLIC AND
     PRIVATE EDUCATION INSTITUTIONS

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
           Congress assembled:


 1             SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Electronic
 2      Gadget-Free Schools Act.”

 3             SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to promote
 4      and protect the physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being of our
 5      youth and learners. Towards this end, policies must be crafted to safeguard their
 6      well-being and enhance their potentials and skills through eliminating distractions
 7      caused by mobile devices and other electronic gadgets and protecting them from
 8      the unwarranted effects of excessive online and gaming use.
 9             SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following
10      terms shall mean:
11             (a)    Mobile Device refers to any form of portable electronic devices such
12                    as but not limited to smartphones, cellular phones, tablet computers,




                                                                                            1
 1                  pocket personal computer (PC), palmtops, smartwatches, virtual
 2                  reality (VR) headsets, smart glasses, wearable cameras, and other
 3                  similar devices which can store, send, receive, and move data through
 4                  the internet or wireless connection.
 5          (b)     Electronic Gadget refers to any portable electronic device that can
 6                  enable its user to make calls, capture photos, stream video, and play
 7                  games such as but not limited to laptops, video games consoles,
 8                  virtual reality headsets, smart glasses, wearable cameras, and other
 9                  similar contraptions.
10          SEC. 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all public and private
11   education institutions offering any level of the basic education curriculum.
12          SEC. 5. Prohibition on the Use of Mobile Devices and Other Electronic
13   Gadgets by Learners. – The Department of Education (DepEd) shall promulgate
14   guidelines on the prohibition of the use of mobile devices and other electronic
15   gadgets by learners from Kindergarten to Senior High School within the school
16   premises during class hours except as provided under Section 7 of this Act.
17          The DepEd shall likewise ensure that private education institutions
18   formulate guidelines in accordance with this Act and include the same in their
19   respective handbooks and school policies.
20          School premises shall include classrooms, laboratories, bathrooms,
21   gymnasiums, canteens or cafeterias, designated school club rooms, swimming
22   pools, teachers’ lounges, and school offices.
23          SEC. 6. Prohibition on the Use of Mobile Devices and Other Electronic
24   Gadgets by Teachers and Faculty Members. – Teachers and faculty members are
25   likewise prohibited from using mobile devices and other electronic gadgets during
26   their designated teaching hours in classrooms, except as provided under Section 7
27   of this Act.




                                                                                       2
 1          SEC. 7. Exceptions. – Mobile devices and other electronic gadgets shall
 2   not be used within the school premises during class hours save for the following
 3   exceptions:
 4          (a)    Learning-related exceptions, such as classroom presentations or
 5                 class-based learning activities, requiring the use of such technologies,
 6                 or for learners for whom a reasonable adjustment to a learning
 7                 program is needed due to learning disability or difficulty;
 8          (b)    Health and well-being-related exceptions, such as learners with
 9                 specific health conditions that may require mobile devices and other
10                 electronic gadgets, as certified by a medical doctor and duly noted by
11                 the School Head; and
12          (c)    Exceptions related to managing risks, such as in case of emergency,
13                 in response to a perceived threat or danger, or in case of field trips
14                 and other school-related activities outside the school premises.
15          SEC. 8. Sanctions for Non-compliance. – All covered public and private
16   education institutions who fail to comply with the provisions of this Act shall be
17   subject to sanctions that may be imposed by the DepEd. Learners shall likewise be
18   subject to appropriate sanctions provided in the school policies set forth by the
19   DepEd, for violation of Section 5 hereof.
20          SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DepEd, in
21   consultation with the Department of Health, representatives from the Parent-
22   Teacher Associations, relevant government agencies, and other private
23   stakeholders shall promulgate the rules and regulations within ninety (90) days
24   after the effectivity of this Act.
25          SEC. 10. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
26   declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts not affected
27   shall remain in full force and effect.
