                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE


Fuel prices affect every Filipino. When oil prices rise sharply, transportation becomes more
expensive, food prices increase, operating costs for businesses grow, and the burden on ordinary
families becomes heavier. During periods of international instability, supply disruption, or energy
crisis, the danger becomes even greater when powerful players in the oil industry take advantage
of the situation through hoarding, profiteering, cartelization, and other forms of market abuse.

Batas Pambansa Blg. 33 enacted in 1979, as amended by Presidential Decree 1865 in 1983, has
defined and penalized certain prohibited acts inimical to the public interest and national security
involving petroleum and/or petroleum products. However, with the changing times and
conditions of our economy, a stronger legal protection is urgently needed.

More recently, the Congress enacted Republic Act No. 12022 in 2024, which recognized that
large-scale hoarding, profiteering, and cartel activity in a critical sector may properly be treated
as economic sabotage and be punished with far greater severity. However, there remains no
direct and comprehensive law that expressly classifies grave and large-scale abuses in the oil
industry as economic sabotage. This gap leaves consumers vulnerable at the very moment when
they most need protection. When the oil industry is manipulated, the consequences are immediate
and widespread – Public transportation fares rise; The cost of food distribution increases;
Farmers and fisherfolk bear heavier operating expenses; Small businesses suffer; Inflation
worsens; And the entire national economy is placed under strain.

This bill seeks to address that gap by defining large-scale hoarding, profiteering, cartelization,
and other serious abuses in the oil industry as forms of economic sabotage, and imposing stiffer
penalties to the already existing nefarious practices in the industry.

In the best interest of our People in these trying times, the immediate passage of this bill is
earnestly sought.

23   SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Anti-Oil Economic Sabotage Act.”

24   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State shall protect the Filipino people against abuse,
25   exploitation, artificial shortages, unreasonable price increases, cartel behavior, and all other grave
26   acts in the oil industry that prejudice public welfare and undermine economic stability.

27   The State recognizes that petroleum products are essential to transportation, agriculture, fisheries,
28   commerce, power generation, food distribution, and the daily life of every Filipino. Any
29   manipulation of oil supply or prices during times of economic hardship, supply instability,
30   emergency, or energy crisis causes widespread harm to consumers and the national economy.

31   Towards this end, the State shall treat certain large-scale and deliberate acts of hoarding,
32   profiteering, cartelization, and market abuse in the oil industry as economic sabotage, and shall
33   impose stiffer penalties and stronger enforcement mechanisms to deter and punish the same.

34   SECTION 3. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all natural and juridical persons engaged in the
35   importation, exportation, refining, processing, storage, transport, shipment, distribution,
36   wholesale, retail sale, or other commercial handling of crude oil, petroleum products, liquefied
37   petroleum gas, and other fuel products under the jurisdiction of the Department of Energy.

26   SECTION 7. The same Act is further amended by adding a new Section 4 to read as follows:

27   “SEC. 4. Acts Constituting Oil Industry Economic Sabotage. — The following acts, when
28   committed on a large scale or in a manner gravely prejudicial to the public and the economy,
29   shall constitute economic sabotage in the oil industry:

30   a)     Hoarding of petroleum products during an abnormal supply and price situation;

31   b)     Profiteering in the sale of petroleum products during an abnormal supply and price
32          situation;

33   c)     Cartelization, including price fixing, supply restriction, market allocation, bid rigging, or
34          coordinated acts to manipulate competition in the oil industry;

35   d)     Deliberate closure, shutdown, throttling, or suspension of refinery, depot, terminal,
36          transport, delivery, or retail operations without lawful justification, where such act
37          causes or is intended to cause artificial shortage or unjustified price spikes;
                                                                                               Page 6 of 9
 1   e)     Fraudulent misdeclaration of inventory, import volume, landed cost, freight charges,
 2          replacement cost, or any other material pricing component in order to justify or conceal
 3          unreasonable increases in fuel prices;

 4   f)      Diversion of supply to selected markets or buyers for the purpose of creating artificial
 5          scarcity in another locality;

 6   g)     Refusal without lawful cause to sell, distribute, or release available petroleum products
 7          during an abnormal supply and price situation;

 8   h)     Tampering with meters, pumps, dispensing devices, stock records, delivery logs, digital
 9          pricing systems, or other commercial records as part of a large-scale scheme to defraud
10          consumers or conceal prohibited acts under this Act;

11   i)     Aiding, abetting, financing, directing, or knowingly protecting any prohibited act under
12          this Act; and

13   j)     Other analogous acts of large-scale manipulation, supply distortion, price exploitation or
14          abuse of market power in the oil industry, as may be determined by the Department of
15          Energy in coordination with the Department of Justice and the Philippine Competition
16          Commission.”
