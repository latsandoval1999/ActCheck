                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

The right to a nationality is a fundamental human right that enables individuals to fully
participate in society and enjoy their civil, political, economic, and social rights. However,
refugees and stateless persons often face significant barriers to acquiring citizenship due to
their unique circumstances, including forced displacement, lack of official documentation, and
limited access to legal remedies.

As a State Party to the 1951 United Nations Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees and
its 1967 Protocol, as well as the 1954 Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons
and the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness, the Philippines has committed to
advancing the protection and integration of refugees and stateless persons.

This proposed measure seeks to establish a facilitated administrative naturalization process for
refugees and stateless persons duly recognized by the Philippine Government. Building upon
Republic Act No. 9139, or the Administrative Naturalization Law of 2000, the bill provides a
more accessible pathway to Philippine citizenship through simplified documentary
requirements, reasonable accommodations for vulnerable individuals, and mechanisms that
promote family unity.

The measure likewise ensures that the grant of citizenship remains subject to appropriate
qualifications, administrative safeguards, and national security considerations, thereby
balancing the country's humanitarian commitments with the protection of public interest.

By providing refugees and stateless persons with a viable pathway toward permanent
integration into Philippine society, this legislation reaffirms the Philippines' commitment to
human dignity, inclusivity, and the protection of fundamental rights.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 “AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE ACQUISITION OF PHILIPPINE CITIZENSHIP
  FOR REFUGEES AND STATELESS PERSONS, THROUGH ADMINISTRATIVE
            NATURALIZATION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”


Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as "The Administrative Naturalization for
Refugees and Stateless Persons Act.”

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – In compliance with the State’s obligations under the 1951
United Nations Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees and its 1967 Protocol, and the
1954 Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons and the 1961 Convention on the
Reduction of Statelessness, and considering the special status and vulnerable circumstances of
refugees and stateless persons, the State recognizes the need to enact measures to fulfil their
right to nationality as a durable solution. Towards this end, the State shall as far as possible
grant Philippine citizenship by facilitating administrative naturalization proceedings to
refugees and stateless persons duly recognized by the State, subject to the requirements as
herein provided, as dictated by national security and public interest.

Section 3. Definition of Terms.

   (a) "1951 U.N. Convention" refers to the 1951 United Nations Convention relating to the
       Status of Refugees;

   (b) "1967 Protocol" refers to the 1967 Protocol Relating to the Status of Refugees;

   (c) "1954 U.N. Convention" refers to the 1954 United Nations Convention relating to the
       Status of Stateless Persons;

   (d) “Dependent Child” is the legitimate, illegitimate or non-marital, or legally adopted
       child of a petitioner who is less than 18 years of age, or regardless of age, is suffering
       from restriction of different abilities, as a result of a mental, physical or sensory
       impairment, to perform an activity in the manner or within the range considered normal
       for a human being.



                                                                                      Page 2 of 12
   (e) "Refugee" is a person who owing to well-founded fear of being persecuted for reasons
       of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political
       opinion, is outside the country of his or her or their nationality and is unable or, owing
       to such fear, is unwilling to avail himself or herself or themselves of the protection of
       that country; or who, not having a nationality and being outside the country of his or
       her or their former habitual residence, is unable or, owing to such fear, is unwilling to
       return to it.

   (f) "Stateless Person" is a person who is not considered as a national by any State under
       the operation of its law.

   (g) “Recognized Refugee or Stateless Person by the Philippine Government” refers to
       petitioners who have been determined as a refugee or stateless by Department of Justice
       – Refugees and Stateless Persons Protection Unit or the appropriate government
       agency1;

   (h) “SCN” refers to the Special Committee on Naturalization constituted under Section 6
       of Republic Act 9139;

   (i) “Sufficient Income” is the income required for the refugee and stateless person to
       provide for their and their dependents’ basic needs.


   (j) “Unaccompanied Child” refers to a recognized refugee or stateless person who is under
       the age of eighteen who is separated from both parents and is not being cared for by an
       adult who by law or custom has responsibility to do so.

Section 4. Basic Principles. This Act shall be governed by the following basic principles:

   (a) Refugees and stateless persons are to be accorded the same respect and dignity as
       citizens of the Philippines, free from all forms of discrimination on account of race,
       color, gender, national origin, political view, religious belief, or physical or mental
       condition;

   (b) The unity of the family of refugees and stateless persons, particularly the protection of
       children, shall at all times be promoted and preserved.

       Unaccompanied children shall be referred to the appropriate government agencies
       and/or non-government organizations for their care, welfare, and access to legal
       services, including the filing of their petition for naturalization.

Section 5. Qualifications. - Subject to the provisions of the succeeding section, any person
desiring to avail of the benefits of this Act must meet the following qualifications:

   (a) The petitioner must be recognized by the Department of Justice – Refugees and
       Stateless Persons Protection Unit (DOJ-RSPPU) or the appropriate government agency
       as a refugee or stateless person;




                                                                                      Page 3 of 12
   (b) The petitioner must be at least 18 years of age at the time of filing of the application,
       except in the case of an unaccompanied child as defined in Section 3j hereof;

   (c) The petitioner must have habitually and actually resided in the Philippines for a
       minimum period of 5 years from the period of first arrival in the Philippines 2;

   (d) The petitioner must be able to communicate in Filipino, any local language of the
       Philippines, or, in the case of persons with disabilities, Filipino Sign Language 3;
       alternative procedures shall be undertaken to assess the condition and ability/ies of
       persons with disabilities pursuant to Republic Act 11106.

   (e) The petitioner must have assimilated with the Filipinos and evinced a sincere desire to
       learn and embrace the customs, traditions and ideals of the Filipino people;

   (f) The petitioner must be of good moral character and believes in the underlying principles
       of the Constitution, and must have conducted himself or herself or themselves in a
       proper and irreproachable manner during his or her or their entire period of residence
       in the Philippines in his or her or their relation with the duly constituted government,
       as well as with the community in which he/she is living;

   (g) The petitioner has enrolled his or her or their minor children of school age, in any of
       the public or private schools recognized by the appropriate agency, where Philippine
       history, government and civics are taught or prescribed as part of the school curriculum,
       during the entire required period of residence in the Philippines prior to the hearing of
       the petition; Provided, that this requirement is not applicable if the petitioner or his or
       her or their minor children were unable to reside in the Philippines during the children’s
       minority due to their status as refugee or stateless persons or other justifiable reasons;
       and4,

   (h) The petitioner must have a known and lawful trade, business, profession or occupation
       from which he or she or they derive sufficient income to support himself or herself or
       themselves and his or her or their family; except in the case of an unaccompanied child
       as defined in Section 3j hereof.


Section 6. Disqualifications. - The following are not qualified to be naturalized as Filipino
citizens under this Act:

   (a) Those opposed to organized government or affiliated with any association or group of
       persons which uphold and teach doctrines opposing any or all forms of organized
       government.

   (b) Those who are opposed to the ideals and policies of the Philippine government;




                                                                                       Page 4 of 12
   (c) Those defending or teaching the necessity of or propriety of violence, personal assault
       or assassination for the success or predominance of their ideas;

   (d) Those who have committed any act that may pose a threat to the national security of
       the Philippines;

   (e) Those who are suffering from mental illness with active symptoms resulting temporary
       impairment and decision-making capacity;


   (f) Those who failed to report their condition to local or state health authorities, as required
       for notifiable diseases, epidemics or public health events of public health concern at the
       time of the petition;

   (g) Those who have pending applications for naturalization or citizenship in another State
       unless such application has been denied or withdrawn with finality;

   (h) Those who have been granted citizenship in another State;

   (i) Those who have been convicted of a crime involving moral turpitude in the Philippine
       territory and sentenced to an imprisonment of more than six (6) years, or those who
       have been convicted more than once for similar crimes regardless of the penalty;

However, these disqualifications shall not be construed in a manner as to discriminate the
petitioner on account of the petitioner’s religion, culture, political opinion, physical or mental
condition. In the assessment of naturalization petitions, the circumstances and vulnerabilities
of refugees and stateless persons shall be taken into consideration.

Section 7. Petition for Citizenship. - (1) Any recognized refugee or stateless person by the
Philippine Government desiring to acquire Philippine citizenship under this Act shall file with
the Special Committee on Naturalization created under Section 6 of R.A. 9139, a petition of
five (5) copies, legibly typed and signed or thumbmarked and verified by him or her or them,
with the latter's passport-sized photograph attached to each copy of the petition, and setting
forth the following:

    (a) The petitioner's name and surname, as appearing in the recognition document, and any
        other name he or she or their has used or by which he or she or their is known;

    (b) The petitioner's present and former places of residence, including those prior to his or
        her or their application for naturalization, from the time of application for recognition
        as a refugee or stateless person;

    (c) The petitioner's place and date of birth;

    (d) If married, the date and place of their marriage, and the name, date of birth, birthplace,
        citizenship and residence of their spouse; and if their marriage is dissolved, by death
        of the spouse or otherwise, the facts of such death or dissolution;




                                                                                        Page 5 of 12
    (e) If the petitioner has children, the name, date and birthplace and residences of their
        children;

    (f) The petitioner's trade, business, profession or occupation, and if married, also that of
        their spouse;

    (g) A declaration that the petitioner is not a public charge;

    (h) A declaration that the petitioner possesses all the qualifications and none of the
        disqualifications under this Act;

    (i) A declaration that it is the petitioner's true and honest intention to acquire Philippine
        citizenship and to renounce absolutely and forever any prince, potentate, State or
        sovereign, and particularly the country of which the petitioner is a citizen or subject,
        except in the case where the petitioner is a recognized stateless person by the Philippine
        Government.

(2) The petition shall be accompanied by:

   (a) Duplicate original or certified photocopies of petitioner's birth certificate or affidavit
       stating the circumstances of his/her birth;

   (b) Duplicate original or certified true copy of the document certifying their refugee or
       stateless status;

   (c) Affidavit of financial capacity by the petitioner and his/her income tax returns for the
       past three (3) years, or any evidence proving sufficient income to support his/her family.
       An affidavit of financial support must be provided for the dependents;

   (d) Sworn statements on the good moral character of the petitioner by at least two (2)
       disinterested Filipino citizens of good reputation stating that they have personally
       known the petitioner for a period of at least two years and that said petitioner has in
       their own opinion all the qualifications necessary to become a citizen of the Philippines
       and is not in any way disqualified under the provisions of this Act;

   (e) Certification of non-forum shopping;

   (f) Clearance/s issued by the National Bureau of Investigation and/or by the Philippine
       National Police;

   (g) Other analogous documentary exhibits tending to provide that the petitioner is qualified
       to be naturalized.

For the unaccompanied child 5, the petition may be filed by the legal guardian or the Department
of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) Secretary, the appropriate Local Social Welfare
and Development Office (LSWDO) where the unaccompanied child resides, child-caring




                                                                                       Page 6 of 12
agency having care and custody of the child or the duly authorized representative, whichever
is applicable, in consideration of the best interest of the child. Provided that among others, the
existence or lack thereof of the citizenship of the child shall be considered.

If the petitioner is unable to provide the foregoing documentary exhibits, except for the
Declaration of Intention, if applicable, and the proof of recognition of refugee or stateless
status, the reason therefor shall be stated in the petition; Provided, that the inability to produce
said documentary exhibits shall not be a ground for the denial of the petition if the totality of
evidence is sufficient to establish that the petitioner meets all of the qualifications and does not
suffer any of the disqualifications as provided for in R.A. 9139.

Section 8. Filing of the Petition. - The petition for naturalization shall be filed with the
secretariat of the Special Committee on Naturalization (SCN). In the case of married
individuals, both spouses may file jointly. However, should the petitioner/s has/have dependent
children who, at the time of the filing of the petition, are already recognized as refugees or
stateless persons by the Philippine Government, they shall be included as petitioners for
derivative naturalization.

In cases where the alien spouse and minor children are recognized refugees or stateless persons
by the Philippine Government during, but prior to the resolution of the petition by the Special
Committee on Naturalization, the petition may be amended for the spouse to join in the
application and for the children to be included as petitioners for derivative naturalization.

Otherwise, the alien spouse may file their petition for naturalization as a principal petitioner.

In cases where the dependent children were recognized as refugees or stateless persons after
the administrative naturalization of either one of the parents, a petition for derivative
naturalization can be filed on their behalf.

Upon filing of the petition, a filing fee of Php 20,000 shall be assessed per principal petitioner,
and Php 10,000 for each dependent child. Thereafter, the petition shall be stamped to indicate
the date of filing and a corresponding docket number.

Section 9. Action on the Petition. - Within fifteen (15) days from the receipt of the petition,
the Committee shall determine whether the petition is complete in substance and in form.

If such petition is complete, the SCN shall immediately publish pertinent portions of the
petition indicating the name, qualifications and other personal circumstances of the petitioner,
once a week for three (3) consecutive weeks in a newspaper of general circulation, and have
copies of the petition posted in any public or conspicuous area.

The SCN shall immediately furnish the DOJ-RSPPU or the appropriate government agency,
Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA), the Bureau of Immigration (BI), the Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA), the local civil registrar (LCR) of the petitioner's place of residence, DSWD,
National Council for Disability Affairs (NCDA), and the National Bureau of Investigation
(NBI) copies of the petition and its supporting documents. These agencies shall have copies of
the petition posted in any public or conspicuous area in their buildings, offices and premises,
and shall, within thirty (30) days from the receipt of the petition, submit to the Committee a


                                                                                         Page 7 of 12
report stating whether or not petitioner has any derogatory record on file or any such relevant
and material information which might be adverse to petitioner's application for citizenship.

Within sixty (60) days from receipt of the report of the agencies which were furnished a copy
of the petition or the date of the last publication of the petition, whichever comes in later, the
SCN shall consider and review all relevant and material information it has received pertaining
to the petition, and may, for the purpose call the petitioner for interview to ascertain his or her
or their identity, the authenticity of the petition and its annexes, and to determine the
truthfulness of the statements and declarations made in the petition and its annexes.

If petitioner fails to appear for interview at least twice despite due notice or to submit additional
documentary requirements as required by the SCN notwithstanding the last paragraph of
Section 6 of this Act, the petition shall be dismissed without prejudice.
If the SCN does not receive a report from the agencies concerned within a period of ninety (90)
days from the receipt of the petition, it shall be deemed that the petitioner does not have a
derogatory record. In such case, the SCN may proceed with the processing of the petition
without prejudice to the subsequent receipt of such report from the appropriate agencies.
If the SCN shall have received any information adverse to the petition, the SCN shall allow the
petitioner to answer, explain or refute the information.
Section 10. Decision on the Petition. - If the SCN believes, in view of the facts before it, that
the petitioner has all the qualifications and none of the disqualifications required for Philippine
citizenship under this Act, it renders a decision granting the petition and henceforth, notify the
petitioner of the fact of such decision. Otherwise, the SCN shall deny the petition.

Copies of the decision shall be furnished to the following agencies:

    (a) DOJ-RSPPU or the appropriate government agency;
    (b) LCR;
    (c) PSA;
    (d) BI;
    (e) United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees;
    (f) In the case of a person with disability, the National Council on Disability Affairs
        (NCDA) and the Persons with Disability Affairs Office; and,
    (g) DSWD, in cases wherein such agency does not represent the petitioner who is an
        unaccompanied child.

Section 11. Oath of Allegiance. – Within a period of sixty (60) days or such period allowed by
the SCN not exceeding three (3) years, from the receipt of the notice of the approval of his/her
petition, the applicant shall:
    (a) Pay to the SCN a naturalization fee of fifteen thousand pesos (P 15,000); and
    (b) Upon payment, take the oath of allegiance to the Republic of the Philippines;




                                                                                          Page 8 of 12
Forthwith, a Certificate of Naturalization shall be issued. Should the petitioner fail to take the
abovementioned oath of allegiance within the said period of time and without justifiable cause,
the approval of the petition shall be deemed abandoned6.

Section 12. Remedies/Appeal Taken - In case of denial of the petition, the petitioner may seek
a reconsideration of the decision within thirty days (30) from receipt of the decision denying
the petition. Only one motion for reconsideration shall be allowed.

If the motion for reconsideration is denied, the petitioner may appeal to the Court of Appeals
under Rule 43 of the Rules of Court.

Section 13. Waiver/Adjustment of Fee - The SCN may adjust the fees as provided for in
Sections 7 and 9 hereof, taking into account the prevailing economic conditions and the
objectives of this Act.
The SCN may, in meritorious circumstances, exempt the payment of fees upon the application
of the petitioner.

Section 14. Change of Name. – The petition may pray for a change of name, including that of
the petitioner’s spouse(s) or child(ren) who are impleaded in the petition through administrative
naturalization or upon compliance with the requisites under Rule 103 of the Rules of Court.
The procedure for change of name through administrative means shall be provided in the
implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

Section 15. Duty of the Bureau of Immigration. - Within fifteen (15) days after the petitioner
has taken his or her or their oath of allegiance, the SCN shall forward copies of the petitioner’s
oath of allegiance and certificate of naturalization to the BI who shall cancel the alien certificate
of registration, if applicable, upon payment of the required cancellation fee. Thereafter, the BI
shall issue the Identification Certificate and forward a copy of the same along with copies of
the oath of allegiance, certificate of naturalization and order of cancellation of the alien
certificate of registration to the proper local civil registrar for recording purposes.

Section 16. Data Privacy and Data Collection. – Matters related to facilitated administrative
naturalization of refugees and stateless persons shall be kept strictly confidential and shall not
be disclosed directly or indirectly to anyone by the parties or participants in such proceedings.

If the SCN finds that disclosure of any information related to the petition is necessary for
security reasons or for other purposes connected to or arising out of the naturalization
proceedings, the SCN shall order the release of such information, restricting, however, the
purposes for which it shall be used and only upon the written consent of the petitioner.

The SCN shall regularly collect data in coordination with the DOJ-RSPPU, PSA, and other
relevant stakeholders. The collection, preservation, processing, and sharing of data shall be
conducted pursuant to R.A. No. 10173 and existing laws, rules, and regulations and in
adherence to the principles of transparency, legitimate purpose and proportionality and towards
the protection and durable solution of refugees and stateless persons.




                                                                                         Page 9 of 12
Section 17. Cancellation or Revocation of the Certificate of Naturalization. - The SCN shall,
subject to due process of law, cancel or revoke a certificate of naturalization issued under this
Act in any of the following cases:

        (a) If it finds that the naturalized person or his or her or their duly authorized
        representative made any false statement or misrepresentation or committed any
        violation of law, rules and regulations in connection with the petition for naturalization,
        or if he or she or their otherwise obtains Philippine citizenship fraudulently or illegally;

        (b) If the naturalized person who acquire Filipino citizenship by virtue of his or her or
        their naturalization shall, within five (5) years following the grant of Philippine
        citizenship, establish permanent residence in a foreign country; Provided, that the fact
        of such person’s remaining for more than one (1) year in his or her or their country of
        former nationality or habitual residence, or two (2) years in any foreign country, shall
        be considered prima facie evidence of intent to permanently reside herein;

        (c) If the naturalized person allows himself or herself or themselves to be used as a
        dummy in violation of any constitutional or legal provision requiring Philippine
        citizenship as a condition for the exercise, use or enjoyment of a right, franchise or
        privilege; and,

        (d) If the naturalized person commits any act inimical to national security.

The cancellation or revocation shall only affect the person who caused the act considered as
ground for the cancellation or revocation.

Section 18. Effect of Removal, Deportation, or Termination of Refugee or Stateless Status.
– Removal or deportation from the Philippines or termination of refugee or stateless status 7
shall not be a ground for the immediate dismissal of the petition, but the reason for such
removal or deportation, or termination of refugee or stateless status may be presented as ground
for the denial of the petition; Provided, that the SCN shall have no authority to prevent the
implementation of the removal or deportation order.

If the refugee or stateless status of the petitioner is terminated, this Rule shall automatically
cease to apply and the petitioner shall inform the SCN within fifteen (15) calendar days from
the notice of finality thereof whether the petitioner would like to proceed under regular
naturalization proceedings.

Section 19. Penalties. - Any person who shall fraudulently make, falsify, forge, change, alter,
or cause or aid any person to do the same, or who shall purposely aid and assist in falsely
making, forging, falsifying, changing or altering a naturalization certificate issued under this
proceeding for the purpose of making use thereof, or in order that the same may be used by
another person or persons, and any person who shall purposely aid and assist another in
obtaining a naturalization certificate in violation of this Act, shall be punished by a fine of not
more than five hundred thousand pesos (P 500,000.00) and by imprisonment for not more than
five (5) years, and in the case that the person convicted is a naturalized citizen, his or her or
their certificate of naturalization shall, if not earlier cancelled by the SCN, be ordered cancelled.




                                                                                        Page 10 of 12
Section 20. Disposition of the Filing Fees. - All filing fees shall accrue exclusively to the
funds of the SCN which amount shall be used for its operations. Said amount shall be treated
as receipts automatically appropriated.

Section 21. Special Committee on Naturalization Secretariat. The SCN created under R.A.
No. 9139, otherwise known as “The Administrative Naturalization Law of 2000” shall have a
Secretariat under the Office of the Solicitor General which shall be under the direct
administrative supervision and control of the Solicitor General as chairperson of the SCN. The
Secretariat shall be composed of such number of legal and administrative staff, as may be
determined by the SCN.

The legal staff of the Secretariat shall be designated by the Solicitor General from the full
complement of the Office of the Solicitor General and shall be entitled to receive monthly
honoraria in the amount to be determined by the Committee, which shall in no case exceed five
percent (5%) of their basic salary 8. For this purpose, the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM) shall promulgate the necessary guidelines. The regular administrative
staff shall be appointed by the Solicitor General and shall receive salary as provided for in the
relevant civil service laws.

Section 22. Honoraria of the Chairperson and Members of the SCN.- The honoraria of the
Chairperson and the members of the Committee as provided for in Section 6 of Republic Act
9139 shall be increased to Php 6,000 and Php 5,000 respectively per meeting attended.

Section 23. Monitoring and Evaluation and Training. – The SCN shall ensure that this Act
is effectively implemented. For this purpose, the SCN and its Secretariat, in coordination with
relevant stakeholders, shall undertake periodic monitoring of the naturalization petitions of
refugees and stateless persons. The SCN Secretariat shall conduct regular trainings for its
personnel and the OSG.

Section 24. Information Dissemination. – The SCN, in coordination with relevant
stakeholders shall conceptualize, conduct, and coordinate information campaigns on the
provisions and implementation of this Act.

Section 25. Funding. To carry out the provisions of this Act and of Republic Act No. 9139,
an initial fund of Php 50,000,000 is hereby appropriated for the operations of the SCN
Secretariat. Thereafter, such amount as may be deemed necessary for the annual operations of
the Secretariat shall be incorporated and included in the annual budgetary appropriations of the
Office of the Solicitor General. The appropriations provided herein shall be in addition to the
fees collected by the SCN as provided for in this law and in R.A. No. 9139.

Section 26. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The SCN, in consultation with the DOJ-
RSPPU, DFA, BI, NCDA, DSWD, PSA, DBM, non-government organizations, UNHCR and
other UN agencies, shall promulgate such rules and regulations within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act.

Section 27. Repealing Clause. - All provisions of existing laws, orders, decrees, rules and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.
