                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Article XII, Section 2 of the 1987 Constitution declares that all natural
resources are owned by the State and that their exploration, development,
and utilization shall be under its full control and supervision for the benefit
of the Filipino people. Complementing this mandate, Article II, Section 16
directs the State to protect and advance the people's right to a balanced
and healthful ecology. These constitutional principles underscore the duty
of government to harness the country's abundant renewable energy
resources in a manner that promotes economic development,
environmental sustainability, and the general welfare of the people.

      The Philippines is among the countries most richly endowed with
solar energy. Its tropical location provides abundant sunlight throughout the
year, making solar power one of the nation's most accessible and
inexhaustible natural resources. At the same time, the country continues to
face rising electricity demand driven by economic growth, urbanization,
industrial expansion, and digital transformation. Despite significant
advances in renewable energy technology, many Filipino households and
businesses remain dependent on increasingly costly and volatile sources of
electricity, resulting in higher living costs and reduced business
competitiveness.

     Unlike conventional power generation, solar energy possesses a
uniquely democratic character. The sun shines on every home, farm,
school, business, and public building regardless of location or income.
Rooftops that once served only as shelter can become productive assets
capable of generating clean and affordable electricity. Properly harnessed,
solar energy enables ordinary consumers to reduce their electricity
expenses, improve their energy security, and participate directly in the
country's transition toward a more sustainable energy future.

      The principal obstacle to widespread solar adoption is no longer the
technology itself, but its upfront cost. While solar energy systems generate
substantial savings over their operational life, the initial investment remains
beyond the financial reach of many Filipino families, farmers, micro, small,
and medium enterprises, and institutions. Consequently, the benefits of
solar energy have largely been confined to those with sufficient capital to
absorb the initial expense.

       This measure seeks to democratize access to solar energy by
reducing these financial barriers through a comprehensive package of
fiscal incentives. It establishes a Solar Investment Tax Credit, additional
income tax deductions, exemptions from Value-Added Tax and Real
Property Tax for qualified installations, duty-free importation of eligible solar
equipment, and incentives to encourage domestic manufacturing of solar
technologies. These measures are intended to make solar energy
ownership more affordable while stimulating investments, creating
employment, strengthening local manufacturing, and expanding the
country's renewable energy industry.

      More importantly, this bill recognizes that fiscal incentives are not
merely tax expenditures but strategic investments in the nation's long-term
economic and energy future. Every solar installation reduces pressure on
the electric grid, lowers dependence on imported fossil fuels, strengthens
energy resilience, and contributes to a cleaner environment. The resulting
savings for consumers, increased private investment, and broader
economic activity have the potential to generate benefits that substantially
outweigh the foregone revenues from the incentives provided under this
Act.
       Throughout history, transformative technologies have reshaped
societies only after they became accessible to ordinary citizens. Electricity,
telecommunications, and digital technologies accelerated economic
development because public policy encouraged their widespread adoption.
Solar energy presents a similar opportunity. By lowering the cost of
adoption through well-designed fiscal incentives, government can
accelerate the transition toward a future where clean, affordable, and
reliable electricity is within the reach of every Filipino.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is
earnestly sought.

                          AN ACT
  ESTABLISHING FISCAL INCENTIVES FOR THE ACQUISITION AND
INSTALLATION OF SOLAR ENERGY SYSTEMS, AMENDING FOR THE
 PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9513, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
 "RENEWABLE ENERGY ACT OF 2008," THE NATIONAL INTERNAL
     REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED, AND THE LOCAL
    GOVERNMENT CODE OF 1991, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Solar Energy
Incentives Act of 2026.”

       Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to promote the widespread adoption of solar energy systems by
reducing the financial cost of acquiring and installing such systems through
a comprehensive package of fiscal incentives. Toward this end, the State
shall:
     (a) encourage households, businesses, agricultural enterprises,
     institutions, and government entities to invest in solar energy
     systems;

     (b) reduce electricity costs through increased utilization of solar
     energy;

     (c) promote domestic manufacturing and assembly of solar energy
     equipment;

     (d) stimulate investments, employment, and technological innovation
     in the renewable energy sector; and

     (e) contribute to national energy security and environmental
     sustainability through increased use of clean and renewable energy.

     Sec 3. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall mean:

     (a) Accredited Solar Provider refers to any person, partnership,
     corporation, cooperative, or entity duly accredited by the Department
     of Energy (DOE) in accordance with standards jointly prescribed by
     the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), the Energy Regulatory
     Commission (ERC), and other concerned agencies to design, supply,
     install, operate, or maintain solar energy systems.

     (b) Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) refers to equipment or
     technologies capable of storing electrical energy for subsequent use,
     including batteries, battery management systems, inverters, and
     associated control systems.

     (c) Commercial Establishment refers to any business establishment
     engaged in trade, commerce, services, tourism, hospitality, office
     operations, or similar commercial activities.

     (f) End-User refers to any residential, commercial, industrial,
     agricultural, institutional, or government consumer of electricity.

     (g) Energy Storage System refers to technologies capable of
     absorbing, storing, and releasing electrical energy for later use,
     including battery storage, thermal storage, and other technologies
     recognized by the DOE.
      (i) Household Solar System refers to a rooftop or ground-mounted
      solar photovoltaic system installed in a residential property primarily
      to generate electricity for household consumption, whether operating
      as a grid-connected, hybrid, or off-grid system.

      (o) Solar Energy Equipment refers to solar photovoltaic panels,
      modules, mounting structures, inverters, batteries, charge controllers,
      smart meters, wiring systems, electrical protection devices,
      monitoring systems, and other components necessary for the safe
      and efficient operation of a solar energy system.

      (p) Solar Energy System refers to an integrated system utilizing solar
      energy for the generation, storage, monitoring, and management of
      electricity, including photovoltaic modules, inverters, battery storage
      systems, mounting structures, electrical wiring, protection devices,
      monitoring equipment, and associated facilities.

      (q) Solar Investment Tax Credit refers to the income tax credit
      granted under this Act equivalent to a prescribed percentage of the
      qualified cost of acquiring and installing eligible solar energy systems.

       Sec 4. Solar Investment Tax Credit. Any qualified taxpayer who
acquires and installs a solar energy system certified by the Department of
Energy shall be entitled to a non-refundable income tax credit equivalent to
fifty percent (50%) of the qualified installation cost.

      The tax credit may be claimed beginning in the taxable year in which
the system is commissioned and may be carried forward for the next five
(5) consecutive taxable years until fully utilized.

      The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), in consultation with the DOE
and DOF, shall prescribe the rules governing eligibility, documentation,
carry-over, and administration of the Solar Investment Tax Credit.

      Sec 5. Qualified Installation Costs. Qualified installation costs shall
include:

      (a) solar photovoltaic panels or modules;

      (b) battery energy storage systems;

      (c) smart inverters;
     (d) mounting structures;

     (e) electrical wiring and protection systems;

     (f) smart meters;

     (g) monitoring and control systems;

     (h) engineering and design services;

     (i) transportation and installation costs;

     (j) testing and commissioning expenses;

     (k) permits and inspection fees; and

     (l) such other equipment and expenses as may be determined by the DOE.

      Only systems installed by accredited solar providers shall qualify for
incentives under this Act.

       Sec 6. Additional Income Tax Deductions. In addition to the Solar
Investment Tax Credit, qualified commercial, industrial, agricultural, and
institutional taxpayers shall be entitled to the following deductions:

     (a) accelerated depreciation of solar energy equipment over a period
     not exceeding five (5) years;

     (b) immediate deduction of expenses for repair and maintenance
     directly attributable to qualified solar systems;

     (c) deduction of expenses incurred for workforce training on solar
     system operations and maintenance; and

     (d) enhanced deduction for research and development expenditures
     related to solar energy technologies.

      The DOF, upon recommendation of the DOE and the BIR, may
prescribe additional allowable deductions consistent with the objectives of
this Act.

       Sec 7. Value-Added Tax (VAT) Exemption. The sale, importation,
installation, repair, maintenance, replacement, and upgrading of accredited
solar energy systems and battery energy storage systems shall be exempt
from Value-Added Tax (VAT), subject to existing customs, taxation, and
accreditation requirements.

     The VAT exemption shall likewise apply to component parts certified
by the DOE as necessary for the safe and efficient operation of solar
energy systems.

      Sec 8. Exemption from Real Property Tax. Consistent with the
policy of encouraging distributed renewable energy generation, all
permanent improvements consisting exclusively of solar energy systems,
battery energy storage systems, mounting structures, electrical equipment,
smart meters, and associated renewable energy facilities installed for own
use shall be exempt from the assessment and payment of Real Property
Tax.

      The assessed value of the land and principal structure shall remain
subject to applicable real property taxes; however, the value attributable
solely to the renewable energy improvements shall not be included in
determining the assessed value of the property.

       No province, city, municipality, or barangay shall impose additional
local taxes, assessments, surcharges, or special levies specifically on
qualified solar energy systems covered by this Act.

       Sec 9. Duty-Free Importation. The importation of solar photovoltaic
modules, smart inverters, battery energy storage systems, specialized
mounting structures, monitoring equipment, and other equipment certified
by the DOE as not locally manufactured in sufficient quantity or quality shall
be exempt from customs duties for a period of ten (10) years from the
effectivity of this Act.

      The Bureau of Customs, in coordination with the DOE and DTI, shall
publish and annually update the list of eligible equipment.

      Sec 10. Incentives for Local Manufacturing. To strengthen domestic
manufacturing capabilities and develop a competitive Philippine solar
industry, qualified manufacturers, assemblers, fabricators, and producers of
solar energy equipment shall be entitled to:

      (a) enhanced deductions for capital investments;

      (b) tax incentives for research, development, and product innovation;
     (c) duty exemptions on imported manufacturing machinery and
     equipment;

     (e) workforce training incentives;

     Preference shall be given to enterprises manufacturing equipment
that complies with Philippine National Standards and internationally
recognized quality certifications.

       Sec 11. Periodic Review of Fiscal Incentives. Within five (5) years
from the effectivity of this Act, and every five (5) years thereafter, the
Department of Finance, in coordination with the Department of Energy and
the Bureau of Internal Revenue, shall evaluate the fiscal impact and
effectiveness of the incentives established under this Act. The review shall
include:

     (a) total incentives granted;

     (b) number and classification of beneficiaries;

     (c) installed solar capacity attributable to the incentives;

     (d) estimated foregone revenues;

     (e) electricity cost savings realized by beneficiaries; and

     (f) recommendations for the continuation, modification, or termination
     of the incentives.

      The Department of Finance, through the Bureau of Internal Revenue
and the Bureau of Customs, in coordination with the Department of Energy,
shall submit an annual report to the President of the Philippines and to both
Houses of Congress not later than June 30 of each year.

      SEC. 12. Non-Duplication of Incentives. No qualified expenditure
shall be entitled to multiple fiscal incentives under this Act and any other
law for the same purpose, unless expressly authorized by law.

      A taxpayer who qualifies for incentives under more than one statute
shall elect which incentive to avail of prior to filing the corresponding tax
return or application.

     Such election shall be irrevocable for the taxable year concerned.
      SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within one
hundred twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of
Finance (DOF), through the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and the
Bureau of Customs (BOC), in coordination with the Department of Energy
(DOE), the Bureau of Local Government Finance (BLGF), the Department
of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and such other agencies as
may be necessary, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for
the effective implementation of this Act.

      SEC. 14. Transitory Provision. Applications for fiscal incentives
pending before the Department of Energy, the Bureau of Internal Revenue,
the Bureau of Customs, or any other concerned government agency upon
the effectivity of this Act shall continue to be processed under the law and
regulations in force at the time the application was filed, unless the
applicant elects to be governed by the provisions of this Act.

     The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe the
procedures governing such election.

      SEC. 15. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby
shall continue to be in full force and effect.

      SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

      SEC. 17. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
