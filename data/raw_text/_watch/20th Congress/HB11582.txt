AN ACT

PROMOTING RESEARCH ON THE PRESENCE OF MICROPLASTICS IN THE
HUMAN BODY, FOOD, DRINKING WATER, AND THE ENVIRONMENT

EXPLANATORY NOTE

Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution declares that the State shall protect and
promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.
Likewise, Article XIII, Section 12 mandates the State to undertake appropriate health

manpower development and research responsive to the country's health needs and problems.

In recent years, microplastics have emerged as one of the most critical public health
concerns in the world due to the worldwide increase in consumption of plastic-based products.
Scientific research conducted in different countries have detected the presence of these
microplastics in food packaging material, salt, drinking water, contaminated seafood,
agricultural products, and even infiltrating our bodies, such as in our blood, lungs, placentas,
breast milk, and even brain tissue. While research is still ongoing, evidence suggests that

prolonged exposure to these microplastics may have implications for human health, including

The Philippines, being one of the world's largest users and contributors of plastic waste
leakage into the marine environment’, faces heightened risks of human exposure to
microplastics through food, water, and air. Despite this, there remains very limited local
scientific research on microplastic prevalence in tissues and organs. The precise entry points
of microplastics are unknown, and their possible health consequences are yet unclear in the
Philippine context. Due to this research gap, government agencies currently lack sufficient data
to formulate specific evidence-based policies. This proposed measure seeks to establish a
National Microplastics Research Program to coordinate scientific studies on microplastics in
the environment, food systems, drinking water, and the human body. It does not impose
regulatory burdens on any sector. Rather, it is solely a research initiative anchored on the
principle that effective policy must first be grounded on sound science. By investing in research
today, the government can better protect public health, strengthen environmental management,
and ensure that future decisions concerning plastic pollution are informed by credible and

locally generated evidence.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

HOUSE BILL NO. 11582

AN ACT

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title — This Act shall be known as the “National Microplastics
Research Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right to health of the people and to undertake scientific research responsive to
emerging environmental and public health concerns. The State recognizes that microplastics
and nanoplastics have emerged as contaminants of global concern and a major threat to
ecosystems and public health, as acknowledged by key environmental agencies including the
Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) and the Climate Change
Commission.’ As studies have detected their presence in biological samples such as human
blood, lungs, placentas, and brain tissue, and given that sufficient local scientific evidence on
the extent of Filipino exposure remains lacking, the State shall promote, institutionalize, and
support precision research and monitoring to provide a sound scientific basis for future policies
and interventions.

(a) Microplastics refers to plastic particles measuring less than five (5) millimeters in
any dimension originating from the breakdown of larger plastic materials or intentionally
manufactured at such size;

(b) Nanoplastics refers to plastic particles generally measuring less than one (1)
micrometer;

(c) Human biomonitoring refers to the scientific assessment of human exposure to
environmental contaminants through analysis of biological samples;

(d) Program refers to the National Microplastics Research Program established under
Section 4 of this Act; and

(e) Steering Committee refers to the National Microplastics Research Steering
Committee established under Section 9 of this Act.

SEC. 4. Establishment of the National Microplastics Research Program. — There is
hereby established a National Microplastics Research Program, to be administered by the
Department of Science and Technology (DOST), as lead ageney, in coordination with the
Department of Health (DOH), the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR),
the Department of Agriculture (DA), the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the Climate
Change Commission (CCC), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), state universities
and colleges (SUCs) — including the University of the Philippines Marine Science Institute
(UP MSI) — and other relevant research institutions. The Program shall serve as the primary
national mechanism for the conduct, coordination, and support of scientific research on
microplastics and nanoplastics in the Philippines.SEC. 5. Scope of Research Activities. The
Program shall undertake, support, and coordinate scientific studies on the following:

(a) the occurrence, quantification, and tracking of microplastics in drinking water,
waterways, marine ecosystems, agricultural products, fisheries, and processed food;

(b) the presence and concentration of microplastics in human biological samples
including blood, urine, placental tissues, breast milk, and respiratory tissues, subject to
applicable ethical standards;

(c) potential relationships between microplastic exposure and human health outcomes;

(d) the identification of vulnerable populations and priority exposure pathways; and

(e) the establishment and enhancement of specialized laboratory facilities, monitoring
technologies, and testing methodologies in collaboration with national research centers and
international entities.

SEC. 7. National Microplastics Research Network and Database. — The DOST,
through its scientific research councils and in consultation with key environmental agencies,
shall:

(a) establish a National Microplastics Research Network composed of participating
government agencies, SUCs, research institutions, accredited laboratories, and relevant
stakeholders to facilitate collaboration, resource sharing, training, and scientific exchange; and

(b) establish and maintain a centralized National Microplastics Database containing
research findings, monitoring results, and exposure assessments generated under this Act,
accessible to government agencies, researchers, and the public subject to applicable data
privacy and research ethics laws.

SEC. 8. Annual Report. — The DOST and DOH shall jointly submit an annual report
to the President of the Philippines, the Senate, and the House of Representatives detailing the
status of implementation, significant scientific findings, identified research gaps, emerging
health concerns, and policy recommendations arising from the Program.

SEC. 9. National Microplastics Research Steering Committee. — A National
Microplastics Research Steering Committee is hereby created, chaired by the Secretary of
Science and Technology and co-chaired by the Secretary of Health. Its members shall include
the following:

(a) Secretaries of DENR and DA;
(b) Vice Chairperson/Executive Director of the Climate Change Commission
(CCC);
(c) Director-General of the FDA;
(d) Two (2) representatives from SUCs engaged in scientific research to be
designated by CHED;
(e) One (1) representative from the medical research community to be designated
by the DOH;
(f) One (1) representative from a recognized environmental or public health
organization, to be designated by the DOST.
The Steering Committee shall formulate national research priorities, approve strategic research
agendas, facilitate inter-agency coordination, monitor Program implementation, and

SEC. 10. International Cooperation. — The DOST and DOH, in coordination with the
Department of Foreign Affairs, may enter into memoranda of agreement or other cooperative
arrangements with foreign governments, international organizations, academic institutions, and
scientific bodies for research collaboration, technical assistance, capacity building, technology
transfer, and knowledge exchange relating to microplastics research, subject to existing laws,
rules, and regulation on international agreements.

SEC. 11. Congressional Oversight — The Committee on Science and Technology of
the Senate and the Committee on Science and Technology of the House of Representatives
shall jointly exercise oversight over the implementation of this Act. They may conduct
oversight hearings, require the Steering Committee and relevant government agencies to
submit reports, and recommend appropriate legislative or administrative measures to address
gaps in Program implementation.

SEC. 12. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOST. Thereafter, such sums
as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOST and DOH, in consultation with other concerned government
agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary Implementing Rules and
Regulations.

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid by a competent court, the remaining provisions shall remain in full
force and effect.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication

in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
