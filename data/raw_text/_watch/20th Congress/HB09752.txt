20              CREATING PROGRAMS FOR INCARCERATED PARENTS
21                          AND THEIR CHILDREN
22
23
24                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
25
26            Persons deprived of liberty (PDLs) are forced to leave their children during the
27   time that they are in detention or commitment. As a result, most of the children of those
28   in Philippine jails and detention facilities are deprived of much of the basic needs for
29   their physical, mental, moral, and psychological growth and development. Our country
30   lacks mechanisms and facilities for detained and committed parents to still enjoy their
31   parental rights, and for the needs of their children to be attended to. Prison walls and
32   jail bars must never negate the best interests of the child, even as their mother or father
33   is still innocent in the eyes of the law, and even when the latter is already sentenced.
34
35          This need to care for incarcerated parents and their children is made more
36   obvious and dire in the case of mothers, especially those who are expecting or nursing,
37   and solo parents who oftentimes have no relatives to whom the rearing of their children
38   could be entrusted.
39
40          The Philippine government has obligations under several international law
41   instruments on the rights of the child, women’s rights, and cruel and inhumane
42   punishment to name a few, to prevent the suffering of parents in jail and their children.
43   Take the cases of Reina Mae Nasino and baby River, and Amanda Echanis and baby
44   Randall Emmanuel. Both mothers are political prisoners arrested amid the raging times
                                                                                                           1
 1   of COVID-19 pandemic. Until now, Echanis is still suffering under fabricated charges.
 2   Meanwhile, in July 2023, the Manila Regional Trial Court Branch 47 dismissed the case
 3   of Nasino and her companions due to lack of evidence. However, her acquittal is years
 4   late since baby River passed away in 2020. Brought by the insufficient prenatal and
 5   post-natal care and the stress caused to Nasino throughout her pregnancy and childbirth,
 6   baby River contracted pneumonia then died at three months old. The court and elements
 7   of the Philippine National Police denied Nasino the right to mourn her daughter in
 8   peace, granting her just a total of six hours to go to her daughter’s wake and making her
 9   attend the funeral in handcuffs, closely surrounded by a score of police officers. A case
10   has also been filed in the Ombudsman in relation to the “hijacking” of baby River’s
11   remains by police forces.
12
13          Echanis, herself a child of incarcerated parents when both her parents became
14   political prisoners in the 1990s, was recently arrested with her newborn son, Randall
15   Emmanuel. As planted arms and explosives assured a non-bailable—yet trumped-up—
16   charge against her, she is now faced with the choice of nursing her newborn in detention
17   or being separated from him, totally unable to nurse him.
18
19          It should be noted, however, that Echanis herself was allowed to stay with her
20   mother, Linda Lacaba Echanis, and also her father, slain Anakpawis leader and National
21   Democratic Front of the Philippines peace consultant Randall Echanis, when both were
22   arrested in August 1990. Government and custodial authorities consented for
23   humanitarian reasons to Amanda’s confinement at Camp Crame in the political
24   prisoner’s wing so that Linda could continue breastfeeding Amanda who was 1 year
25   and 10 months old, which was still short of the two-year minimum period set by WHO
26   guidelines for breastfeeding for survival. Both Linda and Amanda stayed in Camp
27   Crame until Linda was released after six months. In effect, this establishes a
28   humanitarian as well as instructive precedence for the protection of babies of
29   incarcerated mothers.
30
31         “Just and humanitarian considerations” should be enough to accord these
32   mothers, and other parents in jail, and their children the rights to a family, and the love
33   and nurturing of a family. However, current institutions do not.
34
35          This proposed bill aims to establish mechanisms that shall assist imprisoned
36   parents, especially mothers and solo parents, in the performance of their parental and
37   child-rearing obligations particularly during the early and formative years of the child.
38
39         This Bill was originally filed in the 18th Congress by Senator Maria Lourdes
40   Nancy S. Binay, as well as Representative Myra Salvador-Tambunting, Representative
41   Manuel Cabochan and the Party-List Representatives of the Makabayang Koalisyon ng
42   mga Mamamayan (Makabayan).

19                                AN ACT
20              CREATING PROGRAMS FOR INCARCERATED PARENTS
21                          AND THEIR CHILDREN
22
23
24   Be it enacted in the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
25   Congress assembled:
26
27   SECTION 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as “Parents in Jail Act.”
28
29   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State that:
30
31         (1) The State shall defend the rights of children to special protection from all
32   forms of neglect and other conditions prejudicial to their development;
33
34           (2) The State recognizes that all persons deprived of their liberty shall be treated
35   humanely and shall enjoy all the rights guaranteed to free persons, subject to the
36   restrictions that are unavoidable in a closed environment;
37
38          (3) The State shall promote stable and solid relationships between children and
39   parents, who are their primary care-givers and support incarcerated parents to develop
40   and maintain their relationship with their children;
41
42          The State shall assure the right of the child to express his or her views freely in
43   all matters affecting them, the views of the child being given due weight in accordance
44   with their age and maturity.
                                                                                                           1
 1
 2   SEC. 3. Apprising Incarcerated Parents of Their Parental Rights. – In cases
 3   involving incarcerated parents, the court must give an appropriate warning of the special
 4   consequences concerning the parental rights of the accused that may result from a plea
 5   of guilty or conviction, especially the loss of parental authority.
 6
 7   SEC. 4. Placement and Guardianship Options. – After arraignment and upon plea of
 8   guilty, the court shall inquire from the accused if the latter has any minor children and
 9   in whose custody the minor children are. In the event that the accused has minor
10   children, the court shall motu proprio direct a social worker or a representative of the
11   Department of Social Welfare and Development to confer with the accused to discuss
12   various options available to the latter, in relation to the care and custody of their minor
13   children. A compliance report, duly signed by the accused, shall thereafter be submitted
14   to the court, attesting that options have been discussed and the selected option, if any.
15
16         Should the accused be unable to place their minor children under the care of a
17   responsible adult, the minor children shall be placed under the care of the following
18   persons, in the order indicated:
19
20          1.     surviving grandparent;
21          2.     oldest brother or sister, over twenty-one (21) years of age, unless unfit or
22   disqualified;
23          3.     any collateral relative of the accused, over twenty-one (21) years of age,
24   and within the fourth civil degree of consanguinity or affinity of the minor children; and
25          4.     the Department of Social Welfare and Development.
26
27   SEC. 5. Coordinating Body. – A coordinating body, composed of representatives from
28   the Department of Justice, the Bureau of Corrections, the Department of Social Welfare
29   and Development, and the Department of Health, shall be established for purposes of
30   implementing the provisions of this Act.
31
32          Such body shall:
33
34         a.      formulate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
35   implementation of this Act;
36         b.      provide for specific procedures for the placement of children of the
37   accused or for infants born to women already incarcerated in state prisons; and
38
39          c.      establish programs designed to provide counseling, health and other
40   social services to the incarcerated parents and their children. Access of mothers,
41   including those who are expecting and nursing, and children to health care services and
42   medicines, including reproductive health care and medical attention, gynecological and
43   pediatric services shall be assured by the institution where parent and child are detained.
44   For this purpose, all jails and detention facilities shall comply with the mandates of

                                                                                              2
 1   Republic Act 11148 or the “Kalusugan at Nutrisyon ng Mag-Nanay Act” and other
 2   relevant laws. The use of restraints on pregnant women and women who have just given
 3   birth is hereby declared as torture and cruel and degrading punishment.
 4
 5   SEC. 6. Child-Friendly Visitation Programs. – The prison facilities shall establish
 6   child-friendly visitation opportunities for solo parents and children. A special visitation
 7   room for solo parents and children shall be provided for this purpose.
 8
 9   SEC. 7. Independent Monitoring. – Frequent access to prisons shall be granted to
10   relevant institutions such as the Commission on Human Rights, non-governmental
11   human rights organizations, and international organizations. Persons conducting
12   monitoring tasks shall be allowed to make periodic and unannounced visits to the
13   premises and speak in private with imprisoned solo parents and their children.
14
15   SEC. 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this
16   Act is hereby authorized to be appropriated in the budget of the Department of Social
17   Welfare and Development and included in the General Appropriations Act of the year
18   following the enactment of this law and every year thereafter.
19
20   SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provisions of this Act is declared void
21   and unconstitutional, the remaining portions or provisions hereof shall not be affected
22   by such declaration.
23
24   SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations, other
25   issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
26   repealed or modified accordingly.
27
28   SEC. 11. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
29   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
