                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE


        It has been more than 25 years since the passage of Republic Act No. 9136 or
the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA). While this measure restructured
the energy sector in the country to ensure reliable electric supply, crucial
amendments thereto are said to be long overdue to be responsive to the realities
faced by Filipino households.

       One of the main burdens of the Filipino Household is the rising electricity
costs, with the Philippines recorded to have the highest electricity rates in Southeast
Asia1. One of the significant factor to this burden is the practice of on system loss
charges to end-users. Under Section 43(f) of EPIRA, distribution utilities (DUs) and
electric cooperatives (ECs) are legally allowed to pass system loss charges directly
onto end-users. While Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) enforces a recoverable
system loss cap for private DUs at 5.5% to 6.5%, other ECs, particularly those
operating across Mindanao and rural provinces, are permitted pass-through caps of
up to 12.0% to 12.5%. This disparity places an unequal financial strain on households
in rural areas and Mindanao, where consumers pay significantly higher bills to cover
operational inefficiencies, technical line losses, and non-technical losses like power
pilferage and theft.

       Because these losses fall squarely within the operational control of
distributors, current policy unfairly socializes business failures by shifting the cost
onto captive consumers who receive no benefit from energy they never consumed.

       To address this, this measure seeks to reform the system loss pass-through
framework to protect end-users and mandate true utility accountability. Through the
removal of the system loss pass-through charges, we protect consumer interest and
encouraging DUs and ECs to protecting the interest of the Filipino consumers by
ensuring that they no longer pay for “ghost electricity” due to technical and non-
technical system losses, and (2) encourage the DUs and ECs to improve operations
through investment of modern infrastructures and efficient technologies to minimize
or eliminate operational losses and inefficiencies.

       By requiring DUs and ECs to absorb operational inefficiencies rather than
passing them on to end-users, this bill restores fairness to power pricing and
safeguards the welfare of the Filipino households.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT REMOVING SYSTEMS LOSS AS A SEPARATE COMPONENT OF
 ELECTRICITY BILLS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTIONS 25 AND 43
  OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “ELECTRIC
              POWER INDUSTRY REFORM ACT OF 2001”

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as “Systems Loss Char ge
Abolition Act”

         Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to ensure the
affordability of the supply of electric power, ensure transparent and reasonable prices of
electricity in a fair competition and full public accountability, and protect the consumer
interest against the operational inefficiencies and electricity losses attributable to distribution
utilities, electric cooperative, and other industry actors.

         Section 3. Prohibition on the Pass-Through of System Loss. – No distribution
utility or electric cooperative shall bill, collect or otherwise pass on any system loss charge or
equivalent charges, whether as a separate line item, as a component of any other charge, or in
any other form or denomination, to end-users

       All system losses shall be treated as part of the operational responsibility of the
concerned distribution utility, subject to performance standards established by the Energy
Regulatory Commission.

         Section 6. Implementing Rules. – Within ninety (90) calendar days from the
effectivity of this Act, the ERC, in consultation with the Department of Energy, distribution
utilities, consumer groups, and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations
necessary for its implementation.

        The rules shall provide for the determination of prudent and reasonable costs
necessarily incurred in the efficient operation of distribution systems, the disallowance of
costs attributable to causes within the control of distribution utilities, and a phased transition
not exceeding three (3) years from the effectivity of the rules.

        Section 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in full
force and effect.

        Section 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, decrees, rules and
regulations, or any part thereof inconsistent with the provision of this Act are deemed
repealed or modified accordingly.

        Section 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
