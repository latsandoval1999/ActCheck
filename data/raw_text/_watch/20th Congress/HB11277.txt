                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to establish a National Affordable Student Loan Program,
which offers a sustainable, low-interest student financing system for Filipino
learners. It provides affordable education loans with a fixed interest rate, flexible
repayment terms, and borrower protection mechanisms to complement existing
government scholarship and financial assistance programs.

       While the government has expanded access to free tertiary education through
Republic Act No. 10931, otherwise known as the “Universal Access to Quality Tertiary
Education Act”, many Filipino students continue to struggle with expenses that
remain beyond the coverage of free tuition, including books, transportation,
accommodation, digital learning tools, internship costs, review classes, and other
education-related expenses. These financial burdens continue to prevent many
students, particularly those from low-income families, from completing their
education or pursuing graduate and professional studies.

       This measure institutionalizes the National Affordable Student Loan Program
administered by government financial institutions, providing accessible financing at
a fixed two percent (2%) annual interest rate. It likewise introduces a reasonable
grace period, collateral-free borrowing, borrower protection measures, and
transparent lending standards to ensure that financial assistance remains affordable,
sustainable, and responsive to the needs of Filipino learners.

      Investing in education is an investment in the country's future. In making
educational financing more accessible and equitable, the State continues to empower
more Filipinos to acquire knowledge and skills that will strengthen the nation's
workforce, improve social mobility, and promote inclusive economic growth.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL AFFORDABLE STUDENT LOAN PROGRAM,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Affordable
Student Loan Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
make higher education more accessible, inclusive, and high-quality for every citizen
regardless of status, age, sexual orientation, race, disability, gender identity, sex, or
language. It further seeks to ease the financial burden of pursuing and completing
higher education, create a sustainable, low-interest financing scheme alongside
existing scholarships and grants, strengthen government financial institutions' role
in national development, and ensure responsible lending and borrower protection.

       The State recognizes the complementary roles of public and private higher
education and technical-vocational institutions, and values the lasting contributions
of private tertiary schools to education.

       SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act specifically seeks to:

          a. Provide affordable education financing at a fixed interest rate per
       annum;​

          b. Institutionalize long–term funding mechanisms for student credit
       support;​

          c. Ensure equitable access for economically disadvantaged students;

          d.​ Provide flexible repayment structures linked to the borrower’s
      financial capacity; and ​

          e.​ Maintain financial sustainability while minimizing default risks.

       SEC. 4. Creation of the National Affordable Student Loan Program. – There is
hereby established a National Affordable Student Loan Program (NASLP) for all
Filipino students who shall enroll in State Universities and Colleges (SUCs), Local
Universities and Colleges (LUCs), private Higher Educational Institutions (HEIs),
and Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) programs in all
Technical-Vocational Institutions (TVIs) registered under the Technical Education
and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), to be administered by the Land Bank of
the Philippines (LBP), the Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP), and other
banks as may be established by law, hereinafter referred to as “participating banks”:
Provided, That individuals who availed of the loan during their undergraduate
studies may qualify for another cycle of student loan to pursue graduate education,
including medicine and law, upon full payment of their previously obtained loan:
Provided, furthermore, That individuals who did not avail of the loan program during
their undergraduate studies may access the same to pursue graduate education,
including medicine and law: Provided, finally, That individuals who likewise did not
avail of the loan program during their undergraduate studies may apply for the loan
to cover review expenses for licensure examinations administered by the
Professional Regulation Commission.

       SEC. 5. Coverage of the Program. – The NASLP shall cover financing for
following:

          a.​ Tuition and miscellaneous fees;​

          b.​ Books, learning materials, and digital equipment;​

          c.​ Living and transportation allowances;​

          d.​ Thesis, research, internship, licensure, and graduation fees;​

          e.​ Technical–vocational certification costs;​

          f.​ Review programs for licensure examinations; and​

         g.​ Other legitimate education–related expenses as may be defined in the
      implementing rules and regulations.

      SEC. 6. Eligible Beneficiaries. – Eligible borrowers must be:

          a.​ Filipino citizens;​

        b.​ Enrolled or accepted in duly accredited and recognized HEIs, SUCs,
      LUCs, or TVIs;​

         c.​ Not be recipients of full government scholarships covering identical
      expenses; and​

          d.​ Comply with documentary and evaluation requirements.

       SEC. 7. Fixed Interest Rate. – Loans granted under this Act shall bear a fixed
interest rate of two percent (2%) per annum, computed on a simple interest basis.
The rate shall remain fixed throughout the life of the loan and shall not be subject to
adjustment due to inflation, market movements, banking rate changes, escalation
clause, or other supervening circumstances.

       SEC. 8. Loan Limits. – The maximum loanable amount per academic year or
full academic program shall be determined by the participating banks, subject to
actual cost of attendance, income classification of the borrower’s household, and
other just and equitable factors as may be determined by the participating banks:
Provided, That a cumulative loan ceiling may be imposed per borrower.

       SEC. 9. Grace Period and Maturity. – All borrowers shall enjoy a grace period of
three (3) years from the date of the immediately preceding enrollment in an
accredited and recognized HEIs, SUCs, LUCs, or TVIs; or six (6) months after
securing gainful employment, whichever is later: Provided, That no interest
capitalization shall occur during the grace period; Provided, further, That borrowers
may prepay the loan in full or in part at any time without penalty, and such
prepayment shall proportionately reduce the remaining maturity period.

       Loans granted under this Act shall have a maximum maturity period of ten
(10) years from the commencement of repayment, unless otherwise extended by
cases of hardship including involuntary unemployment, permanent disability,
serious illness by the borrower or his or her family member within the second civil
degree, fortuitous events, and other meritorious circumstances as may be
determined by the participating bank: Provided, That the grace period provided
under this Act shall not be included in the computation of the maximum maturity
period: Provided, finally, That in case of willful and continuous default exceeding
twelve (12) months without valid cause, the remaining balance may, subject to due
process, be declared due and demandable.

       SEC. 10. Collateral and Security. – Loans under this Act shall not require
traditional collateral. However, participating banks may require a co–maker or
guarantor, enrollment verification, and/or academic progress certification.

       SEC. 11. Appropriation. – The amount necessary for the implementation of the
NASLP provided under this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act. Furthermore, additional funding may be sourced from
allocations from LBP and DBP lending portfolios and government–backed bond
issuances for education financing;



                                          3
       SEC. 12. Credit Guarantee Fund. – A National Student Loan Guarantee Fund is
hereby created to cover a portion of defaults, ensure sustainability of the two percent
(2%) fixed rate; and absorb systemic repayment risks. The Fund shall be managed
and administered by the Department of Finance (DOF), in coordination with the
participating banks.

     SEC. 13. Transparency Requirements. – Borrowers shall be provided, as a
minimum requirement, with full disclosure of repayment schedules, total repayment
computation, and clear explanation of obligations and penalties.

       SEC. 14. Prohibited Practices. – The following practices shall be prohibited:

          a.​ Imposition of hidden charges;​

          b.​ Unconscionable penalty rates beyond lawful limits;​

          c.​ Harassment in collection practices; and​

          d.​ Unauthorized data sharing, pursuant to Republic Act No. 1405,
       entitled “An Act Prohibiting Disclosure of or Inquiry Into, Deposits with Any
       Banking Institution and Providing Penalty Therefor”, and other data privacy
       laws.

       Violations shall be subject to applicable administrative and criminal penalties.

       SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DOF, in coordination with
the participating banks, and in consultation with the Commission on Higher
Education (CHED), TESDA, Bureau of Internal Revenue, within ninety (90) days
from the approval of this Act, shall formulate the necessary rules and regulations for
the the effective enforcement of this Act. It shall include credit risk modeling, default
monitoring systems, data–sharing agreements with government employment
agencies, without prejudice to privacy laws; and rehabilitation and restructuring
programs for distressed borrowers.

       SEC. 16. Congressional Oversight Committee; Annual Reporting. – There is hereby
created a Congressional Oversight Committee composed of five (5) Members from
the Senate and five (5) Members from the House of Representatives. The Members
from the Senate shall be appointed by the Senate President based on the
proportional representation from the parties or coalition therein with at least one (1)
Member representing the Minority. The Members from the House of Representatives
shall be appointed by the Speaker, also based on proportional representation of the
parties or coalitions therein, with the Chair of the House Committee Banks and
Financial Intermediaries and at least one (1) member representing the Minority.

      The Committee shall be headed by the respective Chairs of the Senate
Committee on Higher, Technical and Vocational Education and the House of
Representatives Committee on Higher and Technical Education. The Secretariat of

      SEC. 17. Separability Clause. – If any provision is declared unconstitutional, the

       SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. – All inconsistent laws, orders, memoranda,
