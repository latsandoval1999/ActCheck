                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      No parent should have to choose between their child’s therapy session and
the family’s other basic needs simply because government assistance does not
adequately reflect the true cost of care.

       This bill grants qualified parents and legal guardians of children and youth
with special needs a Fifty thousand pesos (Php 50,000.00) deduction from taxable
income. The deduction covers expenses for therapy, diagnostic evaluations, tutoring,
transportation to school or medical facilities, and specialized instructional materials
or assistive devices.

       Raising a child with special needs entails substantial and recurring financial
obligations. A nationally representative study conducted with United Nations
Children’s Fund (UNICEF) found that households raising a child with a disability
incur costs forty percent (40%) to eighty percent (80%) higher than other households,
largely due to health expenses. These families also face a roughly fifty percent (50%)
higher risk of falling into poverty.1 Despite the necessity of therapy, developmental
assessments, assistive devices, and specialized instruction, the current tax code
provides no specific relief for these additional expenses.

       The burden is further aggravated by limited access to inclusive education. Of
the estimated five (5) million Filipino children with disabilities, only three hundred
ninety-one thousand eighty-nine (391,089) learners with disabilities were enrolled in
public schools during School Year 2024–2025. This represents roughly eight percent
(8%) of the estimated population. Moreover, sixty percent (60%) of enrolled learners
with disabilities have no access to special education resources in their respective
schools.2 Consequently, parents and legal guardians must turn to privately funded

       This measure provides targeted tax relief to families who shoulder these
costs.Beyond easing household expenses, it recognizes the significant role of parents
and legal guardians in securing access to essential health, developmental, and
educational services, particularly where public resources remain insufficient. It also
affirms that supporting children with special needs must be a shared responsibility,
rather than a burden borne almost entirely by their families.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
GRANTING TAX DEDUCTIONS TO QUALIFIED PARENTS AND LEGAL
GUARDIANS OF CHILDREN WITH SPECIAL NEEDS

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Parents and Legal
Guardians of Children with Special Needs Tax Deduction Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Article II, Section 13 of the 1987 Philippine
Constitution provides that the State recognizes the vital role of the youth in
nation-building and shall promote and protect their physical, moral, spiritual,
intellectual, and social well-being, It shall inculcate in the youth patriotism and
nationalism, and encourage their involvement in public and civic affairs.

       To this end, the State shall pursue inclusion of persons with special needs in
government programs and provide necessary support not only to them but also
their parents and legal guardians through the grant of tax deductions.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purpose of this Act, the following terms
shall mean:

          a) Children with Special Needs (CSNs) - Persons aged eighteen (18)
      years old and below who are either intellectually gifted or fast learners, or
      who have disabilities, impairments, or handicaps that make learning and
      other activities difficult and/or require special attention and services to
      facilitate their rehabilitation. They may be classified as follows:

                1. Children with physical/Orthopedic Disability - Those who have
             limitations on the use of limbs or other physical functions;

                2. Children with Intellectual Disability - Those with conditions
             involving slow progress in mental development;



                                          1
                3. Children with Mental Disability - Those with mental illness or
             disorders affecting mood, thinking, and behavior, such as
             schizophrenia or bipolar disorder;

                4. Children with Psychological Disability - Those with psychological or
             social conditions affecting their ability to interact with others and
             function in daily life;

                5. Children with Visual Disability - Those with impairment of visual
             functioning that persists even after treatment or correction, with a
             specific level of vision loss;

                6. Children with Hearing Disability - Those who are deaf or
             hard-of-hearing;

                7. Children with Learning Disability - Those with conditions that
             make it difficult to focus, comprehend, or retain information, such as
             dyslexia or dysgraphia;

                8. Children with Speech and Language Impairment - Those with
             disorders related to speech, rhythm, or the understanding and
             expression of language;

                 9. Children with Disability due to Chronic Illness - Those with
             disabilities resulting from long-term or chronic disease, such as
             blindness due to diabetic retinopathy or physical limitations due to
             cancer;

                 10. Children with Autism - Those with developmental disabilities
             affecting social communication and interaction, along with restricted
             and repetitive behaviors;

         b)​ Youth with Special Needs (YSN) - Individuals aged fifteen (15) to
      twenty-four (24) years old with the same characteristics as CSNs;

          c)​ Qualified Children and Youth with Special Needs - Legitimate,
      illegitimate, or legally adopted children and youth who are dependent and
      living with the taxpayer. This also includes children and youth with special
      needs placed under the legal custody of an immediate family or relative;

      SEC. 4. Deduction from Taxable income . – A qualified taxpayer shall be granted
a deduction of Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) from taxable income, provided that
said amount is used for any of the following

          1)​ Therapy;

          2)​ Diagnostic evaluations by a medical professional;




                                          2
          3)​ Tutoring;

          4)​ Transportation expenses to and from a school or medical facility;

          5)​ Specialized instructional materials or assistive devices.

        To qualify, the parent or legal guardian must provide more than half of the
total financial support for the child or youth with special needs.

        SEC. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Bureau of Internal Revenue
(BIR), in coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd)- Special Education
Division and the Department of Health (DOH) shall promulgate the relevant
implementing rules and regulations to carry out the objectives of this Act, within
ninety (90) days from its effectivity.

       SEC. 6. Appropriations. – The funding for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be taken from the current year appropriation of the BIR and thereafter, shall be
included in the General Appropriations Act.

      SEC. 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other parts hereof not affected thereby shall remain valid.

       SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, acts, presidential decrees, executive
orders, administrative orders, rules and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to
the provisions of this Act are deemed amended, modified or repealed accordingly.

       SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
