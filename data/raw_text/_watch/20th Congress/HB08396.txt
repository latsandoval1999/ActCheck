                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to institutionalize Mobile Legal Clinics (MLCs) and Barangay
Legal Aid Desks (BLADs) in order to bring free and accessible legal services directly
to underserved, remote, and marginalized communities.

       Access to justice remains uneven across the country. While the Constitution
guarantees equal protection of the laws, many Filipinos, especially those in far-flung
barangays and low-income communities, are unable to consult a lawyer, prepare
basic legal documents, or resolve disputes without resorting to costly and
time-consuming court proceedings. Geographic isolation, financial constraints, and
lack of legal awareness are just some of the factors that continue to prevent citizens
from asserting their rights.

       This proposed measure mandates the Department of Justice (DOJ) to establish
and operate Mobile Legal Clinics nationwide. These clinics will deploy fully
equipped vehicles to remote areas to provide legal consultation and other assistance
in common disputes such as labor, land, and family matters, notarization, and public
legal education. Complementing the mobile clinics, every barangay will establish a
Barangay Legal Aid Desk to serve as a community-based access point for legal
guidance, document preparation, and alternative dispute resolution (ADR).

       By bringing justice closer to the people, this measure affirms that legal
protection should not depend on wealth or location. It transforms access to justice
from a constitutional promise into a practical, community-based reality.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING MOBILE LEGAL CLINICS AND BARANGAY LEGAL
AID DESKS TO PROVIDE ACCESSIBLE AND FREE LEGAL SERVICES TO
UNDERSERVED    COMMUNITIES,    STRENGTHENING    ALTERNATIVE
DISPUTE RESOLUTION, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Community Legal
Access Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
uphold the right of every Filipino to equal access to justice, as guaranteed by the
Constitution. The State shall ensure that legal services are available and accessible,
especially to marginalized and underserved communities, by establishing Mobile
Legal Clinics (MLCs) and Barangay Legal Aid Desks (BLADs) to provide free legal
assistance and strengthen alternative dispute resolution (ADR) mechanisms at the
community level.

       SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act aims to:

          a. Provide free and accessible legal services to underserved and remote
       communities;

          b. Institutionalize Mobile Legal Clinics (MLCs) and Barangay Legal Aid
       Desks (BLADs) nationwide;

          c. Promote alternative dispute resolution to reduce court congestion and
       encourage amicable settlement of disputes; and

          d. Foster collaboration among government agencies, local governments,
       the legal profession, and law schools in delivering legal aid services.

       SEC. 4. Establishment of Mobile Legal Clinics. — The Department of Justice
(DOJ), in coordination with the Public Attorney’s Office (PAO), shall establish and
operate Mobile Legal Clinics (MLCs) nationwide. MLCs shall be fully equipped with
vehicles deployed regularly to remote and underserved areas, providing free legal
services such as:

          a.​ Legal consultation and advice;

          b.​ Mediation and conciliation services;

         c.​ Assistance in labor disputes, land conflicts, family law cases, consumer
      protection, and notarization of documents; and

          d.​ Public legal education sessions.

      MLCs shall be staffed by PAO lawyers, paralegals, accredited volunteer
lawyers, law student interns, and legal aid volunteers.

        SEC. 5. Establishment of Barangay Legal Aid Desks. — Every barangay hall shall
establish a Barangay Legal Aid Desk (BLAD), accessible to all residents, offering free
legal guidance, mediation services, and assistance in preparing legal documents
such as affidavits, contracts, and simple pleadings. BLADs shall be staffed by trained
paralegals, volunteer lawyers, and legal interns. Local Government Units (LGUs)
shall likewise provide appropriate space, basic equipment, and operational funds for
BLADs within barangay halls.

     SEC. 6. Strengthening Alternative Dispute Resolution. — MLCs and BLADs shall
promote the amicable settlement of disputes through alternative dispute resolution
(ADR) mechanisms such as mediation and conciliation, in accordance with Republic
Act No. 9285, otherwise known as the “Alternative Dispute Resolution Act of 2004”.
BLADs shall prioritize the resolution of minor disputes to reduce court caseloads.

       SEC. 7. Volunteer Participation and Incentives. — Volunteer lawyers shall be
entitled to incentives, including:

          a.​ Mandatory Continuing Legal Education (MCLE) credits;

          b.​ Tax deductions for pro bono legal services; and

          c.​ Law internship credits for students in state-run law schools.

       The DOJ, in partnership with the Integrated Bar of the Philippines (IBP) and
state law schools, shall develop a standardized training program for BLAD and
MLC volunteers, ensuring continuous professional development and quality of
service.

     SEC. 8. Barangay Legal Assistance Council. — A Barangay Legal Assistance
Council (BLAC) is hereby created to oversee the implementation and monitoring of

this Act. The BLAC shall be composed of representatives from the DOJ, PAO,
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), IBP, state-run law
schools, and relevant civil society organizations. The BLAC shall:

          a.​ Monitor and evaluate the performance of MLCs and BLADs;

          b.​ Recommend improvements in legal aid delivery; and

          c.​ Submit an annual report to Congress.

       SEC. 9. Funding. — The initial funding for the operation of MLCs and BLADs
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOJ and PAO.Thereafter,
the necessary funds shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act
(GAA). LGUs shall allocate local funds for the establishment, maintenance, and
operation of BLADs within barangay halls.

       SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOJ, in coordination with the PAO, DILG, IBP,
state-run law schools, and other stakeholders, shall issue the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
