                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Republic Act No. 11037 otherwise known as the “Masustansyang Pagkain
Para sa Batang Pilipino Act” institutionalized the National Feeding Program to
address the double burden of malnutrition among Filipino children. However, the
present legal landscape limits this supplemental feeding to learners from
Kindergarten to Grade 6. This leaves learners of junior and senior high school,
particularly those from low-income households, no other sources of sustained
nutritional assistance to attain full development.

      According to Food and Nutrition Research Institute of Department of Science
and Technology, adolescents demand highest recommended nutrients requirements
due to rapid growth spurts, reproduction-related changes and cognitive
development1. In education, persistent risk of lower academic performance,
absenteeism and dropping out were seen associated with malnutrition aggravated by

       At the macro level, the Philippines ranked 67th out of 113 countries in the
2022 Global Food Security Index 5. This indicates country’s relatively low food
security performance, in terms of affordability, availability, quality and safety, and
sustainability and adaption, compared to other Asia-Pacific economies. A United
Nations report further reveals that about 51 million Filipinos experienced moderate
or severe food insecurity from 2021 to 2023 — among the worst in Southeast Asia6.
Subsequently, a study of Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS)
reported high cost of nutritious foods results in low consumption of fruits and
vegetables among Filipino households. Worst dietary quality was observed among
poorer household which led to malnutrition 7.

      Thus, this bill seeks to amend RA No. 11037 or the “Masustansyang Pagkain
Para sa Batang Pilipino Act” by expanding the coverage of the country-wide
feeding program to include Grade 7 to Senior High School learners. The rationale
on this amendment is to improve Filipino learners’ nutritional status, social
protection system and facilitate their full potential from early childhood to
adulthood. Likewise, it aims to strengthen the nation's nutritional intervention
framework through a more comprehensive, sustainable and responsive program to
combat poverty-related malnutrition.

      This also grounded in 1987 Constitution Article XIII, Section 11 which
obligates the state to adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to health
development, ensure access to essential goods and social services at affordable cost,
and prioritize the needs of the underprivileged, including children. In like manner,

SEC. 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Education, in consultation with the
Department of Health, National Nutrition Council, the Department of Science and
Technology thru Food and Nutrition Research Institute, the Department of
Agriculture, the Department of the Interior and Local Government, and concerned
Local Government Units, and other relevant government agencies, non-government
organizations, and development partners shall promulgate their respective rules and
regulations for the efficient and effective implementation of this Act.


SEC. 3. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall continue to be in full force and
effect.


SEC. 4. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
administrative orders, rules and regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent
with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended
accordingly.
