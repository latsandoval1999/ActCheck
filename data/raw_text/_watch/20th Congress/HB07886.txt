                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

For millions of Filipino households, particularly those belonging to
marginalized sectors, the cost of electricity remains a persistent burden that
exacerbates poverty and inequality.

Republic Act No. 9136, otherwise known as the Electric Power Industry Reform
Act of 2001 (EPIRA), introduced the lifeline rate as a socialized pricing
mechanism intended to assist marginalized electricity consumers. This
mechanism was further strengthened by Republic Act No. 11552, which
expanded the lifeline rate program and institutionalized cross-subsidy
mechanisms.

Despite these legislative advances, however, challenges remain in the
accurate identification of beneficiaries, equitable distribution of subsidies,
prevention of leakages, and the adequacy of assistance provided to vulnerable
consumers amid rising energy costs.

This proposed measure seeks to further enhance the implementation of the
lifeline rate by refining the definition of qualified marginalized end-users. We
expressly included minimum wage earners as presumed marginalized end-
users, subject to verification through official wage orders issued by the
Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Boards (RTWPBs) or proof of
employment. This inclusion reflects the realities faced by low-income workers
who may not be covered by existing social protection programs but remain
    exposed to energy poverty and economic vulnerability whose incomes are fixed
    and highly sensitive to inflation.

    Moreover, the bill provides that all qualified marginalized end-users
    consuming not more than one hundred fifty kilowatt-hours (150 kWh) per
    month shall be fully subsidized by the government. This policy shift
    strengthens the social protection intent of the lifeline rate while promoting
    transparency, cost-reflectivity, and fairness in the allocation of subsidies.

    To ensure accountability and continuous improvement, the measure
    mandates regular reporting to Congress and periodic comprehensive
    evaluations of the lifeline rate’s implementation, including validation
    procedures and safeguards against subsidy leakages.

    This bill advances a more inclusive, data-driven, and responsive lifeline rate
    policy—one that balances social equity, regulatory integrity, and fiscal
    responsibility—thereby reaffirming the State’s commitment to protect
    marginalized electricity consumers and promote energy justice.

    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 7          Sec. 2. Transitory Provision. – The current level of consumption,
 8   subsidy, and rate shall continue to be applied to all marginalized end-users
 9   of all distribution utilities until such time that a new level of consumption,
10   subsidy, and rate shall have been determined and approved by the ERC in
11   accordance with Section 1 of this Act.

12          Sec. 3. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to fully subsidize the
13   electricity consumption of qualified marginalized end-users under this Act
14   shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).

15         For the initial implementation of this Act, the funding requirements
16   shall be sourced from available appropriations of national government
17   agencies implementing social amelioration and income-support programs,
18   including but not limited to the Tulong Panghanapbuhay sa Ating
19   Disadvantaged/Displaced Workers (TUPAD), Assistance to Individuals in
20   Crisis Situation (AICS), and other similar “Ayuda” programs, subject to
21   applicable budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules and regulations.

22          The appropriated subsidy funds shall be directly released and
23   downloaded to the National Electrification Administration (NEA), which shall,
24   in turn, immediately remit and distribute the corresponding amounts to
25   electric cooperatives in proportion to the validated lifeline subsidy
26   requirements of their qualified consumers.

27           For privately-owned distribution utilities and local government-owned
28   utilities, the Department of Energy (DOE) shall ensure direct reimbursement
29   in accordance with the validated subsidy amounts approved by the ERC.

30        The release and remittance of funds to distribution utilities shall be
31   made on a monthly basis and shall not be subject to undue delay.

32           In order to protect distribution utilities from financial exposure arising
33   from delayed releases, insufficient appropriations, or failure of reimbursement
34   by the appropriate government agency, distribution utilities are hereby
35   authorized to temporarily suspend the implementation of the full government
36   lifeline rate subsidy under this Act upon notice to the ERC and DOE, until
37   such time that sufficient funds are made available. Provided, That such
38   temporary suspension shall not be construed as a violation of any franchise
39   obligation, regulatory directive, or service mandate under existing laws.
