                        AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE AGRICULTURAL DATA TRANSPARENCY
SYSTEM, PROMOTING OPEN ACCESS TO AGRICULTURAL
INFORMATION, INSTITUTIONALIZING REAL-TIME MARKET DATA
SHARING, STRENGTHENING EVIDENCE-BASED AGRICULTURAL
GOVERNANCE, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE
       Agriculture remains a vital pillar of the Philippine economy and food security
system, providing livelihoods to millions of Filipinos. However, farmers and
fisherfolk continue to face significant challenges due to limited access to timely and
reliable information on commodity prices, agricultural inputs, production trends, and
market conditions. This information gap weakens their bargaining power,
contributes to inefficiencies in agricultural markets, and often results in low farm
incomes despite rising consumer food prices.

       The agricultural sector likewise suffers from fragmented and inaccessible data
systems. Agricultural information is collected by various government agencies and
local government units, often using different methodologies and reporting schedules.
The absence of a centralized and transparent system hinders effective planning,
delays government interventions, and limits the ability of policymakers and
stakeholders to make informed decisions regarding food production, supply
management, and agricultural development.
      Recent experiences involving inflation, climate-related disasters, supply chain
disruptions, and food security concerns have underscored the importance of accurate
and real-time agricultural information. Timely data on production levels, commodity
inventories, market prices, and agricultural inputs are essential in anticipating
shortages, stabilizing markets, responding to emergencies, and ensuring the efficient
delivery of government support to agricultural communities.

       This proposed bill seeks to establish an Agricultural Data Transparency
System that will serve as a centralized and publicly accessible platform for
agricultural information. The measure mandates the publication of real-time
farmgate, wholesale, and retail prices; creates an open-access agricultural data
portal; requires data sharing among government agencies and local government
units; and ensures that information is accessible to farmers and fisherfolk through
websites, mobile applications, and SMS-based services.

      By institutionalizing transparency, accessibility, and interoperability in
agricultural data governance, this measure aims to reduce information asymmetry,
improve market efficiency, strengthen evidence-based policymaking, and enhance
food security. Ultimately, the bill seeks to empower farmers, fisherfolk, consumers,
and local governments with reliable information necessary for building a more
productive, resilient, and competitive agricultural sector.
      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                   ARTICLE I
                            GENERAL PROVISIONS

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Agricultural Data
Transparency Act of 2026”


      SEC 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to:
(a) Promote transparency, accountability, and accessibility in agricultural
markets;

(b) Empower farmers, fisherfolk, consumers, cooperatives, and local
government units through timely and accurate agricultural information;

(c) Reduce information asymmetry in agricultural supply chains;

(d) Strengthen food security through evidence-based agricultural planning and
decision-making;

(e) Utilize digital technologies to improve agricultural productivity, market
efficiency, and public service delivery;

(f) Institutionalize open-data principles in agricultural governance while
protecting confidential and personal information.


SEC 3. Objectives. — This Act shall pursue the following objectives:

(a) Establish a nationwide Agricultural Data Transparency System;

(b) Provide real-time information on farmgate, wholesale, and retail prices;

(c) Improve transparency of agricultural production and supply conditions;

(d) Facilitate agricultural planning and market forecasting;

(e) Ensure equitable access to agricultural information;

(f) Strengthen coordination among government agencies and local
government units.
      SEC 4. Definition of Terms. — For the purposes of this Act, the following
shall be defined;

      (a) Agricultural Data refers to the information relating to production, prices,
      inputs, supply chains, weather, trade, and other agricultural indicators;

      (b) Farmgate Price refers to the price received by the farmer at the point of
      first sale;

      (c) Open Data refers to the government-held agricultural information freely
      accessible and reusable by the public;

      (d) Real-Time Data refers to the information made available immediately or
      within the shortest practicable period after collection and validation;

      (e) Agricultural Inputs refers to the seeds, fertilizer, feeds, fuel, machinery,
      pesticides, irrigation resources, and related agricultural resources;

      (f) Data Interoperability refers to the ability of systems to exchange and
      utilize information efficiently.


                                    ARTICLE II
            AGRICULTURAL DATA TRANSPARENCY SYSTEM


      SEC 5. Establishment of the Agricultural Data Transparency System
(ADTS) — There is hereby established an Agricultural Data Transparency System
(ADTS) under the Department of Agriculture (DA), which shall serve as the primary
national platform for the collection, integration, management, analysis, and
dissemination of agricultural information. The ADTS shall consolidate data
generated by national government agencies, local government units (LGUs),
government-owned and controlled corporations, state universities and colleges, and
other authorized sources involved in agricultural development and food security.


      The ADTS shall promote transparency, accessibility, interoperability, and
evidence-based decision-making by providing timely and reliable information to
farmers, fisherfolk, consumers, researchers, policymakers, and other stakeholders.


      SEC 6. Components of the System. — The ADTS shall consist of
interconnected databases and digital platforms covering agricultural production,
farmgate prices, wholesale and retail prices, agricultural inputs, weather and climate
information, commodity inventories, logistics and transportation data, trade
information, market forecasts, and agricultural advisories.


      The Department may establish additional modules and information systems
as necessary to address emerging agricultural concerns, improve food security
planning, and support digital transformation initiatives in the agriculture sector.


      SEC 7. Agricultural Open Data Portal – The DA shall establish and maintain
an Agricultural Open Data Portal that shall provide free and public access to non-
confidential agricultural information. The portal shall publish updated information
on commodity prices, production volumes, crop calendars, input availability and
prices, importation and exportation statistics, market trends, and other relevant
agricultural indicators.


      All datasets shall be made available in machine-readable and user-friendly
formats to facilitate public use, research, innovation, investment planning, and
policy analysis.
                                ARTICLE III
       DATA COLLECTION, SHARING AND INTEROPERABILITY


      SEC 8. Mandatory Data Sharing. — All offices, bureaus, attached agencies,
and instrumentalities under the DA, as well as relevant government agencies
involved in agriculture, fisheries, food security, trade, statistics, and rural
development, shall regularly share agricultural data with the ADTS.


      The sharing of information shall be subject to standards prescribed by the
Department and shall ensure the timely submission, consistency, and reliability of
data while complying with applicable laws on data privacy and security.


      SEC 9. Local Government Integration. — Provinces, cities, municipalities,
and barangays shall participate in the collection, validation, updating, and
dissemination of agricultural information within their respective jurisdictions. LGUs
shall designate focal persons responsible for coordinating with the Department and
ensuring the timely submission of local agricultural data.


      The Department shall provide technical assistance, training, and digital tools
necessary to strengthen local capacities for agricultural data management and
reporting.


      SEC 10. Data Standards and Interoperability. — The DA, in coordination
with the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) and
the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), shall establish uniform standards,
classifications, reporting formats, and protocols for agricultural data collection,
management, and exchange.


      All participating agencies and LGUs shall adopt interoperable systems that
allow seamless sharing, verification, and utilization of agricultural information
across government platforms.


      SEC 11. Data Submission Requirements. — Government agencies and
LGUs covered by this Act shall submit agricultural information at intervals
prescribed by the Department. Data shall include, whenever applicable, production
estimates, commodity inventories, planting and harvesting schedules, agricultural
losses, market prices, and input availability. The Department may issue guidelines
on reporting frequency, submission procedures, validation requirements, and quality
assurance measures.


                                  ARTICLE VI
                    REAL-TIME PRICE TRANSPARENCY


      SEC 12. National Agricultural Price Monitoring Program. — The DA shall
establish a nationwide real-time monitoring system to collect, validate, analyze, and
disseminate information on farmgate, wholesale, and retail prices of major
agricultural commodities. The program shall identify regional price variations and
support interventions aimed at protecting farmers, consumers, and food security.


      SEC 13. Daily Price Publication. — The DA shall ensure the daily
publication of commodity prices through official websites, mobile applications,
SMS services, local information centers, and agricultural extension offices. Price
reports shall indicate prevailing prices, historical trends, regional comparisons, and
other information that may assist farmers and consumers in making informed
decisions.


      SEC 14. Farmgate Retail Price Transparency Reports. — The DA shall
prepare and publish monthly and quarterly reports comparing farmgate, wholesale,
and retail prices of major commodities. These reports shall identify significant price
disparities, potential market distortions, supply constraints, and other factors
affecting agricultural markets.


      SEC 15. Market Early Warning System. – The DA shall establish a Market
Early Warning System capable of identifying unusual price movements, supply
shortages, production declines, logistics disruptions, and emerging food security
risks. Appropriate alerts, advisories, and recommendations shall be issued to
concerned agencies, LGUs, producers, and consumers to facilitate timely
interventions.


                                   ARTICLE V
                 FARMER ACCESS AND DIGITAL INCLUSION


      SEC 16. SMS-Based Information Service. — The DA shall establish a free
or subsidized SMS-based information service allowing farmers and fisherfolk to
access commodity prices, weather advisories, production forecasts, input
availability, and government support programs. Priority shall be given to
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas where internet connectivity remains
limited.
      SEC 17. Mobile Application Platform. – The DA shall develop and maintain
a mobile application that provides real-time agricultural information, market
intelligence, government advisories, and digital services for farmers and fisherfolk.
The application shall be designed to be user-friendly, accessible, and compatible
with commonly used mobile devices.


      SEC 18. Accessibility Measures. – Agricultural information shall be
disseminated in formats and languages accessible to diverse stakeholders, including
indigenous peoples, women farmers, fisherfolk, senior citizens, and persons with
disabilities. The Department shall utilize multiple communication channels to ensure
broad and equitable access to information.


                                  ARTICLE VI
                      GOVERNANCE AND OVERSIGHT


      SEC 19. Agricultural Data Transparency Council. — There is hereby
created an Agricultural Data Transparency Council to provide policy direction,
oversight, and coordination in the implementation of this Act.


      The Council shall be chaired by the Secretary of Agriculture and shall be
composed of the following members:


      (a) The National Statistician of the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), or
      his/her duly authorized representative;

      (b) The Secretary of the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), or his/her
      duly authorized representative;
(c) The Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST), or
his/her duly authorized representative;

(d) The Secretary of the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT), or his/her duly authorized representative;

(e) The Secretary of the National Economic and Development Authority
(NEDA), or his/her duly authorized representative;

(f) One (1) representative from the League of Provinces of the Philippines;

(g) One (1) representative from the League of Cities of the Philippines;

(h) One (1) representative from the League of Municipalities of the
Philippines;

(i) Two (2) representatives from accredited national farmers' organizations,
with at least one (1) representative from the crop production sector;

(j) One (1) representative from accredited fisherfolk organizations;

(k) One (1) representative from duly registered agricultural cooperatives;

(l) One (1) representative from state universities and colleges with recognized
expertise in agriculture, fisheries, data science, agricultural economics, or
rural development;

(m) One (1) representative from an agricultural research institution or think
tank;
      (n) One (1) representative from the private sector engaged in agricultural
      technology, digital innovation, or agribusiness, to be appointed by the
      Secretary of Agriculture.

      Representatives from non-government sectors shall be appointed by the
Secretary of Agriculture from a shortlist submitted by their respective organizations
and shall serve a term of three (3) years, renewable for one (1) additional term,
provided that they possess demonstrated expertise in agriculture, food systems,
digital governance, data management, rural development, or related fields.


      The Council shall convene at least once every quarter and may hold special
meetings upon the call of the Chairperson or upon the request of at least one-third
(1/3) of its members, with a majority of all members constituting a quorum for the
transaction of business. To effectively carry out its mandate, the Council may
establish technical working groups, advisory committees, and sectoral panels as may
be necessary.

      SEC 20. Functions of the Council – The Council shall formulate policies,
monitor implementation, review system performance, recommend improvements,
and promote stakeholder participation in agricultural data governance. The Council
may establish technical working groups and advisory bodies to address specific
concerns relating to data management, transparency, innovation, and food security.


                           ARTICLE VII
                DATA QUALITY, PRIVACY, AND SECURITY

      SEC 21. Data Quality Assurance. – This Act shall establish verification,
validation, and quality control mechanisms to ensure the accuracy, timeliness,
reliability, and completeness of all information published through the ADTS.
Regular audits, cross-validation procedures, and stakeholder feedback mechanisms
shall be utilized to maintain data integrity.


      SEC 22. Independent Audit. –The Commission on Audit (COA) and other
authorized oversight bodies may conduct periodic assessments of the operational,
financial, and technical performance of the ADTS. Findings and recommendations
shall be used to improve efficiency, transparency, and accountability in system
implementation.


      SEC 23. Data Privacy Protection. – All personal information collected,
processed, and stored under this Act shall be governed by Republic Act No. 10173,
otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”. The DA shall implement
safeguards to prevent unauthorized access, disclosure, misuse, or loss of protected
information.


      SEC 24. Cybersecurity Standards. – This Act shall adopt and regularly
update cybersecurity measures consistent with national and international standards
to protect agricultural information systems from cyber threats and unauthorized
access. Appropriate risk management, incident response, and disaster recovery
mechanisms shall likewise be established.


      SEC 25. Stakeholder Feedback Mechanism. –The ADTS shall establish
accessible mechanisms through which stakeholders may submit corrections,
complaints, recommendations, and local market information relating to agricultural
data. Feedback received shall be regularly reviewed and incorporated into data
validation, policy development, and system improvement processes.
                                  ARTICLE VIII
                              ACCOUNTABILITY

      SEC 28. Annual Report to Congress. – The Council shall submit to Congress
an annual implementation report detailing the status of this Act's implementation,
including compliance with data collection and sharing requirements, progress in
developing and maintaining the national agricultural data system, key performance
indicators, utilization of appropriated funds, challenges encountered, and
corresponding recommendations for legislative or administrative action.


      SEC 29. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts are hereby prohibited:


      (a) Knowingly submitting false, misleading, inaccurate, incomplete, or
      fabricated agricultural data, reports, or information required under this Act;


      (b) Knowingly manipulating, altering, suppressing, destroying, or tampering
      with official agricultural data, records, databases, or information systems
      maintained pursuant to this Act;


      (c) Willfully refusing, obstructing, or unduly delaying the collection,
      validation, integration, sharing, or submission of agricultural data required
      under this Act without lawful justification;


      (d) Unauthorized access to, use, disclosure, transfer, reproduction, or
      distribution of confidential, restricted, or protected agricultural data obtained
      pursuant to this Act, except as otherwise authorized by law;
         (e) Misappropriating or using agricultural data obtained under this Act for
         fraudulent, commercial, political, or other unauthorized purposes in violation
         of this Act or other applicable laws;


         (f) Knowingly interfering with, disrupting, damaging, or compromising the
         operation, integrity, security, or interoperability of the National Agricultural
         Data System or any authorized data-sharing platform established under this
         Act; and


         (g) Any other willful act that undermines the accuracy, integrity, security,
         confidentiality, accessibility, or lawful use of agricultural data and
         information systems established pursuant to this Act.


         SEC 30. Penalties. – Any person who commits any of the prohibited acts
under Section 29 shall, upon conviction, be punished by a fine of not less than Fifty
Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) but not more than One Million Pesos (₱1,000,000.00),
or imprisonment of six (6) months to five (5) years, or both, at the discretion of the
court.


         Provided that the offender is a public officer or employee, the penalties
imposed under this Act shall be without prejudice to administrative sanctions,
including suspension, dismissal from the service, forfeiture of benefits, and
perpetual disqualification from holding public office, as may be provided under
existing laws. Where the offender is a juridical person, partnership, association, or
corporation, the responsible officers, directors, partners, or employees who
participated in, authorized, or knowingly tolerated the commission of the offense
shall be held liable. The imposition of penalties under this Act shall be without
prejudice to any civil, criminal, or administrative liability under existing laws.


                                  ARTICLE IX
                               FINAL PROVISIONS

      SEC 31. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
Department of Agriculture. Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


      SEC 32. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred twenty
(120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Agriculture shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations.


      SEC 33. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


      SEC 34. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

      SEC 35. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
