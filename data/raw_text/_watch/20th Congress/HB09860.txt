                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       In the morning of June 8, 2026, a magnitude 7.8 earthquake struck the southern
coast of Mindanao, with its epicenter 32km offshore west of Maasim in the Province of
Sarangani1. The seismic event was the strongest earthquake to strike the Philippines
and was considered one of the most destructive since 19762. The earthquake triggered
over 130 aftershocks and generated tsunami warnings across multiple Asian nations3.

       As of June 10, the Office of the Civil Defense (OCD) reported that the official
casualty count rose to 45 confirmed deaths, with about 630 persons injured and 17
reported missing4. The earthquake caused severe structural damage across multiple
regions of Mindanao. According to official damage assessments, at least 2,505 homes

        The disaster had an unprecedented impact on the education sector. The
Department of Education (DepEd) reported that the earthquake forced 8,208 schools

infrastructure damage to 1,023 school facilities in Region 12 and 192 in Region 117.
The crisis extends beyond facility damage. Initial DepEd situation reports outline a

Notably, the Matanao National High School experienced collapse of a dilapidated
building9, while students at the Cotabato City Central Pilot Elementary School, who
were conducting earthquake drills when the tremor struck, safely evacuated10.

      Following structural damage caused by the earthquake, commercial operations
at the General Santos International Airport remain suspended. While authorities
conduct inspections and necessary repairs, the facility is currently restricted to military,
government, and humanitarian flights11.

       On June 09, 2026, the National Grid Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP) fully
restored affected power transmission lines in Southwestern Mindanao, specifically
covering General Santos City, Sarangani, and South Cotabato near the epicenter of
the 7.8-magnitude earthquake. Despite transmission recovery, nearly 160,000
consumer connections remain without power. Currently, the region is facing a deficit
of over 500 megawatts of electricity because major generation plants, the 300-MW
SMC Malita Coal Plant and the 237-MW Sarangani Energy Corporation plant,
triggered emergency shutdowns and remain offline12.

requirements that cannot be deferred pending ordinary appropriation cycles. Standard

           Thus, this proposed measure.

        This bill seeks to appropriate Fifteen Billion Pesos (₱15,000,000,000.00) as
supplemental funding to augment the National Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Fund (NDRMFF) for relief, recovery, rehabilitation, repair, and
reconstruction efforts in areas affected, strategically allocated across sectors to
address the full spectrum of disaster impacts. This cross-sectoral approach recognizes
that effective disaster recovery requires simultaneous intervention across
humanitarian, health, educational, livelihood, shelter, and infrastructure domains.
Furthermore, the proposed measure incorporates accountability mechanisms,
including multi-agency coordination, geographic prioritization, comprehensive
transparency requirements, and regular reporting to legislative committees and the
public.

       This measure demonstrates the national government's commitment to the
constitutional principle that the State shall protect and promote the right to health and
well-being of the people13, and shall prioritize the urgent needs of the nation, and its
determination to leave no affected community behind in the path of disaster recovery.

      This version fully adopts Senate Bill No. 2259 filed by Senator Francis “Kiko”
N. Pangilinan in the 20th Congress as its counterpart bill in the House of
Representatives.


           The prompt enactment of this measure is respectfully urged.
