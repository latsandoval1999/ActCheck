                                AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE PROFESSIONAL COMPENSATION SYSTEM IN THE
PHILIPPINES, CREATING THE PROFESSIONAL WAGE BOARD AS AN ATTACHED
AGENCY OF THE DEPARTMENT OF LABOR AND EMPLOYMENT WITH QUASI-JUDICIAL
POWERS, ESTABLISHING THE PROFESSIONAL WAGE COUNCIL AS ITS CONSULTATIVE
ARM, CREATING REGIONAL PROFESSIONAL WAGE OFFICES, ALIGNING MINIMUM
PROFESSIONAL COMPENSATION WITH THE SALARY STANDARDIZATION LAW,
COVERING ALL GOVERNMENT AND PRIVATE EMPLOYERS, PROVIDING FOR
PERMANENT FUNDING, PRESCRIBING MINIMUM PROFESSIONAL COMPENSATION
STANDARDS, DEFINING AND PENALIZING PROHIBITED ACTS, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines produces some of the most highly trained licensed professionals in
Asia — nurses, engineers, architects, pharmacists, physicians, accountants, teachers,
and hundreds of others — yet systematically fails to retain them because domestic
compensation does not reflect the investment, expertise, and public responsibility
they carry. No existing law establishes minimum compensation standards specifically
calibrated to licensed professional practice. The Labor Code and regional minimum
wage system were designed for ordinary workers and are structurally ill-suited to
capture the unique attributes of professional labor: mandatory years of education,
state-administered licensure, continuing professional development obligations,
personal and public liability, and the direct bearing of professional services on public
health, safety, infrastructure, justice, and national development.

This Act proposes a permanent systemic solution through two complementary
institutions: the Professional Wage Board (PWB), an attached agency of the
Department of Labor and Employment with quasi-judicial authority to issue and
enforce binding Professional Wage Orders; and the Professional Wage Council (PWC),
a broad multi-sector consultative body that conducts stakeholder consultations and
endorses draft wage orders to the Board. This two-tier architecture separates the
political legitimacy of the consultation process from the legal authority of the wage-
setting institution.

                                                                                      1
A defining feature of this Act is its alignment with the existing Salary Standardization
Law (SSL). Rather than inventing compensation benchmarks from scratch, the Board
shall use the SSL salary grade equivalents for each covered profession in government
service as the statutory minimum floor for both government and private sector
employment. This approach leverages a proven, legislatively validated, and regularly
updated compensation framework and ensures parity between public and private
professional compensation. Private employers may not pay less than the SSL-
equivalent rate for a given profession; the Board may set higher rates when brain drain
data and market conditions warrant.

This Act expressly covers all employers — every national government agency, local
government unit, state university and college, government-owned and controlled
corporation, government financial institution, private corporation, partnership, sole
proprietorship, cooperative, foundation, and international organization operating in
the Philippines — without distinction. For government employers, a dedicated budget
alignment provision ensures appropriation compliance within one budget cycle. For
private employers, graduated administrative sanctions, criminal penalties with
imprisonment, civil liability, and a whistleblower reward program provide credible
enforcement.

The Chairpersonship is open to any seasoned licensed professional with demonstrated
leadership — pharmacists, nurses, engineers, and all other licensed professionals
qualify alongside lawyers and economists. The Board is organized around the PRC's
four professional clusters, with two seats per cluster. The Selection Committee is
composed of the heads of the Civil Service Commission, Professional Regulation
Commission, NEDA, and DOLE — or their designated alternates of at least
Undersecretary rank — ensuring credible, practical, and expedient screening without
overburdening already busy officials.

Approval of this measure is urgently sought.

                         ARTICLE I — GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known and cited as the "Philippine
Professional Compensation Act" or the "PPCA."

SEC. 2. Declaration of State Policy. The State hereby declares that:

   (a) It is the policy of the Republic of the Philippines to protect and promote the
       right of all licensed professionals to fair, equitable, and adequate
       compensation commensurate with their educational investment, professional
       responsibilities, and contribution to national development;
   (b) Chronic underpayment of licensed professionals constitutes structural
       injustice that undermines the national credentialing system, incentivizes
       emigration, depletes critical sectors, and injures the public that depends on
       professional services;



                                                                                     3
   (c) The State shall establish a permanent, adaptive, expert-administered system
       of minimum professional compensation standards — anchored to the Salary
       Standardization Law framework and responsive to market and economic
       conditions — that does not require repeated legislative intervention to remain
       relevant; and
   (d) This Act shall be construed liberally in favor of covered professionals,
       consistent with the constitutional mandate to afford full protection to labor.

SEC. 3. Universal Employer Coverage. This Act covers all employers engaging the
services of covered professionals in the Philippines, without distinction as to sector,
ownership, or organizational form, including:

   (a) All national government agencies, departments, bureaus, offices, and
       instrumentalities of the national government;
   (b) All local government units, including provinces, cities, municipalities, and
       barangays;
   (c) All state universities and colleges and other government educational
       institutions;
   (d) All government-owned and controlled corporations, government financial
       institutions, and their subsidiaries and affiliates, whether or not performing
       proprietary functions;
   (e) All private corporations, partnerships, sole proprietorships, and joint ventures,
       regardless of capitalization or size;
   (f) All cooperatives, foundations, associations, and non-stock, non-profit
       organizations; and
   (g) All international organizations, foreign missions, and foreign-funded projects
       operating within Philippine territory, to the extent consistent with applicable
       treaties and international agreements.

No employer falling within any of the foregoing categories may pay a covered
professional below the Minimum Professional Compensation established under this
Act and applicable Professional Wage Orders. The fact that an employer is a
government entity or that its compensation structure is governed by existing law shall
not be a defense to liability under this Act, except insofar as the budget alignment
provision of Section 71 applies during the transition period.

SEC. 4. Coverage of Professionals. This Act covers all natural persons holding a valid
certificate of registration and professional identification card issued by the
Professional Regulation Commission or by any other professional regulatory body
established by law, regardless of whether they are employed, self-employed, or
engaged as independent contractors or consultants in Philippine territory.

SEC. 5. Non-Diminution of Benefits. Nothing in this Act shall reduce, diminish, or
impair any benefit, right, privilege, or compensation that a covered professional is

                                                                                      4
receiving or is entitled to receive under any existing law, collective bargaining
agreement, employer policy, practice, or individual contract. Minimum professional
compensation rates established hereunder constitute a floor, not a ceiling.
SEC. 6. Relationship with Existing Wage and Compensation Laws. This Act operates
in addition to the minimum wage system under the National Wages and Productivity
Commission. Where a covered professional is also covered by a regional minimum
wage order, whichever rate is higher shall apply. This Act operates in addition to, and
not in derogation of, the Salary Standardization Law — the SSL shall serve as the
statutory floor reference for professional wage-setting as provided in Article VII
hereof. The Board shall coordinate with the NWPC and the Department of Budget and
Management to prevent inconsistencies.

                         ARTICLE II — DEFINITION OF TERMS


SEC. 7. Definition of Terms. As used in this Act:

   (a) "Accredited Professional Organization" or "APO" refers to the integrated
       professional organization of a given profession duly accredited by the PRC or
       the relevant regulatory body as the official organization of that profession;
   (b) "Board" or "PWB" refers to the Professional Wage Board created under this
       Act;
   (c) "Council" or "PWC" refers to the Professional Wage Council created under this
       Act as the consultative and recommendatory arm of the Board;
   (d) "Covered Professional" refers to any person holding a valid certificate of
       registration and professional identification card issued by the PRC or any other
       professional regulatory body established by law;
   (e) "Employer" refers to any person or entity — natural or juridical, government
       or private — that engages, hires, contracts, or retains the services of a covered
       professional for compensation, as more particularly enumerated in Section 3
       hereof;
   (f) "Minimum Professional Compensation" or "MPC" refers to the lowest lawful
       compensation, in any form, that an employer may pay a covered professional
       for professional services rendered, as determined by applicable Professional
       Wage Orders and anchored to the SSL floor reference;
   (g) "Professional Cluster" refers to one of the four groupings of professions
       recognized under this Act, consistent with the PRC's existing classification
       framework;
   (h) "Professional Compensation Fund" or "PCF" refers to the dedicated special
       trust fund of the Board established under this Act;
   (i) "Professional Wage Order" or "PWO" refers to the binding order issued by the
       Board prescribing MPC rates for a covered profession or group of professions,
       which shall have the force and effect of law upon effectivity;



                                                                                      5
   (j) "Salary Grade" or "SG" refers to the salary grade classification under the Salary
       Standardization Law (Republic Act No. 11466, as amended) and its
       implementing rules, as periodically updated by the Department of Budget and
       Management;
   (k) "SSL Floor Reference" refers to the monthly basic salary corresponding to the
       SSL salary grade assigned to the equivalent government position for a given
       covered profession, as determined by the Board in consultation with the DBM
       and PRC, which shall serve as the statutory minimum for MPC rate-setting;
   (l) "Salary Differential" refers to the difference between the applicable MPC and
       the actual compensation received by a covered professional, which the
       employer is obligated to pay in full; and
   (m) "Technical Secretariat" refers to the permanent administrative and technical
       staff of the Board.

                   ARTICLE III — THE PROFESSIONAL WAGE BOARD


SEC. 8. Creation. There is hereby created the Professional Wage Board as an attached
agency of the Department of Labor and Employment. The Board shall exercise its
quasi-judicial, regulatory, and enforcement powers free from the direction or control
of the DOLE Secretary, except as otherwise expressly provided by law. The Board shall
have its principal office in Metro Manila and Regional Professional Wage Offices in
every administrative region of the Philippines.

SEC. 9. Composition. The Board shall be composed of twelve (12) voting members as
follows:

   (a) A Chairperson, appointed by the President;
   (b) Eight (8) Regular Members representing the four Professional Clusters — two
       (2) per cluster:
       (i) Cluster A — Health and Allied Sciences: Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy,
             Dentistry, Medical Technology, Physical Therapy, Nutrition and Dietetics,
             Midwifery, Optometry, Radiologic Technology, Respiratory Therapy, and
             allied health professions;
       (ii) Cluster B — Engineering, Architecture, and Technology: Civil, Electrical,
             Mechanical, Chemical, Electronics, Geodetic, Naval Architecture and
             Marine Engineering, Sanitary Engineering, Architecture, and related
             technical professions;
       (iii) Cluster C — Business, Management, and Social Sciences: Accountancy,
             Criminology, Customs Brokerage, Real Estate Service, Psychology, Social
             Work, Guidance Counseling, and related professions; and
       (iv) Cluster D — Education, Arts, and Humanities: Teaching profession,
             Librarianship, Interior Design, Landscape Architecture, and related
             professions;

                                                                                      6
   (c) One (1) representative from the private employer sector, nominated by the
       Philippine Chamber of Commerce and Industry; and
   (d) One (1) representative from the labor sector, nominated by the most
       representative labor federation as certified by the Bureau of Labor Relations.

The Secretary of Labor and Employment, or a permanently designated alternate of at
least Undersecretary rank, shall serve as a permanent non-voting resource person and
shall attend all Board sessions in that capacity. The PRC Chairperson, or a designated
alternate, shall likewise serve as a permanent non-voting technical resource person.

SEC. 10. Nomination of Cluster Representatives. For each Professional Cluster, the
Accredited Professional Organizations within the cluster shall, through a process
facilitated by the PRC, collectively nominate three (3) candidates for each of the two
(2) cluster seats. Nominees must be licensed members of a profession within the
represented cluster with at least ten (10) years of active professional practice or
organizational leadership. The PRC shall transmit the shortlisted nominees to the
Selection Committee within thirty (30) days of request.

SEC. 11. The Selection Committee. A Selection Committee shall screen and endorse
nominees for the Chairperson and Regular Member positions. It shall be composed of:
   (a) The Chairperson of the Civil Service Commission, as Presiding Officer;
   (b) The Chairperson of the Professional Regulation Commission;
   (c) The Director-General of the National Economic and Development Authority;
       and
   (d) The Secretary of Labor and Employment.

Each member of the Selection Committee may designate a permanent alternate of
not lower than Undersecretary rank or its equivalent to act on their behalf in all
Selection Committee proceedings. Such designation shall be in writing, addressed to
the Presiding Officer, and shall remain effective until revoked in writing. The
designated alternate shall have full authority to deliberate, vote, and sign documents
on behalf of the principal. The Selection Committee shall convene within thirty (30)
days from any vacancy, promulgate its own procedural rules, and submit an endorsed
shortlist of three (3) nominees per position to the President within forty-five (45) days
from convening. Decisions shall be by majority vote, with the Presiding Officer casting
the deciding vote in case of a tie. The Technical Secretariat shall provide administrative
support to the Committee.

SEC. 12. Qualifications of the Chairperson. The Chairperson shall:

   (a) Be a natural-born Filipino citizen, at least forty (40) years of age;
   (b) Hold a valid professional license issued by the PRC or any recognized
       professional regulatory body in any field of practice — including but not limited


                                                                                        7
       to health sciences, engineering, architecture, business, law, education, or any
       allied profession;
   (c) Have at least fifteen (15) years of combined experience in any of the following
       fields: professional practice, public administration, professional organization
       leadership, labor relations, compensation management, human resource
       management, or regulatory administration;
   (d) Have demonstrated knowledge of or significant exposure to compensation
       policy, labor markets, professional regulation, or institutional governance;
   (e) Be of proven integrity and probity with no final conviction of any crime
       involving moral turpitude; and
   (f) Not be a current officer of any political party at the time of appointment.

SEC. 13. Qualifications of Regular Members. Each Regular Member shall:

   (a) Be a natural-born Filipino citizen, at least thirty-five (35) years of age;
   (b) In the case of cluster representatives: hold a valid professional license within
       their represented cluster;
   (c) In the case of the employer and labor representatives: possess at least ten (10)
       years of relevant experience in employer relations, labor relations, or
       workforce development;
   (d) Be of good moral character with no final conviction of any crime involving
       moral turpitude; and
   (e) Not be a current officer of any political party at the time of appointment.

SEC. 14. Term of Office. The Chairperson and Regular Members shall each serve a
single non-renewable term of six (6) years. To ensure staggered succession among the
first set of appointees, four (4) members determined by lot shall serve an initial term
of three (3) years; the remainder shall serve the full six (6) years. Vacancies shall be
filled within sixty (60) days in the same manner as the original appointment; the
appointee shall serve only the unexpired term.

SEC. 15. Compensation. The Chairperson shall receive compensation and benefits
equivalent to a Department Secretary. Regular Members shall receive those
equivalents to an Undersecretary. All members shall receive corresponding
allowances, insurance coverage, GSIS, PhilHealth, and PAGIBIG benefits.

SEC. 16. Conflict of Interest. During tenure, no member shall hold any paid position in
any professional organization or private enterprise within the Board's jurisdiction;
accept gifts or benefits from parties to any proceeding; or engage in partisan political
activity. Within two (2) years after their term, no member shall appear before or
accept employment with any party that had a pending case before the Board during
their tenure.



                                                                                      8
SEC. 17. Causes for Removal. Members may be removed by the President upon
recommendation of the Selection Committee for: culpable violation of this Act or the
Constitution; graft and corruption; gross neglect or incompetence; grave misconduct;
undisclosed conflict of interest; or permanent physical or mental incapacity. No
removal shall be effected without written notice, opportunity to be heard, and formal
investigation.

SEC. 18. Quorum and Voting. Seven (7) members shall constitute a quorum. Decisions
require a majority of members present. Issuance of a Professional Wage Order
requires the affirmative vote of at least seven (7) members. Reduction of existing
minimum rates requires nine (9) affirmative votes.

SEC. 19. Meetings. The Board shall hold regular meetings at least twice a month.
Deliberations on wage orders shall be in open session. Minutes shall be published
within fifteen (15) days of each meeting.

                ARTICLE IV — THE PROFESSIONAL WAGE COUNCIL


SEC. 20. Creation and Nature. There is hereby created the Professional Wage Council
as the permanent consultative and recommendatory arm of the Board. The Council is
not a separate government agency; it operates within the Board's institutional
framework, serviced by the Technical Secretariat, and funded from the Professional
Compensation Fund. Its function is recommendatory: the Council conducts
stakeholder-driven multi-sector consultations on proposed Professional Wage Orders
and transmits formal endorsed recommendations to the Board for deliberation and
issuance.

SEC. 21. Composition. The Council shall be composed of twenty-three (23) members:

   (a) Sixteen (16) professional sector seats — four (4) per Professional Cluster —
       allocated among the APOs within each cluster through a rotation mechanism
       prescribed by the Board's implementing rules, ensuring that every APO within
       a cluster is represented within a three-year cycle;
   (b) Four (4) employer sector seats — one (1) each from: the Philippine Chamber
       of Commerce and Industry; the Employers Confederation of the Philippines;
       the Financial Executives Institute of the Philippines; and a representative of
       micro, small, and medium enterprises nominated by the Department of Trade
       and Industry;
   (c) One (1) labor sector seat, nominated by the most representative labor
       federation;
   (d) One (1) civil society seat, nominated by accredited consumer and civil society
       coalitions; and



                                                                                   9
   (e) One (1) academic or research seat — a compensation economist or labor
       market specialist nominated by the Commission on Higher Education from
       among university faculty.

SEC. 22. Officers of the Council. The Council shall elect from among its professional
cluster members a President, a Vice-President, and a Secretary, each serving a two-
year term renewable once. The President shall preside over all Council sessions and
formally transmit the Council Recommendation to the Board. The Executive Director
of the Technical Secretariat shall serve as the non-voting administrative liaison of the
Council.

SEC. 23. Term of Council Members. Each Council member shall serve a term of two
(2) years, renewable for one (1) additional term. APO seats shall rotate among
constituent APOs within each cluster in accordance with the implementing rules.

SEC. 24. Functions of the Council. The Council shall:

   (a) Upon request of the Board or upon its own initiative, conduct multi-sector
       consultations on proposed Professional Wage Orders, including cluster-
       specific and profession-specific sessions;
   (b) Gather, consolidate, and analyze position papers, compensation data, and
       stakeholder inputs from all represented sectors;
   (c) Prepare and transmit to the Board a formal Council Recommendation for each
       proposed wage order, specifying: (i) recommended MPC rates with their basis;
       (ii) a summary of each sector's position; (iii) areas of consensus and
       disagreement; and (iv) the Council's reasoning;
   (d) Monitor implementation of issued wage orders and report compliance
       concerns to the Board;
   (e) Conduct or commission independent research on professional compensation
       and brain drain, and share findings with the Board; and
   (f) Submit an Annual State of Professional Compensation Report to the Board.

SEC. 25. Consultative Process. When the Board initiates a wage review, it shall
simultaneously direct the Council to commence the consultative process. The Council
shall:

   (a) Publish a Notice of Consultation at least forty-five (45) days before
       transmitting its Recommendation;
   (b) Conduct not less than four (4) regional consultations distributed across Luzon,
       Visayas, Mindanao, and Metro Manila;
   (c) Conduct at least one (1) cluster-specific or profession-specific technical session
       per affected cluster;



                                                                                      10
   (d) Accept written submissions for at least thirty (30) days from the last public
       consultation; and
   (e) Transmit the Council Recommendation to the Board within fifteen (15) days
       after the close of the submission period.
SEC. 26. Effect of Council Recommendation. The Board shall not issue a Professional
Wage Order without first receiving and considering the Council Recommendation,
except under the urgent economic necessity procedure of Section 38. Upon receipt,
the Board shall: publish the Recommendation on its official website within five (5)
working days; deliberate on it in open session; and, if the issued wage order deviates
materially from the Recommendation, publish a detailed written explanation
simultaneously with the wage order.

SEC. 27. Binding Authority of the Board. The Council Recommendation carries
persuasive weight but does not bind the Board. The Board's quasi-judicial authority to
fix MPC rates remains plenary and independent. Deviation from the
Recommendation, properly explained and documented, shall not constitute a ground
for annulment of a wage order.

SEC. 28. Compensation of Council Members. Council members shall receive per
diems, transportation allowances, and reimbursement of reasonable expenses in
amounts fixed by the Board's implementing rules, funded from the Professional
Compensation Fund. Council membership does not constitute government service for
GSIS purposes.

   ARTICLE V — TECHNICAL SECRETARIAT AND REGIONAL PROFESSIONAL WAGE
                                 OFFICES


SEC. 29. Technical Secretariat. The Board shall be supported by a permanent Technical
Secretariat headed by an Executive Director — a licensed professional with at least
ten (10) years of relevant experience — appointed by the Chairperson with the
concurrence of a majority of Regular Members. The Secretariat shall consist of:

   (a) Research and Standards Division — compensation studies, wage methodology,
       economic modeling, and technical preparation of draft wage orders;
   (b) Legal and Adjudication Division — legal research, quasi-judicial proceedings,
       enforcement coordination;
   (c) Stakeholder Engagement Division — servicing the Council, organizing
       consultations, liaison with APOs and employer associations;
   (d) Compliance and Enforcement Division — receiving complaints, conducting
       inspections, monitoring compliance, operating the Professional Wage
       Violation Registry; and
   (e) Finance and Administrative Division — budget, human resources, information
       technology, public communications, and records management.

                                                                                   11
SEC. 30. Regional Professional Wage Offices. The Board shall establish one (1)
Regional Professional Wage Office (RPWO) in each administrative region, co-located
with the DOLE regional office wherever practicable. Each RPWO shall be headed by a
Regional Director and staffed with at minimum a legal officer, a research officer, and
a compliance officer. RPWOs shall receive complaints, conduct regional inspections,
facilitate Council consultations, and refer cases beyond their jurisdiction to the Board
with a complete record.

SEC. 31. Civil Service Coverage. All positions in the Technical Secretariat and RPWOs
shall be classified under the civil service and filled in accordance with Civil Service
Commission rules. Existing DOLE personnel performing related functions may be
transferred to the Board with their status, rank, and benefits intact.

              ARTICLE VI — POWERS AND FUNCTIONS OF THE BOARD


SEC. 32. Powers and Functions. The Board shall:

   (a) Determine, fix, review, and adjust MPC rates for all covered professions
       through Professional Wage Orders, anchored to the SSL floor reference system
       established in Article VII;
   (b) Conduct or commission research and surveys on professional compensation,
       brain drain, and international benchmarks;
   (c) Hear and decide all complaints and disputes arising under this Act in the
       exercise of quasi-judicial authority, with primary, exclusive, and original
       jurisdiction;
   (d) Issue subpoenas, subpoenas duces tecum, and other compulsory processes;
       enforce compliance through contempt proceedings;
   (e) Conduct employer inspections without prior warrant, in the exercise of
       visitorial powers consistent with Article 128 of the Labor Code;
   (f) Issue cease-and-desist orders and writs of execution;
   (g) Promulgate and enforce implementing rules and regulations;
   (h) Impose administrative sanctions, order salary differential payments, and
       award damages;
   (i) Maintain and publish the Professional Wage Violation Registry;
   (j) Coordinate with BIR, SSS, GSIS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG for compensation
       record cross-matching;
   (k) Coordinate with the Civil Service Commission and DBM for enforcement
       involving government employers;
   (l) Publish all wage orders, decisions, and reports in print and online; and
   (m) Recommend legislative amendments to Congress as conditions warrant.



                                                                                     12
SEC. 33. Resolution of Cases. The Board shall resolve all cases within thirty (30)
working days from completion of pleadings or formal hearings, extendible once by the
Chairperson for another thirty (30) working days upon written order.

SEC 34. Urgent Economic Necessity. In cases of grave national economic emergency
officially declared by the President, the Board may, by an affirmative vote of nine (9)
members, issue an interim Professional Wage Order without awaiting the full Council
consultative process. Such interim order shall be effective for not more than one (1)
year and shall be replaced through the full normal process within that period.

 ARTICLE VII — SALARY STANDARDIZATION LAW ALIGNMENT AND PROFESSIONAL
                             WAGE ORDERS


SEC. 35. SSL as the Statutory Minimum Floor. The Salary Standardization Law
(Republic Act No. 11466, as amended, and all future SSL enactments) shall serve as
the statutory minimum floor reference for all Professional Wage Orders issued under
this Act. Specifically:

   (a) For each covered profession, the Board shall identify the equivalent
       government position and its corresponding SSL Salary Grade (SG) in
       consultation with the Civil Service Commission and the Department of Budget
       and Management;
   (b) The monthly basic salary corresponding to the identified SSL Salary Grade —
       at its lowest step increment — shall constitute the SSL Floor Reference for that
       profession;
   (c) No Professional Wage Order may prescribe an MPC rate below the applicable
       SSL Floor Reference for the covered profession; and
   (d) When the SSL is amended or updated by Congress or by the President, raising
       the monthly basic salary for any Salary Grade, the MPC rate for all professions
       whose SSL Floor Reference is pegged to that Salary Grade shall automatically
       adjust upward to match the new SSL rate within thirty (30) days of the SSL
       amendment's effectivity, without need of a new wage order. The Board shall
       publish a notice of such automatic adjustment within fifteen (15) days of the
       SSL amendment's effectivity.

SEC. 36. SSL Grade Equivalency Schedule. Within ninety (90) days from effectivity of
this Act and within sixty (60) days from the enactment of any amendment to the SSL,
the Board shall publish and maintain a Professional SSL Grade Equivalency Schedule,
which shall:

   (a) List all covered professions and their corresponding SSL Salary Grade
       equivalent;
   (b) Specify the applicable SSL Floor Reference rate for each profession;


                                                                                    13
   (c) Be developed in consultation with the Civil Service Commission, DBM, PRC, and
       the Professional Wage Council; and
   (d) Be updated within sixty (60) days of any amendment to the SSL.

The Professional SSL Grade Equivalency Schedule shall be published in the Official
Gazette, in two (2) newspapers of general circulation, and on the Board's online portal,
and shall have the force of a Board resolution.

SEC. 37. Board Authority to Set Higher Rates. Nothing in this Article shall prevent the
Board from setting MPC rates above the SSL Floor Reference for any profession. The
Board shall set rates above the SSL Floor Reference when:

   (a) Brain drain data demonstrates that the SSL Floor Reference is insufficient to
       retain professionals in the domestic labor market;
   (b) Prevailing private sector compensation rates significantly exceed the SSL Floor
       Reference;
   (c) International or ASEAN-regional benchmarks demonstrate that the SSL Floor
       Reference is globally non-competitive; or
   (d) The Council Recommendation, supported by documented evidence,
       recommends higher rates.

SEC. 38. Authority to Issue Wage Orders. The Board shall issue Professional Wage
Orders fixing MPC rates classified by profession or group of professions, industry or
sector, geographic area, and career level or years of experience where appropriate.
All wage orders shall be anchored to the SSL Floor Reference system and shall comply
with the mandatory consultative process.

SEC. 39. Wage-Setting Criteria. In determining MPC rates above the SSL Floor
Reference, the Board shall consider and document in a Technical Basis Report:

   (a) The SSL Floor Reference and the basis for any upward adjustment;
   (b) Cost of living and prevailing domestic compensation rates for the profession;
   (c) Educational requirements, licensure obligations, and continuing professional
       education burdens;
   (d) Professional liability and public trust inherent in the practice;
   (e) International and ASEAN-regional benchmark compensation;
   (f) Documented brain drain rate and trend, using PRC, POEA, and CFO data;
   (g) Supply and demand conditions in the domestic professional labor market;
   (h) Financial capacity of employers, distinguishing enterprise sizes and sector
       types;
   (i) Overall macroeconomic conditions; and
   (j) The Council Recommendation and APO and employer association submissions.


                                                                                     14
SEC. 40. Mandatory Consultative Process. No Professional Wage Order prescribing
rates above the SSL Floor Reference shall be issued without compliance with the
following:
    (a) Simultaneous notice to the Board and the Council initiating the review;
    (b) Council consultation of at least forty-five (45) days including regional public
        hearings;
    (c) Publication of the Council Recommendation and the Technical Basis Report
        before Board deliberation; and
    (d) Board deliberation in open session, with the written explanation requirement
        of Section 26 observed if deviating from the Council Recommendation.

Wage orders that merely implement automatic SSL floor adjustments under Section
35(d) are not subject to this consultative process, as they operate by force of law.

SEC. 41. Effectivity of Wage Orders. Every Professional Wage Order shall be published
in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation and in the Official Gazette and
shall take effect not earlier than thirty (30) days after complete publication. It shall
specify the professions covered, the applicable SSL Floor Reference, the MPC rates,
and the period of validity.

SEC. 42. Mandatory Review. The Board shall conduct a mandatory review of all wage
orders at least once every three (3) years. An extraordinary review shall be initiated
within sixty (60) days upon: a cumulative CPI increase exceeding twelve percent (12%)
since the last order; a petition by an APO supported by thirty percent (30%) of its
membership; a petition by a recognized employer association; or a majority Board
resolution.

SEC. 43. Non-Diminution. No wage order shall reduce existing MPC rates below the
applicable SSL Floor Reference, ever. No wage order shall reduce MPC rates above the
SSL Floor Reference except upon: a finding of extraordinary economic dislocation
supported by verified data; nine (9) affirmative Board votes; and endorsement by the
affected APOs. Any such reduction shall be temporary, not exceeding two (2) years.

SEC. 44. Exemptions. An employer may apply for temporary exemption within thirty
(30) days of a wage order's effectivity if it is: a distressed employer with net operating
losses in the two (2) preceding fiscal years evidenced by certified audited financial
statements; a micro-enterprise in its first three (3) years of operations; or a duly
registered non-stock, non-profit organization with certified limited funding. No
exemption shall reduce MPC below the SSL Floor Reference. Exemptions shall not
exceed two (2) years and are subject to monthly financial reporting. Government
agencies, GOCCs, and large enterprises are ineligible for exemptions.

        ARTICLE VIII — OBLIGATIONS OF EMPLOYERS AND PROFESSIONALS

                                                                                       15
SEC. 45. Duty to Pay. Every employer — whether government or private — shall pay
covered professionals not less than the applicable MPC in Philippine currency, not less
frequently than twice a month. No scheme, device, or intermediary arrangement shall
be used to reduce effective compensation below the MPC.

SEC. 46. Government Employer Compliance. All government agencies, local
government units, SUCs, GOCCs, and other government employers shall include in
their annual budget submissions to the DBM the amounts necessary to comply with
applicable Professional Wage Orders. The DBM shall prioritize the funding of MPC
compliance in the National Expenditure Program. Government employers shall not
use insufficiency of appropriations as a defense to the payment of salary differentials
beyond the transition period prescribed in Section 71.

SEC. 47. Record-Keeping. All employers shall keep for at least ten (10) years:
employment contracts; payroll records and pay slips; copies of applicable wage
orders; and professional license records of all engaged professionals. Records shall be
produced to Board inspectors within forty-eight (48) hours of demand. Failure to
produce records creates a prima facie presumption of violation.

SEC. 48. Annual Compliance Report. Employers engaging five (5) or more covered
professionals shall file a sworn Annual Employer Compliance Report with the Board
not later than January 31 of each year, disclosing: number and classification of covered
professionals engaged; their compensation relative to applicable MPC rates; any
exemptions in effect; and the SSL Floor Reference applicable to each profession
engaged.

SEC. 49. Posting Requirement. Every employer shall post conspicuously at the
workplace the text or a Board-approved summary of all applicable Professional Wage
Orders, including the SSL Floor Reference rates, and include this in every contract
offered to a covered professional.

SEC. 50. Non-Waiver. No covered professional shall, under coercion, duress, or any
form of pressure, waive any right or benefit under this Act or an applicable wage order.
Any such waiver shall be void ab initio.

            ARTICLE IX — PROHIBITED ACTS AND UNLAWFUL PRACTICES


SEC. 51. Prohibited Acts of Employers. The following are prohibited:

   (a) Paying a covered professional below the applicable MPC — whether directly
       or through wage-splitting, false classification, labor-only contracting,
       intermediary arrangements, or any other device;

                                                                                     16
   (b) Requiring any covered professional to return, rebate, or give back any part of
       compensation paid;
   (c) Coercing or inducing a covered professional to waive any right or benefit under
       this Act;
   (d) Retaliating against a covered professional for: filing a complaint; participating
       in Board proceedings; providing information to Board inspectors; or exercising
       any right under this Act — including through demotion, suspension, pay
       reduction, constructive dismissal, non-renewal, or blacklisting;
   (e) Obstructing, delaying, or refusing a lawful Board inspection or investigation;
   (f) Maintaining false, incomplete, or fraudulent payroll or employment records;
   (g) Misrepresenting to a covered professional the applicable MPC rates or SSL
       Floor Reference;
   (h) Failing to file the Annual Employer Compliance Report; and
   (i) Aiding, abetting, or inducing another employer or intermediary to commit any
       of the foregoing.

SEC. 52. Prohibited Acts of Intermediaries. No placement agency, staffing firm, or
labor contractor shall: place a covered professional for compensation below the MPC;
deduct fees that effectively reduce compensation below the MPC; or assist in any
scheme to evade employer obligations. Intermediaries in violation are jointly and
solidarily liable with the employer and subject to cancellation of their license to
operate.

SEC. 53. Prohibited Acts of Board Members and Staff. No member of the Board,
officer, or employee of the Secretariat or RPWO shall: solicit or accept gifts or benefits
from parties to any proceeding; disclose confidential information for personal gain;
participate in proceedings where they have a conflict of interest; or issue a wage order
or decide a case without following this Act's mandatory procedures.

             ARTICLE X — ENFORCEMENT, SANCTIONS, AND PENALTIES


SEC. 54. Complaint Procedure. Any covered professional, representative, labor
organization, or any person may file a complaint with the Board or any RPWO.
Complaints are free of charge, confidential upon written request, acknowledged
within forty-eight (48) hours, and assigned a docket number. Any adverse
employment action taken against a complainant within six (6) months of filing is prima
facie evidence of retaliation.

SEC. 55. Inspection Powers. Authorized Board representatives may enter any
workplace at reasonable times; examine all employment records, payrolls, and
contracts; interview any covered professional in private; and photograph or copy
relevant documents. Obstruction of inspection is a separate violation.



                                                                                       17
SEC. 56. Salary Differential Recovery. A violating employer shall pay the full salary
differential with legal interest at the BSP-prescribed rate, from the date the obligation
fell due. The right to recover shall not prescribe in less than three (3) years from
discovery.

SEC. 57. Administrative Sanctions. The Board may impose the following, separately
or jointly:

   (a) Order of Payment of the full salary differential with interest within fifteen (15)
       working days from finality;
   (b) Administrative Fine per affected professional:
       (v) First violation: Php 50,000 to Php 200,000;
       (vi) Second violation: Php 250,000 to Php 500,000; and
       (vii) Third and subsequent violations: Php 750,000 to Php 2,000,000;
   (c) Suspension of operations for up to six (6) months;
   (d) Recommendation for revocation of business permit or license to operate;
   (e) Disqualification from government procurement for up to three (3) years; and
   (f) Listing in the Professional Wage Violation Registry.

For government employers, the Board shall, in lieu of business sanctions, refer final
and executory violations to the Civil Service Commission, the Commission on Audit,
and the Office of the Ombudsman for appropriate administrative action against the
responsible officers. All fines collected shall be remitted to the Professional
Compensation Fund.

SEC. 58. Criminal Penalties. The following are criminal offenses prosecuted before the
Regional Trial Court:

   (a) Willful refusal to pay MPC after a final Order of Payment: imprisonment of two
       (2) to six (6) years, or a fine of not less than Php 1,000,000 per affected
       professional, or both;
   (b) Retaliation against a covered professional for exercising rights under this Act:
       imprisonment of one (1) to three (3) years, or a fine of not less than Php
       500,000, or both, plus mandatory reinstatement with full back pay;
   (c) Obstruction of a lawful Board inspection: imprisonment of six (6) months to
       two (2) years, or a fine of not less than Php 250,000, or both;
   (d) Submission of false payroll records or compliance reports: imprisonment of
       two (2) to eight (8) years, or a fine of not less than Php 500,000, or both; and
   (e) Violation by an intermediary of Section 52: imprisonment of one (1) to four (4)
       years, or a fine of not less than Php 300,000, or both.

If the offender is a corporation or other juridical entity, criminal liability shall be
imposed upon all responsible officers — including the President, CEO, CFO, CHRO, and

                                                                                      18
any officer who directed, authorized, ratified, or knowingly failed to remedy the
violation — in addition to the fine imposed on the entity. For government employers,
responsible officials shall be liable under Republic Act No. 3019 (Anti-Graft and
Corrupt Practices Act) and Republic Act No. 6713, in addition to liability under this Act.

SEC. 59. Civil Liability. A violating employer shall be civilly liable for: the full salary
differential with legal interest; actual damages; moral damages of not less than Php
50,000 per affected professional; exemplary damages when the violation is attended
by fraud, malice, or bad faith; and attorney's fees and litigation costs.

SEC. 60. Solidary Liability. The principal employer and any intermediary shall be jointly
and solidarily liable for all salary differentials, fines, and civil liability under this Act.

SEC. 61. Whistleblower Protection and Reward. Any person reporting a violation to
the Board shall be entitled to: confidentiality of identity upon request; protection from
retaliation; the right to file a separate retaliation complaint; and a monetary reward
equal to ten percent (10%) of the total administrative fine collected, funded from the
PCF.

SEC. 62. Prescription. Complaints shall be filed within three (3) years from the
violation or its discovery, whichever is later. The period is tolled by filing a complaint
with the Board or any RPWO, or by written employer acknowledgment of the
obligation.

SEC. 63. Contempt. The Board may cite any person for contempt for defying or
obstructing its orders. Fines of up to Php 50,000 per act may be imposed; for indirect
contempt, imprisonment of up to six (6) months may be recommended to the
appropriate court.

SEC. 64. Compromise and Settlement. Parties may settle at any stage subject to Board
approval. No compromise shall be valid if the professional receives less than the full
salary differential, unless the Board finds genuine factual uncertainty. Administrative
fines are not reducible through compromise without Board approval.

                     ARTICLE XI — APPEALS AND JUDICIAL REVIEW


SEC. 65. Appeals from RPWOs. Parties aggrieved by an RPWO decision may appeal to
the Board within fifteen (15) working days from receipt. Filing an appeal does not stay
execution unless the Board issues a TRO upon motion.

SEC. 66. Appeals from the Board. Parties aggrieved by a final Board decision may
appeal to the Court of Appeals within fifteen (15) days from receipt, by verified


                                                                                           19
petition for review under Rule 43 of the Rules of Court. No administrative appeal shall
lie to the DOLE Secretary or the Office of the President.
SEC. 67. Stay of Execution. An appeal does not stay execution of a salary differential
payment order unless the Court of Appeals so directs upon the employer's posting of
a cash bond equal to the full amount adjudged, held in escrow until final resolution.

SEC. 68. Execution of Final Orders. Final decisions shall be enforced through: writs of
execution implemented with law enforcement assistance; garnishment of bank
accounts; levy on property; and referral to the BIR for tax lien purposes.

     ARTICLE XII — FUNDING AND THE PROFESSIONAL COMPENSATION FUND


SEC. 69. Initial Appropriation. The amount of Five Hundred Million Pesos (Php
500,000,000.00) is hereby appropriated from the General Fund for the initial
operational requirements of the Board, including its principal office, all Regional
Professional Wage Offices, staffing, and information technology infrastructure.

SEC. 70. Annual Appropriation. The funds necessary for continued operations shall be
included annually in the General Appropriations Act under a dedicated budget line
item for the Professional Wage Board. The Board shall prepare and submit its budget
directly to the DBM. DOLE shall not modify, reduce, or condition the Board's budget
submission.

SEC. 71. Professional Compensation Fund. There is established the Professional
Compensation Fund as a special trust fund of the Board, maintained in a government
depository bank and audited by the Commission on Audit, funded from:

   (a) Fifty percent (50%) of all administrative fines collected;
   (b) Two percent (2%) of all professional regulatory fees collected by the PRC
       annually, remitted as a statutory charge directly to the PCF;
   (c) Grants and donations from domestic or foreign entities, subject to applicable
       law; and
   (d) Income from authorized investments in risk-free government securities.

The PCF shall be used for: operational expenses not covered by annual appropriation;
Council member per diems and allowances; the whistleblower reward program; legal
aid for covered professionals without means; and research and data-gathering
programs. A full audited accounting shall be published in each Annual Report.

SEC. 72. Fiscal Autonomy. The Board shall allocate and disburse its appropriated
budget and PCF resources without prior DOLE or DBM approval, subject to COA
auditing rules.


                                                                                    20
 ARTICLE XIII — TRANSPARENCY, REPORTING, AND CONGRESSIONAL OVERSIGHT


SEC. 73. Annual Report. The Board shall submit a comprehensive Annual Report to
the President, the Senate, and the House of Representatives not later than March 31
of each year, covering: all wage orders in effect including SSL Floor References; brain
drain statistics by profession; enforcement statistics; PCF financial report; compliance
assessment by sector and region; and legislative recommendations.

SEC. 74. Public Transparency Portal. The Board shall maintain a continuously updated
online portal containing: all Professional Wage Orders and the Professional SSL Grade
Equivalency Schedule; the Professional Wage Violation Registry; all Annual Reports
and Technical Basis Reports; Council Recommendations; minutes of open Board
meetings; and the Board's budget and PCF status.

SEC. 75. Congressional Oversight. The Senate Committee on Labor, Employment, and
Human Resources Development and the House Committee on Labor and Employment
shall jointly conduct annual oversight hearings, to which the Chairperson and
Executive Director shall respond in person.

                ARTICLE XIV — TRANSITORY AND FINAL PROVISIONS


SEC. 76. Priority Wage Orders. Within one hundred eighty (180) days from effectivity,
the Board shall publish the initial Professional SSL Grade Equivalency Schedule and
issue initial Professional Wage Orders for the ten (10) professions with the highest
documented overseas migration rates. An expedited Council consultation of not less
than forty-five (45) days may be used for these initial orders.

SEC. 77. Complete Coverage Timeline. Within three (3) years from effectivity, the
Board shall have issued Professional Wage Orders covering all PRC-regulated
professions. An implementation timeline shall be published within ninety (90) days of
the Board's organization.

SEC. 78. Government Employer Transition. Government agencies, LGUs, SUCs, and
GOCCs are granted a transition period of one (1) full budget cycle — the period from
the effectivity of an applicable wage order to the end of the immediately following
fiscal year — to secure appropriations for compliance. During this transition period,
the affected government employer shall not be subject to administrative fines, but
shall remain obligated to pay salary differentials with legal interest from the date the
obligation fell due. After the transition period, all sanctions under this Act apply fully
to government employers, and their responsible officers are subject to administrative
and criminal liability.

                                                                                       21
SEC. 79. Implementing Rules and Regulations. The Board, in consultation with the
Council, DOLE, CSC, DBM, PRC, and stakeholder groups, shall promulgate the
Implementing Rules and Regulations within ninety (90) days from effectivity, to be
published in two (2) newspapers of general circulation and effective fifteen (15) days
after publication.

SEC. 80. Transition of Personnel. DOLE officers and employees currently performing
functions related to professional compensation administration may be transferred to
the Board with their civil service status, rank, salaries, and benefits intact.

SEC. 81. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared invalid, the
remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 82. Repealing Clause. All laws, executive orders, and regulations inconsistent
with this Act are hereby amended, modified, or repealed accordingly. The Labor Code
of the Philippines, Republic Act No. 8981, and Republic Act No. 11466, insofar as not
inconsistent with this Act, shall apply suppletorily.

SEC. 83. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation,
whichever comes earlier.
