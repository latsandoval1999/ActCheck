                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


      The Pre-Employment Medical Examination (PEME) is intended to ensure that
Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) are medically fit to perform their duties abroad.
However, in practice, PEME has increasingly become a source of abuse, financial
burden, and exploitation—particularly for seafarers and other vulnerable migrant
workers.
        Numerous OFWs are compelled to undergo PEME only in clinics designated
by manning agencies or employers, often at excessive cost, with repeated testing,
questionable medical findings, and little transparency. Many are unfairly declared
“unfit” based on vague or outdated standards, resulting in lost employment
opportunities, mounting debt, and psychological distress. These practices undermine
the very purpose of PEME and violate the spirit of the Migrant Workers and Overseas
Filipinos Act of 1995.
      This measure seeks to restore PEME to its rightful role as a protective health
mechanism, not a barrier to employment. It affirms the right of OFWs to choose
accredited medical facilities, ensures fair cost allocation, strengthens data privacy,
modernizes medical and psychological standards, and imposes accountability on
PEME clinics and stakeholders.
        By institutionalizing fairness, proportionality, and transparency, this bill
advances the State’s constitutional duty to protect the dignity, welfare, and rights of
Filipino workers, wherever they may be employed.
      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
  ESTABLISHING FAIR, TRANSPARENT, AND RIGHTS-BASED STANDARDS
FOR THE PRE-EMPLOYMENT MEDICAL EXAMINATION (PEME) OF OVERSEAS
    FILIPINO WORKERS, PROVIDING SAFEGUARDS AGAINST ABUSIVE
               PRACTICES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Pre-Employment
Medical Examination (PEME) for OFWs Act.”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect the rights,
dignity, and welfare of Overseas Filipino Workers by ensuring that pre-employment
medical examinations are conducted fairly, ethically, transparently, and in accordance
with international standards, without imposing undue financial or psychological burden
on workers.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act, the following terms
are hereby defined:

       a) Accredited Medical Facility refers to a clinic that passed the accreditation
          requirements of the Department of Health to conduct Pre-Employment
          Medical Examination for land-based overseas workers and seafarers on
          inter-island and overseas employment.

       b) Fit-to-Work refers to the determination, as reflected in a valid medical
          certificate, that an Overseas Filipino Worker is reasonably expected to be
          able to meet the minimum physical, mental, and psychological requirements
          necessary to safely and effectively perform the routine and emergency




                                                                                           1
          duties specific to the position for which the worker is being deployed, during
          the period of validity of the medical certificate.

          Such medical certificate shall not be construed as a certification of general
          health nor as a declaration of the absence of illness or medical condition,
          but solely as an assessment of the worker’s fitness in relation to the actual
          demands and risks of the specific job to be performed.

      c) Manning agency refers to a natural or juridical person duly licensed by the
         Secretary of the Department of Migrant Workers to engage in the
         recruitment and placement of seafarers;

      d) Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) refers to a Filipino who is to be engaged,
         is engaged, or has been engaged in remunerated activity in a country of
         which he or she is not an immigrant, citizen, or permanent resident or is not
         awaiting naturalization, recognition, or admission, whether land-based or
         sea-based regardless of status; excluding a Filipino engaged under a
         government-recognized exchange visitor program for cultural and
         educational purposes. For purposes of this provision, a person engaged in
         remunerated activity covers a person who has been contracted for overseas
         employment but has yet to leave the Philippines, regardless of status, and
         includes “Overseas Contract Workers”. The term “OFW” is synonymous to
         “Migrant Worker”;

      e) Recruitment agency refers to a natural or juridical person duly licensed by
         the Secretary of the Department of Migrant Workers to engage in the
         recruitment and placement of land-based OFWs;

      f) Pre-Employment Medical Examination (PEME) refers to the complete
         medical examination being conducted to an applicant as a requirement for
         screening to determine whether physically and mentally fit to work abroad.

      g) Principal refers to an employer or foreign placement agency hiring or
         engaging Filipino workers for overseas employment through a licensed
         private recruitment/manning agency.

      SEC. 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all OFWs, including seafarers, land-
based workers, and other migrant workers required to undergo Pre-Employment
Medical Examinations as a condition for overseas employment.

       SEC. 5. Freedom of Choice of Pre-Employment Medical Examination
Clinic. – OFWs shall have the right to undergo their Pre-Employment Medical
Examination (PEME) in any medical facility accredited by the Department of Health
(DOH), subject to compliance with uniform PEME standards and protocols to be jointly
prescribed by the DOH and the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW).

      No employer, principal, or manning agency shall require or compel an OFW to
undergo PEME exclusively in a designated or preferred clinic as a condition for
deployment.

       To ensure quality, consistency, and medical integrity, the DOH, in coordination
with the DMW and other concerned agencies, shall:

   a) Prescribe standardized PEME examination protocols, diagnostic requirements,
      and reporting formats applicable to all DOH-accredited PEME clinics;
   b) Establish a system of periodic audit, monitoring, and compliance review of
      PEME clinics to ensure adherence to prescribed standards;
   c) Maintain and publish an updated list of accredited and compliant PEME clinics,
      including those subject to suspension or revocation of accreditation for
      violations.

        An employer, principal, or manning agency may raise a written objection to a
PEME result only on the basis of non-compliance with prescribed standards or
protocols, and not on the mere choice of clinic. In such cases, the OFW shall be
entitled to a confirmatory examination in another DOH-accredited clinic.

       Nothing in this section shall be construed to diminish the State’s duty to protect
the health, safety, and welfare of OFWs, nor to allow discriminatory, arbitrary, or
coercive medical practices.

     SEC. 6. Cost           of    Pre-Employment        Medical     Examination      and
Reimbursement. –

   (a) The cost of PEME shall be borne by the employer or principal.
   (b) In cases where the OFW advances payment for PEME, full reimbursement shall
       be made upon issuance of a Fit-to-Work Certificate, not later than the first salary
       payment or deployment date, whichever comes first.
   (c) No additional, repeat or confirmatory medical examination shall be charged to
       the OFW without clear medical justification.

       SEC. 7. Regulation of Medical and Psychological Examinations. –

   (a) All medical and psychological examinations shall comply with updated,
       evidence-based, and internationally accepted medical standards.
   (b) Psychological assessments shall be conducted only by licensed professionals
       in accordance with Republic Act No. 10029 or the Philippine Psychology Act
       and regulated by the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) through the
       Board of Psychology.
   (c) Outdated, unvalidated, or discriminatory testing methods are strictly prohibited.




                                                                                        3
       SEC. 8. Protection of Medical Records and Data Privacy. –

   (a) PEME results and medical records shall be the property of the OFW.
   (b) Disclosure of medical information to employers, agencies, or third parties shall
       require the written and informed consent of the OFW.
   (c) All handling of medical data shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173 or the
       Data Privacy Act of 2012.

       SEC. 9. Accountability of Pre-Employment Medical Examination Clinics. –
Medical facilities conducting PEME may be held jointly and severally liable for
repatriation costs, damages, or losses if a medical condition that should have been
reasonably detected during PEME leads to premature termination of employment
abroad.

       SEC. 10. Oversight and Inter-Agency Coordination. – The Department of
Health (DOH), Department of Migrant Workers (DMW), and other concerned agencies
shall establish a coordinated oversight mechanism to ensure compliance with this Act
and to investigate complaints related to PEME practices.

       SEC. 11. Prohibited Acts. –

       The following acts are hereby prohibited:

       (a) Forcing OFWs to use exclusive or designated PEME clinics;
       (b) Imposing excessive, repetitive, or unnecessary medical tests;
       (c) Withholding or falsifying PEME results;
       (d) Unauthorized disclosure of medical information;
       (e) Any act that circumvents the rights guaranteed under this Act.

       SEC. 12. Penalties. – Any person, agency, or medical facility found violating
this Act shall be subject to administrative, civil, and criminal liabilities, including fines,
suspension or revocation of accreditation, and other penalties as may be determined
by law.

       SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, DMW, and the PRC, in coordination with other
concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary implementing rules and
regulations of this Act.

      SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any section or provision of this Act is held
unconstitutional or invalid, the other sections or provisions hereof shall not be affected
and thereby remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, and rules
and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
