           STRENGTHENING ANTI-LITTERING MEASURES, PROVIDING
            CORRECTIVE WORK ORDERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The problem is highlighted each time floodwaters subside, or after typhoons.
Tons of garbage wash up on the shores around Manila Bay, and litter Roxas Boulevard
and the surrounding roads. Similar scenes are seen across Metro Manila's flood-prone
areas.

       Not just plastic products, but all sorts of materials discarded indiscriminately
accumulate and clog drainage systems, polluting waterways: tin cans, glass
containers, paper, styrofoam and wood products, and discarded appliances. Drains
are clogged even by the cigarette butts dropped anywhere, and the candy wrappers
and used tissue paper tossed out the windows of motor vehicles. 1 National policy
already prohibits littering, and even the act of "causing or permitting the same,"2 Yet
enforcement remains weak where offenders are hard to identify or apprehend.

        This bill strengthens deterrence through a clear, graduated regime: swift
administrative citation for minor violations, and court prosecution for aggravated
cases. It squarely addresses two recurring enforcement gaps: Elevated littering, where
waste can be traced to a unit but the thrower often evades accountability; and vehicle-
related littering, where the act occurs in transit and identification is difficult. These
special rules are crafted to be evidence-based, rebuttable, and consistent with due
process.

      The measure also emphasizes correction rather than just punishment. For
aggravated cases, it authorizes Corrective Work Orders, sanitation-related
community service under court supervision, so offenders repay the community
through visible, restorative work. This is consistent with the policy of community

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Anti-Littering and
Corrective Work Order Act of 2026."

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect public
health and the environment by preventing littering and improper disposal of waste;
strengthening deterrence through proportionate and graduated penalties; and
promoting discipline and civic responsibility through corrective work orders and
community-based sanitation programs.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

       (a) Littering refers to the act of throwing, dropping, scattering, dumping,
placing, leaving, or depositing any waste matter in any public place or common area,
or causing or permitting the same, except when placed inside an appropriate waste
receptacle or in accordance with authorized waste collection procedures.

       (b) Waste matter refers to any discarded material, whether solid or semi-solid,
regardless of size, weight, or biodegradability, that is capable of being left or deposited
in places covered by this Act.

      (c) Public places include, but are not limited to, roads, sidewalks, parks, plazas,
waterways, canals, esteros, beaches, terminals, public markets, government premises,
and other places open to or commonly used by the public.

      (d) Common area includes hallways, stairways, corridors, lobbies, elevators,
rooftops, parking areas, and similar shared spaces of condominiums, apartments,
dormitories, commercial buildings, and other multi-unit premises.


                                           Page 3 of 8
       (e) Elevated littering refers to littering committed by throwing, dropping, or
allowing waste to fall from any building, structure, or elevated platform (including
windows, balconies, roofs, corridors, and similar openings), whether intentional,
reckless, or negligent, causing waste to land in any public place, common area, or
another person's premises.

      (f) Corrective Work Order (CWO) refers to a court-imposed order requiring an
offender to perform sanitation-related community service, including litter-picking,
sweeping, canal cleaning, or similar work, for a specified number of hours, under
supervision.

      (g) Vehicle-related littering refers to the throwing, dropping, or allowing the
discharge of any waste matter from a moving or stationary vehicle.

       SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. — The following acts are prohibited:

       (a) Littering in any public place or common area;

       (b) Improper receptacle disposal, including placing waste on top of, beside,
outside, or wedged into railings/ledges near a receptacle, or in any manner that
results in or is likely to result in scattering, overflow, falling, or exposure of waste,
when a waste receptacle is available and usable;

      (c) Vehicle-related littering, whether from a private or public vehicle, stationary
or moving;

       (d) Elevated littering, as defined in this Act;

       (e) Knowingly causing or permitting littering within premises open to the
public by an owner, operator, manager, or person in charge, when such person has
the authority and reasonable opportunity to prevent or stop the prohibited act.

      SEC. 5. Prohibited Waste. — For avoidance of doubt, "waste matter" and "litter"
under this Act include, but are not limited to, the following:

      (a) Cigarette-related waste: cigarette butts, cigarette ashes, empty cigarette
packs/packaging, discarded matches, discarded lighters;

      (b) Small litter: tissue paper, napkins, receipts, candy/snack wrappers, plastic
packaging, paper scraps;

      (c) Food and beverage waste: leftover food, fruit peels, chewing gum, cups,
straws, lids, food containers, styrofoam boxes;

      (d) Containers and packaging: plastic bottles, aluminum cans, glass bottles,
take-away containers, bubble wraps, plastic bags;


                                         Page 4 of 8
      (e) Electronic waste (e-waste): computers, mobile phones, televisions, and
household appliances.

       (f) Household and personal waste: masks, wet wipes, cotton buds, sanitary
waste, tissues used for cleaning;

       (g) Construction or bulky waste: wood pieces, bricks, tiles, metal scraps,
furniture parts.

      Construction or bulky waste may be treated as an aggravated circumstance
under Section 9 of this Act when it poses heightened danger, obstruction, or cleanup
cost.

       SEC. 6. No "Harmless,” "Small," or "Biodegradable" Exception. —

      (a) Size is irrelevant. — No item shall be considered exempt from this Act by
reason alone of its small size.

       (b) Biodegradability is not a defense. — No item shall be considered exempt
from this Act by reason alone that it is biodegradable, including leaves, fruit peels, or
similar matter, when deposited or left in a manner prohibited by this Act.

       SEC. 7. Administrative Citation for Minor Littering.

       (a) Administrative enforcement. — For minor littering and improper receptacle
disposal, an authorized enforcement officer may issue an administrative citation ticket
requiring payment of a fine or appearance to contest the citation.

      (b) Authorized enforcement officers. — The following may be designated as
enforcement officers for purposes of this Act:

       (i)     local environmental and sanitation officers;
       (ii)    barangay tanods or barangay public safety officers, if duly deputized by
               the LGU through ordinance; and
       (iii)   other officers as may be authorized by law or ordinance, subject to
               training requirements prescribed in the implementing rules.

       (c) Administrative fine amounts. — Unless a higher penalty is warranted under
Section 8:

       (1)     First offense: Two thousand pesos (₱2,000.00);
       (2)     Second offense: Four thousand pesos (₱4,000.00);
       (3)     Third offense: Ten thousand pesos (₱10,000.00).




                                         Page 5 of 8
       (d) Contents of citation ticket. — The citation ticket shall state the act
complained of, the date, time, and place; the identity of the cited person if available;
the fine; the period to pay; and the procedure to contest.

       (e) Period to pay, contest, or hearing. — The cited person shall have not less
than seven (7) days from receipt to pay or to contest. If contested, the LGU shall
conduct a summary administrative hearing, with notice and an opportunity to be
heard, and shall render a written resolution.

       (f) Referral upon non-payment; enforcement. — If the cited person

       (i)     fails to pay within the prescribed period without contest, or
       (ii)    loses the contest and fails to pay within the period stated in the
               resolution, the LGU may enforce collection through lawful means
               and/or file the appropriate complaint in accordance with applicable
               laws and rules.

SEC. 8. Aggravated Cases; Criminal Prosecution; Corrective Work Order. —

       (a) Aggravated cases. — The following acts shall constitute aggravated
violations and shall be prosecuted as criminal offenses under this Act, without
prejudice to liability under other applicable laws:

       (i)     Elevated littering;
       (ii)    Vehicle-related littering that endangers persons or causes an accident;
       (iii)   Disposal of construction, demolition, or bulky waste in public places or
               other areas not designated for such waste;
       (iv)    Repeated commission of any violation under this Act after three (3) prior
               final administrative dispositions against the same offender; or
       (v)     Littering that results in substantial obstruction of drainage, waterways,
               sidewalks, or public access.

       (b) Institution of criminal action; proper court. — Criminal actions for
aggravated violations under this Act shall be instituted and tried in the proper court
with jurisdiction, in accordance with the Rules of Court and applicable special rules.

       (c) Court penalties. — Upon conviction, the court may impose a fine of up to
Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) and/or such other penalties as may be allowed
under applicable law. The court may likewise order the offender to perform corrective
work or such other community-based measures as may be authorized under this Act
and the implementing rules.

       (c) Corrective Work Order (CWO). — In addition to fines and other penalties,
the court may order the offender to render sanitation-related community service as a
Corrective Work Order (CWO), under such terms as the court shall determine, taking



                                         Page 6 of 8
into account the gravity of the offense and the circumstances of the case. The court
shall issue a written order specifying:

       (i)    the number of hours to be worked, which shall be not less than three (3)
              hours and not more than twelve (12) hours, and
       (ii)   the period within which to complete the service. The CWO shall be
              under the supervision of a probation officer, in coordination with the
              LGU or a designated implementing unit where the service will be
              performed.

       (d) Failure to comply with CWO. — If the offender unjustifiably fails to comply
with the terms of the CWO, the court may, after notice and hearing, revoke the CWO
and order the service of the corresponding penalty in accordance with law, and/or
impose such other sanctions as may be proper under the Rules of Court.

      SEC. 10. Elevated Littering: Duty of Unit Owner/Occupier; Administrative
Presumption. —

       (a) Duty to prevent. — The unit owner, lessee, or lawful occupant who has
actual possession or control of a unit shall exercise reasonable diligence to prevent
elevated littering originating from the unit, including by adopting reasonable
preventive measures appropriate to the unit's use and configuration, as may be further
specified in the implementing rules.

       (b) Rebuttable administrative presumption. — For purposes of issuance and
enforcement of an administrative citation under this Act, where waste is shown by
substantial evidence to have originated from a particular unit, there shall be a
rebuttable presumption that the lawful occupant is responsible, unless the occupant
shows that reasonable preventive measures were in place and that another identifiable
person committed the act without the occupant's participation, consent, or negligence.

      (c) Administrative nature; without prejudice. — Liability under this section is
administrative in character and is without prejudice to liability under other laws
where the act constitutes a distinct offense.

      SEC. 11. Vehicle-Related Littering: Liability of Actual Offender; Limited
Responsibility of Driver in Control. —

       (a) Primary administrative liability of the actual offender. — The person who
           actually commits vehicle-related littering, whether the driver or any
           passenger, shall be primarily liable for the administrative violation and
           penalties under this Act.

      (b) Administrative liability of the driver in control for the passenger's acts. —
The driver or person in actual control of the vehicle at the time of the act shall likewise
be administratively liable only when the driver

       (i)     committed the act;
       (ii)    knowingly authorized, tolerated, or directed the littering; or
       (iii)   having actual knowledge of the littering and a reasonable opportunity
               and ability to prevent or stop it, negligently failed to do so.

        (c) Without prejudice clause. — The administrative liability created under this
section is without prejudice to any civil or criminal liability arising from the same act,
if applicable.

       SEC. 12. Implementing Roles of LGUs and DENR. — Local government units
(LGUs) shall implement this Act consistent with segregation-at-source requirements,
and may enact ordinances to strengthen local enforcement consistent with national
law. The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) shall provide
technical assistance and issue implementing standards/guidelines within its
mandate, and shall coordinate with LGUs to ensure harmonized implementation.

       SEC. 13. Citizen Participation and Reporting. — LGUs may establish citizen
reporting and participation mechanisms, including hotlines and digital reporting
portals, subject to data privacy and due process requirements. LGUs may share
verified reports and enforcement statistics with DENR for monitoring, technical
support, and program evaluation, consistent with applicable laws and issuances.

       SEC. 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid, the
other provisions not affected shall remain in force.

      SEC. 15. Repealing — All laws, decrees, rules, regulations, and parts thereof
inconsistent with this Act are hereby amended or modified accordingly.

     SEC. 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
