                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 15 of Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that
the State shall protect and promote the right to health of the people, while Section
11 of Article XIII directs the State to adopt an integrated and comprehensive
approach to health development and make essential health services available to
all. Section 10 of Article XIV likewise recognizes the importance of science and
technology to national development and directs the State to give priority to
research and development, innovation, and their utilization. These mandates
support the development of a coordinated national policy for the responsible use
of advanced genomic technologies in health care, public health, and research.

       Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) is increasingly used in clinical and
public health applications, but research shows that its reliability depends on
standardized laboratory procedures, analytical validation, bioinformatics, quality
control, interpretation, and reporting. International studies have emphasized the
continuing need for technical standards and systematic quality monitoring in
clinical NGS laboratories.1 Proficiency-testing research likewise demonstrates

        Philippine studies demonstrate the growing application of NGS in national
 health programs, including its integration into the Philippine National
 Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance Program to identify antimicrobial-
 resistance mechanisms and transmission patterns, its use in detecting and
 sequencing SARS-CoV-2 variants, and its application in analyzing the genomic
 evolution and distribution of SARS-CoV-2 in the country. At the same time,
 research on Philippine respiratory-virus surveillance has identified challenges
 involving laboratory capacity, information management, and system integration,
 highlighting the need for stronger coordination and standardized genomic
 surveillance systems.3

        Recognizing these research findings and the existing institutional
 landscape, this measure seeks to establish a National Next-Generation
 Sequencing (NGS) Laboratory Reference Framework that sets common national
 standards, provides criteria for laboratory qualification and designation,
 establishes a nationwide referral network, strengthens quality assurance and
 proficiency testing, and promotes secure technical interoperability. The measure
 does not create another physical sequencing center or replace existing clinical-
 laboratory licensing, specialized referral systems, research institutions, or data-
 protection requirements. Instead, it provides a coordinated framework for
 maximizing existing NGS capabilities and addressing the identified gap in
 national standards, referral, quality assurance, and interoperability.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
 sought.

                                    ARTICLE I
                           GENERAL PROVISIONS


      SECTION 1. Short Title.           This Act shall be known as the                  N
Genomic Sequencing Laboratory Reference Act.


      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health of the people and to advance the responsible
application of science and technology in health care, public health, and research.
Toward this end, the State shall establish a national reference policy for Next-
Generation Sequencing (NGS) laboratories that provides uniform technical and
quality standards, facilitates interoperability, and establishes coordinated referral
mechanisms among qualified NGS laboratories to promote accurate, reliable,
timely, and consistent NGS laboratory services.


      SEC. 3. Objectives.      This Act shall pursue the following objectives:
      (a) Establish a decentralized national reference framework that links
      qualified NGS laboratories and optimizes available sequencing capacity;
      (b) Prescribe baseline technical, operational, and quality standards for NGS
      laboratory activities;
      (c) Establish a nationwide, disease-agnostic referral network for NGS
      laboratory services;
      (d) Establish common technical standards for the secure exchange of NGS
      laboratory and genomic data consistent with applicable laws on privacy
      and information security; and
      (e) Establish a national quality assurance and proficiency testing
      mechanism for qualified NGS laboratories.


      SEC. 4. Definition of Terms.      As used in this Act:
      (a) Bioinformatics refers to the application of computational methods and
      tools to the processing, analysis, interpretation, and management of
      biological and genomic data;
      (b) Interoperability refers to the technical capability of NGS laboratories,
      health institutions, and authorized research entities to securely exchange
      and process standardized NGS laboratory and genomic data;
      (c) Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) refers to high-throughput DNA and
      RNA sequencing technologies that permit the parallel sequencing of
      biological samples;
      (d) NGS Laboratory refers to a public or private clinical or research
      laboratory that performs NGS-related activities, including sample

                                                                                 2
      preparation, sequencing, bioinformatics, data analysis, or genomic
      reporting;
      (e) Qualified NGS Laboratory refers to an NGS laboratory that complies
      with the applicable national NGS standards prescribed under this Act and
      is designated to participate in the National NGS Laboratory Reference
      Framework;
      (f) Quality Assurance refers to systematic activities undertaken to ensure
      the accuracy, reliability, consistency, and validity of NGS laboratory
      processes and results; and
      (g) National NGS Laboratory Reference Framework refers to the
      coordinated network of qualified NGS laboratories, national NGS
      standards, referral mechanisms, quality assurance arrangements, and
      interoperability standards established under this Act.


                                   ARTICLE II
             NATIONAL NGS LABORATORY REFERENCE
                    FRAMEWORK AND STANDARDS


      SEC. 5. National NGS Laboratory Reference Framework.                  The
Department of Health (DOH) shall establish and administer the National NGS
Laboratory Reference Framework as a coordinated and decentralized network of
qualified NGS laboratories.
      The Framework shall:
      (a) Establish a coordinated system for NGS laboratory referral and service
      routing;
      (b) Facilitate the complementary use of available NGS capacity among
      qualified laboratories;
      (c) Promote uniform NGS laboratory practices and quality requirements;

                                                                               3
       (d) Facilitate access to qualified NGS laboratory services; and
       (e) Promote coordination among government agencies, academic
       institutions, research institutions, health institutions, and qualified public
       and private NGS laboratories.
       The Framework shall operate as a coordinated national network and policy
mechanism and shall not require the creation of a single physical NGS reference
facility.


       SEC. 6. National NGS Laboratory Standards. The DOH, in consultation
with the Philippine Genome Center (PGC), the Department of Science and
Technology (DOST), the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT), relevant government agencies, scientific institutions,
academic institutions, and professional organizations, shall formulate, issue, and
periodically update national standards for NGS laboratories.
       The standards shall cover, as applicable:
       (a) Physical facilities, biosafety controls, environmental conditions, and
       NGS instrumentation;
       (b) Specimen collection, identification, handling, storage, transport, and
       acceptance or rejection;
       (c) Sample preparation, sequencing workflows, quality control, validation,
       calibration, and equipment maintenance;
       (d) Bioinformatics pipeline validation, data analysis, variant detection, and
       interpretation;
       (e) Post-analytical processes, including verification and reporting of NGS
       results;
       (f) Personnel qualifications, competency, training, and continuing
       professional development; and

          ensure the accuracy, reliability, reproducibility, and integrity of NGS
          laboratory services.

          SEC. 7. Qualification and Designation of NGS Laboratories. The DOH
shall establish criteria and procedures for the qualification and designation of an
NGS laboratory for participation in the National NGS Laboratory Reference
Framework.
          The DOH shall designate an NGS laboratory upon determination that the
laboratory complies with the applicable national NGS standards and other
requirements prescribed under this Act and its implementing rules and
regulations.
          Designation under this Act shall be limited to participation in the National
NGS Laboratory Reference Framework and shall not constitute a substitute for
any license, registration, accreditation, permit, or institutional authority required
under existing laws.
          The DOH shall periodically assess the compliance of a designated NGS
laboratory with the applicable standards and may suspend or revoke the
designation pursuant to procedures prescribed in the implementing rules and
regulations.


          SEC. 8. Nationwide NGS Referral Network.          The DOH shall establish
and maintain a nationwide NGS referral network to facilitate the appropriate and
timely routing of specimens, requests, sequencing services, and laboratory results

                                                                                    5
among health institutions, public health institutions, research institutions, and
qualified NGS laboratories.
      The referral network shall:
      (a) Establish standardized referral procedures and documentation;
      (b) Identify qualified NGS laboratories according to technical capability
      and the NGS service required;
      (c) Establish procedures for specimen handling, transport, tracking, receipt,
      processing, and reporting;
      (d) Facilitate referrals across clinical specialties and public health
      applications without limitation to a specific disease category; and
      (e) Establish coordination procedures when the required NGS capability is
      unavailable in the referring institution.
      Specialized referral systems established under existing laws shall continue
to operate according to their respective mandates.


                                   ARTICLE III
          QUALITY ASSURANCE AND INTEROPERABILITY


      SEC. 9. National Quality Assurance and Proficiency Testing. The DOH
shall establish and implement a national quality assurance program for qualified
NGS laboratories participating in the National NGS Laboratory Reference
Framework.
      The program shall include:
      (a) External quality assessment;
      (b) Regular proficiency testing;
      (c) Evaluation of sequencing fidelity and analytical performance;
      (d) Assessment of bioinformatics accuracy and data analysis;
      (e) Assessment of the accuracy and reliability of NGS laboratory reporting;

                                                                                 6
      (f) Identification and correction of laboratory deficiencies; and
      (g) Technical assistance and capacity-building for qualified NGS
      laboratories.
      Participation in the quality assurance and proficiency testing program shall
be a condition for continued designation under the National NGS Laboratory
Reference Framework.


      SEC. 10. Data Interoperability and Protection.               The DOH, in
coordination with the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT), the National Privacy Commission (NPC), the Philippine
Genome Center (PGC), the Department of Science and Technology (DOST), and
other relevant institutions, shall prescribe common technical standards for the
secure exchange, transmission, storage, and processing of NGS laboratory and
genomic data.
      The standards may include:
      (a) Common data formats and data fields;
      (b) Data exchange and communication protocols;
      (c) Minimum metadata requirements;
      (d) Data integrity and authentication requirements;
      (e) Access-control and authorization mechanisms; and
      (f) Information security and cybersecurity requirements.
      The processing of genetic information, health information, and other
sensitive personal information under this Act shall comply with Republic Act No.

                                  ARTICLE IV
   ROLE DELINEATION AND INTER-AGENCY COORDINATION


      SEC. 11. Lead Agency.         The DOH shall be the lead agency in the
implementation of this Act and shall:
      (a) Formulate and periodically update the national NGS laboratory
      standards;
      (b) Establish and administer the National NGS Laboratory Reference
      Framework;
      (c) Establish criteria and procedures for the qualification and designation
      of NGS laboratories;
      (d) Establish and supervise the nationwide NGS referral network;
      (e) Establish and implement the national quality assurance and proficiency
      testing program;
      (f) Prescribe, in coordination with the DICT, NPC, and relevant
      institutions, technical interoperability standards;
      (g) Monitor compliance with national NGS standards;
      (h) Coordinate technical assistance, training, and capacity-building
      activities;
      (i) Coordinate with relevant government agencies, academic institutions,
      research institutions, scientific organizations, health institutions, and
      professional organizations; and
      (j) Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out the
      objectives of this Act.


      SEC. 12. Responsibilities of the Philippine Genome Center and the
Department of Science and Technology. The PGC and the DOST shall provide

consistent with their respective mandates.

       The DOST shall facilitate coordination with relevant scientific and
research institutions on matters involving NGS technology, research, and
scientific capacity-building.


       SEC. 13. Coordination With Other Agencies and Institutions.              The
DOH shall coordinate, as necessary, with the DICT, NPC, the Virology and
Vaccine Institute of the Philippines (VIP), the Philippine Cancer Center, the
National Institutes of Health, universities, research institutions, professional
organizations, health institutions, and other relevant entities in implementing this
Act.
       Such coordination shall promote the complementary use of existing NGS
capabilities, facilitate technical cooperation, and minimize duplication of
functions and resources.

                                         ARTICLE V
                                 FINAL PROVISIONS


       SEC. 15. Appropriations.             The initial amount necessary for the
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the DOH, subject to existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and
regulations.
       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.

      SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations.               Within one hundred
twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Health, in
consultation with the Executive Director of the PGC, the Secretary of Science
and Technology, the Secretary of Information and Communications Technology,
the National Privacy Commission, and representatives of other relevant agencies
and institutions, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations
necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.
      The implementing rules and regulations shall include the national NGS
laboratory standards, criteria and procedures for laboratory designation, referral
procedures,    quality   assurance    and     proficiency   testing   requirements,
interoperability standards, and monitoring mechanisms.


      SEC. 17. Periodic Review of Standards.           The DOH shall periodically
review and update the national NGS laboratory standards and the National NGS
Laboratory Reference Framework in consultation with relevant government,
scientific, academic, professional, and research institutions.
      The review shall consider developments in NGS technologies,
bioinformatics, laboratory quality, interoperability, privacy and information
security, and applicable laws and regulations.


      SEC. 18. Separability Clause.         If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


      SEC. 19. Repealing Clause.       All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
