                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE


According to a study by Roxas et.al., “Transportation noise derived from roadside traffic continues
to be a concern in highly urbanized areas such as Metro Manila, where frequent and excessive
exposure to noise may negatively impact human behavior and performance.”

From residential neighborhoods disturbed late at night, to students unable to concentrate in
schools, to patients recovering in hospitals, the impact of unnecessary engine noise due to loud
modified mufflers is deeply felt. While motor vehicles are essential to mobility and economic
activity, they must not operate at the expense of public peace and health. Excessively loud exhaust
systems, often modified to produce “open pipe” or racing-style sounds, contribute to noise
pollution that can cause stress, sleep disturbance, and possible ear damage.

The LTO currently sets the national standard for exhaust noise at a maximum of 99 decibels (dB),
which is considered “incredibly loud.” It’s similar to hearing the roaring engine of a nearby
chainsaw, which produces roughly 90 to 110 dB. According to Yale Environmental Health and
Safety, prolonged exposure to noise at 90 dB can result in hearing loss.

It must be emphasized that Section 34 of Republic Act No. 4136, otherwise known as the Land
Transportation and Traffic Code, already requires every motor vehicle to be equipped with a
muffler to prevent excessive or unusual noise. However, while this provision exists, enforcement
challenges, evolving vehicle modifications, and the proliferation of aftermarket exhaust systems
have rendered it ineffective. The current penalties and regulatory mechanisms have proven
insufficient if not ineffective to address this widespread and recurring issue. There are various City
Ordinances like in Navotas, Manila and in Quezon City prohibiting the use of noisy mufflers.

This proposed measure does not seek to duplicate existing law and ordinances but to strengthen,
modernize, and make it more uniform. The bill establishes clear and measurable national decibel
standards, defines prohibited modifications such as open-pipe and straight exhaust systems, and
introduces graduated penalties to discourage repeat violations. It also holds sellers, distributors,
and installers of illegal mufflers accountable, filling in gaps not fully addressed under existing
provisions.
Current regulations exist but enforcement remains inconsistent, and penalties are often insufficient
to deter repeat violations. Moreover, the continued sale and installation of illegal mufflers
undermine efforts to maintain order on our roads.

This proposed measure seeks to establish clear national standards for permissible exhaust noise
levels, prohibit the installation and use of modified or open-pipe mufflers that exceed those limits,
and impose graduated penalties for violators. It also holds sellers and installers accountable,
ensuring that regulation addresses both supply and use.

The bill empowers the Land Transportation Office (LTO), in coordination with the Philippine
National Police (PNP) and local government units, to enforce uniform noise-testing procedures
and conduct inspections consistent with due process.

This measure is not intended to target responsible motorists or motorcycle enthusiasts who comply
with the law. Rather, it aims to strike a fair balance between individual freedom and the
community’s right to peace and quiet.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
 PROHIBITING EXCESSIVE MOTOR VEHICLE MUFFLER AND EXHAUST NOISE,
                AND PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR


      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title.- This Act shall be known as the “Anti-Excessive Muffler Noise Act of
2026.”

SEC 2. Declaration of State Policy—It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect public
health, safety, and general welfare from harmful noise pollution.


SEC 3. Coverage—This Act shall apply to all motor vehicles operating/using on national, city,
municipal and barangay roads nationwide, including motorcycles, tricycles, passenger vehicles,
and similar transport equipment.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms—For purposes of this Act:

       a. Muffler refers to device designed to reduce engine exhaust noise.

       b. Modified Muffler is any alteration of the factory-installed exhaust system that increases
noise output beyond legal limits.

       c. Open Pipe/Straight Pipe is an exhaust system without a functional muffler or noise-
reduction device.
        d. Excessive Noise refers to exhaust sound exceeding 85 decibels (dBA) measured at a
distance of 50 centimeters from the exhaust outlet at 2,000–2,500 RPM for motorcycles, or as may
be set by implementing regulations.

SEC. 5. Prohibited Acts—It shall be unlawful to:

   1. Operate a motor vehicle without a properly functioning muffler;
   2. Install, sell, distribute, or use modified or open-pipe exhaust systems that amplify noise
      beyond legal limits;
   3. Engage in unnecessary engine revving or backfire modifications causing public
      disturbance; and
   4. Tamper with factory-installed noise-reduction systems.

SEC. 5. Penalties

a. For the first offense, a fine amounting to five thousand pesos (Php 5,000) shall be imposed.

b. For the second offense, a fine amounting to ten thousand pesos (Php 10,000) shall be imposed
together with the suspension of the offender’s driver’s license for a period to be determined by the
appropriate authority.


c. For the Third and Subsequent Offenses, the vehicle involved shall be impounded, and the
offender’s driver’s license shall be revoked. The vehicle shall only be released upon mandatory
restoration to its factory specifications and full compliance with the provisions of this Act.

Manufacturers, retailers or installers found selling or installing illegal mufflers shall be fined
amounting to fifty thousand pesos (Php50,000) for the first offense, and shall be fined amounting
to seventy thousand pesos (Php 70,000) for the second offense. For the third and subsequent
repeated offenses, the establishment may face suspension of its business permit and possible
revocation of its license to operate, subject to due process and in accordance with applicable laws
and regulations.

SEC. 7. Enforcement

   1. The Land Transportation Office (LTO), in coordination with the Philippine National Police
      (PNP) and local government units (LGUs), shall enforce this Act.
   2. The LTO shall establish uniform testing procedures and decibel limits consistent with
      international safety standards.
   3. Random roadside inspections and noise testing operations may be conducted.

SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR)—Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the LTO shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
implementation.

SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid,
the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and regulations or parts
thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
