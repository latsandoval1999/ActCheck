                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

Electricity is an essential daily need for every Filipino family and a key driver of our local economy.
However, for years, consumers have been forced to shoulder high electricity bills—partly because
of "system loss charges." Electric companies are allowed to pass the cost of lost electricity straight
to consumers, whether the loss is from aging power lines, poor equipment, administrative errors,
or power theft.

Given that the distribution utilities can easily pass these costs to the public, these companies have
little reason to modernize their equipment, fix operational issues, or stop power theft. The burden
falls entirely on the paying consumer.

This bill, the "System Loss Charge Abolition Act" amends Republic Act No. 9136 (EPIRA) to
fix this unfair arrangement. Under this measure the power companies can no longer list system
loss as a separate pass-through fee on monthly bills, shall strictly direct the Energy Regulatory
Commission (ERC) to strictly ban utilities from recovering costs caused by power theft, poor
maintenance, inefficiency, or administrative mistakes and provides a transition period up to three
years (3) years to give utilities time to fix their systems without disrupting power service.

By forcing distribution utilities to take full responsibility for their own technical and operational
losses, this bill will make power rates fair, hold power companies accountable, and shall give relief
to everyday electricity consumers.

             For these reasons, the immediate passage of this bill is urgently sought.

          SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “System-Loss Charge Abolition
Act”


          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy.- It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect electricity consumers from charges arising from theft, inefficiency, negligence and other
causes within the control of distribution utilities, while allowing the recovery of only reasonable
costs incurred in the efficient delivery of electricity.

      SECTION. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the ERC, in consultation with the Department of Energy, distribution
utilities, consumer groups, and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations
necessary for its implementation.


      The rules shall provide for the determination of prudent and reasonable costs necessarily
incurred in the efficient operation of distribution systems, the disallowance of costs attributable
to causes within the control of distribution utilities, and a phased transition not exceeding three
(3) years from the effectivity of the rules.


      SECTION. 6.         Separability clause.      – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.
