                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The emergence of digital labor platforms has created new opportunities for
Filipinos to earn a living through delivery services, transportation, online freelancing,
virtual assistance, and other forms of platform-based work. While these arrangements
provide flexibility and additional sources of income, they also expose workers to
challenges concerning fair compensation, job security, social protection, and access
to effective grievance mechanisms.

       Digital platform workers often have limited control over platform policies and
automated systems that determine their compensation, work assignments,
performance ratings, and continued access to employment opportunities. Account
suspension, unexplained deductions, and changes in compensation may adversely
affect their livelihood without adequate notice or opportunity to contest such
decisions.

        This measure seeks to establish minimum standards for the protection of digital
platform and gig workers by requiring transparency in compensation and platform
policies, providing safeguards against arbitrary account deactivation, promoting
access to social protection benefits, and ensuring fair treatment in platform-based
work arrangements.

      It likewise recognizes the right of workers to organize and seek appropriate
remedies while maintaining the distinction between employees and independent
contractors under existing labor laws.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                                AN ACT
     PROVIDING FOR THE PROTECTION OF THE RIGHTS AND WELFARE OF
     DIGITAL PLATFORM AND GIG WORKERS, PROMOTING FAIR WORKING
     CONDITIONS,   ENSURING   TRANSPARENCY   IN  DIGITAL  LABOR
     ARRANGEMENTS,    AND   PROVIDING   MECHANISMS  FOR  SOCIAL
     PROTECTION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1        SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Digital Platform
 2   and Gig Worker Protection Act."
 3
 4          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
 5   State to promote the rights, welfare, and economic security of digital platform and gig
 6   workers. The State shall ensure fair working conditions, transparent compensation
 7   arrangements, access to social protection, and protection against abusive and
 8   discriminatory platform practices, while recognizing the evolving nature of digital
 9   employment and independent work.
10
11          SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:
12          (a) Digital Platform refers to an online application, website, or digital system
13   that facilitates, organizes, or allocates work or services between workers and
14   customers or clients.
15          (b) Digital Platform Provider refers to any person or entity that owns,
16   operates, or manages a digital platform.
17          (c) Digital Platform Worker refers to any individual performing work or
18   providing services through a digital platform, regardless of employment classification.
19          (d) Gig Worker refers to an individual engaged in short-term, task-based,
20   project-based, or service-based work through a digital platform.
 1           (e) Automated Decision-Making System refers to any algorithm, artificial
 2   intelligence, or automated system used to determine or influence work assignments,
 3   compensation, performance evaluation, suspension, or deactivation of workers.
 4
 5          SECTION 4. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all digital platform providers
 6   operating in the Philippines, whether locally or foreign-based, and all workers
 7   performing services through such platforms.
 8
 9          It shall cover both:
10          (a) Location-based platforms, including transportation, delivery, and courier
11   services; and
12          (b) Web-based platforms, including freelancing, virtual assistance, content
13   creation, and other online services.
14
15          SECTION 5. Minimum Standards and Protection of Workers. - All digital
16   platform providers shall observe the following minimum standards:
17          (a) Written Agreement - Workers shall be provided with an accessible
18   agreement specifying the nature of work, compensation, deductions, applicable
19   platform policies, and conditions of engagement;
20          (b) Fair Compensation - Workers shall receive their agreed compensation,
21   with all commissions, service fees, deductions, and other charges clearly disclosed;
22          (c) Timely Payment - Workers shall receive payment for completed tasks or
23   services within the agreed payment period;
24          (d) Occupational Safety - Platform providers shall adopt reasonable
25   measures to promote worker safety and provide information on applicable safety
26   standards and available assistance;
27          (e) Non-Discrimination - Workers shall be protected against discrimination
28   and retaliation in the performance of their work; and
29          (f) Right to Organize - Workers shall have the right to form, join, or assist
30   workers' associations and organizations in accordance with existing laws.
31
32          SECTION 6. Transparency in Automated Decision-Making. - Digital
33   platform providers utilizing automated systems that materially affect workers'
34   compensation, work assignments, performance ratings, or account status shall
35   disclose the general criteria and processes used by such systems.
36
37            Workers shall have the right to request an explanation of decisions affecting
38   their livelihood and seek human review when such decisions result in material adverse
39   consequences.
40
41         SECTION 7. Protection Against Arbitrary Account Suspension and
42   Deactivation. - No digital platform provider shall arbitrarily suspend or deactivate a
43   worker's account without valid grounds.
44
45   The affected worker shall be provided with written or electronic notice stating the
46   reasons for the action and shall be given an opportunity to explain, contest, or appeal
47   the decision.
48
 1   In cases requiring immediate suspension due to security concerns, fraud, or threats
 2   to public safety, the provider shall furnish an explanation and an opportunity to appeal
 3   within a reasonable period.
 4
 5   Undisputed earnings for completed work shall not be withheld solely because of
 6   account suspension or deactivation.
 7
 8          SECTION 8. Access to Social Protection Benefits. - The Department of
 9   Labor and Employment (DOLE), in coordination with the Social Security System (SSS),
10   Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), and Home Development Mutual
11   Fund (Pag-IBIG Fund), shall establish mechanisms to facilitate the enrollment and
12   continued contributions of digital platform and gig workers to existing social protection
13   programs.
14
15          Digital platform providers shall facilitate access to information and voluntary
16   contribution arrangements, including mechanisms that accommodate workers earning
17   from multiple platforms.
18
19         Nothing in this Section shall diminish the mandatory obligations of employers
20   under existing laws.
21
22          SECTION 9. Dispute Resolution Mechanism. - Digital platform providers
23   shall establish accessible internal grievance mechanisms for complaints involving
24   compensation, deductions, account suspension, deactivation, and other violations of
25   workers' rights.
26
27         Unresolved disputes may be referred to the DOLE or other appropriate
28   government agencies for conciliation, mediation, or appropriate legal action.
29
30           The resolution of disputes under this Act shall not automatically classify every
31   digital platform worker as an employee. The existence of an employer-employee
32   relationship shall be determined in accordance with existing labor laws and
33   jurisprudence.
34
35         SECTION 10. Implementing Agency. - The DOLE shall be the primary
36   implementing agency of this Act and shall:
37         (a) Monitor compliance by digital platform providers;
38         (b) Receive and investigate complaints involving violations of this Act;
39         (c) Coordinate with concerned government agencies regarding social protection
40   and worker welfare; and
41         (d) Promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
42   implementation.
43
44          SECTION 11. Penalties. - Any digital platform provider found violating the
45   provisions of this Act shall be subject to an administrative fine of not less than Fifty
46   thousand pesos (₱50,000.00) but not more than Five hundred thousand pesos
47   (₱500,000.00), depending on the gravity and frequency of the violation.
 1            Repeated violations shall be subject to higher penalties in accordance with the
 2   implementing rules and regulations of this Act.
 3            The imposition of administrative penalties shall be without prejudice to other
 4   liabilities under existing laws.
 5
 6         SECTION 12. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
 7   implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
 8   DOLE. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual
 9   General Appropriations Act.
10
11         SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90)
12   days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOLE, in consultation with concerned
13   government agencies, digital platform providers, and workers' organizations, shall
14   promulgate the necessary rules and regulations.
15
16            SECTION 14. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
17   unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain
18   in full force and effect.
19
20          SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
21   and regulations, ordinances, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with
22   the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
23
24          SECTION 16. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
25   its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
