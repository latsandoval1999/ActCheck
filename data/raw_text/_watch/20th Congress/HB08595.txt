ESTABLISHING QUALITY AND SAFETY STANDARDS FOR MINERAL,

SOURCE PROTECTION, MONITORING, LABELING, RECALLS, AND ENFORCEMENT,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Access to safe and clean drinking water is essential to protecting public health. Today,
many Filipino families, schools, and workplaces rely on bottled water, such as mineral,
puri ied, sparkling, or distilled water, as a daily source of drinking water. Because
these products are widely consumed, it is important to ensure that all bottled drinking
water sold in the country is safe, free from harmful contaminants, and accurately
labeled so that consumers know exactly what they are buying.

The government already has laws that regulate food and health products and set
standards for drinking water safety. Agencies such as the Department of Health and
the Food and Drug Administration are responsible for monitoring product quality,
inspecting facilities, and enforcing health standards. However, despite these existing
powers, gaps remain in the bottled water sector. In some cases, there are unclear rules
on protecting water sources, inconsistent testing and monitoring, weak recall
procedures when contamination occurs, and labels that do not clearly explain the
water’s source or treatment.

This bill seeks to address these concerns by establishing the Safe Bottled Water Act.
The measure sets clear national standards to ensure that bottled drinking water is
safe, properly tested, and produced from protected sources. It also strengthens

clearer procedures for recalling products and informing the public if a health risk is

By strengthening safety standards and improving accountability in the bottled water
industry, this bill aims to protect public health and ensure that consumers can trust
the drinking water products available in the market. The measure promotes
transparency, safety, and con idence in bottled water for all Filipinos.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Safe Bottled Water Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote public health by ensuring that bottled drinking water sold or distributed in
the Philippines is safe, potable, accurately labeled, and produced under sanitary
conditions. The State shall adopt a comprehensive regulatory framework for bottled
drinking water that provides for science-based standards, source protection,
monitoring and inspections, timely recalls and public advisories, and effective
enforcement, through proper delegation and coordination of concerned agencies.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) “Bottled Drinking Water” refers to water intended for human consumption
       that is sealed in bottles or other containers and offered for sale or distribution,
       including but not limited to puri ied water, distilled water, mineral water,
       spring water, artesian water, well water, carbonated water, sparkling water,
       and vended or re illed water in sealed containers.

                                                                              3|Page
   (b) “FDA” refers to the Food and Drug Administration under the Department of
       Health (DOH), as strengthened under Republic Act No. 9711.

   (c) “DOH” refers to the Department of Health.

   (d) “Approved Source” refers to a water source that meets criteria established
       under this Act and the implementing rules and regulations (IRR), including
       sanitary protection and vulnerability controls, and is authorized for use in
       bottled drinking water manufacturing.

   (e) “Health-Based Standard” refers to a maximum contaminant level, guideline
       value, or other quality parameter based on public health protection, as set
       under applicable national drinking water standards and FDA/DOH
       regulations.

SECTION 4. Coverage; Harmonization With Existing Standards. — This Act shall
apply to all manufacturers, bottlers, processors, importers, distributors, wholesalers,
and sellers of Bottled Drinking Water.

The standards and requirements under this Act shall be implemented in a manner
consistent with: (i) the FDA’s regulatory mandate over health products and
establishments; and (ii) the DOH’s jurisdiction to prescribe drinking water standards
and accredit laboratories for water examinations.

SECTION 5. Quality Standards for Bottled Drinking Water. —

   (a) Baseline Applicability of National Drinking Water Standards. — The
       applicable national drinking water standards and health-based requirements
       prescribed by the DOH and enforced through FDA regulations shall apply to all
       Bottled Drinking Water.

   (b) Product-Speci ic Standards and De initions. — Within twelve (12) months
       from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, through the FDA and after due
       consultation with stakeholders, shall establish and publish speci ic standards
       and de initions for categories of Bottled Drinking Water, including mineral
       water and carbonated water. These shall include, at a minimum:

       (1) limits for total dissolved solids, sulfate, sodium, and disinfection by-
           products such as trihalomethanes, as applicable; and


                                                                           4|Page
      (2) other limits and parameters as determined by established health-based
          drinking water standards and risk assessment.

SECTION 6. Source Protection; Approved Sources. — Within twelve (12) months
from the effectivity of this Act, the FDA shall:

   (a) Establish criteria and procedures for identifying, approving, and periodically
       re-evaluating Approved Sources of Bottled Drinking Water; and

   (b) Prescribe minimum sanitary protection criteria for Approved Sources,
       including, as applicable:

      (1) minimum construction and integrity standards for water wells and intake
          structures;
      (2) minimum separation distances or protective buffers from potential
          sources of contamination, including upstream wastewater discharges,
          septic tanks, land ills, waste impoundments, and abandoned wells; and
      (3) vulnerability and contamination-risk assessment requirements.

In implementing this Section, the FDA shall coordinate with concerned LGUs and the
DENR, consistent with integrated water quality management policies.

SECTION 7. Licensing, Monitoring, Reporting, and Inspection. —

      (a) Facility Licensing and Compliance. — The FDA shall require manufacturers
          and bottlers of Bottled Drinking Water to secure and maintain appropriate
          FDA authorizations and certi icates of compliance, and to comply with
          sanitary and good manufacturing practice requirements, in accordance
          with law.

      (b) Monitoring Program. — Within twelve (12) months from the effectivity of
          this Act, the FDA shall establish a bottled drinking water monitoring
          program that, at a minimum:

          (1) requires periodic testing for regulated contaminants and, where
              necessary, monitoring for contaminants of emerging concern based on
              risk;
          (2) prescribes sampling frequency proportionate to production volume
              and risk pro ile; and


                                                                         5|Page
          (3) requires that analysis or testing be performed only by laboratories
              duly accredited by the DOH.

       (c) Reporting Program and Public Advisories. — The FDA shall establish a
           reporting program that shall:

          (1) set timelines and procedures for reporting test results, deviations, and
              incidents affecting safety;
          (2) provide for prompt public advisories and noti ications when a product
              is found non-compliant with health-based standards or otherwise
              presents a public health risk;
          (3) maintain a national registry of bottled drinking water facilities, their
              authorizations, and compliance status, subject to con identiality and
              lawful disclosure rules; and
          (4) require retention of sampling and analysis records for not less than two
              (2) years, which shall be available for of icial review upon lawful
              request.

       (d) Inspection Program. — The FDA shall establish a facility inspection
          program that includes, at a minimum, two (2) scheduled inspections per
          year and one (1) risk-based unannounced inspection per year, subject to
          adjustments based on compliance history and risk classi ication.

SECTION 8. Recall and Corrective Actions. — Within six (6) months from the
effectivity of this Act, the FDA shall:

   (a) establish recall procedures and public noti ication guidelines for bottled
       drinking water that fails to meet applicable standards or poses a health risk;
       and

   (b) require each manufacturer or bottler to develop, maintain, and submit a recall
       plan, including traceability, customer noti ication, product retrieval, and
       disposal procedures.

SECTION 9. Prohibition of Dual Use of Bottling Equipment. — Within twelve (12)
months from the effectivity of this Act, the FDA shall prescribe sanitary rules that
prohibit the processing and bottling of non-carbonated Bottled Drinking Water using
equipment primarily used for milk, fruit juice, or other food products likely to
contribute nutrients for microbiological growth, unless effective cleaning, sanitation,
and validation protocols approved by the FDA are implemented.

                                                                           6|Page
SECTION 10. Bottling, Packaging, and Storage Study. — The FDA shall conduct a
comprehensive study of contaminants and degradation risks arising from bottling,
packaging materials, handling, transport, and storage of Bottled Drinking Water, and
shall publish evidence-based recommendations. The FDA may adopt additional
standards based on the study, consistent with this Act.

SECTION 11. Labeling; Consumer Information. — Within six (6) months from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOH, through the FDA, shall establish and enforce clear,
uniform labeling requirements for all Bottled Drinking Water products, which shall
include, at a minimum:

   (a) the declared water type/category and its de inition;
   (b) the source (e.g., spring, well, municipal source), stated in plain language;
   (c) the type of treatment or process applied, if any;
   (d) the date of bottling or production lot code with a consumer-facing explanation;
   (e) the name and address of the manufacturer/bottler, and the FDA authorization
       reference;
   (f) numerical speci ication of sodium content, where applicable; and
   (g) such other consumer information necessary to prevent misleading labeling.

SECTION 12. Enforcement Powers; Coordination. — The FDA shall be the primary
implementing and enforcement agency for this Act, in coordination with the DOH,
LGUs, and other relevant agencies. For purposes of this Act, the FDA may conduct
inspections, collect samples, require reports, and take appropriate enforcement
actions consistent with its charter and applicable laws.

SECTION 13. Penalties. — Any person who manufactures, imports, distributes, sells,
or offers for sale Bottled Drinking Water that is misbranded, adulterated, or non-
compliant with applicable standards and orders issued pursuant to this Act shall be
penalized in accordance with applicable laws enforced by the FDA, without prejudice
to administrative sanctions, product seizure, suspension or revocation of
authorizations, and recall orders.

SECTION 14. Appropriations. — The initial amount necessary to implement this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the FDA under the DOH.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for continued implementation shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 16. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
