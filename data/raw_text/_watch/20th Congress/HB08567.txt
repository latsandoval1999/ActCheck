                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

In 1986, we turned our backs on a 100% complete, extremely well-built, and ready to
run nuclear power plant. Since then, we have tried all other technologies for power
generation, but have failed to make the price and supply of electricity cheap, clean, or
reliable.


Public energy policy favoring a nuclear direction in the 1970s and early 1980s was
driven by the 1973 Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) -led oil
crisis, where we saw large jumps in prices and long lines at gasoline stations, including
in the United States. Today, ~53 years later, we find ourselves in a similar situation as
another energy crisis is emanating from the same region of the world. The Iran War of
2026. Who knows what the outcome of this world event will end up being, for the
Philippines.


40 years later, unlike for Korea and France, our would-be exit from fossil fuel energy
dependence, the PNPP-1 remains unused, but ready to be commissioned. Such would
have been a monumental step toward preserving foreign exchange wealth and ushering
in a poverty-ending economy.


In pursuing post-1986 electricity supply, we ended up today with 65% coal and oil, 20%
Malampaya gas, for a total of 85% fossil fuel, and almost 13% of mainly hydro, and
geothermal, with the balance being wind and solar. And unfortunately, Malampaya gas
is fast running out and will have to be imported as liquefied natural gas (LNG) for future
incremental capacity.


Our incremental growth will almost certainly be heavily skewed toward imported fossil
fuels. And because of our ban on new coal, it will most likely be even more expensive
imported LNG. As can be seen in the recent and ongoing war in Iran, LNG can be quite
unreliable as a source of energy. It is also very volatile in price as a fuel source. Being
the preferred fuel source of wealthy countries like Europe, Korea, Taiwan, Japan, China,
and others. Countries we cannot possibly outbid in a price war.


Going nuclear means that we can avoid 95% of the expense of importing these fossil
fuel sources on an equivalent energy output basis because of uranium’s (nuclear fuel)
millions of times higher energy density. Thus, saving our country tens of billions of
dollars in avoided annual foreign exchange expenditures.


Nuclear also eliminates the huge burden on our and allied navies’ task of defending
against blockade by aggressor countries which, if it came to pass, could immediately
knockout our electricity supply causing economic pain, hardship, and possible collapse.


Unlike fossil fuels which are “just-in-time” sources of energy, and because of the
immense physical inventories needed by generating plants, it is impractical to have
enough physical storage space and working capital for said fossil fuels for more than a
couple of months or even weeks. On the other hand, nuclear has its entire fuel inventory
good for 18-24 months stored inside its reactor. Thus, eliminating the huge merchant
fleet of bulk coal and bulk LNG carriers necessary to keep fossil fueled electricity

                                                                                         2
running. With nuclear, a refueling load good for 18-24 months fits in the physical space
of a single jeepney. Imagine the benefit of eliminating this merchant fleet to our
environment and economy as running costs, investments, and pollution are eliminated.


Now that there is a clear worldwide shift in attitudes towards nuclear power to the
positive, with 38 countries declaring that the world must triple nuclear capacity so that
there would be a chance of meeting “net-zero” climate goals by 2050; it behooves us to
have a clear plan and policy in place to use nuclear as the main solution for cheap, clean,
yet reliable electricity supply. This would enable the Filipino people to finally be
empowered towards an end to poverty.


Nuclear is standalone and baseload, unlike wind and solar, which requires LNG and
massive storage to become technically viable. Such crutches are inevitably ultra
expensive and require multiple levels of investment for workarounds. These ultimately
lead to the Filipino paying much more for electricity than he would need to if the
solution were to be nuclear.


It has been said that the Philippine Nuclear Power Plant I (PNPP-1) is a white elephant.
It has also been said that PNPP-1 cost the Philippines 2.2billion USD. The truth is
PNPP-1 completed its hot functional testing in May of 1984. This means that it had run
on and off at around 5 megawatts to test and prove all its systems. Thus, proving for all
who are interested in facts that PNPP-1 was NOT a white elephant.


It was also certified as ready for core loading by an Operational Safety Review Team
(OSART) II mission of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) in March of
1985. With the nuclear fuel being on hand at around the same timeframe, PNPP-1 is
clearly a victim of partisan politics. The other truths are: PNPP-1 cost 1.421 Billion
USD in principal and 0.699 Billion USD in “out of pocket” interest by August of 2007,
the year PNPP-1’s debt was fully paid. “Out of pocket” because she was not allowed to
earn by operating, in order to pay this interest and principal. One of her sister plants,
the KORI Unit II nuclear plant in Busan, Korea, costs similar and paid for itself,

                                                                                         3
including interest, in only six years of operation. Surely, we must take cognizance of
our past mistakes moving forward.


It is surprising to me that it is now March of 2026, and in my opinion, the Philippine’s
nuclear assets: the plant, the plant site, the power line, and the nuclear village in Bagac,
are still worth in the vicinity of 2 Billion USD 40 years after the fact. It surely seems
that this was a quality investment in its time.


As the U.S. scrambles, to reactivate nuclear plants, some of which are up to 10 years
older than PNPP-1 and worn through use, including the site of the worst nuclear incident
in American history: the Three Mile Island (TMI) nuclear power plant, we should not
put our brand-new nuclear power plant to waste. Instead, we should finally allow it to
demonstrate what it has been worth all these years. It has the potential to at least begin
to halve Philippine electricity prices which will, in turn, have a direct impact on our
gross domestic product (GDP).


It behooves us to use the value of our Philippine nuclear assets as equity so that the
burden of financing could possibly be eliminated as a burden to our General
Appropriations Act (GAA). This is the clearest and quickest step forward for the
Philippines to enter the nuclear age.


Should we decide not to go down that path, we need to appropriate the required equity
so that we may embark on nuclear buildout in localities where social license is clear and
unambiguous.


Further, to launch a broader nuclear energy program, we must as a matter of urgency
ensure structural and comprehensive whole-of-government support, including
sovereign guarantees and other mechanisms, to facilitate the establishment of nuclear
energy projects beyond the PNPP site, as well as a thriving nuclear energy industry in
the Philippines.

It is my prayer that we aspire to at least 16,000 MW of nuclear being online in the
Philippines before the end of 2045. Such, and even twice more, is with sound basis, and
is incremental and not replacement in nature. This topic and its basis will be fertile
ground for productive discussion and debate.


For the Philippines, the context of going nuclear is perfect because we are a fossil fuel
poor country. Nuclear fits us to a “T”.


This measure seeks to provide society with a clear policy path and method for
substantial nuclear to be pursued, financed, and implemented, in the most efficient
manner possible.


The enactment of this bill is most earnestly sought.

                                ARTICLE I:
              TITLE, DECLARATION OF POLICIES AND DEFINITIONS


Section 1. Short Title
This Act shall be known as the “Nuclear Energy Sector Integration Act”, referred to herein as
the “Act”.

Sec.2. Declaration of Policies
It is hereby declared to be the policy of the State to:
       (a)     Achieve reduced cost of electricity to drive economic growth and promote
               sustainable industrialization;
       (b)     Minimize the national foreign exchange requirements for fuel importation;
       (c)     Ensure energy security and independence by reducing reliance on imported
               fuel;
       (d)     Recognize the role of the Government of the State in providing the enabling
               environment necessary to establish the Philippine nuclear energy sector and
               due to matters of national security, energy security, nuclear security and non-
               proliferation, and fossil fuel independence;



                                                 1
       (e)       Recognize the key role of nuclear energy in achieving reduced greenhouse gas
                 emissions and achieving Sustainable Development Goal 7 of access to
                 affordable, reliable and sustainable energy;
       (f)       Recognize nuclear energy as an environmentally sustainable zero-emissions
                 energy source;
       (g)       Deem the nuclear energy sector as a priority investment sector that will
                 regularly form part of the Philippine Development Plan, recognizing the
                 contribution nuclear energy can make to economic and social transformation
                 to reinvigorate job creation and accelerate poverty reduction by steering the
                 economy back on a high-growth path;
       (h)       Provide an enabling environment to accelerate and support the development of
                 the nuclear energy sector in the Philippines including to generate jobs, spur
                 enterprise growth, attract investments, grow globally competitive and
                 innovative industries and upgrade the country’s participation in regional and
                 global value chains;
       (i)       Safeguard and improve the integrity, reliability and stability of the country’s
                 electric power grid;
       (j)       Facilitate the establishment of a quality and safety culture throughout the
                 Philippine nuclear industrial landscape;
       (k)       Enter into nuclear cooperation agreements with countries supplying uranium
                 and other nuclear material, thorium, nuclear reactor technology, equipment,
                 components and services to the nuclear sector; and
       (l)       Establish the necessary infrastructure and mechanisms to carry out the
                 mandates specified in this Act and other existing laws.

Sec.3. Scope
This Act shall establish the framework to accelerate and sustain the development of the nuclear
energy sector in the Philippines by encouraging Filipino and foreign investment, establishing
and mandating an Office of Nuclear Energy within the Department of Energy (DOE), and
ensuring the availability of Government mechanisms and incentives to support the nuclear
energy sector.

                             ARTICLE II:
          NUCLEAR CAPACITY TARGETS AND FOSSIL FUEL PHASEOUT


Sec.5. Purpose of Article II
Provisions under Article II are designed to establish the State’s policies with respect to the
achievement of nuclear energy generating capacity targets and the responsible phase out of
fossil fuel.

Sec.6. Nuclear Generation Capacity Targets by 2045
It is declared to be the policy of the State to achieve (i) at least 16,000 MWe of new nuclear
generation capacity online by 2045, or (ii) up to 32,000 MWe of new nuclear generation



                                                4
capacity online by 2045 if the trajectory of economic growth of the State necessitates such
expansion.

Sec.7. Nuclear Generation Capacity Targets by 2060
Before end of the year 2042, Congress shall establish new, forward-looking targets for nuclear
generation capacity to be achieved by 2060. Such targets shall be greater than the targets set
forth in Sec. 6.

Sec.8. Replacement of Fossil Fuel
It is declared to be the policy of the State to eventually replace fossil fuel generation with
baseload generation from hydro, geothermal or nuclear energy, provided that the total capacity
inadequacy, including reserve capacity, no longer exists.

The DOE is mandated to ensure the physical construction, operation, and availability of
adequate spinning reserve capacity. Such spinning reserve shall mean online generation
capacity that is unused but can be rapidly dispatched to handle sudden generation losses,
unexpected load surges, or intermittency from wind or solar sources. The adequacy of the
spinning reserve shall be determined by DOE in consultation with the National Grid
Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP).

The Philippine Grid Code notwithstanding, synchronized reserve capacity may be created in
one of two ways: (a) running a plant at minimum rated output; or (b) running a plant at high
output but short of its maximum output. The percentage outputs are to be defined explicitly
for both cases.

The State shall determine how such reserve capacity will be paid for, both as far as capital
expenditures and operating expenses are concerned.

In addition, other categories of reserve capacity, online and offline in type, shall be made
available for connection to the grid to satisfy a certain percentage mandate as determined by
this Act.

Sec.9. Plan to cease fossil fuel generating capacity
Within one (1) year of the passage of the Act, the DOE and the owners of fossil fuel generating
facilities shall work cooperatively to produce a fossil fuel generating plant closure forecast by
estimating the remaining economic life of all such facilities.


                                                5
The DOE shall also:

   (a) Identify reserve fossil fuel generating facilities that, despite being closed, shall be
       maintained in a state such that they can be deployed in an emergency or to fulfill
       reserve capacity; and

   (b) Develop a proposal for how such reserve facilities can be efficiently compensated for
       providing this service, including consideration of how they will procure necessary fuel.


                                   ARTICLE III:
                            OFFICE OF NUCLEAR ENERGY


Sec.10. Purpose of Article III
Provisions under Article III are designed to establish and mandate the Office of Nuclear Energy
within the Department of Energy.

Sec.11. Establishment of Office of Nuclear Energy and general responsibilities
There is hereby established an Office of Nuclear Energy within the DOE, which shall, until
6,000 MWe of new nuclear generation capacity is achieved, report directly to the Office of
the President and, thereafter, report to the Secretary of Energy.
The Office shall be responsible for:
       (a) Facilitating the achievement of the nuclear capacity targets set forth in Article II
           of this Act;
       (b) The promotion, planning and development of domestic nuclear energy generation
           and related technologies and industries; and
       (c) Implementing the mechanisms provided for in this Act to support the development
           of the nuclear energy sector in the Philippines.

Sec.12. Budget
The Office of Nuclear Energy shall have an annual budget that is equivalent to or greater than
the budget allocated to the Renewable Energy Development Program of the DOE, which shall
be included in the Department’s annual budget.
In addition, there shall be a separate allocation necessary to establish the Office of Nuclear
Energy and a budget for the appointment of external advisers, both domestic and foreign, in
areas of engineering, law, commerce and finance.

                                               6
A per cent of every annual budget of the Office of Nuclear Energy must be dedicated to public
relations and community engagement related to nuclear energy in pursuit of the policy of the
State as specified in this Act, provided that such per cent shall be equivalent to or greater than
the equivalent expenditure of the Renewable Energy Development Program of the Department
of Energy.

Sec.13. Appointment of Undersecretary and Staff
The Undersecretary for Nuclear Energy shall be the head of the Office of Nuclear Energy and
shall be exclusively dedicated to matters of nuclear energy.
The Undersecretary for Nuclear Energy shall be appointed by the President and shall have the
following phrase in the oath of office: “I shall strive to achieve at least (i) 16,000 MWe of new
nuclear generation capacity online by 2045; or up to 32,000 MWe of new nuclear generation
capacity online by 2045 if the trajectory of economic growth of the State necessitates such
expansion to 32,000 MWe”.
The Office of Nuclear Energy shall have the number of Plantilla positions equivalent to or
greater than the number allocated to the Renewable Energy Development Program of the
Department of Energy. Notwithstanding Republic Act No. 7638, otherwise known as
“Department of Energy Act of 1992”, appointments to these positions shall be made by the
Undersecretary of the Office of Nuclear Energy.

Sec.14. Specific duties defined in this Act
The Office of Nuclear Energy shall:
    (a)      Develop a detailed and practical roadmap to achieve the nuclear generation
             capacity targets specified in Sec. 6 of this Act by 2045;
    (b)      Develop the plan to phase out fossil fuel generating facilities in accordance with
             Article II of this Act;
    (c)      Identify and cultivate domestic industry that may participate in the localization of
             nuclear services, materials, and technology providers in the Philippines and for
             future nuclear exports;
    (d)      Develop a cohesive strategy for engagement with foreign governments of both
             mature and embarking civil nuclear energy nations, as well as international export
             credit agencies, development banks, and other lending institutions, to encourage
             civil nuclear power cooperation;


                                                 7
    (e)     Within twelve (12) months of the effective date of this Act, take the lead, in
            cooperation with other relevant ministries, to ensure the ongoing validity of the
            Philippine’s current bilateral nuclear cooperation agreements and the entry into
            new bilateral nuclear cooperation agreements to facilitate nuclear trade with all
            countries supplying uranium, thorium and other nuclear material, nuclear reactor
            technology, equipment, components and services to the nuclear sector;
     (f)    Institute a comprehensive public relations and community engagement strategy to
            promote understanding of the benefits of nuclear energy within the Philippines;
    (g)     Complete every twelve (12) months of the effective date of this Act, a report to
            Congress on nuclear power generation priority within an economic dispatch model,
            being a model that minimizes the cost of electricity generation without sacrificing
            quality and reliability; and
    (h)     Perform other functions and responsibilities that are necessary to fulfill its mandate
            as set forth in this Act.


                                ARTICLE IV:
                  SUPPORT TO NEW NUCLEAR POWER PROJECTS


Sec.15. Purpose of Article IV
Provisions under Article IV are designed to further the achievement of the policy objectives
of this Act by providing investment support and reducing financial risk prior to commercial
operation of a new nuclear power plant, as well as enabling Government-owned and operated
nuclear power projects.

Sec.16. Exemption from the Electric Power Industry Reform Act for Nuclear Power
Investment
Notwithstanding any provision to the contrary under Republic Act No. 9136, otherwise known
as the “Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001”, nuclear power plants shall be exempt
from restrictions that limit Government investment in, support to, or ownership in electricity
generation facilities. This exemption shall apply exclusively to nuclear power plant
development, construction, operation, electricity sales and related activities as defined under
this Act.




                                                8
Sec.17. Government Investment via the National Development Company
To achieve the nuclear capacity targets established under Article II, the Government is entitled
to invest in nuclear power plant developments provided they are being advanced by a proponent
which has a Certificate of Compliance from the Energy Regulatory Commission (or ERC) or,
in the determination of the ERC, is capable of obtaining such a certificate.

National Development Company (the “NDC”) shall establish a nuclear energy division with a
dedicated manager and staff.

The Government shall channel all nuclear power investment and financing through the NDC.

The NDC is hereby authorised to invest in, or extend financing to, nuclear power projects
owned and operated by private entities incorporated in the Philippines, including those in which
such entities are substantially owned by foreign persons or corporations.

Government investment in nuclear energy projects through the NDC may include:

   (a) Taking an equity investment;
   (b) Providing a loan or loan guarantee;
   (c) Providing a guarantee of payments under a power purchase agreement;
   (d) Through its existing power of eminent domain, purchasing land which is suitable to site
       a nuclear power plant, and subsequently contributing the land as an in-kind investment
       in a nuclear power project; and
   (e) Any other investment which takes the form of a financial asset.

Sec.18. Direct Government Support
The Government may also provide support which does not take the form of a financial asset
and is not provided through the NDC. In this case, the support shall be provided directly by the
Government and may include the following:

   (a) A sovereign guarantee;
   (b) Cost overrun insurance;
   (c) Allocation of appropriately zoned land to a proponent under a concessional long-term
       lease; and
   (d) Other forms of support as determined by the Government.
With respect to Government cost overrun insurance, the Office of Nuclear Energy shall advise
the Government on the terms, conditions, prerequisites, audit and oversight, project ownership
                                               9
structure and policy threshold as a percentage of the initially planned budget for any project
seeking to access Government cost overrun insurance.

In the event a Government-provided construction cost overrun insurance policy is required to
pay out funds to a project, the payment shall be in return for commensurate equity in the project
granted to the NDC.

Sec.19. Government Ownership
Where a developer is unable to continue a project for any reason, the NDC may acquire, hold,
or assume an ownership interest in a nuclear power plant and, at the direction of the President
of the Philippines, assume responsibility for its development.

Sec.20. Government Development and Ownership of Nuclear Power Plants
The President of the Philippines may direct the NDC to develop, finance, construct, own and
operate a nuclear power plant where the Department of Energy (DOE) advises that one or
more of the following circumstances is likely to occur:

   (a) Private developers are unlikely to build sufficient nuclear energy capacity to meet the
       nuclear capacity targets in Sec. 6;
   (b) Power system security is, or may be compromised due to:
           (1) Insufficient dispatchable generation capacity; or
           (2) Inadequate spinning reserve, voltage control or other operational means of
               maintaining a stable electricity supply;
   (c) There is inadequate security of fuel supply; or
   (d) Electricity affordability is, or may be, compromised because of insufficient
       investment in dispatchable generation capacity.

A directive issued under this Section shall authorize the NDC to commence planning activities
only. No expenditure or borrowing shall occur except in accordance with appropriations made
by law and financing arrangements approved by the Secretary of Finance.

Notwithstanding Article IV, Republic Act No. 12305 applies to all nuclear power plants in the
territory of the Republic of the Philippines.

Sec.23. Construction-Phase Investment Tax Credits
To encourage private investment into new nuclear power plants, the first such investment made
by an owner shall be eligible for Construction-Phase Investment Tax Credits (“CP-ITCs”).
Subsequent investments in nuclear power projects shall not be eligible for CP-ITCs but can
utilize the Reinvestment and Productivity Credit Scheme provided for in Sec. 28.
To encourage a high degree of Philippine ownership, nuclear power projects with greater than
fifty percent (50%) combined ownership by Philippine corporations, Philippine nationals or
the Government shall be eligible for a CP-ITC with a rate of fifty percent (50%) of eligible
spend. Nuclear power plant projects with less than fifty percent (50%) combined ownership by
Philippine corporations, Philippine nationals or the Government shall be eligible for a CP-ITC
rate of thirty percent (30%) of eligible spend.
Nuclear power plant CP-ITCs shall be:
   (a) Granted annually from the time of the final investment decision (“FID”) to the
       commencement of commercial operation of the owner’s first (1st) nuclear power project
       (the “Construction Phase”);
   (b) Pre-agreed and based on the project cost estimate used to sanction FID, including the
       expected annual expenditures throughout the Construction Phase;

                                                  13
   (c) Claimed annually throughout the Construction Phase and shall not be awarded for
       unclaimed eligible expenditure incurred in previous years;
   (d) Transferable;
   (e) Non-refundable; and
   (f) Able to be carried forward for up to fifteen (15) years.
Within one (1) year of the passage of this Act, the Office of Nuclear Energy and the BIR shall
promulgate implementing rules and regulations governing the administration of the CP-ITCs,
including the following:
   (a) Establishing procedures for the annual submission, verification, and approval of
       eligible Construction Phase expenditures, including timelines for cost certification by
       independent accredited auditors or public infrastructure verifiers;
   (b) Defining the roles and responsibilities of the Office of Nuclear Energy as the technical
       lead for the assessment of project eligibility and Construction Phase progress
       verification, and the BIR as the fiscal authority responsible for issuing CP-ITCs,
       compliance tracking, and enforcement of claw-back provisions;
   (c) Designing and implementing mechanisms to detect, deter, and disqualify improper
       claims, including:
             (1) Claw-back provisions for non-compliance;
             (2) Related-party transaction controls, including enhanced disclosure and audit
                triggers for capital expenditures involving sponsors, affiliates, or subsidiaries;
                and
             (3) An eligibility ceiling per project, expressed as a proportion of total qualified
                expenditures as forecast at the time of FID.
   (d) Integrating a robust regulated project accounts ledger to record all CP-ITC claims,
       approvals, and adjustments, ensuring auditability, traceability, and public transparency;
       and
   (e) Requiring the annual publication of CP-ITC amounts awarded per project, linked to
       qualifying benchmarks such as Philippine ownership share, employment generation and
       local content requirements.

                                 ARTICLE V:
                      FOREIGN OWNERSHIP AND INVESTMENT



Sec.24. Purpose of Article V
This Article governs foreign investment and ownership in the Philippine nuclear power sector
including the investment conditions applicable to foreign investment, and operational
conditions applicable to foreign-owned entities.

Sec.25. Foreign Ownership of Nuclear Power Plants
Foreign entities may own nuclear power plants in accordance with the Foreign Investment Act
(RA7042), the Philippine Competition Act (RA10667), and other applicable laws including
this Act.

Foreign corporations shall be entitled to access financing mechanisms, fiscal incentives, and
other forms of support provided under this Act, subject to compliance with its conditions.

Sec.26. Operation of Nuclear Facilities by Foreign Corporations
Foreign corporations may operate nuclear power plants within the territory of the Republic of
the Philippines, subject to the following conditions:

   (a) The designated plant manager or the chief nuclear officer shall be a Philippine national;
   (b) By no later than the tenth (10th) year of commercial operations, at least fifty percent
       (50%) of the certified reactor operators shall be Philippine nationals. This proportion
       shall not fall below fifty percent (50%) thereafter;
   (c) By no later than the fifth (5th) year of commercial operations, at least eighty percent
       (80%) of the total workforce at the nuclear power plant shall be Philippine nationals.
       This proportion shall not fall below eighty percent (80%) at any time thereafter.

The Office of Nuclear Energy shall monitor and ensure compliance with this Section in
coordination with the PhilATOM

In the event of non-compliance, the operating license of the foreign corporation may be
suspended. Upon recommendation by the PhilATOM and subject authorization by PhilATOM,
the Government may appoint an interim body to assume operational control and management
of the facility until such time as full compliance with the above conditions is established.




                                               15
Sec.27. Conditional Repatriation of Earnings by Foreign Owners
Within one (1) year of the passage of this Act, the Office of Nuclear Energy, the Board of
Investments and the Securities and Exchange Commission shall promulgate rules and
implementing guidelines governing the repatriation of earnings by foreign partial or full owners
of nuclear power plants, in accordance with the following principles:
   (a) Unconditional Repatriation Period - Permission to repatriate earnings without
          restriction until the full recovery of their initial capital outlay.
   (b) Conditional Repatriation After Capital Recovery – Repatriation permitted upon
          recovery of the initial capital outlay subject to:
          (1) A minimum of fifty percent (50%) of annual earnings shall be reinvested within the
                 Republic of the Philippines;
          (2) The remaining fifty percent (50%) may be repatriated, subject to verification of
                 compliance by the designated oversight and governance panel; and
          (3) Earnings arising from qualified reinvestment activities shall be exempt from any
                 subsequent repatriation restrictions.
   (c) Scope of Qualified Reinvestment Activities – Reinvestment shall include:
          (i)       Equity, debt, or project-based investment in Philippine-based businesses or
                    commercial enterprises; and
          (ii)      Development, financing or enhancement of public infrastructure in coordination
                    with relevant Government agencies.
   (d) Linkage to the Reinvestment and Productivity Credit (RPC) Scheme - Where
          reinvestment activities quality under the RPC Scheme established in Sec, 31 of this Act,
          foreign partial or full owners shall be entitled to claim the corresponding credits, subject
          to the criteria and procedures provided therein.
The Office of Nuclear Energy, the Board of Investments, and the Securities and Exchange
Commission shall jointly implement governance and oversight mechanisms to determine the
eligibility, verification, and regulatory treatment of earnings repatriation by foreign partial or
full owners of nuclear power plants, including earnings derived from reinvestment activities.
Such oversight shall include audit protocols, period reporting and mechanisms for review or
appeal.




                                                     16
                         ARTICLE VI:
    GOVERNMENT PROCUREMENT, GRID ACCESS, AND REINVESTMENT
          MEASURES FOR COMMERCIAL NUCLEAR ENERGY


Sec.28. Purpose of Article VI
This Article is intended to further the achievement of the policy objectives of this Act by
supporting nuclear power plants when they begin operating. It allows the Government to
purchase or support the purchase of nuclear electricity, ensures that nuclear power plants
receive priority on the power grid, and offers incentives for owners to reinvest earnings into
new generation, capacity uprates, productivity enhancements, and skilled workforce
development.

Sec.29. Government Procurement of Nuclear Energy
To support stable market development for nuclear energy, the Government may establish,
acquire ownership of, or finance an electricity retail entity solely for the purpose of entering
electricity offtake agreements with nuclear power projects. Any such entity—whether
established, acquired, or financed by the Government—shall:
   (a) Operate as a self-financing entity through electricity sales and related business
       activities;
   (b) Align procurement activities with the objectives of affordability, national energy
       security, emissions reduction, industrial competitiveness and long-term market
       viability;
   (c) Avoid practices that distort competitive electricity markets or displace private sector
       functions, except where explicitly required to accelerate nuclear energy adoption;
   (d) Enter into offtake agreements exclusively to support nuclear energy procurement and
       financing; and
   (e) Publish periodic reports detailing procurement decisions, financial performance, and
       alignment with strategic energy objectives set forth above, in accordance with
       transparency requirements to be defined by the Department of Energy.

Sec.30. Priority Dispatch for Nuclear Power Plants
The Energy Regulatory Commission shall promulgate a rule recognizing nuclear power plants
as “must-run” power generating assets. Nuclear power plants shall receive priority dispatch in
the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market, irrespective of the prevailing economic order-of-merit
dispatch protocol.
                                               17
The ERC shall include safeguards to prevent distortion of competitive market dynamics,
including provisions for periodic review of dispatch conditions.

Sec.31. Reinvestment and Productivity Credit Scheme
To accelerate the growth of the Philippine nuclear energy sector, the Office of Nuclear Energy,
in coordination with the Bureau of Internal Revenue, shall develop and administer a
Reinvestment and Productivity Credit Scheme ("RPC Scheme") to promote the reinvestment
of retained earnings into:
   (a) Additional nuclear generating capacity;
   (b) Productivity-enhancing investment into existing nuclear power plants; and
   (c) Nuclear workforce training and related infrastructure.
The RPC scheme shall be designed and implemented in accordance with the following
principles. The RPC Scheme shall:
   (a) Stimulate reinvestment of retained earnings into the areas identified in Sec. 31 above;
   (b) Reduce financial barriers to reinvestment while upholding standards of transparency;
   (c) Support the objectives of affordability, national energy security, emissions reduction,
       and industrial competitiveness; and
   (d) Apply to all registered entities engaged in the ownership, operation, or expansion of
       nuclear power plants.
The RPC scheme shall consist of the following four incentive components:
   (a) Under the Reinvestment and Productivity Account Incentive, nuclear power plant
       owners may contribute retained earnings to a ledger recognized by the BIR for
       qualified reinvestment purposes. Expenditures from the Reinvestment and
       Productivity Account shall earn transferable Tax Credit Certificates (TCCs) equivalent
       to twenty-five percent (25%) of qualified spend. TCCs may be transferred to other
       registered entities or offset against eligible tax liabilities.
   (b) Under the Redeemable Tax Holiday Incentive, any nuclear power plant owner who
       makes qualified reinvestments from the Reinvestment and Productivity Account
       exceeding forty percent (40%) of net income in a given financial year shall earn a one-
       year income tax holiday, redeemable in any of the subsequent four (4) financial years.
       Redemption shall require certification from the Office of Nuclear Energy confirming
       that the reinvestment threshold has been met. Once a nuclear power plant owner earns
       a tax holiday, a five-year incentive cycle shall commence, during which no additional

                                                 18
       tax holidays may be earned. A new eligibility cycle may begin only upon the expiry
       of the prior redemption window.
   (c) Under the Accelerated Depreciation Incentive, qualifying capital investments may be
       depreciated over a period not less than fifty percent (50%) of the duration permitted
       under existing tax laws.
   (d) Under the Bonus Deduction Incentive, nuclear power plant owners may claim an
       additional twenty-five percent (25%) tax deduction on qualified expenditures under
       the RPC Scheme.
The RPC Scheme shall recognize the following as qualified investments or expenditures:
   (a) Construction of new nuclear generating capacity;
   (b) Uprating of existing nuclear units;
   (c) Investments enhancing availability, uptime, or operational productivity;
   (d) Training expenses for nuclear plant operators, technicians, and engineers;
   (e) Investment into nuclear sector training centres and related infrastructure; and
   (f) Deployment of automation and other productivity-enhancing technologies for the
       nuclear sector, provided that,
in all cases, compliance with the requirements of PhilATOM, including for safety upgrades
after the issuance of the operating license for the respective facility, are not qualified
investments or expenditures for the purpose of the RPC Scheme.
The RPC Scheme is a targeted investment support mechanism to achieve the nuclear generating
capacity targets outlined in Article II of this Act and shall not constitute a permanent
preferential tax regime for the nuclear energy sector. This PRC Scheme shall expire upon the
earlier of: (a) 31 December 2060, or (b) the calendar year in which the Philippines achieves its
nuclear generating capacity target as set out under Article II.
Sec.32. Nuclear Electricity Revenue Stabilization Mechanism
To ensure the bankability and timely development of nuclear power plants, the Government
may establish long-term revenue stabilization arrangements for nuclear energy projects
accredited under this Act.
The ERC may approve long-term electricity supply arrangements for nuclear power plants for
periods not exceeding twenty (20) years.
Such arrangements may take the form of:
   (a) Regulated tariffs;
   (b) Long-term power supply agreements;
                                               19
   (c) Contracts for difference; and
   (d) Other mechanisms ensuring stable revenue recovery up to full return of investment.
These mechanisms shall ensure predictable revenue streams sufficient to support financing of
nuclear facilities while protecting consumers from unreasonable electricity prices.
Sec.33. Regulatory and Fiscal Stability For Accredited Nuclear Projects
Nuclear power plant projects that have reached final investment decision and obtained the
required nuclear construction and/or operating license from PhilATOM shall be entitled to a
stable regulatory and fiscal framework for the duration of the capital recovery period, in all
cases, not exceeding twenty (20) years.
Accordingly, with respect to such projects:
   (a) Fiscal incentives granted under this Act shall remain in force for such projects, and
       shall not be withdrawn or materially reduced.
   (b) If the PhilATOM imposes amendments to regulatory requirements, the PhilATOM
       must justify such amendments based on nuclear safety, nuclear security, or
       compliance with international obligations, and for any such amendments that impair
       the financial viability of the project, the ERC is mandated to make adjustments to
       compensate.
   (c) Regulatory requirements of the ERC shall not be modified in a manner that
       materially impairs the financial viability of the project.
   (d) In the event that future legislation materially alters the investment framework, the
       Government shall adopt appropriate transitional measures or compensatory
       adjustments to preserve the originally approved investment conditions to the extent
       consistent with the constitution and applicable law, to protect the integrity and
       sanctity of contractual agreements, national obligations, and commitments.


                            ARTICLE VII:
              SECURING COMMUNITY AND PUBLIC ACCEPTANCE

Sec.34. Purpose of Article VII
This Article is intended to further the achievement of the policy objectives of this Act by
ensuring local communities benefit from a nuclear power plant in their region and
complementing existing provisions in the Electric Power Industry Reform Act RA9136
Chapter III, Section 37(n).



                                               20
Sec.35. Host Community Electricity Benefit
In recognition of its role as host to energy infrastructure and in addition to existing
entitlements, residents within the relevant host Local Government Unit (or LGU) may receive,
free of charge, an allocation of electricity generated by the facility of up to one percent (1%)
of its theoretical annual output, calculated using a ninety percent (90%) capacity factor. This
allocation shall be non-cumulative and earmarked exclusively for use within the host
community.
The free electricity benefit shall be used solely within the geographic boundaries of the host
LGU. Transfer or sale outside the LGU is prohibited, except as expressly authorized by the
Secretary of Energy for contiguous regional economic zones directly benefiting the host
locality.
The free electricity benefit shall be allocated and distributed within the host locality in
accordance with the following principles:
   (a) A portion may be allocated to local enterprises, cooperatives, or industrial locators to
        incentivize investment, improve competitiveness, and stimulate employment. Priority
        shall be given to sectors with high local economic multiplier effects, including
        manufacturing, agribusiness, and digital infrastructure.
   (b) A portion may be allocated to public institutions and services—such as schools,
        hospitals, water utilities, transport hubs, and digital infrastructure—to improve service
        delivery and reduce operating costs.
   (c) The Office of Nuclear Energy, in consultation with the host LGU, shall design the free
        electricity distribution framework, which shall specify eligible end-uses, allocation
        mechanisms, beneficiary selection criteria, and monitoring procedures. Annual reports
        detailing electricity usage and beneficiary sectors shall be published by the LGU and
        made accessible to the public.
Each year, the host LGU shall prepare and submit a proposed allocation schedule for the free
electricity benefit. This nomination shall include:
   (a) Proposed distribution across:
              (i) Industrial and commercial use;
              (ii) Public utilities and infrastructure; and
              (iii) Social service institutions.
   (b) Consideration of high-demand periods relevant to the host community where
        electricity support would yield outsized economic or social benefit.

                                                   21
   (c) Alignment with anticipated electricity availability, coordinated with the Office of
       Nuclear Energy and the nuclear power plant operator.
   (d) A summary of stakeholder consultations involving local business associations,
       cooperatives and educational and health institutions. The final nomination shall be
       publicly disclosed before submission.
The proposed allocation shall be submitted to the Department of Economy, Planning and
Development (DEPDEV), for assessment of its potential benefit to the host community,
including impacts on local productivity and employment generation.
The Undersecretary of the Office of Nuclear Energy shall have regard to the DEPDEV’s
assessment and must ratify the free electricity benefit before it may be implemented. The
Undersecretary of the Office of Nuclear Energy may prescribe conditions or revisions as
necessary to ensure alignment with the free electricity distribution framework.
The free electricity benefit shall reflect generation and availability, with no benefit drawn
during refuelling cycles, planned maintenance or unscheduled outages. The Office of Nuclear
Energy, in coordination with the facility operator, shall publish a rolling availability forecast
to enable proactive planning by the host LGU and beneficiaries.
The free electricity benefit, or any portion thereof, is forfeited if it is not used within the
respective calendar year.

Sec.36. Determination of the Sufficiency of Public Support
Prior to the commencement of site preparation for any new nuclear power plant, the owner or
operator shall secure demonstrable support from the local community. This requirement shall
not apply to:
      (a)    The existing Bataan Nuclear Power Plant site, located in Morong, Bataan; or
      (b)    Any future development involving additional reactors or capacity expansions at
             sites that have previously satisfied the public support requirement under this
             Section.
Support shall be determined through a ballot administered by the Commission on Elections
(COMELEC), in accordance with the following provisions:
      (a)       Participation in the local ballot shall be limited to individuals who:
                (1) Are registered voters in the Philippines, including registered overseas
                   voters;



                                                22
               (2) Maintain a primary residence within the relevant Municipality in which the
                   proposed perimeter fence of the nuclear power station site is located; and
               (3) Are Filipino citizens.
      (b)    Majority support shall be deemed sufficient only if both of the following
             conditions are met:
               (1) At least five percent (5%) of eligible voters, as defined in this Section,
                   participate in the local ballot; and
               (2) More than fifty percent (50%) of participating voters affirm support for the
                   development of the proposed nuclear power station at the designated
                   location.
                   A ballot that fails to meet the minimum participation threshold shall be
                   considered null and void.
COMELEC shall, within one (1) year from the passage of this Act, establish and implement
the procedures necessary to conduct the local ballot as prescribed herein.
Following the conclusion of a local ballot, COMELEC shall certify the results and publicly
release a report detailing:
      (a)    Total number of eligible voters;
      (b)    Total number of participating voters;
      (c)    Vote counts in favor and against the proposed nuclear power station; and
      (d)    Confirmation of whether the minimum support threshold has been met.
The certified results shall be published within fifteen (15) calendar days from the close of the
ballot, through official gazettes and publicly accessible platforms designated by COMELEC.
In the event that a ballot is declared void due to insufficient participation, COMELEC shall
conduct a re-ballot within ninety (90) calendar days. Such re-ballot shall be subject to the
same eligibility criteria and procedural requirements as the original ballot.


                                      ARTICLE VIII:
                              SECURITY OF SUPPLY MEASURES


Sec.37. Purpose of Article VIII
This Article is intended to mitigate the impact of energy commodity price fluctuations on
domestic electricity prices, enhance energy security, and reduce reliance on foreign currency
reserves for the procurement of fuels on international markets.

                                                23
Sec.38. Strategic Uranium Stockpile

A strategic uranium reserve (“Reserve”) is hereby established under the direction of the Office
of Nuclear Energy to safeguard consumers against commodity price volatility in uranium
markets.

The reserve shall comprise any combination of:

   (a) Physical inventory of natural uranium (yellowcake), or intermediate forms such as
         enriched uranium product; and
   (b) Commercial instruments including futures contracts, options, book transfers or
         bilaterial storage and fabrication arrangements with states that produce uranium or
         provide fuel cycle services, provided such states have a nuclear cooperation agreement
         in place with the Republic of the Philippines.

The quantity represented by the Reserve shall equal three (3) years of nuclear fuel requirements
for the installed nuclear generating capacity of the Philippines, calculated using a ninety
percent (90%) capacity factor.

The ERC shall establish an Energy Security Tariff, the proceeds of which shall be disbursed to
a fund managed by the Office of Nuclear Energy to finance the Reserve in all its accepted
forms.

The ERC shall set the Energy Security Tariff at a rate sufficient to procure the required
incremental reserve quantity within five (5) years of new nuclear generating capacity
commencing commercial operation.

The tariff-setting methodology shall prioritize affordability and long-term tariff stability, while
ensuring timely achievement of energy security objectives.

Physical inventory shall be stored in suitable facilities in the Philippines (such as military bases
or roofed interim storage low level or intermediate level waste facilities), or held under
arrangements with states producing uranium or providing fuel cycle services and with which
the Philippines has a nuclear cooperation agreement in place.

Sec.39. Use of the Reserve

The Office of Nuclear Energy shall promulgate rules requiring nuclear power plants to utilise
the Reserve periodically to maintain liquidity and manage the condition and disposition of the
Reserve.

                                                 24
Permitted uses shall include:
       (a) Civil nuclear energy generation, as directed by the Office of Nuclear Energy; and
       (b) Government research, as directed by the Office of Nuclear Energy, in all cases, as
              required for the promotion and sustainment of the Philippine civil nuclear energy
              sector.

Sec.40. Safety Stock of Fabricated Fuel Assemblies

Unless the Office of Nuclear Energy determines otherwise, to ensure continuity of nuclear
operations during supply chain disruptions, each operating reactor shall maintain an onsite
safety stock of fabricated fuel assemblies equivalent to one refueling cycle reload for
conventional light water reactors and one (1) year of continuous operation for other types of
reactors.

Costs incurred in maintaining the safety stock shall be classified as legitimate operating and
maintenance (O&M) expenditures and recovered as passthrough costs under ERC regulations.

Sec.41. Reducing the Electricity Sector’s Reliance on Foreign Currency

To recognize the strategic contribution of nuclear energy in mitigating the erosion of the
Philippines’ foreign currency reserves, the ERC shall, within one hundred eighty (180) days
of the effectivity of this Act, in consultation with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas BSP and the
Office of Nuclear Energy, establish the credit mechanism described in this Section and
integrate its principles into electricity tariff-setting protocols, including generation mix
valuation, technology benchmarking, and foreign exchange sensitivity modeling.

This mechanism shall be design to:

   (a) Quantify the foreign exchange advantages attributable to nuclear energy, including:

       (i)       Avoided depletion of foreign exchange reserves associated with importing
                 fuels such as coal and liquified natural gas;

       (ii)      The dual stability of nuclear fuel procurement, arising from:

                 a. Commodity price insulation, due to long-term contracting and lower
                        market volatility; and

                 b. Intrinsic energy density, which allows nuclear generation to produce large
                        amounts of electricity from small volumes of fuel, resulting in fuel costs

                                                  25
                   comprising a substantially smaller proportion of total generation costs,
                   thereby reducing sensitivity to fluctuations in the foreign current exchange
                   value of the Philippine peso.

       (iii)   The share of nuclear capital investment payable in domestic currency,
               compared to technologies with high foreign exchange intensity.

   (b) Recognize the role of nuclear energy in safeguarding the value of overseas remittances
       by reducing the proportion diverted to commodity imports for the electricity sector;
       and

   (c) Inform ERC decision-making on generation mix planning, technology valuation, and
       long-tern tariff setting, with weighting for lifecycle foreign currency exposure.


                                ARTICLE IX:
                   ZERO-EMISSIONS ENERGY SOURCES PARITY



Sec.42. Purpose of Article IX
The purpose of this Article is to establish the principle that all zero-emissions energy sources
primarily used for electricity generation shall receive equal treatment with respect to
concessions, revenue mechanisms, and other benefits afforded under Republic Act No. 9513,
otherwise known as the “Renewable Energy Act of 2008”.

Sec.43. Parity between Zero-Emissions Energy Sources for Electricity Generation
Within six (6) months of the passage of this Act, the DOE and ERC shall revise all rules,
regulations and eligibility criteria to ensure that all zero-emissions energy sources primarily
used for electricity generation have equal access to all concessions, revenue mechanisms, and
other benefits enjoyed by electricity generating technologies identified in (RA9513) in
accordance with the following principles:
   (a) The DOE and ERC shall revise applicable rules, regulations, and eligibility criteria as
       if all references to "renewable", "renewables", "solar", "wind", "hydro", and "biomass"
       within RA9513 were substituted with the term "zero-emissions energy source" which
       shall include nuclear energy;
   (b) No zero-emissions energy source shall be granted preferential treatment or greater
       access to concessions, benefits, or revenue mechanisms defined in RA9513 relative to
       another, when measured on a per megawatt-hour (MWh) basis;
                                               26
   (c) In the event that Republic Act No. 9513 is repealed, any zero-emissions energy source
       that did not qualify for the concessions, revenue mechanisms, or benefits under RA9513
       prior to the enactment of this Act shall continue to receive equivalent access to such
       concessions, mechanisms, and benefits for a period sufficient to ensure parity in
       duration of access across all zero-emissions energy sources;
   (d) The Office of Nuclear Energy shall evaluate differences in project development
       timelines among zero-emissions energy sources and develop appropriate rules to ensure
       that technologies with longer gestation periods receive access to concessions, revenue
       mechanisms, and benefits for a duration that provides equitable long-term impact. The
       DOE and ERC shall administer and enforce these rules as promulgated by the Office
       of Nuclear Energy.

Sec.44. Prohibiting Benefits Stacking
A nuclear power plant shall not be eligible to receive both a concession, revenue mechanism,
or benefit granted under this Act and an equivalent incentive provided under RA9513.
Within one (1) year of the passage of this Act, the Office of Nuclear Energy shall promulgate
rules to prevent benefit stacking by nuclear power plant developers. These rules shall include
criteria for determining equivalency of concessions, revenue mechanisms, and benefits. The
DOE and ERC shall be responsible for administering and enforcing these rules.
Each nuclear power plant developer shall report to the Office of Nuclear Energy the specific
concessions, revenue mechanisms, or benefits it elects to receive under this Act or RA9513,
and affirm that no equivalent benefit is concurrently being accessed.


                          ARTICLE X:
USE OF THE EXISTING NUCLEAR ENERGY ASSETS OF THE PHILIPPINES OR
 ALTERNATIVE GOVERNMENT DEVELOPMENT OF FIRST NEW NUCLEAR
                     GENERATION CAPACITY

Sec.45. Purpose of Article X
The purpose of this Article is to establish the principles by which the existing nuclear energy
assets of the Philippines will be identified and utilised as the basis of and to develop the nuclear
power program of the Philippines. This Article also provides for an alternative approach to
development of the first 6,000 MWe of new nuclear generation capacity by the Government.




                                                 27
Sec.46. Identification of Existing Nuclear Energy Assets
This Act recognises the following infrastructure as the existing nuclear energy assets of the
Philippines:
   (a) All existing nuclear facilities, including the Bataan Nuclear Power Plant Unit 1 and all
       associated equipment, studies, permits, and intellectual property;
   (b) All existing grid interconnections, substations, corridors and rights-of-way historically
       designed or constructed for nuclear integration;
   (c) All real estate properties owned or controlled by the Government originally designated
       or suitable for nuclear energy purposes, including the Bataan site;
   (d) The Caliraya Pumped Storage Power Plant and its original tandem peak loading support
       function as strategic nuclear complement infrastructure; and
   (e) Any existing Government-held nuclear technology assessments, feasibility reports, or
       engineering packages,
       together, the “Existing Assets”.

Sec.47. Disposition of Existing Assets
Ownership of the Existing Assets of the Philippines is hereby transferred to the National
Development Corporation (NDC) for the primary purpose of using such Existing Assets to
develop and bring into commercial operation existing and new nuclear generating capacity in
the Philippines.
Upon transfer of the Existing Assets to the NDC, the NDC shall be responsible for the
maintenance and upkeep of these Existing Assets to ensure they retain their readiness for future
service.

Sec.48. Principles of Use of Existing Assets
The following uses, and any approved additional uses, may be made of the Existing Assets:
   (a) Use of the assets as an equity contribution towards the refurbishment and startup of the
       BNPP;
   (b) Use of revenues and net proceeds derived from the following sources, which shall be
       deposited into a Restricted Nuclear Development Account (“RNDA”) maintained by
       the NDC:
       (i)     Dividends and distributions from any equity participation; and
       (ii)    Receipts from asset-backed financing.
Subject to approval, funds in the RNDA may only be disbursed to:
                                               28
       (iv)      Finance the construction of new nuclear generating capacity; or
       (v)       Pay for pre-construction development activities, including site assessments,
                 feasibility studies, preliminary design work, licensing activities, community
                 engagement and other development-related tasks.
The NDC shall prepare annual report to Congress, which shall include details of receipts and
disbursements from the RNDA.
Sec.49. Special Transitional Clause
The rehabilitation, recommissioning, and operation of the Bataan Nuclear Power Plant Unit 1
are hereby declared a continuation of an existing national infrastructure project and shall not
be subject to renewed local consent, plebiscite, zoning approval, or community endorsement.
Sec.50. Alternative Government Development
As an alternative to Sec. 46 to 49 inclusive, the development of the first 6,000 MWe of new
nuclear generation capacity shall be undertaken by the NDC and the Government shall:
    (a) Appropriate the equity needed to ensure 6,000 MWe of new nuclear generation
            capacity becomes operational; and
    (b) Provide a sovereign guarantee.
In these circumstances, all the other forms of support shall also be available in accordance with
this Act.


                               ARTICLE XI:
                NUCLEAR POWER AS A STRATEGIC NATIONAL ASSET

Sec.51. Purpose of Article XI
The purpose of this Article is to ensure nuclear power plants, nuclear fuel supply chains and
associated national infrastructure are recognised and treated as strategic national assets.
Sec.52. Nuclear Power as a Strategic National Asset
Nuclear power plants, nuclear fuel supply chains, and associated national infrastructure are
hereby declared strategic national assets whose continuity of development and operation are
indispensable to national security, energy security, macroeconomic stability, and protection
of public welfare.
Sec.53. Exclusive Supreme Court Jurisdiction; Fast Resolution
All challenges involving nuclear power plants shall be filed directly with the Supreme Court
and only heard (i) where the party bringing the challenge has standing, without exception; and
                                                29
(ii) the challenge is not frivolous or vexatious. The Supreme Court shall resolve any challenge
with deliberate dispatch.
Sec.54. Prohibition on Interference
No court, except the Supreme Court, shall issue a temporary restraining order, preliminary
injunction, or preliminary mandatory injunction against any person or entity, whether public or
private, acting under the direction and authorization of the PhilATOM, when such order would
directly or indirectly delay, suspend, restrain, obstruct, or otherwise interfere with nuclear
power plant development or operations.
The Supreme Court may issue such injunctive relief solely on the basis of demonstrated and
material non-compliance with applicable law, regulations, decrees, and terms and conditions
of relevant authorizations.
Sec.55. Financial and Contractual Inviolability
No action or proceeding shall impair or modify:
     (a) EPC contracts and milestone schedules;
     (b) Power supply agreements, capacity payments, or offtake obligations;
     (c) Approved tariffs and cost-recovery mechanisms;
     (d) Sovereign guarantees, letters of support, or credit enhancements; or
     (e) Debt service obligations to domestic or foreign lenders.
Sec.56. Preemption of Local and Sectarian Action
Local governments and non-state actors shall have no power to approve, disapprove, delay,
condition, or obstruct nuclear activities authorized under this Act.


                                       ARTICLE XII
                                    FINAL PROVISIONS

Sec.57. Implementing Rules and Regulations
Within one hundred and eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the relevant
agencies shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act.




                                               30
Sec.58. Separability Clause
If a provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions shall not
be affected and shall remain in full force and effect.

Sec.59. Repealing Clause
All Acts, executive orders, administrative orders, proclamations, rules and regulations
inconsistent with any provisions of this Act, are repealed or modified accordingly.

Sec.60. Effectivity
This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a
newspaper of general circulation.
