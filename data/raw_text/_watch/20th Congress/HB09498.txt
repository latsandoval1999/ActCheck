                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The 1987 Philippine Constitution (Article XV, Section 4) explicitly
mandates the State to protect and promote the right to health of the people and to
afford full protection to the elderly. While the Expanded Senior Citizens Act of
2010 (R.A. No. 9994) and the Universal Health Care Act (R.A. No. 11223) have
laid crucial foundations for elderly welfare, systematic gaps persist in delivering
holistic, preventive, and wellness-oriented care tailored to the needs of senior
citizens.

      Despite existing benefits such as free hospitalization and discounted
medicines, many senior citizens continue to face preventable health deterioration,
unmet mental health needs, and limited access to rehabilitative and wellness
programs. The lack of structured preventive care contributes to avoidable
hospitalizations, reduced quality of life, and unnecessary financial burdens on
families and the healthcare system.

     The Expanded Senior Citizens Health and Wellness Act addresses these
gaps by institutionalizing a comprehensive and proactive approach to senior
  health and wellness. The measure provides for mandatory annual preventive
  check-ups, the establishment of age-friendly wellness centers nationwide with
  rehabilitative and mental health services, mobile and telehealth programs for
  homebound and rural seniors, and strengthened inter-agency coordination among
  the DOH, DSWD, LGUs, and PhilHealth. It also includes digital literacy and
  inclusion programs to empower senior citizens in navigating online health
  services and combating misinformation, with dedicated funding mechanisms to
  ensure sustainability.

         With the Filipino population rapidly aging and projected to reach fourteen
  percent by 2030, this legislation is urgent. By emphasizing preventive care,
  wellness, and digital inclusion, it seeks to uphold the dignity and welfare of senior
  citizens, reduce long-term healthcare costs, and foster a healthier, more resilient,
  and digitally empowered society.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:



SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Expanded Senior
Citizens Health and Wellness Act".


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote and protect the health, dignity, and overall well-being of senior citizens
and to recognize their continuing role in national development. Toward this end,
the State shall ensure accessible, comprehensive, and responsive health services
for senior citizens, including preventive, curative, rehabilitative, and wellness
programs.
      The State shall likewise promote active aging and support mechanisms that
enable senior citizens to live healthy, productive, and meaningful lives, regardless
of socio-economic status.

SEC. 4. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act. Additional funding support
may be sourced from revenues allocated for health programs under existing laws
and from contributions or grants from government-owned or controlled
corporations and other lawful sources, subject to existing budgeting, accounting,
and auditing rules and regulations.


SEC. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health, the Department of Social
Welfare and Development, the Department of Information and Communications
Technology, and the National Commission of Senior Citizens shall promulgate
the implementing rules and regulations necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act.

SEC. 6. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.


SEC. 8. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
