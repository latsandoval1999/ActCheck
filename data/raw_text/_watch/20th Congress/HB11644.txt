                                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

     “A false witness will not go unpunished, and whoever pours out lies will not go free.”
                                         Proverbs 19:5

The society has entered the age of Artificial Intelligence (AI), which gained prominence with
the release of ChatGPT in 2022. Since then, AI has permeated various industries and aspects
of everyday life, influencing everything from critical infrastructure to mobile applications. As
this era is still in its early stages, AI’s transformative potential for efficiency and innovation is
expected to significantly impact governance, economics, healthcare, climate management,
finance, and agriculture. However, as with every new technology, the advent of AI has brought
with it its own share of pitfalls.

In 2022, fraudsters in Thailand successfully scammed thousands from the public by using
deepfake technology to make it appear to victims that they were police officers.1 In early
2024, a multinational company in Hong Kong lost more than US$25 million in a scam after its
employees were fooled by deepfake technology, with one incident involving a digitally
recreated version of its chief financial officer ordering money transfers in a video conference
call.2

The misuse of AI has become even more prevalent in the Philippines, with its more than 97
million internet users, of whom more than 90 million are on social media. In fact, the
Philippines saw the largest increase in deepfakes in 2023, up by 4500% from 2022. And it is
not limited to large scams; several Filipino personalities had their pictures, videos, and voices
used by fraudsters to sell products without their knowledge and consent.3

The dangers of the misuse of AI technology to compromise critical state processes, such as
elections, have not escaped the awareness of the government. In September 2024, the

Commission on Elections promulgated Resolution No. 11064, providing for guidelines on the
use of AI, among others, for digital election campaigns, with a particular emphasis on
prohibiting and punishing its misuse for disinformation and misinformation in connection with
the 2025 national and local elections.4

However, while this particular resolution by the election body is welcome, it remains a stopgap
measure nonetheless. The primary legislation in our legal system, the Data Privacy Act and
the Cybercrime Prevention Act, both enacted in 2012, were crafted long before the legislature
could define how AI and its related technologies could be used and misused in different ways.

The proposed measure aims to establish a long-term and forward-looking framework for the
regulation of AI and its related technologies, with a particular focus on preventing, through
legislative criminalization, the malicious and unethical use of AI, including the deliberate
creation and alteration of media, and the circulation and use thereof, to injure persons and
reputations, fabricate false realities, compromise safety and privacy, and ultimately erode the
public’s confidence in legitimate information and technology. It modernizes laws such as the
Revised Penal Code and the Cybercrime Prevention Act by expressly defining AI-enabled and
-related offenses, providing for increased penalties where AI and its related technologies are
used and misused to commit crimes and other violations. It also holds the creators of these
technologies accountable by mandating the implementation of reasonable safeguards.

This proposed legislation aims to balance the state’s interests in preserving the safety, privacy,
free speech, and general welfare of its people with the authority and effectiveness that the
law needs to not only respond to but also preempt the effects of the misuse of AI.

Through this measure, the misuse of AI may be prevented while ensuring that its potential is
harnessed for the benefit of the Filipinos and society.

In view of the foregoing, the early approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
     PENALIZING THE MALICIOUS CREATION, DISTRIBUTION, AND USE OF
  DIGITALLY MANIPULATED MEDIA THROUGH ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND
    OTHER HARMFUL APPLICATIONS THEREOF, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Safeguarding Against Fraud and
Exploitation by Artificial Intelligence (SAFE AI) Act.”

Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes both the transformative potential of
artificial intelligence and the profound risks associated with its malicious use. The creation and
spread of deceptive digital media undermines public trust, endangers personal dignity, and
threatens national security and democratic processes. Thus, it is hereby declared the policy
of the State to protect its citizens from the harms of deceptive AI-generated content. This
shall be achieved by criminalizing its creation, distribution, and use when intended to defraud,
deceive, or cause harm, while upholding the freedom of expression and fostering responsible
innovation.

Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. –

   a. “Artificial Intelligence (AI)” refers to a set of technologies that seek to simulate human
      traits such as knowledge, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, learning and
      planning, and, depending on the AI model, produce an output or decision;

   b. “Bots” refer to a software program or hardware system designed to perform
      automated, repetitive, pre-defined tasks on the internet often used on social media to
      simulate or imitate human user behavior;

   c. “Critical Infrastructure” refers to an asset or system, whether physical or virtual, so
      essential to the maintenance of vital societal functions or to the delivery of essential
      public services that the incapacity or destruction of such systems and assets would
      have a debilitating impact on national defense and security, national economy, public
      health or safety, there administration of justice, and other functions analogous thereto.
      It may include, but is not limited to, an asset or system affecting telecommunications,
      water and energy supply, emergency services, food security, fuel supply, banking and
      finance, transportation, radio and television, information systems and technology,
      chemical and nuclear sectors;

   d. “Deepfakes” refer to images, videos, or audio files created using artificial intelligence
      to fabricate realistic representations of people, places, events, or statements;
   e. “Digitally manipulated” or “synthetic media” refers to images, videos, or audio files
      created or altered using artificial intelligence technology to fabricate or intentionally
      re-contextualize realistic representations of people, events, or statements, making it
      appear that an action, statement, or event transpired differently from reality or did not
      transpire at all, including deepfakes and other forms of media manipulation or re-
      contextualization;

   f.   “Disinformation” refers to false or misleading content that is spread with an intention
        to deceive or secure economic or political gain and which may cause public harm;

   g. “Fake accounts” refer to social media profiles or internet profiles created using false,
      misleading, or inauthentic identities, or otherwise manipulated to deceive users into
      believing they are interacting with genuine individuals or entities, often with the intent
      to mislead, manipulate, or deceive the public;

   h. “Internet technology” refers to the interconnected hardware, software,
      communications protocol, applications, and services that collectively enable global data
      transmission processing and exchange;

   i.   “Malinformation” refers to information that stems from the truth but is often
        exaggerated or used out of context to mislead, manipulate, or cause harm;

   j.   “Misinformation” refers to false or misleading content shared without malicious intent
        although the effects can still be harmful, such as when the same stems from a reckless
        disregard for the truth or falsity of the content; and

   k. “Online platforms” refer to websites and applications that enable users to create,
      share, and exchange content online, engage in social networking, and interact with
      others in real-time or asynchronously, often involving user-generated content and
      facilitated through various forms of communication and media sharing.

Sec. 4. Artificial Intelligence Technology Offenses. – The following acts constitute the
offense of unlawful use and application of AI technology punishable under this Act:

   a. The intentional creation of synthetic media or alteration of any audio, visual, or
      audiovisual media using AI technology to produce a digitally manipulated or synthetic
      media that:

               (i) Falsely depicts an identifiable person engaging in an act or making a
               statement they did not actually engage in or make, or one that they did engage
               in or make but with a significantly altered meaning;

               (ii) Falsely represents an event to have occurred when in fact it did not, or
               misrepresents how an event actually occurred.

   b. The intentional distribution, publication, circulation, or dissemination to the public or a
      segment thereof, of digitally manipulated or synthetic media, such as deepfakes or
      other fraudulent AI-generated content, with knowledge of its falsity and with malicious
      intent, regardless of whether the distributor is the original creator;
   c. The unauthorized acquisition, use, misuse, transfer, possession, alteration, or deletion
      of identifying information belonging to another person using AI technology, without
      the knowledge and consent of the person concerned, for impersonation, deception,
      fraud, or other malicious purposes;

   d. The creation, deployment, or management of AI-powered bots or fake accounts
      intended to spread disinformation, misinformation, or malinformation, commit fraud,
      or otherwise engage in other unlawful activities as defined by this Act or other existing
      laws; and

   e. The use of AI technology to generate or systematically propagate disinformation,
      misinformation, or malinformation with the intention to incite hate or incite violence
      against a particular person, group or sector, create widespread fear and panic,
      endanger the public or compromise public safety, or undermine public order or public
      trust.

For purposes of this section, developers, providers, or distributors of software or applications
using AI technologies capable of generating synthetic media or altering or manipulating media
shall ensure that such tools contain a built-in mechanism to automatically embed conspicuous,
permanent, and non-removable watermarks or equivalent disclosures in any output generated
using such technology, clearly indicating that it was created, generated, or altered using AI.

Sec. 5. Liability under Other Laws. – All crimes defined and penalized by the Revised
Penal Code, as amended, and special laws, if committed by, through, and with the use of AI
technology shall be covered by the relevant provisions of this Act: Provided, That the penalty
to be imposed shall be one (1) degree higher than that provided for by the Revised Penal
Code, as amended, and special laws, as the case may be.

A prosecution under this Act shall be without prejudice to any liability for violation of any
provision of the Revised Penal Code, as amended, or other special laws.

Sec. 6. Enforcement and Supervision. – The National Telecommunications Commission
(NTC), in coordination with the Department of Information and Communications Technology,
the Department of Justice, and other relevant agencies, shall exercise primary enforcement
authority under this Act. For this purpose, the NTC is hereby empowered to:

   a. Issue immediate takedown or blocking orders directing the concerned internet service
      providers (ISPs), domain-name registries, content-delivery networks, social media
      platforms, mobile application stores, or any other digital intermediary to remove,
      disable access to, or render inaccessible within the Philippines, any and all content
      including but not limited to images, videos, audio files, or other synthetic media or
      altered or manipulated media — found to be in violation of this Act: Provided, That
      takedown or blocking orders shall be limited only to the specific illicit material and shall
      not extend to the entire website, platform, or application unless it is shown that such
      website, platform, or application is primarily used, or has repeatedly been used, to
      create, host, distribute, or facilitate content that is prohibited under this Act.

   b. Impose administrative sanctions including fines, suspension, or revocation of
      certificates, franchises, or licenses upon any digital intermediary that, without
      justifiable cause, fails to obey a lawful blocking or takedown order.
For purposes of this section, digital intermediaries shall implement the blocking or takedown
order within thirty-six (36) hours after formal notice is received: Provided, That if the content
is sexual in nature or constitutes non-consensual intimate content, the order shall be executed
within twelve (12) hours from receipt of such notice: Provided, further, That if the content
poses a danger to critical infrastructure or to national security, or in cases involving sexually
explicit content depicting minors, the order shall be implemented immediately upon receipt of
the notice.

The Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center, jointly with the National Bureau of
Investigation (NBI) Cybercrime Division and the Philippine National Police (PNP) Anti-
Cybercrime Group, shall have the following responsibilities:

   a. To investigate any violation under this Act;

   b. To determine and verify whether AI technology was used in the commission of any
      violation of this Act; and

   c. To conduct digital forensic investigations to preserve electronic evidence necessary for
      prosecution.

In the issuance and implementation of any and all orders under this provision, the State shall
be guided by the principle of protecting the general interest and welfare of the public. To
prevent ongoing or imminent harm, these orders shall be immediately executory, subject to
judicial review and without prejudice to lawful orders issued by a competent court.

Sec. 7. Penalties. — Any person found guilty of the unlawful acts defined in Section 4 of this
Act, where such acts are not already covered with higher penalties under the amendments to
the Revised Penal Code or any other special laws, shall be punished as follows:

   a. The acts defined under Section 4(a) and (b) shall be punishable by prision correccional
      or a fine ranging from One Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 100,000.00) to Five Hundred
      Thousand Pesos (Php 500,000.00), or both.

       If the digitally manipulated or synthetic media generated or distributed is of a sexual
       nature or constitutes non-consensual intimate content, the penalty shall be prision
       mayor or a fine ranging from Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 500,000.00) to One
       Million Pesos (Php 1,000,000.00), or both.

       If the victim is a minor, or if the digitally manipulated or synthetic media generated or
       distributed is used to facilitate child sexual abuse or child exploitation materials, the
       penalty shall be reclusion perpetua or a fine ranging from One Million Pesos (Php
       1,000,000.00) to Five Million Pesos (Php 5,000,000.00), or both.

   b. The acts defined under Section 4(c) shall be punishable by prision mayor or a fine
      ranging from Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 200,000.00) to One Million Pesos
      (Php 1,000,000.00), or both.

   c. The acts defined under Section 4(d) shall be punishable by prision correccional or a
      fine ranging from Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php 50,000.00) to Three hundred Thousand
      Pesos (Php 300,000.00), or both.
   d. The acts defined under Section 4(e) shall be punishable by prision mayor or a fine
      ranging from One Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 100,000.00) to One Million Pesos
      (Php 1,000,000.00), or both.

       If such disinformation, malinformation, or misinformation endangers critical
       infrastructure or national security, the penalty shall be reclusion temporal or a fine
       ranging from Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (Php 500,000.00) to Five Million Pesos
       (Php 5,000,000.00), or both.

       If the offender is a juridical person, the penalty shall be a fine of double the amount
       specified for the offense, and the officer or officers responsible for the offense shall be
       subject to the prescribed imprisonment. If the offender is an alien, he or she shall, in
       addition to the penalties prescribed, be deported immediately after serving the
       sentence.

Sec. 8. Exceptions. — The provisions of this Act shall not apply to:

   a. The creation, distribution, or use of digitally manipulated or synthetic media for
      legitimate purposes such as, but not limited to, marketing, advertising, parody, satire,
      commentary, artistic expression, academic research, education, or news reporting,
      provided that:

          i.   There is clear disclosure accompanying the content with a watermark clearly
               indicating that it has been created, generated, or modified using AI technology
               and that it does not represent the authentic image, speech, or conduct of the
               person depicted or the actual event portrayed. For purposes of this section, the
               disclosure must be placed in a conspicuous location to a reasonable viewer or
               listener; or
         ii.   In the case of parody or satire, the transformative nature of the work makes it
               apparent that it is not a factual representation.

   b. AI-generated content created with the informed consent of every individual whose
      likeness or voice is used and only for the purpose to which they have consented.

The burden of proving that the creation, distribution, or use falls under these exceptions lies
with the person using, commissioning, or distributing the AI-generated content.

Sec. 9. Liability for the Provision of Malicious or Unsafe AI Systems. – It shall be
unlawful for any developer or provider to make an AI system available to the public, whether
for commercial gain or otherwise, under any of the following circumstances:

   a. The AI system is primarily designed or explicitly marketed for the purpose of
      committing any of the unlawful acts defined in this Act, the Revised Penal Code, and
      other special penal laws; or

   b. The developer or provider knows, or is grossly negligent in not knowing, that the AI
      system will be predominantly used for such unlawful acts, and fails to implement
      reasonable safeguards to prevent such use.

For the purposes of this section, “reasonable safeguards” include, but are not limited to, user
identity verification, content moderation mechanisms, digital watermarking of AI-generated
content, and maintaining effective systems for reporting abuse.
Any developer or provider of AI systems who fails to implement reasonable safeguards as
defined in this Section shall be subject to administrative penalties, based on the gravity and
repetition of the offense:

   a. For failures that pose only minimal harm, result in no actual damage, or pertain
      primarily to procedural deficiencies that do not materially impair the safeguard’s
      function –

           1. The first offense shall be punished by censure and a fine of Fifty Thousand
              Pesos (Php 50,000.00).
           2. The second offense shall be punished by a fine of One Hundred Fifty Thousand
              Pesos (Php 100,000.00).
           3. The third offense shall be punished by the temporary suspension of the permit
              for the specific AI system for a period not exceeding six (6) months.

   b. For failures that create a significant potential public harm, financial loss, or involve
      gross negligence in addressing a known or foreseeable vulnerability –

           1. The first offense shall be punished by a fine of Two Hundred Thousand Pesos
              (Php 200,000.00) and the temporary suspension of the permit for the specific
              AI system for up to six (6) months.

           2. The second offense shall be punished by a fine of Five Hundred Thousand
              Pesos (Php 500,000.00) and the temporary suspension of the primary license
              to conduct business for a period of six (6) months to one (1) year.

           3. The third offense shall be punished by the permanent revocation of the license,
              registration, or permit to conduct business.

   c. Failures that create a clear and present danger to public order, national security, or
      undermine critical infrastructure shall be punished by a fine of not less than One Million
      Pesos (Php 1,000,000.00) and the permanent revocation of the license, registration,
      or permit to conduct business.

The imposition of these administrative penalties shall be without prejudice to any separate
civil or criminal liability that may arise from the harm caused by the content generated by the
AI system they developed or provided.

Sec. 10. Liability of Online Platforms. — Online platforms shall not be held liable for the
creation or dissemination of their users of content that is violative of this Act, provided that
upon receipt of:

   a. A valid complaint from an end-user, the platform conducts a reasonable and prompt
      assessment to determine whether the reported content appears to violate the
      provisions of this Act, and, if warranted, takes expeditious action to remove or disable
      access to the material; or

   b. A lawful order from the NTC or of a competent court, the platform shall immediately
      remove or disable access to the subject content within the period prescribed in such
      order.
Failure to act within a reasonable period after due notice, or to comply with a lawful order
without justifiable cause, shall subject the platform to liability, in addition to any applicable
administrative sanctions that may be imposed.

Sec. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Justice, the Secretary of Information and
Communications Technology, and the Commissioner of the National Telecommunications
Commission, in consultation with relevant stakeholders from academe, industry, and civil
society, shall jointly promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

Sec. 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.

Sec. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, or issuances contrary to
or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

Sec. 14. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
