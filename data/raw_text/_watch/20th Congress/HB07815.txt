                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

          In these changing times, marked by increasingly competitive economic conditions
across the ASEAN region, rapid and enormous flows of information driven by technological
advancements, digitalization, and the proliferation of Artificial Intelligence (AI), as well as the
heightened awareness of environmental and climate resilience, there is a compelling
necessity for swift and flexible reforms to adapt effectively to the evolving global investment
landscape.
          The Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA), established pursuant to Republic Act
No. 7916, as amended, was created with the enactment of the PEZA Law on 25 February
1995. Under its Charter, PEZA is mandated to oversee the establishment, development,
management, and promotion of special economic zones; generate employment opportunities;
enhance export growth; and spur balanced countryside development.
          Being the pioneer in ecozone development and huge contributor to the country’s
economy, as of year 2024 data from the Philippine Statistics Authority and the Bangko Sentral
ng Pilipinas (BSP), PEZA accounts for 12.89% of the country’s Gross Domestic Product;
54.03% of the country’s Export Commodities and Goods; and 36.88% of service exports.
          As of December 2025, PEZA supervises 436 public and privately-developed economic
zones operating nationwide proclaimed under its mandate, consisting of 309 IT Parks and
Centers; 83 Manufacturing Economic Zones; 24 Agro-Industrial Parks; 17 Tourism Parks; and
3 Medical Tourism Parks/Centers. These economic zones are hosts to the 4,437 multinational
and global-brand locator-companies and projects.
          For year 2025, PEZA posted record-breaking investment approvals at Php 260.89
Billion investments across manufacturing, IT-BPM, logistics, utilities, facilities, domestic
market enterprises, tourism, and ecozone development, a 21.91% growth from its January-
December 2024 approvals.
          Collectively, these projects generated 78,741 direct jobs for Filipinos, reflecting an
8.74% increase compared to 2024, and generated up to US$ 11.522 Billion in export
revenues, marking a significant 145.73% increase versus the same period in 2024. Notably,
41 big-ticket projects amounting to Php 214,603 billion were approved last year, underscoring
sustained investor confidence, the competitiveness of PEZA-registered enterprises, and the
strong global demand for Philippine-made products.
          Accordingly, PEZA has consistently served as a key driver in generating diverse
economic activities. Through its proactive investment promotion strategies, PEZA has
attracted substantial foreign and domestic investments, created quality employment, boosted
exports, and accelerated regional development. Over the years, PEZA has made significant
contributions to the national coffers through substantial remittances and has been consistently
recognized for its vital role in supporting national socio-economic initiatives while maintaining
high standards of governance.
         In 2026, PEZA commenced the year with a solid investment momentum, approving 18
new projects from 13 locators with total investments amounting to Php 12.86 billion. These
initiatives are expected to generate US $59.74 million in exports and will create 1,005
employment opportunities, further reinforcing the Philippines’ strategic position in regional and
global value chains.
       With these triumphs, PEZA is now more inspired to break traditions and rise to the
challenge and be one with the Administration to make the Philippines the premiere investor’s
choice. This is where our organic law, the PEZA Law, must come to the fore to support and
provide the legal bases and authority for PEZA to fully attain its new thrusts and strategies
under the Bagong Pilipinas.
        While the nation looks toward the future, the Philippine economy faces both new
challenges and emerging opportunities. The growing importance of digital industries, green
technologies, sustainable manufacturing, and environmental and climate adaptation
necessitates a revitalized economic zone framework that is responsive to these evolving
sectors. Moreover, the global trend toward supply chain diversification and the increasing
demand for climate-resilient infrastructure underscore the need to reassess and strengthen
the governance and strategic priorities of the PEZA.
        With the PEZA Law now in its 30th year, the time is ripe for meaningful reforms.
Amending the law to align with today’s economic landscape will position the country to attract
higher-value investments, generate skilled and better-paying jobs, and expand coverage to
emerging industries. This includes green technologies, innovation-driven enterprises,
sustainable sectors, and digital and hybrid operations, ensuring alignment with national
development goals and global competitiveness standards.
    This proposed measure seeks to:
    1. Enhance PEZA’s institutional and organizational capacity by expanding and
        strengthening its powers and functions to ensure greater and effective responsiveness
        to the dynamic requirements of existing and potential investors.
    2. Encourage the development of economic zones that are sustainable, climate-resilient,
        environmentally responsible, and inclusive, ensuring balanced growth across all
        regions.;
    3. Streamline procedures through digitalization and enhanced ease of doing business
        mechanisms in order to reduce bureaucratic inefficiencies, simplify and expedite the
        processing of permits and licenses, and rationalize ownership restrictions to
        encourage greater investment participation;
    4. Align investment incentives with the provisions of the newly enacted Corporate
        Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE MORE) Act while ensuring
        fiscal responsibility; and
    5. Enhance coordination among government agencies including Local Government Units
        (LGU) to ensure efficient and effective service delivery to locators and investors and
        encourage regional economic growth.
    Ultimately, the Philippines will strengthen its competitiveness in regional and global
markets and secure its standing as a leading investment destination in the ASEAN region,
guided by the Bagong Pilipinas vision — a comprehensive, inclusive framework for economic
and social transformation that aims to uplift every Filipino and unlock the nation’s full potential.
    In view of the foregoing, immediate approval of this measure is earnestly requested.

    translate into practical realities the following State policies and mandates in the

           (A) “THE STATE SHALL PROTECT AND ADVANCE THE RIGHT OF THE
           PEOPLE TO A BALANCED AND HEALTHFUL ECOLOGY IN ACCORD
           WITH THE RHYTHM AND HARMONY OF NATURE.” (SEC. 16, ART. II)

           (B) "The State recognizes the indispensable role of the private sector,
           encourages private enterprise, and provides incentives to needed
           investments." (Sec. 20, Art II)

           OVER FOREIGN INVESTMENTS WITHIN ITS NATIONAL JURISDICTION

           RECIPROCITY.” (SEC. 13, ART. XII)

     legitimate and productive foreign investments.”

SECTION 25. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The Department of Trade and
Industry, the Department of Economy, Planning and Development, and the Philippine
Economic Zone Authority shall formulate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act
within one hundred twenty (120) days after its effectivity. Such rules and regulations shall
take effect fifteen (15) days after their publication once in a newspaper of general circulation
in the Philippines.

SECTION 26. Transitory Provisions and Non-Impairment of Contracts. - Prior to the
effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations of this Act, the provisions of Republic Act
No. 7916, as amended, shall remain in force. All existing contracts, permits and licenses duly
granted by the PEZA prior to effectivity of this Act shall remain valid and unimpaired, and shall
continue to be recognized by all parties and the government.

SECTION 27. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulation,
memoranda of agreement, issuances, or any parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 28. Separability Clause. – Any portion or provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining parts of the provisions hereof not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SECTION 29. Effectivity- This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
