                               AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A REGULATORY FRAMEWORK FOR DIGITAL PLATFORMS,
PROMOTING RESPONSIBLE DIGITAL ENGAGEMENT AND ONLINE SAFETY FOR THE
PROTECTION OF CHILDREN FROM ONLINE HARMS

                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

The rapid advancement of information and communications technology has fundamentally reshaped the
manner by which Filipinos communicate, access information, pursue education, conduct business, and
participate in civic life. Digital platforms, including social networking services, messaging applications,
online gaming platforms, content-sharing services, livestreaming platforms, and other interactive digital
technologies have become indispensable tools in modern society. While these technologies have
significantly contributed to economic growth, innovation, and digital inclusion, they have likewise
created unprecedented challenges, particularly with respect to the protection of children in the online
environment.

The increasing accessibility of digital platforms has correspondingly heightened the exposure of children
and adolescents to online harms such as cyberbullying, online grooming, identity theft, privacy
violations, harmful and age-inappropriate content, deceptive digital practices, excessive screen time, and
other forms of digital exploitation. These risks have become more complex as digital platforms continue
to evolve, underscoring the need for a comprehensive regulatory framework that balances technological
innovation with the State's constitutional duty to protect the welfare of its citizens.

Empirical evidence demonstrates the urgency of legislative intervention. In Sexual Exploitation and
Abuse of Children Online in the Philippines: A Review of Online News and Articles, Hernandez, Lacsina,
Ylade, Aldaba, Lam, Estacio, and Lopez documented the alarming prevalence of Online Sexual
Exploitation and Abuse of Children (OSEAC) in the Philippines. The study identified recurring incidents
involving livestreamed child sexual abuse, child sexual abuse materials, and sextortion, with victims
ranging from infancy to adolescence. It further observed that the widespread availability of digital
technologies, coupled with socioeconomic vulnerabilities and inadequate online safeguards, has
facilitated the proliferation of these offenses, thereby highlighting the necessity for stronger preventive
measures and institutional responses.

Complementing these findings, a study published in the Philippine Social Science Journal emphasizes
that children's increasing engagement with digital platforms extends their exposure beyond criminal
exploitation to a broader spectrum of psychosocial and developmental risks. The study identifies
cyberbullying, problematic digital use, diminished parental supervision, privacy and data security
concerns, and harmful online interactions as emerging issues that require coordinated interventions
involving government, families, educational institutions, and digital platform operators. It likewise
underscores that digital literacy, platform accountability, and child-centered online safety mechanisms
are essential components of an effective child protection framework in the digital age.

Although the Philippines has enacted significant legislation such as Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise
known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012; Republic Act No. 10175, or the Cybercrime Prevention Act of
2012; and Republic Act No. 11930, or the Anti-Online Sexual Abuse or Exploitation of Children and
Anti-Child Sexual Abuse or Exploitation Materials Act, these laws principally address specific unlawful
acts or regulate particular aspects of digital governance. At present, there remains no comprehensive
statutory framework that establishes uniform safety standards, preventive obligations, and accountability
mechanisms applicable to digital platform operators for the protection of children.

This proposed measure seeks to address this legislative gap by establishing a comprehensive regulatory
framework governing digital platforms operating within the Philippines. It mandates the adoption of age-
appropriate safeguards, privacy-preserving age verification systems, digital well-being features, parental
supervision mechanisms, transparent platform policies, and effective reporting and redress systems. It
likewise institutionalizes inter-agency coordination among the Department of Information and
Communications Technology, the National Telecommunications Commission, the National Privacy
Commission, the Department of Health, the Department of Education, the Department of Social
Welfare and Development, the Presidential Communications Office, the Council for the Welfare of
Children, and other concerned agencies to ensure a coherent and whole-of-government approach to
digital safety and child protection.

Ultimately, this measure affirms the State's commitment to uphold the constitutional rights and welfare
of children while fostering a safe, secure, and trustworthy digital ecosystem that promotes responsible
innovation, protection, and ensures that technological advancement remains consistent with the public
interest.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                                ARTICLE I
                                         PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS

 1       SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Digital Platform Safety and Protection
 2   Act."

 3        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote a safe, secure,
 4   inclusive, and rights-based digital environment that protects users, particularly children and adolescents,
 5   from online harms while upholding the constitutional rights to freedom of expression, privacy, due process,
 6   access to information, and the best interests of the child. Towards this end, the State shall:

 7       (a) promote the responsible, transparent, and accountable operation of digital platforms;
 8       (b) safeguard children against online exploitation, abuse, cyberbullying, harmful and age-
 9           inappropriate content, deceptive digital practices, excessive screen exposure, and other
10           foreseeable online risks;
11       (c) encourage technological innovation while ensuring that digital development remains consistent
12           with public welfare, child protection, and human rights;
13       (d) strengthen the protection of personal information consistent with Republic Act No. 10173 or the
14           Data Privacy Act of 2012;
15       (e) promote digital literacy, digital citizenship, media and information literacy, cyber ethics, and
16           healthy digital engagement;
17       (f) empower parents, guardians, educators, and communities to guide children in the responsible use
18           of digital technologies;
19       (g) establish age-appropriate safeguards governing children's access to digital platforms and
20           electronic gadgets;
21       (h) strengthen cooperation among national government agencies, local government units,
22           educational institutions, digital platform operators, parents, civil society organizations,
23           professional associations, and community stakeholders in advancing child online safety; and
24      (i) adopt a whole-of-government and whole-of-community approach in protecting children from
25          online harms while promoting responsible participation in the digital environment.

26        SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all covered digital platforms that make their
27   services available to users located within the Philippines, regardless of the place of incorporation or
28   principal place of business of the platform operator, insofar as such services are directed to, accessed by,
29   or substantially affect users within Philippine jurisdiction.

30      This Act shall likewise establish age-based regulatory measures governing the use of digital platforms
31   and electronic gadgets from birth until eighteen (18) years of age, including age-appropriate access
32   restrictions, digital well-being standards, privacy protections, parental support mechanisms, educational
33   interventions, and platform obligations corresponding to the developmental needs and evolving capacities
34   of children and adolescents.

35      Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish rights and protections guaranteed under the
36   Constitution and existing laws relating to privacy, freedom of expression, child protection, cybersecurity,
37   education, consumer protection, and data privacy.

38        SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be understood as
39   follows:

40      (a) Child refers to any person below eighteen (18) years of age, consistent with existing child
41          protection laws, and shall likewise include persons eighteen (18) years of age or older who,
42          because of physical or mental disability or condition, are unable to fully protect themselves from
43          abuse, neglect, cruelty, exploitation, or discrimination.
44      (b) Digital Platform refers to any internet-based service, website, application, online system, or
45          digital technology that enables users to create, access, communicate, distribute, exchange,
46          discover, recommend, monetize, or interact with digital content, goods, services, or information,
47          including social media platforms, messaging services, online gaming platforms, content-sharing
48          platforms, livestreaming services, search engines, online marketplaces, and similar interactive
49          digital services.
50      (c) Digital Platform Operator refers to any natural or juridical person that owns, develops, controls,
51          manages, operates, or provides a digital platform or exercises substantial control over its design,
52          operation, policies, algorithms, or data processing activities.
53      (d) Social Media Platform refers to any internet-based application, website, or digital service that
54          enables users to create profiles, communicate, establish social networks, publish or share user-
55          generated content, or interact with other users through recommendation systems, personalized
56          feeds, messaging, livestreaming, commenting, or similar interactive features.
57      (e) High-Risk Digital Platform refers to a digital platform whose design, functions, services,
58          algorithms, or business practices present a reasonably foreseeable risk of exposing children to
59          harmful or age-inappropriate content, online exploitation, cyberbullying, manipulative or
60          addictive platform features, privacy risks, excessive screen use, or other significant online harms,
61          as may be determined by the Department of Information and Communications Technology
62          (DICT) through the Implementing Rules and Regulations.
63      (f) Age Assurance Measures refer to privacy-preserving policies, technologies, systems, or
64          procedures used to verify, estimate, or otherwise establish the age or age range of users while
65          ensuring proportionality, transparency, accountability, data minimization, and compliance with
66          applicable data privacy laws.
67      (g) Algorithmic Safety refers to any automated computational process, including those utilizing
68          artificial intelligence, machine learning, predictive analytics, ranking, personalization, or similar
69          technologies, that selects, recommends, prioritizes, amplifies, suppresses, or organizes digital
 70          content, advertisements, products, services, or user accounts based on user behavior, preferences,
 71          characteristics, or other data.
 72      (h) Digital Well-Being refers to the healthy, balanced, safe, and responsible use of digital
 73          technologies that promotes the physical, mental, emotional, psychological, cognitive,
 74          educational, and social well-being of children and other users while minimizing risks associated
 75          with excessive or harmful digital engagement.
 76      (i) Electronic Gadget refers to any internet-enabled or electronic device capable of accessing digital
 77          platforms or digital content, including smartphones, mobile phones, tablets, laptops, desktop
 78          computers, gaming consoles, wearable technologies, smart televisions, virtual or augmented
 79          reality devices, and similar electronic devices.
 80      (j) Harmful Online Content refers to digital content, activities, or communications that may
 81          reasonably be expected to expose children to exploitation, abuse, cyberbullying, online
 82          grooming, child sexual abuse or exploitation materials, self-harm or suicide promotion, violent
 83          or sexually explicit material, gambling-related content, fraudulent schemes, hate speech
 84          prohibited by law, or other content that poses a significant risk to their health, safety,
 85          development, or well-being.
 86      (k) Parent or Guardian refers to the biological parent, adoptive parent, legal guardian, foster parent,
 87          or any person exercising lawful parental authority or substitute parental care over a child pursuant
 88          to existing laws.
 89      (l) Community Stakeholders refer to Local Government Units (LGUs), Local Councils for the
 90          Protection of Children (LCPCs), Barangay Councils for the Protection of Children (BCPCs),
 91          Barangay Health Workers (BHWs), Barangay Child Development Workers (BCDWs),
 92          educational institutions, civil society organizations, youth organizations, professional
 93          associations, faith-based organizations, healthcare professionals, parents' organizations, and
 94          other public or private entities participating in child protection, digital literacy, and digital
 95          wellness initiatives.
 96      (m) Digital Literacy refers to the knowledge, skills, competencies, and ethical values necessary to
 97          safely, responsibly, critically, and effectively access, evaluate, create, communicate, and
 98          participate in digital environments, including media and information literacy, cybersecurity
 99          awareness, privacy protection, responsible digital citizenship, and the appropriate use of artificial
100          intelligence and emerging technologies.

101                                           ARTICLE II
102                            GENERAL OBLIGATIONS OF DIGITAL PLATFORMS

103      SEC. 5. General Duties of Digital Platforms. - All digital platform operators providing services to
104   users within the Philippines shall:

105      (a) establish and implement age-based access regulations consistent with the developmental needs
106          of children;
107      (b) adopt privacy-preserving age assurance mechanisms in accordance with this Act;
108      (c) ensure that children's accounts are configured with the highest available privacy and safety
109          settings by default;
110      (d) obtain verifiable parental or guardian consent whenever required under applicable laws;
111      (e) establish accessible, transparent, and child-friendly reporting and complaint mechanisms for
112          cyberbullying, online exploitation, scams, harmful content, privacy violations, and other online
113          harms;
114      (f) provide digital well-being tools, including parental controls, screen-time monitoring, break
115          reminders, usage dashboards, content filtering, and other protective features;
116      (g) maintain transparent community standards, content moderation policies, advertising practices,
117          and data processing policies written in language understandable to users;
118      (h) conduct regular child impact and online safety risk assessments;
119      (i) cooperate with competent government authorities in the enforcement of this Act; and
120      (j) comply with all applicable laws relating to child protection, cybersecurity, consumer protection,
121          digital safety, and data privacy.

122   SEC. 6. Age-Appropriate Digital Platform Regulation. - Digital platform operators shall implement age-
123   based safeguards that correspond to the developmental needs of children. The following age-based
124   standards shall serve as the minimum national guide:

125      (a) Ages 0–2 years. Screen exposure shall be prohibited, except for medically necessary purposes or
126          supervised voice or video communication with immediate family members, recognizing that this
127          developmental stage requires direct human interaction for healthy physical, cognitive, emotional,
128          and social development.
129      (b) Ages 2–5 years. Screen use shall be limited to not more than one (1) hour per day of high-quality,
130          age-appropriate educational content under the active supervision of a parent, legal guardian, or
131          caregiver.
132      (c) Ages 6–12 years. Screen use shall be balanced with physical activity, adequate sleep, face-to-
133          face social interaction, and learning, and shall not exceed two (2) hours per day for non-school-
134          related purposes, subject to parental guidance and supervision.
135      (d) Ages 13–15 years. Digital platform use shall promote digital literacy, responsible online
136          behavior, privacy awareness, cyber safety, balanced screen time, and parental guidance. Platform
137          operators shall provide enhanced privacy protections, age-appropriate content moderation, and
138          digital well-being features.
139      (e) Ages 16–17 years. Digital platform use shall encourage responsible digital citizenship and
140          supervised independence. High-risk digital platforms shall provide youth safety features,
141          enhanced privacy settings, content moderation, digital well-being tools, and mechanisms that
142          discourage excessive or harmful online engagement while recognizing the evolving capacities of
143          older adolescents.

144      The Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Department of Information and
145   Communications Technology (DICT), Department of Education (DepEd), Early Childhood Care and
146   Development (ECCD) Council, and the Council for the Welfare of Children (CWC), shall periodically
147   review these standards based on prevailing scientific evidence and child development research.

148       SEC. 7. Age-Assurance and Verification. All high-risk digital platform operators shall implement
149   privacy-preserving age assurance systems to ensure compliance with the age-based access requirements
150   under this Act. Age assurance systems shall:

151      (a) utilize reasonable, proportionate, and reliable methods to determine the age of users;
152      (b) provide multiple verification options so that users are not compelled to submit government-issued
153          identification unless strictly necessary;
154      (c) minimize the collection, processing, retention, and disclosure of personal data in accordance with
155          Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012;
156      (d) ensure that personal information collected for age assurance shall be used solely for age
157          verification and shall not be processed for advertising, profiling, behavioral targeting, or any
158          unrelated commercial purpose;
159      (e) permanently delete or anonymize personal information immediately upon completion of the
160          verification process, unless a longer retention period is expressly authorized by law; and
161       (f) undergo periodic security and privacy audits as may be required by the Department of
162           Information and Communications Technology (DICT) and the National Privacy Commission
163           (NPC).

164      The DICT, in coordination with the NPC, shall periodically review age assurance technologies to
165   ensure that such systems remain effective, proportionate, technologically current, and respectful of
166   children's rights and privacy.

167       SEC. 8. Duty of Care of Digital Platforms. – Every covered digital platform accessible within the
168   Philippines owes children a statutory duty of care to identify, prevent, minimize, and mitigate reasonably
169   foreseeable risks arising from the design, operation, business practices, and use of its services. In fulfilling
170   this duty, digital platforms shall adopt reasonable, proportionate, and effective measures to safeguard the
171   rights, safety, privacy, health, and well-being of children and adolescents, consistent with the obligations
172   provided under this Act.

173     SEC. 9. Obligations Under the Duty of Care. – The duty of care shall be proportionate to the nature,
174   scale, and level of risk presented by the platform and shall include, at a minimum, the following
175   obligations:

176       (a) Risk Assessment. – Conduct, document, and regularly update comprehensive child safety risk
177           assessments identifying potential harms arising from platform design, algorithms, content
178           moderation systems, business models, and user interactions. Such assessments shall be reviewed
179           at least annually and submitted to the DICT..
180       (b) Risk Mitigation. – Implement appropriate and effective measures to address identified risks,
181           including:
182            (i)       Algorithmic Safety. - Designing or adjusting recommendation and ranking systems to
183                      minimize the amplification or promotion of harmful, exploitative, manipulative,
184                      violent, sexually explicit, self-harm, gambling-related, age-inappropriate, or
185                      disinformation-related content directed at children and adolescents;
186            (ii)      Content Moderation. - Maintaining effective, age-appropriate content moderation
187                      systems capable of detecting, reviewing, restricting, or removing harmful online
188                      content and conduct in accordance with this Act;
189            (iii)     Privacy and Safety by Default. - Ensuring that accounts belonging to users below
190                      eighteen (18) years of age are configured with the highest available privacy and safety
191                      settings by default, including private profiles, restricted messaging, limited content
192                      sharing, and similar protective measures;
193            (iv)      Restrictions on Addictive Design Features. - Limiting, disabling, or otherwise
194                      mitigating platform features that exploit the psychological vulnerabilities of children,
195                      including infinite scrolling, autoplay, manipulative notifications, variable reward
196                      mechanisms, lock-in loops, and similar engagement-maximizing designs;
197            (v)       Reporting and Redress Mechanisms. - Providing accessible, child-friendly, and
198                      effective mechanisms through which users may report harmful content, harassment,
199                      exploitation, abuse, or other violations, and ensuring timely review, response, and
200                      appropriate remedial action;
201            (vi)      Transparency. - Publishing clear, accessible, and age-appropriate Terms of Service,
202                      Community Standards, and Privacy Notices that explain platform rules, moderation
203                      practices, and data processing affecting children and adolescents;
204            (vii)     Human Oversight. - Ensuring that automated content moderation and decision-
205                      making systems are subject to meaningful human review, particularly where decisions
206                      significantly affect the rights, safety, or access of children and adolescents; and
207           (viii)    Stakeholder Consultation. - Regularly consulting child development specialists,
208                     educators, healthcare professionals, parents, youth representatives, child rights
209                     organizations, and other relevant civil society organizations in the development,
210                     implementation, and periodic evaluation of child safety policies, risk mitigation
211                     measures, and digital well-being initiatives.

212                                   ARTICLE III
213            DIGITAL WELL-BEING, GADGET REGULATION, AND CHILD SUPPORT

214     SEC. 10. Regulation of Gadget Use in Home and Community Settings. - The regulation of electronic
215   gadget use by children outside school premises shall primarily recognize the constitutional rights and
216   responsibilities of parents and legal guardians while promoting healthy child development through
217   education, voluntary participation, and community support. Implementation shall be guided by the
218   following framework:

219      (a) Children Five (5) Years Old and Below. - For children five (5) years of age and below, including
220          those not yet enrolled in school or outside school hours, guidance on age-appropriate gadget use
221          shall be undertaken by the ECCD Council, in coordination with the DOH the CWC, and LGUs
222          through their:
223
224               ●    Barangay Health Workers (BHWs);
225               ●    Barangay Child Development Workers (BCDWs);
226               ●    Child Development Centers; and
227               ●    LCPCs.

228      Such guidance shall focus on healthy child development, responsive caregiving, parent education,
229   developmental milestones, and responsible screen use.

230      (b) Children Six (6) Years Old to Eighteen (18) Years Old. - For children six (6) years of age up to
231          eighteen (18) years of age outside school hours, implementation shall be coordinated by the
232          Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) through the LGUs, in coordination
233          with:
234
235           (i)      DOH;
236           (ii)     DSWD;
237           (iii)    DICT;
238           (iv)     CWC;
239           (v)      LCPCs;
240           (vi)     BCPCs;
241           (vii)    BHWs;
242           (viii)   BCDWs; and
243           (ix)     Accredited civil society organizations.

244      Programs shall promote balanced gadget use, digital wellness, mental health, online safety,
245   cyberbullying prevention, responsible gaming, and healthy family digital practices.

246      (c) Home-Based Guidance. Parents, legal guardians, and caregivers shall remain the primary
247          decision-makers regarding children's gadget use within the home.
248      (d) Government agencies shall provide educational guidance and support without imposing intrusive
249          monitoring or compulsory household inspections.
250      SEC. 11 Regulation of Gadget Use in Educational Institutions. - All public and private basic
251   education institutions shall establish age-appropriate policies governing the possession and use of
252   electronic gadgets within school premises and during school-authorized activities.

253       The Department of Education (DepEd), in consultation with the DICT, DOH, ECCD Council, CWC,
254   teachers' organizations, Parents–Teachers Associations (PTAs), School Governing Councils (SGCs),
255   school administrators, and child development experts, shall prescribe uniform minimum standards
256   covering:

257      (a) permitted classroom use;
258      (b) prohibited use during instructional hours;
259      (c) digital well-being policies;
260      (d) responsible use of educational technologies;
261      (e) procedures for temporary confiscation, safekeeping, documentation, and return of electronic
262          gadgets when school rules are violated;
263      (f) immediate notification of parents or guardians following confiscation; and
264      (g) protection against loss, theft, or damage while gadgets remain under school custody.

265      Nothing in this Act shall prohibit the use of school-owned digital equipment utilized for officially
266   approved instructional, research, assessment, or assistive learning purposes under the direct supervision
267   of teachers.

268      SEC. 12. Permitted Use of Electronic Gadgets. Notwithstanding the age-based recommendations
269   under this Act, children may use electronic gadgets under the following circumstances:

270      (a) participation in supervised educational activities approved by schools or educational institutions;
271      (b) use of assistive technologies for children with disabilities, developmental conditions, or special
272          educational needs;
273      (c) telemedicine, health monitoring, psychological counseling, or medically necessary healthcare
274          services;
275      (d) emergency communication;
276      (e) supervised voice or video communication with parents, guardians, or immediate family members;
277      (f) access to government services, emergency information, or public safety announcements;
278      (g) officially sanctioned extracurricular, academic, cultural, or civic activities; and
279      (h) such other circumstances as may be determined by the implementing agencies, provided that the
280          use remains consistent with the developmental needs and best interests of the child.

281       SEC. 13. Safeguards for Privacy, Family Autonomy, and Home Sanctity. - The implementation of
282   this Act shall at all times respect the constitutional rights of parents and families, including the privacy
283   of the home and the primary right and duty of parents in the upbringing and development of their children.
284   Accordingly:

285      (a) all home-based digital wellness activities shall be voluntary, educational, and supportive in
286          nature;
287      (b) no provision of this Act shall authorize warrantless entry into private residences or intrusive
288          surveillance of household digital practices;
289      (c) participation in Digital Wellness Pledges, family screen-time programs, parenting seminars, or
290          home-based digital wellness initiatives shall be entirely voluntary;
291      (d) all personal information collected under this Act shall be treated as confidential and processed in
292          accordance with Republic Act No. 10173; and
293      (e) all government interventions under this Act shall prioritize parental empowerment, child
294          development, education, counseling, and voluntary compliance rather than punitive enforcement
295          against parents or caregivers.

303       (a) age-appropriate instruction on responsible internet use, fact-checking, digital citizenship, and

305       (b) awareness of cyberbullying, online grooming, online sexual exploitation, scams, misinformation,

307       (c) practical instruction on privacy protection, cybersecurity, digital well-being, and healthy screen-
308           time management;
309       (d) responsible and ethical use of artificial intelligence and emerging digital technologies;
310       (e) training materials for teachers, school administrators, guidance counselors, parents, and
311           caregivers; and
312       (f) periodic review of instructional materials to ensure consistency with evolving technologies and
313           evidence-based child development standards.

314                                           ARTICLE V
315                                  ADMINISTRATION ENFORCEMENT

316       SEC. 15. Lead Implementing Agency and National Coordination. - The DICT shall serve as the
317   lead implementing agency of this Act. It shall formulate national policies, establish technical and
318   operational standards, monitor compliance, coordinate inter-agency implementation, conduct compliance
319   audits, issue advisory guidelines, and oversee the effective implementation and enforcement of this Act.

320       To ensure a coordinated and whole-of-government approach, the following agencies shall perform
321   the functions consistent with their respective mandates:

322      (a) DICT - Serve as the lead agency responsible for policy formulation, implementation, monitoring,
323          accreditation of age-assurance systems, compliance audits, digital platform regulation, public
324          awareness campaigns, capacity-building programs, and overall enforcement coordination.
325      (b) DOH - Conduct research and issue evidence-based recommendations on the physical, cognitive,
326          emotional, psychosocial, and developmental effects of children's use of digital platforms and
327          electronic gadgets. The DOH shall formulate public health guidelines on healthy screen use,
328          digital well-being, and child development.
329      (c) DepEd - Integrate digital literacy, cyber ethics, online safety, responsible digital citizenship, and
330          digital well-being into the basic education curriculum. DepEd shall likewise issue policies
331          governing the responsible use of electronic gadgets in schools, provide teacher training, and
332          coordinate with parents, PTAs, and school administrators in implementing this Act.
333      (d) DSWD - Develop family-oriented interventions, parental education programs, counseling
334          services, and community-based child protection initiatives. The DSWD shall assist children and
335          families affected by online harms and coordinate social welfare interventions with LGUs.
336      (e) DILG - Supervise and monitor the implementation of this Act by LGUs strengthen local child
337          protection mechanisms, and ensure that LGUs establish community-based digital wellness and
338          online safety programs.
339      (f) ECCD Council - Lead the implementation of age-appropriate digital wellness programs for
340          children from birth to five (5) years of age by developing parenting resources, early childhood
341          interventions, and guidance promoting healthy child development and responsible screen
342          exposure.
343      (g) CWC - Ensure that all policies and programs implemented under this Act remain consistent with
344          the best interests of the child and the Philippine Child Protection Framework. The CWC shall
345          coordinate child rights advocacy, stakeholder engagement, and policy monitoring.
346      (h) NTC - Assist in monitoring digital platform operators and telecommunications entities, ensure
347          compliance with lawful directives issued pursuant to this Act, and coordinate with the DICT in
348          enforcing regulations involving electronic communications services.
349      (i) NPC. - Ensure compliance with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the Data Privacy
350          Act of 2012, particularly in relation to age-assurance systems, processing of children's personal
351          data, and privacy-preserving technologies.
352      (j) CHED - Promote digital literacy, online safety, cybersecurity awareness, and responsible digital
353          citizenship programs in higher education institutions, including teacher education institutions.
354      (k) Presidential Communications Office (PCO). - Lead nationwide information and advocacy
355          campaigns to promote public awareness on responsible digital engagement, child online
356          protection, digital wellness, and the objectives of this Act.

357      The DICT shall establish an Inter-Agency Committee on Digital Platform Safety and Child Online
358   Protection, composed of representatives from the agencies identified in this Section and such other
359   agencies as may be necessary. The Committee shall coordinate implementation, facilitate information
360   sharing, recommend policy improvements, monitor implementation, and submit an annual report to
361   Congress on the implementation and effectiveness of this Act.

362       SEC. 16. Local Implementation Framework. - The implementation of this Act at the local level
363   shall be undertaken by the LGUs through a coordinated and community-based approach involving the
364   LCPCs, BCPCs, Local School Boards, City or Municipal Social Welfare and Development Offices, Local
365   Health Offices, BHs), BCDWs, educational institutions, Parents–Teachers Associations (PTAs), SGCs,
366   civil society organizations, professional associations, and other community stakeholders.

367      The LCPC shall serve as the principal coordinating body for the local implementation of this Act at
368   the barangay, municipal, city and provincial levels and shall ensure the integration of child online
369   protection, digital wellness, and responsible technology use into existing local child protection programs.
370   For this purpose:

371      (a) LGUs shall allocate appropriate financial and material resources for community-based digital
372          literacy campaigns, and coordinate with national government agencies in monitoring and
373          evaluating compliance with this Act.
374      (b) LCPCs shall coordinate all child online protection initiatives within their jurisdiction, convene
375          stakeholder consultations, monitor emerging digital risks affecting children, and recommend
376          local policies and interventions.
377      (c) BCPCs shall promote awareness activities at the barangay level, assist in identifying children
378          requiring appropriate intervention, and support community-based digital wellness initiatives.
379      (d) City or Municipal Social Welfare and Development Offices shall provide counseling, case
380          management, referral services, and family interventions for children exposed to online
381          exploitation, cyberbullying, digital addiction, or other online harms.
382      (e) Local Health Offices, BHWs, and BCDWs shall conduct community education on healthy
383          screen use, child development, mental health, and responsible gadget use, particularly for young
384          children and their parents or caregivers.
385      (f) Local School Boards, Schools, Teachers, and School Administrators shall implement school-
386          based digital literacy and online safety programs, enforce school policies on gadget use,
387          coordinate with parents and PTAs, and promote responsible digital engagement among learners.
388      (g) PTAs and SGCs shall support schools in promoting responsible digital citizenship, organize
389          parent education activities, encourage family participation in digital wellness programs, and
390          strengthen school-community partnerships.
391      (h) Civil Society Organizations, Professional Associations, Academic Institutions, Youth
392          Organizations, Faith-Based Organizations, and Other Stakeholders shall assist in research,
393          advocacy, capacity-building, community education, policy consultations, and the development
394          of innovative programs that advance child online safety and digital well-being.

395      Local implementation under this Act shall emphasize education, prevention, capacity-building,
396   stakeholder collaboration, and voluntary family participation, while respecting parental authority,
397   children's rights, and the privacy of the home. Community-based activities shall be advisory and
398   supportive in nature and shall not authorize compulsory household inspections or intrusive monitoring of
399   families.

400       SEC. 17. School-Based Implementation. - Public and private educational institutions shall implement
401   this Act through:

402      (a) school administrators;
403      (b) Teachers;
404      (c) guidance counselors;
405      (d) PTAs;
406      (e) SGCs;
407      (f) Student organizations; and
408      (g) other appropriate school officials.

409      Schools shall:

410       (a) implement age-appropriate gadget-use policies;
411       (b) conduct digital literacy and online safety activities;
412       (c) provide counseling and intervention programs for learners affected by online harms;
413       (d) orient parents regarding responsible digital engagement;
414       (e) ensure safe educational use of digital technologies; and
415       (f) coordinate with the LCPCs and local government units when necessary.
416
417      SEC. 19. Home and Community Implementation. - Implementation within the home shall recognize
418   that parents and legal guardians have the primary responsibility for guiding children's digital engagement.
419   Participation in home-based digital wellness initiatives shall remain voluntary.

420     For children from birth to five (5) years of age, the ECCD Council, together with the DOH, LGUs,
421   BHWs, and BCDWs, shall provide developmental guidance and parent education.

422      For children six (6) years of age until below eighteen (18) years of age, LGUs, LCPCs, schools,
423   community organizations, and relevant government agencies shall provide continuing education on
424   healthy gadget use, digital wellness, cyber safety, and responsible online behavior.
425        No provision of this Act shall authorize compulsory household inspections or intrusive monitoring
426   of private homes.

463      SEC. 21. Periodic Review. - The DICT, in coordination with the DOH, DepEd, DILG, DSWD, ECCD
464   Council, CWC, NTC, NPC, CHED, LGUs, LCPCs, and other concerned agencies and stakeholders, shall
465   conduct a comprehensive review of the implementation and effectiveness of this Act every two (2) years.
466
467      Such review shall assess the effectiveness of age-appropriate regulations, child online safety measures,
468   digital literacy initiatives, platform accountability mechanisms, and enforcement strategies, taking into
469   consideration emerging technologies, scientific and pediatric evidence, international best practices, and
470   evolving educational approaches. The DICT shall submit the results of such review, together with
471   recommendations for legislative or policy amendments, to Congress.
472        SEC. 23. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of
473   this Act, the DICT shall, in coordination with the all concerned government agencies with their mandates
474   under this Act, promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations for the effective
475   implementation and enforcement of this Act.

476       The DICT shall also conduct consultations with parents, children and youth organizations, teachers'
477   associations, PTAs, SGCs, civil society organizations, professional associations, digital platform
478   providers, academic institutions, healthcare professionals, and other relevant stakeholders to ensure that
479   the implementing rules are evidence-based, child-centered, and responsive to technological
480   developments.

481      SEC. 24. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the
482   remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

483      SEC. 25. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, administrative
484   issuances, rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
485   repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

486      SEC. 26. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
487   Gazette or in at least one (1) newspaper of general circulation.
