                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Philippine Constitution, Article XIII, Section 3, mandates the State to ensure
humane conditions of work, a living wage, and participation of workers in policy and decision-
making processes affecting their rights and benefits. This constitutional guarantee underscores the
State’s obligation to address long-standing inequities affecting the nursing workforce in the
country.

        The Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) Board of Nursing recognizes nursing as
one of the backbone professions of the Philippine healthcare system. Nurses play a pivotal and
transformative role in the delivery of health services, particularly in the context of Republic Act
No. 11223, or the Universal Health Care (UHC) Act, where they serve as frontline providers in
achieving accessible and equitable healthcare for all Filipinos.

        Despite their critical contribution, Filipino nurses continue to face persistent and systemic
challenges, including insufficient compensation, unsafe and overburdened working conditions,
limited opportunities for professional advancement, and the underutilization of their competencies.
These long-standing concerns have contributed to the continued migration of nurses seeking better
opportunities abroad, driven by the need for fair compensation, career growth, and safer work
environments.

        This sustained outflow of nursing professionals has resulted in a significant shortage of
nurses in the country, thereby weakening the overall capacity of the healthcare system and
hindering the full and effective implementation of the Universal Health Care Act. The shortage is
particularly felt in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, where access to quality
healthcare remains a persistent challenge.

        While the Philippines is globally recognized for producing highly skilled, competent, and
compassionate nurses, the country has not fully maximized the potential of its nursing workforce
to address persistent gaps in healthcare delivery. There is a growing need to strengthen the
profession by expanding clinical roles, enhancing training pathways, and enabling nurses to
practice to the full extent of their education and competencies under a clear and unified regulatory
framework.

        International experience shows that countries which have strengthened and expanded
advanced nursing roles have achieved significant improvements in healthcare access, quality, and
efficiency, including reduced hospital congestion, improved chronic disease management,
enhanced patient education, and better continuity of care, particularly in underserved areas. Global
evidence further highlights that strengthening nursing roles is a key strategy in achieving Universal
Health Coverage and reinforcing primary healthcare systems.

                         PROFESSIONAL REGULATORY BOARD OF NURSING

23   SEC. 4. Creation and Composition of the Board – There shall be created, under the administrative
24   supervision of the PRC, the Professional Regulatory Board of Nursing which shall be composed
25   of seven (7) Members who shall elect from among themselves a Chairperson to serve for one (1)
26   year only but may be re-elected as Chairperson on a year-to-year basis. The President of the
27   Republic of the Philippines shall appoint the members of the Board from among those
28   recommended and ranked from a list of at least three (3) nominees per vacancy as provided by the
29   AIPO, other recognized nursing professional organizations, or higher educational
30   institutions/colleges of nursing: Provided, That the membership of the Board shall be distributed
31   to proportionately represent nursing practice.
32
33   SEC. 5. Mission of the Professional Regulatory Board of Nursing - The mission of the Board is
34   to protect and promote the welfare of the people of the Philippines by ensuring that each person
35   holding a license as a nurse in the Philippines is competent to practice safely. The Board fulfils its
36   mission through the regulation of the practice of nursing.
37   The Board, acting in accordance with the highest standards of ethics, accountability, efficiency,
38   effectiveness, and transparency, shall approach its mission with a deep sense of purpose and
39   responsibility and affirms that the regulation of nursing is a public trust.
40
41   SEC. 6. Qualifications of the Chairperson and Members of the Board - The Chairperson and
42   Members of the Board must be citizens of the Philippines and have resided therein for at least five
43   (5) consecutive years immediately preceding the date of their respective appointments.
44   Furthermore, at the time of their appointment, they must possess the following:
45      a) A valid and current certificate of registration and professional identification card as nurse;
46      b) A master’s degree holder in nursing and preferably with relevant doctorate degree
47          conferred by a duly recognized higher education institution (HEI);
48      c) A minimum of ten (10) years of continuous practice of the profession prior to appointment:
49          Provided, However, That the last five (5) years of such practice must have been served in
50          the Philippines;
51      d) Not have been convicted of any offense involving moral turpitude; and
52      e) Physical and mental fitness.
53
54   SEC. 7. Powers and Duties of the Board - The Board shall regulate and supervise the practice of
55   the nursing profession and shall have the following powers, duties and functions:
56       a) Ensure the proper conduct of the Nurse Licensure Examination (NLE), including the

 7   SEC. 30. Scope of Nursing Practice - Nursing practice encompasses autonomous and
 8   collaborative care of individuals across all ages and health conditions, in various settings covering
 9   physical, psychological, and spiritual aspects of care. This includes the promotion of health, illness,
10   disease prevention and the provision of care for the sick, disabled, and dying.
11
12   The provision of responsive, population interventions is also an important focus. It also emphasizes
13   the critical role of advocacy in creating safe environments and involves participation in health
14   policy, research, patient and health system management, and educational initiatives within the
15   nursing profession. The key areas of nursing practice include nursing service, nursing education,
16   research, and leadership and governance.
17
18   The scope of nursing practice refers to the services that a nurse is authorized and competent to
19   perform, as defined by their professional license and applicable regulations.
20   It shall be the duty of the nurse to:
21        a) Provide direct care to patients through the conscientious observance of the holistic nursing
22            process while adhering to practice standards. This includes primary health care, health
23            teaching, counseling, comfort and hygiene measures, therapeutic use of self, execution of
24            health care techniques and procedures including intravenous and other parenteral therapies,
25            implementation of legally prescribed medical orders, immunizations, newborn care,
26            maternal care during the perinatal period, internal examination during labor in the absence
27            of antenatal bleeding only as necessary, and delivery of babies. In case of suturing of
28            perineal lacerations, special training shall be provided according to an established protocol;
29        b) Provide health education and promotion that contributes to empowerment of individuals,
30            families, population groups, and communities;
31        c) Manage the delivery of nursing services across diverse settings, including hospitals, clinics,
32            industrial environments, and community facilities;
33        d) Provide and organize nursing care so that the care provided is holistic, comprehensive and
34            responsive to the needs of the individual patient, family and community;
35        e) Establish linkages with community resources and coordinate effectively with healthcare
36            workers across various settings;
37        f) Provide supervision and instruction to other nursing personnel and students, fostering
38            clinical skills and professional development;
39        g) Engage in consultation services and activities that leverage the expertise and decision-
40            making skills of registered nurses, particularly for the APRN;
41        h) Adhere to the ethical principles and standards in the Code of Ethics for nurses as
42            established by the Board;
43        i) Commit to lifelong learning through continuing professional development to enhance
44            professional competencies and remain responsive to evolving needs of clients and the
45            health care system;
46        j) Engage in nursing and health human resource development training and research for
47            evidence-informed nursing practice and continuous quality improvement; and
48        k) Exercise the core competencies in the performance of their respective roles and
49            responsibilities in accordance with the Philippine Professional Nursing Practice Standards.

51   SEC. 31. Qualifications to Practice Nursing - To practice nursing in the Philippines, the
52   individual must meet the following requirements:
53       a) Be a Filipino citizen;
54       b) Hold a Bachelor of Science in Nursing from a duly recognized HEI by the CHED;
55       c) Possess a valid PRC license; and
56       d) Have an updated PRC Professional Identification Card (PIC).

 1   SEC. 32. Generalist Practice Nursing (GPN) - A Generalist Practice Nurse is a registered nurse
 2   who provides broad foundational nursing skills in a general practice setting, often as part of a
 3   multidisciplinary team. These nurses deliver a broad range of healthcare services within the scope
 4   of nursing practice including patient care, health promotion, and disease management. Their role
 5   encompasses advocacy and the provision of safe, holistic, and quality care to individuals, families,
 6   population groups and communities which include assessment, nursing diagnosis, planning,
 7   implementation, and evaluation of evidence-based care, whether in hospital, community settings,
 8   or other health care facilities.
 9
10   Public Health Nursing is an aspect of GPN practice which encompasses a wide range of primary
11   care services, including health education and promotion, disease prevention, and emergency
12   preparedness. These nurses play a vital role in identifying health trends and risks within
13   communities and facilitating access to essential health services, including immunization and
14   screenings, maternal and child health care, reproductive health services, nutritional services,
15   disease prevention and control, environmental health services, mental health, and emergency and
16   disaster preparedness.
17
18   Public Health Nurses are registered nurses who practice in community health settings, focusing on
19   promoting and protecting the health of populations and communities. Often working as part of a
20   multidisciplinary team, these nurses assess community health needs, develop and implement health
21   programs, and advocate for policies that enhance public health outcomes.
22
23   SEC. 33. Specialty Nursing Practice (SNP) - Specialty Nursing Practice refers to the skills,
24   knowledge, and competencies that nurses acquire to provide expert care in specific areas of
25   nursing. This practice is characterized by a focused scope that addresses distinct patient
26   populations, health conditions, or healthcare settings, allowing nurses to enhance patient outcomes
27   through specialized interventions and evidence-based practices.
28
29   Specialty Nurses are registered nurses who have pursued additional education and training to
30   develop expertise in a particular field of nursing. They shall be certified by the Board and shall be
31   referred to as “Nurse Specialists.”
32
33   A Nurse Specialist must meet the following minimum qualifications:
34      a) Possess expert-level nursing competencies in the particular field of specialization;
35      b) Engage in research or evidence-based initiative or innovation in the area of specialization;
36      c) Have a minimum of five (5) years of clinical experience in the area of specialization;
37      d) Earn at least 12 academic units towards a master’s degree in nursing along a field of
38         specialization;
39      e) Be a member of the appropriate accredited specialty nursing organization;
40      f) Successfully complete a specialization training program offered by a Board accredited
41         specialty nursing organization; and
42      g) Certified by the Board accredited specialty nursing organization.
43
44   SEC. 34. Advanced Practice Nursing (APN) - It refers to the practice of nurses who have acquired
45   the expert knowledge-base, complex decision-making skills and clinical competencies for
46   mutually agreed-upon based care. It is grounded in graduate-level education and is characterized
47   by autonomy in practice, expert clinical judgment, and leadership in healthcare delivery, policy,
48   education, and research, and aims to enhance delivery of safe, effective, and evidence-informed
49   care across various health settings. Nurses who practice within this field are referred to as
50   “Advanced Practice Registered Nurses (APRN).”
51
52   The specific scope of practice of APRNs shall be defined by regulatory standards set by the Board
53   and aligned with national health priorities. They provide advanced nursing care based on the
54   CPSPN and the Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF), a quality assured national system for
55   the development, recognition and award of qualifications at defined levels based on standards of
 1   knowledge, skills and values acquired in different ways and methods by professionals, learners
 2   and workers.
 3
 4   An APRN must have the following minimum qualifications:
 5      a) Possess advanced and highly specialized nursing competencies relevant to the field of
 6         advanced practice nursing;
 7      b) Hold a master’s degree in Nursing (Advanced Practice Nursing Track) from a CHED
 8         recognized HEI with a specific area of specialization;
 9      c) Have a minimum of eight (8) years of clinical experience in the relevant nursing specialty;
10      d) Engaged in research or evidence-based innovation contributing to the advancement of
11         nursing practice;
12      e) Based on the guidelines set by the Board, they should successfully pass the certification
13         examination administered by the nursing specialty board; and
14      f) Maintain the recognition issued by the Board, renewable every five (5) years.

16   SEC. 35. Roles and Responsibilities of an Advanced Practice Registered Nurse (APRN) - APRNs
17   are expected to perform the following roles and responsibilities:
18       a) Ensure safe, holistic, and quality care to individuals, families, including population groups
19          or communities towards achieving quality, accessible, and affordable health care for all
20          Filipinos;
21       b) Act as navigator and coordinator of care, serving as the initial and ongoing point of contact

28   SEC. 50. Nursing Staff Complement - The Board, in consultation with the DOH and relevant
29   stakeholders, shall ensure the provision of a competent and safe nursing workforce across all
30   healthcare settings guided by the following principles:
31       a) Use of reliable and up-to-date evidence-based data for staffing and workforce planning and
32          making timely adjustments in staffing standards;
33       b) Regular review and updating of staffing based on up-to-date evidence and best practice on
34          the nurses' practice environment and nurse-sensitive patient, organizational and system
35          outcomes;
36       c) Nurses are not substituted with personnel from other categories in performing functions

38       d) Nurses in management positions must not be delegated to augment staffing shortages,

42          staffing;
43       f) Active participation of direct care nursing staff and nursing management in all phases of

45       g) The nursing workforce is planned to ensure that patient safety, quality service delivery, and

50   working environment and conditions of support staff are met. The maximum number of patients
51   cared for by each nurse assigned in a healthcare facility, setting, or unit shall be determined by the
52   nurse staffing standard in a general nursing service, or in the case of a general ward of a hospital,

54   to effectuate positive practice environment, safe, and quality nursing care for patients.

 6                       x. Making mandatory, as a pre-hire requirement, training that should have

17   SEC. 59. Sanctions - A fine of not less than one hundred thousand pesos (Php 100,000.00) nor
18   more than three hundred thousand pesos (Php 300,000.00) or imprisonment of not less than one
19   (1) year nor more than six (6) years, or both, shall be imposed, at the discretion of the court, for
20   the commission of any of the prohibited acts enumerated in Section 58 (a) of this Act.
21
22   A fine of not less than three hundred thousand pesos (Php300,000.00) or more than five hundred
23   thousand pesos (Php500,000.00) or imprisonment of not less than one (1) year or more than six
24   (6) years, or both, shall be imposed, at the discretion of the court, for the commission of any of the
25   prohibited acts enumerated in Section 58 (b) hereof. In addition, suspension or revocation of
26   license to operate the health facility or institution or industrial establishment may be ordered at the
27   discretion of the court. In case the violation is committed by a partnership, corporation, association,
28   or any other juridical person, the managing partner, president, managing director/s, or manager
29   who has committed or consented to such violation shall be held directly liable and responsible for
30   the acts as principal or as co-principal with the other participants, if any.
31
32   SEC. 60. Refund and Compensation - Any nurse found to have been a victim under Section 58
33   (b.3) hereof shall be entitled to a full refund of all fees illegally collected and the payment of unpaid
34   salary, if any, which should not be less than the applicable wage for services rendered. This is
35   without prejudice to the other liabilities of the violators under applicable laws.
