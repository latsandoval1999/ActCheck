                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

Electricity is a basic service and an essential input to household welfare,
public services, and economic activity. However, electricity charges may rise
sharply when international fuel prices increase, particularly where generation
depends on coal, natural gas, diesel, fuel oil, or other fuel sources exposed to
global markets, foreign-exchange movements, transport costs, and supply
disruptions. When value-added tax is computed on the entire increased
charge, the tax correspondingly magnifies the burden on households, micro,
small and medium enterprises, and other electricity consumers.

In Small Power Utilities Group and other missionary, off-grid, and isolated-
grid areas, electricity is frequently supplied by diesel and oil-fired generators.
The cost and logistical difficulty of transporting fuel to remote areas may
result in materially higher generation costs and may increase the need for
subsidies and other support mechanisms. Fuel-related increases may
therefore affect both the electricity bills of consumers and the fiscal burden
associated with maintaining reliable electricity service.

Under Section 108 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended
by Republic Act No. 9337, the sale of electricity by generation, transmission,
and distribution companies is treated as a sale or exchange of services subject
to value-added tax on gross receipts. The Bureau of Internal Revenue has
likewise clarified that generation, transmission, distribution companies, and
electric cooperatives are subject to value-added tax on their sale of electricity.
This bill retains the statutory value-added tax rate and instead limits the
taxable base for specified electricity sales. It establishes a Fixed VAT Price
Benchmark, expressed in Philippine pesos per kilowatt-hour, as the
maximum VAT taxable base for covered electricity transactions. Value-added
tax shall be computed on the lower of the actual gross receipts or selling price
per kilowatt-hour and the applicable benchmark. The portion of the actual
electricity charge exceeding the benchmark shall be excluded from the VAT
taxable base, without being treated as a VAT-exempt or zero-rated transaction
and without creating an additional entitlement to input-tax refund or credit.

This measure recognizes that generation technologies have different cost
structures and exposure to fuel-price shocks. The Energy Regulatory
Commission shall therefore determine, validate, review, and publish the
benchmarks using objective and verifiable standards.

International energy-crisis measures demonstrate the policy validity of using
targeted tax relief and price-stabilization tools to protect electricity
consumers. The Fixed VAT Price Benchmark is a Philippine legislative
adaptation of such recognized policy tools, designed to provide relief from
extraordinary electricity-price increases while preserving a predictable and
administratively workable VAT base for the National Government.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

34   submission of contracts, invoices, fuel-cost data, generation and dispatch
35   data, WESM settlement data, foreign-exchange data, billing records, and
36   other information necessary to determine whether the correct benchmark has
37   been applied to a covered electricity sale.
38         The BIR shall have exclusive authority to audit the taxpayer’s VAT
39   returns, invoices, books of accounts, and other tax records; determine the
1    correct VAT taxable base using the applicable benchmark determined and
2    published by the ERC; and assess and collect any deficiency VAT, surcharge,
3    interest, and penalty under this Code.
4          In the conduct of a VAT audit or assessment involving this Section, the
5    BIR shall rely on the applicable fixed VAT price benchmark issued by the ERC.

 7   classification, fuel exposure, billing component, energy quantity, applicable
