                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE


        According to Article 2 of the Local Government Code of 1991, the State shall establish a
more responsive and accountable local government structure through a system of
decentralization, whereby local government units (LGUs) are granted greater powers, authority,
responsibilities, and resources. This policy recognizes that empowering LGUs enables them to
respond more effectively to the needs of their respective communities and deliver public services
more efficiently.


        However, despite the enactment of the Local Government Code more than three decades
ago, significant challenges remain in the devolution of functions and responsibilities from the
national government to LGUs. While the Supreme Court's ruling in the Mandanas-Garcia case
substantially increased the National Tax Allotment (NTA) of LGUs by expanding the basis for its
computation, the decision did not address the long-standing structural inequities in the
distribution of national transfers


        Municipalities remain particularly disadvantaged. Despite serving around 60 percent of
the country's population and delivering critical services such as healthcare, agriculture, local
infrastructure, disaster response, and social welfare, municipalities receive only thirty-four
percent (34%) of the NTA. In 2023, cities generated and spent approximately P10,966 per capita
on average, compared to only P3,186 per capita for municipalities, reflecting a substantial
disparity in fiscal capacity.


        However, fiscal inequality likewise exists within LGU categories. Among cities, the
proportion with Annual Regular Income (ARI) per capita below the national average increased
from 45.65% in 2015 to 59.73% in 2024, indicating that revenue growth has become increasingly
concentrated among a small number of fiscally stronger cities. Similar disparities are evident
among provinces and other LGUs.




                                                                                                 1
       This bill seeks to strengthen local fiscal autonomy by granting LGUs an additional five
percent (5%) share in all national taxes, to be phased in at one percentage point (1%) annually.
Recognizing the fiscal constraints faced by municipalities, seventy percent (70%) of the
additional allocation shall be distributed to municipalities, while ten percent (10%) each shall
accrue to provinces, cities, and barangays.


       The bill further introduces an Income Classification Equalization Factor to provide
greater support to lower-income LGUs. By enhancing municipal fiscal space, this measure is
expected to improve local development outcomes, reduce reliance on congressional insertions
for basic infrastructure, and enable LGUs to better fulfill their devolved functions under the
LGC and the Mandanas–Garcia framework.


       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                               AN ACT
  STRENGTHENING LOCAL FISCAL AUTONOMY THROUGH AN ADDITIONAL FIVE
 PERCENT (5%) SHARE IN ALL NATIONAL TAXES FOR LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS


        Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Local Fiscal Autonomy
Enhancement Act of 2026.”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to strengthen
local governance and promote equitable development by ensuring that municipalities are
adequately resourced to meet the needs of their communities. Recognizing that a significant
portion of the country's population resides in municipalities and that these local government
units serve as the primary providers of essential public services, the State shall pursue measures
to enhance their fiscal capacity. Increasing the National Tax Allotment (NTA) share of local
government units is necessary to enable them to deliver responsive, effective, and sustainable
programs that support local growth and uphold the welfare of their constituents.

        SEC. 3. In addition to the forty percent (40%) share of local government units in national
taxes provided under Section 284 of Republic Act No. 7160, local government units shall be
entitled to an additional share equivalent to five percent (5%) in all national taxes.

        SEC. 4. The additional five percent (5%) share shall be implemented gradually at the rate
of one (1) percentage point per year, commencing in the first fiscal year following the effectivity
of this Act, until the full additional five percent (5%) share is attained, as follows:

             (a) On the first year of the effectivity of this Act, one percent (1%);
             (b) On the second year, two percent (2%);
             (c) On the third, three percent (3%);


                                                     1
              (d)​ On the fourth year, four percent (4%);
              (e)​ On the fifth year and thereafter, five percent (5%).

​         SEC. 5. The additional five percent (5%) share provided under this Act shall be allocated
among local government units as follows:


              (a)​ Provinces – Ten percent (10%)
              (b)​ Cities – Ten percent (10%)
              (c)​ Municipalities – Seventy percent (70%)
              (d)​ Barangays – Ten percent (10%).


          SEC. 6. The share of each province, city, municipality, and barangay shall be determined
    on the basis of the following formula:


              (a)​ Population - twenty-five percent (25%);
              (b)​ Land area - twenty-five percent (25%); and
              (c)​ Income Classification Equalization Factor - fifty percent (50%).


                  Fifty percent (50%) of the total allocation shall be distributed on the basis of
                  income classification equalization.


                  For this purpose:


                  (1) Each province, city, municipality, or barangay shall be assigned the following
                  income classification weight, as determined by the Department of Finance
                  pursuant to existing laws:


                  First Class – 1.00
                  Second Class – 1.10
                  Third Class – 1.25
                  Fourth Class – 1.40
                  Fifth Class – 1.60


                  (2) The weighted population of each province, city, municipality, or barangay
                  shall be computed as: Population x Income Classification Weight.


                  (3) The share of each province, city, municipality, or barangay under this
                  paragraph shall be determined by: (Weighted Population of the Province, City,
                  Municipality, or Barangay + Total Weighted Population of All Provinces, Cities,
                  Municipalities, or Barangays, as the case may be) × 50% of the allocation.


          SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The Department of Finance, the
    Department of the Interior and Local Government, the Department of Budget and
