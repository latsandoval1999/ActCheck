                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The safety and welfare of students participating in school-sponsored activities
are matters of paramount public concern. Educational tours, athletic competitions,
leadership camps, community immersion programs, field-based learning activities,
and other experiential learning opportunities play a vital role in student
development. These activities promote leadership, teamwork, resilience, civic
engagement, and practical learning beyond the classroom.

       Recent incidents involving student injuries and fatalities have highlighted the
need to strengthen safety planning, emergency preparedness, risk management, and
institutional accountability. While the Department of Education (DepEd) and the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED) have adopted regulations governing
certain activities, there remains a need for a comprehensive statutory framework
establishing minimum national standards applicable to school-sponsored activities
involving elevated risks.

       This measure adopts a prevention-oriented approach. It does not create new
criminal offenses, alter existing standards of civil liability, or establish duplicative
approval systems. Instead, it seeks to strengthen student safety through risk
assessment requirements, emergency preparedness measures, transportation safety
standards, accident coverage mechanisms, incident reporting systems, corrective
action plans, and continuous safety improvement processes.

       The bill also harmonizes existing DepEd and CHED regulations, preserves
existing legal remedies, protects whistleblowers, and establishes evidence-based
oversight mechanisms to identify recurring risks and prevent future tragedies.
      By encouraging proactive safety management while preserving the
educational value of experiential learning activities, this measure seeks to reduce
preventable injuries and deaths and ensure that students may participate in
educational activities under safer conditions.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                                 CHAPTER I
                             GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Student Safety in School-
Sponsored Activities Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect students
participating in school-sponsored activities by establishing a prevention-oriented
framework for safety management, risk assessment, emergency preparedness,
incident reporting, and continuous improvement.

     The State recognizes that educational institutions exercising authority and
supervision over students bear a corresponding responsibility to adopt reasonable
measures to prevent foreseeable injury, illness, disability, and death.

     It is further declared that this Act is prevention-oriented and intended to
strengthen institutional preparedness, risk management, emergency response,
incident reporting, and corrective action mechanisms. Nothing in this Act shall be

     SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all public and private basic and higher

                               CHAPTER IV
                   DUTIES OF EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS


SEC. 14. Duty of Care. – Educational institutions shall exercise reasonable care
under the circumstances to protect students participating in covered activities.


SEC. 15. Factors for Determining Compliance. – Compliance with the duty of care
shall be evaluated in light of:

     (a) The age and maturity of participants;

     (b) The nature of the activity;

     (c) The foreseeability of harm;

     (d) Environmental conditions;

     (e) Available safety resources;

     (f) Applicable regulations;

     (g) Recognized safety practices; and


     (h) Other relevant circumstances.


SEC. 16. Non-Delegation of Responsibility. – Educational institutions shall remain
responsible for compliance with this Act notwithstanding the engagement of third-
party organizers, transportation providers, camp operators, resorts, consultants,
contractors, or other service providers.


SEC. 17. Safety Management Systems. – Educational institutions regularly
conducting Level III Activities shall establish Safety Management Systems
appropriate to the scale and complexity of their operations.

     SEC. 18. Safe Harbor Provision. – Substantial compliance with applicable
     regulations, approved safety plans, and recognized safety practices shall be
     considered a mitigating circumstance in administrative proceedings arising under

SEC. 31. Emergency Communications. – Educational institutions shall ensure the

            The adequacy of rescue capability shall be determined based on:

           (a) Nature of hazards;

SEC. 34. Emergency Exception. – Activities undertaken in response to emergencies,
disasters, evacuations, public safety threats, or similar urgent situations shall be
exempt from procedural requirements under this Act only to the extent reasonably
necessary to address the emergency and protect life, health, or safety.

SEC. 35. Good Faith Emergency Assistance. – No student, teacher, coach, trainer,
volunteer, parent, guardian, employee, or other person who, in good faith, renders
emergency assistance during a covered activity shall be liable for damages arising
from such assistance except in cases of gross negligence or willful misconduct.

travel, upon arrival, and prior to return travel.


SEC. 42. Transportation Risk Management. – Transportation arrangements shall be
incorporated into Risk Assessments and Emergency Action Plans whenever
transportation forms part of a covered activity.

                                    CHAPTER XI

                          WHISTLEBLOWER PROTECTION

SEC. 74. Protection Against Retaliation. – No student, employee, parent, guardian,

       (a) Reporting unsafe conditions;

suspension, discrimination, harassment, intimidation, academic penalties, denial of
opportunities, or any adverse action resulting from protected activities under this
Act.

     SEC. 85. Five-Year Congressional Review. – Within ve (5) years after effectivity of
     this Act, DepEd and CHED shall submit a comprehensive evaluation of
     implementation and recommendations for amendments, if necessary.


     SEC. 86. Preservation of Civil Remedies. – Nothing in this Act shall diminish rights
     and remedies available under the Civil Code of the Philippines.


     SEC. 87. Preservation of Criminal Liability. – Nothing in this Act shall affect
     criminal liability under the Revised Penal Code or other penal laws.


     SEC. 88. Preservation of Administrative Remedies. – Existing disciplinary,
     regulatory, and administrative authority under other laws shall remain unaffected.


     SEC. 89. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred eighty (180)
     days from effectivity of this Act, DepEd, CHED, DOH, OCD, BFP, PCG, PSC, and
     other relevant agencies shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations.


            The Implementing Rules and Regulations shall harmonize this Act with
     existing rules governing educational tours, eld trips, off-campus activities, athletic
     activities, child protection, and disaster risk reduction and management and shall
     avoid unnecessary duplication of documentary and administrative requirements.


            The Implementing Rules and Regulations shall be promulgated after
     consultation with educational institutions, student organizations, parent groups,
     athletic associations, child safety experts, and other stakeholders.


     SEC. 90. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
     this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the implementing
     agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual
     General Appropriations Act.
