EXPLANATORY NOTE

Alas-sais ng umaga sa isang pampublikong kolehiyo sa lalawigan. Binuksan ni Ana,
nasa ikatlong taon ng kursong edukasyon, ang student portal upang alamin kung na-renew
ang kaniyang iskolarsip. Isang linya lamang ang lumabas: “Not qualified for renewal."
Walang paliwanag. Nang magtanong siya sa tanggapan ng iskolarsip, ang sagot sa kaniya:

ang sistema ang nagranggo sa mga aplikante, at wala silang magagawa.

Si Ana ay tauhang binuo upang ilarawan ang isang panganib na hindi pa hinaharap ng
ating batas: ang paggamit ng mga sistemang artificial intelligence (AI) sa mga pasyang
humuhubog sa kinabukasan ng mag-aaral at ng guro. Kabilang dito ang pagtanggap sa
paaralan, ang iskolarsip, ang pinal na grado, ang pagpapatuloy ng pag-aaral, at ang
pagtanggap, pagsusuri at promosyon ng mga guro sa kolehiyo. Hindi ang teknolohiya ang
problema. Ang problema ay ang pasyang walang taong mananagot at walang paliwanag na

maibibigay sa naapektuhan.

The risk is not hypothetical. In August 2020, after examinations in England were
cancelled, the moderating algorithm of the qualifications regulator lowered 39.1 per cent of
teacher-predicted A-level grades, and students reported losing university offers as a result.!
When an automated output is applied at scale without individual human review, it can alter

the academic futures of thousands of students in a single day.

Philippine regulation already recognizes the core safeguard. Under National Privacy
Commission Advisory No. 2024-04, institutions that use AI systems to process personal data
must allow data subjects to question and contest automated decisions, and must provide for
meaningful human intervention.” The advisory, however, is guidance on the processing of
personal data under Republic Act No. 10173. It is not a rule of academic procedure. It does
not tell a student which office will answer, within what period, or who has the authority to

reverse the decision.

Sa kasalukuyan, may 2,334 na institusyon ng mas mataas na edukasyon sa bansa,
hindi pa kabilang ang mga nasa Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao.?
Ayon sa Commission on Higher Education (CHED), pinag-aaralan pa nito ang isang
pambansang patakaran sa AI, habang ang mga kolehiyo at unibersidad ay bumubuo ng kani-
kaniyang panloob na patakaran.* Dahil dito, ang proteksiyon ng isang mag-aaral o guro laban

sa pasyang ginawa ng makina ay nakasalalay sa paaralang kaniyang kinabibilangan.

The data on which these systems run deserves the same care. Student records, grades,
financial information, health and disability accommodations, and faculty evaluations are
increasingly processed through third-party AI services. These services are not beyond risk: in
September 2026, Reuters reported that OpenAI was working to understand the full scope of
agent activity as a user-data leak emerged.° Colleges and universities that entrust educational

data to such services must know what they share, why, and under what safeguards.

The bill rests on five measures. First, no higher education institution may make a
high-impact academic decision on the basis of an AI output alone; an accountable person
must make or approve it and may depart from the output. Second, a student or faculty member
adversely affected must be told that an AI system was used, be given the main reasons for the
decision in plain language, and be heard on reconsideration by a person who did not take part
in the original decision, within fixed periods. Third, each institution must publish an AI
governance policy that lists the systems it uses in such decisions and names the office that
receives complaints. Fourth, before an AI system is used in such decisions, the institution
must document its purpose, data, limitations and safeguards against error and bias. Fifth,
educational data processed by AI must be limited to what is necessary, protected, and not

used for purposes incompatible with those disclosed, consistent with Republic Act No. 10173.

The bill also states what it does not do. It does not prohibit the use of AI in academic
administration. It does not tell any institution whom to admit, what to teach, or how to assess
its students; these remain within institutional academic freedom, which, as the Supreme Court

AN ACT ESTABLISHING SAFEGUARDS FOR THE USE OF ARTIFICIAL
INTELLIGENCE IN HIGH-IMPACT ACADEMIC DECISIONS AND FOR
THE GOVERNANCE OF EDUCATIONAL DATA IN HIGHER
EDUCATION INSTITUTIONS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in

Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Gabay-Aral Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — Pursuant to Article II, Section 17, Article III,
Section 1, and Article XIV, Sections 1 and 5(2) of the Constitution, it is the policy of the State
to:

(a) Ensure that decisions affecting the admission, academic standing, and careers of

students and faculty members in higher education institutions are made by
accountable persons, including where artificial intelligence systems are used to

assist in making them;

(b) Guarantee to every person affected by such decisions notice, a meaningful

explanation, and an opportunity to be heard;

(c) Protect educational data processed by artificial intelligence systems, consistent

and

(d) Respect the academic freedom of higher education institutions to determine on
academic grounds who may teach, what may be taught, how it shall be taught, and
who may be admitted to study.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

(a) Artificial intelligence system or Al system refers to a machine-based system that,
for explicit or implicit objectives, infers from the input it receives how to generate
outputs such as predictions, scores, rankings, classifications, recommendations, or

decisions;

(b) Educational data refers to information relating to an identifiable student,
applicant, faculty member, or employee of a higher education institution that is
collected, generated, inferred, or processed in relation to admission, teaching,

learning, assessment, research, student support, or academic administration;

(c) Faculty member refers to a person engaged by a higher education institution to

teach or conduct research, whether on full-time, part-time, or contractual basis;

(d) High-impact academic decision refers to a decision of a higher education
institution on the admission, scholarship or financial assistance, final course grade,
academic ranking or honors, retention, academic probation, or graduation of a
student, or on the hiring, performance evaluation, promotion, or tenure of a faculty

member;

(e) Higher education institution or HEI refers to a public or private institution offering
higher education programs, including state universities and colleges and local
universities and colleges, that is subject to the supervision or regulation of the
Commission on Higher Education under Republic Act No. 7722, otherwise known

as the "Higher Education Act of 1994";

(f) Human reviewer refers to a natural person designated by the HEI who has authority
over the decision concerned, the competence to assess the relevant record, and the

power to depart from the output of an AI system;

(g) Material use refers to the use of an AI system in a high-impact academic decision
where the output of the system is a factor in the decision, including where it
determines which applicants or cases are considered, scored, ranked, or flagged;

and

(h) Profiling refers to the automated processing of educational data to evaluate,
analyze, or predict a person's academic performance, conduct, reliability, or

likelihood of success, withdrawal, or failure.

SECTION 4. Scope. — This Act applies to the material use of AI systems by HEIs
in high-impact academic decisions and to the processing of educational data by AI systems.
It does not govern the use of AI in teaching, learning, and assessment, the disclosure of AI

use in academic work, or the use of Al-detection software in academic-integrity proceedings.

This Act shall be construed in harmony with Republic Act Nos. 7722, 8292, and
10173 and other applicable laws. Nothing in this Act shall diminish any right or protection

available under such laws.

SECTION 5. Human Decision Rule. — In making a high-impact academic decision,
an HEI shall observe the following:

(a) No high-impact academic decision shall be based solely on the output of an Al

system;

(b) Every high-impact academic decision in which an AI system is materially used
shall be made or approved by a human reviewer who has considered the relevant

record and who may depart from the output of the AI system;
(c) The output of an AI system shall not be treated as conclusive of any fact; and

(d) No HEI shall use an AI system to infer sensitive personal information, as defined

in Republic Act No. 10173, for use as a factor in a high-impact academic decision.

SECTION 6. Notice, Explanation, and Reconsideration. — Where an AI system
is materially used in a high-impact academic decision that is adverse to a student or faculty

member, the HEI shall:
(a) Inform the affected person in writing that an AI system was used in the decision;

(b) State in plain language the main factors on which the decision rests;

(c) Allow the affected person, within fifteen (15) days from receipt of the notice, to
submit an explanation and supporting evidence and to request reconsideration by

a human reviewer who did not take part in the original decision; and

(d) Resolve the request for reconsideration in writing within thirty (30) days from its

Nothing in this Section shall require the disclosure of trade secrets, source code, or
the personal information of other persons, but the HEI shall not withhold the main factors of

the decision on that ground.

SECTION 7. Institutional AI Governance Policy. — Every HEI that makes
material use of AI systems in high-impact academic decisions or processes educational data

through AI systems shall adopt and publish an AI governance policy that shall:
(a) List the AI systems so used and the purpose of each;

(b) Designate the office that receives inquiries, requests for reconsideration, and

complaints under this Act;
(c) Prescribe the procedure for designating human reviewers;

(d) Provide reasonable accommodations for persons affected by disability, limited

connectivity, or limited access to devices; and

(e) Provide for review of the policy at least once every two (2) years, with the

participation of students and faculty members.

An HEI may adopt rules that are more protective of students and faculty members

than those required by this Act.

SECTION 8. Assessment Before Use. — Before making material use of an Al
system in high-impact academic decisions, an HEI shall document the purpose of the system,
the educational data it uses, its known limitations, the measures taken against error and bias,
and the human review procedure that will apply. The HEI shall update the assessment
whenever the system or its use is materially changed, and shall furnish a copy to the

Commission on Higher Education upon request.

SECTION 9. Educational Data Governance. — An HEI that processes educational
data through an AI system shall, consistent with Republic Act No. 10173:

SECTION 10. Role of the Commission on Higher Education. — Within one
hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Commission on Higher
Education (CHED), in consultation with the National Privacy Commission, the Department
of Information and Communications Technology, HEIs, and organizations of students and
faculty members, shall issue minimum standards on the notices, reconsideration procedure,
assessment, and institutional policy required by Sections 6, 7, and 8 of this Act, together with
model policies and forms that HEIs may adopt.

The standards shall be limited to administrative and procedural matters. They shall
not prescribe the curriculum, methods of teaching, methods of assessment, or criteria for

admission of any HEI, nor require any HEI to procure an AI system.

Subject to the availability of appropriations, the CHED shall provide technical
assistance to HEIs in complying with this Act, giving priority to public HEIs serving

geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

SECTION 13. Transitory Provision. — Sections 5 and 6 of this Act shall apply to

SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty
(180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the CHED, in consultation with the National
Privacy Commission and the Department of Information and Communications Technology,
shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act. The implementing rules
shall prescribe only administrative and procedural matters and shall not enlarge or restrict the
rights and duties established by this Act.

The absence or delay of the implementing rules and regulations shall not suspend

rights and duties that are sufficiently defined by this Act.

SECTION 15. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
Commission on Higher Education. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations

Act.

SECTION 16. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force

and effect.
