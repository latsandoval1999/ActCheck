 SECTION 148 OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS
                           AMENDED

                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

Recent tensions and escalating conflict in the Middle East have once again
demonstrated the Philippines’ vulnerability to fluctuations in global oil prices. Nearly
90 percent of the country’s oil imports are sourced from the Middle East, making the
Philippines among the worst affected by surging oil prices.1 According to the
Department of Energy - Oil Industry Management Bureau, the continued increase in
the price per barrel of oil can result in fuel price increases of up to P10 per liter.2 This
rise in oil prices is also expected to create a domino effect, driving up the prices of
food and other basic goods.

While the National Internal Revenue Code provides a safety net provision for
suspending the scheduled increases in the excise tax to address possible fuel price
shocks, this provision only covered the years 2018-2020. This leaves the government
with little flexibility to address global emergencies and to cushion their economic
impact on Filipinos.

While there are more immediate steps that can be taken to mitigate the effects of rising
fuel prices, such as adopting temporary work-from-home arrangements to reduce fuel

This bill proposes to grant the President of the Philippines with the authority to reduce
or suspend the imposition of excise tax on fuel when the Dubai crude oil price based
on Mean of Platts Singapore, the primary pricing benchmark for refined petroleum in
Southeast Asia,3 for a period of two months reaches or exceeds $80 per barrel.
Currently, excise tax on unleaded premium gasoline is P10.00 per liter, while diesel is
subject to P6.00 per liter.4 Temporarily reducing these amounts or suspending their
imposition would help ease the burden of fuel price increases.

To minimize the impact on government revenues, this bill provides the appropriate
safeguards governing the President’s exercise of this power. The authority may only
be exercised upon the recommendation of the Secretaries of Finance and Energy, and
only when the specified conditions are met. The suspension of excise tax shall also
be lifted once the conditions that triggered said suspension are no longer present. This
way, there is a flexible mechanism to swiftly provide economic relief to Filipino citizens
that does not unduly endanger our country’s financial stability.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Section 148 of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC) of 1997, as
amended, is hereby further amended by inserting a new paragraph to read as follows:

      SEC. 148. Manufactured Oils and Other Fuels. - There shall be
      collected on refined and manufactured mineral oils and motor fuels, the
      following excise taxes which shall attach to the goods hereunder
      enumerated as soon as they are in existence as such:

      xxx

      For the period covering 2018 to 2020, the scheduled increase in the
      excise tax on fuel as imposed in this Section shall be suspended when
      the average Dubai crude oil price based on Mean of Platts Singapore
      (MOPS) for three (3) months prior to the scheduled increase of the
      month reaches or exceeds Eighty dollars (USD 80) per barrel.

      Provided, That the Department of Finance shall perform an annual
      review of the implementation of the excise tax on fuel and shall, based

       budget coordination Committee, as reconciled from the conditions as
       provided above, recommend the implementation or suspension of the

       given on a yearly basis: Provided, finally, That any suspension of the
       increase in excise tax shall not result in any reduction of the excise tax
       being imposed at the time of the suspension.

       NOTWITHSTANDING THE PROVISION ABOVE, THE PRESIDENT
       OF THE PHILIPPINES MAY, UPON THE RECOMMENDATION OF
       THE SECRETARY OF FINANCE AND SECRETARY OF ENERGY,
       REDUCE OR SUSPEND THE IMPOSITION OF EXCISE TAX ON
       FUEL THROUGH AN EXECUTIVE ORDER, IN TIMES OF NATIONAL
       EMERGENCY OR WHEN PUBLIC INTEREST REQUIRES;

       PROVIDED, THAT THE DUBAI CRUDE OIL PRICE BASED ON
       MEAN OF PLATTS SINGAPORE (MOPS) FOR TWO (2) MONTHS
       REACHES OR EXCEEDS EIGHTY DOLLARS (USD 80) PER
       BARREL;

       PROVIDED, FINALLY, THAT SUCH SUSPENSION OR REDUCTION
       SHALL BE LIFTED WHEN THE CONDITIONS THAT WARRANT THE
       SUSPENSION OR REDUCTION OF EXCISE TAX NO LONGER
       EXISTS.

SEC. 2. Reportorial Requirement. – The President, through the Secretary of
Finance, shall, within fifteen (15) days from the issuance of an Executive Order
reducing or suspending the excise tax on fuel pursuant to this Act, and every month
thereafter, submit a report to the Congress of the Philippines, clearly stating the factual
basis for such reduction or suspension, the estimated forgone revenues, and the
actual and expected impact on fuel prices and other relevant economic activities.

SEC. 3. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part hereof is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provisions not otherwise affected shall
remain valid and subsisting.

SEC. 4. Repealing Clause. — Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation contrary to or
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed, modified, or amended
accordingly.

SEC. 5. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
