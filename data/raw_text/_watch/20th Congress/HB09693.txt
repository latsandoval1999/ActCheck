                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive and full-cycle reintegration
program for Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) and returnee-OFWs. It provides for
employment facilitation, skills and qualification recognition, entrepreneurship development,
financial literacy, social protection, psychosocial support, and other interventions necessary
to help returning OFWs successfully reintegrate into Philippine society and the local
economy.

        Overseas Filipino Workers continue to make immense contributions to the country
through their hard work, skills, and remittances. Yet many OFWs who return home after
years of working abroad encounter difficulties in finding suitable employment and
establishing sustainable livelihoods that are necessary for a successful transition back to
their families and communities in the country. Existing reintegration initiatives, while
valuable, remain fragmented and often insufficient to address the diverse needs of returning
migrant workers.

        This measure institutionalizes a coordinated national reintegration framework by
providing access to employment opportunities, entrepreneurship and financing programs,
business capacity-building, financial literacy training, psychosocial counseling, social
assistance, and skills recognition mechanisms. It likewise establishes a Returnee-OFWs
Management Information System designed to ensure that reintegration services are
accessible, responsive, and tailored to the needs of OFWs and their families.

        The nation owes a lasting debt of gratitude to the millions of OFWs whose hard
work and sacrifices have helped sustain countless Filipino families and contributed
significantly to national development. It is therefore imperative that the State provide
meaningful opportunities and comprehensive support to ensure their successful return,
reintegration, and continued participation in the country's growth and progress.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE AND SUSTAINABLE REINTEGRATION
PROGRAM FOR OVERSEAS FILIPINO WORKERS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Bagong Balikbayan Act.”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby the declared policy of the State to recognize
the invaluable contributions of Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) to national development
and to affirm its commitment to uphold their fundamental rights, including the right to
equal opportunity, decent living, dignity, and participation in decision-making processes
affecting their lives and well-being.

        To this end, the State shall implement comprehensive and sustainable reintegration
programs for returning OFWs, regardless of documentation status, that address their
economic, social, psychosocial, cultural, and gender-responsive needs. Such programs shall
be tailored to the skills and circumstances of OFWs, integrated throughout the migration
cycle, and designed to promote access to employment, entrepreneurship, agriculture,
fisheries, aquaculture, and other livelihood opportunities, as well as financial literacy and
economic self-sufficiency.

        The State shall likewise foster conditions in which migration is a matter of choice
rather than necessity and strengthen collaboration among public and private stakeholders in
the development, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of reintegration programs.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

           a. Full-cycle Reintegration Program refers to interventions starting with the
       pre-departure stage, on-site support during employment, and assistance upon
       return;

           b. Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) refers to a Filipino who is to be engaged, is
       engaged, or has been engaged in remunerated activity in a country of which they are
       not an immigrant, citizen, or permanent resident or is not awaiting naturalization,
       recognition, or admission, whether land-based or sea-based regardless of status;

       excluding a Filipino engaged under a government-recognized exchange visitor
       program for cultural and educational purposes;

           c.​ Reintegration refers to a multi-dimensional process enabling OFWs to
       re-establish economic, social, and psychosocial well-being upon return;

           d.​ Returnee-OFW or Balikbayan refers to a Filipino migrant worker who has
       completed, terminated, or returned from overseas employment and has resettled or
       is in the process of resettling in the Philippines, including those returning due to
       contract completion, repatriation, distress, or voluntary return. This also includes
       Filipinos returning to the country after extended residence abroad; and

           e.​ Undocumented OFW, also known as irregular OFW, refers to an OFW not
       registered with the Philippine government as an OFW, or whose documents are
       incomplete or valid.

        SEC. 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all Overseas Filipino Workers and
returnee-OFWs, including undocumented OFWs and those requiring targeted assistance,
such as senior citizen OFWs and solo parent-OFWs. Where applicable, the families of
returnee-OFWs may likewise avail of reintegration services and support provided under
this Act.

        SEC. 5. Access to Employment Opportunities and Qualification Recognition. – To ensure
equitable access to employment and fair recognition of qualifications, the State shall employ
the following:

           a.​ Job Fairs. – The Department of Migrant Workers (DMW), together with the
       Overseas Workers Welfare Administration (OWWA) shall, in coordination with the
       Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), Department of Agriculture (DA),
       Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of the Interior and Local
       Government (DILG), Department of Education (DepEd), Department of Health
       (DOH), Civil Service Commission (CSC), Commission on Higher Education (CHED),
       Public Employment Services Office, and in consultation with concerned business
       chambers and groups in the private sector, private enterprises recognized for
       ensuring security of tenure, humane conditions of work, and a living wage, and
       relevant Local Government Units (LGUs), plan and conduct regular series of job fairs
       in strategic sites and LGUs where there are significant presence of returnee-OFWs.

               These job fairs shall facilitate employment opportunities in: (1) the private
       sector; (2) the national or local government, including their agencies, bureaus, offices,
       instrumentalities, and government-owned and/or controlled corporations (GOCCs),
       for civil service qualified OFWs; and (3) academic and non-academic positions in
       public schools, and state colleges and universities. Such job fairs shall take into
       consideration the specific skills, credentials, eligibilities, and experience acquired by
       OFWs from working overseas.

           b.​ Expanded Employment Facilitation. – The DMW and other concerned
       government agencies shall expand the employment facilitation offering for OFWs in
       the public, private, and development sectors based on progressive eligibility or
       qualification criteria, in partnership with the relevant national government agencies,
       the Private Sector Advisory Council, industry or professional associations, and other
       stakeholders.

          c.​ Civil Service Eligibility. – The CSC shall formulate guidelines to grant
       appropriate civil service eligibility to non-civil service qualified OFWs, based on the
       education, training, and experience they acquired while living or working overseas.

          d.​ Skills and Academic Recognition. – The Technical Education and Skills
       Development Authority (TESDA) and the CHED shall provide the necessary rules
       and regulations to grant pertinent national certification or appropriate degree
       diploma(s), respectively, recognizing the education, training, and experience OFWs
       acquire while living or working overseas.

       SEC. 6. Initiation of Reintegration Process. – The reintegration process of concerned
OFWs shall be initiated by the relevant Migrant Workers Offices (MWOs) six (6) months
before the intended return of the OFW to the Philippines. This process shall commence by
providing access to four (4) paths for reintegration, namely:

           a.​ Education, upskilling/retooling/micro-credentialing (Kaalaman);

           b.​ Psychosocial and social support services (Kalinga);

           c.​ Entrepreneurship (Negosyo); and

           d.​ Employment (Hanapbuhay).

       SEC. 7. Returnee-OFWs Management Information System and Reintegration Management
System. – To ensure an efficient, coordinated, and data-driven reintegration of
returnee-OFWs, the State, through the DMW shall:

           a.​ Establish and maintain the Returnee-OFWs Management Information System
       (ROMIS), which shall serve as the primary information network and database
       containing information on the educational attainment, skills level, and experience of
       all returning OFWs. The ROMIS shall function as a referral system to the private
       sector and government institutions to facilitate job matching, possible hiring, and/or
       skills upgrading.

            b.​ Within the ROMIS framework, finalize the development and
       operationalization of the Reintegration Management System (RMS). The RMS shall
       be an online system and platform designed to streamline the reintegration process
       for returnee-OFWs. It shall provide access to a range of services, including financial
       literacy programs, livelihood training, and access to funding opportunities.

           c.​ Both the ROMIS and RMS shall facilitate effective connections with
       government and private sector partners to ensure a smooth transition for
       returnee-OFWs back into local employment or entrepreneurship. The RMS shall
       further aim to provide efficient support for the holistic needs of returnee-OFWs and
       their families, thereby seeking to empower them to reintegrate successfully and
       continue to national development.

      SEC. 8. Institutional Framework for Recognition. – The DMW, in coordination with the
OWWA and the National Reintegration Center for OFWs (NRCO), shall serve as the lead
agency for the implementation, coordination, and monitoring of the full-cycle reintegration
program established under this Act.


                                              3
       The NRCO shall serve as the central coordinating body for reintegration policies,
programs, and services. It shall promote the active participation of relevant government
agencies, LGUs, private sector partners, civil society organizations, and other stakeholders
to ensure the effective, efficient, and responsive delivery of reintegration services to
returnee-OFWs and their families.

       The NRCO shall develop and institutionalize reintegration frameworks, strategies,
and standards addressing the economic, social and psychological aspects of reintegration. In
coordination with the OWWA and other concerned agencies, it shall oversee the
implementation of reintegration programs and services, conduct policy and program
research, and ensure that reintegration initiatives are evidence-based, coherent, and aligned
with national migration and development objectives.

       The MWOs and OWWA Overseas Welfare Offices (OWOS) shall be strengthened to
provide pre-return reintegration counseling, information, referral, and other appropriate
support services to OFWs abroad, including undocumented OFWs, to facilitate their
successful reintegration upon return to the Philippines.

        SEC. 9. Pre-Return and Reintegration Orientation Seminar. – There shall be a Pre-Return
and Reintegration Orientation Seminar (PRROS) to be offered by MWOs worldwide, similar
in intent to pre-employment, pre-departure, and post-arrival orientation seminars, but
concentrating on essential information for a returning OFW seeking to be reintegrated back
into the Philippine economy and society.

        SEC. 10. Business-Oriented Capacity-Building Training. – To promote entrepreneurship
and equip OFWs and returnee-OFWs with the knowledge, skills, and support necessary to
establish and sustain viable enterprises, the State shall provide business-oriented
capacity-building training and related support services.

​       The DOLE, in coordination with the DTI and the DA, shall provide business-oriented
capacity-building training for OFWs seeking to establish their own businesses. Such training
shall focus on:

           a.​ Business conceptualization and conduct of feasibility studies;

          b.​ Registration and other administrative matters related to starting and
       operating a business;

          c.​ Safe and sustainable business financing, incorporating the DMW’s Financial
       Awareness Seminar-Small Business Management Training (FAS-SBMT), which
       promotes financial awareness and provides training for the development and
       management of small businesses;

           d.​ Business promotion, advertising, and marketing; and

           e.​ Product and service delivery.

​      The DMW shall strengthen and expand the FAS-SBMIT and shall collaborate with
relevant government agencies and stakeholders to provide opportunities for
entrepreneurship development, business incubation, marketing, and mentoring.

​       The DA, through the Agricultural Credit Policy Council Support (ACPC), shall
provide capacity-building support to returnee-OFWs applying for its loan programs,
particularly those engaged or intending to engage in agriculture, fisheries, aquaculture, and
other agri-fishery enterprises. Such support shall complement available credit programs and
assist beneficiaries in acquiring the necessary skills to improve their business management.

​      The Small Business Corporation (SB Corp.), a government-owned and -controlled
corporation attached to the DTI, shall support reintegration efforts by providing
capacity-building programs and access to credit facilities.

         The training and support services provided under this Section shall likewise
facilitate mentorship opportunities by assisting returnee-OFWs in identifying and
connecting with mentors in their chosen industries.

       Priority for participation in the training and related support services under this
Section shall be given to returnee-OFWs with no background in establishing and operating
their own businesses, particularly those intending to establish sole proprietorships and who
have no experienced business partners.

        SEC. 11. Components of Reintegration Services. – Reintegration services under this Act
shall be provided throughout the migration cycle and shall include the following:

           a.​ Pre-Departure Services. - These shall include:

                   1.​ Mandatory pre-departure orientation seminars on available
               reintegration programs, services, and opportunities, including support
               mechanisms for undocumented OFWs;

                  2.​ Financial literacy training focused on income management, savings,
               investments, and long-term financial planning; and

                   3.​ Family counseling and preparedness programs to help OFWs and
               their families cope with separation and eventual reunification.

           b.​ On-Site Reintegration Support. - These shall include:

                   1.​ Accessible support services through MWOs for OFWs in distress,
               including undocumented workers;

                  2.​ Skills development, upskilling, and training opportunities designed to
               enhance employability and preparedness upon return; and

                  3.​ Mechanisms for addressing exploitation, abuse, and other forms of
               vulnerability, including access to legal assistance and psychosocial support.

           c.​ Return and Post-Return Reintegration Services. - These shall include:

                   1.​ Reintegration counseling and case management                to   assist
               returnee-OFWs in developing tailored reintegration plans;




                                              5
                   2.​ Economic reintegration assistance, including employment facilitation,
               entrepreneurship training, livelihood support, and access to financing
               programs;

                  3.​ Social reintegration support, including assistance in accessing
               housing, civil documentation, education, healthcare, and social protection
               programs;

                  4.​ Psychosocial services addressing mental health concerns and
               challenges arising from migration and reintegration; and

                   5.​ Specialized assistance for vulnerable OFWs, including victims of
               trafficking, persons with disabilities, senior citizen OFWs, solo-parent OFWs,
               and those with specific medical or psychosocial needs.

        SEC. 12. Protection of Undocumented OFWs. – No undocumented OFW shall be denied
reintegration services due to their status. Mechanisms shall be established for the regulation
of undocumented OFW’s status upon return to facilitate access to services and benefits.
Information dissemination campaigns shall inform undocumented OFWs of their rights and
available reintegration support. A dedicated hotline and online platform shall be established
for confidential assistance to undocumented OFWs.

       SEC. 13. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The DMW shall develop a monitoring and
evaluation framework to assess the reintegration program’s effectiveness and conduct
regular impact assessments to ensure program responsiveness to OFW’s evolving needs.

       SEC. 14. Dedicated Financing and Loan Programs. – To promote sustainable
reintegration through entrepreneurship and livelihood development, the State shall provide
dedicated financing and loan programs for returnee-OFWs, as follows:

           a.​ Development and Provision. – The DMW and the OWWA shall, in
       coordination with the Department of Finance (DOF), the Department of Budget and
       Management (DBM), the DA, particularly its Agribusiness and Marketing Assistance
       Services (AMAS), and ACPC, Government Financial Institutions (GFIs) and qualified
       financial institutions, develop and provide dedicated financing or loan programs.

           b.​ Purpose and Terms. – These programs shall facilitate the establishment,
       operation, sustainability, or expansion of enterprises and livelihood projects,
       including micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs), owned by
       returnee-OFWs, under minimal and affordable terms and conditions.

          c.​ Eligibility and Support. – The dedicated programs under this Section shall
       primarily cater to returnee-OFWs with no existing enterprise or livelihood projects.

                 i.​ Returnee-OFWs with an existing enterprise that is dismally
               performing shall also be eligible: Provided, That the DMW, OWWA, DOF,
               DBM, DA (through AMAS and ACPC), and GFIs shall provide financial and
               other support, including technical guidance, necessary to revitalize the
               undertaking.

                 ii.​ Returnee-OFWs with existing enterprises deemed robust may be
               referred to the standing programs of the DTI for appropriate assistance.

        SEC. 15. Programs for Qualified OFWs to Teach in Academe. – The DMW shall, in
coordination with the CHED and TESDA, provide a program to invite qualified and
highly-skilled OFWs to teach in universities and colleges recognized by CHED and/or in
training centers accredited by the TESDA.

       SEC. 16. Social Assistance Measures. – The DMW shall, together with the OWWA, the
Department of Social Welfare and Development, and the DA, shall include returnee-OFWs
as beneficiaries in their respective social protection and other relevant programs.

     The DMW and the OWWA, through their regional offices, shall encourage
community engagements for returnee-OFWs and their families.

       All reintegration programs and services, including social assistance measures under
this Act, shall be guided by the principles mandated under Section 17 of Republic Act (RA)
No. 11641, otherwise known as the “Department of Migrant Workers Act,” ensuring a
multi-dimensional reintegration program that effectively addresses the economic, social,
psychosocial, and cultural aspects of reintegration.

        The implementing rules and regulations of this Act shall contain detailed and
specific provisions on social assistance and protection to ensure that more OFWs are
included in the various social protection programs offered by the government, the private
sector, and non-government organizations (NGOs).

       SEC. 17. Right to Participate. – Former OFWs who are to be reintegrated into the
Philippine domestic economy shall, as much as practicable, participate in policy and
decision-making processes affecting their rights and benefits under existing laws and under
the provisions of this Act.

        SEC. 18. Application of Anti-Age Discrimination Law. – To ensure equal employment
opportunities and full compliance with the Anti-Age Discrimination in the reintegration and
local employment of former OFWs, the State shall:

           a.​ Compliance Obligation. – In the extension of services to former OFWs and in
       all matters concerning their local or domestic employment, all government agencies,
       GOCCs, and all private corporations, partnerships, sole proprietorships, including
       NGOs, as well as other entities operating within the Philippines, shall ensure strict
       compliance with RA No. 10911, otherwise known as the “Anti-Age Discrimination in
       Employment Act”.

           b.​ Monitoring and Coordination. – The DMW and the DOLE shall constantly
       coordinate to monitor the plight and situation of all former OFWs employed in
       local/domestic jobs to ensure full compliance with the aforementioned law.

       SEC. 19. Psychological Support and Counseling. – The DMW shall, together with
OWWA and the DOH, facilitate psychosocial support and reintegration counseling for
returnee-OFWs and their families.

       SEC. 20. National Reintegration Network. – The National Reintegration Network
(NRN) shall be established through formal partnership agreements with relevant
government and private partner agencies. It shall serve to strengthen and institutionalize
collaborative efforts aimed at ensuring the effective delivery of reintegration programs and


                                             7
services. The NRN shall facilitate convenient and seamless access to such services through
the creation of both Physical and Online One-Stop-Shops, wherein all relevant programs
and services are integrated and made available on a unified platform. All government
agencies and instrumentalities offering services to OFWs shall form part of the NRN.

      SEC. 21. Creating/Upgrading Offices and Personnel. – The DMW and other concerned
government agencies, upon consultation with and approval of the DBM, shall create or
upgrade the necessary offices and hire appropriate personnel to ensure the effective
implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 22. Non-Diminution of Benefits. – All rights, privileges, and benefits previously
enjoyed by returnee or reintegrated OFWs before the effectivity of this Act, including those
set forth in RA No. 8042, otherwise known as the “Migrant Workers and Overseas Filipino Act
of 1995,” as amended; RA No. 10801, otherwise known as the “Overseas Workers Welfare
Administration Act”; and other relevant laws and issuances, including pertinent Supreme
Court decisions, shall continue to be recognized and shall not be diminished.

        SEC. 23. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DMW shall, together with the
OWWA and the National Reintegration Center for OFWs (NCRO), promulgate the
necessary implementing rules and regulations within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of
this Act.

        The promulgation shall be made in accordance with the key government agencies
mentioned in this Act, and in consultation with concerned GOCCs, especially those giving
grants, extending loans, and providing technical assistance to MSMEs, and other relevant
stakeholders in the private sector and NGOs.

       SEC. 24. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the departments and
agencies concerned. Thereafter, such amount shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 25. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 26. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
amended accordingly.

        SEC. 27. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
