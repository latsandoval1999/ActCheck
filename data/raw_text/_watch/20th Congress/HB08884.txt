                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Article X, Section 9 of the 1987 Constitution mandates that the

legislative bodies of local government units (LGUs) include sectoral

representation, as may be prescribed by law. In recognition of this

constitutional directive, the Local Government Code of 1991 introduced

local    sectoral     representation       in    the    Sangguniang         Panlalawigan,1

Sangguniang Panlungsod,2 and Sangguniang Bayan3 to strengthen

democratic participation and ensure that traditionally underrepresented

sectors are given a voice in local governance. Under this framework, three

sectoral representatives representing women, workers, and a third sector,

as determined, shall become members of the local legislative body.4

      However, despite this clear constitutional mandate, local sectoral

representation remains largely unimplemented. The Local Government

Code of 1991 does not specify the procedure and mechanisms for selecting

the sectoral representatives. Instead, it delegated this responsibility to the

Commission on Elections,5 which has not yet promulgated the necessary

rules and regulations for this purpose. In the absence of these rules, certain

LGUs have attempted to fill this gap by establishing people’s councils

through local ordinances as a means of facilitating civil society participation

in local governance.

      While these initiatives demonstrate the willingness of LGUs to

encourage participatory governance, the absence of a national framework

has resulted in inconsistent implementation across the country. Without a

clear and standardized system, meaningful sectoral representation remains

uneven and vulnerable to issues of transparency, accessibility, and political

interference.

      This bill, therefore, seeks to fully implement the constitutional

mandate on local sectoral representation by institutionalizing the people’s

encourage local organizing.

1    Section 1. Short Title. This Act will be known as the “People’s Council
2    Act.”

3    Section 2. Declaration of Policy. The State affirms its duty to guarantee
4    meaningful participation of citizens in local governance through local
5    sectoral representation. It is likewise the policy of the State that:

6       a. Local sectoral representation is a fundamental component of
7            democratic local governance and must be fully realized in all local
8            government units.
9       b. Local sectoral representation shall be effectively implemented
10           through the institutionalization of the People’s Council in every
1          province, city, and municipality as a permanent and recognized
2          participatory body.
3       c. The People’s Council serves as a mechanism to ensure organized,
4          inclusive, and sustained participation of civil society organizations
5          and basic sectors in local governance.
6       d. Sectoral Representatives, through the People’s Council, must be
7          empowered to meaningfully participate in local legislative processes,
8          development planning, budgeting, and monitoring of programs and
9          projects.
10      e. Adequate administrative, technical, and institutional support is
11         needed to strengthen the capacity, independence, and effectiveness of
12         the People’s Council and its Sectoral Representatives.

13                                    CHAPTER I
14                                 General Provisions

15   Section 3. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act, the following
16   terms are defined as follows:

17      a. Local Sectoral Representation - refers to a system whereby
18         representatives of identified local sectors are selected to sit as
19         members     of    the     Sangguniang    Panlalawigan,   Sangguniang
20         Panlungsod,       and     Sangguniang     Bayan.    These     Sectoral
21         Representatives shall participate in the enactment of local legislation
22         and the formulation of policies addressing the concerns of the sectors
23         they represent.

24      b. Accredited Organization – refers to a civil society organization,
25         nongovernment organization, or people’s organization that is
26         registered with the Department of the Interior and Local Government
27         (DILG) for purposes of membership in the People’s Council and
28         participation in local sectoral representation.
1       (i)    Civil Society Organization (CSO) - refers to a non-state,
2              non-profit association organized for the purpose of promoting
3              social welfare, development, or the advancement of public
4              interests. This includes, but is not limited to nongovernment
5              organizations, people’s organizations, civic organizations,
6              community-based organizations, faith-based organizations,
7              cooperatives,    labor    unions   and     federations,   workers’
8              associations, homeowners associations, social movements,
9              professional    groups,   and   business    groups   engaged    in
10             community or development work.

11      (ii)   Nongovernment Organization (NGO) - refers to a non-
12             stock, non-profit organization that works with different sectors
13             and communities to promote their welfare and development,
14             and which may provide services, advocacy, or capacity-building
15             support to people’s organizations and communities. Social
16             development organizations, foundations, and independent
17             research institutions fall under this category.

18     (iii)   People’s Organization (PO) - refers to an association of
19             residents within a barangay or group of barangays, established
20             to promote public interest and characterized by an identifiable
21             leadership structure and membership. People’s organizations
22             are formed among the marginalized or underrepresented
23             sectors, such as workers, women, farmers, fisherfolk, persons
24             with disabilities, senior citizens, victims of calamities and
25             disasters, youth, students, children, and the urban poor.

26   c. People’s Council - refers to the institutional body composed of
27     accredited organizations within a local government unit. This shall
1          serve as a platform for citizen engagement and act as the mechanism
2          for the exercise of local sectoral representation under this Act.

3       d. Sectoral Assembly - refers to a gathering of accredited organizations
4          belonging to a specific sector convened for the purpose of nominating
5          and selecting a Sectoral Representative to the Sangguniang
6          Panlalawigan, Sangguniang Panlungsod, or Sangguniang Bayan.

7       e. Local Special Bodies – refers to the Local Development Council,
8          Local Health Board, Local School Board, Local Housing Board,Local
9          Peace and Order Council, and such other local bodies as may be
10         provided by law.

11      f. Local Chief Executive – refers to the duly elected officials who head
12         local government units, namely, the Provincial Governor, the City
13         Mayor, and the Municipal Mayor.

14                                   CHAPTER II
15                                People’s Council

16   Section 4. Creation of the People’s Council. A People’s Council shall be
17   established in every province, city, and municipality.

18   The People’s Council will be composed of accredited organizations operating
19   with a principal office, branch, chapter, or affiliate within the territorial
20   jurisdiction of the local government unit.

21   Within one hundred twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, every
22   Sangguniang Panlalawigan, Sangguniang Panlungsod, and Sangguniang
23   Bayan must enact an enabling ordinance recognizing and institutionalizing
24   the People’s Council in accordance with the provisions of this Act.

25   Section 5. Structure of the People’s Council. The People’s Council shall
26   have the following structure:
1       a. General Assembly – the highest policymaking body composed of all
2          accredited organizations participating in the People’s Council; and

3       b. Executive Committee – the coordinating and administrative body
4          selected by the General Assembly.

5    The Executive Committee is composed of representatives from different
6    sectors in a local government unit. The composition must ensure balanced
7    sectoral representation.

8    The Executive Committee shall select from among its members, by majority
9    vote of all members, a Chairperson who will serve as the head and presiding
10   officer of the People’s Council.

11                                      CHAPTER III
12                               Accreditation Process

13   Section 6. Accreditation of Civil Society Organizations. All CSOs,
14   NGOs, and POs seeking participation in the People’s Council must undergo
15   an accreditation process.

16   A uniform national accreditation system shall be prescribed by the DILG to
17   govern the accreditation process. The process must ensure transparency,
18   accessibility   to   legitimate    organizations,   protection   from   political
19   interference, and uniform application across all local government units.
20   Accreditation should not be subject to the discretion or influence of the Local
21   Chief Executive or the Sanggunian.

22   Section 7. Identification of Sectors. The Executive Committee of the
23   People’s Council, in consultation with the General Assembly, must ensure
24   representation of the sectors mandated under the Local Government Code
25   of 1991, particularly women, workers, and other sectors as determined,
1    which shall at all times be represented in the Sangguniang Panlalawigan,
2    Sangguniang Panlungsod, and Sangguniang Bayan.

3    The General Assembly of the People’s Council must determine the third
4    Sectoral Representative, which may be selected from among sectors such as
5    the urban poor, persons with disabilities, senior citizens, students,
6    LGBTQIA+ persons, farmers, fisherfolk, or such other marginalized or
7    underrepresented sectors, based on the demographic, social, and economic
8    conditions of the local government unit.

9    The People’s Council may also identify additional sectors for purposes of
10   participation in local special bodies, public consultations, and committee
11   deliberations of the local government unit.

12                                 CHAPTER IV
13                       Local Sectoral Representation

14   Section 8. Sectoral Assemblies. Each identified sector will convene a
15   Sectoral Assembly composed of accredited organizations belonging to that
16   sector. The Sectoral Assembly will serve as a forum for discussing issues,
17   proposed policies and ordinances, and other matters relevant to the sector
18   concerned. It will select from among its qualified nominees a Sectoral
19   Representative who shall be in constant consultation and engagement with
20   the Sectoral Assembly.

21   Section 9. Number of Sectoral Representatives. There shall be three (3)
22   Sectoral Representatives in the Sangguniang Panlalawigan, Sangguniang
23   Panlungsod, or Sangguniang Bayan, as the case may be, consistent with the
24   provisions of the Local Government Code.

25   The Sectoral Representatives shall represent the following sectors:

26      a. Women;
1       b. Workers; and

2       c. One (1) sector chosen from among the urban poor, persons with
3          disabilities, senior citizens, students, LGBTQIA+ persons, farmers,
4          fisherfolk, or such other marginalized or underrepresented sectors as
5          may be determined by the People’s Council.

6    Sectoral Representatives serve as additional members of the Sangguniang
7    Panlalawigan, Sangguniang Panlungsod, and Sangguniang Bayan, and will
8    not be counted against the regular elected membership thereof. They do not
9    include the mandatory representative of indigenous peoples or indigenous
10   cultural communities under The Indigenous Peoples’ Rights Act of 1997 and
11   the president of the Pederasyon ng Sangguniang Kabataan under the
12   Sangguniang Kabataan Reform Act of 2015, who shall be guaranteed seats
13   in the Sanggunian in addition to the seats of the Sectoral Representatives.

14   Section 10. General Qualifications and Disqualifications of Sectoral
15   Representatives. A Sectoral Representative must have the same
16   qualifications and none of the disqualifications of a member of the
17   Sangguniang Panlalawigan, Sangguniang Panlungsod, or Sangguniang
18   Bayan, as the case may be, under existing law. In addition, a Sectoral
19   Representative must:

20      a. Be at least eighteen (18) years of age on the date of their selection;

21      b. Be a resident of the local government unit for at least one (1) year
22         immediately preceding the day of their selection;

23      c. Be a bona fide member of the sector they seek to represent for at least
24         one (1) year immediately preceding their selection;

25      d. Be nominated by an accredited organization within the sector;
1       e. Not be related within the fourth civil degree of consanguinity or
2          affinity to any incumbent elective or appointive public official; and

3       f. Not have held any elective or appointive public office prior to or during
4          their term of office.

5    Section 11. Specific Qualifications of Sectoral Representatives. A
6    Workers’, Farmers’, or Fisherfolk’s Sectoral Representative must have been
7    working within the territorial jurisdiction of the local government unit for
8    at least one (1) year immediately preceding their selection. A Students’
9    Sectoral Representative must have been enrolled within the territorial
10   jurisdiction of the local government unit for at least two (2) semesters
11   immediately preceding their selection.

12   Section 12. Powers and Functions of Sectoral Representatives. A
13   Sectoral Representative shall:

14      (a) Sit as a member of the Sangguniang Panlalawigan, Sangguniang
15         Panlungsod, or Sangguniang Bayan, as the case may be;

16      (b) Chair committees relevant to the sector they represent;

17      (c) Participate in committee deliberations and legislative proceedings;

18      (d) Vote on ordinances and resolutions;

19      (e) Introduce and sponsor legislation concerning sectoral interests;

20      (f) Conduct consultations with their respective sectors; and

21      (g) Perform such other functions as may be provided under this Act or by
22         the   rules   of   the   Sangguniang     Panlalawigan,     Sangguniang
23         Panlungsod, or Sangguniang Bayan, as the case may be.
1    Section 13. Representation in Local Special Bodies. Within forty-five
2    days (45) days after the local general elections, the People’s Council must
3    identify their principal and alternate representatives to the local special
4    bodies. The People’s Council must submit a written notice stating the names
5    of the chosen representatives to the Sangguniang Panlalawigan,
6    Sangguniang Panlungsod, or Sangguniang Bayan and the DILG Provincial
7    Director, City Director, City Local Government Operations Officer, or
8    Municipal Local Government Operations Officer, as the case may be.

9    In no case shall an organization or a representative be a member of more
10   than one local special body. The selected representative must not be an
11   employee or related within the fourth civil degree of consanguinity or
12   affinity to any incumbent elective or appointive official of the provincial,
13   city, or municipal government.

14                                  CHAPTER V
15                                    Selection

16   Section 14. Manner of Selection. The selection of the Sectoral
17   Representative shall be held during a Sectoral Assembly duly called for that
18   purpose. The selection shall be conducted by secret ballot within thirty (30)
19   days from the date of the local general election.

20   Only members of the Sectoral Assembly composed of accredited
21   organizations belonging to the sector concerned shall have the right to vote
22   in the selection. The nominee receiving the highest number of votes shall be
23   declared as the duly selected Sectoral Representative. In case of a tie, the
24   Sectoral Assembly shall conduct a run-off selection between the tied
25   nominees. The results of the selection shall be certified by the Chairperson
26   of the People’s Council and transmitted to the Sangguniang Panlalawigan,
27   Sangguniang Panlungsod, or Sangguniang Bayan, as the case may be.
1    Immediately thereafter, the selected Sectoral Representatives shall take
2    their oath or affirmation of office and assume office.

3    The selection process must be democratic, transparent, and free from
4    political interference from officials of the local government unit.

5    Section 15. Term of Office. The term of office of the Sectoral
6    Representative is three (3) years, coterminous with the term of office of the
7    members of the Sangguniang Panlalawigan, Sangguniang Panlungsod, or
8    Sangguniang Bayan, as the case may be. A Sectoral Representative shall
9    serve for only one (1) term.

10   Section 16. Permanent Vacancy. A permanent vacancy occurs upon the
11   death, resignation, removal from office, permanent incapacity, or loss of
12   qualification of a Sectoral Representative.

13   In case of a permanent vacancy, the People’s Council, through the concerned
14   sector, shall convene a Special Sectoral Assembly within thirty (30) days
15   from the occurrence of the vacancy for the purpose of selecting a
16   replacement Sectoral Representative. The selection shall follow the same
17   democratic and transparent process provided under this Act. The person
18   selected to fill the permanent vacancy shall serve only the unexpired portion
19   of the term of the predecessor.

20   Section 17. Temporary Vacancy. A temporary vacancy occurs when a
21   Sectoral Representative is temporarily unable to perform the duties of the
22   office due to physical, legal, or other valid causes, including but not limited
23   to a leave of absence, official travel, or suspension from office.

24   An Officer-in-Charge (OIC) shall be designated to exercise the powers and
25   perform the duties and functions of the Sectoral Representative during the
26   period of temporary incapacity. The Sectoral Representative causing the
27   vacancy shall designate in writing an OIC from among the bona fide
1    members of the accredited organizations belonging to the sector they
2    represent, which shall be subject to the confirmation of the Executive
3    Committee of the People’s Council.

4    In the absence of designation, the Executive Committee of the People’s
5    Council must designate an interim representative from among the
6    accredited organizations belonging to the same sector, who shall serve as
7    OIC until the Sectoral Representative resumes their performance of official
8    duties and functions. The OIC shall perform the duties and functions of the
9    Sectoral Representative temporarily and shall not serve beyond the period
10   of the vacancy.

11                                   CHAPTER VI
12                             Funding and Support

13   Section 18. Funding. Local government units shall allocate at least one
14   percent (1%) of their annual budget to the operation and activities of the
15   People’s Council, including:
16      (a) Administrative and logistical support;

17      (b) Sectoral consultations and assemblies;

18      (c) Capacity-building programs for Sectoral Representatives; and

19      (d) Research and policy development initiatives.

20   The People’s Council may also receive grants, donations, and other lawful
21   sources of funding.

22   Section 19. Administrative Support. The local government unit must
23   provide the People’s Council with appropriate office space, access to relevant
24   public documents, and administrative assistance necessary for the
25   performance of its functions.
1                                     CHAPTER VII
2                                    Final Provisions
3    Section 20. Non-Interference. Officials of the local government unit,
4    including the Local Chief Executive, shall not interfere in the accreditation
5    of CSOs, NGOs, and POs, the identification of sectors, or the selection of
6    Sectoral Representatives. Any attempt to influence, manipulate, or control
7    these processes will constitute an administrative offense punishable in
8    accordance with law.
9    Section 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within sixty (60)
10   days from the effectivity of this Act, the DILG, in consultation with CSOs,
11   NGOs,    POs,   and     local   government   units,   shall   promulgate     the
12   Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) for the effective implementation
13   of this Act. Failure of the DILG to promulgate the IRR will not excuse any
14   Sangguniang Panlalawigan, Sangguniang Panlungsod, or Sangguniang
15   Bayan from the duty of enacting the ordinance required within the period
16   provided under Section 4 of this Act

17   Section 22. Separability Clause. If, for any reason, any part or provision
18   of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, other parts or provisions
19   thereof not affected thereby will continue to be in full force and effect.

20   Section 23. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders,
21   proclamations, rules and regulations, and other issuances or part or parts
22   thereof contrary to or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
23   modified accordingly.

24   Section 24. Effectivity. This Act takes effect fifteen (15) days after its
25   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
