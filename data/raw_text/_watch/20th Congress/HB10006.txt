                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Article XIV, Section 1 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution provides that the State shall
protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and shall take
appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all.

        To this end, the Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission of Higher
Education (CHED) have exerted efforts to improve the quality of education, providing adequate
seminars and making education accessible especially to indigent students through the
establishment of Local Universities and Colleges (LUCs), which are established and undertaken
by the Local Government Units (LGUs) under Republic Act No. 10931, LUCs refer to CHED-
accredited public higher education institutions established by LGUs through an enabling
ordinance, financially supported by the LGU concerned, and compliant with CHED policies,
standards, and guidelines.

       In fact, starting School Year 2018 to 2019, students of 78 local universities across the
country no longer have to pay tuition fees since the CHED decided to include 78 LUCs covered
by Republic ACT No. 10931 otherwise known as the Universal Access to Quality Tertiary
Education Act.

       Meanwhile, the Philippines encounters a lot of disasters yearly and social services are in
demand. Many registered social workers are required to provide psychosocial support and
interventions for victims of disasters and abuse. Although, there are quite a number of social
workers already employed in government offices, there is still undeniably a great need for them.
The standard worker–beneficiary ratio for individual casework is 1:25 children for placement; 1:20
children in need of special protection and persons in crisis needing casework; 1:15 children in
conflict with the law; 1:60 children for independent living, education purposes, temporary
residential care due to circumstances at home; 1:25 disadvantaged women / internally displaced
person (IDPs); 1:15 women with dependents; 1:25 senior citizens; and 1:15 persons with
disabilities.1Clearly, it can easily be noted how overloaded the social workers are just by the
standard ratio of worker to beneficiary.

        This proposed measure aims to augment the number of social workers in the country by
directing LUCs to establish or expand CHED-recognized social work degree programs, and by
ensuring that eligible students may avail of free tuition and other school fees under RA No.10931.

        Recent events, like school-based violence, natural disasters like earthquakes and strong
typhoons with flooding have left so many traumatized. Hence, there is a need for social workers
to provide interventions and support to affected families and individuals.

       It is time that the State invests in providing free education to students who are willing to
be the government’s partners in implementing social services efficiently and with utmost
dedication.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

           Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1          Section 1. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
 2   and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and to take appropriate steps
 3   to make such education accessible to all. Further, the State shall promote the welfare of its people
 4   through the provision of adequate social services.
 5
 6           Toward these ends, the State shall strengthen the pipeline for the social work profession by
 7   requiring local universities and colleges (LUCs), in coordination with the Commission on Higher
 8   Education (CHED), to establish, expand, or ensure access to a CHED-recognized social work
 9   degree program, while ensuring that eligible students may avail of free tuition and other school
10   fees under Republic Act No. 10931, otherwise known as the Universal Access to Quality Tertiary
11   Education Act.
 1          Section 2. Free Social Work Course in LUCs. – All local universities and colleges are
 2   hereby directed to establish or expand a CHED-recognized Bachelor of Science in Social Work
 3   (BSSW) program, subject to compliance with CHED policies, standards, and guidelines. Where
 4   immediate offering is not feasible, the LUC shall, in coordination with CHED, ensure access to a
 5   BSSW program through consortium, twinning, resource-sharing, or similar arrangements with a
 6   CHED-recognized higher education institution authorized to offer BSSW, under guidelines to be
 7   provided in the IRR.

 8          Section 3. Screening of Eligible Students for the Program. – The LUCs shall adopt the
 9   provisions of RA No. 10931 in the screening and granting of free tuition and other school fees, and
10   such other assistance as may be authorized under existing law and implementing rules, to students
11   who would like to pursue social work in their respective LUCs.

12           Section 4. Appropriations. – The amounts necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act
13   shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. For purposes of implementing the free
14   tuition and other school fees subsidy in LUCs under Republic Act No. 10931, the amount required
15   shall be determined in accordance with the budgeting framework provided under said Act.

16           Nothing in this Act shall be construed to prohibit local government units from providing
17   supplemental appropriations or other forms of support to their respective LUCs for the
18   establishment, expansion, or improvement of social work programs.

19           Section 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
20   effectivity of this Act, the CHED and other stakeholders in higher education shall promulgate the
21   necessary rules and regulations to implement this Act.
22
23           Section 6. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
24   or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
25
26           Section 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations, or parts
27   thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
28   accordingly.
29
30          Section 8. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
31   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
