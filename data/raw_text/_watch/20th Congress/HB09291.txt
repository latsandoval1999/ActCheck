                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Probation and parole workers play a vital role in the Philippine criminal justice
system. They help supervise and guide individuals who are serving their sentences in
the community, ensuring that they follow the law while working toward rehabilitation
and reintegration. Their work promotes public safety and gives second chances to
those willing to reform. However, this job often requires them to work in challenging
and sometimes risky environments, dealing with complex social and behavioral
issues.

Despite their important responsibilities, many probation and parole workers face
dif iculties such as limited bene its, lack of career growth opportunities, and
insuf icient recognition for the risks they take. These challenges can affect their
morale and their ability to deliver effective services. There is a clear need to support
and protect these workers so they can continue to perform their duties with
professionalism and dedication.

This bill proposes a Magna Carta for Probation and Parole Workers to address these
concerns. It seeks to provide fair compensation, hazard pay, and other bene its, as well
as clear opportunities for career development and advancement. It also promotes
proper working conditions, protection from harassment, and strong ethical standards
to ensure accountability in public service.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta for Probation
and Parole Workers.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote and
improve the social, moral, and economic well-being of Probation and Parole Workers;
enhance their living and working conditions and terms of employment; and
strengthen and professionalize community corrections services for the bene it of
probationers, parolees, pardonees, irst-time minor drug offenders, and juveniles in
con lict with the law under supervision.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act, unless the context provides
otherwise, the following terms shall be construed to mean as follows:

   (a) “Probation and Parole Work” — refers to all of icials and employees of the
       Parole and Probation Administration (PPA) under the Department of Justice
       (DOJ), whether assigned in the central, regional, provincial, or city of ices.




                                                                           3|Page
   (b) “Probation” — a disposition under which a defendant, after conviction and
       sentence, is released subject to conditions imposed by the trial court and to
       the supervision of the proper PPA of icer.

   (c) “Parole” — the conditional release of an offender from con inement after
       serving the minimum period of the sentence, subject to the conditions of
       parole and to supervision; violation of such conditions may result in
       revocation and recommitment in accordance with law.

   (d) “Executive Clemency” — refers to reprieve, commutation of sentence,
       conditional pardon, or absolute pardon, which rests within the constitutional
       discretion of the President.


   (e) “Probationer, Parolee, Pardonee ” — refer to persons placed under
       probation, released on parole, or released under conditional pardon with
       supervision conditions, respectively.

   (f) “First-time minor drug offender and Juvenile in Con lict with the Law” —
       refer to persons placed under community-based rehabilitation and
       supervision pursuant to applicable laws and court dispositions.

   (g) “Trial Court” — the court which originally tried and decided the criminal case.

SECTION 4. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to all Probation and Parole Workers of
the PPA-DOJ.

SECTION 5. Recruitment and Selection. — Recruitment and selection policies for
Probation and Parole Workers shall be clearly de ined and publicly issued by the PPA-
DOJ, consistent with civil service laws, rules, and merit principles.

SECTION 6. Security of Tenure. — No Probation and Parole Worker holding a
permanent position shall be removed, suspended, demoted, or otherwise disciplined
except for cause and after due process, in accordance with the Constitution and
applicable civil service laws and rules.

SECTION 7. Transfer or Reassignment. — No Probation and Parole Worker shall be
transferred or reassigned except in the interest of the service and upon written notice
stating the reasons therefor. Any Probation and Parole Worker who believes that a
transfer or reassignment is unjusti ied may appeal to the Civil Service Commission

                                                                           4|Page
(CSC). No transfer or reassignment shall be made within three (3) months
immediately preceding any local or national election, unless exigencies of the service
clearly require otherwise and the reasons are stated in writing.

SECTION 8. Merit and Promotion and Career Development. — The PPA-DOJ shall
maintain and periodically update a career and personnel development plan
applicable to all Probation and Parole Workers, which shall include merit promotion,
performance evaluation, in-service training and scholarships, and incentives and
awards.

SECTION 9. Code of Conduct. — The Code of Ethics applicable to Probation and
Parole Workers and Republic Act No. 6713 shall govern ethical standards and
administrative discipline.

SECTION 10. Safeguards in Disciplinary Procedures. — In any administrative
disciplinary proceeding, every Probation and Parole Worker shall have, at each stage,
the right to:

   (a) be informed of the charges in writing;
   (b) access evidence on record, subject to lawful con identiality;
   (c) be heard and to defend himself/herself personally and/or through counsel or
       a representative of choice;
   (d) confront and cross-examine witnesses, and present evidence and witnesses;
   (e) appeal to clearly designated authorities; and
   (f) reimbursement of reasonable expenses incurred in his/her defense in case of
       exoneration, subject to rules to be provided in the implementing regulations.

SECTION 11. Normal Hours of Work. — Normal hours of work shall not exceed eight
(8) hours a day or forty (40) hours a week, subject to civil service rules and exigencies
of service.

SECTION 12. Overtime Work and Compensatory Time-Off. — When exigencies of
service require work beyond normal hours, including weekends and holidays, the
Probation and Parole Worker shall be entitled to compensatory time-off (CTO) and/or
other compensation in accordance with civil service and budgeting rules.

SECTION 13. Compensation. — The salary grades and compensation of Probation
and Parole Workers shall be governed by existing compensation and position
classi ication laws and rules. Any complaint arising from alleged violations of

compensation rights may be brought before the proper authority in accordance with
civil service rules.

SECTION 14. Leave Bene its. — Probation and Parole Workers shall be entitled to
leave bene its and privileges under existing laws and rules, including maternity,
paternity, vacation, sick leave, and special privilege leave. Upon separation from
service, they shall be entitled to the commutation of accumulated leave credits in
accordance with law and applicable regulations.

SECTION 15. Retirement. — Retirement bene its of Probation and Parole Workers
shall be governed by existing retirement laws. Any proposal for “automatic”
upgrading of salary grade upon retirement shall be subject to existing compensation
and retirement frameworks and shall be implemented only as may be provided by law
and applicable rules.

SECTION 16. Additional Bene it. — In addition to bene its under existing laws, rules,
and issuances, Probation and Parole Workers shall be entitled to the following, subject
to budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules:

   a. Hazard Pay — Probation and Parole Workers who are exposed to occupational
      risks, threats to life, or danger in the performance of of icial duties shall receive
      hazard pay of not less than Two Thousand Five Hundred Pesos (Php2,500.00)
      per month, subject to standards to be prescribed in the implementing rules.

   b. Living Quarters or Living Quarters Allowance — The PPA-DOJ shall endeavor
      to provide living quarters to personnel assigned at least ifty (50) kilometers
      away from their place of residence. When living quarters cannot be provided,
      a living quarters allowance of not less than Three Thousand Pesos
      (Php3,000.00) per month shall be granted, subject to implementing standards.

   c. Longevity Pay — A monthly longevity pay equivalent to ive percent (5%) of
      the latest monthly basic pay shall be granted for every ive (5) years of
      continuous, ef icient, and meritorious service, as certi ied by the head of of ice,
      commencing after the effectivity of this Act.

   d. Clothing Allowance — All Probation and Parole Workers shall be entitled to a
      minimum clothing allowance of Five Thousand Pesos (Php5,000.00) per year,
      subject to adjustment as may be necessary.

   e. Medical Allowance — All Probation and Parole Workers shall receive a medical
      allowance of not less than Five Hundred Pesos (Php500.00) per month, subject

SECTION 17. Compensation from Injuries. — Probation and Parole Workers shall
be protected against work-related injuries in accordance with applicable labor, civil
service, and compensation laws and rules.

SECTION 18. Rights of the Probation and Parole Workers. — Probation and Parole
Workers shall have the right to:

   (a) protection from discrimination on account of sex, sexual orientation, age,
       political or religious beliefs, civil status, physical characteristics/disability, or
       ethnicity;
   (b) protection from interference, intimidation, harassment, or punishment in the
       performance of duties, including arbitrary reassignment;
   (c) join, organize, or assist organizations or unions for lawful purposes;
   (d) professional independence in applying appropriate interventions within the
       bounds of law and policy; and
   (e) opportunities for continuing professional growth and development.

SECTION 19. Human Resource Development. — The PPA-DOJ shall conduct
periodic human resource development and management studies to ensure:

   (a) adequate facilities and resources for quality community corrections services;
   (b) continuing professional development and training opportunities;
   (c) democratic consultations and feedback mechanisms;
   (d) staf ing patterns and standards to ensure quality service; and
   (e) upgrading of working conditions and rationalization of positions consistent
       with civil service and compensation laws.

SECTION 20. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to carry out the provisions
of this Act shall be included in the General Appropriations Act (GAA) for the year
following its enactment and thereafter. The initial amount of Seventy Million Pesos
(Php70,000,000.00) shall be programmed in the appropriate GAA, subject to existing
budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules.

SECTION 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within six (6) months from
effectivity, the PPA-DOJ, upon approval of the Secretary of Justice and after

SECTION 22. Penal Provisions. — Any person who willfully interferes with,
restrains, coerces, intimidates, or harasses any Probation and Parole Worker in the
lawful discharge of of icial duties, or who commits any act in violation of this Act or
its implementing rules, shall, upon conviction, be punished by a ine of not less than
Twenty Thousand Pesos (Php20,000.00) but not more than Forty Thousand Pesos
(Php40,000.00), or imprisonment of not more than one (1) year, or both, at the
discretion of the court. If the offender is a public of icer, the court may impose the
accessory penalty of disquali ication from public of ice, as allowed by law.

SECTION 23. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, other provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 24. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 25. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
