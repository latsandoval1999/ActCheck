                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

The ongoing conflict in the Middle East due to the escalation of hostilities between
the United States of America, the State of Israel, and the Islamic Republic of Iran has
severely affected the global economy by disrupting oil supply chains, especially
through critical chokepoints such as the Strait of Hormuz, through which a significant
portion of the world’s oil passes. As a result, global crude oil prices have surged
beyond USD 100 per barrel1, with projections of further increases should the conflict
persist.

The Philippines, importing at least 98% of its oil needs, became the first country to
declare an energy emergency after local diesel and petrol prices more than doubled
in the country2. Fuel prices have risen sharply since the conflict started, with recent
projections indicating increases of up to PHP 19.00 per liter for diesel, which will cost
PHP 166 per liter of diesel3.

Recent global reports further underscore that the ongoing Middle East conflict is
driving up not only energy costs but also food prices and essential commodities, due

In this context, the urgency of reducing dependence on imported fossil fuels and
building resilient, low-cost transportation systems has become even more
pronounced. Active mobility—comprising walking, cycling, and other human-powered
modes of transport—offers a practical, immediate, and sustainable response to fuel
price volatility. By minimizing reliance on oil-dependent transport, active mobility can
shield households from recurring fuel shocks while promoting public health, reducing
congestion, and lowering greenhouse gas emissions.

Notably, several local government units in the Philippines have already taken proactive
steps through local ordinances and programs to promote active mobility:
   • Naga City has implemented policies on car-free Sundays5, amended their
       ordinance on bicycles6, and created a council which serves as the primary
       policymaking body on urban transport and mobility in the city7.
   • Pasig City enacted its Active Transport Ordinance institutionalizing protected
       bicycle lanes and pedestrian infrastructure8.
   • Makati City implemented its Bicycle Lane Ordinance and expanded pedestrian-
       friendly policies in its central business district9.
   • Quezon City adopted its Green Transport Ordinance, which includes provisions
       for bike lanes, walkways, and car-free initiatives10.
   • Iloilo City has also established a comprehensive bike lane network through
       local legislation and executive action, positioning itself as a model for bike-
       friendly urban development11.

However, these efforts remain fragmented and uneven across the country,
highlighting the need for a cohesive national framework.

This proposed measure seeks to establish and integrate a Safe Pathways Network
composed of protected bicycle lanes, expanded walkways, slow streets, and
institutionalized car-free days, supported by dedicated funding and strong
coordination between national government agencies and LGUs.

By promoting active mobility in the context of recurring global oil shocks and
geopolitical instability, this measure will:

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Active Mobility Act”.
 2
 3   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The Constitution mandates the State to protect
 4   and advance the right of the Filipino people to a balanced and healthful ecology in
 5   accord with the rhythm and harmony of nature, to protect and promote the right to
 6   health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.
 7
 8   The State also affirms the need for infrastructure and a change in mindset to
 9   encourage and accommodate sustainable and healthy modes of active transport such
10   as walking, biking, and the use of other non-motorized vehicles and to provide safe
11   and convenient pathways for commuters. It further acknowledges the need to
12   integrate active transport infrastructures to improved public transportation.
13
14   Establishing and integrating active transport with public transportation will result in
15   reduced carbon emissions, reduced road congestion, less use of energy and resources,
16   as well as less dependence on oil, lightening the impact of oil price shocks. It will also
17   provide multiple health benefits and enhance the quality of life.
18
19   SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, these terms are defined as
20   follows:
21       (a) Active mobility refers to physical activity undertaken as a means of transport,
22           such as walking or cycling and the use of other non-motorized vehicles;
23       (b) Electric personal mobility devices refer to any electric motorized device
24           exclusively used for transporting an individual. This includes electric bicycles
 1          and electric kick scooters, excluding motorcycles, tricycles, and other similar
 2          modes of transport;
 3      (c) End-of-trip facilities refer to facilities that cater to active transport users needs
 4          at their destinations, which may include shower and changing rooms, short-
 5          and long-term bicycle parking, bicycle repair stations, and lockers, among
 6          others;
 7      (d) Non-motorized vehicles refer to all forms of transportation that do not rely on
 8          an engine or motor for movement, including, but not limited to, bicycles and
 9          small wheeled non-motorized transport, such as skateboards, push scooters,
10          and handcarts; and
11      (e) Slow streets refer to local roads providing safe and convenient access and use
12          by restricting motor vehicle access, use, or speed limits on certain days or hours
13          in the day, or any combination of such measures as may be determined by the
14          concerned local government unit (LGU), and prioritizing access and use by
15          pedestrians, cyclists, and other non-motorized vehicle users.
16
17   SEC. 4. Safe Pathways Network. – Within twelve (12) months from the effectivity
18   of this Act, the Safe Pathways Network shall be established for the use of active
19   transport: Provided, That the use of electric personal mobility devices may be allowed
20   subject to the guidelines set by the LGUs, in consultation with the Department of
21   Transportation (DOTr) and Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH).
22
23   The Safe Pathways Network shall consist of the following:
24      (a) Bicycle lanes. LGUs, in consultation with the DOTr and the DPWH, shall identify
25          and create a network of bicycle lanes, which may be temporary or permanent
26          in nature. The temporary lanes alternatively known as pop-up lanes shall be
27          set up, whenever necessary, including during emergency situations: Provided,
28          That along national roads in Metro Manila, the DOTr, in coordination with the
29          DPWH and the Metro Manila Development Authority (MMDA), shall be the lead
30          implementing agency. Pop-up bicycle lanes may be marked with removable
31          tapes and mobile signs, which can easily be placed and removed as necessary.
32          Other physical barriers may also be used.
33
34         The bicycle lanes shall, as much as practicable, directly connect users to
35         essential destinations, such as medical facilities, public and private facilities
36         frequented by people, schools, and other similar places that may be identified.
37         Motorcycles, tricycles, and other similar modes of transport, whether powered
38         by fossil fuel or electricity, are prohibited from using the bicycle lanes unless
39         otherwise designed for sharing with such modes of transport.
40
41      (b) Walkways. Walkways shall be designated or constructed for the use of
42          pedestrians, including the use of strollers and other children’s mobility
43          equipment. Walkways may also be shared with non-motorized vehicles and
44          electric personal mobility devices, subject to the guidelines set by the LGUs and
45          taking into account the safety of pedestrians.
46
47      (c) Slow streets. To complement the network of bicycle lanes, LGUs shall create a
48          designated network of people-oriented slow streets, which may be temporary
 1          or permanent that will provide safe and convenient access and use of specific
 2          local roads. Speed limits, access on certain days or hours in the day by
 3          motorized vehicles in slow streets, or any combination of such measures shall
 4          be determined by the concerned LGU. Motor vehicles of residents, delivery and
 5          trash collection vehicles servicing the area of the slow streets, and emergency
 6          services shall have access to slow streets subject to restrictions to be imposed
 7          by the LGU.
 8
 9          Slow streets shall be clearly marked with signage specifying the restrictions.
10          LGUs shall identify priority areas for implementation within their jurisdiction.
11          LGUs shall conduct a formal coordination with the DPWH Regional Office in
12          cases where a proposed slow street intersects with or affects a national road.
13          The DOTr and DPWH may provide assistance in the identification of slow
14          streets.
15
16          LGUs shall seek written concurrence or a formal position from the concerned
17          DPWH Regional Office prior to adopting or enforcing measures affecting speed,
18          access, or traffic circulation, while also providing a clear, time-bound process
19          to prevent undue delays.
20
21   SEC. 5. Active Mobility Infrastructure Responsibility. – LGUs shall coordinate
22   with public and private institutions within their jurisdiction frequented by the public,
23   such as but not limited to healthcare facilities and institutions, schools, churches, and
24   government offices, to establish bicycle lanes, walkways and slow streets that can
25   connect and provide safe access for the employees, customers and clients of such
26   institutions as well as the general public, and to encourage the establishment of end-
27   of-trip facilities within such institutions. Special attention shall be given towards the
28   safety of healthcare workers and patients who use active transport to hospitals and
29   other healthcare facilities.

30   The DPWH shall be the lead agency on active mobility infrastructure on national roads,
31   including facilities for pedestrians, cyclists, and electric personal mobility devices. The
32   DPWH and LGUs shall have clear lines of communication, coordination, and decision-
33   making between to ensure that projects are not delayed by jurisdictional uncertainty.
34   LGUs shall retain responsibility for local roads and play a critical role in planning,
35   implementation, and maintenance, but always within a nationally consistent
36   framework led by DPWH.

37   SEC. 6. Traffic Rules and Regulations. – All users of non-motorized vehicles and
38   electric personal mobility devices shall, as far as applicable, obey traffic signals, signs
39   and markers, road pavement markings, right-of-way, and other traffic rules and
40   regulations.
41   The LGUs, in consultation with the DOTr and other appropriate agencies, shall issue
42   guidelines for the safe use of bicycle lanes and the use of non-motorized vehicles and
43   electric personal mobility devices.
44
45   SEC. 7. Bicycle Racks and other End-of-Trip Facilities. – All public places,
46   government offices, schools, places of work, mass transit and public utility vehicle
 1   terminals, and commercial establishments, including but not limited to malls, banks,
 2   and hospitals, shall provide, as far as practicable, secure bicycle or multi-use racks
 3   and other end-of-trip facilities, as needed.
 4
 5   SEC. 8. Public Transportation Integration. – The routes with Safe Pathways
 6   Network shall be integrated with the public transportation system including but not
 7   limited to the Metro Rail Transit, Light Rail Transit, trains, ferry, buses, and other
 8   public utility vehicles.
 9
10   SEC. 9. Car-free Days. – Within six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act, LGUs
11   shall create an ordinance designating at least one major road under its jurisdiction as
12   “car-free” for designated hours at least once a week. Motor vehicles shall be prohibited
13   from accessing the designated road on the specified day in order to promote active
14   mobility such as walking and cycling.
15
16   SEC. 10. Role of Public and Private Sectors. – Both the public and private sectors
17   shall promote active transport as a sustainable and healthy means of transportation
18   and develop the Safe Pathways Network as provided under Section 4 of this Act, by
19   putting up the necessary infrastructure facilities and programs. Further, the said
20   sectors shall also provide, as far as practicable, additional support, benefits, subsidies,
21   and training that will promote active transport.
22
23   LGUs shall incorporate the creation of the Safe Pathways Network and their
24   corresponding infrastructure in their local development plans.
25

26   Private sector establishments shall provide secure, accessible, and adequate bicycle
27   parking based on demand forecasting, weather-protected racks, shower and changing
28   facilities, lockers, and basic bicycle repair stations, as well as safe and well-lit
29   connections to the Safe Pathways Network. In addition, private establishments shall
30   be encouraged to adopt complementary programs such as employee incentives for
31   active commuting, flexible workplace policies, and information or awareness
32   campaigns.

33   SEC. 11. Information and Education Campaign. – The DOTr, in coordination
34   with other government agencies and the private sector, shall establish an information
35   and education campaign on the importance of a shift to a sustainable and healthy
36   mode of transportation, and the establishment and use of the Safe Pathways Network.
37   The campaign shall be designed for all present and potential active transport users,
38   traffic enforcers, motorists, non-motorized vehicle and electric personal mobility
39   device users, the commuting public, and the community in general.
40
41   SEC. 12. Design and Construction of Permanent Bicycle Lanes and
42   Walkways. – Permanent bicycle lanes and walkways shall be designated or
43   constructed with safety as the paramount concern. As much as practicable, permanent
44   bicycle lanes and walkways shall be physically separated from the main road through
45   physical barriers, or shall be elevated or otherwise constructed separately from the
46   main road.
 1   In areas where the physical separation of bicycle lanes and walkways is not possible,
 2   the roads shall be adapted, as far as practicable, for shared use with active transport
 3   users. Painted road markers may also be used for roads with limited space.
 4
 5   The DPWH, in coordination with the DOTr and LGUs, shall revise its manuals and
 6   guidelines for road design and construction to include non-motorized vehicles and
 7   electric personal mobility devices in the allocation of road space on national and local
 8   roads, and to promote the safety of active transport and electric personal mobility
 9   device users.
10
11   SEC. 13. Repair and Maintenance of Active Mobility Infrastructure. The
12   concerned agencies, specifically the LGUs, the DPWH, and the DOTr, shall incorporate
13   active mobility infrastructure into their respective annual maintenance projects.
14   Further, any government or private entity performing road works or utility repairs that
15   affect active mobility infrastructure shall restore such assets to their original or
16   improved standard.
17
18   SEC. 14. Reportorial Requirement. – The DOTr shall submit an annual report to
19   Congress on the establishment and use of the Safe Pathways Network for purposes
20   of policy formulation and determining funding requirements. The report shall include
21   relevant data such as the number of users of active transport and electric personal
22   mobility devices, and mapping of the Safe Pathways Network in the country. To this
23   end, LGUs shall submit to the DOTr the relevant information needed to formulate the
24   report.
25
26   SEC. 15. Sources of Funding. – The funds necessary for the implementation of this
27   Act shall be sourced from the amounts set aside by LGUs for infrastructure projects
28   within their jurisdiction, as well as from the appropriations of the DOTr and DPWH
29   under the General Appropriations Act, and the Special Road Fund from the Motor
30   Vehicle User’s Charge as provided under Republic Act No. 8794, as amended.
31
32   SEC. 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
33   unconstitutional or otherwise invalid, the validity of the other provisions shall not be
34   affected thereby.
35
36   SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations,
37   other issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
38   repealed or modified accordingly.
39
40   SEC. 18. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
41   in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
