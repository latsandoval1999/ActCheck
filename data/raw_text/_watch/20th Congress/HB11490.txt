                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

Patients should not be deprived of their liberty, nor should their families be prevented from
securing their release or obtaining necessary medical documents, solely because of their inability to
immediately settle hospital bills and medical expenses.

Republic Act No. 9439, otherwise known as the “Anti-Hospital Detention Law”, was enacted to
protect patients from being detained in hospitals and medical clinics on account of unpaid hospital
bills or medical expenses. Despite the protection provided by law, cases continue to arise where
patients and their families experience difficulty securing discharge, obtaining medical documents,
or claiming benefits. There is therefore a need to strengthen the safeguards provided under the law
and ensure that its protections are effectively implemented.

This measure seeks to expressly prohibit the detention of patients, their relatives, representatives,
or caregivers, as well as the withholding of newborn children, solely on account of unpaid hospital
bills, medical expenses, or professional fees. It likewise seeks to ensure the prompt release of the
remains of deceased patients and the timely issuance of the necessary documents. The bill further
provides reasonable mechanisms by which financially incapacitated patients may secure their
unpaid obligations.

The enactment of this measure will strengthen the protection of patients and their families,
promote humane and dignified treatment in health facilities, and ensure that inability to
immediately pay for medical treatment does not become a ground for unlawful detention or
withholding of essential documents.


For these reasons, the immediate passage of this bill is urgently sought.

SECTION. 2. Access to Government Medical Assistance Programs. - The DOH
shall ensure that patients covered under this Act shall have access to the medical
assistance programs of the government to cover unpaid promissory notes issued by
indigent and financially incapacitated patients, subject to existing guidelines.
SECTION 3. Patient Grievance and Discharge Protocol. - The DOH shall
establish a grievance and redress mechanism for patients and their families who are
unlawfully detained in hospitals, medical clinics or other similar facilities, due to
non-payment, in part or in full, of hospital bills, medical expenses, or professional
fees.


SECTION 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The DOH, jointly with the
DSWD, Philhealth, GSIS, SSS, and the PCSO, shall within six (6) months from the
effectivity of this Act, promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the proper
implementation of this Act.


SECTION 5. Separability Clause. – If for any reason, any provision of this Act
is declared unconstitutional or invalid, such parts not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.


SECTION. 6. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations and other issuances or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act
are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.


SECTION. 7. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in any two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
