EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines continues to face significant challenges in reducing the volume of waste
generated, and ensuring that waste is properly segregated before collection and disposal.
Existing environmental Jaws have already established important policies and obligations
concerning ecological solid waste management, producer responsibility, pollution prevention,
and sustainable resource use. However, persistent problems remain in the actual
implementation! of waste segregation and reduction at the household, establishment,
community, collection, and local government levels.

Contamination represents the most significant challenge in waste segregation systems.
This occurs when inappropriate materials end up in recycling streams, rendering entire batches
unusable. While waste segregation is to build a systematic process of sorting different types of
waste materials based on their composition, poor system design undermines even well-
intentioned efforts. Insufficient training leaves people guessing about proper sorting.

Effective implementation across different environments, such as offices, schools, and
public spaces, relies on clear visual labeling, convenient placement, and proper training to
prevent user mistakes. Proper segregation delivers significant financial and environmental
benefits by reducing waste processing fees, generating revenue from clean recyclables, and
lowering overall carbon footprints.

This measure seeks to establish a stand-alone national framework focused specifically on
waste reduction, source segregation, separate collection, and accountability throughout the
ordinary waste collection chain. It does not seek to replace or amend the existing ecological solid

AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL WASTE REDUCTION AND SEGREGATION SYSTEM,
PRESCRIBING UNIFORM STANDARDS FOR WASTE SEGREGATION, SEPARATE

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be Known as the “National Waste Reduction and
Segregation Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect the
right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology, and to promote the reduction of waste
generation and the proper segregation of waste at source. The State recognizes that effective
waste reduction and segregation shall complement, and shall be implemented consistently with,
existing environmental laws and regulations. The State shall mandate the accurate measurement
and reporting of waste generation, collection, recovery, and disposal under the accountability of
waste generators, collectors, transporters, contractors, and local government units (LGUs), and
shall provide incentives for measurable improvements in waste reduction and segregation.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

(a) Waste Generator refers to any person, household, establishment, institution,
commercial entity, industrial facility, government office, or other source that generates
waste;

(b) Source Segregation refers to the separation of waste by the waste generator at or as near
as practicable to the point where waste is generated;

(c) Segregated Waste refers to waste that has been separated according to the applicable
waste classification;

(d) Residual Waste refers to waste remaining after reasonable measures for reduction,
reuse, recycling, composting, recovery, or other applicable diversion have been
undertaken and which is not reasonably recoverable under available systems;

(e) Separate Collection refers to the collection of segregated waste in a manner that
prevents its re-mixing with other waste categories;

===== [page 4] =====
(f) Waste Mixing refers to the intentional or negligent combining of separately segregated
waste streams during collection, transportation, handling, or delivery;

(g) Waste Reduction refers to measures that reduce the quantity or volume of waste
requiring collection, treatment, or final disposal; and

(h) Authorized Waste Collector refers to a person, entity, contractor, or service provider duly
authorized under applicable law to collect or transport waste.

SEC. 4. Objectives. — This Act shall pursue the following objectives:

(a) Reduce the volume of waste requiring final disposal;

(b) Increase compliance with segregation-at-source requirements;

(c) Prevent the mixing of segregated waste during collection, transportation, and handling;

(d) Establish uniform national standards for waste classification, identification, containers,
and collection;

(e) Establish measurable waste reduction and segregation performance indicators;

(f) Improve transparency in the collection and destination of waste;

(g) Strengthen accountability among waste generators, waste collectors, transporters,
contractors, and LGUs;

(h}) Encourage communities, establishments, and LGUs to reduce residual waste generation;

(i) Improve the reliability of national waste data; and

(j) Strengthen the implementation of existing environmental laws without diminishing or
transferring the powers and responsibilities established thereunder.

SEC. 5. Scope. — This Act shall apply to waste generators, waste collectors, waste
transporters, waste collection contractors, and LGUs insofar as their activities relate to waste
reduction, segregation, collection, transportation, reporting, and accountability.

This Act shall not regulate matters concerning the design, manufacture, importation,
marketing, life-cycle responsibility, circular procurement, or other economic aspects of products
and materials, except insofar as such matters are directly necessary to implement waste
segregation and reduction requirements under this Act.

SEC. 6. National Waste Segregation Standards. — The DENR, through the Environmental
Management Bureau (EMB), in coordination with the National Solid Waste Management
Commission (NSWMC), shall establish and periodically update uniform national standards for:

(a) waste categories and identification;

(b) color coding, labels, symbols, and markings;

(c) minimum specifications for waste containers;

(d) segregation practices;

(e} separate collection procedures;

(f) transport and handling of segregated waste;

(g) measurement and reporting of waste quantities; and

(h) other operational standards necessary to achieve the objectives of this Act.

===== [page 5] =====
The standards established under this Section shall serve as minimum national standards.
LGUs may adopt stricter standards appropriate to their circumstances, provided that such
standards are consistent with existing laws and national regulations.

SEC. 7. Segregation at Source. — All waste generators shall comply with applicable
segregation-at-source requirements established under existing law and the national standards
issued pursuant to this Act. No waste generator shall knowingly place waste in a container
designated for a different waste category.

For residential premises, the obligation shall apply to the household or person
responsible for the generation or disposal of the waste.

For commercial, institutional, industrial, governmental, and other establishments, the
owner, operator, administrator, or person exercising control over the premises shall ensure
compliance.

SEC. 8. Separate Collection and Prevention of Re-Mixing. — LGUs and authorized waste
collection service providers shall implement collection systems that preserve, to the greatest
extent practicable, the segregation of waste from the point of collection until delivery to the
appropriate destination.

No authorized waste collector, transporter, contractor, or employee thereof shall
knowingly mix source-separated waste during collection, transportation, or handling.
Where a segregated waste stream has become contaminated and is no longer reasonably
recoverable, the collector or transporter shall document the contamination in accordance with
standards prescribed by the DENR.

SEC. 9. Waste Collection Transparency. — Every LGU shall make publicly available,
through appropriate physical or electronic means:

(a) collection schedules by waste category;

(b) designated collection points;

(c) the identity of authorized waste collection service providers;

(d) the facilities or destinations to which collected waste is delivered;
(e) applicable segregation requirements; and

(f} mechanisms for reporting violations or complaints.

The DENR shall establish minimum information and reporting standards for purposes of
this Section.

SEC. 10. Waste Reduction and Segregation Performance Standards. — The DENR, in
coordination with the DILG and NSWMC, shall establish measurable national performance

indicators for waste reduction and segregation.

The indicators may include:

===== [page 6] =====
(a) reduction in residual waste generation;

(b) source segregation compliance;

(c) separate collection compliance;

(d) reduction in waste sent for final disposal;

{e} recovery or diversion of segregated materials;

(f) frequency of waste-mixing incidents;

(g} accuracy and completeness of waste reports;

(h) public accessibility of collection information; and

(i) other indicators necessary to measure the effective implementation of this Act.

The standards shall take into account differences in population, urbanization, waste
generation patterns, geography, and available waste management infrastructure.

SEC. 11. Local Waste Reduction and Segregation Plans. — Every city and municipality shall
incorporate into its existing waste management planning processes a Waste Reduction and
Segregation Plan containing, ata minimum:

(a) baseline waste generation data;

(b) baseline residual] waste generation;

(c) source segregation targets;

(d} measures for preventing waste mixing;

(e) collection and transportation protocols;

(f) public education measures;

(g) mechanisms for monitoring compliance; and
(h} annual waste reduction and segregation targets.

Nothing in this Section shall require the creation of a separate planning body or planning
process where an existing local waste management plan may be supplemented to satisfy the
requirements of this Act.

SEC. 12. Accountability of Waste Collectors and Transporters. — Authorized waste
collectors and transporters shall:

(a) preserve segregation during collection and transportation;

(b) use properly identified collection equipment appropriate to the waste stream;

(c) deliver waste to authorized destinations;

(d) maintain records of the quantity and category of waste collected;

(e) report incidents involving the mixing or contamination of segregated waste; and

(f) comply with applicable environmental, sanitation, occupational safety, and other laws.

Repeated or substantial violations may constitute grounds for suspension, cancellation,
disqualification, or other administrative sanctions under applicable law and regulations.

SEC. 13. Waste Generator Responsibilities. — Waste generators shall:

(a) segregate waste at source;

===== [page 7] =====
(b) use designated containers where provided;

(c) comply with applicable collection schedules;

(d) refrain from intentionally mixing segregated waste;

(e) take reasonable measures to reduce residual waste; and

(f) comply with applicable rules and standards issued pursuant to this Act.

SEC. 14. Public Information and Education. — The DENR, DILG, Department of Education
(DepEd), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA), LGUs, and other concerned agencies shall conduct continuing public
information and education programs concerning:

(a) waste reduction;

(b) proper segregation;

(c) responsible disposal;

(d} prevention of waste mixing;

(e) collection schedules and systems;

(f) environmental and public health impacts of improper waste management; and
(g) the rights and responsibilities of waste generators and communities.

SEC. 15. National Waste Reduction and Segregation Information System. — The DENR shall
establish and maintain a national electronic information system containing, to the extent
practicable:

(a) waste generation data;

(b) residual waste data;

(c} source segregation compliance;

(d) separate collection compliance;

(e) waste-mixing incidents;

(f) diversion and recovery data;

(g) collection and transportation information;
(h) destination and disposal information; and
(i) local government performance indicators.

The information system shall be publicly accessible to the extent allowed by law, subject
to legitimate confidentiality, security, and data protection requirements.

SEC. 16. Reporting by Local Government Units. — LGUs shall submit annual reports to the
DENR containing information necessary to assess compliance with this Act.

The DENR shall prescribe a standardized reporting format to minimize duplication with
reporting requirements under existing environmental laws. Whenever substantially similar
information is already required to be submitted under existing law, the DENR shall, to the extent
practicable, allow such information to satisfy the corresponding reporting requirement under
this Act.

===== [page 8] =====
SEC. 17. National Waste Reduction and Segregation Performance Index. — The DENR shall
establish a National Waste Reduction and Segregation Performance Index to measure and
publicly report the performance of LGUs.

The Index shall consider, among others:

(a) waste reduction;

(b) residual waste generation;

(c) source segregation compliance;

(d) separate collection compliance;

(e) waste-mixing incidents;

(f} accuracy of waste data;

(g) public disclosure of waste collection information;
(h) citizen complaints and verified violations; and

(i) other appropriate indicators.

The DENR shall publish the results annually.

SEC. 18. Incentives for High-Performing Local Government Units. — LGUs demonstrating
substantial and sustained improvement in waste reduction and segregation may be granted
priority access to:

(a) technical assistance;

(b) capacity-building programs;

(c) waste collection and segregation equipment;

(d) grants and other lawful financial assistance;

(e) pilot programs and technology assistance; and

(f} other incentives authorized under applicable laws and regulations.

The grant of incentives shall be subject to existing budgeting, accounting, auditing, and
procurement Jaws and regulations.

SEC. 19. Community and Establishment Recognition Program. — The DENR, in
coordination with the DILG and LGUs, shall establish a recognition program for barangays,
schools, establishments, institutions, subdivisions, and other communities that demonstrate
measurable and sustained improvement in waste reduction and segregation.

Recognition may include certification, public recognition, technical assistance, capacity
building, or eligibility for lawful incentives.

SEC. 20. Prohibited Acts. — The following acts are prohibited:

(a) knowingly mixing source-separated waste during collection, transportation, or
handling;

(b) knowingly representing mixed waste as properly segregated waste;

(c) falsifying records submitted pursuant to this Act;

===== [page 9] =====
(d} unauthorized disposal or delivery of collected waste to a destination not authorized
under applicable law;

(e) knowingly interfering with or tampering with systems established for monitoring waste
collection or reporting; and

{f}) other acts expressly prohibited by the implementing rules and regulations that are
necessary to prevent circumvention of this Act.

This Section shall be implemented without prejudice to prohibited acts and penalties
provided under other applicable laws.

SEC. 21. Administrative Sanctions. — Violations of this Act shal] be subject to appropriate
administrative sanctions, taking into consideration the nature, frequency, gravity, extent, and
environmental impact of the violation.

For households, first violations involving improper segregation shall, as a general rule, be
addressed through written warning, information, education, or community environmental
service, except in cases involving substantial environmental! harm or repeated violations.

Commercial, institutional, industrial, and other establishments may be subjected to
graduated administrative penalties based on the scale and gravity of the violation.
Waste collectors, transporters, and contractors may be subjected to fines, suspension,
cancellation of accreditation, disqualification from government contracts, or other sanctions
authorized by law.

Nothing herein shall preclude civil or criminal liability under other applicable laws.

SEC. 22. Corrective Action for Persistent Noncompliance. — Where a local government unit
or its authorized waste collection contractor repeatedly and substantially fails to comply with
the standards established under this Act, the DENR and DILG may require the submission of a

corrective action plan.

The corrective action plan shall identify the causes of noncompliance, specific remedial
measures, responsible offices or entities, and a reasonable implementation timetable.

Any action affecting national government assistance or intergovernmental transfers shall
be undertaken only in accordance with due process and applicable laws governing local
government fiscal autonomy and intergovernmental relations.

SEC. 23. Inter-Agency Coordination. — The DENR shall serve as the lead agency for the
implementation of this Act and shall coordinate with the DILG, NSWMC, Department of Finance
(DOF), Department of Education (DepEd), CHED, TESDA, Department of Trade and Industry
(DTI), LGUs, and other concerned agencies.

The agencies shall coordinate their programs to avoid duplication and ensure that the
implementation of this Act complements existing environmental, solid waste management,

producer responsibility, and circular economy policies.

SEC. 24. Construction in relation to Existing Laws. — Nothing in this Act shall be construed
as repealing, amending, diminishing, or transferring any right, power, duty, responsibility,
standard, obligation, or penalty established under:

(a) Republic Act No. 9003, otherwise known as the “Ecological Solid Waste Management Act
of 2000”;

(b) Republic Act No. 11898, otherwise known as the “Extended Producer Responsibility Act
of 2022”;

(c} Republic Act No. 8749, otherwise known as the “Philippine Clean Air Act of 1999”;

(d) other applicable environmental, public health, sanitation, local government, or waste-
related laws; or

(e) any subsequent law establishing a national framework for circular economy.

This Act shall be interpreted and implemented as a complementary and supplemental
measure focused on waste reduction, source segregation, separate collection, monitoring,
transparency, and accountability.

In case of an apparent overlap between this Act and another law, the concerned agencies
shall, to the extent legally permissible, adopt an interpretation that gives effect to both laws and
avoids duplication of regulatory requirements. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish
the level of environmental protection, waste management standards, or obligations established
under existing law.

SEC. 25. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DENR, in coordination with the DILG, NSWMC, concerned national
government agencies, LGUs, and relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules
and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 26. Transition Period. — LGUs, waste collectors, transporters, contractors,
establishments, and other covered entities shal! be given a reasonable transition period, as
determined in the implementing rules and regulations, to comply with the standards established

under this Act.

The transition period shall take into account the availability of necessary infrastructure,
equipment, personnel, and financial resources.

SEC. 27. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DENR and other concerned
agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 28. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 29. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved.
