EXPLANATORY NOTE

Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates that
“The State shall protect and promote the right to health of the people and
instill health consciousness among them.” While significant strides have been
made to expand healthcare access across the country, access alone is
insufficient if the care provided is unsafe. Preventable medical harm, adverse
events, and systemic errors remain a significant but often underreported
threat to public health and national development.

Currently, the approach to medical errors in the healthcare sector is
heavily punitive. This environment discourages healthcare workers from
reporting near-misses and adverse events out of fear of administrative, civil,
or criminal retaliation. Without transparent reporting, the healthcare system
loses critical opportunities to analyze root causes, correct systemic flaws, and
prevent future harm.

The proposed bill mandates the creation of the National Healthcare
Safety Authority (NHSA), an independent body attached to the Department of
Health (DOH). The NHSA will be tasked with developing mandatory national
patient safety standards, conducting safety audits, and establishing a
confidential, non-punitive national incident reporting system. By legally
protecting good-faith reporters, the State empowers healthcare professionals
to become active partners in identifying systemic risks without the fear of
unjust reprisal.

Furthermore, this bill introduces a public Healthcare Safety Rating
System to empower patients with transparent data regarding facility
compliance and safety outcomes. It also mandates the integration of these
safety metrics into PhilHealth’s accreditation and reimbursement systems,

===== [page 2] =====
ensuring that public funds are directed toward institutions that prioritize
high-quality, reliable care.

Healthcare must be designed to heal, not harm. By establishing a
dedicated authority and a proactive, evidence-based framework for patient
safety, this legislation will strengthen public trust in our medical institutions
and ensure that every Filipino receives the safe, high-quality healthcare they
deserve.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “National
Healthcare Safety and Reliability Act”.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the health of all Filipinos through a comprehensive, integrated, and
sustainable healthcare system that delivers safe and high-quality services.

Acknowledging that preventable medical harm threatens public health and
national development, the State shall ensure equitable access to care,
advance patient safety and health literacy, drive continuous improvement in
medical standards, and enforce public accountability through inter-agency
coordination.

Accordingly, the State shall:
a) Establish a national system for healthcare safety and reliability;

b) Institutionalize a culture of safety, transparency, accountability, and
continuous learning throughout the healthcare system;

c) Ensure that all healthcare facilities comply with evidence-based patient
safety standards;

d) Encourage the reporting, analysis, and prevention of healthcare-related
harm;

Near Miss - an incident that could have caused harm but did not, either

Sentinel Event - unexpected occurrence resulting in death or serious

Healthcare Worker - any licensed or authorized health professional or

Just Culture - refers to a work environment that encourages reporting
of errors while maintaining accountability for reckless or willful
misconduct.

Patient Safety refers to the prevention of errors and adverse effects
associated with healthcare.

Preventable Medical Harm refers to injury, disability, or death resulting
from healthcare management rather than the patient's underlying
condition and which could reasonably be avoided through existing
evidence-based practices.

SEC. 5. Creation of the National Healthcare Safety Authority (NHSA). -
There is hereby created a National Healthcare Safety Authority (NHSA),
attached to the Department of Health (DOH) for policy coordination. The
NHSA shall exercise technical and operational autonomy in the performance
of its functions.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish or transfer the
licensing, permitting, inspection, or regulatory authority of the Department of
Health and other agencies under existing laws.

The NHSA may recommend regulatory actions and corrective measures
to the appropriate authorities based on its findings and evaluations.

SEC. 6. Governing Policy Board. —- The NHSA shall be governed by a Policy
Board composed of:

a) Secretary of Health as Chairperson, ex-officio;

b) President and CEO of PhilHealth as Vice-Chairperson, ex officio;
c) NHSA Director-General

d) PRC Chairperson as member;

e) Representative from healthcare professional organizations; and
f) Representative from patient advocacy groups.

The Board may constitute Technical Advisory Committees composed of
recognized experts in medicine, nursing, health economics, patient safety,
and other relevant fields, whose membership shall be determined by the
Board in consultation with stakeholders.

SEC. 7. Director-General. — The NHSA shall be headed by a Director-General
appointed by the President for a fixed term of five (5) years unless removed for

cause. The Director-General shall:

a) Implement Board policies;

c) Oversee national programs and enforcement; and

SEC. 8. Organizational Structure and Staffing. — The organizational
structure, staffing pattern, and personnel requirements of the NHSA shall be
determined by the DOH, in consultation with the Director-General, subject to
the evaluation and approval of the DBM and in accordance with Civil Service
Commission (CSC) rules and regulations and existing laws on position
classification and compensation.

SEC. 9. Powers and Functions — The NHSA shall:

a) Develop, recommend, monitor compliance with, and _ evaluate
implementation of national patient safety standards.

b) Conduct safety audits and system reviews of healthcare facilities;
c) Establish a mandatory national incident reporting system;

d) Analyze safety data to identify systemic risks and recommend corrective
actions;

e) Publish annual safety reports;

f} Conduct patient safety reviews, root-cause analyses, and investigations
of major healthcare safety incidents for the purpose of systems
improvement and prevention of future harm; and

g) Advise Congress, DOH, and other agencies on patient safety policy
reforms;

SEC. 10. Mandatory Reporting and Protection of Reporters. — A Just
Culture framework shall be adopted in the reporting of adverse events and
near misses to the NHSA, in accordance with prescribe guidelines. No
healthcare worker shall be subjected to disciplinary, civil, or criminal liability
solely by reason of the good-faith reporting of an adverse event, near miss, or
patient safety concern under this Act. Reporting under this Act shall be:

a) Confidential and non-punitive;

b) Protected from use for disciplinary or criminal action, except in cases
of gross negligence, willful misconduct, or criminal intent;

c) Intended solely for system improvement and harm prevention.

SEC. 11. Healthcare Safety Rating System — The NHSA shall establish a
public safety rating system based on:

a) Compliance with standards;

b) Patient safety outcomes;

c) Reporting performance;

d) Workforce safety indicators; and
e) Corrective action compliance.

SEC. 12. Integration with PhilHealth. - The integration of patient safety
indicators into accreditation and reimbursement systems shall be undertaken
by PhilHealth in coordination with the NHSA and the DOH.

SEC. 13. Workforce Development. — All healthcare facilities shall ensure
regular patient safety training for healthcare workers, including orientation,
continuing education, and leadership training. Patient safety principles shall
be integrated into health professional education curricula in coordination
with relevant regulatory bodies.

SEC. 14. Funding. — The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. The NHSA
may also receive grants, donations, and technical assistance, subject to
existing laws and auditing rules.

SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with the NHSA and
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary Implementing Rules
and Regulations.

SEC. 16. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

SEC. 18. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
