                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Without a dentist in every Rural Health Unit, the Philippine primary care
system leaves oral health to the few who can afford to travel to the city. This bill
ensures no Filipino is left behind when it comes to dental care.

      This bill establishes a dental unit in every Rural Health Unit (RHU) under the
supervision of the Department of Health (DOH), as part of the State’s Primary
Health Care Approach. Each unit has a public health dentist and a trained Barangay
Health Worker who serves as a dental aide. The measure complements the Universal
Health Care Act by making dental care part of primary care at the municipal level.

        Many RHUs, especially in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas,
have a doctor, a nurse, and a midwife, but no dentist. Families in upland barangays
and island municipalities spend on fare and lose a day’s wages just to reach a dental
clinic in the city or the provincial capital. Many simply endure the pain, which the
“tiis lang” (just endure it) habit embedded in Filipino culture makes worse, until a
treatable cavity ends in infection or extraction. Children miss classes because of
toothaches, and pregnant mothers and senior citizens go without care. Dental
services also depend heavily on what each local government unit can afford, which
leaves poorer municipalities behind.

       This bill makes dental care a permanent part of every RHU, so prevention,
screening, and early treatment reach communities where people live. Barangay
Health Workers extend oral health education and services down to the sitio level.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
CREATING A DENTAL UNIT IN EVERY RURAL HEALTH UNIT UNDER THE
DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH AS PART OF THE PRIMARY APPROACH IN THE
DELIVERY OF HEALTH SERVICES AND PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Local Dental Health Unit
Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
pursue the Primary Health Care Approach in the delivery of health services and to
undertake human resource development for this policy and to promote preventive
dental healthcare in the rural areas as part of the Primary Health Care strategy.

        SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

           a. Rural Health Unit, also known as Main Health Center, refers to a
        primary health facility in a municipality, composed of a physician, a nurse,
        and a midwife, which carries out the public health functions of the
        Department of Health (DOH), including preventive and curative health care;
        and

            b. Dental Unit refers to a unit composed of a public health dentist and a
        trained Barangay Health Worker serving as a dental aide.

      SEC. 4. Establishment of Dental Units in Rural Health Units. – As part of the
Primary Health Care Approach, a dental unit is hereby established in every Rural
Health Unit under the supervision of the Department of Health (DOH).

        SEC. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DOH, in coordination with
local government units, shall issue the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act within six (6) months of the effectivity hereof.
​       SEC. 6. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be funded from the current fiscal year’s appropriation for the DOH.
Thereafter, such amount as may be necessary for the implementation of this Act shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 7. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
