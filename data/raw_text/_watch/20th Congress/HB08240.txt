                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Reliable telecommunications and internet services are no longer luxuries—they are
essential public utilities that sustain commerce, education, health services, public safety,
governance, and daily life. In an increasingly digital economy, disruptions in connectivity
translate directly into lost productivity, financial harm, communication breakdowns, and
diminished access to essential services.

       While public telecommunications entities (PTEs) and internet service providers
(ISPs) are subject to regulatory oversight, current consumer protection mechanisms largely
depend on individual complaints to trigger refunds or billing adjustments. In practice,
many subscribers who experience prolonged service degradation do not file claims due to
inconvenience, lack of awareness, or the difficulty of proving network-level impairment.
As a result, consumers are often billed for services that were materially unusable or
substantially impaired.

       This proposed measure addresses that regulatory gap by institutionalizing a
mandatory, automatic, and proportionate rebate mechanism in cases of Substantial Service
Degradation (SSD). Rather than placing the burden on subscribers, the bill requires
providers to proactively apply billing credits when objectively measurable service failures
occur.

       The measure establishes:

       • Clear, technical, and verifiable definitions of SSD based on speed delivery
         thresholds, latency, packet loss, signal quality, and other Quality of Service (QoS)
         parameters;
       • Objective performance monitoring and audit mechanisms;
       • Automatic rebate computation based on billable hours and verified degradation
         duration;
       • Transparency requirements for consumer disclosure;
       • Administrative enforcement mechanisms consistent with due process; and
       • Proportionate penalties for non-compliance.
       Importantly, the bill is carefully crafted to be constitutionally sound and regulatorily
balanced. It expressly preserves existing agency jurisdiction, respects franchise rights, and
ensures that regulatory measures are reasonable, proportionate, and non-confiscatory.
While scheduled maintenance and force majeure events do not exempt providers from
granting proportionate rebates for actual service unavailability, such circumstances may
mitigate administrative penalties where good faith and prompt remediation are shown.

        By linking measurable performance failures to automatic financial accountability,
the bill reduces information asymmetry between providers and consumers. It strengthens
transparency, enhances regulatory credibility, and incentivizes sustained network reliability
without imposing arbitrary or punitive burdens on industry players.

       Ultimately, this measure affirms a simple but fundamental principle: consumers
should pay only for services actually delivered at compliant standards. In doing so, it
advances consumer protection, promotes service quality, and reinforces public trust in the
nation’s communications infrastructure.

       Immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT MANDATING PUBLIC TELECOMMUNICATIONS ENTITIES AND
 INTERNET SERVICE PROVIDERS TO INSTITUTE A MANDATORY SYSTEM
   OF AUTOMATIC REFUND MECHANISM FOR SUBSTANTIAL SERVICE
  DEGRADATION IN TELECOMMUNICATIONS AND INTERNET SERVICES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Mandatory Automatic
Rebates for Substantial Service Degradation Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
consumers from being charged for communications services that are materially unusable or
substantially impaired, and to ensure that when measurable and verifiable service
degradation occurs, affected subscribers receive fair, prompt, proportionate, and automatic
rebates without the need for individual demand.

       The State likewise affirms its duty to establish objective, transparent, and
technically verifiable standards for evaluating service performance; to promote
accountability among public telecommunications entities and other covered service
providers; and to exercise its police power to regulate public services in the interest of
consumer protection pursuant to Article XII of the 1987 Constitution.

        In implementing this policy, regulatory measures shall be reasonable, proportionate,
non-confiscatory, and consistent with administrative due process, existing franchise rights,
and the broader public interest in maintaining reliable and resilient communications
infrastructure.

SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to any natural or juridical person engaged in
providing for a fee the public:

     (a) Telecommunications services; and

     (b) Fixed or mobile internet or broadband access services.
Covered entities shall include public telecommunications entities (PTEs), internet service
providers (ISPs), and other public services subject to regulation under the Public Service
Act, as amended, and existing telecommunications laws, rules, and regulations.

 Nothing in this Act shall be construed to enlarge, diminish, or otherwise modify the
existing regulatory jurisdiction of any government agency, nor to confer regulatory
authority beyond that expressly provided under applicable law.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:

     (a)​ Substantial Service Degradation (SSD) refers to a sustained and objectively
          verifiable failure of an internet access or telecommunications service to meet the
          applicable minimum service performance standards prescribed by the National
          Telecommunications Commission (NTC), other competent regulatory issuances,
          or the provider’s published and contracted service commitments, as validated
          through industry-accepted measurement methodologies.

          SSD shall be deemed to exist when any of the following conditions occur, other
          than during scheduled maintenance, force majeure, or subscriber-caused
          impairment:

           1.​ Delivery of less than eighty percent (80%) of the contracted or advertised
               minimum speed, capacity, or signal quality;

           2.​ Excessive Latency, Jitter, or Packet Loss – Network latency, jitter, or
               packet loss exceeds applicable NTC regulatory benchmarks or industry
               standards by more than fifty percent (50%) for a sustained period
               materially affecting ordinary use of the service;

           3.​ Signal Quality Degradation (Mobile Services) – Signal strength, call
               completion rate, dropped call rate, or data session success rate falls
               materially below NTC minimum quality-of-service standards in the
               subscriber’s registered service area.

     (b)​ Automatic Rebate refers to a mandatory refund or billing credit applied without
          the need for consumer request.

     (c)​ Covered Service Provider refers to entities under Section 3.

     (d)​ Network-Level Degradation refers to SSD affecting a geographic service area or
          statistically significant cluster of subscribers.

     (e)​ Subscriber-Level Degradation refers to SSD affecting an individual subscriber as
          confirmed through system logs, remote diagnostics, or accredited testing
          methodologies.

     (f)​ Scheduled Maintenance refers to service-affecting work with at least forty-eight
          (48) hours prior public notice specifying affected areas and duration.

     (g)​ Force Majeure refers to extraordinary events beyond reasonable control,
          including natural disasters, war, government-ordered shutdowns, or similar
          events.

     (h)​ Billable Hours refers to the total number of hours within a billing cycle for which
          a subscriber is charged service access.
SEC. 5. Performance Monitoring and Evaluation Mechanism. - To ensure objective
and verifiable assessment of service performance, all Covered Service Providers shall
maintain automated monitoring systems capable of accurately logging and recording speed,
uptime, latency, packet loss, signal quality, and service interruptions in accordance with
standards prescribed by the National Telecommunications Commission (NTC). Providers
shall retain all relevant performance logs and supporting data for a minimum period of two
(2) years and shall make such records available to the NTC upon lawful request for
purposes of audit, verification, or investigation. Covered Service Providers shall likewise
submit quarterly performance reports to the NTC containing, at a minimum, average
delivered speeds, uptime percentages, the frequency and duration of SSD incidents, the
geographic distribution of affected areas, and the total rebates granted within the reporting
period.

        The NTC is authorized to conduct random or targeted audits, require submission of
anonymized raw performance data, deploy independent testing tools, and validate reported
SSD occurrences using statistically reliable and representative methodologies. Verified
findings derived from system logs, independent audits, or NTC-validated data shall give
rise to a rebuttable presumption of the occurrence and duration of SSD, subject to the
presentation of contrary evidence by the Covered Service Provider. All regulatory actions
undertaken pursuant to this Section shall observe the requirements of administrative due
process and shall be supported by substantial evidence in accordance with existing law and
jurisprudence.

SEC. 6. Mandatory Automatic Rebates. - When SSD, as defined under this Act, occurs,
Covered Service Providers shall grant automatic rebates that are proportionate to the
duration and severity of the service impairment. Such rebates shall be applied without the
need for any request, complaint, or demand from the affected subscriber.

       In cases of recurring SSD within the same billing cycle, the duration of all
qualifying incidents shall be cumulative for purposes of computing the total rebate due. For
postpaid subscribers, the rebate shall be reflected as a billing credit in the next billing
statement; for prepaid subscribers, the corresponding credit shall be applied to the
subscriber’s balance within seven (7) days from verification or determination of the SSD.
Failure to grant the required automatic rebate within the prescribed period shall constitute
an administrative violation subject to enforcement under this Act.

       For purposes of entitlement to an automatic refund under this Section, SSD shall be
compensable when the degradation results in an aggregate duration of at least twenty-four
(24) hours within a billing month, whether arising from a single continuous event or from
multiple cumulative incidents.

SEC. 7. Computation of Rebates. - Rebates mandated under this Act shall be computed
on a proportionate basis according to the duration of the verified SSD within the applicable
billing cycle.

       The rebate shall be computed by dividing the monthly subscription fee by the total
number of billable hours in the billing cycle, and then multiplying the resulting hourly rate
by the number of hours during which SSD occurred.

       For purposes of uniformity and regulatory clarity, the National Telecommunications
Commission (NTC) shall prescribe detailed computational standards, including the method
of determining billable hours, rounding rules, treatment of partial hours, and application of
cumulative incidents within a billing cycle. The NTC may likewise provide for reasonable
percentage adjustments or multipliers in cases of repeated or aggravated SSD occurrences
within the same billing period, provided that such adjustments remain proportionate,
nonconfiscatory, and consistent with the remedial nature of the rebate.

SEC. 8. Scheduled Maintenance. – Substantial Service Degradation arising from
Scheduled Maintenance shall not exempt Covered Service Providers from the obligation to
grant automatic rebates corresponding to the actual duration of the SSD experienced by
subscribers.

        Scheduled Maintenance shall be recognized only where the provider has issued
prior public notice of at least forty-eight (48) hours specifying the affected geographic
areas, the nature of the work to be undertaken, and the expected duration thereof, and
where such maintenance is conducted within a reasonable and necessary timeframe.
Compliance with notice and reasonableness requirements may mitigate the imposition of
administrative penalties; however, it shall not extinguish the subscriber’s right to
proportionate rebate for actual SSD.

SEC. 9. Force Majeure. - In the event that the SSD results from a force majeure
occurrence, as defined under this Act, Covered Service Providers shall remain obligated to
grant automatic rebates corresponding to the actual duration of the SSD. However, the
existence of a duly established force majeure event may be considered by the National
Telecommunications Commission (NTC) in determining the imposition, extent, or
mitigation of administrative penalties, provided that the provider demonstrates prompt
remedial action, good faith efforts to restore compliant service within the shortest
practicable time, and absence of gross negligence or willful misconduct. The burden of
proving force majeure and the causal connection between the event and the SSD shall rest
upon the Covered Service Provider.

SEC. 10. Transparency and Consumer Disclosure. - Covered Service Providers shall
ensure transparency in service performance and rebate implementation by maintaining
realtime or regularly updated publicly accessible advisories identifying affected geographic
areas, the nature of the service impairment, and the estimated time of restoration.

        Providers shall itemize all automatic rebates in the corresponding billing statements
or electronic account records, clearly indicating the period of SSD and the amount credited.
They shall likewise establish and maintain consumer-accessible platforms or portals
through which subscribers may verify service performance data, degradation history, and
rebate credits applicable to their accounts. Failure to comply with the disclosure and
transparency requirements under this Section shall constitute a separate administrative
violation subject to enforcement under this Act.

SECTION 11. Administrative Enforcement and Penalties. - The National
Telecommunications Commission (NTC) shall exercise primary enforcement authority
under this Act pursuant to Executive Order No. 546, the Public Service Act, and other
applicable telecommunications laws. All enforcement actions shall be undertaken only
after due notice and hearing, consistent with administrative due process, and shall be
supported by substantial evidence reduced into written findings.

      In the exercise of its regulatory authority, the NTC may impose the following
administrative sanctions and corrective measures:
     (a)​ Monetary Administrative Fines. For violations of this Act or its implementing
          rules and regulations, the NTC may impose a fine of not less than Twenty
          Thousand Pesos (₱20,000.00) but not more than One Hundred Thousand Pesos
          (₱100,000.00) for each count of violation.

     (b)​ Corrective and Compliance Measures. The NTC may issue compliance
          directives, order refund verification audits, require public disclosure of
          violations, mandate remedial action plans, and temporarily suspend the authority
          of a Covered Service Provider to market or accept new subscriptions in affected
          service areas until compliance is certified.

     (c)​ Willful or Fraudulent Violations. In cases involving falsification, concealment,
          or deliberate manipulation of performance data, the NTC may impose the
          maximum allowable administrative fines, recommend franchise review to
          Congress where warranted, and institute proceedings for the suspension or
          revocation of certificates of public convenience or other operating authorities in
          accordance with existing law.

        No certificate, franchise, or operating authority shall be suspended or revoked
except in accordance with procedures prescribed by law. All penalties imposed under this
Act shall be reasonable, proportionate to the gravity of the violation, and consistent with
constitutional limitations on regulatory authority.

SEC. 12. Consumer Remedies. - The grant of automatic rebates under this Act shall be
without prejudice to the exercise by subscribers of any other rights or remedies available
under existing laws, rules, and regulations, including the filing of administrative
complaints before the appropriate regulatory agency and the institution of civil actions for
damages where warranted. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to limit or diminish
causes of action arising from contractual breach, fraud, negligence, or other violations of
law. The remedies provided herein are cumulative and shall operate in addition to, and not
in substitution for, other legal remedies recognized under Philippine law.

SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty (60) days from effectivity,
the NTC, in coordination with the Department of Information and Communications
Technology and relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations.

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. - All laws, rules, regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent
with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
