                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The increasing burden of end-stage organ failure and chronic diseases requiring
transplantation continues to pose a major public health challenge in the Philippines. Thousands
of Filipinos suffer annually from kidney failure, end-stage liver disease, serious lung and heart
disease, and other life-threatening illnesses for which organ transplantation remains the most
effective and often the only definitive treatment.

         Despite advances in transplantation medicine and surgery, the country continues to
face significant challenges in organ donation and transplantation, including very low organ
donation rates, inadequate registration systems, fragmented coordination among hospitals and
transplant centers, inequitable access to transplantation services, limited public awareness, and
insufficient regulatory oversight.

        While Republic Act No. 7170 or the “Organ Donation Act of 1991” provides the legal
basis for organ donation after death, there remains a need to institutionalize a comprehensive,
cohesive, integrated, ethical, and sustainable national organ donation and transplantation
system, as in several countries that have had successful national organ donation programs.

         The World Health Assembly, the World Health Organization and the Declaration of
Istanbul have repeatedly called upon member states to strengthen governance mechanisms for
organ donation and transplantation by promoting voluntary and unpaid donation, equitable
allocation systems, transparency, traceability, patient safety, and safeguards against organ
trafficking and transplant commercialism.

         This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize organ donation and transplantation
activities nationwide through the establishment of the National Organ Donation and
Transplantation Commission (NODTC), which shall serve as the primary policy-making,
coordinating, regulatory, and oversight body for organ donation and transplantation in the
Philippines. For brevity, the word organ shall refer to tissues as well.

3            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall protect and promote the right to
4    health of all Filipinos and to foster health consciousness. Toward this end, the State shall
5    establish and sustain a nationally coordinated, ethical, equitable, and transparent system for
6    organ and tissue donation that shall:
7            (a) Promote, uphold, and protect the dignity of organ donation and to achieve
8                excellence in organ transplantation through accreditation, transparency and
9                accountability;
10           (b) Increase the availability of organs and tissues through a coordinated system of
11               voluntary donation, including legacy and culturally grounded donations, while
12               ensuring that all donations, both from deceased and living donors, are made with
13               free, prior, informed, and voluntary consent, and with full respect for donor
14               autonomy within ethical limits;
15           (c) Prioritize medical need, clinical appropriateness, and urgency, in accordance with
16               organ-specific allocation criteria grounded on fairness, and non-discrimination.
17           (d) Uphold and protect the rights, dignity, and welfare of donors and recipients,
18               through independent donor evaluation, appropriate counselling, long-term medical

2           undue influence;
3        (e) Reinforce national self-sufficiency in organ donation and transplantation consistent
4            with WHO Guiding Principles, the Declaration of Istanbul and internationally
5            recognized ethical standards;
6        (f) Ensure accountability, traceability, transparency, win public trust through
7            mandatory reporting, quality assurance, and auditable national registries and
8            allocation processes, while upholding confidentiality and data protection;
 9       (g) Strengthen health system capacity by institutionalizing coordination among health

1       SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:
2    1. Certificate of Accreditation - a certificate issued by the HFSRB to Transplant
3       Facilities and tissue banks that have met the minimum standard requirements for the
4       purpose of organ transplantation and organ or tissue retrieval. In the case of tissues,
5       they should meet requirements for proper long-term tissue-specific storage.
6    2. Decedent - is a deceased individual and includes a stillborn infant or fetus.
 7   3. Death - is the irreversible cessation of circulatory and respiratory functions or the
 8      irreversible cessation of all functions of the entire brain, including the brain stem which
 9      is determined in accordance with the acceptable medical practice and diagnosed
10      independently by two (2) physicians both of whom must be appropriately qualified,
11      suitably experienced in the care of such patients and none of whom are members of the
12      transplant team. Further attempts at resuscitation or continued supportive
13      maintenance would not be successful in restoring the aforementioned natural functions.
14      The person is then deemed to be medically and legally dead
15   4. Graft - a vascularized organ or tissue that has been removed from the body of an organ
16      donor for transplantation into a recipient.
17   5. Health Facilities and Services Regulatory Bureau (HFSRB) – the bureau of
18      DOH in charge with enforcement of regulatory standards in all government and private
19      hospitals and other health facilities.
20   6. Hospital Transplant Candidate Waiting List - is the database of all potential
21      organ recipients of a particular transplant facility. This shall include the names of
22      transplant candidates who may or may not have identified living donors.
23   7. Legal Next-of-Kin - refers to the legal spouse, children of legal age, parents, and
24      siblings of legal age of the potential organ donor.
25   8. Living Donation- is the act of giving an organ, or a portion of an organ, by an
26      individual during his lifetime to a transplant candidate.
27                  a. Living directed donation - the organ donation is to a specific identified
28                     organ transplant candidate known to the donor volunteer, usually a
29                     relative.
30                  b. Living non-directed donation - the organ donation is to an individual not
31                     known to the donor volunteer, also known as stranger donation.
32   9. National Transplant Candidate Waiting List - is the national database of all
33      potential organ recipients. The candidates who shall be registered in this list shall be
34      enrolled by the DOH-accredited transplant facilities in the Philippines.
35   10. Organ Allocation - refers to the assignment of organ grafts to organ transplant
36       candidates who are listed in the National Transplant Candidate Waiting List, based on
37       organ-specific allocation scoring system.

 5           SEC. 13. National Transplant Ethics Committee (NTEC). - There is hereby
 6   established a National Transplant Ethics Committee (NTEC) under the Commission, which
 7   shall serve as the primary advisory and oversight body on ethical matters in organ and tissue
 8   donation and transplantation. The NTEC shall formulate national ethical standards and
 9   guidelines on organ donation and transplantation that will cover both the living donor and the
10   OTC, as well as the deceased donor and his/her family, aligned with the provisions of this Act.
11   These shall ensure the voluntariness of the donation and the protection of the rights, welfare
12   and safety of the organ donor, guided by the following principles:
13          a) The Philippines shall maintain a voluntary or opt-in system of organ donation and
14             actively promote it through information and education campaigns.
15          b) Living directed donation of relatives or emotionally related individuals, with proof
16             beyond doubt of the relationship, is allowed. Living non-directed donation is
17             absolutely prohibited.
18          c) All living donor organ transplants must undergo assessment and approval by the
19             appropriate authority prior to procedure.
20          d) Organ donation without prejudice to age, gender, race, creed or socioeconomic
21             status is allowed. A living legacy can be made by an individual at least 18 years of
22             age of sound mind with a good understanding of what it entails. Actual donation as
23             a living donor, however, may be executed only by those with the age of 21 years old
24             or above.

25          e) Organ donation, be it from living or deceased donors, should be totally gratuitous,
26             altruistic, free of coercion and commodification. Organ trade is prohibited. No
27             undue remuneration should be given to organ donors. Allowable reimbursements
28             should be reasonable and include costs directly related to the act of donation i.e.,
29             donor evaluation, actual operation and postop care.
30          f) Human organs are invaluable and no grafts retrieved from deceased donors must
31             be wasted. Filipino patients in the national transplant waiting lists take priority for
32             available organ grafts. Organs from deceased donors are assigned to OTCs based
33             on organ-specific allocation criteria. Foreign nationals may be enlisted in the local
34             national waiting lists provided that they understand and accept that they take last
35             priority. Grafts may be offered to foreigners on the waiting list if and only when
36             there are no qualified Filipino OTCs.
37          g) Organ donation for the purpose of organ transplantation, may be considered upon
38             determination and declaration of death in accordance with accepted medical
39             standards of practice.
