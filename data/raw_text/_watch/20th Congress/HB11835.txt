AN ACT

NATIONAL STANDARDS AND IMPLEMENTATION MECHANISMS

EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines faces a fundamental reality: natural hazards cannot be
prevented, but their impacts can be reduced through better planning, stronger
infrastructure, and reduced vulnerability.

The WorldRiskReport 2026 ranks the Philippines first in the
WorldRiskIndex, underscoring the country's exceptionally high exposure to
natural hazards and significant societal vulnerability. The WorldRiskReport 2025,
meanwhile, provides a more detailed analysis of flood risk in the Philippines and
highlights the importance of land use, infrastructure, natural retention areas,
wetlands, mangroves, and floodplains in reducing flood risk.

These findings reinforce a principle already embodied in the 1987
Constitution, which directs the State to protect and advance the people's right to
a balanced and healthful ecology and to protect and promote the right to health.
They are likewise consistent with the State's existing commitments under
Republic Act No. 9729, or the Climate Change Act of 2009, which requires
climate change to be integrated into government policies, plans, programs, and
development decisions, and recognizes the need to build national and local
resilience to climate-related disasters.

Similarly, Republic Act No. 10121, or the Philippine Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Act of 2010, requires a holistic, comprehensive,
integrated, and proactive approach to reducing disaster risks and addressing the
root causes of vulnerability. Republic Act No. 10174, which established the
People’s Survival Fund, further provides a long-term financing mechanism for
climate change adaptation, including activities involving water resources,
infrastructure, and natural ecosystems.

Yet these mandates must be translated more deliberately into the way
infrastructure is planned and built.


===== [page 2] =====
Blue and green infrastructure offers that bridge. Wetlands, mangroves,
riparian buffers, urban forests, parks, permeable surfaces, rain gardens,
bioswales, natural drainage systems, and other nature-based solutions can
retain and slow water, reduce runoff, mitigate heat, protect ecosystems, and
complement conventional drainage and flood-control infrastructure.

The proposed “Blue and Green Resilient Infrastructure Development and
Governance Act” or BRIDGE Act therefore institutionalizes blue and green
infrastructure as a national approach to climate-resilient and sustainable
development, integrating nature-based solutions and water-sensitive design into
infrastructure and development planning and establishing national standards and
implementation mechanisms suited to the diverse conditions of Philippine
communities.

The bill seeks to bridge conventional infrastructure with nature-based
solutions, infrastructure planning with ecological planning, and present
development needs with long-term climate and disaster resilience. It does not
seek to replace conventional infrastructure. Rather, it seeks to bridge engineered
and natural systems so that roads, drainage and flood-control structures, public
spaces, buildings, waterways, and ecological assets work together to reduce risk
and strengthen resilience.

For a country highly exposed to storms, floods, and other natural hazards,
resilience cannot begin when disaster strikes. It must begin in the way we plan,
design, and build.

This measure gives concrete expression to the constitutional mandate for
a balanced and healthful ecology and complements the country's existing
climate-change and disaster-risk reduction laws by making resilience and
ecological protection integral considerations in infrastructure development.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING BLUE AND GREEN INFRASTRUCTURE FOR
CLIMATE-RESILIENT AND SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT, INTEGRATING

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Blue and Green
Resilient Infrastructure Development and Governance (BRIDGE) Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to protect the right of the people to health and to a balanced and healthful
ecology, promote sustainable development, reduce disaster and climate-related
risks, protect communities and critical infrastructure, and ensure that
infrastructure and development are planned and implemented in harmony with
natural systems.

The State recognizes that flooding, stormwater, erosion, extreme heat, water
scarcity, ecosystem degradation, and other climate and environmental risks are
influenced not only by natural hazards but also by land-use patterns, the loss of
natural drainage and retention areas, watershed degradation, inadequate
stormwater management, and the manner in which infrastructure and
settlements are planned and developed.

The State shall therefore promote an integrated approach in which natural
systems, green spaces, waterways, wetlands, watersheds, forests, soils,
vegetation, and other ecological assets are recognized as essential components
of the country's infrastructure, water management, and resilience systems.

Towards this end, blue-green infrastructure and nature-based solutions shall
complement, and where appropriate be integrated with, conventional gray
infrastructure. The State shal! pursue infrastructure solutions that work with
natural processes wherever feasible, while employing engineered measures
where necessary to protect life, property, essential services, and public safety.

In the planning, design, and development of infrastructure and settlements,
the State shall give due priority to the avoidance and protection of natural

===== [page 4] =====
systems; the restoration and rehabilitation of degraded ecosystems; the
retention, detention, infiltration, absorption, and beneficial use of water; and the
sustainable management and conveyance of excess water, before resorting to
additional engineered interventions, consistent with site conditions, technical
feasibility, environmental integrity, life-cycle costs, and community needs.

The State shall build not only to withstand the hazards of today, but to
preserve the natural systems that will protect generations yet to come.
Infrastructure shall therefore be conceived not merely as structures that resist
nature, but as enduring systems that work with nature, strengthen communities,
safeguard the environment, and secure a more resilient and sustainable future
for ail Filipinos.

SEC. 3. Objectives. — This Act shall have the following objectives:

(a) Establish a national framework for the planning, development,
implementation, operation, maintenance, and monitoring of blue-green
infrastructure and nature-based flood management;

(b) Integrate nature-based solutions, water-sensitive design, and blue-green
infrastructure into national and local development, land-use, infrastructure,
climate adaptation, and disaster risk reduction planning;

(c) Reduce flood and stormwater risks while improving water retention,
infiltration, drainage, water quality, ecological integrity, and urban
environmental quality;

(d) Protect and restore natural systems that provide flood regulation, water
retention, groundwater recharge, erosion control, cooling, biodiversity, and other
ecosystem services;

(e) Promote infrastructure solutions that are technically sound,
environmentally appropriate, economically efficient, and resilient throughout
their life cycle;

(f) Strengthen coordination among national government agencies, local
government units, communities, the private sector, academe, and other
stakeholders;

(g) Develop national standards, technical guidelines, planning tools, data
systems, and performance indicators for blue-green infrastructure;

(h) Strengthen the capacity of national government agencies and local
government units to plan, finance, implement, operate, maintain, and monitor
blue-green infrastructure; and

(i) Establish mechanisms for sustained financing, research, innovation,
monitoring, accountability, and public participation in the implementation of this
Act.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

===== [page 5] =====
(a) Blue Infrastructure refers to natural, restored, enhanced, or engineered
water-related systems and features that manage, store, convey, infiltrate, filter,
or otherwise regulate water, including but not limited to rivers, streams,
waterways, wetlands, ponds, detention and retention areas, rain gardens,
bioswales, constructed wetlands, floodplain areas, drainage corridors, and water-
sensitive systems;

(b) Blue-Green Infrastructure refers to a network of interconnected natural,
restored, enhanced, or engineered systems that use water-related and
vegetated features to manage stormwater and flood risks, support ecological
functions, improve environmental quality, and provide social and climate
resilience benefits;

(c) Green Infrastructure refers to natural or vegetated systems and features
that provide environmental, hydrological, climate, or public-space functions,
including forests, urban trees, parks, greenways, green roofs, vegetated
corridors, rain gardens, permeable landscapes, wetlands, riparian vegetation,
and similar features;

(d) Gray Infrastructure refers to conventional engineered infrastructure
primarily constructed from concrete, steel, asphalt, masonry, pipes, channels,
levees, flood-control structures, pumping systems, and other engineered
materials or systems intended to provide drainage, flood protection, water
management, transportation, or other public infrastructure functions;

(e) Nature-Based Solutions refers to actions that protect, conserve, restore,
sustainably manage, or create natural or modified ecosystems to address
societal challenges while simultaneously providing human well-being, ecosystem,
resilience, and biodiversity benefits;

(f) Nature-Based Flood Management refers to the use of natural processes,
ecosystems, and nature-based solutions, alone or in combination with
engineered measures, to reduce flood hazards, slow, store, infiltrate, filter, or
safely convey water, and reduce exposure and vuinerability to flooding;

(g) Water-Sensitive Design refers to the planning and design of infrastructure
and development in a manner that recognizes water as an integral component of
the built and natural environment and seeks to manage rainfall and stormwater
close to where it falls through retention, detention, infiltration, reuse, treatment,
and controlled conveyance;

(h) Flood Risk refers to the potential adverse consequences arising from the
interaction of flood hazards with exposure and vulnerability of people, property,
infrastructure, economic activities, and ecosystems;

(i) Watershed or Catchment refers to an area of land from which surface
water drains to a common outlet, water body, river, stream, groundwater
system, or other receiving environment;

(j) Retention refers to the permanent or relatively long-term storage of water
within a site or system for purposes including infiltration, reuse, groundwater
recharge, ecological support, or other beneficial use;

===== [page 6] =====
(k) Detention refers to the temporary storage of stormwater or floodwater for
controlled release, infiltration, treatment, or other risk-reduction purposes;

(|) Infiltration refers to the movement of water from the surface into the soil
or subsurface;

(m) Natural Retention Area refers to a natural or restored area capable of
storing, absorbing, slowing, filtering, or otherwise regulating water, including
wetlands, floodplains, riparian areas, forests, soils, natural depressions, and
other similar areas; and

(n) Blue-Green Corridor refers to a connected system of waterways, riparian
areas, wetlands, parks, green spaces, trees, drainage corridors, and other
natural or designed features that perform complementary ecological,
hydrological, recreational, or climate-resilience functions.

SEC. 5. National Blue-Green Infrastructure Framework. — The Department of
Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), as the lead agency, shall formulate
a National Blue-Green Infrastructure Framework, hereinafter referred to as the
Framework, which shall establish the long-term national policy and institutional
framework for blue-green infrastructure and nature-based flood management.

The Framework shall provide the overarching principles, objectives,
standards, strategies, institutional arrangements, and policy directions for
integrating blue-green infrastructure, nature-based solutions, and water-
sensitive design into national and local development.

The Framework shall, among others:

(a) Establish national principles and policy directions for blue-green
infrastructure and nature-based flood management;

(b) Establish the blue-green infrastructure hierarchy and the principles
governing the appropriate integration of natural, blue-green, and gray
infrastructure;

(c) Establish national technical and performance standards, consistent with
site-specific conditions and applicable environmental, engineering, land-use,
water, and public safety Jaws;

(d) Provide criteria for the identification and protection of watersheds,
waterways, wetlands, floodplains, riparian areas, natural drainage systems,
natural retention areas, green spaces, and other ecological assets relevant to
flood and climate resilience;

(e) Establish principles for the integration of blue-green infrastructure into
infrastructure planning, land-use and human settlements planning, climate
change adaptation, disaster risk reduction and management, watershed
management, and local development planning;

===== [page 7] =====
(f) Establish the institutional roles and responsibilities of national government
agencies and local government units;

(g) Establish principles and standards for risk-informed project assessment,
investment prioritization, operation and maintenance, monitoring, evaluation,
and accountability;

(h) Provide a national framework for research, innovation, technology
development, data management, and capacity-building;

(i) Establish principles for sustainable financing and investment, including the
appropriate use of public resources, climate finance, official development
assistance, and other lawful financing mechanisms;

(j) Provide for meaningful public participation and stakeholder engagement,
and

(k) Establish the basis for phased, capacity-responsive, and risk-informed
implementation.

In formulating the Framework, the DENR shall coordinate with the
Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), Department of Human
Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD), Department of the Interior and
Local Government (DILG), Department of Economy, Planning, and Development
(DEPDev), Department of Science and Technology (DOST), Department of
Agriculture (DA), Climate Change Commission (CCC), Office of Civil Defense
(OCD), Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA), local government
units (LGUs), academe, professional organizations, civil society, the private
sector, and other relevant stakeholders.

The Framework shall be consistent with the Constitution and applicable laws
and shall complement existing national policies and frameworks on climate
change adaptation, disaster risk reduction and management, environmental
protection, water resources, land use, human settlements, public infrastructure,
and sustainable development.

The Framework shall be reviewed at least once every ten (10) years, or
earlier when necessary, to respond to significant changes in climate and disaster
risks, scientific knowledge, technology, national development priorities, or
applicable laws.

The Plan shall translate the policies, principles, standards, and strategies

SEC. 15. Blue-Green Infrastructure Hierarchy. — In planning and evaluating
infrastructure and development projects, the following hierarchy shall be
considered:

(a) Avoid and Protect — avoid unnecessary disturbance, obstruction, filling,
conversion, or degradation of natural drainage systems, wetlands, floodplains,
riparian areas, forests, natural retention areas, and other ecosystems that
provide water-regulation functions;

(b) Restore and Rehabilitate — restore degraded natural systems and
rehabilitate waterways, wetlands, riparian zones, watersheds, and other areas
capable of providing flood and climate-resilience functions;

(c) Retain, Detain, Infiltrate, and Absorb — maximize, where technically and
environmentally appropriate, the retention, detention, infiltration, absorption,
storage, reuse, and natural treatment of rainfall and stormwater at or near the
site where it falls;

(d) Manage and Convey — manage and safely convey excess water through
natural, restored, vegetated, or appropriately engineered drainage and
conveyance systems; and

(e) Engineer and Protect — employ gray or other engineered infrastructure
where necessary to address residual risks, protect life and property, maintain
essential services, or satisfy applicable engineering and safety requirements.

The foregoing hierarchy shall not require the exclusive use of any single
intervention. Appropriate combinations of blue, green, and gray infrastructure
may be employed based on site conditions, risk, feasibility, lifecycle cost, and
public safety.

SEC. 16. National Blue-Green Infrastructure Standards. — The DENR, in
coordination with the DPWH, DHSUD, DILG, DEPDev, DOST, CCC, OCD, DA,

The standards shall, as appropriate, address:

(a) Stormwater retention and detention;

(b) Infiltration and groundwater recharge;

(c) Natural drainage and floodplain functions;

(d) Protection and restoration of waterways and riparian areas;

(e) Tree preservation and urban vegetation;

(f} Green and open spaces;

(g) Permeable and water-sensitive surfaces;

(h) Water quality;

(i) Soil protection and erosion control;

(j) Biodiversity and ecological connectivity;

(k) Urban heat reduction;

(1) Flood risk reduction;

(m) Climate resilience;

(n) Accessibility, public safety, and community use;

(0) Life-cycle costs and maintenance requirements; and

(p) Site-specific environmental, geological, hydrological, climatic, and socio-
economic conditions.

The standards shall, whenever practicable, be performance-based and shall
not prescribe uniform physical requirements where different site conditions
require different solutions.

ecosystems;

(g) Downstream and cumulative impacts;

(h) Exposure of people, property, critical infrastructure, and essential
services;

(i) Availability and suitability of blue-green, gray, or hybrid solutions;
(j) Life-cycle costs, including operation and maintenance;
(k) Climate and disaster resilience; and

(1) Applicable environmental, engineering, land-use, water, and public safety
requirements.

SEC. 18. Integration into National Infrastructure Programs. — National
government agencies and government-owned or controlled entities undertaking
covered infrastructure projects shall integrate applicable blue-green

infrastructure measures during project planning, design, construction,
rehabilitation, operation, and maintenance.

Existing infrastructure may be assessed for retrofitting, rehabilitation, or
supplementation with blue-green infrastructure where technically feasible and
cost-effective.

No project shall be required to undergo substantial redesign solely for
purposes of this Act where such redesign would materially compromise public
safety, cause substantial and unjustified delay, or result in disproportionate cost,
as determined under the implementing rules and regulations.

SEC. 19. Loca/ Blue-Green Infrastructure Plans. — Within two (2) years from
the effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations, each province, city,
and municipality shall integrate blue-green infrastructure and nature-based flood
management measures, as appropriate to its circumstances and capacity, into
its applicable local development and land-use plans.

The !ocal framework shall identify:

(a) Flood and stormwater risk areas;

(b) Watersheds, waterways, wetlands, floodplains, riparian areas, and natural
retention areas;

(c) Existing blue-green and gray infrastructure;

===== [page 17] =====
(d) Priority areas for protection, restoration, retention, infiltration, drainage,
and engineered intervention;

(e) Priority projects and programs;

(f} Operation and maintenance responsibilities;
(g) Financing sources; and

(h) Monitoring indicators.

SEC. 20. Priority Areas. — Priority shall be given, subject to available
resources and risk-based assessment, to areas characterized by:

(a) Recurrent or significant flooding;

(b) Degraded or critical watersheds;

(c) Waterways and drainage systems subject to obstruction or degradation;
(d) Wetlands and floodplains providing natural water-retention functions;

(e) Highly urbanized areas with significant impervious surfaces or limited
green and open spaces;

(f) Areas exposed to extreme heat;
(g) Areas vulnerable to erosion, sedimentation, or slope instability;
(h) Areas containing critical water resources or ecosystems;

(i) Areas where infrastructure and settlement expansion may significantly
affect downstream flood risk; and

(j) Other areas identified through national or local risk assessment.

SEC. 21. Protection of Natural Drainage Systems and Floodplain Functions. —
National government agencies and LGUs shall, consistent with existing
environmental, water, land-use, and public safety laws, seek to avoid
unnecessary obstruction, narrowing, filling, alteration, or degradation of natural
drainage systems, waterways, wetlands, floodplains, riparian areas, and natural
retention areas.

Where alteration cannot reasonably be avoided because of overriding public
necessity, appropriate mitigation, restoration, rehabilitation, or other measures
required under applicable law shall be undertaken.

SEC. 22. Public Open Spaces and Government Properties. — National
government agencies and LGUs shall assess, where appropriate, the potential of
government-owned parks, open spaces, roadsides, medians, school grounds,
government compounds, drainage corridors, easements, and other public lands
to serve blue-green infrastructure functions.

===== [page 18] =====
Where feasible and consistent with their primary purpose, such areas may
incorporate trees, vegetation, permeable surfaces, rain gardens, bioswales,
retention or detention features, green corridors, and other appropriate
measures.

SEC. 23. Private Developments. — The DHSUD, in coordination with the
DENR, DPWH, DILG, and other concerned agencies, shall develop appropriate
guidelines for integrating blue-green infrastructure and water-sensitive design
into covered private development.

Such guidelines may include, as appropriate and proportionate to project
scale and risk:

(a) Stormwater management;

(b) Retention and detention facilities;

(c) Permeable surfaces;

(d) Preservation of natural drainage and retention areas,

(e) Tree and vegetation preservation;

(f) Green and open spaces;

(g) Rainwater harvesting and reuse;

(h) Erosion and sediment control; and

(i) Other site-specific blue-green measures.

SEC. 24. Blue-Green Corridors. — LGUs may establish blue-green corridors
connecting waterways, wetlands, riparian areas, parks, open spaces, forests,
urban trees, drainage corridors, and other natural or designed features.

Where a blue-green corridor crosses administrative boundaries, the
concerned LGUs shall coordinate planning, implementation, operation,

maintenance, and monitoring.

National government agencies may provide technical and financial assistance
for priority corridors identified under the Framework and Plan.

SEC. 25. National Blue-Green Infrastructure and Nature-Based Flood
Management Program. — There is hereby established a National Blue-Green
Infrastructure and Nature-Based Flood Management Program, hereinafter
referred to as the Program, as the principal implementation mechanism of this

Act.
The Program shall support, among others:

(a) Blue-green infrastructure in national public works;

===== [page 19] =====
(b) Local blue-green infrastructure projects;

(c) Restoration and rehabilitation of watersheds, wetlands, waterways,
floodplains, riparian areas, and other natural systems;

(d) Stormwater and flood management;

(e) Urban greening and heat-resilience measures;

(f) Blue-green corridors;

(g) Pilot and demonstration projects;

(h) Research, innovation, and technology development;

(i) Technical assistance and capacity-building; and

(j) Monitoring, evaluation, and knowledge management.

The Program shall be implemented through existing programs, projects,
mandates, and appropriations of concerned agencies to the extent practicable
and shall avoid duplication of existing government programs.

SEC. 26. Investment Prioritization. — The DEPDev, in coordination with the
Council and concerned agencies, shall develop appropriate criteria for prioritizing
major investments under the Program.

Such criteria shall consider, as appropriate:

(a) Flood, climate, and disaster risk;

(b) Population and critical assets exposed;

(c) Ecological and watershed significance;

(d) Expected risk-reduction and environmental benefits;

(e) Technical feasibility and project readiness;

(f) Life-cycle costs and operation and maintenance requirements,

(g) Social and economic benefits;

(h) Institutional capacity;

(i) Compatibility with national and local development priorities; and

(j) Availability and sustainability of financing.

SEC. 27. Financing. -- The implementation of this Act shall be financed from:

(a) Appropriations authorized in the annual General Appropriations Act;

(b) Existing appropriations and programs of concerned national government
agencies;

(c) Lawful appropriations and local sources of funds of LGUs, consistent with
applicable laws;

(d) Grants, official development assistance, climate finance, and other
external financing permitted by law;

(e) Private sector participation and other lawful financing arrangements; and

(f) Such other sources as may be authorized by law.

SEC. 28. Public-Private Partnerships. — Blue-green infrastructure projects
may be undertaken through public-private partnerships, subject to Republic Act
No. 11966, otherwise known as the “Public-Private Partnership Code of the
Philippines,” and its implementing rules and regulations, as well as other
applicable laws and regulations.

The PPP Center may provide technical assistance to implementing agencies
and LGUs in the preparation and development of eligible projects.

SEC. 29. Research, Innovation, and Technology. — The DOST, in coordination
with the DENR, DPWH, ERDB, academic institutions, research organizations, and
other concerned agencies, shall promote research and innovation on:

(a) Nature-based flood management;

(b) Water-sensitive design;

(c) Urban ecosystem management;

(d) Soil and groundwater systems;

(e) Hydrological and climate modeling;

(f) Green engineering and hybrid infrastructure;

(g) Low-cost and locally appropriate technologies;

(h) Monitoring and remote sensing;

(i) Infrastructure life-cycle assessment; and

(j) Other technologies relevant to blue-green infrastructure.

SEC. 30. Capacity Building and Technical Assistance. — The DENR, through
the ERDB and in coordination with concerned agencies, shall establish a

continuing capacity-building program for national government agencies and
LGUs.

The program shall cover, among others:

(a) Risk and watershed assessment;

(b) Blue-green infrastructure planning and design;

(c) Nature-based solutions;

(d) Water-sensitive design;

(e) Project appraisal and life-cycle costing;

(f) Operation and maintenance;

(g) Monitoring and evaluation;

(h) Data and geospatial technologies; and

SEC. 31. Operation and Maintenance. — No covered blue-green infrastructure
project shall be considered fully implemented without an appropriate operation
and maintenance plan.

The implementing agency or LGU shall identify the responsible office, funding
source, maintenance schedule, performance requirements, and monitoring

mechanism for each project.

Operation and maintenance requirements shall be incorporated into project
planning, budgeting, procurement, and implementation.

SEC. 32. National Blue-Green Infrastructure Database. — The DENR, through
the ERDB and in coordination with concerned agencies, shall establish and
maintain a National Blue-Green Infrastructure Database.

The Database shall, to the extent practicable, include:

(a) Existing and planned blue-green infrastructure;

(b) Watersheds, waterways, wetlands, floodplains, riparian areas, and natural
retention areas;

(c) Flood and climate risk information;

(d) Project locations and characteristics;
(e) Performance and monitoring data;

(f) Operation and maintenance information;

(g) Relevant research and technical standards; and

===== [page 22] =====
(h) Other information necessary for planning and implementation.

The Database shall be interoperable with relevant government information
systems, subject to data protection, national security, confidentiality, and other
applicable laws.

Public access shall be provided to appropriate non-sensitive information.

SEC. 33. Monitoring and Evaluation. ~ The DENR, through the ERDB and in
coordination with the Council, shall establish a national monitoring and
evaluation framework.

The framework shall, as appropriate, measure:

(a) Flood and stormwater risk reduction;

(b) Water retention and detention;

(c) Infiltration and groundwater recharge;

(d) Restoration or protection of natural drainage and retention areas;

(e) Tree cover and green-space improvement;

(f) Riparian and waterway condition;

(g) Water quality;

(h) Surface temperature and heat exposure;

(i) Ecological connectivity and biodiversity benefits;

(j) Infrastructure performance and resilience;

(k) Life-cycle costs and maintenance performance; and

(I) Other indicators prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations.

Monitoring shall focus on measurable outcomes and performance rather than
compliance based solely on the presence of blue-green infrastructure features.

(d) Progress against national performance indicators;

SEC. 35. Prohibited Acts. — Without prejudice to liability under existing laws,
it shall be unlawful for any responsible public officer or other person, in
connection with a project covered by this Act, to:

(a) Falsify or knowingly cause the falsification of monitoring, performance,
environmental, engineering, financial, or other reports required under this Act;

(b) Knowingly misrepresent compliance with applicable standards or project
requirements;

(c) Deliberately obstruct or prevent authorized monitoring, inspection, or
audit; or

(d) Divert or use funds specifically appropriated for the implementation of
this Act for purposes other than those authorized by law.

The penalties for violations under this Section shall be without prejudice to
applicable provisions of the Revised Penal Code, special penal laws, the
Government Procurement Reform Act and its successor laws, anti-graft laws,
auditing laws, and other applicable statutes.

SEC. 36. Phased Implementation. — The implementation of this Act shall be
undertaken in phases:

(a) Phase I — Institutionalization and Readiness. Within two (2) years from
the effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations, the Council and
concerned agencies shall establish the Framework and Plan, standards, technical
guidelines, database, monitoring framework, capacity-building mechanisms, and
priority project pipeline;

(b) Phase II — Integration and Scaling. During the succeeding three (3)
years, national government agencies and LGUs shall progressively integrate the
requirements of this Act into applicable planning, programming, project
development, and investment processes, subject to institutional and financial
capacity; and

(c) Phase III — Full Implementation and Review. Thereafter, the
requirements of this Act shall be fully implemented in accordance with the
Framework and Plan, national standards, and applicable laws, subject to periodic
review.

===== [page 24] =====
The implementing rules and regulations shail establish differentiated
compliance pathways for LGUs based on population, urbanization, hazard
exposure, institutional capacity, fiscal capacity, and other relevant factors.

SEC. 37. Pilot and Demonstration Projects. — The Council may identify and
support pilot and demonstration projects in representative geographic, climatic,
ecological, and urban settings.

Pilot projects shalt be evaluated for effectiveness, cost, scalability, operation
and maintenance requirements, community acceptance, and environmental and
resilience outcomes.

Lessons from pilot projects shall inform the periodic updating of national
standards, guidelines, and investment strategies.

SEC. 38. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty
(180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DENR, through the ERDB as
Secretariat and in coordination with the Council, shall promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of
this Act.

SEC. 39. Transitory Provisions. — Existing projects that have been
substantially completed prior to the effectivity of the implementing rules and
regulations shall not be required to undergo redesign solely to comply with this
Act.

Ongoing projects shall be assessed for feasible integration of blue-green
infrastructure measures, provided that such integration shail not cause
substantial delay, compromise public safety, violate existing contractual
obligations, or result in disproportionate cost.

New projects initiated after the applicable compliance date shall comply with
the requirements of this Act to the extent provided in the Framework and Plan,
standards, and implementing rules and regulations.

SEC. 40. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the _ initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the DENR and other concerned agencies, subject to applicable budgeting,
accounting, and auditing laws.

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation
of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 41. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SEC. 42. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, or other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
