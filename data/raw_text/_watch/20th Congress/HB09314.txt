                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to provide for the partial transfer of supervision over electric
cooperatives registered with the Cooperative Development Authority (CDA), where
their administrative and corporate oversight is assigned to CDA, while retaining in the
National Electrification Administration (NEA) its technical, developmental, and
specialized functions.

        Electric cooperatives continue to play a vital role in delivering electricity services
to millions of Filipinos, particularly in rural and underserved communities. Since the
enactment of Presidential Decree No. 269, as amended, the NEA has exercised broad
supervisory authority over electric cooperatives in support of the State’s rural
electrification program.

      Over time, many electric cooperatives have become duly registered cooperatives
under the CDA, thereby placing them under the cooperative principles, governance
standards, and regulatory framework established under Republic Act No. 9520,
otherwise known as the “Philippine Cooperative Code of 2008”.

       The existence of overlapping supervision has created regulatory inefficiencies,
administrative burdens, and governance ambiguities. As CDA-registered cooperatives,
these entities should primarily fall under the CDA with respect to cooperative
governance, elections, membership rights, and corporate administration. At the same
time, the NEA should continue to exercise its recognized expertise in electrification
planning, engineering standards, system development, financial rehabilitation, and
other technical matters necessary to ensure reliable electric service.

        This measure therefore seeks to harmonize the mandates of the CDA and NEA,
clarify their respective jurisdictions, strengthen cooperative autonomy, and improve
institutional efficiency while preserving the State’s commitment to rural electrification
and quality power service delivery.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                          AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE PARTIAL TRANSFER OF SUPERVISORY POWERS
OVER ELECTRIC COOPERATIVES TO THE COOPERATIVE DEVELOPMENT
AUTHORITY,  RETAINING   THE TECHNICAL AND SPECIALIZED
FUNCTIONS OF THE NATIONAL ELECTRIFICATION ADMINISTRATION,
AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 269, AS
AMENDED

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Electric Cooperative
Governance Rationalization Act.”

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote efficient, accountable, and responsive governance of electric cooperatives,
uphold the autonomy of cooperatives under the Constitution and existing laws,
ensure reliable electric service to consumers, and rationalize overlapping
governmental functions affecting electric cooperatives.

       SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all electric cooperatives duly
registered with the Cooperative Development Authority (CDA), without prejudice to
the powers of other agencies under existing laws.

     SEC. 4. Section 4-A of Presidential Decree (PD) No. 269, as amended by
Republic Act (RA) No. 10531, is hereby further amended to read as follows:

             “SEC. 4-A. Supervisory Powers of the NEA Over Electric
       Cooperatives. – In the exercise of its power of supervision over electric
       cooperatives, the NEA shall have the following powers:

                     “(a) issue orders, rules and regulations, motu proprio or

              referenda and other similar actions on all matters affecting the

       SEC. 5. Joint Regulatory Framework. – Within one hundred twenty (120) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the CDA and NEA shall jointly promulgate rules
defining their respective jurisdictions, coordination mechanisms, dispute resolution
procedures, and reporting systems to avoid duplication of functions.

      SEC. 6. Non-Impairment of Existing Obligations. – Nothing in this Act shall
impair existing loan agreements, contractual obligations, or liabilities of electric
cooperatives with the NEA, government financial institutions, or private entities.

       SEC 7. Separability Clause. – Should any part of this Act be declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions hereof that are not affected thereby
shall continue to be in full force and effect.

       SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations,
rules and regulations, and issuances, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette, or in any newspaper of general circulation.
