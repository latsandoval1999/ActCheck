                             Explanatory Note



      The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is one of the Philippines’ most
important ecological systems. Extending from Cagayan in the north to Quezon
in the south, it contains forests, watersheds, rivers, biodiversity corridors,
protected areas, and interconnected coastal ecosystems that provide essential
environmental services to millions of Filipinos. It serves as a natural defense
against typhoons, flooding, landslides, and other hazards, while sustaining
water resources, biodiversity, livelihoods, and climate resilience.

       Despite its immense ecological and national-security value, the Sierra
Madre continues to face threats from illegal logging, mining, wildlife
trafficking, land conversion, habitat fragmentation, and uncoordinated
development. Existing governmental mandates are distributed among
numerous national agencies, local government units, protected-area
management boards, and other institutions, resulting in fragmented decision-
making and difficulties in managing the mountain range as one
interconnected ecosystem.

      This proposed measure seeks to address these challenges by
establishing the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority (SMMRA) as the
lead range-wide policy, planning, coordinating, and regulatory body for the
protection, conservation, restoration, and sustainable development of the
Sierra Madre Mountain Range.

      The bill provides for the formulation of a 25-year Sierra Madre
Ecological Integrity and Sustainable Development Master Plan that will
establish science-based ecological limits, management zones, conservation
corridors, no-go areas, restoration priorities, climate-resilience measures,
sustainable livelihood strategies, and measurable long-term outcomes. It
adopts a landscape-scale and ridge-to-reef approach that recognizes the
ecological and hydrological connections among forests, watersheds, rivers,
lowlands, and coastal ecosystems.




                                                                              1
       The measure likewise introduces an innovative Sierra Madre Nature
Credit System designed to mobilize private-sector resources for verified
ecological protection, restoration, and stewardship. Covered enterprises that
materially benefit from Sierra Madre ecosystem services may be required to
purchase and retire nature credits, with revenues directed toward lawful
rights holders, Indigenous Peoples, local communities, registered project
developers, and ecosystem stewards based on verified performance and
existing tenure and customary rights.

      Importantly, the bill preserves the mandates of existing institutions.
The SMMRA will not replace the environmental and natural-resource
authorities of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources, the
powers of local governments, the Protected Area Management Boards, or the
rights and authority of Indigenous Peoples under the Indigenous Peoples’
Rights Act. Instead, it establishes mechanisms for harmonization, data
sharing, conformity review, and coordinated enforcement while maintaining
the permits, clearances, tenure instruments, and consent requirements
imposed by existing laws.

       The proposed Authority will also strengthen science-based monitoring
through ecosystem accounting, geospatial technologies, remote sensing,
digital systems, and a public Sierra Madre Nature Credit Registry. It will
establish safeguards for market integrity, independent validation and
verification, transparency, conflict-of-interest prevention, and accountability.

      The bill recognizes that environmental protection must be accompanied
by social protection. Accordingly, it provides for meaningful participation of
Indigenous Peoples and local communities, respect for customary laws and
Free, Prior and Informed Consent, sustainable livelihood opportunities, and
humane and lawful resettlement where relocation becomes necessary for
environmental protection, disaster risk reduction, or ecosystem restoration.

      The protection of the Sierra Madre is not merely an environmental
concern. It is a matter of national security, public safety, water security,
food security, biodiversity conservation, climate resilience, and
intergenerational equity. The State must therefore ensure that the economic
and development decisions affecting the mountain range recognize and
protect the ecological systems upon which present and future generations
depend.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                                  ARTICLE I
                             General Provisions
      SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Sierra Madre
Mountain Range Protection, Conservation, and Sustainable Development
Act.”

       Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. Consistent with Article II, Section 16 of
the 1987 Constitution, which mandates that the State shall protect and
advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology, it is
hereby declared the policy of the State to protect, conserve, restore, and
sustainably manage the Sierra Madre Mountain Range as a nationally
significant ecological, climate, water-security, and disaster-resilience system.
The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is one of Luzon’s principal ecological
systems. Its topography influences tropical-cyclone winds and rainfall. The
range includes the Northern Sierra Madre, which Republic Act No. 9125
recognizes as the widest remaining tropical rainforest in Luzon, together with
other forest, watershed, riverine, and coastal ecosystems, some of which are
fragmented or degraded. These ecosystems store and sequester carbon,
sustain biodiversity and ecological connectivity, and act as a regulator of
climate and water flow. Protecting the Sierra Madre is thus indispensable to
biodiversity conservation, climate resilience, water security, and disaster risk




                                                                              3
reduction. Their protection, restoration, and sustainable management are
therefore of national importance.

      To achieve these ends, the State shall adopt a science-based,
ecosystem-based, and landscape-scale approach to management, integrating
conservation, restoration, and sustainable development. This includes
applying ridge-to-reef planning, recognizing the ecological and hydrological
links among uplands, lowlands, rivers, and coastal areas, and using nature-
based solutions where appropriate to strengthen resilience.

       The State recognizes that degradation persists because existing
economic and governmental decision systems permit land conversion,
extraction, and conventional development to generate immediate and
identifiable financial returns, while ecological, climate, water, and disaster
costs are transferred to the public. Institutions, Indigenous Peoples, local
communities, and lawful tenure holders responsible for protection,
restoration, and long-term stewardship do not receive reliable revenues
proportionate to the ecosystem services they maintain. Fragmented authority
enables this misalignment and prevents ecological limits and economic
responsibilities from being applied consistently across the mountain range.

      Accordingly, the State shall: (a) establish binding ecological limits, no-
go areas, decision principles, and consistency requirements governing covered
plans, zoning ordinances, permits, projects, procurement, and public
investment; (b) account for the extent, condition, service flows, beneficiaries,
and economic contribution of Sierra Madre ecosystems in accordance with
Republic Act No. 11995, or the Philippine Ecosystem and Natural Capital
Accounting System Act; (c) require Covered Enterprises to satisfy Nature
Credit Purchase Obligations proportionate to their measured dependency on
and benefit from Sierra Madre ecosystem services; (d) require persons causing
environmental damage to avoid harm and fully restore affected ecosystems,
independently of and in addition to compliance with the Philippine
Environmental Impact Statement System; and (e) direct Sierra Madre Nature
Credit revenues to lawful rights holders, registered nature-project developers,
and ecosystem stewards in accordance with existing tenurial instruments,
customary rights, and verified performance.

      The avoidance hierarchy, precautionary principle, polluter-pays
principle, beneficiary-pays principle, ecological connectivity, intergenerational
equity, and the more protective applicable standard shall govern
implementation. No purchase, credit, or payment under this Act shall
authorize prohibited activity, excuse noncompliance with an Environmental




                                                                               4
Compliance Certificate, or compensate for harm to an irreplaceable ecosystem
that should have been avoided.

       The Authority established under this Act shall harmonize, interface,
and rationalize the mandates, programs, and functions of national agencies,
local government units, and other stakeholders, thereby ensuring a coherent,
integrated, and ecosystem-scale governance framework. All policies and
actions shall be evidence-based, incorporating the latest scientific data on
biodiversity, hydrology, and climate impacts. The State shall likewise
strengthen enforcement and penalties against illegal activities like illegal
logging, mining, wildlife trafficking, land conversion, and the like, to deter
destruction of this critical landscape.

      In line with Republic Act No. 8371, known as the Indigenous Peoples
Rights Act (IPRA) of 1997 and the Constitution, the State recognizes and
protects the rights of Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples
(ICCs/IPs) within the Sierra Madre. All management plans and projects shall
respect ancestral domains, recognize customary laws, and secure Free, Prior
and Informed Consent (FPIC) from affected communities. Indigenous and local
communities shall have meaningful participation in decision-making and
benefit-sharing, as stewards of the environment. Nothing in this Act shall
diminish the lawful powers of the DENR, LGUs, PAMBs, the NCIP, or
Indigenous governance institutions; such powers shall be exercised
consistently with the ecological floors, conflict rules, and decision procedures
established herein.

      Sec. 3. Scope and Coverage. This Act applies to the entire Sierra Madre
Mountain Range, the longitudinal mountain chain from Cagayan Province in
the north to Quezon Province in the south, including all existing and future
protected areas within it. It covers all forestlands, watersheds, river basins,
and coastal zones up to their tidal limits that lie within the range. Buffer
zones, ecological corridors, and ridge-to-reef linkages necessary to maintain
connectivity and integrity are included. All components of the National
Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS), as amended, within Sierra Madre
shall be governed by this integrated framework alongside their existing
management bodies.

                                  Article II
                    Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority

      Sec. 5. Creation of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority.
There is hereby created the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority
(SMMRA), hereinafter referred to as the Authority, as a government-owned or
-controlled corporation with an original charter under Republic Act No.
10149, vested with the corporate and regulatory powers expressly granted by
this Act. It shall be attached to the Office of the President for policy
coordination and administrative supervision, and shall be subject to the
oversight of the Governance Commission for GOCCs and other applicable
laws. For policy and technical alignment, the Authority shall closely
coordinate with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources
(DENR) and other relevant national agencies. The Authority shall be the lead
policy, planning, coordinating, and regulatory body, and the range-wide
ecological governance and nature-market authority, tasked with managing
the Sierra Madre landscape as a whole.

      Its principal mandate is to change the governmental and economic
decision systems affecting the mountain range by: (a) establishing and
enforcing the Sierra Madre Master Plan and binding ecological limits, and
administering the Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate process;
(b) compiling operational ecosystem, enterprise-dependency, and sector-
dependency accounts in coordination with the agencies responsible under
Republic Act No. 11995; (c) delineating and periodically updating Ecosystem-
Service Catchments and identifying the cities, municipalities, economic zones,
industrial estates, utility service areas, locators, and other beneficiary areas
within them; (d) establishing methodologies, safeguards, and integrity
requirements for Sierra Madre Nature Credits; (e) administering project and
developer registration, credit issuance, ownership, transfer, purchase,
retirement, and the Sierra Madre Nature Credit Registry; (f) determining and
enforcing Nature Credit Purchase Obligations of Covered Enterprises; (g)
assessing ecological loss and enforcing restoration performance; (h) enabling
private, community, Indigenous, and public project proponents to access the
standards, information, lawful tenure pathways, and regulated demand
needed to finance protection, restoration, and long-term stewardship; and (i)
integrating the exercise of the lawful mandates of the DENR, LGUs, PAMBs,
the NCIP, and other institutions through the decision rules and procedures
established by this Act.

     The Authority shall not originate, sponsor, design, develop, construct,
implement, own, operate, manage, finance, invest in, underwrite, or
guarantee any nature, restoration, infrastructure, or other project that is




                                                                             11
eligible or expected to seek registration, Sierra Madre Nature Credits, a Sierra
Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate, or another decision from the
Authority. It shall not act as project proponent, developer, investment adviser,
arranger, broker, trader, buyer, or beneficial owner of Sierra Madre Nature
Credits.

      In carrying out these functions, the Authority shall observe the non-
duplication safeguard in Section 14 and the functional delineation with the
DENR in Section 68. Ecosystem accounting shall inform decisions under this
Act but shall not, by itself, create ownership, damages, a saleable unit, or a
compliance obligation.

      The capital stock of the Authority shall be subscribed and paid for solely
by the National Government as provided in Section 22 of this Act, and no
share shall be issued to, or held by, a private person. Its property, borrowing,
compensation, budget, audit, legal-service, and tax treatment shall be
governed by this Act and the laws applicable to a government-owned or -
controlled corporation with an original charter. Nothing in this Act grants a
tax exemption, sovereign guarantee, or borrowing authority beyond that
expressly approved under applicable fiscal and debt laws.

      Conformity Certificate within twenty (20) working days, unless
      another period is expressly fixed by this Act. It shall notify the
      applicant once, within five (5) working days, of any material
      deficiency. Failure to act shall not constitute approval, but the
      applicant may seek expedited administrative review. A Nature
      Credit Compliance Clearance involving no disputed assessment
      shall be issued within five (5) working days after Registry-confirmed
      retirement. No permit gate shall cause abrupt interruption of
      potable water, electricity, health, public transport, or another
      essential service while a timely administrative review is pending;
      the Authority may issue a conditional clearance with security and
      a compliance schedule without extinguishing the underlying
      obligation.

       Sec. 7. Resettlement and Relocation. – The Authority shall, in
coordination with the DHSUD, concerned local government units (LGUs), the
National Housing Authority (NHA), the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD), the NCIP, and other concerned agencies, formulate and
implement policies and programs for the humane, orderly, and sustainable
resettlement or relocation of qualified occupants within areas covered by this
Act, whenever such resettlement or relocation is necessary for environmental
protection, disaster risk reduction, public safety, ecosystem restoration, or
the implementation of approved management plans.

      Any resettlement or relocation undertaken pursuant to this Act shall:

         (a) Be carried out only after adequate consultation with the affected
             persons and communities, and, where applicable, with the Free,
             Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC) of ICCs/IPs in accordance
             with RA 8371, otherwise known as the Indigenous Peoples' Rights

         (b) Comply with Republic Act No. 7279, otherwise known as the
             Urban Development and Housing Act of 1992, as amended,
             Republic Act No. 7586, as amended by Republic Act No. 11038,
             and other applicable laws, rules, and regulations;
         (c) Ensure the provision of adequate relocation sites, access to basic
             services, livelihood opportunities, and other appropriate social
             support measures; and
         (d) Respect the rights of legitimate occupants, ICCs/IPs, and other
             affected stakeholders, and shall not result in arbitrary or forced
             eviction.

     Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize the Authority to
undertake eviction or demolition except in accordance with existing laws and
upon compliance with the requirements of due process.

       Sec. 8. Powers and Functions. The Authority shall exercise the
following powers and functions in a coordinated, science-based, ecosystem-
wide manner:




                                                                            16
(a) Sierra Madre Master Plan. Formulate, adopt, implement, and
    periodically update the Sierra Madre Master Plan, a
    comprehensive 25-year blueprint for conservation and
    sustainable development across the mountain range. This
    plan shall integrate land use, biodiversity conservation,
    watershed and climate resilience strategies, and sustainable
    livelihoods. It shall align with existing laws such as NIPAS and
    international commitments. The plan shall be reviewed at
    least every three years to incorporate new scientific data and
    shall be updated, in case of fortuitous events, and to address
    emerging challenges. The Plan shall include binding ecological
    limits, no-go areas, management zones, measurable five-, ten-
    , and twenty-five-year outcomes, an investment plan,
    ecosystem accounts, sector-dependency accounts, and rules
    for generating and verifying ecological outcomes and issuing
    Sierra Madre Nature Credits;

(b) Policy, Plan, and Decision Alignment. Harmonize, interface,
    and integrate national, regional, and local land-use and
    development plans affecting the Sierra Madre. This includes
    aligning local Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs),
    Regional Development Plans, Ancestral Domain Sustainable
    Development and Protection Plan (ADSDPP) and sectoral plans
    with the Sierra Madre Master Plan. All government entities
    and LGUs shall, within their authorities, ensure consistency
    of their projects and ordinances with the Sierra Madre Master
    Plan, to prevent fragmentation of policies and achieve an
    integrated ridge-to-reef management approach; Provided, That
    the Authority shall issue Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity
    Certificates for covered plans, zoning actions, projects,
    procurement, and public investments, each supported by
    written findings; Provided, further, That no such certificate
    shall replace any ECC, permit, PAMB approval, FPIC, or other
    consent required by law;




                                                                 17
(c) Ecological Standards, Accounts, and Monitoring.
    Establish and periodically update range-wide ecological floors
    and ecological standards and science-based benchmarks for
    biodiversity conservation, forest cover, watershed health, and
    climate resilience. Develop an environmental monitoring
    system using the most advanced innovative technologies like
    remote sensing/geographic information system (RS/GIS) and
    Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), Automated Weather
    Stations (AWS), among others, to track these indicators.
    Compile ecosystem extent, condition, service-supply, service-
    use,    beneficiary,   economic-contribution,    avoided-loss,
    degradation, and restoration accounts in coordination with
    the PSA, the DENR, and other agencies responsible under
    Republic Act No. 11995. Publish regular assessments of the
    range’s Health Index, together with the baselines, targets, and
    results, to inform adaptive management;

(d) Ecosystem Connectivity and Conservation Corridors. The
    Authority shall identify, delineate, establish, and maintain
    interconnected ecological networks across the Sierra Madre
    Mountain Range to ensure genetic flow, species movement,
    and long-term ecosystem resilience. Such networks shall
    include, but not be limited to:

   1.   Biodiversity Corridors. Landscape-scale linkages
        connecting protected areas, forest fragments, and critical
        habitats to enable wildlife movement and ecological
        processes;
   2.   Riparian Buffers. Protected strips along rivers, streams,
        and water bodies to maintain hydrological integrity,
        prevent erosion, and support aquatic and terrestrial
        biodiversity;
   3.   Key Biodiversity Areas (KBAs). Sites of global or
        national significance for biodiversity conservation, which
        shall be prioritized for strict protection and integration
        into corridor planning;
   4.   Buffer Zones. Areas surrounding protected areas and
        critical habitats, managed to provide an added layer of
        protection against external pressures and incompatible
        land uses;




                                                                18
   5.  Local Conservation Areas (LCAs). Areas designated by
       local government units for biodiversity conservation and
       sustainable resource management, consistent with
       national standards; and
   6. Indigenous Peoples’ and Community Conserved Areas
       (ICCAs). Territories conserved and managed by
       Indigenous Peoples and local communities, recognized in
       accordance with customary laws, governance systems,
       and applicable national policies.
            The Authority is hereby empowered to declare and
     classify such corridors and connectivity systems as
     Protected Critical Areas, subject to appropriate
     management regimes, use restrictions, and conservation
     standards.

(e) Enforcement Coordination. Lead and coordinate
    enforcement of environmental and natural resource laws
    within the Sierra Madre. In partnership and collaboration
    with the DENR, Philippine National Police (PNP), Armed
    Forces, and other agencies, plan and execute intelligence-
    driven operations against illegal logging, mining, wildlife
    trafficking, and destructive land conversion. The Authority
    shall have no permitting authority but shall support
    enforcement by providing information, logistical support, and
    unified command as needed;

(f) Climate Change, Disaster Risk Reduction, and Nature-
    Based Solutions. The Authority shall develop, integrate, and
    implement science-based and climate-resilient programs for
    climate change adaptation and mitigation, and disaster risk
    reduction and management, tailored to the Sierra Madre
    Mountain Range;




                                                               19
          Provided, That such programs shall prioritize watershed
   protection and rehabilitation for flood attenuation and water
   security; the application of soil and water conservation
   measures, including but not limited to slope stabilization,
   erosion control, and assisted natural regeneration, to reduce
   landslide risks; and the adoption of nature-based solutions
   (NbS), such as restoration using native species, mangrove
   restoration in coastal zones, and the protection of wetlands

   hydrological flows, sequester carbon, and enhance ecosystem

(g) Nature-Market Administration and Enabling Conditions.
    The Authority shall mobilize, generate, and manage funds to
    support     conservation,    restoration,  and    sustainable
    development initiatives within the Sierra Madre Mountain
    Range, including the establishment of a Sierra Madre Fund

    operationalize innovative financing mechanisms, including

    private partnerships, to augment available resources;

    provisions     for  equitable    community    benefit-sharing,

    administer the Sierra Madre Nature Credit System; approve
    methodologies; register projects and developers; determine
    Covered Enterprise classes and Nature Credit Purchase
    Obligations; publish priority outcome needs and model long-
    term nature credit purchase agreements; and ensure
    transparent, auditable, and tenure-based allocation of
    revenues. The Authority shall enable, but shall not develop,
    finance, invest in, own, implement, or guarantee, nature-
    credit or infrastructure projects;

(h) Data Management and Public Registry. Establish and
    maintain a centralized Sierra Madre Data and Monitoring

    restoration orders, payments, ownership, and benefit
    allocation, subject to lawful protection of sensitive ecological,
    personal, and Indigenous knowledge;

(i) Sustainable Livelihoods. Promote sustainable livelihood

(j) Capacity-Building and Technical Support. Assist Protected
    Area Management Boards, LGUs, and community groups by
    providing training in conservation planning, landscape
    monitoring, and enforcement techniques. Facilitate exchanges
    of best practices and science-based management protocols;

(k) Integration of Indigenous Knowledge. Ensure that
    traditional ecological knowledge and customary governance
    systems of ICCs/IPs are integrated into conservation
    strategies. The Authority shall recognize ICCAs as integral
    parts of the landscape, and support indigenous co-
    management models consistent with IPRA;

(l) Sector Dependency and Compliance. Determine, after notice

    enterprises materially dependent on Sierra Madre ecosystem

(m)   Restoration       Orders     and     Environmental      Cost
   Assessment. After notice, opportunity to be heard, and a

       Sec. 9. Emergency Environmental Management. – Whenever the
President determines that conditions within the Sierra Madre Mountain
Range constitute an environmental emergency or ecological crisis requiring
immediate and coordinated government intervention, the President may,
upon the recommendation of the Authority, direct all departments, bureaus,
offices, agencies, instrumentalities of the Government, including government-
owned or -controlled corporations, to undertake such measures as may be
necessary and lawful to protect, rehabilitate, restore, and secure the Sierra
Madre Mountain Range, consistent with the Constitution and existing laws.

       The Authority shall act as the principal coordinating body in
implementing such presidential directives and shall ensure that all actions
are science-based, transparent, proportionate, time-bound, and respectful of
the rights of affected communities, including ICCs/IPs;

      Sec. 10. Sierra Madre as Critical Natural Foundation for National
Security. The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is hereby declared a critical
natural foundation essential to national security, disaster risk reduction, and
climate resilience.

       Recognizing its function as one of the country’s longest natural barrier
against typhoons, storm surges, flooding, and landslides, the State shall
prioritize its protection as a matter of national survival and public safety.
       Accordingly:

      a. All policies, plans, and programs affecting the Sierra Madre shall
         consider national security implications, particularly in relation to
         disaster risk reduction and protection of life, property, and critical
         foundation;




                                                                            23
b. The Authority shall coordinate with the National Disaster Risk
   Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC), Department of
   National Defense (DND), and other relevant agencies to integrate
   ecosystem-based disaster risk reduction strategies into national
   security planning;

c. Any activity that significantly weakens the ecological integrity of the
   Sierra Madre and increases disaster vulnerability shall be deemed
   contrary to national security and may be subject to prohibition or
   heightened regulatory scrutiny under this Act.

       Sec. 14. Delineation of Powers and Functions. The Authority shall
operate as a coordinative and integrative body, and shall not duplicate or
supersede the existing mandates of national agencies, LGUs, Protected Area
Management Boards (PAMBs), or Environmental Impact Committees (EICs)
established under existing laws, rules, and regulations. It shall not issue an
ECC, forestland tenure instrument, protected-area permit, NCIP certification,
FPIC, CLUP, zoning ordinance, local business permit, or other permit, license,
or clearance that is under the legal jurisdiction of the DENR, Environmental
Management Bureau (EMB), other agencies, or local government units. It
shall not exercise customary governance or determine ownership of ancestral
domains. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish, modify, or
supplant the Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) System and the
functions of the Environmental Impact Committee (EIC) in the evaluation of
projects requiring an Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC). Likewise,
it shall not override LGU autonomy, except to the extent necessary to enforce
consistency with national environmental laws and the Sierra Madre Master
Plan. Instead, the Authority’s role is to harmonize policies, set scientific
standards, integrate plans, provide technical and scientific inputs, where
appropriate, to the EIA process, and coordinate enforcement across
jurisdictions, thereby enhancing a unified, ecosystem-based management of
the Sierra Madre Mountain Range.

      The Authority shall, however, exclusively exercise the range-wide
functions created by this Act, including adoption and enforcement of the
Sierra Madre Master Plan and ecological floors; issuance of Sierra Madre
Master Plan Conformity Certificates; operational ecosystem and sector-
dependency accounting; nature-market methodologies and safeguards;
project and developer registration; issuance and retirement of Sierra Madre
Nature Credits; assessment of Nature Credit Purchase Obligations;
maintenance of the Sierra Madre Nature Credit Registry; ecological-loss and
restoration assessment; and market-integrity enforcement.

      To preserve the independence of these functions, the Authority shall
not develop, implement, construct, finance, invest in, own, or manage a
project over which it may exercise registration, certification, credit-issuance,
compliance, restoration, or enforcement powers. It shall not receive project




                                                                             31
revenue, investment return, or Sierra Madre Nature Credits from such a
project. Public agencies may continue to implement projects under their own
mandates, but the Authority shall apply the same registration, certification,
and issuance rules to them as to other proponents.

       Existing agencies, LGUs, and PAMBs shall exercise their respective
powers subject to these requirements as additional statutory conditions. A
permit or tenure issued by another institution shall not be deemed a Sierra
Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate or market registration. Conversely,
a Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate or project registration shall
not constitute the underlying permit, tenure, consent, or FPIC.

      Disagreements shall be resolved through the conflict rules in Section
25. Nothing in this Act shall diminish Indigenous Peoples' rights, PAMB
powers under E-NIPAS, the DENR's ECC authority, lawful tenure, or due
process. The more protective applicable ecological standard shall govern
where requirements overlap.

                                  Article V
                          Sierra Madre Master Plan

       Sec. 24. Formulation and Adoption. Within one (1) year from the
effectivity of this Act, the Authority shall formulate, adopt, and commence the
implementation of a Sierra Madre Master Plan covering a period of twenty-five
(25) years. The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall serve as the comprehensive,
science-based, ecosystem-oriented, and climate-resilient blueprint for the
conservation, protection, restoration, and sustainable development of the
Sierra Madre Mountain Range.

       The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall integrate, among others, land use
and development planning; biodiversity conservation; watershed and ridge-
to-reef ecosystem management; climate change adaptation and mitigation;
disaster risk reduction and management; sustainable livelihoods;
infrastructure compatibility; and the traditional ecological knowledge,
customary governance systems, and sustainable resource management
practices of ICCs/IPs, consistent with RA 8371, otherwise known as the
"Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997." The Authority shall likewise
recognize ICCAs and support indigenous co-management approaches
consistent with applicable laws.

      The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall harmonize and integrate national,
regional, and local development and land use plans affecting the Sierra Madre
Mountain Range, including Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs), Regional
Development Plans (RDPs), ADSDPPs, protected area management plans, and
other relevant sectoral plans, to ensure policy coherence and an integrated
ridge-to-reef management approach.




                                                                               39
      The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall establish a Landscape Zoning
System, which shall include, but not be limited to, strict protection zones,
sustainable use zones, ecological restoration zones, climate resilience zones,
disaster risk and hazard zones, biodiversity corridors, and other appropriate
management zones necessary to guide conservation, development, and
resource use throughout the Sierra Madre Mountain Range.

      The Landscape Zoning System shall use the following statutory classes:
(1) Strict Protection and Irreplaceable Ecosystem Zones; (2) Ecological
Corridor and Connectivity Zones; (3) Watershed and Catchment Protection
Zones; (4) Restoration and Recovery Zones; (5) Sustainable Production and
Community Stewardship Zones; and (6) Existing Settlement and Compatible
Development Zones. the Sierra Madre Master Plan may create subzones only
within these classes and for the purposes stated in this Act.

       A no-go designation may be made only for primary or old-growth forest,
critical habitat, an irreplaceable ecosystem, a legally protected strict-
protection zone, a critical water-source area, an ecological corridor whose loss
would materially sever connectivity, or an area where credible evidence shows
that the proposed class of activity presents a serious risk of irreversible
ecological harm that cannot be avoided through location or design. Each
designation shall identify the legal and scientific basis, map the affected
parcels, address existing lawful rights and uses, and follow the notice,
consultation, review, transition, and compensation requirements of this Act
and other applicable law.

The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall likewise contain: (a) ecosystem extent and
condition accounts; (b) ecosystem-service supply and use accounts; (c) a
separately delineated Ecosystem-Service Catchment for each material service,
showing the source ecosystem, service-flow pathway, beneficiary area, and
geographic basis used; (d) maps and schedules identifying the cities,
municipalities, economic zones, industrial estates, utility service areas,
industries, locators, infrastructure systems, supply chains, and communities
within each catchment that receive or depend on the service; (e) sector-
dependency and economic-contribution accounts, including revenue
exposure, avoided loss and replacement, risk-mitigation, and business-
continuity costs; (f) quantified ecological baselines and five-, ten-, and twenty-
five-year outcomes; (g) the protection, restoration, and connectivity
investment required to achieve those outcomes; and (h) the nature-credit
market, public-finance, and accountability arrangements for delivery.
Catchment boundaries and beneficiary schedules shall state their scientific
basis, data sources, and date of validity, shall be subject to consultation with
affected LGUs and sectors, and shall be reviewed at least every five (5) years.




                                                                               40
       The initial geographic coverage shall be shown in an official
georeferenced map attached to this Act as Annex A. It shall include the Sierra
Madre mountain system and the watersheds, ecological corridors, and source
ecosystems physically connected to it. A beneficiary city, municipality, or
business location outside the mountain boundary may be included in an
Ecosystem-Service Catchment only for beneficiary accounting and Nature
Credit Purchase Obligation assessment; such inclusion shall not, by itself,
subject all land within that locality to Sierra Madre land-use zoning. A
boundary may be corrected for technical mapping error after public notice
and consultation, but a material expansion of the mountain-range coverage
shall require an Act of Congress.

       In formulating the Sierra Madre Master Plan, the Authority shall build
upon existing management plans prepared under the NIPAS, as amended,
utilize the best available scientific information and ecological data, and
conduct meaningful consultations with concerned national government
agencies, local government units, FICCs/IPs, local communities, the
academe, civil society organizations, and other stakeholders.

       The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall expressly identify binding ecological
limits, no-go areas, permissible uses, restoration priorities, and decision
rules. Harmonization shall be governed by ecological integrity, precaution,
avoidance before mitigation, polluter pays, beneficiary pays, connectivity,
intergenerational equity, the more protective applicable standard, and respect
for ancestral domains, customary law, tenurial rights, and FPIC.

      The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall be reviewed at least once every three
(3) years and may be updated earlier, whenever necessary, to incorporate
advances in scientific knowledge, respond to fortuitous events and
environmental emergencies, address emerging threats, or reflect significant
changes in ecological, climatic, or socio-economic conditions.

     Sec. 26. Components of the Sierra Madre Master Plan. The Sierra
Madre Master Plan shall include, at minimum, the following components:

    (a) Landscape Zoning System. – A comprehensive spatial classification
        and management framework for the Sierra Madre Mountain Range,
        developed using the best available scientific information, ecological
        assessments, hazard mapping, and Indigenous and local knowledge,
        which shall include, but not be limited to:

        1. Strict Protection Zones (SPZs). Areas of critical ecological
           significance, including primary and old-growth forests, critical
           watersheds, habitats of threatened, endemic, and migratory
           species,    Key    Biodiversity   Areas    (KBAs),    and    other
           environmentally sensitive areas, where human activities shall be
           strictly regulated or prohibited except for scientific research,
           biodiversity monitoring, habitat restoration, and other activities
           expressly authorized under existing laws. Restoration Zones
           and Disaster Risk Zones may be designated within Strict
           Protection Zones, where necessary, to facilitate ecosystem
           rehabilitation, assisted natural regeneration, slope stabilization,
           erosion control, watershed rehabilitation, and other measures
           essential for biodiversity conservation, climate resilience, and
           disaster risk reduction.




                                                                             44
          Any establishment, modification, or adjustment of the
    boundaries of Strict Protection Zones shall be supported by
    scientific evidence, stakeholder consultations, and the
    recommendation of the Authority, and shall be undertaken only
    to strengthen the conservation, protection, restoration, and
    sustainable management of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range,
    consistent with existing laws.

2. Sustainable Use Zones. Areas where environmentally
   sustainable and regulated economic and community activities
   may be undertaken, including but not limited to agroforestry,
   sustainable agriculture, ecotourism, non-timber forest product
   enterprises, recreation, traditional and cultural uses, settlement
   areas recognized under existing laws, and other special uses
   compatible with the ecological integrity of the Sierra Madre
   Mountain Range. Within ancestral domains, such activities shall
   be consistent with the applicable ADSDPPs, customary laws, and
   the rights of ICCs/IPs under RA 8371, otherwise known as the
   "Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997."

3. Buffer Zones. Areas surrounding protected areas, critical
   habitats, biodiversity corridors, and other environmentally
   sensitive areas that shall be managed to minimize adverse
   impacts from adjacent land uses, enhance ecological
   connectivity, and provide an additional layer of protection for
   core conservation areas.

4. Special Management Zones. Areas requiring specific
   management interventions due to unique ecological, cultural,
   socio-economic, or disaster risk considerations, including but
   not limited to multiple-use areas, ecological restoration sites,
   hazard-prone areas, cultural landscapes, and other areas
   identified by the Authority.




                                                                  45
(b) Watershed and Ridge-to-Reef Management. An integrated
    watershed and ridge-to-reef management strategy covering
    upland forests, watersheds, river systems, wetlands, estuaries,
    mangroves, and coastal ecosystems to maintain ecological
    connectivity, water security, and biodiversity. Such strategy shall
    include     measures    for   watershed rehabilitation, slope
    stabilization, soil and water conservation, erosion control, flood
    mitigation, landslide risk reduction, and the protection and
    restoration of downstream ecosystems.

(c) Biodiversity Corridors and Ecological Connectivity. The
    identification, establishment, protection, and management of
    biodiversity corridors, Key Biodiversity Areas (KBAs), riparian
    corridors, wildlife movement corridors, and other ecological
    networks necessary to maintain genetic exchange, species
    movement, ecosystem functions, and long-term landscape
    resilience.

(d) Climate Change Adaptation, Disaster Risk Reduction, and
    Nature-Based Solutions. Strategies integrating climate change
    adaptation and mitigation, disaster risk reduction and
    management, ecosystem-based adaptation, and nature-based
    solutions, including forest and watershed restoration, carbon
    sequestration, green infrastructure, natural flood management,
    mangrove rehabilitation, and the application of climate science in
    planning and decision-making.

(e) Sustainable Livelihood and Community Development.
    Programs promoting environmentally sustainable livelihoods,
    including agroforestry, ecotourism, sustainable agriculture, non-
    timber forest product enterprises, ecosystem restoration
    employment, conservation incentives, and other nature-positive
    economic activities. Such programs shall prioritize ICCs/IPs,
    upland communities, local resource-dependent communities,
    and other qualified beneficiaries through capacity-building,
    access to finance, technology transfer, market development, and
    enterprise support.




                                                                    46
(f) Transfer of Development Rights (TDR). A policy framework for
    the    voluntary   transfer    of   development      rights    from
    environmentally critical or ecologically sensitive areas to
    appropriate receiving areas, subject to applicable laws, local land
    use plans, and implementing guidelines adopted by the Authority
    in coordination with the concerned national government agencies
    and local government units.

(g) Infrastructure Compatibility Guidelines. Standards and
    criteria to ensure that infrastructure projects within the Sierra
    Madre Mountain Range are planned, designed, constructed, and
    maintained in a manner that is environmentally sustainable,
    climate-resilient, and compatible with ecosystem conservation
    objectives. Such guidelines shall require the consideration of
    ecological connectivity, avoidance of critical habitats where
    practicable, incorporation of appropriate mitigation measures,
    including wildlife crossings and habitat restoration, and
    compliance with applicable environmental laws and the
    Philippine Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) System.

(h) Transition Program. A phased implementation program to
    facilitate the adoption of the Sierra Madre Master Plan by national
    government agencies, local government units, PAMBs, ICCs/IPs,
    and other stakeholders, including institutional coordination,
    capacity-building,      technical      assistance,   and     policy
    harmonization measures.

(i) Rationalization and Relocation of Incompatible Land Uses
    and Occupancies. A program for the gradual rationalization,
    relocation, or resettlement of settlements, facilities, and other
    land uses determined to be incompatible with the Sierra Madre
    Master Plan, including Integrated Forest Management Agreement
    (IFMA) areas, where warranted and consistent with existing laws,
    subject to due process, adequate consultation, the rights of
    affected communities and Indigenous Peoples, and the
    resettlement and relocation policies provided under this Act.




                                                                    47
(j) Responsible Mineral Resource Transition. – A strategy for the
    gradual phase-out of mining activities within ecologically critical
    areas of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range, consistent with
    existing mining agreements, vested rights, due process,

    conservation, watershed protection, ecosystem restoration, and
    sustainable development. The strategy shall include measures for
    mine rehabilitation, environmental restoration, livelihood
    transition, and just transition mechanisms for affected workers
    and communities.

(k) Ecosystem-Service, Beneficiary, and Sector-Dependency
    Accounts. – Accounts prepared consistently with Republic Act
    No. 11995 showing ecosystem extent, condition, service supply,
    service use, beneficiaries, sectoral dependency, economic
    contribution, avoided loss, replacement cost, degradation, and
    restoration.

(l) Sierra Madre Nature Credit and Nature-Finance Program. –
    Approved credit categories and methodologies; project and

    long-term nature credit purchase agreements; and rules

    excuse prohibited harm.

(m)      Restoration and Environmental Liability Program. –
   Procedures for ecological-loss, full-restoration-cost, and interim-
   public-loss assessment; restoration orders; performance
   security; on-site and affected-area restoration; registered third-
   party delivery; continuing monitoring; independent verification;
   and liability for nonperformance.

(n) Results and Accountability Framework. – For each major
    outcome, the baseline, target, responsible entity, lawful rights
    holder, cost, funding source, deadline, verification method,
    payment rule, and consequence for failure, together with public

    connectivity, resilience, service delivery, buyer compliance,
    investment mobilized, and revenues received by lawful rights
    holders and stewards.

       Sec. 27. Harmonization, Interface, and Integration with Existing
Plans. All local CLUPs, Regional Development Plans, and other sectoral plans
affecting the Sierra Madre shall be harmonized with the adopted Sierra Madre
Master Plan. Affected agencies and LGUs must coordinate with the Authority

                                  Article VI
                       Enhanced Enforcement Mechanisms

      Sec. 28. Sierra Madre Environmental Enforcement System
(SMEES). An integrated enforcement system is hereby established for the
Sierra Madre, institutionalizing a whole-of-government, whole-of-society
approach. The SMEES shall coordinate national oversight with regional
operations and community-based actions to prevent, detect, and prosecute
environmental crimes and illegal resource activities in the range.

      Sec. 29. Structure of SMEES. The SMEES shall consist of:

         (a)   National Coordinating Council. Chaired by DENR, this
               council provides strategic command. Members include heads of
               PNP, AFP, National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), Department
               of Justice (DOJ), and Bureau of Customs (BOC). The council
               formulates national enforcement strategies, ensures inter-
               agency coordination, allocates resources, and issues directives
               for operations in the Sierra Madre.
         (b)   Regional Enforcement Task Forces. Established in key Sierra
               Madre regions, each task force includes DENR field officers,
               PNP, including maritime and environmental units, specialized
               AFP units, and LGU representatives. These task forces plan and
               execute joint enforcement raids, intelligence sharing, and rapid
               responses to violations within their jurisdictions, guided by the
               council’s strategies.




                                                                             49
         (c) Local Rapid Response Units (LRRUs). At the barangay or
         municipality level, community-based teams are organized composed
         of forest rangers, barangay officials, and trained representatives of
         Indigenous Peoples. LRRUs act as frontline defenders: monitoring
         forests, reporting illegal activities, and conducting initial patrols
         under the oversight of the regional task forces.

       Sec. 30. Anti-Illegal Logging Task Force. A specialized Anti-Illegal
Logging Task Force (AILTF) is created under the SMEES. This unit focuses on
timber syndicates and trafficking networks. Key activities include: intelligence
surveillance to identify culprits; permanent and mobile checkpoints on
logging routes; mobile ground patrols and aerial reconnaissance of forests;
coordination with courts for expedited search warrants; and maintaining a
shared database of offenders, seized logs, and enforcement outcomes.
Successful models like interagency aids have shown that coordinated action
is essential to stop “chainsaw mills” before they move stolen timber out of the
mountains.

      Sec. 31. Use of Innovative Technologies and Geospatial Information
Systems. The Authority, in coordination with concerned national government
agencies, LGUs, and enforcement units, shall adopt and utilize modern,
science-based, and innovative technologies to strengthen environmental
monitoring, law enforcement, planning, research, and decision-making within
the Sierra Madre Mountain Range.

      Such technologies shall include, but not be limited to, remote sensing
and Geographic Information Systems (RS/GIS), satellite-based forest and
land cover monitoring with near real-time alerts, Unmanned Aerial Vehicles
(UAVs) or drones, Global Positioning System (GPS) technologies, automated
weather stations (AWS), artificial intelligence-assisted analytics, and other
appropriate digital tools for environmental monitoring, biodiversity
assessment, forest protection, disaster risk reduction and management,
permit verification, evidence gathering, and reporting.

       The Philippine Space Agency (PhilSA), in coordination with the
Authority, the DENR, and NAMRIA, shall serve as the principal technical
partner for space-based Earth observation under this section. The Authority
shall provide the geographic coverage, Sierra Madre Master Plan zones, no-go
areas, ecological corridors, Ecosystem-Service Catchments, restoration sites,
and other monitoring questions required for implementation. The DENR,
NAMRIA, LGUs, PAMBs, and other responsible agencies shall provide
available forest classifications, project boundaries, permits, tenurial
instruments, and other reference data needed to interpret observed change.




                                                                             50
Using available government, commercial, or open satellite data and
appropriate remote-sensing and geospatial methods, PhilSA shall
produce: (a) baseline and time-series information on forest extent, forest
type, tree-cover density, and land cover; (b) change-detection products
showing reforestation, forest recovery, deforestation, degradation, fire
scars, and material changes in vegetation condition; and (c) mapped
alerts on new roads, clearings, structures, quarrying or mining
footprints, land conversion, and other observable signs of economic
activity or encroachment within forests, no-go areas, corridors,
catchment protection areas, and registered restoration sites.

      PhilSA shall transmit to the Authority: (a) quarterly hotspot
reports, to the extent technically feasible, identifying the location,
extent, timing, and confidence level of detected changes and the areas
requiring priority verification; and (b) an annual Sierra Madre Earth
Observation Assessment describing forest-cover trends, restoration
progress, recurring degradation hotspots, observed encroachment
patterns, data limitations, and recommended monitoring priorities. The
reports and underlying non-sensitive geospatial layers shall be
integrated into the Sierra Madre Data and Monitoring System in
interoperable and machine-readable form.

       Satellite-derived information shall be used for planning,
performance monitoring, inspection targeting, and enforcement
intelligence. A satellite alert or classification shall not, by itself,
establish that an activity is unlawful or identify the person responsible.
The DENR, the relevant LGU or PAMB, the Authority, or another legally
competent body shall validate material alerts through permit checks,
higher-resolution analysis, field inspection, or other evidence before
imposing a sanction or taking final enforcement action. Public
disclosure shall protect sensitive ecological information, Indigenous
knowledge, personal data, and information restricted for lawful security
or enforcement purposes.

      The Authority shall provide for the agreed cost of recurring PhilSA
products, data acquisition, secure storage, and technical integration in
its annual budget and inter-agency program, subject to appropriations
and applicable budgeting rules. PhilSA products shall include
confidence levels, cloud or data limitations, and version information.
The DENR and NAMRIA shall retain responsibility for official forest,
land-classification, and base-map records within their respective
mandates.

       Sec. 32. Community-Based Enforcement. Local communities and
ICCs/IPs are recognized as key partners. They may be deputized as Forest
Protection Stewards with authority to report and assist in enforcement,
consistent with their rights. They shall receive training, equipment like GPS
and cameras, and incentives such as livelihood support, and hazard pay to
participate. The Government shall provide legal protection for citizen
informants against intimidation, following examples like barangay forest
watch systems.

      Sec. 33. Legal Services and Coordination with Environmental
Courts. The Authority shall establish and maintain a Legal Office, headed by
a qualified legal officer, to provide legal advice and representation in the
exercise of its powers and functions, including the institution or defense of
actions, the preparation of legal opinions, the conduct of administrative
proceedings, and the enforcement of this Act and other applicable
environmental laws.

      The Authority may, when necessary and subject to existing laws, rules,
and regulations, engage the services of retained counsel or other legal experts
to assist in the prosecution or defense of cases involving the Authority or
matters affecting the Sierra Madre Mountain Range, without prejudice to the
powers and functions of the Office of the Solicitor General (OSG) as the
principal law office of the Government.

      The Authority shall closely coordinate with the DOJ, the National
      Prosecution Service, the O, law enforcement agencies, and the judiciary,
      including courts designated to hear environmental cases under existing
      laws and rules, to strengthen the investigation, prosecution, and

      affecting the Sierra Madre Mountain Range. The Authority may provide
      technical, scientific, and documentary assistance, including expert

      Sec. 34. Incentives and Protection for Enforcers. All personnel
engaged in Sierra Madre enforcement, from national, LGU, and deputized
community forces, shall receive appropriate incentives and safeguards. This
includes hazard pay or risk allowances commensurate with field duties,
access to legal and medical assistance, and insurance coverage for injury or
death in the line of duty.



                                 Article VII
      Protection, Conservation, and Sustainable Development Programs

      Sec. 35. Guiding Principles. All programs under this Article shall be
guided by constitutional and international principles: intergenerational
equity, the precautionary and public trust doctrines, and the polluter-pays
principle. The Sierra Madre is declared an inviolable ecological zone; its
protection is as much a human rights issue as an environmental one.

      Sec. 36. Forest Protection and Management. The Authority shall
enforce strict forest governance:

   (a) No-logging in Primary Forests. All logging, road-building for
       extraction, and forest conversion in primary old-growth forests are
       absolutely prohibited. Only scientifically justified removals like for
       research or severe pest control may be allowed by special authority.
       This reflects findings that even small disturbances in intact forest can
       undermine the Sierra Madre’s role as storm shield.




                                                                            53
(b) Criminalization of Illegal Logging. Illegal logging of any forest within
    the Sierra Madre like harvesting, transport, and possession or trade of
    timber without proper permit, shall be punished by severe penalties:
    imprisonment of six (6) to twelve (12) years; fines from ₱1,000,000 to
    ₱10,000,000; forfeiture of equipment and vehicles used; and permanent
    disqualification from public office if the offender is an official. These
    penalties are without prejudice to penalties under Republic Act No.
    9175 or the Illegal Logging Act.

(c) Sierra Madre Forest Protection Corps. The Authority shall organize a
    special Forest Protection Corps, trained and deputized to work with the
    PNP and AFP in forest patrols and enforcement. This corps will carry
    out joint operations, establish rapid response teams, and assist in
    disaster monitoring. Their creation addresses gaps in manpower and
    ensures forest protection is sustained.

(d) Technology-Assisted Monitoring. Mandatory use of satellite imagery,
    drone surveillance, and artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted forest
    analytics shall be integrated into enforcement. Real-time alerts shall
    trigger patrols. A public website or dashboard may be established so
    communities and NGOs can also monitor alerts, similar to citizen
    science platforms.

(e) Restoration Obligation. Any individual, company, or government
    entity found responsible for forest destruction, through illegal logging
    or conversion, shall be ordered to undertake full ecological restoration
    of the affected area at their own expense. Restoration standards shall
    achieve ecological equivalence, specifically similar forest structure and
    species diversity, and be supervised by the Authority and DENR.
    Failure to restore shall be an additional offense, liable to fines or
    injunctions.

        Sec. 37. Biodiversity Conservation. To safeguard endemic and
   endangered species:
                  a. All hunting, capture, killing, or trade of threatened
                      and or endemic species is strictly prohibited.
                  b. Violators shall face imprisonment of six (6) to ten
                      (10) years and fines of ₱500,000 to ₱5,000,000.
                      Corporate offenders may have licenses revoked and
                      be blacklisted. These penalties are in addition to
                      those under the Wildlife Act or RA 9147.




                                                                          54
                      c. The Authority shall establish wildlife rescue and
                         rehabilitation centers in or near the Sierra Madre,
                         for injured or confiscated animals and exotics, and
                         encourage captive breeding of critically endangered
                         species.
                      d. Critical habitat designation. Key habitats such as
                         breeding grounds, nesting sites, spawning rivers,
                         among others, not already in PAs shall be
                         designated as critical and strictly protected.
                      e. Ecological corridors. The Authority will, in
                         coordination with DENR, formally establish
                         corridors identified in the Sierra Madre Master Plan
                         and enforce no-development buffers along them,
                         recognizing that species from the famed Philippine
                         Eagle to lowland fishes rely on such connectivity.
                      f. Liability for Biodiversity Damage. Parties
                         causing loss of biodiversity including habitat
                         destruction or pollution, shall be liable for
                         ecological damages.

      Compensation shall at minimum cover full restoration costs and the
value of lost ecosystem services, as determined by scientific assessment,
consistent with watershed valuation studies.

       Sec. 38. Strict Regulation of Mining, Quarrying, and Other
Extractive Activities. All commercial and non-commercial extractive
activities within the Sierra Madre Mountain Range shall be subject to the
highest standards of environmental protection and sustainable resource
management, without prejudice to the powers of the DENR under existing
laws.

  (a) Commercial      and     Non-Commercial       Extractive     Activities.
      Commercial mining, quarrying, sand and gravel extraction, earth
      extraction, and other extractive activities undertaken for profit or
      commercial purposes shall be subject to the requirements of this Act
      and all applicable environmental, mining, and natural resources laws.
      Non-commercial extraction of minerals, sand, gravel, rocks, and other
      naturally occurring materials intended solely for personal, household,
      subsistence, cultural, or community use, and not for sale or
      commercial distribution, may be allowed only in areas not otherwise
      prohibited by law and subject to such rules, limitations, and
      safeguards as may be prescribed by the Authority, in coordination with
      the DENR and concerned LGUs.




                                                                          55
(b) No-Go Zones. Primary forests, old-growth forests, protected areas,
    strict protection zones, critical watersheds, identified biodiversity
    hotspots, key biodiversity areas, and other environmentally critical
    areas, as determined by law or by the Authority in coordination with
    the DENR, are hereby declared absolute no-go zones for commercial
    mining, quarrying, and other commercial extractive activities. Existing
    permits covering such areas shall be reviewed for compliance with
    existing laws and may be suspended, modified, or revoked, as
    warranted

(c) Landscape-Scale and Cumulative Impacts under the Philippine EIS
    System. For all proposed commercial extractive projects covered by the
    Philippine Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) System, the DENR or
    the EMB shall ensure that the environmental impact assessment, at
    the level of detail appropriate to the project and site, evaluates the
    direct, indirect, induced, cumulative, and long-term environmental,
    social, and economic impacts of the proposed activity on forest
    ecosystems, watersheds, biodiversity, ecological connectivity, climate
    and disaster resilience, ecosystem services, indigenous cultural
    communities, and affected local communities.

   The Authority shall transmit to the DENR or the EMB the applicable
   Sierra Madre Master Plan maps, ecological limits, management zones,
   corridor and catchment data, carrying-capacity information, and
   adopted scientific standards, and may participate as a resource agency
   in scoping and technical review. The DENR or the EMB shall retain
   responsibility for EIA scoping, impact assessment, public participation,
   technical review, and the ECC decision. No separate Cumulative
   Environmental Impact Assessment or Authority-issued ecological
   impact clearance shall be required under this Act.

(d) Strict Liability. Operators of commercial extractive industries shall be
    held strictly liable, regardless of fault or negligence, for any
    environmental damage, habitat degradation, pollution, or loss of
    biodiversity resulting from their activities, without prejudice to
    applicable civil, criminal, and administrative liabilities under existing
    laws.

(e) Rehabilitation and Environmental Guarantee Fund. No commercial
    mining or quarry permit shall be issued without proof of a
    Rehabilitation and Environmental Guarantee Fund, rehabilitation
    bond, or equivalent financial security sufficient to cover the full cost of




                                                                            56
      site restoration, environmental remediation, and post-closure
      monitoring, as determined by the DENR and the Authority. Such fund
      or bond shall be maintained throughout the life of the project and may
      be forfeited or utilized by the government should the operator fail to
      undertake the required rehabilitation.

  (f) Suspension and Revocation. Any commercial extractive operation
      that causes significant environmental damage, violates the terms and
      conditions of its ECC, permit, or other environmental authorization, or
      poses an imminent threat to public health, public safety, or ecological
      integrity shall be subject to the immediate suspension or revocation of
      its permit, without prejudice to the imposition of other administrative,
      civil, or criminal sanctions provided by law.

  (g) Penalties. Any person, corporation, partnership, association,
      government-owned or controlled corporation, or public official found
      guilty of violating this Section shall, in addition to any penalties
      provided under existing environmental and mining laws, suffer
      imprisonment of eight (8) years to fifteen (15) years, a fine of Five Million
      Pesos (₱5,000,000.00) to Fifty Million Pesos (₱50,000,000.00), and the
      mandatory closure of the commercial extractive operation. Where the
      offender is a juridical entity, the responsible officers shall be held
      personally liable, without prejudice to the suspension or revocation of
      the entity's permits, licenses, or authority to operate in accordance with
      law.

      Sec. 39. Protected Area Expansion. The Authority shall work with
DENR, LGUs, and Indigenous communities to identify additional areas for
formal protection under the NIPAS. This includes:

  a. Recommending new protected areas to Congress for inclusion in E-
     NIPAS, especially areas that complete ecological corridors or buffer
     existing parks;
  b. Recognizing ICCAs. Formally recognizing ICCAs within ancestral
     domains, granting them legal protection equivalent to PAs under IPRA
     (RA 8371) and strengthening community rights to manage these areas;
  c. Designating buffer zones around core reserves, regulated by local
     ordinances, to minimize edge effects; and
  d. Regulating ecotourism by carrying-capacity. The Authority shall set
     maximum visitor limits, require permits, and enforce standards like
     waste management, to ensure tourism is truly sustainable.




                                                                                57
       Sec. 40. Ecosystem Restoration and Rehabilitation. The Authority
shall launch science-based restoration programs:

   a. Native Species Restoration. All restoration must use indigenous tree
      species suited to local ecology. Exotic plantation forests are discouraged
      in protection zones.

   b. Assisted Natural Regeneration (ANR). Where feasible, priority will be
      given to ANR techniques like fencing, weeding, enrichment planting,
      among others, as a cost-effective way to recover degraded forests
      naturally.

   c. Agroforestry and Landscape Re-greening. Promote agroforestry like
      integrating trees with crops or livestock on denuded lands to restore
      soil and provide livelihoods. Support farmers with training, seedlings,
      and market access for agroforestry products such as coffee, fruit,
      rattan, among others.

   d. Liability for Restoration. Entities such as companies, communities,
      and government, failing to meet court- or Authority-ordered restoration
      shall be fined and potentially blacklisted from future projects, similar
      to environmental compliance penalties under RA 9275 or Clean Water
      Act and RA 9003, Solid Waste Act.

      Sec. 41. Ecosystem Connectivity and Fragmentation. The
fragmentation of habitats undermines biodiversity and ecosystem services,
and violates the State’s duty under Article II, Section 16. The Authority shall
implement the following measures to preserve and restore connectivity:

   a. Biodiversity Corridors. Identify and formally establish wildlife
      corridors such as connecting forest fragments, riparian zones, and
      elevational gradients, among others as protected areas. These corridors
      shall be managed to allow species migration and climate-driven range
      shifts. The Authority shall coordinate their integration into local land
      use plans.




                                                                             58
b. Avoidance and Infrastructure Planning. All proponents shall
   demonstrate avoidance of core forests, critical habitats, corridors,
   riparian systems, and irreplaceable ecosystems. Require that all new
   infrastructure such as roads, bridges, and power lines in the Sierra
   Madre be planned to avoid habitat fragmentation. Projects that would
   bisect core forests or corridors are prohibited unless absolutely
   unavoidable for overriding public interest. If no alternative exists, the
   proponent shall minimize harm and fully restore the affected ecosystem
   and service flows, including through wildlife crossings and habitat
   restoration. Prohibited or irreversible harm shall not be approved or
   offset. Departments like DPWH must submit proof of compliance like
   corridor gap analyses before project approval.

c. Conformity with the Sierra Madre Master Plan. No conversion of
   land or fragmenting project within the Sierra Madre shall receive final
   approval without a Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate
   issued by the Authority. The Authority shall determine only whether the
   proposed location, land use, and development footprint conform with
   the applicable management zone, ecological limits, no-go areas,
   biodiversity corridors, watershed and catchment protections, permitted
   and prohibited uses, and development standards established by the
   Sierra Madre Master Plan and this Act. The Authority shall deny the
   certificate if irreversible fragmentation is likely.

d. The Authority shall not conduct a separate environmental impact
   assessment, prescribe an environmental management plan,
   determine the environmental acceptability of the project, or issue
   an Ecological Impact Clearance. Assessment of direct, indirect,
   induced, cumulative, and long-term project impacts; alternatives;
   avoidance,    mitigation,    and    rehabilitation  measures;    public
   participation; technical review; and the ECC decision shall remain with
   the DENR or the EMB under the Philippine EIS System. The Authority
   shall transmit the certificate, applicable maps, standards, and written
   findings directly to the DENR or the EMB and other concerned agencies.




                                                                         59
  (f) Strict Liability and Environmental Cost. Any person or entity causing
      habitat fragmentation or corridor destruction shall be held strictly
      liable for the full restoration cost, interim public loss, continuing
      monitoring, and independent verification. Upon conviction, penalties
      include six (6) to twelve (12) years imprisonment and fines of
      ₱1,000,000 to ₱15,000,000, plus mandatory ecosystem restoration at
      full cost, including a multi-year monitoring plan. Permits and licenses
      of offending projects shall be suspended or revoked, without prejudice
      to higher penalties under existing environmental laws. The Authority
      shall assess such costs through an approved methodology and may
      require a performance bond, escrow, or other financial assurance
      sufficient to cover nonperformance and reversal.

  (g) Restoration and Rehabilitation of Fragmented Ecosystems. The
      Authority shall implement corridor restoration projects: reforesting
      deforested buffers, removing barriers including old roads and fences,
      where possible, and rehabilitating streams. All LGUs in the Sierra
      Madre must integrate these corridors and connectivity goals into their
      CLUPs and ordinances, subject to Authority review. The responsible
      proponent shall restore the impacted site and affected area under an
      approved plan. Where the Authority determines that qualified third-
      party delivery will achieve more effective recovery, the proponent may
      finance a registered nature-project developer operating in the affected
      area, and payments shall be released only upon independently verified
      milestones and results. A proponent undertaking restoration itself shall
      be subject to the same registration, monitoring, disclosure, verification,
      and nonperformance rules. Restoration under this subsection shall not
      generate compensatory credits usable against unrelated harm.

  (h) Citizen Enforcement. Any citizen may petition for enforcement of this
      connectivity provision, including through environmental legal remedies
      like Writ of Kalikasan and continuing mandamus, consistent with
      prevailing environmental laws. Citizen suits to stop fragmenting
      projects shall be encouraged, and environmental courts shall expedite
      such cases.

     Sec. 42. Sustainable Livelihood and Economic Development. The
Authority shall foster green rural economies, an economic system in non-
urban areas that is low-carbon, resource-efficient, and socially inclusive:




                                                                             60
   a. Agroforestry and Non-Timber Enterprises. Encourage
      agroforestry including coffee, cacao, fruit trees under canopy, to
      boost farm income while restoring forests, and develop
      community enterprises for sustainable non-timber forest
      products such as rattan, honey, pandan, and medicinal plants.

   b. Eco-Tourism and Cultural Heritage. Promote eco-tourism like
      trekking, birdwatching, and cultural tourism in a manner that
      respects carrying capacities and benefits local communities.
      Develop tourism zones in peripheral areas, outside strict zones,
      with strict guidelines and visitor limits.

   c. Community Participation in the Sierra Madre Nature Credit
      System. Assist upland communities, Indigenous Peoples, and
      lawful tenure holders to participate as registered nature-project
      developers under Section 63, so that watershed protection,
      afforestation, and erosion-control outcomes they deliver are
      independently verified, issued as Sierra Madre Nature Credits,
      and paid for by the downstream water utilities, irrigation
      districts, and other Covered Enterprises that depend on those
      services.

   d. Support to ICCs/IPs and Communities. Give priority technical
      and financial assistance to ICCs/IPs and upland farmers to
      implement these programs, recognizing their role as custodians
      of the Sierra Madre.

    Sec. 43. Ecologically Compatible and Climate-Resilient
Infrastructure. All infrastructure proposed within or materially
affecting the Sierra Madre shall comply with the Sierra Madre Master
Plan, applicable ecological limits, the avoidance hierarchy, and strict
environmental and climate standards:
   a. Projects must undergo the rigorous EIA process under the
       Philippine EIS System and secure a Sierra Madre Master Plan
       Conformity Certificate confirming that the proposed location,
       land use, and development footprint conform with the Sierra
       Madre Master Plan, incorporating green design like
       bioengineering, and energy efficiency.




                                                                     61
        b. Any project causing prohibited harm or irreversible ecological
           damage to an irreplaceable ecosystem, including clearcutting of
           critical forest and destruction of coral reefs, is prohibited.
           Lawfully unavoidable damage shall create a direct duty to restore
           the affected ecosystem and service flows, in addition to all ECC
           conditions, and environmental cost shall include ecological loss,
           full restoration cost, and interim public loss.
        c. All proposals must include a Climate Risk and Adaptation
           Assessment (CRA) to ensure infrastructure is climate-proof like
           flood-proof bridges ad slope stabilization for roads.
        d. All proponents shall provide the restoration, monitoring, and
           financial assurance required by the ECC, this Act, and other
           applicable laws before final project approval, procurement, or
           funding.

         The Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate shall not
     constitute an EIA or ECC. The Authority shall provide its official maps,
     ecological limits, corridor and catchment data, and adopted standards
     to the DENR or the EMB, but the DENR or the EMB shall independently
     conduct the environmental review and determine whether to issue,
     condition, or deny the ECC.

        Restoration may be undertaken by the proponent under continuing
     monitoring and independent verification or, when approved by the
     Authority, by a registered nature-project developer in the affected area.
     Third-party payment shall be results-based and shall not discharge the
     proponent until verified restoration outcomes are delivered and
     maintained. The purchase or retirement of unrelated Sierra Madre
     Nature Credits shall not substitute for this duty.

     Sec. 44. Research, Innovation, and Knowledge Systems. The
Authority shall promote an evidence-based management culture:

  a. Research Partnerships. Forge partnerships with academic
     institutions, research institutes, and NGOs to conduct biodiversity
     surveys, climate vulnerability studies, and pilot projects on restoration
     or eco-techniques. Encourage joint fact-finding missions in high-risk
     sites.
  b. Centralized Ecological Database. Expand the data system (Sec. 6(h))
     into an open-access portal where researchers and planners can access
     environmental data such as species lists, deforestation rates, and
     climate data.




                                                                           62
        (c) Indigenous and Local Knowledge. Integrate scientific research
        with indigenous knowledge systems. Support documentation of
        traditional forest management practices and ancestral narratives,
        and incorporate them into adaptation strategies.

      Sec. 45.    Citizen    Suits   and   Accountability.     To   strengthen
enforcement:

  (a) Citizen Suits. Any Filipino, including future generations, may sue to
      enforce any provision of this Act. This includes filing Writs of Kalikasan
      for massive environmental damage and continuing mandamus, to
      compel agencies to act.

  (b) Public Officer Liability. Any government official who willfully fails to
      implement this Act or grossly neglects the Sierra Madre shall be
      administratively, civilly, and criminally liable. This includes failing to
      coordinate with the Authority, issuing contrary permits, or conspiring
      with violators.

     Sec. 46. Harmonization with Other Laws. This Article shall be
implemented in harmony with existing environmental laws such as PD 705,
RA 9175, RA 9147, RA 11038, among others. In case of conflicting standards,
the most stringent provision for environmental protection shall prevail.

                                   Article VIII
                         Integration of Protected Areas

      Sec. 47. Sierra Madre Protected Area Network (SMPAN). All protected
areas, protected landscapes and seascapes, critical habitats, and other
conservation areas within the Sierra Madre Mountain Range shall be
managed as an integrated Sierra Madre Protected Area Network (SMPAN)
under a landscape-scale conservation approach to strengthen ecological
connectivity, biodiversity conservation, and climate resilience. The
management regime for the Network shall promote the interconnectivity of
ecological corridors among protected areas and other key biodiversity areas
to ensure the long-term ecological integrity of the Sierra Madre Mountain
Range. Coordinated planning, zoning, monitoring, and enforcement shall be
undertaken to prevent conservation leakage and to enhance the effectiveness
of protected area management.

     In particular:




                                                                             63
          (a) The Authority, in coordination with the DENR-Biodiversity
              Management Bureau (BMB), PAMBs, concerned local
              government units, ICCs/IPs, and other stakeholders, shall
              establish, restore, and maintain a network of ecological
              corridors linking fragmented protected areas and other critical
              habitats to facilitate wildlife movement, gene flow, and climate-
              induced species migration, including those of threatened and
              endemic species such as the Philippine Eagle.

          (b) The Authority shall utilize geospatial technologies, biodiversity
              assessments, and scientific data to identify ecological
              bottlenecks and habitat fragmentation between the northern,
              central, and southern Sierra Madre ranges, and shall prioritize
              the restoration and reconnection of these ecological corridors
              through appropriate conservation and habitat restoration
              measures.

      Sec. 48. Role of Protected Area Management Boards (PAMBs).
Existing PAMBs remain the primary site managers of their respective PAs. The
Authority and the PAMBs shall harmonize their management plans and
health indicators with the with existing set standards. The Authority shall
provide technical support and standardize metrics to measure conservation
success uniformly across SMPAN.

      Sec. 49. Recognition of Local Conservation Areas. The Authority
shall work to formally integrate non-legislated Local Conservation Areas
(LCAs) and Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (OECMs) into
the broader network. This includes:

   (a) Pathway to Protected Areas under NIPAS. Facilitating proposals for
       LCAs, managed by LGUs or ICCs/IPs, to be incorporated into the
       protected areas under NIPAS, providing technical and legal support.
   (b) Legal Protection of Community Forests. Grant legal protection to
       community-managed forests as buffer zones around core PAs,
       recognizing their role in stopping encroachment and invasive species.

      Sec. 50. Unified Management and Monitoring Protocol. The
Authority shall, in coordination with the DENR, the BMB, PAMBs, and other
concerned agencies, establish and maintain a unified management and
monitoring protocol for all areas within the SMPAN to promote standardized
data collection, interoperability, evidence-based decision-making, and
coordinated enforcement.




                                                                            64
The Protocol shall include, but not be limited to, the following:

(a) The establishment of a Sierra Madre Integrated Environmental
    Information System, which may include a Sierra Madre Digital Twin
    or other appropriate geospatial and digital technologies, as a
    centralized platform integrating available ecological, biodiversity,
    climate, hydrological, carbon, land use, and environmental threat
    data from protected areas and other conservation sites within the
    Network;

(b) The adoption and integration of existing and future government-
    approved biodiversity and protected area monitoring systems and
    technologies, including, where appropriate, the Spatial Monitoring
    and Reporting Tool (SMART), Protected Area Information System
    (PAIS), Biodiversity Monitoring System (BMS), ArcGIS, and other
    comparable platforms, to facilitate patrol planning, biodiversity
    monitoring, incident reporting, geospatial analysis, and information
    sharing among the Authority, DENR, PAMBs, law enforcement
    agencies, and other concerned stakeholders; and

(c) The development of interoperable standards and protocols for data
    collection, validation, storage, analysis, and sharing to ensure
    transparency, synchronized monitoring and enforcement, and the
    effective management of ecological corridors and protected areas
    across the Sierra Madre Mountain Range;

(d) Ecosystem and Sector Accounts. The integration of ecosystem
    extent, condition, service-supply, service-use, beneficiary, sector-
    dependency, economic-contribution, avoided-loss, degradation, and
    restoration accounts prepared in coordination with the PSA, the
    DENR, and other responsible agencies under Republic Act No.
    11995;

(e) Sierra Madre Nature Credit Registry. A publicly accessible
    registry of registered developers and projects, lawful tenure or
    rights-holder consent, baselines, approved methodologies,
    validation and verification reports, issued, transferred, and retired
    Sierra Madre Nature Credits, Covered Enterprises, Nature Credit
    Purchase Obligation assessments, Nature Credit Compliance
    Clearances, restoration orders, payments, benefit allocation,
    reversals, and enforcement actions;




                                                                      65
      (f) Integrity and Access. Unique serial numbers, controls against
          double issuance and double claiming, independent registry audit,
          secure interoperability with relevant government systems, and
          lawful safeguards for personal data, sensitive ecological
          information, and Indigenous knowledge;

      (g) Public Dashboard. Watershed- and corridor-level disclosure of
          baselines, targets, ecological condition, service delivery, obligated
          demand, capital mobilized, restoration performance, and revenues
          allocated under tenurial and stewardship arrangements; and

      (h) Data Governance and Cybersecurity. The Authority shall adopt
          data-classification, access-control, retention, correction, breach-
          notification,   business-continuity,    and    cybersecurity    rules
          consistent with Republic Act No. 10173 and applicable government
          security standards. Public disclosure shall not reveal personal data,
          trade secrets, sensitive locations of threatened species, protected
          Indigenous knowledge, or information whose release would create a
          material enforcement or security risk. A person affected by an
          enterprise or project record shall have a documented procedure to
          seek correction without altering the audit trail.

       These measures shall promote data-driven conservation, strengthen
inter-agency coordination, and prevent localized management gaps from
undermining the ecological integrity of the Sierra Madre Protected Area
Network.



                                  Article IX
                Penal Provisions and Administrative Sanctions

       Sec. 51. Violations of Unified Protocol. Any person or entity found
guilty of violating the zoning or protection protocols established by this Act
such a illegal occupancy of strict zones and bypassing corridors, shall be
liable for:

   a. A restoration fine of ₱500,000 to ₱10,000,000 per violation, depending
      on severity. In cases involving primary forests or critical habitats, the
      maximum fine shall accrue daily until restoration begins.

   b. Mandatory execution of an Authority-approved restoration plan to
      achieve ecological equivalence in the damaged area, supervised by the
      Authority and the relevant PAMB.




                                                                            66
      Sec. 52. Liability of Public Officials. Any public official or employee
who willfully fails to enforce the protocols required under this Act, including
ignoring monitoring data or approving illegal permits, commits gross neglect
of duty or grave misconduct, shall be liable in accordance with the following
procedures:

      With respect to the Administrator, the Board may exercise disciplinary
authority, including removal for cause, upon a majority vote of the members
of the Board who actually took part in the investigation and deliberation,
consistent with Republic Act No. 10149 and subject to existing Civil Service
laws, rules and regulations.

      With respect to other officials and employees of the Authority,
disciplinary proceedings shall be conducted in accordance with the Civil
Service rules on administrative cases.

        Sec. 53. Obstruction of Scientific Monitoring. Any person who
willfully damages, tampers with, or obstructs forest monitoring equipment
such as satellite cameras, LiDAR stations, and telemetry collars, shall face
one (1) to three (3) years imprisonment and a ₱1,000,000 fine, plus civil
liability for replacing the equipment.

      Sec. 54. Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPP).
Any lawsuit filed with the intent to harass or intimidate persons enforcing this
Act, including environmental defenders and officials, shall be declared a
SLAPP. Such suits must be dismissed outright, and the defendant shall
recover double damages plus attorney’s fees from the filer. This encourages
public participation and protects citizens’ right to petition for environmental
protection.

      Sec. 55. Graduated Penalty Escalation Matrix. In addition to specific
penalties provided in this Act and existing environmental laws, violations
shall be subject to a graduated penalty system based on (1) severity of
environmental damage, (2) scale of operation, (3) presence of aggravating
circumstances, and (4) repetition of offense:

      (a) Classification of Violations
          1. Minor Violations – procedural or first-time infractions with
             minimal ecological impact;
          2. Major Violations – activities causing measurable environmental
             degradation such as forest clearing and pollution discharge;




                                                                             67
         3. Grave Violations – large-scale, organized, or irreversible damage
            such as destruction of primary forest, critical habitats, or
            watersheds.

      (b) Escalation of Penalties
          1. First Offense: Base penalty under this Act;
          2. Second Offense: Fine increased by 100% and imprisonment
             increased by one (1) to two (2) years;
          3. Third and Subsequent Offenses: Fine increased by 200%,
             maximum        imprisonment       imposed,   and     permanent
             disqualification from engaging in any activity within the Sierra
             Madre.

       (c) Aggravating Circumstances, warranting maximum penalties
         1. Commission by a syndicate or corporation;
         2. Involvement of public officials or abuse of authority;
         3. Occurrence within Strict Protection Zones or critical habitats;
         4. Use of heavy equipment or destructive technology; and
         5. Obstruction of enforcement or destruction of evidence.

       (d) Corporate Liability. Corporate officers who knowingly allowed or
          failed to prevent violations shall be held solidarily liable. Penalties
          include license revocation, blacklisting from government contracts,
          and forfeiture of assets used in the commission of the offense.

      (e) Daily Accrual of Fines. For continuing violations, fines shall accrue
          daily until full cessation and commencement of restoration, as
          certified by the Authority.



                             Article X
    Police and Enforcement Powers; Immunity and Indemnification

      Sec. 56. Police and Enforcement Powers. In the exercise of its police
and enforcement powers under this Act, the Authority shall coordinate with
and may deputize or enlist, subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations,
the Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP), as frontliners in the performance
of public safety, security, environmental protection, and law enforcement
functions within the Sierra Madre Mountain Range.




                                                                              68
      The Authority may likewise request the assistance of the Philippine
National Police (PNP), the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), the
Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), local government
units (LGUs), and other concerned government agencies, which shall, to the
extent permitted by law, extend the necessary assistance in the
implementation and enforcement of this Act.

       Subject to existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and
regulations, a portion of the fees, charges, fines, and other revenues collected
by the Authority, as may be determined by the Board, may be allocated to the
AFP for the utilization of personnel and the acquisition, operation, and
maintenance of equipment, assets, and other resources necessary for the
effective enforcement of this Act within the Sierra Madre Mountain Range.

       Consistent with the continuing protection, conservation, rehabilitation,
management, and maintenance of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range, the AFP
shall likewise coordinate with the PNP for the effective implementation and
enforcement of this Act.

      Sec. 57. Protection from Personal Liability. The members of the
Board of Directors, the Administrator, officers, and employees of the
Authority, including the AFP and PNP personnel assigned to exercise police
and enforcement powers under this Act, shall not be held personally liable for
any civil, criminal, or administrative action arising from any act performed or
omitted in the lawful exercise of their official duties and functions, unless
such act or omission is attended by willful misconduct, evident bad faith,
gross negligence, or is otherwise contrary to law.

      Sec. 58. Indemnification and Legal Assistance. The Authority shall
indemnify the members of the Board of Directors, the Administrator, officers,
employees, and the AFP and PNP personnel assigned to exercise police and
enforcement powers under this Act against any liability, loss, claim, demand,
damage, deficiency, cost, or expense arising from the lawful performance of
their official duties and functions, unless such liability results from willful
misconduct, evident bad faith, gross negligence, or a willful violation of this
Act or other applicable laws.




                                                                             69
      The Authority shall provide legal representation or legal assistance and
may advance reasonable litigation costs and expenses, including attorney's
fees and other necessary legal expenses, subject to existing budgeting,
accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations, to the members of the
Board of Directors, the Administrator, officers, employees, and the AFP and
PNP personnel assigned to exercise police and enforcement powers under this
Act in connection with any civil, criminal, administrative, or other action or
proceeding instituted against them by reason of, or in connection with, the
exercise of their powers or the performance of their duties under this Act.

      The legal protection provided under this section shall not apply to any
action or proceeding initiated by the Authority against any member of the
Board of Directors, the Administrator, officer, employee, or assigned
enforcement personnel for acts committed in violation of this Act or other
applicable laws.

      Any member of the Board of Directors, the Administrator, officer,
employee, or assigned enforcement personnel who resigns, retires, transfers
to another government agency, or is otherwise separated from the service shall
continue to be entitled to the legal protection and indemnification provided
under this section with respect to acts or omissions performed in good faith
during his or her tenure with the Authority.

       In the event of a settlement or compromise, indemnification shall be
limited to matters covered by such settlement or compromise upon the written
advice of legal counsel that the person seeking indemnification did not commit
willful misconduct, evident bad faith, or gross negligence.

       The Authority may advance the costs and expenses incurred in
defending any action or proceeding upon receipt of a written undertaking by,
or on behalf of, the person concerned to reimburse the Authority should it be
finally determined by a court of competent jurisdiction or other competent
authority that such person is not entitled to indemnification under this Act.

                               Article XI
         Indigenous Peoples’ Participation and Global Standards

      Sec. 59. Recognition of Rights and Customary Governance. The
State reaffirms full recognition of ICCs/IPs’ rights over their Ancestral
Domains within the Sierra Madre.




                                                                           70
      Customary laws and practices of ICCs/IPs shall be respected and
integrated into management plans. Tribal communities may exercise their
customary governance and enforcement, provided they align with the
objectives of this Act and respect agreed conservation standards.

       Sec. 60. Co-Management and Self-Determination. ICCs/IPs shall be
partners and co-managers of conservation in their territories. The Authority
shall formally recognize ICCAs as management zones, where indigenous
institutions lead conservation efforts with support from government agencies.
This co-management shall be consistent with IPRA, ensuring autonomy and
benefit-sharing.

      Sec. 61. Adoption of Global Conservation Standards. The Authority
shall implement adaptive, landscape-level governance in accordance with
international best practices:
         1. Landscape-Level Governance. Policies and management shall
            transcend administrative boundaries, focusing on ecological
            units.
         2. Ridge-to-Reef (R2R) Management. Emphasize hydrological
            linkages. Upland forest protection shall be coordinated with
            coastal and fisheries management, recognizing the Sierra Madre’s
            role in watershed regulation and marine ecosystem health.
         3. Adaptive Management. The Authority shall periodically review
            policies using the latest science like climate projections and
            biodiversity assessments, and adjust strategies accordingly.
         4. Nature-Based Solutions (NbS). Priority shall be given to
            ecosystem-based adaptation and mitigation such as restoration to
            prevent floods and mangrove restoration for storm buffering. The
            Authority shall draw lessons from global examples where NbS
            have effectively enhanced resilience.

                                 Article XII
                     Financing Mechanisms and Incentives

       Sec. 62. Sierra Madre Fund. A special Sierra Madre Fund is created,
managed by the Authority, solely to finance its statutory public-good and
market-governance functions under this Act, including ecosystem
accounting; preparation and review of the Sierra Madre Master Plan; scientific
research and public data; methodology and safeguards development;
operation and security of the Registry; independent audits; market-integrity
supervision; monitoring and enforcement systems; public consultation; and
training, standard tools, and general capacity support offered on equal terms
to Indigenous Peoples, communities, lawful tenure holders, project
developers, investors, and Covered Enterprises. Initial capitalization and
sources include:

         1. Annual appropriations from the national budget;
         2. Ten percent (10%) of environmental charges or fees collected by
            LGUs within the Sierra Madre such as, but not limited to
            restoration fees and watershed charges;
         3. Penalties, forfeitures, and restoration recoveries when lawfully
            assigned to the Fund;
         4. Grants, donations, and technical-assistance funds from local and
            international agencies or partners, restricted to the non-project
            purposes stated in this section;
         5. Net income of the Authority lawfully transferred after providing
            for approved operating costs and reserves; and
         6. Other lawful non-project sources. Fees and the Market
            Administration Charge collected under Section 11 shall first be
            recorded as corporate operating revenue of the Authority and
            shall not automatically accrue to the Fund.

       The Fund shall not originate, design, develop, construct, implement,
own, operate, manage, finance, invest in, underwrite, or guarantee a project
eligible or expected to seek registration, Sierra Madre Nature Credits, a Sierra
Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate, or another Authority decision. It
shall not acquire an ownership, revenue, repayment, security, or credit
interest in such a project. A grant received by the Authority may be used for
standards, accounts, public data, Registry infrastructure, safeguards,
training, or other non-project functions authorized by this section. Project-
specific grants or investments shall be made directly by the donor or financier
to an eligible proponent, or administered by another legally competent and
operationally independent institution, and shall not pass through the
Authority's project-selection or credit-issuance functions.




                                                                             72
       Private payments for Sierra Madre Nature Credits shall be transmitted
through an approved settlement or escrow arrangement directly to the lawful
credit owner, registered developer, and other entitled parties in accordance
with the Registry and applicable tenurial instruments. Such payments shall
not accrue to the Fund except for an expressly disclosed fee or Market
Administration Charge authorized by Section 11.

       No expenditure shall be released without an identified baseline, target,
responsible entity, delivery schedule, and verification method. All
expenditures from the Fund shall be audited and subject to transparency
rules, and all public funds shall remain subject to COA audit, government
accounting, procurement, and disclosure laws.

      Sec. 63. Sierra Madre Nature Credit System and Conservation
Incentives. There is hereby established a compliance-enabled system
through which eligible protection, restoration, and long-term stewardship
results within the Sierra Madre Mountain Range are independently verified,
issued as Sierra Madre Nature Credits, purchased by Covered Enterprises,
and retired to satisfy Nature Credit Purchase Obligations.

      a. Promotion Branding. Private investors who finance Sierra Madre
         conservation such as restoration projects, corridor restoration, and
         sustainable agroforestry, shall be eligible for income tax credits of
         up to 50% of their investment. This is to encourage corporate
         stewardship of the landscape.
      b. Ecosystem-Service Catchments and Covered Enterprises. For
         each material ecosystem service, the Authority shall delineate the
         Ecosystem-Service Catchment and publish a map and beneficiary
         schedule identifying the cities, municipalities, economic zones,
         industrial estates, utility service areas, and other locations that
         receive or depend on that service. The Authority shall use these
         maps to identify the revenue-generating enterprises and locators
         that must undergo a Covered Enterprise assessment. An enterprise
         shall be finally designated as a Covered Enterprise only upon
         evidence that it receives a material, measurable, and geographically
         traceable direct or indirect economic benefit from, or that its
         revenue-producing operations are materially dependent on, the
         specified service. During the initial implementation period, Covered
         Enterprise assessments shall be limited to water utilities and
         concessionaires;     irrigation   and    agribusiness    enterprises;
         hydropower and other energy operators; industrial estates and
         locators; tourism and real-estate enterprises; transport and other
         infrastructure operators; and other businesses whose revenues,




                                                                            73
   assets, operations, or supply chains materially depend on water
   regulation, sediment retention, flood and landslide attenuation,
   climate regulation, biodiversity, connectivity, or related services.
   Location within a mapped catchment shall establish geographic
   eligibility for assessment but shall not, by itself, create a Nature
   Credit Purchase Obligation. Households, subsistence users, public
   schools and hospitals, non-revenue public services, and entities
   below materiality thresholds shall not be Covered Enterprises solely
   because they receive a general public benefit.
c. Catchment and Sector-Dependency Assessment. For each
   ecosystem service, the Authority shall determine the source
   ecosystem, physical service flow, geographic catchment, beneficiary
   class, materiality, and economic consequence of impairment. The
   assessment shall consider: (1) quantity and criticality of service use;
   (2) revenue, value added, production, or assets demonstrably
   exposed; (3) additional operating or treatment cost; (4) replacement
   or substitute infrastructure cost; (5) risk-mitigation, insurance, and
   business-continuity cost; (6) avoided losses and service
   interruptions; (7) causal and geographic connection to the relevant
   ecosystem; and (8) enterprise size and capacity. The Authority shall
   coordinate with the PSA, NAMRIA, DENR, DILG, DHSUD, DTI, SEC,
   PEZA, and other economic-zone or sector authorities, utilities, and
   affected LGUs, and shall publish the maps, nonconfidential data,
   and methodology used. Monetary valuation shall not replace
   physical accounts, ecological limits, or protection of intrinsic and
   cultural values.
d. Statutory Standards and Sector Rules. A class may be covered
   only upon a public finding, supported by substantial evidence, that:
   (1) its revenue-producing activity has the nexus stated in subsection
   (b); (2) the applicable ecosystem service and service area can be
   measured with sufficient reliability; (3) the annual ecological
   outcome need is identified in the Sierra Madre Master Plan; (4) the
   allocation method is proportionate, administrable, and no more
   burdensome than reasonably necessary to advance that need; and
   (5) equivalent obligations under another national law are credited to
   prevent double compliance. Before effectivity, a sector rule shall
   undergo the procedure in Section 13 and obtain concurrence from
   the relevant sector regulator and the DOF as to regulatory and
   distributional impacts.
e. Annual Nature Credit Purchase Obligation. The Sierra Madre
   Master Plan shall state, separately for each ecosystem service,
   outcome class, and Ecosystem-Service Catchment, the annual
   quantity of Sierra Madre Nature Credits required to achieve




                                                                       74
   watershed, corridor, and landscape targets. Quantities from
   different services or catchments shall not be combined into a single
   undifferentiated requirement. Each Covered Enterprise's Nature
   Credit Purchase Obligation shall be the lesser of: (1) its
   proportionate share of that approved need, calculated from
   dependency weights and measured benefit or exposure; and (2) its
   attributable share of the verified avoided-loss, replacement,
   treatment, risk-mitigation, and business-continuity cost associated
   with impairment of the relevant service over the compliance period,
   adjusted for enterprise size, capacity, and applicable rate
   regulation. The resulting obligation shall not exceed the enterprise's
   documented annual Ecosystem-Service Dependency value for the
   relevant service, that is, the amount that the enterprise would
   reasonably and efficiently incur during the same period to replace,
   treat, secure, insure against, or operate without the impaired
   service. This is the statutory maximum and may not be increased
   by the Board or the implementing rules and regulations. Congress
   may enact lower sector ceilings after considering pilot, affordability,
   and fiscal evidence. The formula shall not be based on gross revenue
   alone and shall state data sources, materiality thresholds, de
   minimis exclusions, sector weights, treatment of MSMEs, and
   correction for benefits already financed by the enterprise. Each
   enterprise shall receive a written assessment, access to supporting
   nonconfidential data, and an opportunity to correct data and seek
   independent administrative review.
f. Regulatory Character; Separation of Purchase Proceeds and
   Authority Charges. A Nature Credit Purchase Obligation is a
   statutory conservation-performance obligation satisfied through the
   purchase and retirement of independently verified Sierra Madre
   Nature Credits rather than payment to the Authority. It is not an
   Authority fee, tax, permit to operate, or payment for permission to
   cause harm. The price of the credits shall pass through an approved
   escrow or settlement arrangement to entitled credit owners and
   project-delivery parties. Only the specific fee or Market
   Administration Charge authorized by Section 11 may be deducted
   or collected for the Authority, and it shall be separately shown in
   the transaction record. Any tax, special assessment, or cash
   collection for general public purposes shall require separate
   statutory authority.
g. Consumer, Competition, and Affordability Safeguards. A
   regulated utility or concessionaire shall not automatically pass the
   obligation through to captive consumers. Any proposed rate
   recovery shall require approval by the competent sector regulator




                                                                       75
   after proof of prudence, least-cost procurement, net ecosystem-
   service benefit, and protection of lifeline and basic-service users.
   The Authority and the Philippine Competition Commission shall
   monitor concentration, self-dealing, price manipulation, and
   barriers to participation by community and Indigenous projects.
h. Eligible Nature Credits and Methodologies. Separate Sierra
   Madre Nature Credit classes may represent verified avoided
   degradation, improved ecosystem condition, watershed security,
   flood and landslide resilience, sediment retention, biodiversity,
   ecological connectivity, restoration, or carbon benefits. These
   classes are not interchangeable. A credit represents only the
   measured ecological outcome stated in its methodology and does
   not confer ownership of land, water, biodiversity, an ecosystem,
   sacred knowledge, or a cultural value. Every methodology shall
   specify the baseline, counterfactual, unit, project boundary, service
   class, eligible catchment, temporal matching, safeguards,
   additionality where applicable, durability, leakage, reversal, buffer
   or replacement mechanism, monitoring, uncertainty, and
   verification requirements, and shall undergo public consultation,
   independent scientific peer review, and periodic reassessment. A
   credit may satisfy an obligation only when its service class,
   catchment or approved connected area, vintage, and durability
   match the dependency on which the obligation was based. Cross-
   service substitution is prohibited; geographic substitution may be
   allowed only where an approved methodology demonstrates the
   same service flow and beneficiaries. Credits shall be issued only
   after independent ex post verification and recorded with unique
   serial numbers. An avoided-degradation methodology shall use a
   conservative, independently reviewed counterfactual, exclude
   protection already secured by an adequately enforced legal duty
   unless the methodology proves a financing gap material to
   performance, and discount uncertainty and reversal risk.
i. Project and Developer Eligibility. Before registration, a project
   shall demonstrate lawful tenure or a valid agreement with the lawful
   rights holder; PAMB approval where required; NCIP certification and
   FPIC where applicable; an approved methodology and baseline;
   environmental and social safeguards; a grievance mechanism; a
   benefit and revenue-allocation plan; and independent validation.
   The Authority and the Sierra Madre Fund are ineligible to act as
   project proponent, developer, investor, owner, or credit recipient.
   Registration shall not confer title, extinguish existing rights, or
   authorize disclosure of protected Indigenous knowledge. No unit




                                                                     76
   shall be issued while a required permit, consent, or safeguard
   obligation is suspended or materially breached.
j. Purchase and Retirement. A Covered Enterprise shall satisfy its
   Nature Credit Purchase Obligation by purchasing and retiring
   eligible Sierra Madre Nature Credits through the Registry or an
   approved     long-term    nature    credit   purchase      agreement.
   Procurement shall be competitive or demonstrably least-cost over
   the credit life, while allowing duly justified local, Indigenous,
   community, and small-developer participation measures. Forward
   commitments and milestone finance may be used to mobilize
   capital, but a credit may be used for compliance and final payment
   shall arise only upon verified delivery, except for disclosed risk-
   sharing arrangements approved by the Authority.
k. Ownership and Revenue Allocation. Initial ownership and
   entitlement shall follow existing tenurial instruments, ancestral-
   domain and customary rights, lawful stewardship agreements, and
   contracts with registered developers. The Authority shall record, but
   shall not unilaterally create or reallocate, proprietary rights.
   Indigenous Peoples, communities, and tenure holders shall not be
   deprived of rights or revenues without consent and, where
   applicable, FPIC. A project affecting an ancestral domain or a
   community-managed area shall have a community-approved
   benefit-sharing plan, adopted through the community's recognized
   decision process, stating collective and household benefits, project
   and verification costs, grievance procedures, disclosure rules, and
   protections against unauthorized use of Indigenous knowledge.
   Independent legal and technical assistance shall be made available
   before approval. LGUs and PAMBs may receive a share only for a
   documented lawful tenurial or stewardship contribution disclosed
   in the Registry. The Authority and the Fund shall receive no project
   or credit share; they may receive only the separately disclosed fees
   and Market Administration Charge authorized by Section 11.
l. No License to Harm. Purchase or retirement shall not authorize
   prohibited activity, replace an ECC or permit, excuse breach of an
   ECC condition, or satisfy a proponent's duty to avoid, minimize, and
   restore its own damage. Irreplaceable ecosystems, sacred and
   culturally restricted areas identified through lawful processes,
   prohibited harm, and legally required remediation are ineligible for
   compensatory offset credits. Mandatory restoration may receive
   results-based finance but shall be separately labelled and may not
   be used to compensate for another entity's harm.
m. Compliance Administration and Gatekeeper Agencies. The
   Authority shall have exclusive responsibility for identifying Covered

   Enterprises, issuing annual Nature Credit Purchase Obligation
   assessments, maintaining compliance accounts, validating credit

   determining deficiencies. A Covered Enterprise shall obtain a Nature

n. Authoritative Compliance Evidence. A Nature Credit Purchase
   Obligation shall be satisfied only when eligible, uniquely numbered

   Enterprise's compliance account. A receipt, private certification,

   showing the enterprise identifier, compliance period, final Nature
   Credit Purchase Obligation, retired credit serial numbers,
   settlement reference, status, and validity period, capable of direct

o. Purchase and Settlement Channels. Sierra Madre Nature Credits
   may be purchased through an approved auction, marketplace, or
   exchange; a registered bilateral or long-term nature credit purchase
   agreement; or an approved pooled-purchase or aggregation facility.

   Market Administration Charge. The credit price shall be distributed

   revenue-allocation record; only the expressly authorized Authority

   payment unsupported by Registry-confirmed settlement and credit
   retirement shall not satisfy a Nature Credit Purchase Obligation.

                             Article XIII
                  Prohibited Acts and Enforcement

Sec. 66. Prohibited Acts. In addition to the acts prohibited under
existing environmental and natural resources laws, the following acts
are likewise prohibited within the Sierra Madre Mountain Range:
   1. Unauthorized Land Conversion. The conversion of forest lands
       to agricultural or other non-forest uses without the required
       authority under existing laws and, where applicable, without first
       securing a Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate;

   2. Destructive Extractive Activities. The conduct of large-scale
      mining or commercial or non-commercial quarrying in a manner
      that causes significant or irreversible environmental damage,
      including, but not limited to, the destruction of old-growth
      forests, critical habitats, or watersheds, and the pollution or
      contamination of rivers and other water bodies; and

   3. Introduction of Invasive Species. The deliberate introduction,
      release, or propagation of non-native or invasive species of flora
      or fauna that harm native species, biodiversity, or ecosystem
      integrity;




                                                                      82
         4. Unauthorized Commencement of Covered Works. Beginning,
            financing, directing, or causing site clearing, tree cutting,
            grading, excavation, road opening, land development,
            construction, installation, expansion, or other physical works for
            a covered project before obtaining a valid Sierra Madre Master
            Plan Conformity Certificate or other Authority document
            expressly required by this Act, together with the ECC, PAMB
            approval, FPIC, tenure instrument, LGU permit, or sector
            authorization otherwise required by law. A permit issued by one
            institution shall not excuse the failure to obtain a separate
            certification, approval, or consent required by this Act or another
            law;

         5. Continuation after Notice or Order. Continuing covered works
            after receipt of a lawful stop-work, cease-and-desist, suspension,
            or restoration order issued by the Authority, the DENR, a PAMB,
            an LGU, a court, or another competent body, or removing an
            official seal or obstruction placed to enforce that order; and

         6. False Compliance Evidence. Knowingly submitting, using,
            altering, or causing the submission or use of a false Sierra Madre
            Master Plan Conformity Certificate, Nature Credit Compliance
            Clearance, registry record, electronic confirmation record, or
            other Authority document to obtain a permit, financing,
            procurement award, disbursement, or authority to proceed.


       Emergency works strictly necessary to prevent an imminent threat to
life or serious damage to essential public infrastructure shall not constitute
an offense if the proponent gives notice to the Authority and the competent
permitting agencies within twenty-four (24) hours, limits the work to the
emergency, and submits to post-action review, restoration, and all other
requirements of law.

      Sec. 67. Penalties.
      (a) Administrative Compliance Violations. Failure to file a required
          report, maintain an accurate account, timely purchase and retire a
          final assessed requirement, or comply with a procedural or Registry
          duty, without fraud or material ecological harm, shall be subject
          only to the administrative sanctions expressly provided in this
          Section after notice and hearing.




                                                                            83
(b) False Documents and Market Fraud. A person who knowingly
    falsifies or alters an Authority certificate, compliance clearance,
    Registry record, verification report, or settlement confirmation;
    intentionally causes double issuance or double claiming;
    manipulates the Registry; or diverts credit revenues shall, upon
    conviction, be punished by imprisonment of two (2) years and one
    (1) day to six (6) years and a fine of not less than Five Hundred
    Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00) nor more than Ten Million Pesos
    (₱10,000,000.00), or three (3) times the economic benefit obtained,
    whichever is higher, without prejudice to prosecution under another
    law carrying a higher penalty.

(c) Unauthorized Works Causing Material Ecological Harm. Violators of
    Sec. 66 on prohibited acts who willfully begin or continue works
    without the required Authority document and other approvals, and
    thereby cause material ecological harm, shall, upon conviction, be
    punished by imprisonment of six (6) years and one (1) day to twelve
    (12) years and fines of ₱1,000,000 to ₱50,000,000, depending on
    the harm caused. The highest range shall apply only where the act
    affects an irreplaceable ecosystem, causes serious or irreversible
    harm, continues after a lawful stop-work order, or forms part of
    repeated or organized unlawful conduct. In addition, corporate
    offenders may have their business permits revoked by the issuing
    agency or a competent court, and their assets restrained only upon
    judicial order or express statutory authority, to cover rehabilitation
    costs. These penalties are in addition to other penalties under
    existing environmental laws.

(d) Responsible Officers. Where the offender is a juridical entity,
    criminal liability shall attach only to an officer or employee who
    authorized, directed, knowingly participated in, or knowingly failed
    to prevent the offense despite a legal duty and actual capacity to do
    so. The court may order restitution, restoration, and forfeiture as
    authorized by law.




                                                                       84
(e) Unauthorized Commencement of Covered Works. Without
    prejudice to criminal prosecution and penalties under another law,
    a private person or juridical entity that violates Sec. 66(4) shall, after
    notice and hearing, be subject to: (1) an immediate stop-work or
    cease-and-desist order; (2) an administrative fine of not less than
    One Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00) nor more than Five
    Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00), or three (3) times the documented
    economic benefit or compliance cost avoided, whichever is higher,
    plus not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) nor more than
    Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱200,000.00) for each day the work
    continues after a lawful notice or order; (3) restoration of the
    affected ecosystem at full cost; (4) payment of interim public loss
    until verified recovery; and (5) forfeiture of the economic benefit
    obtained or compliance cost avoided, as determined by a court or
    other authority expressly empowered by law. In fixing the
    administrative fine within the statutory range, the Authority shall
    consider the assessed ecological loss and interim public loss, area
    and ecological condition affected, sensitivity and irreplaceability,
    duration and reversibility of harm, economic benefit or avoided
    compliance cost, concealment, prior violations, cooperation, and
    timely restoration. The fine shall punish the violation and shall not
    replace restoration, compensation, or liability under another law.

(f) Assessment of Ecological Loss and Interim Public Loss. The
    Authority shall determine ecological loss and interim public loss
    through published methodologies consistent with Republic Act No.
    11995, official physical ecosystem accounts, and applicable DENR
    standards. The assessment shall identify the pre-impact baseline;
    extent and condition lost; affected ecosystem services and
    beneficiaries; full cost of site stabilization, rehabilitation,
    restoration, monitoring, and independent verification; reasonable
    replacement, treatment, disaster-risk, infrastructure-protection,
    and public-service costs; the period to verified recovery;
    uncertainty; and any irreversible loss. Monetary valuation shall
    support restoration and compensation but shall not make
    prohibited or irreplaceable harm permissible. The responsible
    person may contest the assessment through the procedure in
    subsection (k), and no item may be recovered twice under this Act
    or another law.




                                                                           85
(g) Nature Credit Purchase Deficiency. After a final assessment and
    notice and hearing, a Covered Enterprise that fails without lawful
    excuse to satisfy its Nature Credit Purchase Obligation shall pay the
    unpaid deficiency value, interest at the legal rate applicable to
    forbearance of money under the Civil Code and applicable Bangko
    Sentral ng Pilipinas issuances, and an administrative penalty not
    exceeding twenty-five percent (25%) of the deficiency value, taking
    into account gravity, duration, prior violations, good-faith
    correction, and capacity. The Authority may require a compliance
    plan and may recommend suspension from a benefit or
    procurement program only where the governing law of that program
    authorizes it.

(h) Market and Registry Violations. Fraudulent credit issuance or
    verification; intentional double claiming; falsification or alteration of
    a Nature Credit Compliance Clearance, settlement confirmation, or
    registry record; submission of fabricated transaction evidence; use
    of a cancelled or previously retired nature credit; unauthorized
    registry access or manipulation; undisclosed self-dealing; diversion
    of nature credit revenues; or material violation of safeguards may
    result, after notice and hearing, in cancellation or suspension of
    registration or recognition, cancellation of invalid credits,
    restitution, disqualification for a period not exceeding five (5) years,
    and an administrative fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand
    Pesos (₱100,000.00) nor more than Five Million Pesos
    (₱5,000,000.00), or three (3) times the economic benefit obtained,
    whichever is higher. Criminal conduct shall be referred to the
    Department of Justice or other competent body.

(i) Restoration Nonperformance. Failure to comply with a final
    restoration order may result in drawing on performance security,
    third-party performance at the responsible person's cost, recovery
    of full restoration cost and interim public loss, and an
    administrative fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos
    (₱50,000.00) nor more than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos
    (₱500,000.00) for each day of unjustified delay after the final
    compliance deadline. The total fine shall not exceed three (3) times
    the assessed full restoration cost and interim public loss. Payment
    shall not extinguish the duty to restore.




                                                                          86
(j) Disposition and Use of Collections. Administrative fines imposed
    and collected by the Authority under this Act shall accrue to a
    restricted Enforcement, Restoration and Monitoring Account within
    the Sierra Madre Fund. Amounts recovered as restoration cost or
    interim public loss shall be separately accounted for and transferred
    or disbursed, as authorized by law, to the competent public body,
    lawful rights holder, or independently procured restoration provider
    responsible for restoring and monitoring the affected area and
    service flows, or to reimburse verified response or replacement
    costs. The Authority shall assess, order, monitor, and verify
    restoration but shall not act as project developer or acquire credits
    or project revenue from the remedial work. Collections that another
    law requires to accrue to the Integrated Protected Area Fund, a
    Protected Area Retained Income Account, or another dedicated
    public fund shall be remitted to that fund and shall not be diverted
    to the Sierra Madre Fund. The account may also finance
    inspections, scientific assessment, enforcement systems, and
    independent verification. It shall not be used for bonuses,
    performance incentives, or an enforcement quota, and projected
    fines shall not be treated as a regular operating-revenue target.
    Criminal fines, forfeitures, and court-awarded damages shall be
    disposed of as the court directs and as provided by this Act and
    other applicable laws. All receipts and disbursements shall be
    subject to budgeting, accounting, COA audit, and public-disclosure
    requirements.

(k) Procedure and Review. No final administrative sanction shall be
    imposed without written notice of the charge, access to the
    nonconfidential record, reasonable opportunity to submit evidence
    and be heard, a decision by an impartial officer supported by
    substantial evidence, and written findings on liability and sanction.
    Where ongoing work presents an imminent threat of serious or
    irreversible harm, the Authority may issue an interim stop-work
    order effective immediately, provided that it gives the affected party
    a prompt opportunity to be heard and resolves whether to continue,
    modify, or lift the order within the period fixed by law or the IRR.
    Review shall lie with the Administrative Review Panel, without
    prejudice to judicial review. The IRR may prescribe procedure and
    penalty-adjustment rules but shall not create a new offense or
    sanction.

      (l) Coordination of Restoration and Recovery. Where the same
          injury is subject to an ECC condition, a DENR or PAMB restoration
          order, LGU action, or an Authority proceeding, the institutions shall
          designate a lead agency and, where practicable, issue a joint or

                                Article XIV
                           Transitory Provisions

      Sec. 68. DENR Coordination and Functional Delineation. Consistent
with Section 14 of this Act:
      a. The DENR shall retain exclusive authority over environmental
          permitting systems, including ECCs; and
      b. The Authority shall adopt and apply the Sierra Madre Master Plan,
          issue Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificates, and provide
          the DENR or the EMB with the applicable official maps, ecological
          limits, management zones, corridor and catchment data, carrying-
          capacity information, and adopted scientific standards. The
          Authority shall exercise coordination and scientific evaluation
          functions complementary to DENR processes, but shall not conduct
          a separate environmental impact assessment, issue an Ecological
          Impact Clearance, or determine whether a project is
          environmentally acceptable under the Philippine EIS System.
      c. The proponent of a project covered by this Act shall obtain a Sierra
          Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate from the Authority as an
          additional requirement for final action on its ECC application. The
          EMB may accept and evaluate the ECC application concurrently
          with the Authority's review, but the DENR or the EMB shall require
          the certificate and shall not issue or amend the final ECC, or take
          another final favorable action under the Philippine EIS System, until
          the Authority has transmitted the certificate and the DENR or the
          EMB has electronically confirmed that it is valid and current.
      d. The Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate and its
          supporting written findings shall state the project and proponent
          identifiers, proposed location, land use, and development footprint;
          applicable management zone, ecological limits, no-go areas,
          corridors, catchment protections, permitted and prohibited uses,
          and development standards; conformity finding; validity period; and
          electronic confirmation reference. They shall be transmitted directly




                                                                            88
         through an interoperable government system and shall form part of
         the ECC administrative record.
      e. The Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate shall be an
         additional statutory prerequisite, not an ECC or substitute permit.
         The DENR shall independently exercise its authority under the
         Philippine EIS System and may impose stricter conditions, require
         further study, deny the ECC, or enforce environmental law. A valid
         certificate shall not compel ECC approval.
      f. A Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate shall not replace
         an ECC, PAMB approval, NCIP certification, FPIC, local permit, or
         other approval required by law. When standards overlap, the more
         protective applicable ecological requirement shall govern, without
         diminishing Indigenous Peoples' rights, lawful tenure, due process,
         or compensation required by law.
      g. The DENR, the Authority, and other agencies shall establish
         interoperable review, monitoring, and enforcement procedures,
         including shared data, joint inspections, restoration tracking, and
         referral of violations. The Authority may recommend suspension or
         cancellation, but the issuing agency shall exercise the
         corresponding statutory power and state its action in writing.

                             Congressional Oversight

      Sec. 70. Congressional Oversight Committee on the Sierra Madre.
There is hereby created a Joint Congressional Oversight Committee to monitor
and evaluate the implementation of this Act.

       The Committee shall be composed of the Chairpersons of the
Committees on Government Corporations and Public Enterprises of the
Senate of the Philippines and the Committee on Government Enterprises and
Privatization of the House of Representatives; and Three (3) Members each
legislative chamber, designated by their respective leaderships.

      The Committee shall have the following powers and functions:
        a. Review the implementation of this Act and assess policy
           effectiveness;
        b. Conduct periodic hearings and require the submission of reports
           from the Authority and relevant agencies;
        c. Recommend remedial legislation or policy adjustments; and
        d. Ensure that funds appropriated under this Act are used
           efficiently and in accordance with law.

     The Authority shall submit an annual report to the Oversight
Committee detailing accomplishments, financial status, enforcement
outcomes, and ecological indicators, and containing:
        (a) Ecosystem-account baselines and changes;
        (b) The status of binding ecological limits, zoning, and Sierra Madre
            Master Plan Conformity Certificates;
        (c) Covered sectors and Covered Enterprises, assessed Nature
            Credit Purchase Obligations, credit purchases, retirements, and
            deficiencies;
        (d) Registered projects, issued Sierra Madre Nature Credits,
            reversals, and market-integrity findings;
        (e) Ecological loss, restoration orders, third-party delivery, and self-
            restoration performance;
        (f) Public and private capital mobilized;
        (g) Nature credit revenues allocated by tenurial instrument, rights
            holder, developer, LGU, PAMB, and Fund;
        (h) Enforcement and sanctions; and (i) progress against five-, ten-,
            and twenty-five-year ecological, resilience, and stewardship-

      (g) Compliance by Covered Enterprises;

      (i) Restoration-order performance and recovery of environmental cost;
      (j) Revenues received by Indigenous Peoples, communities, and lawful

      (k) Market-integrity and public-disclosure performance.

      Sec. 73. Periodic Congressional Review. This Act shall be subject to
a comprehensive review by Congress every five (5) years from its effectivity,
based on independently verified evidence, to assess its effectiveness, impact
on biodiversity conservation, climate resilience, and governance efficiency.

      The review shall determine whether:
      (a) The Sierra Madre Master Plan and Sierra Madre Master Plan
          Conformity Certificates control covered decisions;
      (b) Ecological limits and conflict rules are applied consistently by
          agencies, LGUs, PAMBs, and other institutions;
      (c) Ecosystem accounts identify service flows, beneficiaries, economic
          contribution, and public costs;
      (d) Statutory Nature Credit Purchase Obligations create predictable
          demand and mobilize private capital;
      (e) Protection, restoration, and stewardship have become financially
          and economically more competitive with conversion and extraction;
      (f) Obligated buyers comply without disproportionate burdens or
          evasion;
      (g) Actual damage is restored and environmental costs are recovered;
      (h) Outcomes are real, additional where required, durable, and free
          from double counting;
      (i) Revenues follow tenurial instruments, customary rights, and
          verified stewardship; and
      (j) Biodiversity, connectivity, water security, climate resilience, and
          disaster-risk indicators improve against the baseline. The Authority
          shall commission an independent evaluation at least six (6) months
          before each review.

       Sec. 74. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). Within one
hundred eighty (180) days from effectivity, the Sierra Madre Authority, in
consultation with the DENR, PSA, DEPDev, DOF, DBM, GCG, DILG, DHSUD,

Change Commission, sector regulators, concerned LGUs and PAMBs, CSOs,

holders, communities, validators, verifiers, and scientific institutions, shall
promulgate the IRR of this Act. The IRR shall detail procedures for
coordination, enforcement, plan adoption, and citizen suits under this Act,
and shall further cover: coverage and boundaries; Sierra Madre Master Plan
adoption and the Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate procedure;
PENCAS-aligned operational accounts; sector-dependency and beneficiary
assessment; methodology approval and peer review; project and developer
eligibility; tenure, PAMB, and FPIC interfaces; ownership and benefit
allocation; registry, cybersecurity, and unique enterprise identifiers;
validation, verification, issuance, transfer, and retirement; reversal and buffer

accreditation and transaction reconciliation; auction, bilateral, and pooled-
purchase reporting; the fee schedule and Market Administration Charge
authorized by Section 11, including payer, charging event, base, rate,

cure periods and conditional clearances; least-cost procurement and
consumer safeguards; restoration and financial assurance; Fund governance;
complaints, enforcement, administrative adjudication, and appeals; public

authority expressly provided by this Act.

      Sec. 75. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act or its
application to any person or circumstances is held unconstitutional or invalid,
the remainder of the law or its application to other persons or circumstances
shall not be affected.

      Sec. 76. Construction with Other Laws; Repealing Clause.

      (a) This Act shall be construed in harmony with Republic Act No. 8371,
          Republic Act No. 7586 as amended by Republic Act No. 11038,
          Republic Act No. 11995, Republic Act No. 7160, Presidential Decree
          No. 1586, Republic Act No. 10149, and other environmental,
          Indigenous Peoples’, local-government, public-finance, competition,
          data-protection, and corporate-governance laws.

      (b) The Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate, nature-credit
          requirements, and restoration procedures created by this Act are
          additional statutory requirements within their stated scope. Nothing
          herein shall be construed to:
                          1. Dispense with an ECC, PAMB approval, tenure
                             instrument, FPIC, NCIP certification, LGU action,
                             or other approval required by law;
                          2. Diminish ancestral-domain, customary, tenure,
                             property, or due-process rights;
                          3. Validate an act prohibited under another law; or
                          4. Prevent an agency, PAMB, or LGU from applying
                             a more protective standard within its lawful
                             authority.

      (c) All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules and
          regulations, or parts thereof expressly irreconcilable with a specific
          provision of this Act are hereby modified only to the extent of the
          inconsistency. No repeal of a protected right, criminal law, revenue
          measure, or agency mandate shall be implied.

      Sec. 77. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
