                            AN ACT
  IMPOSING CRIMINAL PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS OF OCCUPATIONAL
SAFETY AND HEALTH STANDARDS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC
ACT NO. 11058 OTHERWISE KNOWN AS “AN ACT STRENGTHENING
COMPLIANCE WITH OCCUPATIONAL SAFETY AND HEALTH STANDARDS AND
          PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF”

                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Occupational accidents, injuries and deaths are tragic reflections of how safety and health
standards here in the country are often taken for granted and the welfare of workers is sacrificed in
the favor of economic gains.

        The 1987 Philippine Constitution declares labor as a primary social economic force, and
mandates the State to protect the rights of workers and promote their welfare. Pursuant to this,
Republic Act No. 11058, otherwise known as “An Act Strengthening Compliance with
Occupational Safety and Health Standards and Providing Penalties for Violations Thereof” was
enacted to strengthen compliance with occupational safety and health standards, and to provide
penalties for violation thereof, including the imposition of administrative fines for willful non-
compliance with occupational and health standards (OSH) and compliance orders.

        Under the law and its implementing rules, daily administrative fines may be imposed
until correction of the violation, and separate administrative fines may likewise be imposed for
violations that expose or result in death, serious injury, or serious illness, and for obstruction,
misrepresentation, or retaliation; these are expressly without prejudice to the filing of criminal or
civil cases in the regular courts, as applicable.
        However, despite these measures, serious workplace hazards and willful disregard of
OSH requirements persist, particularly in high-risk industries. While administrative fines are
essential to ensure immediate correction and compliance, the State likewise has a strong interest
to define and penalize grave and willful violations that expose workers to grave and imminent
danger or result in injury or death so as to strengthen deterrence and accountability.

       The 2021/2022 Integrated Survey on Labor and Employment covering establishments
employing at least 20 workers showed that there was a decline in work-related accidents by
36.3% or 3,322 establishment out of 36,342 in 2021 as compared to the 10.9% or 4,186 out of
38,305 recorded in 2019.1

       The same survey also noted that the total occurrences of occupational accidents have
decreased by 32.4% or 27,636 cases from the 2019 record of 40,892.

       Despite these, workplace risks remain significant and compliance gaps continue to exist.
Many establishments, employers, contractors and sub-contractors still remain lax in terms of
compliance to occupational safety and health standards.

       In fact, according to the Department of Labor and Employment’s (DOLE) Bureau of
Working Conditions (BWC) 52 out of 95 or 55% of the constructions projects that it inspected
covering the period of August 1 to 15, 2023 in Metro Manila were found to have violated basic
occupational safety and health standards. 2

        Among the top violations were lack of personal protective equipment (PPE) for workers
that include safety shoes, helmets, harnesses, gloves, and goggles. If there were PPE, these were
found to be inadequate or incorrectly used.3 There were also no designated OSHS personnel such as
safety officers and first aiders, no safety and health committee, no mandatory OSHS training for
workers and no copy of the construction safety and health program in the workplace.

        It is high time that the government adopt measures that criminalize grave and willful
violations of occupational safety and health standards to reinforce compliance and protect the
welfare of the worker. Hence, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.
