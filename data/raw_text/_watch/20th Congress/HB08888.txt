                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

           The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to protect and promote
   the right to health of the people (Article II, Section 15) and to undertake appropriate
   health human resource development responsive to the country’s needs and problems
   (Article XIII, Section 12).

          Medical technologists play a critical role in the health care system as frontliners
   in laboratory diagnostics, providing essential data that guide clinical decision-making,
   disease detection, and treatment management. Their work underpins accurate
   diagnosis and effective medical intervention, particularly in the detection and
   monitoring of infectious diseases, chronic conditions, and public health threats. They
   perform complex laboratory procedures involving hematology, microbiology, clinical
   chemistry, immunology, and molecular diagnostics, often under demanding and high-
   risk conditions.

          Despite their indispensable contributions, medical technologists in government
   service remain comparatively disadvantaged in terms of compensation relative to the
   technical complexity, level of responsibility, and occupational risks inherent in their
   profession. Many operate in resource-constrained environments and are exposed to
   biohazards, yet their current salary grade does not adequately reflect the critical
   nature of their work.

          At the same time, the sustained global demand for medical technologists has
   contributed to the continued outmigration of Filipino health care professionals seeking
   better compensation and career opportunities abroad. This trend exacerbates
   workforce shortages in public health facilities and undermines the country’s capacity
   to deliver timely, accurate, and reliable diagnostic services.

          This measure seeks to upgrade the minimum entry-level salary grade of
   government medical technologists from Salary Grade 11 to Salary Grade 15. The
   proposed adjustment aims to ensure equitable compensation, improve workforce
   retention, and align their remuneration with similarly situated healthcare
   professionals.

           Ultimately, this measure affirms the State’s commitment to uphold decent work
   standards in the public health sector and to strengthen the delivery of accessible,
   efficient, and quality healthcare services for all Filipinos.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
   sought.

                              “AN ACT
            UPGRADING  THE    MINIMUM SALARY GRADE OF
            GOVERNMENT MEDICAL TECHNOLOGISTS FROM
            SALARY GRADE 11 TO SALARY GRADE 15”

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Government
Medical Technologists' Salary Upgrading Act of 2026. "

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to ensure adequate and just compensation for government medical
technologists in order to attract and retain competent professionals in the public
health sector, consistent with the State’s mandate to provide accessible and quality
healthcare services to all Filipinos.

      SECTION 3. Upgrading the Government Medical Technologists' Minimum
Salary Grade Level - The present minimum salary grade of government medical
technologists (Medical Technologist I) shall be upgraded from Salary Grade 11 to
15. Salary Grade 15.

       The salary grades of medical technologists occupying higher positions shall be
correspondingly adjusted in accordance with existing compensation and position
classification systems.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all medical technologists
employed in public health facilities and other health-related institutions owned and
operated by the national government, local government units, and other government
instrumentalities.

     SECTION 5. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s available
appropriations of the Department of Health (DOH) and other concerned agencies.

       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
       SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the Civil Service
Commission (CSC), shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its
effective implementation.

     SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

      SECTION 9. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
