                           AN ACT
REPEALING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8479, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
“DOWNSTREAM OIL INDUSTRY DEREGULATION ACT OF 1998”, CREATING AN
OIL INDUSTRY REGULATORY BOARD, ESTABLISHING A STRATEGIC
PETROLEUM RESERVE, MANDATING A FUEL SUBSIDY PROGRAM FOR
PUBLIC TRANSPORT WORKERS AND OTHER VULNERABLE SECTORS, AND
FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Oil Industry Reform
and Consumer Protection Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Oil and other petroleum products being vital
to national security and the continued supply thereof at reasonable prices being
essential to the general welfare, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

   a. Ensure a stable, adequate, and continuous supply of petroleum products;
   b. Protect consumers and vulnerable sectors, particularly public transport
      workers, from the adverse effects of volatile oil prices;
   c. Promote a fair, transparent, and accountable downstream oil industry;
   d. Regulate the downstream oil industry in the public interest; and
   e. Safeguard the welfare of public transport workers, the commuting public and
      other vulnerable sectors.

     SEC. 3. Repeal. - Republic Act No. 8479, otherwise known as the Oil
Deregulation Law of 1998, is hereby repealed.

       SEC. 4. Regulation of the Downstream Oil Industry. – To attain the
objectives of this Act, the State shall re-establish regulatory authority over the pricing,
importation, refining, distribution, and sale of petroleum products. The Oil Industry
Regulatory Board, hereinafter referred to as “Board”, is hereby created under the
administrative supervision and control of the Department of Energy (DOE).

        SEC. 5. Composition and Qualification of the Board. – The Board shall be
composed of a Chairperson and four (4) Members to be appointed by the President,
with the consent of the Commission on Appointments. The Chairperson and the
Members of the Board shall be natural-born citizens and residents of the Philippines.
In addition, the Chairperson and Members of the Board shall be persons of good moral
character, at least thirty-five years of age, and of recognized competence in the field
of law, economics, finance, banking, commerce and industry, management, or labor.
No Chairperson or Member of the Board who has an interest in, or is related within the
fourth civil degree of consanguinity or affinity with an officer of an oil company shall be
appointed as such. The Chairperson and the Members of the Board shall devote their
full time to the Board and shall not accept any other employment.

       SEC. 6. Term of Office of the Board. – The term of office of the Chairperson
and the Members of the Board shall be three (3) years. No person may be appointed
to serve on the Board for more than two (2) consecutive terms.

        SEC. 7. Quorum. – Three (3) members od the Board shall constitute a quorum,
and the vote of at least three (3) members shall be necessary for the adoption of any
rule, ruling, order, resolution, decision, or other act of the Board in the discharge of its
functions. The Board shall have its office in Metro Manila or such other place as may
be designated, and may hold hearings or any proceedings at such times and places
within the Philippines as it may provide by an order in writing.

       SEC. 8. Compensation. – The Chairperson shall receive compensation equal
to that of a Department Undersecretary, while the Members shall each receive
compensation equal to that of an official next in rank to a Department Undersecretary.
They, upon completion of their terms or upon becoming eligible for retirement under
existing laws, shall be entitled to the corresponding retirement benefits and privileges
provided for by law.

        SEC. 9. Staffing. – The Board shall appoint and maintain an adequate staff,
which shall include a Secretary who shall be a member of the Integrated Bar of the
Philippines with at least five (5) year-experience in the active practice of law in the
Philippines or in the discharge of an office requiring as an indispensable requisite
admission to the practice of law in the Philippines. The Secretary shall be the recorder
and official reporter of the proceedings of the Board and shall have the authority to
administer oaths in all matters falling within the jurisdiction of the Board. The Secretary
shall likewise be responsible for the effective implementation of the policies, rules and
directives promulgated by the Board, shall coordinate and supervise activities of the
different operating units of the Board, and shall perform such functions as may be
assigned by the Chairperson and/or by the Board. The members of the technical staff,
except those performing purely clerical functions, shall possess at least a bachelor’s
degree in the line of specialization required by their respective positions: Provided,
That, except for positions that are policy-determining, highly technical, or primarily
confidential in nature, all positions in the Board shall be subject to the provisions of the
Civil Service Law and its implementing rules and regulations.

        SEC. 10. Jurisdiction, General Powers and Functions of the Board. – When
warranted and when public interest and necessity requires as it may determine, the
Board shall have jurisdiction and supervision over all persons, natural or juridical, firms
or entities engaged in the business of importing, exporting, shipping, transporting,
processing, refining crude oil or other petroleum products, storing, marketing,
distributing, or selling, both at wholesale and retail, gasoline, gas, oil, kerosine, and
other crude or refined petroleum products, and shall have the power to supervise and
regulate their operations and activities including the following:

   a. To set conditions for the accomplishment of the purposes of this Act under
      which persons, natural or juridical, and business entities can engage or
      continue engaging in business of importing, exporting, shipping, transporting,
      processing, refining, storing, distributing, marketing or selling of crude oil,
      diesel, gasoline, kerosene, gas, and other refined petroleum products or by-
      products;
   b. To study the importation of crude oil into the Philippines and to determine its
      most economical sources, transportation routes, and freight rates;
   c. To look into all available sources of supply of crude oil, draw up plans to cope
      up with such contingencies as may arise should prevailing sources of supply
      become closed, insufficient or inaccessible, and enter into such preliminary
      negotiations or arrangements with possible alternative sources as may be
      necessary to assure a stable, adequate and continuous supply of crude oil and
      refined petroleum products under the most economic and competitive terms
      possible;
   d. To recommend to the President that the State, through such agency or
      agencies as the President may designate, acquire equity in any oil company or
      refinery now existing or that may be organized hereafter: Provided, however,
      That equity so acquired shall be made available to the public;
   e. To set and approve price ceilings for petroleum products based on transparent
      cost structures;
   f. To regulate profit margins of oil companies to prevent excessive pricing;
   g. To require full disclosure of pricing components, including import costs, taxes,
      and markups;
   h. To monitor and penalize anti-competitive practices, including hoarding,
      cartelization, and price manipulation; and
   i. To do and perform any and all such acts as may be necessary and proper to
      carry out the purpose of this Act.
SEC. 11. Powers of the Board Upon Notice and Hearing. – The Board shall have
power, upon proper notice and hearing, in accordance with the provisions of this Act:

   a. To determine whether current market prices of petroleum products are
      reasonable, providing for a fair and reasonable return;
   b. To regulate and fix prices of oil and other petroleum products. No changes in
      prices may thereafter be made by the Board without prior pubic notice and
      hearing at which parties who may be affected may appear and participate. In
      setting prices or making changes thereof, the Board shall be guided by the
      following considerations:

      1. Whether claimed increases or decreases in costs, sales or profits are real
         of stimulated;
      2. Whether expenses and costs which are claimed to have been incurred,
         being incurred, or are to be incurred are necessary and reasonable;
      3. The impact of the proposed prices or change therein on the economy and
         on the consuming public, particularly low-income groups and industries
         essential to the national welfare of security; and
      4. The cost profit levels of the industry.

   c. To regulate the capacities of new refineries or additional capacities of existing
      refineries so as to accomplish the purposes and objectives of this Act;
   d. To regulate the operations and trade practices of the oil industry in order to
      encourage orderly competition, prevent monopolies and collusive practices
      within the industry, giving due regard to the ecological and environmental needs
      of the country;
   e. To review the cost at which crude oil had been imported into the Philippines
      within the preceding year and to look for alternative source for a lower cost;
   f. To take adequate steps to prevent monopolies and combinations in restraint of
      trade within the petroleum industry, or involving enterprises engaged in the oil
      industry; and
   g. To recommend to the President that the government, through such agency as
      the President may designate, take over the operation of any refinery or other
      firm. Corporation or entity engaged in the oil industry whenever the public
      welfare or national interest so requires or when (1) such refinery, firm,
      corporation or entity ceases or threatens to cease or substantially reduces or
      threatens to reduce substantially its operations; and (2) its cessation or
      threatened cessation or reduction or threatened reduction of operations
      threatens the continued supply of petroleum products at reasonable prices to
      the general public or to industries dependent on petroleum products for sources
      of energy, or otherwise creates a clear and present danger to the national
      welfare or national security: Provided, however, That the operation by the
      government shall continue only for such period of time as the threat or danger
      persists: Provided, further, That just compensation shall be paid for the use of
       the property: and Provided, finally, That any unrecovered investment made by
       the government during the take-over shall be reimbursed by the refinery, firm,
       corporation or entity.

      SEC. 12. Powers of the Board Without Hearing. – The Board shall have the
power to do the following without hearing:

   a. Require importers of crude oil and petroleum products to file with the Board
      data on their import and the landed costs thereof at the time of purchase
      including port and brokerage charges, bank charges, wharfage, and other
      material information relative thereto;
   b. Require oil refineries to file with the Board their refining costs, storage costs,
      marketing and transportation or shipping costs, among many others.
   c. Require all dealership agreements to be filed and registered with the Board as
      public documents;
   d. Issue subpoena ad testificandum and subpoena duces tecum in any inquiry,
      study, hearing, investigation, or proceedings which it may decide to undertake
      in the exercise of its functions, powers and duties under this Act; and
   e. Perform such other acts as may be necessary or conducive to the effective and
      efficient exercise of its functions, powers and duties under this Act.

       SEC. 13. Other Powers and Duties of the Board. – The Board shall undertake
a continuing study of the oil or petroleum industry in its domestic and international
aspects, gather and collate information and statistics bearing on the industry, submit
an annual report to Congress and to the President of the Philippines on its activities
and the results of its studies, including such matters as it may deem appropriate
subjects of legislation or executive action. The Board shall keep itself regularly and
thoroughly informed of conditions in the industry in order to enable it to perform its
functions, exercise its powers and discharge its duties efficiently and effectively. In
furtherance to the objective of this Act, the Board shall regularly conduct unannounced
inspection of the premises and facilities owned, leased or operated by persons and
corporations engaged in the oil or petroleum industry in the conduct of their business.
The Board shall have authority to spot-audit and inspect books of accounts and other
corporate records of these persons and corporations.

       SEC. 14. Procedure. – The Board shall have the power to promulgate rules
and regulations that shall govern the conduct of inquiries, studies, hearings,
investigations and proceedings before it as well as the enforcement of its orders, rules
and decisions. The Board shall not be bound by the technical rules of evidence but
the Rules of Court shall apply in a suppletory character; Provided, That said rules and
regulations promulgated shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication thereof in
the Official Gazette: Provided, further, That the Board may summarily punish for
contempt by a fine not exceeding Five Thousand Pesos (Php5,000.00) or by
imprisonment not exceeding thirty (30) days or both, any person found guilty of
misconduct in the presence of the Board in any hearing or session or any proceedings
before it, or who willfully fails or refuses without just cause to comply with a summons
or subpoena legally issued by the Board or, being present at a hearing, session or
investigation, refuses to be sworn as a witness or to answer questions when lawfully
required to do so, or to furnish information required by the Board under this Act.

       SEC. 15. Review of Board’s Orders and Decisions. – A party adversely
affected by any order, ruling or decision of the Board in the exercise of its powers
under this Act may, within a period of fifteen (15) days from receipt of said order, ruling
or decision, or from receipt of denial of a motion for reconsideration files, file an appeal
to the Supreme Court by Certiorari. The order, ruling or decision of the Board shall
become final and executory unless an appeal is seasonably filed. The appeal or the
pendency thereof shall not bar execution or implementation of such order, ruling or
decision in the absence of a Temporary Restraining Order issued by the Court.

       SEC. 16. Requirements to Oil Companies. – Every oil company shall
maintain a minimum inventory requirement of thirty (30) day-supply of both crude and
refined oil as buffer stock to ensure the security of oil supply and shall regularly submit
to the Board indicative retail price which shall be unbundled as follows:

  a.   Landed cost of crude oil at the time of purchase; and

  b.   Additional costs to include port charges, brokerage and bank fees, wharfage,
       documentary stamp taxes, refining costs. Storage costs, and marketing and
       transportation costs.

       SEC. 17. Prohibited Acts and Penalties. – To ensure fair competition and
prevent cartels and monopolies in the oil industry, and to effectively attain the policy
and purposes of the State through this Act, the following acts are hereby declared
prohibited and illegal:

   a. Giving false or misleading data or information or willfully or though gross
      negligence, concealing or falsifying a material fact, in any investigation, inquiry,
      study or other proceedings held by the Board pursuant to this Act, which shall
      be punishable by imprisonment of not less than six (6) years and one (1) day
      nor more than seven (7) years and fine of not less than One Million Pesos
      (Php1,000,000.00) nor more than Two Million Pesos (Php2,000,000.00);

   b. Violation of any provision of this Act or any order, ruling, decision, or rule or
      regulation issued by the Board, which shall be punishable by imprisonment of
      not less than seven (7) years nor more than eight (8) years and a fine of not
      less than Two Million Pesos (Php2,000,000.00) nor more than Three Million
      Pesos (Php3,000,000.00);
   c. Predatory pricing, which means selling or offering to sell any petroleum product
      at a price unreasonably below the industry average cost to attract customers to
      the detriment of competitors, which shall be punishable by imprisonment of not
      less than seven (7) years nor more than eight (8) years and a fine of not less
      than Two Million Pesos (Php2,000,000.00) nor more than Three Million Pesos
      (Php3,000,000.00); and

   d. Cartelization, which means any agreement, combination or concerted action by
      oil refiners, importers, dealers or distributors or their representatives to fix
      prices, restrict outputs or divide markets, either by products or by areas, or
      allocating markets, either by products or by areas, in restraint or trade of free
      competition, which shall be punishable by imprisonment of not less than ten
      (10) years nor more than twelve (12) years and a fine of not less than Five
      Million Pesos (Php5,000,000.00) nor more than Eight Million Pesos
      (Php8,000,000.00).

       i.    If the offender is a juridical person, the penalty shall be imposed on the
             officer or officers, responsible for or permitting or causing the violation;
      ii.    If the offender is a Member of the Board or any government official or
             employee, the maximum of the penalty prescribed shall be imposed and
             the offender shall suffer the additional penalty of perpetual disqualification
             from holding any public office, without prejudice to any administrative
             action or criminal prosecution under other pertinent laws; and
      iii.   If the offender is an alien, in addition to the penalty herein provided, the
             offender shall be deported after service of sentence and shall not be
             permitted reentry into the Philippines.

       SEC. 18. Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR). – To ensure energy security,
price stability, and adequate supply of petroleum products, especially during periods
of global supply disruptions, emergencies, and extraordinary price volatility, the
government shall establish and maintain a Strategic Petroleum Reserve which shall
consist of government-owned stocks of crude oil and refined petroleum products to be
maintained for emergency use and market stabilization.

     The SPR shall maintain at least thirty (30) days of national petroleum
consumption, subject to periodic review.

      SEC. 19. Objectives of the SPR. - The SPR shall be maintained to:

   a. Provide an emergency buffer against supply disruptions;
   b. Stabilize domestic petroleum prices during periods of excessive volatility;
   c. Ensure continuous availability of fuel for critical sectors, including public
      transportation, agriculture, and national defense; and
   d. Support the implementation of government fuel subsidy and price mitigation
      programs.

      SEC. 20. Administration of the SPR. – The SPR shall be administered by the
DOE in coordination with the Board.

       SEC. 21. Mandatory Fuel Subsidy Program. – The government shall provide
a fuel subsidy to qualified public transport workers and other vulnerable groups to
mitigate the impact of increases in petroleum product prices. The subsidy shall be
automatically implemented upon the occurrence of any of the conditions set forth in
this Act.

   a. Coverage – The fuel subsidy shall be granted to:
      1. Drivers and operators of public utility jeepneys (PUJs), buses, UV Express,
         taxis, Transport Network Vehicle Services (TNVS), and other duly
         registered public utility vehicles;
      2. Other transport sectors as may be determined by the Department of
         Transportation (DOTr), in coordination with the Land Transportation
         Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB), Department of Social Welfare
         and Development (DSWD) and local government units (LGUs);
      3. Other vulnerable sectors as identified by the government.

   b. Trigger Mechanism. — The subsidy shall be released when:
        1. The average retail price of diesel or gasoline exceeds a threshold to be
           determined by the DOTr, in consultation with relevant agencies; or
        2. There is a sustained increase in global crude oil prices, as determined
           by the Department of Energy (DOE); or
        3. Such other conditions as may be prescribed in the Implementing Rules
           and Regulations (IRR) to address extraordinary price volatility.

   c. Amount of Subsidy. — The amount shall be determined by the DOTr, in
      coordination with the DOE and the Department of Budget and Management
      (DBM).

       SEC. 22. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the proper
implementation of this Act for the current year is hereby appropriated out of any funds
in the National Treasury not otherwise appropriated. Thereafter, the necessary
amount for the full implementation of this Act shall be included in the General
Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 23. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within sixty (60)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOE, in coordination with relevant agencies,
shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations.
      SEC. 24. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 25. Repealing Clause. – R.A. No. 8479, otherwise known as the
“Downstream Oil Deregulation Act of 1998,” is hereby repealed. All laws, decrees,
executive orders, and regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

       SEC. 26. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
