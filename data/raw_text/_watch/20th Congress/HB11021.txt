                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Flooding remains one of the most persistent and destructive natural
hazards in the Philippines, causing significant loss of life, displacement of
communities, and damage to infrastructure and the economy. The increasing
intensity of rainfall due to climate change, combined with rapid urbanization and
environmental degradation, has further heightened the country’s vulnerability
to flooding.

       Despite substantial investments in flood control infrastructure, many
existing systems remain fragmented, reactive, and insufficiently coordinated,
often focusing on isolated structural interventions rather than comprehensive,
basin-wide solutions.

      Japan is widely recognized as a global leader in flood management, having
developed highly advanced systems that integrate engineering excellence, river
basin management, land use planning, early warning systems, and community
preparedness.1 Its approach is characterized by a multi-layered flood control
strategy, combining structural and non-structural measures and grounded in
long-term planning and scientific data.

      This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize a National Flood Control
Benchmark Framework, drawing from Japan’s proven practices while adapting
them to Philippine conditions. The objective is not merely to replicate foreign
models, but to establish a unified, science-based standard for flood control
across the country, ensuring consistency, efficiency, and resilience.

       The measure is anchored on the State’s constitutional duty to protect the
right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology (Article II, Section 16) and
to promote the general welfare. It also complements existing laws such as the
Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010 and the Climate Change
Act of 2009, by strengthening the integration of infrastructure, environmental
management, and disaster preparedness.

      Importantly, the bill respects the mandates of existing agencies and the
autonomy of local government units, positioning the national framework as a
coordinating and standard-setting mechanism rather than a centralizing
authority.

       By adopting international best practices and institutionalizing integrated
flood control standards, this measure aims to enhance the country’s capacity to
prevent, mitigate, and respond to flooding, ultimately safeguarding lives,
protecting property, and promoting sustainable development.

     It is for the foregoing premises that the approval of this Bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
      ADOPTING A NATIONAL FLOOD CONTROL BENCHMARK BASED ON
    INTERNATIONAL BEST PRACTICES, INCLUDING JAPAN’S FLOOD
   MANAGEMENT STANDARDS, STRENGTHENING INTEGRATED RIVER
         BASIN MANAGEMENT, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “National Flood
Control Benchmark Act.”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect the people from the adverse impacts of flooding through science-
based, climate-resilient, and integrated flood control systems and adopt
international best practices, including Japan’s advanced flood management
standards, to enhance national capacity. Toward this end, the State shall ensure
sustainable infrastructure development consistent with Article II, Section 16 of
the Constitution (right to a balanced and healthful ecology).

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Flood Control Benchmark refers to engineering, environmental, and
          governance standards used to design and implement flood control
          systems;
      (b) Integrated River Basin Management (IRBM) refers to the coordinated
          management of water, land, and related resources within a river basin;
      (c) Multi-layered Flood Control System refers to a system combining
          structural (e.g., dams, levees) and non-structural measures (e.g.,
          zoning, early warning systems).

      SECTION 4. Adoption of National Flood Control Benchmark. - The State
shall adopt a National Flood Control Benchmark Framework, drawing from
Japan’s river basin-wide flood control planning, high-standard infrastructure
design, early warning and evacuation systems, and land use regulation and
floodplain management.

      The framework shall be adapted to Philippine conditions.

      SECTION 5. Establishment of Flood Control Benchmark Standards. - The
Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), in coordination with
relevant agencies, environmental groups, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)
organizations, shall establish:

      a. Minimum engineering design standards for flood control infrastructure;
      b. Basin-wide flood risk assessment protocols;
      c. Standards for urban drainage and flood mitigation systems;
      d. Climate-resilient and nature-based solutions.

      SECTION 6. Lead Implementing Agencies.– Implementation shall be
undertaken by Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) for the
infrastructure standards and projects, Department of Environment and Natural
Resources (DENR) for the watershed and river basin management, National
Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) for disaster
preparedness, Department of Science and Technology (DOST) for the flood
modeling and forecasting, and Local Government Units (LGUs) for land use and
zoning enforcement.

      SECTION 7. Integration with Local Government Plans. - LGUs shall align
their Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs) and disaster risk reduction plans
with the National Flood Control Benchmark Framework.

       SECTION 8. Non-Impairment of Existing Mandates. – Nothing in this Act
shall be construed to diminish the powers of DPWH, DENR, or LGUs and repeal
existing flood control laws, except insofar as inconsistent.

      The Act shall operate as a supplemental and harmonizing framework.

       SECTION 9. Data Sharing and Flood Information Systems. – All agencies
shall establish an integrated flood data system, ensuring real-time data sharing,
interoperability, public access to flood risk information.
      SECTION 10. Appropriation. – The amount necessary for the
implementation of the provisions of this Act shall be incorporated in the annual
General Appropriations Act.


       SECTION 11. Capacity-Building. - The government shall undertake
training and technical cooperation, including partnerships with Japan and
international institutions, for knowledge transfer.

     SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from effectivity, DENR, in coordination with relevant agencies, shall
promulgate the Implementing Rules and Regulations.

      SECTION 13. Separability Clause. – If for any reason, any part or provision
of this Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or
provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 15. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
