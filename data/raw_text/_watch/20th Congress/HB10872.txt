                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Reliable electricity is not a convenience for hospitals, it is a matter of life
and death. Ventilators, incubators, blood banks, operating rooms, dialysis
machines, and cold-chain storage for vaccines and medicines all depend on an
uninterrupted power supply. Yet many of our public hospitals, particularly those
in far-flung and off-grid areas, continue to grapple with unstable, intermittent,
or altogether absent electricity service. During typhoons, earthquakes, and other
calamities and public health emergencies, precisely when hospitals are needed
most, power interruptions place patients' lives at risk and compromise the
delivery of essential health services.

      At the same time, public hospitals that do enjoy stable grid access
shoulder some of the highest and most continuous electricity demands among
government facilities, placing a significant and recurring burden on public funds
that could otherwise be channeled toward direct patient care, medical supplies,
and health workforce needs.

       Solar energy offers a practical, sustainable, and increasingly cost-effective
solution to both dependence on costly and often unreliable conventional
electricity sources, lower long-term operating costs, and, when paired with
appropriate energy storage, provide a critical layer of resilience that keeps
essential hospital services running through outages, calamities, and
emergencies. This is consistent with the policy already established under
Republic Act No. 9513, or the "Renewable Energy Act of 2008," which promotes
the development and utilization of renewable energy to strengthen the country's
energy security and reduce its dependence on fossil fuels.

      This bill mandates the installation, integration, and maintenance of solar
energy systems in national hospitals, local government unit hospitals, state and
university hospitals, and other government-owned and controlled hospitals. To
ensure that this mandate is achievable rather than aspirational, the bill provides
for a phased implementation: covered hospitals are required to achieve solar
energy generation capability equivalent to at least twenty percent (20%) of their
annual electricity demand within one (1) year from the law's effectivity, and at
least fifty percent (50%) within three (3) years, subject to standards and
guidelines to be jointly established by the Department of Health (DOH) and the
Department of Energy (DOE), in coordination with other relevant agencies.

      The bill likewise directs covered hospitals to integrate energy storage and
backup capabilities where technically and financially feasible, so that critical
hospital facilities and services remain powered during grid outages and
emergencies.

       In recognition of the role of local industry in nation-building, the bill gives
priority to local businesses in the contracting and purchase of solar energy
systems from accredited solar photovoltaic installers, while ensuring that all
procurement remains fully compliant with existing bidding and procurement
laws and is conducted with transparency.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is urgently sought.

 AN ACT MANDATING THE USE AND UTILIZATION OF SOLAR ENERGY IN
 NATIONAL HOSPITALS, LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNIT HOSPITALS, STATE
 AND UNIVERSITY HOSPITALS, AND OTHER GOVERNMENT-OWNED AND
    -CONTROLLED HOSPITALS, AND PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Solar-Powered
Public Hospitals”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right to health and support the delivery of essential health
services, particularly in public hospitals during emergencies, calamities, and
public health crises, when reliable electricity is critical to preserving life.
Consistent with Republic Act No. 9513, or the “Renewable Energy Act of 2008,”
the State likewise promotes the development and utilization of renewable
energy to strengthen energy security, reduce dependence on fossil fuels, and
ensure a sustainable energy supply.

      The State recognizes that the high and continuous electricity demands of
public hospitals place a significant burden on public funds. The use of solar
energy, as an abundant, renewable, and sustainable resource, can reduce this
burden while ensuring the continuity of critical hospital services during power
interruptions and emergencies.

      SECTION 3. Definitions. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall mean:
      (a) Public hospital refers collectively to national hospitals, LGU hospitals,
      state and university hospitals, other agency hospitals, and GOCC
      hospitals as defined under this Act;

      (b) National hospital refers to a hospital owned and operated by the
      national government, including those under the direct supervision and
      control of the DOH or attached agencies thereof;

      (c) Local government hospital refers to a hospital established, operated,
      and maintained by a province, city, or municipality pursuant to Republic
      Act No. 7160, or the "Local Government Code of 1991";

      (d) State and university hospitals refer to hospitals owned, operated, and
      maintained by state universities and colleges (SUCs) as part of their
      health, training, and public service functions;

      (e) Government-owned and controlled hospitals refer to hospitals
      owned, operated, or controlled by government-owned or controlled
      corporations, as defined under Republic Act No. 10149, or the "GOCC
      Governance Act of 2011";

      (f) Power applications refer to renewable energy systems or facilities that
      produce electricity;

      (g) Off-Grid systems refer to electrical systems not connected to the wires
      and related facilities of the On-Grid Systems of the Philippines;

      (h) On-grid systems refers to electrical systems composed of
      interconnected transmission lines, distribution lines, substations, and
      related facilities for the purpose of conveyance of bulk power on the grid
      of the Philippines;
      (i) Solar energy refers to the energy derived from solar radiation that can
      be converted into useful thermal or electrical energy; and
      (j) Solar energy systems refer to energy systems which convert solar
      energy into thermal or electrical energy;


        SECTION 4. Coverage. – The Act applies to all public hospitals at the
primary, secondary, and tertiary levels of the Philippine Heath Care delivery
facilities. It shall cover installation, integration, and maintenance of solar energy
systems in:

         (a) Existing and public hospitals that are not connected to the
             electricity grid;
          (b) Existing public hospitals located in areas with intermittent,
              unstable, or unreliable electricity supply, as determined by the DOH
              and the DOE or any of their attached agencies, including the
              National Electrification Administration (NEA), and the National
              Power Corporation (NPC);

          (c) Existing public hospitals with stable grid access, which shall
              nonetheless be required to integrate a minimum level of soalr energy
              capacity as determined by the DOH and DOE; and

          (d) All newly constructed public hospitals, which shall be designed and
              constructed to be solar-ready, with the cost of solar energy systems
              integrated into the total construction cost of the school.

       SECTION 5. Solar Energy Generation. – All covered hospitals shall install
and utilize solar energy systems as a primary or supplemental source of
electricity, consistent with their energy requirements, available space, location,
and existing electrical infrastructure. Such solar energy systems shall be
designed to reduce dependence on conventional sources of electricity, lower
energy costs, and support the continuous delivery of essential hospital services.
Where technically and financially feasible, as determined by the DOE and DOH,
covered hospitals shall integrate energy storage and other appropriate backup
capabilities to ensure the availability of electricity for critical hospital facilities
and services during power interruptions, calamities, and emergencies.

      SECTION 6. Grid-Connected and Off-Grid Solar Energy Systems. – Covered
hospitals shall adopt solar energy systems appropriate to their location and
power supply conditions, as follows:

          (a) Grid-Connected Hospitals – Hospitals located in areas served by an
              existing and reliable electricity grid shall install grid-connected solar
              energy systems that may operate as on-site generation, subject to
              applicable laws, rules, and regulations. Where feasible, such
              systems shall be integrated with energy storage and backup systems
              to provide electricity to designated critical loads during grid outages;
          (b) Off-Grid or Unreliable Connected Hospitals – Hospitals located in
              off-grid areas or areas with unreliable, inadequate, or frequently
              interrupted electricity supply shall prioritize the installation of solar
              energy systems with sufficient energy storage and backup capability
              to ensure the continuous operation of critical hospital facilities and
              services. Such systems may operate as standalone or hybrid
              systems, as appropriate to local conditions; and
          (c) Hybrid and Other Appropriate Systems – Covered hospitals may
              adopt hybrid solar energy systems that combine solar generation
              with grid electricity, battery energy storage, or other duly authorized
            backup generation technologies, as may be necessary to meet their
            operational power requirements, subject to applicable technical,
            safety, and regulatory standards.

      SECTION 7. Mandatory Installation and Phased Implementation. – All
public hospitals covered by this Act shall, where feasible and technologically
practicable, progressively install and utilize solar energy systems in accordance
with the following phases:

         (a) Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, covered hospitals
             shall achieve a solar energy generation capability equivalent to at
             least twenty percent (20%) of their annual electricity demand;
         (b) Within three (3) years, covered hospitals shall achieve a solar
             generation capability equivalent to at least fifty percent (50%) of their
             average annual electricity demand; and
         (c) The installation and expansion of solar energy systems shall be
             undertaken in phases, taking into account the hospital’s energy
             requirements, available space, existing electrical infrastructure,
             structural capacity, technical feasibility, and other relevant
             conditions.

The DOH, in coordination with the DOE, the DPWH, the DILG, and the ERC, and
other concerned government agencies establish the standards and guidelines for
determining solar energy generation capability of covered hospitals, facilitate the
phased implementation of the requirements under this Section, and monitor
compliance therewith.

     SECTION 8. Sourcing for Solar Photovoltaic Installers. – In the contracting
and purchase of solar energy systems from accredited Solar PV installers, local
business shall be given priority in supplying the government.

      All bidding and procurement done for the purpose of this Act shall be in
accordance with the law and existing rules and regulations. The same shall be
made in public with all pertinent documents available.

      SECTION 9. Funding. – The amount necessary for the immediate
implementation of this Act shall be charged against any available fund of the
government agencies covered by this Act. Thereafter, such sums as may be
necessary to implement this Act shall be included in the General Appropriations
Act (GAA).

      SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
after the effectivity of this Act, the DOE shall, in coordination with the
Interagency Energy and Conservation Committee created under Republic Act No.
11285 or the Energy Efficiency and Conservation Act, and in consultation with
the DOH, the DPWH, DILG, and ERC, and other relevant government agencies
and stakeholders in the electric power and renewable energy industry, including
civil society organizations, people’s organizations, and other pertinent on-the-
ground stakeholders, promulgate the rules and regulations to effectively
implement the provisions of this Act.

       SECTION 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 13. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
