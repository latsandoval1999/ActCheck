EXPLANATORY NOTE

It is a normal biological process and a significant part of good health, but a great many
Filipinos, especially students and people coming from low-income families, have trouble getting
enough menstrual hygiene products due to their high cost and lack of availability. This situation
can have an impact on personal hygiene, on health, on attendance at school, on participation in
educational activities, and on personal dignity.

The Constitution of 1987 states that the right to health exists and requires the State to pursue
an integrated and comprehensive strategy for the development of health. Moreover, Article XIII,
Section 11 states that the State should aim at making essential goods and health and other social
services available to all people at an affordable cost, giving priority to the underprivileged, women,
and other vulnerable groups.

Republic Act No. 11223, which is also called the Universal Health Care Act, likewise
shows the government's dedication to equitable access to comprehensive health services. This law
considers preventive and promotive health services as an essential part of universal health care and
aims at protecting Filipinos from suffering financial difficulty due to their health needs.

The proposed measure does not assert that sanitary napkins currently constitute a specific
benefit under the Universal Health Care Act; instead, it aims at putting the principles of universal,
equitable, preventive, and promotive health into practice by means of a concrete national
intervention in the area of menstrual health and hygiene.

The availability of safe and sufficient menstrual hygiene products is closely linked to
preventive and promotive health; if suitable products are not available, people who menstruate
may have to use inadequate alternatives or have to last longer with the products they do have,
which could undermine proper hygiene. It follows that securing access to menstrual hygiene
products can help eliminate an avoidable obstacle to keeping one's health and dignity.

The educational aspects are just as important. Pupils who do not have access to menstrual
hygiene products might find it difficult to attend school or to take full part in classroom activities
during their periods. Giving these products in public schools can help make sure that menstruation
does not become a hindrance to education.

Republic Act No. 10354, which is known as the Responsible Parenthood and Reproductive
Health Act of 2012, also underscores the importance of accessible reproductive health information,
services, supplies, and facilities; menstrual health forms an important part of the wider

===== [page 2] =====
reproductive health continuum and must therefore be dealt with by suitable public health and
educational! measures.

A national Menstrual Health and Hygiene Programme is therefore set up to supply free
menstrual hygiene products in all public schools and public health centres. The measure also
includes menstrual health education so that people have access to the products and at the same
time receive accurate information on menstruation, proper hygiene and on the conditions which
may need medical treatment.

The measure in question is deliberately narrow in scope; it does not make any changes to
the current benefit package offered by the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation nor does it
decide to include menstrual hygiene products among the existing benefits under the Universal
Health Coverage scheme. Rather, it establishes a particular government programme, which will be
financed by the national budget and mainly carried out by the Department of Health and the
Department of Education.

The State can tackle a practical financial obstacle which affects access to health, education,
and dignity by supplying menstrual hygiene products at places where they are most required, such
as public schools and public health centers, and thus help achieve the wider goals of universal
health care.

Menstruation should not stand in the way of access to health, education, or human dignity.
Since it has both the constitutional obligation and the public health justification, the State should
take reasonable steps to make it possible for every Filipino to manage their menstruation safely
and with dignity, no matter their economic situation.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL MENSTRUAL HEALTH AND HYGIENE PROGRAM,
PROVIDING FREE MENSTRUAL HYGIENE PRODUCTS IN PUBLIC SCHOOLS AND
PUBLIC HEALTH CENTERS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “National Menstrual Health

and Hygiene Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is the policy of the State to protect and promote the
right to health of every Filipino and to ensure equitable access to essential health goods and

services.

The State recognizes menstrual health and hygiene as an important component of
preventive and promotive health. Access to safe and adequate menstrual hygiene products enables
menstruating persons to maintain proper hygiene, protect their health, participate in education, and

live with dignity.

Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the Universal Health Care Act, establishes
the State's commitment to equitable access to comprehensive health services, including preventive
and promotive health services, without financial hardship. While Republic Act No. 11223 does not

expressly provide sanitary napkins as a specific benefit, this Act seeks to advance its principles by

The State likewise recognizes the importance of reproductive health under Republic Act

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

(a) Menstrual hygiene products refer to sanitary napkins or pads, tampons, menstrual cups,
reusable menstrual products, period underwear, and other products intended to safely

manage menstrual blood and approved by the appropriate government authorities; and

(b) Public health centers refer to government-operated primary health care facilities,
including rural health units, urban health centers, barangay health stations, and other

government health facilities designated by the Department of Health (DOH).

SEC. 4. National Menstrual Health and Hygiene Program. — There is hereby established
a National Menstrual Health and Hygiene Program, jointly implemented by the DOH and the

Department of Education (DepEd).
The Program shall:
(a) Provide free menstrual hygiene products in public schools and public health centers,
(b) Promote menstrual health education and proper hygiene practices; and

(c) Establish standards and mechanisms for the procurement, distribution, monitoring, and

evaluation of menstrual hygiene products provided under this Act.

SEC. 5. Free Menstrual Hygiene Products in Public Schools. — All public elementary
and secondary schools shall make adequate menstrual hygiene products available free of charge to

students who need them.

The products shall be distributed through mechanisms that protect the privacy and dignity

of students.

No student shall be required to prove financial hardship or disclose personal medical

information as a condition for receiving menstrual hygiene products.

SEC. 6. Free Menstrual Hygiene Products in Public Health Centers. — Ail public health
centers shall make adequate menstrual hygiene products available free of charge to persons who

need them.

The DOH, in coordination with local government units, shall establish appropriate
mechanisms for procurement, distribution, storage, and replenishment, with priority given to

geographically isolated and disadvantaged communities.

SEC. 7. Menstrual Health Education. — The DepEd shall integrate age-appropriate

menstrual health and hygiene education into existing basic education and school health programs.

Such education shall include information on menstruation, proper hygiene practices,
appropriate use and disposal of menstrual hygiene products, recognition of conditions requiring

medical attention, and the reduction of stigma and misinformation concerning menstruation.
The DOH shall provide appropriate technical guidance and educational materials.

SEC. 8. Responsibilities of Implementing Agencies. — The DOH shall establish health,
safety, and quality standards for menstrual hygiene products provided under this Act and shall

oversee implementation in public health centers.

The DepEd shall oversee implementation in public schools and the integration of menstrual

health education into appropriate programs.

Local government units shall assist in implementation through their public health facilities

and may provide additional resources for the Program.

SEC. 9. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOH and DepEd, subject to existing

budgeting, procurement, accounting, and auditing laws.

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act

shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOH and DepEd, in coordination with relevant government agencies
and local government units, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its effective

implementation.

SEC. 11. Relation to Existing Health Laws. — Nothing in this Act shall be construed as
declaring menstrual hygiene products to be an existing benefit under Republic Act No. 11223 or

as amending the benefit package of the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation.

This Act shall instead serve as a specific legislative measure advancing the preventive,

promotive, equitable, and financially protective objectives of universal health care.

SEC. 12. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or

unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, regulations, and
other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or

modified accordingly.

SEC. 14. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in

the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
