                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Social media has become an important part of children's daily lives.
While it provides opportunities for communication, learning, and self-
expression, children may also be exposed to harmful content, unsafe
interactions, exploitation, and excessive or unhealthy use.
       Children need stronger protection in the digital environment. The State
must ensure that social media platforms adopt safeguards appropriate to a
child's age and development, while respecting children's rights to privacy,
access to information, and freedom of expression.
      This measure establishes clear standards for children's access to social
media. It prohibits children below thirteen (13) years of age from creating or
maintaining social media accounts and requires parental or guardian consent
and supervision for users aged thirteen (13) to below eighteen (18). It likewise
requires platforms to implement age verification, privacy and safety measures,
parental controls, content safeguards, and accessible reporting mechanisms.
       The measure also promotes transparency in the use of algorithms,
strengthens protection against harmful and misleading content, and provides
for digital literacy and responsible social media use in schools. It further
encourages communities to provide meaningful activities that promote healthy
interaction beyond the digital environment.
      Protecting children online is a shared responsibility. This measure seeks

to ensure that children can participate in the digital world in a manner that is
safe, responsible, and appropriate to their age and development.
      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
      ESTABLISHING SAFETY STANDARDS AND REGULATORY MEASURES
         FOR CHILDREN’S USE OF SOCIAL MEDIA AND PROMOTING
                  RESPONSIBLE DIGITAL ENGAGEMENT

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
           Congress assembled:


 1             SECTION 1. Short Title. – – This Act shall be known as the “Children’s
 2      Social Media Safety Act.”

 3             SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – – The State recognizes the vital role of the
 4      youth in nation building and shall promote and protect their physical, moral,
 5      spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being. The State likewise recognizes the
 6      growing influence of social media on children’s development and the risks arising
 7      from their exposure to harmful content, unsafe interactions, and design features that
 8      encourage excessive or compulsive use.
 9             Accordingly, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to regulate children's
10      access to and use of social media, ensure that social media platforms provide safe
11      and age appropriate digital environments, and adopt measures that protect children
12      from online harm while protecting their rights to access information, privacy, and
13      freedom of expression.

 2   be defined as follows:
 3         (a) Age verification measures – refer to verifiable, privacy preserving, and
 4             sound methods used to determine or reasonably estimate the age of a user,
 5             including age verification technologies, age estimation systems, and other
 6             comparable mechanisms, designed to ensure accuracy and reliability
 7             while minimizing the collection and processing of personal data in
 8             accordance with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data
 9             Privacy Act of 2012”;
10         (b) Algorithms – refer to any computational process, set of rules, or
11             automated process, including machine learning systems, used to process
12             data and determine or influence the selection, ranking, prioritization,
13             recommendation, or display of content, information, or advertisements on
14             a social media platform;
15         (c) Artificially generated or altered content – refers to content that has been
16             created, modified, or manipulated through automated or digital means in
17             a manner that does not accurately reflect real events, persons, or
18             statements, and which may mislead or deceive users as to its authenticity,
19             including content that realistically depicts a person as saying or doing
20             something that the person did not actually say or do;
21         (d) Child – refers to a person who is below eighteen (18) years of age
22             consistent with existing Philippine laws, including but not limited to the
23             Civil Code of the Philippines, Republic Act No. 7610 (Special Protection
24             of Children Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act), and
25             Republic Act No. 9344 (Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006);
26         (e) Digital system – refers to any system, tool, or process that operates using

27             data, software, or programmed instructions, including one or more

15          SEC. 4. Absolute Prohibition. – No child below thirteen (13) years of age

16   shall be allowed to create, maintain, or use an account on any social media platform.

17   SMPs shall:

18          (a) Prohibit the creation, registration and use of accounts by children below

19             thirteen (13) years of age;

20          (b) Implement effective, reliable, and continuous age verification measures

21             to enforce this prohibition;

20            absence of such consent;

21         (d) Periodic re-verification of age and parental or guardian consent under the

 1          (e) Provision of accessible tools that enables parents or guardians to monitor,

 2             manage, and control the adolescent user’s account, including privacy

 4          (f) Application of the highest level of privacy and safety settings by default.

 5          SEC. 6. Platform Safeguards for Children’s Accounts. – SMPs identified
 6   by DICT as accessible in the Philippines, and which are used or reasonably likely to
 7   be used by children, shall ensure that accounts belonging to children at least thirteen
 8   (13) years of age but below eighteen (18) years of age are subject to the following
 9   safeguards:
10          (a) Implement age verification measures; Provided, That any personal data
11             collected for this purpose shall be limited to what is strictly necessary,
12             securely processed and stored, and used solely in accordance with the
13             provisions of this Act, in full compliance with the Data Privacy Act;
14          (b) Require verifiable parental or guardian consent prior to account creation
15             and as a continuing requirement for access;
16          (c) Provide adequate, accessible and efficient notification and control
17             mechanisms to enable parents, or guardians to exercise the following
18             functions, including:
19                 (1) Supervise the child’s account and access its privacy settings;
20                 (2) Set daily time restrictions and implement breaks during which the
21                     child cannot access the account; and
22                 (3) Withdraw consent for a child’s continued access as an account
23                     holder;
24          (d) Establish appropriate limitations and restrictions on the use of, and
25             interactions with the account of a child;
26          (e) Ensure that platform features relating to the account of a child:

 1                (1) Provide tools that allow parents or guardians to supervise use,
 2                    manage privacy settings, and set reasonable time restrictions;
 3                (2) Include controls that track and limit the duration and frequency of
 4                    use;
 5                (3) Restrict the access to, and sharing of the geolocation of the child;
 6                (4) Restrict the purchases and financial transactions by the child,
 7                    where applicable;
 8                (5) Prevent automatic redirection to external websites that may expose
 9                    the child to harm; and
10                (6) Apply the highest level of privacy and safety settings by default
11                    for accounts owned by a child;
12         (f) Ensure that algorithms and digital systems used to select, recommend, or
13             display content:
14                (1) Do not promote prohibited content;
15                (2) Do not design processes that mislead children or are detrimental to
16                    their development;
17         (g) Take reasonable steps to detect, limit, and mitigate exposure to prohibited
18             content;
19         (h) Ensure that artificially generated or altered content that falsely depicts a
20             person or a child or is reasonably likely to mislead or deceive is removed.
21         (i) Develop and maintain a readily accessible and user-friendly reporting
22             mechanism that allows children, parents, or guardians to report harmful
23             content, unsafe interactions, or violations of this Act, and ensure that
24             appropriate action is taken within a reasonable period.

25      All measures, systems, and processes implemented under this Act shall be
26   necessary and proportionate to the objective of protecting children from harm.

 1          SEC. 7. Mandatory Digital Literacy and Ethical Use Program. – The
 2   DICT shall, in collaboration with Department of Education (DepEd), integrate a
 3   “Digital Literacy and Ethical Use of Social Media” module into the K-12 basic
 4   education curriculum. This program shall focus on:
 5          (a) Critical evaluation of online information and awareness of algorithmic
 6              influence;
 7          (b) Recognition and reporting of cyberbullying, online exploitation, and
 8              harmful content;
 9          (c) Promoting positive digital citizenship and ethical online interaction; and
10          (d) Strategies for maintaining digital well-being and managing healthy screen
11              time.
12          The DICT shall also conduct periodic training for teachers and awareness
13   seminars for parents and children below eighteen (18) years of age to ensure a
14   consistent approach to digital safety both in school and at home.
15          SEC. 8. Monitoring and Enforcement. – The DICT shall:
16          (a) Establish and maintain a monitoring and reporting system to track
17              compliance and reserve complaints to ensure and protect the rights of
18              children in matters relating to information communications technology
19              (ICT) under this Act;
20          (b) Notify the SMPs and provide details on violations identified in its
21              monitoring efforts or complaints received from third parties, and require
22              the platform to take appropriate action within a reasonable period, which
23              shall not exceed ninety (90) days from receipt of this notice: Provided,
24              That the SMPs shall promptly notify the account holder concerned and
25              provide an opportunity to respond or contest the allegation within a
26              reasonable period to be prescribed under the implementing rules and
27              regulations: Provided, further, That the SMPs concerned shall evaluate
28              the response and take appropriate action consistent with this Act and

14          SEC. 9. Restricting or Blocking Access to Prohibited Accounts. – When a
15   registered account or page on any social media platform is found to be in violation
16   of the provisions of this Act, the DICT may, after prior notice and opportunity to be
17   heard, issue an order to restrict or block access to such account or page: Provided,
18   That the National Telecommunications Commission shall be notified to monitor
19   compliance with such order.
20          SEC. 10. Duty of Social Media Platforms. – SMPs shall comply with the
21   cease-and-desist orders within forty-eight (48) hours of the issuance of the cease-
22   and-desist order and must disable the pages bearing said accounts of children
23   prohibited under this Act by implementing an effective technical measure to disable
24   or prevent access which may include:
25             (a) Prioritizing the disabling of accounts of children below thirteen (13)
26                 years of age;
27             (b) Preventing repeated fraudulent account creation intended to
28                 circumvent this Act;

 1             (c) Blocking domain names that provide access to the prohibited pages or
 2                 accounts, including through domain name system blocking (DNS
 3                 blocking);
 4             (d) Blocking uniform resource locators (URLs) that provide access to the
 5                 prohibited pages or accounts;
 6             (e) Blocking internet protocol (IP) addresses that provide access to the

10          SEC. 11. Transparency and Use of Algorithms. – SMPs shall ensure
11   transparency in the use of algorithms and digital systems, particularly in relation to
12   children, and shall:
13          (a) Provide clear, accessible, and understandable information to users,
14             including children and their parents or guardians, on how content is
15             selected, recommended, or displayed on the platform;
16          (b) Disclose when digital systems or algorithms, are used to influence or
17             determine the content shown to a user;
18          (c) Ensure that the operation of algorithms and digital systems is subject to
19             meaningful human oversight, including timely review and intervention by
20             natural persons, particularly where content, recommendations, or actions
21             may affect the safety or well-being of children; and
22          (d) Submit periodic transparency reports to the DICT at intervals prescribed
23             by the DICT, detailing the use of algorithms and digital systems, the
24             measures adopted to ensure age appropriate content and protect children
25             from harm, and the actions taken to detect, label, restrict, or remove
26             artificially generated or altered content.
27          SEC. 12. Prevention of Circumvention. – SMPs shall implement robust
28   technical measures to prevent, detect or mitigate the use of tools, including virtual




                                                                                        10
 1   private networks (VPNs) or proxy services, that are used to circumvent age-
 2   verification systems. In the implementation of these measures, the platform shall:
 3          (a) Regularly update its detection algorithms to identify patterns of traffic
 4             associated with circumvention services;
 5          (b) Notify users who are flagged for using circumvention tools that must
 6             verify their age or provide evidence of their age through authorized,
 7             privacy-preserving verification methods to continue accessing the
 8             service; and
 9          (c) Ensure that such detection mechanisms do not infringe upon the user’s
10             right to data privacy and are consistent with the Data Privacy Act.
11          SEC. 13. Penalties. – SMPs that fail or refuse to act upon requests to take
12   down prohibited accounts of children shall be liable as follows:
13          (a) For the first violation, a fine of Five million pesos (P5,000,000.00);
14          (b) For the second violation, a fine of Seven million five hundred thousand
15             pesos (P7,500,000.00); and
16          (c) For the third and subsequent violations, a fine of Ten million pesos
17             (P10,000,000.00).
18          SMPs that fail to comply with the obligations under Section 4 and Section 8
19   of this Act shall be liable to a fine of not less than Twenty million pesos
20   (P20,000,000.00) but not more than Fifty million pesos (P50,000,000.00) for each
21   violation, depending on the nature, gravity, duration, frequency, and impact of the
22   violation, including the number of affected child users.
23          In addition to the foregoing fines, SMPs found non-compliant with Section 4
24   of this Act, despite prior notice and opportunity to comply, may, upon
25   recommendation by the DICT and after due process, be subject to temporary
26   restriction of access within the Philippines until full compliance is achieved, or, in
27   cases of repeated and serious violations, be prohibited from operating in the
28   Philippines.

 1          SEC. 14. Promotion of Community-Based Recreational Activities. – To
 2   reduce excessive reliance on social media and internet consumption as a primary
 3   source of recreation, all local government units (LGUs) are hereby mandated to
 4   develop and implement age-appropriate, community-based programs for children
 5   below thirteen (13) years old. The LGUs, through their Local Council for the
 6   Protection of Children (LCPC), shall:
 7          (a) Designate and maintain “device-free” community spaces, such as public
 8             parks, libraries, and youth centers;
 9          (b) Organize regular extracurricular activities, including sports, arts, and
10             civic engagement programs, designed to encourage physical activity and
11             social interaction; and
12          (c) Allocate a portion of their annual budget, as may be determined by
13             existing laws, for the development and sustainability of these programs.
14          SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
15   from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Information and Communications
16   Technology shall, in coordination with the Secretaries of the Department of Justice,
17   Department of Education, Department of the Interior and Local Government
18   (DILG), heads of other appropriate government agencies, and other stakeholders,
19   issue the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this
20   Act.
21          SEC. 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
22   declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining parts or provisions not affected
23   remain in full force and effect.
24          SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
25   proclamations, rules and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the
26   provisions of this Act are amended or repealed accordingly.
27          SEC. 18. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
28   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
