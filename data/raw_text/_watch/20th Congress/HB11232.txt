                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Public solid waste management workers perform one of the most essential yet
often overlooked public services. Every day, they collect, transport, process, and
dispose of solid waste, ensuring the cleanliness of communities, protecting public
health, and safeguarding the environment. Their work is indispensable to the
effective implementation of ecological solid waste management programs and the
promotion of sustainable urban and rural development.

      Despite the hazardous nature of their duties, public solid waste management
workers continue to face numerous challenges, including prolonged exposure to
harmful substances, infectious waste, toxic chemicals, extreme weather conditions,
and occupational accidents. Many of them also endure inadequate compensation,
limited access to healthcare and insurance, and the absence of comprehensive legal
framework that recognizes and protects their rights and welfare.

      While existing laws, such as Republic Act No. 9003 or the Ecological Solid
Waste Management Act of 2000, provide the policy framework for solid waste
management, they do not comprehensively address the employment conditions,
occupational safety, benefits, and professional development of the workers who
carry out these vital functions. It is therefore imperative to establish a Magna Carta
that institutionalize their rights, promote their welfare, ensure safe and humane
working conditions, and recognize their invaluable contribution to society.

     This proposed measure seeks to provide public solid waste management
workers with just compensation, hazard pay, appropriate personal protective
equipment, health benefits, opportunities for training and capacity development, and
protection against discrimination and unjust labor practices, among others. It
likewise mandates the adoption of occupational safety standards and appropriates
the necessary funds to ensure the effective implementation of these benefits.

     By recognizing the dignity of their profession and investing in their welfare,
the State affirms its commitment to protecting workers who perform vital
environmental and public health services while strengthening the country’s solid
waste management system.

    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF PUBLIC SOLID
   WASTE MANAGEMENT WORKERS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
           THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Magna Carta of Public
Solid Waste Management Workers Act."
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
recognize the indispensable role of public solid waste management workers in
safeguarding public health, protecting the environment, promoting ecological
balance, and ensuring the effective implementation of ecological solid waste
management programs.
       The State shall protect and promote the rights and welfare of public solid
waste management workers by ensuring humane working conditions, just and
equitable compensation, occupational safety and health, adequate social protection,
opportunities for training and capacity development, and protection against
discrimination, harassment, exploitation, and unsafe working conditions. Towards
this end, the State shall adopt measures that enhance their dignity, improve their
quality of life, and strengthen the delivery of public solid waste management
services.
SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:
      a. Public Solid Waste Management Workers refer to all personnel employed
      by national government, local government units (LGUs), government owned
      and controlled corporations (GOCCs), state universities and colleges (SUCs),
      and other government instrumentalities, whether on a regular, casual,
      contractual, coterminous, or other lawful employment status, who are directly
      engaged in the collection, segregation, transfer, transport, recycling,
      composting, treatment processing, recovery, disposal, monitoring, regulation,
      enforcement, maintenance, operation, and administration of solid waste
      management facilities, equipment, and programs.
      b. Solid Waste Management refers to the systematic administration of
      activities which provide for the segregation, collection, storage, transfer,
      transport, processing, recycling, composting, recovery, treatment, and
      disposal of solid waste in accordance with existing environmental laws.
      c. Hazardous Working Conditions refer to work environment where
      employees are exposed to infectious materials, hazardous chemicals, toxic
      substances, sharp objects, noxious odors, smoke, dust, extreme weather
      conditions, vermin, dangerous machinery, heavy equipment, vehicular traffic,
      and other conditions that pose risks to life, health, or safety
      d. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) refers to protective clothing and
      equipment including gloves, safety shoes, face masks or respirators, goggles,
      helmets, reflective vests, rain gear, protective suits, hearing protection
      devices, and other protective equipment necessary to safeguard workers
      against occupational hazards
      e. Occupational Safety and Health Standards refer to the standards prescribed
      under existing laws, rules and regulations governing workplace safety and
      health
SEC. 4. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to all Public Solid Waste Management
Workers including:
a. Waste collectors and hauling personnel;
b. Garbage truck drivers and helpers;
c. Streetsweepers under Solid Waste Management (SWM) operations;
d. Landfill personnel;
e. Materials Recovery Facility personnel;
f. Waste segregation and recycling workers;
g. Drainage clearing personnel under SWM operations;
h. Equipment operators assigned to SWM activities; and
i. Supervisory, technical, and field personnel directly engaged in SWM operations.
SEC. 5. Rights of Public Solid Waste Management Workers. – Public solid waste
management workers shall have the following rights:
    a. To receive just and equitable compensation and benefits;
    b. To a safe and healthy workplace consistent with occupational safety and
health standards;
    c. To be provided, free of charge, with complete and appropriate personal
protective equipment and uniforms;
     d. To receive hazard pay, night shift differential and overtime pay, laundry
allowance and other allowances as provided under this act;
     e. To free periodic medical examinations, vaccinations, mental health services,
and occupational health programs;
     f. To opportunities for training, competency and capacity development;
     g. To organize and participate in lawful organizations or associations;
      h. To protection against discrimination, harassment, violence, intimidation,
retaliation, and other forms of abuse arising from the performance of official duties;
and
     i. To such other rights and benefits as may be provided by law.
SEC. 6. Recruitment and Qualification Standards. – Recruitment policy and
minimum requirements with respect to the selection and appointment of a public
solid waste management worker shall be developed and implemented by the
appropriate government agencies concerned in accordance with policies and
standards of the Civil Service Commission.
SEC. 7. Normal Hours of Work. – The normal hours of work of public solid waste
management workers shall be eight (8) hours a day or forty (40) hours a week,
exclusive of time for meals, unless a different work schedule is required by the
exigencies of the service or is otherwise authorized by law.
     Where the nature of the service requires continuous operation, work schedules
may be arranged on a shifting basis, provided that no worker shall be required to
render service beyond the prescribed hours except in cases of emergency or when
public interest so requires, and subject to the payment of the appropriate
compensation and benefits under existing laws and this Act.
SEC. 8. Overtime Work and Compensation. – Public solid waste management
workers who are required to render service beyond the normal hours of works, or
during rest days or legal holidays, shall be entitled to overtime compensation in
accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations, or to compensatory time-off
where authorized.
     In no case shall a worker be compelled to render overtime work without the
corresponding compensation or compensatory time-off, except during emergencies
involving threats to public health, public safety, natural or human-induced disasters,
or others analogous circumstances.
SEC. 9. Hazard Pay. - Public solid waste management workers whose duties
expose them to hazardous conditions, including but not limited to exposure to
infectious waste, toxic or hazardous substances, noxious gases, contaminated
materials, vermin, disease-causing organisms, dangerous machinery, heavy
equipment, extreme weather conditions, and other occupational hazards, shall
receive hazard pay equivalent to at least twenty-five percent (25%) of their monthly
basic salary.
SEC. 10. Laundry and Uniform Maintenance Allowance. – Public solid waste
management workers who are required to wear uniforms and personal protective
equipment in the performance of their duties shall be entitled to a laundry and
uniform maintenance allowance, in addition to the annual clothing allowance
authorized under existing laws.
      The amount and guidelines for the grant of the allowance shall be prescribed
by the DBM in consultation with the CSC and government agencies concerned.
SEC. 11. Transportation Allowance. Public solid waste management workers who
are assigned to field operations, waste collection routes, sanitary landfills, transfer
stations, materials recovery facilities, composting facilities, and other off-site
workplaces shall be entitled to a transportation allowance or transportation service,
subject to the availability of funds and applicable budgeting, accounting, and
auditing rules.
      Nothing herein shall preclude LGUs and other local government agencies from
granting higher transportation benefits consistent with law.
SEC. 12. Night Shift Differential. – Public solid waste management workers whose
regular work schedule falls between ten o’clock in the evening (10:00 p.m.) and six
o’clock in the morning (6:00 a.m.) shall be entitled to a night shift differential in
accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations governing government
personnel.
     Such benefits shall be in addition to hazard pay and other benefits provided
under this Act.
SEC. 13. Personal Protective equipment and Safety Clothing Uniforms. – All
public solid waste management workers shall be provided, free of charge, with
appropriate, complete, and quality personal protective equipment (PPE), safety
clothing uniforms and other protective gear commensurate with the nature and
hazards of their respective duties.
      The minimum PPE and safety uniforms to be provided by the concerned
government agency, GOCC, SUC, or LGU, shall include, as applicable, the
following:
       (a) For garbage collectors and waste collection workers: safety helmets or hard
hats, high-visibility reflective vests or uniforms, heavy-duty puncture-and cut-
resistant gloves, safety boots or other appropriate protective footwear, face masks or
respirators, eye and face protection, protective coveralls or work clothing, and
raincoats or other appropriate rain gear.
      (b) For street sweepers and road cleaning workers: high-visibility reflective
uniforms or vests, protective gloves, safety boots or closed protective footwear, face
masks or respirators, protective eyewear, wide-brimmed hats or equivalent head
protection, and raincoats or other appropriate rain gear.
      (c) For workers assigned to materials recovery facilities, recycling centers,
composting facilities, transfer stations, and similar facilities: safety helmets, high-
visibility reflective uniforms or vests, cut-and puncture-resistant gloves, safety
boots, protective eyewear, face masks or appropriate respirators, protective
coveralls, hearing protection, and other PPE appropriate to the machinery, materials,
and process involved.
       (d) For workers assigned to sanitary landfills and waste disposal facilities:
safety helmets, high-visibility reflective uniforms or vests, heavy-duty gloves, safety
boots, protective coveralls, eye and face protection, appropriate respiratory
protection, hearing protection, and other PPE necessary to protect against chemical,
biological, physical, and mechanical hazards;
        (e) For drainage clearing personnel and workers engaged in the clearing,
desilting, cleaning, maintenance, or removal of solid waste, debris, silt, and other
obstruction from drainage systems, canals, waterways, culverts, and similar
facilities: Waterproof protective clothing or coveralls, heavy-duty puncture-and-cut-
resistant gloves, safety boots or waterproof protective footwear, safety helmets, face
masks or appropriate respirators, eye and face protection, life jackets or other
appropriate floatation devices where there is a risk of drowning, and other PPE
appropriate to the hazards of the work;
      (f) For equipment operators assigned to solid waste management activities,
including the operation of garbage trucks, dump trucks, compactors, loaders,
excavators, bulldozers, shredders, and other heavy equipment and machinery: safety
helmets, high-visibility reflective uniforms or vests, safety boots, protective gloves,
eye and face protection, hearing protection, appropriate respiratory protection, and
other PPE necessary for the safe operation of the equipment and protection against
occupational hazards.
       (g) For other public solid waste management workers: such PPE, safety
clothing uniforms, and protective equipment as may be determined by the nature of
their duties and the occupational hazards to which they are exposed.
      The PPE and safety uniforms provided under this Section shall be issued to
every covered worker annually and shall be considered sufficient for a period of one
(1) year, unless earlier replacement is warranted by the nature of the work, excessive
wear and tear, damage, contamination, loss of protective quality, or change in
assignment.
      Each covered worker shall be provided with enough sets of PPE and safety
uniforms as may be prescribed by the concerned agencies, taking into consideration
the frequency of work, exposure to waste and contaminants, prevailing weather
conditions, and the need for regular cleaning and replacement.
      No public solid waste management worker shall be required or permitted to
perform collection, sweeping, segregation, processing, transportation, treatment,
disposal, or other hazardous solid waste management activities without the
appropriate PPE and safety uniforms required for the work assignment.
SEC. 14. Occupational safety and Health Standards. – The DENR, in
coordination with DOLE, DOH, CSC, and DILG, shall establish, implement, and
regularly update occupational safety and health standards specifically applicable to
public solid waste management operations.
      Such standards shall cover, among others, safe handling and disposal of solid
waste, exposure control measure, sanitation protocols, emergency response
procedures, and the prevention of occupational injuries and diseases. All concerned
agencies and LGUs shall ensure strict compliance with such standards and shall
allocate sufficient resources for their effective implementation.
SEC. 15. Medical Surveillance and Health Services. – All public solid waste
management workers shall be entitled to regular medical surveillance, periodic
health examinations, and access to basic occupational health services at no cost to
the worker.
      The DOH, in coordination with DOLE, DENR, DILG and other concerned
agencies, shall ensure the establishment of appropriate health monitoring programs,
including baseline and periodic medical examinations, immunization programs, and
early detection and treatment of work- related illnesses.
     Workers diagnosed with work-related illnesses or injuries shall be entitled to
appropriate medical treatment, rehabilitation services, and where applicable,
compensation benefits under existing laws.
SEC. 16. Protection of Employment and Prioritization for Regularization. – No
public solid waste management worker shall be removed, suspended, or demoted
except for just or authorized causes and after due process of law. Casual, contractual,
or job order workers performing solid waste management functions shall, to the
extent allowed by law, be accorded protection against arbitrary termination and shall
be prioritized for regularization or conversion to permanent positions when qualified
and when positions are available.
SEC. 17. Training and Capacity Development. – The DENR, in coordination with
CSC, DOLE, TESDA, and DILG, shall develop and implement a comprehensive
and continuous training program for public solid waste management workers.
      Such training shall include waste segregation and management technique,
occupational safety and health, emergency response, use of equipment and
machinery, environmental protection, and other relevant skills necessary for the
effective performance of duties.
     LGUs and concerned agencies shall ensure that all public solid waste
management workers are given access to regular upskilling and retooling
opportunities to enhance efficiency, safety, and career development.
SEC. 18. Grievance Machinery. - A grievance machinery shall be established in
all government agencies, GOCCs, SUCs, and LGUs employing public solid waste
management workers to address complaints, disputes, and concerns relating to
employment conditions, benefits, and workplace safety.
     The grievance machinery shall be designed to ensure prompt, impartial, and
accessible resolution of issues, without prejudice to the right of workers to seek
redress through appropriate administrative or judicial forums.
SEC. 19. Prohibition Against Discrimination and Retaliation. – No public solid
waste management worker shall be subjected to discrimination, harassment, or
retaliation for asserting rights under this Act, reporting unsafe working conditions,
or participating in lawful union or employee organization activities.
     Any act of discrimination or retaliation shall be subject to administrative, civil,
and criminal liability as may be provided by law.
SEC. 20. Merit, Performance and Service Recognition. – A system of recognition
and incentives shall be established to reward public solid waste management
workers who demonstrate exemplary performance, innovation, efficiency, or
extraordinary service in the delivery of solid waste management services.
     Awards and incentives may include commendations, certificates, performance
incentives, training opportunities, career advancement, and other forms of
recognition, subject to existing laws, rules and regulations.
SEC. 21. Right to Organize. – Public solid waste management workers shall have
the right to form, join, or assist employees’ organizations or associations for
purposes not contrary to law.
    No worker shall be discriminated against or subjected to retaliation by reason
of membership in or lawful participation in the activities of a duly recognized
employees’ organization.
    Nothing in this Act shall be construed as impairing the rights of government
employees under existing civil service laws and regulations.
SEC. 22. Right to Information and Participation. – Public solid waste
management workers shall be informed and consulted on policies, programs, and
measures that directly affect their working conditions, occupational safety and
health, compensation, welfare, and career development.
       Government agencies and LGUs shall establish appropriate mechanisms for
consultation and participation of workers or their duly recognized representatives in
the formulation and implementation of policies affecting their welfare and working
conditions.
SEC. 23. Social Protection and Welfare Services. – Public solid waste
management workers shall be entitled to the benefits and services provided under
existing social protection laws, including those administered by the Government
Service Insurance System (GSIS), Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
(PhilHealth), Home Development Mutual Fund (HDMF), Employees’
Compensation Commission (ECC), and other social protection institutions.
      The concerned government agencies and LGUs shall ensure that all covered
workers are properly registered with and continuously covered by applicable social
protection programs.
      No employer shall evade its obligations under existing social protection laws
by engaging workers under arrangements designed to defeat or circumvent their
statutory benefits and protections.
SEC. 24. Welfare and Support Facilities. – National government agencies,
GOCCs, SUCs and LGUs employing public solid waste management workers shall
provide, whenever practicable and appropriate to the nature of the work, adequate
facilities for the workers, including clean and safe changing rooms, washing and
bathing facilities, toilets, rest areas, drinking water, first-aid stations, and storage
facilities for personal protective equipment and work uniforms.
    Such facilities shall be maintained in a sanitary and safe condition and shall
comply with applicable occupational safety and hazards standards.
SEC. 25. Worker’s Safety and Emergency Protection. – No public solid waste
management worker shall be required to perform an activity that presents an
imminent and serious danger to life or health without appropriate safety measures
and protective equipment.
     In the event of an accident, fire, hazardous material incident, natural disaster,
epidemic, or other emergency involving solid waste management operations, the
concerned agency or LGU shall immediately activate appropriate emergency
response and worker protection measures.
      Workers shall receive appropriate training in emergency response, first aid,
evacuation procedures, incident reporting, and other safety measures relevant to their
duties.
SEC. 26. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, DENR, DILG, DOLE, DOH, CSC, DBM, TESDA, and
other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for
the effective implementation of this Act.
      In the formulation of the implementing rules and regulations, the concerned
agencies shall consult public solid waste management workers and their duly
recognized organizations, LGUs, and other relevant stakeholders.
SEC. 27. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the national
government agencies, GOCCs, SUCs, LGUs, and other government
instrumentalities concerned, subject to existing budgeting, accounting and auditing
laws, rules, and regulations.
     Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation
of this Act shall be included in the Annual General Appropriations Act for the
national agencies, GOCCs, SUCs and other government instrumentalities
concerned.
      The corresponding funding requirements of LGUs shall likewise be provided
in their respective annual budgets, subject to the provisions of Republic Act No.
7160, otherwise known as the “Local Government Code of 1991,” and other
applicable laws.
SEC. 28. Non-Diminution of Benefits. - Nothing in this Act shall be construed as
reducing, diminishing, or otherwise adversely affecting the existing salaries,
benefits, privileges, and other rights of public solid waste management workers
under existing laws, rules and regulations, collective negotiation agreements, local
ordinances, or other issuances.
    All benefits currently enjoyed by covered workers which are higher than those
provided under this Act shall continue to be granted.
SEC. 29. Penalties. – Any person who willfully violates the rights and benefits of
public solid waste management workers provided under this Act, or any responsible
officer who willfully refuses or unjustifiably fails to comply with the mandatory
provisions of this Act, shall be subject to appropriate administrative, civil, and
criminal liability under existing laws, without prejudice to the imposition of
penalties that may be prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations.
      If the offender is a public officer or employee, the violation shall constitute a
ground for appropriate administrative proceedings, without prejudice to criminal and
civil liability.
SEC. 30. Separability Clause. – if any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.
SEC. 31. Repealing Clause. – all laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
SEC. 32. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
