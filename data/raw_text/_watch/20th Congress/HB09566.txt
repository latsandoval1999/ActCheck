                                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

    Adolescent pregnancy remains a persistent and urgent public health and social
development challenge in the Philippines. Despite efforts by the government and civil
society, the country continues to record thousands of births among girls aged 10 to 19
each year, underscoring the need for stronger, coordinated, and evidence-based policy
interventions.

       Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) and the National Demographic
and Health Survey (NDHS) show that in 2018 alone, 183,967 adolescents aged 10 to 19
gave birth, followed by 180,916 in 2019, 136,302 in 2021, and 150,138 in 2022. These
figures illustrate that adolescent pregnancy remains a persistent national concern that
demands sustained policy attention.

       More alarming is the rising number of pregnancies among very young adolescents
aged 10 to 14 years old. Recorded births in this age group increased from 2,411 in 2019,
2,113 in 2020, and 2,320 in 2021, to 3,135 in 2022, 3,343 in 2023, and 3,612 in 2024.1
According to the Commission on Population and Development (CPD), an average of
twenty-four (24) babies are born to teenage mothers every hour in the Philippines,
highlighting the magnitude of the issue2. Even more concerning, 95.1 percent of babies
born to adolescent mothers below fifteen years old are born out of wedlock, exposing

        Adolescent pregnancy carries profound and long-term consequences. Young
mothers face higher risks of maternal complications, while their infants are more likely to
experience poor health outcomes. According to Save the Children Philippines, educational
disruption is also a significant consequence with 71.4 percent of teenage pregnancies
involving adolescents who have no formal education or who are out of school. Early
pregnancy often forces young girls to abandon their education, limiting their employment
opportunities and perpetuating intergenerational cycles of poverty and inequality. Beyond
the social impact, adolescent pregnancy imposes an estimated P33 billion economic
burden annually. Addressing adolescent pregnancy is therefore not only a public health
priority but also a critical investment in the country’s human capital and sustainable
development.

       This proposed measure is firmly grounded in the 1987 Constitution, which
mandates the State to protect and promote the right to health of the people (Art. XIII,
Sec. 11), recognize the vital role of the youth in nation-building (Art. II, Sec. 13), ensure
the fundamental equality of women and men (Art. II, Sec. 14), and strengthen the Filipino
family as the foundation of the nation (Art. XV). At the same time, the bill supports the
country’s commitments under the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals,
particularly SDG 3, SDG 4, and SDG 5, while advancing the long-term national vision of
Ambisyon Natin 2040 and the priorities of the Philippine Development Plan in
strengthening human capital and youth development.

       No child should be forced to become a parent before they are ready. Addressing
adolescent pregnancy requires decisive and sustained national action. This measure
provides a comprehensive and forward-looking framework that empowers adolescents,
strengthens families, and supports communities in safeguarding the future of the nation’s
youth.

             For these reasons, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 5         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy - It is the policy of the State to:
 6         a. Prevent adolescent pregnancy as a national and local development priority and
 7            ensure that every province, city, municipality, and barangay adopts responsive,
 8            community-based measures to address its root causes and consequences;
 9         b. Recognize the role of women and girls in nation-building and ensure the
10            fundamental equality of women and men, while adopting gender-responsive
11            measures that protect adolescents, especially young women, from
12            discrimination, abuse, coercion, and conditions that increase their vulnerability
13            to early pregnancy;
14         c. Recognize the vital role of the youth in nation-building and promote and protect
15            their physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, emotional, and social well-being
16            through programs that enable them to grow in safe, healthy, and supportive
17            homes, schools, and communities;
18         d. Protect and promote the right to health of the people, instill health
19            consciousness among them and ensure corresponding interventions that could
20            respond to the socioeconomic, health, and emotional needs of adolescents and
 1             youth, especially young women. To this end, adolescent pregnancy shall be
 2             approached as a health issue;
 3          e. Ensure that adolescents are provided with science-based, accurate, age-
 4             appropriate, and culturally sensitive information that will equip them to make
 5             informed and responsible decisions, prevent early and unintended pregnancies,
 6             and understand their long-term consequences on health, education, and future
 7             opportunities; and
 8          f. Strengthen the role of families, schools, local government units, and
 9             communities in providing adolescents with guidance, protection, and support,
10             and in creating an environment that promotes responsible decision-making,
11             dignity, and respect for self and others.

10          SEC. 12. Social Protection for Adolescent Mothers and/or Parents. - A
11   comprehensive social protection service shall be provided by LGUS to adolescents who
12   are currently pregnant or have given birth and to their partners, in order to prevent repeat
13   pregnancies and to ensure their well-being while assuming the responsibilities of being
14   young parents. Such services shall include the following:
15
16         a) Maternal health services including pre-natal and post-natal check-ups and
17         facility-based delivery;
18         b) Post-natal family planning counseling and services for both adolescent parents;
19         c) Home-based or online, in-school, or tech-vocational education for adolescent
20         parents
21         d) Personal PhilHealth coverage, making mandatory enrollment and membership
22         of indigent adolescent -parents;
23         e) Enrolment to social insurance like the Social Security Services;
24         f) Training, skills development, and support to livelihood programs for the
25         household of the adolescent parents especially for the indigents;
26         g) Continuing CHSE for adolescent parents;
27         h) Workshops on couples counseling, parenting, and positive discipline for the
28   parents;
29         i) Psycho-social support and mental health services for adolescent parents; and
30         j) In the case of solo adolescent parents, preferential access to benefits under the
31         Expanded Solo Parent-Welfare Act.
32
33   Discriminatory and exclusionary practices that harm and discourage the education of
34   adolescent parents shall be prohibited. All efforts shall be taken by school administrations
35   to ensure and encourage the continuation of education of all adolescent parents,
36   especially girls.

38   Adolescent mothers shall be entitled to maternity leave benefit, and their partners to
39   paternity leave, if applicable. Termination, forced resignation, suspension, diminution of
40   benefits, and other discriminatory acts in the workplace against non-minor pregnant girls

26          SEC. 14. Care and Management for First Time Parents. - All pregnant
27   adolescents, especially among the poor and hard-to-reach groups shall have access to

29   providers shall strive to provide as many adolescent mothers with their birth plans that
30   details their intended place of childbirth delivery, availability of transport to these health

32   pregnant mothers during obstetric care.

34   Workshops, classes, and seminars for first time parents shall be provided with pre- and

36   breastfeeding, infant feeding and care, positive discipline, responsible parenthood,
37   mental health wellness, and safe sex practices. The classes shall be made available free
38   of charge and at times most convenient for the adolescent parents.

33           SEC. 20. Creation of a National Monitoring, Research, and Information
34   System on Adolescent Health and Development.- The Department of Health
35   (DOH), Department of Education (DepEd), and Department of Social Welfare and
36   Development (DSWD), in coordination with the Commission on Population and
37   Development (CPD) and in consultation with the National Implementation Team (NIT)
38   under Republic Act No. 10354, the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), and other relevant
39   agencies, shall establish and maintain a National Monitoring, Research, and Information
40   System on Adolescent Health and Development. Such system shall ensure the systematic
41   collection, integration, analysis, and dissemination of data necessary to monitor, evaluate,
42   and improve the implementation and outcomes of the National Program of Action and
43   Investment Plan for the Prevention of Adolescent Pregnancy, and other related policies
44   and programs affecting adolescents and youth.
 1
 2          For this purpose, the system shall:
 3          (a) Generate and maintain reliable, age- and sex-disaggregated data on adolescent
 4   health, development, education, and well-being, including indicators related to early and
 5   unintended pregnancy, school participation, access to services, and repeat pregnancy;
 6          (b) Support evidence-based planning, policy formulation, and program
 7   implementation at the national and local levels;
 8          (c) Ensure data sharing and interoperability among concerned agencies, subject
 9   to existing laws on data privacy, confidentiality, and child protection;
10          (d) Integrate and harmonize existing data systems and studies conducted by
11   national government agencies and local government units; and
12          (e) Provide for a results-based monitoring and evaluation framework, including
13   periodic reporting and public dissemination of key findings. The Philippine Statistics
14   Authority (PSA), in coordination with concerned agencies, shall conduct periodic national
15   surveys on adolescent health and development, including the enhancement or expansion
16   of existing studies, to generate comprehensive and updated data. Where necessary, the
17   PSA may conduct targeted and age-appropriate studies, including data collection
18   involving younger adolescents, in accordance with accepted ethical research standards
19   and applicable laws on the protection of minors.

40         SEC 22. Integration of Adolescent Health and Responsible Parenthood
41   Indicators in the Seal of Good Local Governance- The Department of the Interior
42   and Local Government (DILG) shall integrate adolescent health, responsible parenthood,
43   and adolescent pregnancy prevention indicators, as may be appropriate, into the
44   assessment criteria of the Seal of Good Local Governance (SGLG) under the governance
 1   areas of social protection and sensitivity, health compliance and responsiveness, and
 2   sustainable education, consistent with Republic Act No. 11292, otherwise known as the
 3   “Seal of Good Local Governance Act of 2019.”
 4
 5          Such indicators shall be designed to promote, measure, and recognize the
 6   commitment of local government units to the protection of adolescents, the prevention
 7   of early and unintended pregnancy, the support for adolescent parents, and the
 8   strengthening of community-based and family-oriented interventions.
 9
10          For this purpose, the DILG, in consultation with the National Implementation Team
11   under Republic Act No. 10354, the Department of Health, the Department of Education,
12   the Department of Social Welfare and Development, the Commission on Population and
13   Development, and other relevant agencies, shall develop the appropriate indicators,
14   means of verification, and performance standards, and may formulate corresponding
15   incentives, technical assistance, and capacity-building measures to support LGUs in
16   complying with this Act.

18           SEC. 23. Appropriations. - All concerned government agencies shall include in
19   their annual budget, the necessary funds for strategies and activities within their
20   mandates that contribute to the implementation of this Act. Agencies and LGUs may also
21   utilize their Gender and Development (GAD) budget in implementing programs and
22   activities to carry out the objectives of this Act.

24          SEC. 24. Annual Report. - No later than April 30 of every year, a joint

26   the Philippines and the Congress. The report shall provide a definitive and comprehensive
27   assessment of the implementation of its programs and those of other government
28   agencies in relation to the implementation of this Act and recommend priorities for
