                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to prohibit workplace bullying, harassment, and other forms of
violence. It provides for its prevention, reporting, investigation, and resolution in
both the public and private sectors.

        Workplace bullying may manifest in physical, verbal, psychological, social,
digital, or financial forms. Repeated humiliation, intimidation, threats, malicious
conduct, exclusion, and abuse of authority can cause physical, psychological, and
emotional harm and cultivate negative workplace dynamics. Such conduct may also
impair professional relationships, disrupt the performance of duties, and undermine
a workplace culture founded on respect, fairness, and accountability.

        The bill establishes uniform standards for workplaces in the public and
private sectors and defines workplace bullying to cover physical, verbal,
psychological, social, digital, and authority-based conduct. It requires employers and
heads of offices to adopt written anti-bullying policies, establish accessible and
confidential reporting mechanisms, act promptly on complaints, and ensure
impartial investigations with due process. It likewise requires the protection of
complainants, witnesses, and persons who report or assist in an investigation from
retaliation.

       Every worker deserves to perform his or her duties in an environment free
from bullying, intimidation, humiliation, and abuse. Workplace dignity should be
protected as a matter of law, not left to the discretion or goodwill of certain
employers or offices.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
PROHIBITING WORKPLACE BULLYING, HARASSMENT, AND OTHER
FORMS OF VIOLENCE, PROVIDING FOR ITS PREVENTION, AND
PRESCRIBING PENALTIES THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Anti-Bullying in the
Workplace Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
uphold the dignity of labor and promote a safe, respectful, and human working
environment. Towards this end, the State shall protect the physical, mental, and
emotional well-being of workers from workplace bullying and establish measures
for its prevention, reporting, investigation, and resolution.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

           a. Workplace Bullying refers to any repeated and unreasonable conduct,
       whether committed by one (1) or more persons, directed against a worker or
       group of workers, that causes or is reasonably likely to cause physical,
       psychological, or emotional harm, humiliation, intimidation, degradation, or
       substantially disrupting the work process or the orderly operation of an
       institution or business;​

           b. Workplace refers to any place, whether physical or virtual, where work
       is performed, including places outside the employer’s premises when the
       conduct arises from or is connected with work, and work-related travel,
       activities, events, functions, or communications;

         c. Employer refers to any person or entity that exercises control over the
       work of a worker, whether in the public or private sector; and

         d.​ Worker refers to any person who performs work or services for an
      employer, whether under an employment relationship or other work
      arrangement/s recognized by law.​

     SEC. 4. Prohibited Workplace Bullying. – Workplace bullying is prohibited in all
workplaces covered by this Act. Workplace bullying includes, but is not limited to:

         a.​ Unwanted physical contact, assault, or other physical act or gesture
      directed against a worker;

         b.​ Repeated insults, name-calling, ridicule, humiliation, degrading
      remarks, or other verbal or psychological conduct directed against a worker;

         c.​ Deliberate and repeated acts intended to undermine a worker’s
      reputation, professional standing, or workplace relationships, including the
      dissemination of malicious or knowingly false information and unjustified
      exclusion from work-related activities or communications;

         d.​ Workplace bullying committed through electronic communications or
      other digital means, including electronic mail, messaging applications, social
      media, or other online platforms used in connection with work;

          e.​ Threats, intimidation, coercion, or other conduct intended to instill fear
      or compel a worker to act or refrain from acting, except when arising from a
      legitimate exercise of managerial authority; and

          f.​ Repeated and unreasonable use of a position of authority to humiliate,
      intimidate, undermine, or deliberately obstruct a worker in the performance
      of his/her duties or access to workplace opportunities.

​     Performance evaluations, disciplinary actions, work assignments,
supervision, or other exercise of authority, when undertaken in good faith and in
accordance with law and applicable workplace policies, shall not constitute
workplace bullying.

       SEC. 5. Duties of Employers and Heads of Offices. – Employers and heads of
offices shall take reasonable measures to prevent and address workplace bullying.
For this purpose, they shall:

         a.​ Adopt and implement a written workplace anti-bullying policy
      consistent with this Act;

         b.​ Establish accessible and confidential system for reporting and
      resolving complaints;

        c.​ Act promptly upon receipt of a complaint or information concerning
      workplace bullying;



                                          2
         d.​ Ensure that complaints are investigated fairly and impartially and that
      the parties are afforded due process;

          e.​ Protect complainants, witnesses, and persons who in good faith report
      or assist in the investigation of workplace bullying from retaliation; and

         f.​ Provide appropriate assistance or referral to available support services
      to workers affected by workplace bullying.

        SEC. 6. Workplace Anti-Bullying Policies. – The workplace anti-bullying policy
shall, at a minimum, provide for:

          a.​ The acts prohibited under Section 4 of this Act;

          b.​ The rights and responsibilities of workers, employers, and heads of
      offices;

        c.​ Accessible, confidential, and, when appropriate, anonymous reporting
      mechanisms;

         d.​ Procedures for receiving, documenting, investigating, and resolving
      complaints;

          e.​ Measures to protect complainants, witnesses, and persons assisting in
      an investigation from retaliation;

          f.​ Standards for confidentiality and the protection of personal
      information in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as
      the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”, and other applicable laws; and

         g.​ Appropriate administrative sanctions consistent with existing laws,
      rules, regulations, and applicable workplace policies.

       SEC. 7. Reporting of Workplace Bullying. – A worker may report acts of
workplace bullying to the employer, head of office, human resources department,
designated human relations officer, or other mechanism established under the
workplace anti-bullying policy; and shall comply with such policy and cooperate in
the investigation and resolution of complaints.

      An anonymous report may be received for purposes of initiating appropriate
preventing or fact-finding measures: Provided, That no disciplinary action shall be
imposed solely on the basis of an anonymous report.

​      SEC. 8. Investigation and Resolution. – Upon receipt of a complaint or report,
the employer, head of office, or designated human relations officer shall promptly
determine the appropriate action and, when warranted, conduct an impartial
investigation. The parties shall be afforded due process, and the investigation and its
records shall be treated with utmost confidentiality.


                                          3
       SEC. 9. Protection against Retaliation. – No employer, head of office, or other
person shall retaliate against a complainant, witness, or person who in good faith
reports workplace bullying or participates in an investigation. Retaliatory acts shall
be a separate ground for administrative liability, without prejudice to other liability
under existing laws.

      SEC. 10. Reportorial Requirements and Capacity Building. – Employers and
heads of offices shall maintain records of complaints, investigations, and actions
taken under this Act and submit such reports as may be required by the Department
of Labor and Employment (DOLE), for the private sector, or the Civil Service
Commission (CSC), for government service.

      The DOLE and CSC shall ensure that workplace bullying prevention and
response are integrated into existing occupational safety and health, human
resource, or equivalent training programs, within their respective jurisdictions.

      SEC. 11. Administrative Liability. – Any person found, after due process, to
have committed or unknowingly participated in workplace bullying, shall be subject
to the appropriate administrative sanctions prescribed by the DOLE and CSC,
without prejudice to any civil or criminal charges that may be filed under existing
laws.

        An employer, head of office, or responsible officer who, without justifiable
reason, fails to comply with a duty imposed under this Act, including the duty to
investigate or act on a complaint of workplace bullying, shall likewise be
administratively liable. Where such failure is committed maliciously, the responsible
administrative officer shall be subject to the corresponding administrative sanction.
The imposition of administrative sanctions under this Act shall be without prejudice
to civil, criminal, or other liability under existing laws.

       SEC. 12. Implementing the Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOLE and the CSC shall jointly promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations to effectively implement the provisions of this Act.

       SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
