                        AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF MUNICIPAL SMALL-
SCALE AND SUBSISTENCE FISHERFOLK, STRENGTHENING THEIR
RIGHTS, PROTECTION, AND ACCESS TO SUPPORT SERVICES, AND
FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 7, Article XIII of the 1987 Constitution guarantees the protection
of the rights of subsistence fishermen, especially of local communities, to the
preferential use of local marine and fishing resources, both inland and offshore.
It further mandates the State to provide support through appropriate technology
and research, adequate financial, production, and marketing assistance, and other
services; to protect, develop, and conserve such resources; to safeguard offshore
fishing grounds against foreign intrusion; and, to ensure that fishworkers receive
a just share from their labor in the utilization of marine and fishing resources.

       Complementing this mandate, Section 5, Article XIII of the Constitution
directs the State to provide support to agriculture and fisheries, while Section 16,
Article II guarantees the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology,
and Section 18, Article II affirms the State’s duty to protect the rights of workers
and promote their welfare. Taken together, these provisions establish the State’s
obligation not only to secure the dignity, welfare, and economic resilience of
fisherfolk, but also to ensure the sustainability of fisheries resources.
      Municipal small-scale and subsistence fisherfolk remain among the most
economically vulnerable sectors in the country, despite their indispensable role
in ensuring food security, sustaining coastal communities, and supporting local
economies. Their contributions extend beyond food production, as they also serve
as frontline stewards of marine and coastal ecosystems. However, structural
challenges continue to limit their full participation in economic growth and
development.

       While Republic Act No. 8550, otherwise known as the Philippine Fisheries
Code of 1998, as amended, recognizes their preferential rights in municipal
waters and provides the legal framework for fisheries management, many
fisherfolk continue to face persistent and overlapping challenges. These include
limited access to markets, inadequate post-harvest and cold storage facilities,
exposure to exploitative trading practices, vulnerability to climate change and
natural disasters, and increasing operational costs, particularly due to fuel price
fluctuations.

       Existing government interventions, such as fuel subsidy programs, credit
and insurance facilities, and livelihood assistance, have provided some relief.
However, these initiatives remain fragmented, unevenly implemented, and often
insufficient to address the long-term and systemic needs of the sector. Moreover,
the growing role of digital technologies in governance and market systems
highlights the need to improve the fisherfolk’s access to information, services,
and economic opportunities through more integrated and accessible platforms.

       This proposed Magna Carta seeks to address these gaps by establishing a
comprehensive and integrated policy framework for municipal small-scale and
subsistence fisherfolk. It consolidates and reinforces their rights, strengthens
access to fisheries support services, promotes fair and equitable market
participation, and institutionalizes social protection mechanisms, particularly
during emergencies such as natural disasters, fuel price shocks, and other
disruptions affecting fisheries operations.

       The measure further promotes the adoption and use of digital systems to
enhance access to government services, improve data-driven policy
implementation, and ensure more efficient delivery of programs. It also
strengthens inter-agency coordination and the targeting of beneficiaries through
the integration of existing registry systems.

     By reinforcing existing laws and addressing implementation gaps, this
measure aims to uplift the welfare of fisherfolk, enhance their resilience to
economic and environmental shocks, and ensure the sustainable use of marine

  economic growth, and the protection of coastal and marine ecosystems.

                              ARTICLE I
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

     Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta of
Municipal Small-Scale and Subsistence Fisherfolk.”

       Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – Article XIII, Section 7 of the 1987
Constitution mandates the State to protect the rights of subsistence fishermen,
especially of local communities, to the preferential use of local marine and fishing
resources, both inland and offshore. It shall provide support through appropriate
technology and research, adequate financial, production, and marketing
assistance, and other services; protect, develop, and conserve such resources;
safeguard offshore fishing grounds against foreign intrusion; and ensure that

                                                                                  4
fishworkers receive a just share from their labor in the utilization of marine and
fishing resources.
       Pursuant further to Article XIII, Section 5, and consistent with Article II,
Section 16 of the Constitution, the State shall provide support to the fisheries
sector while ensuring the sustainable use and conservation of marine and coastal
resources.
       Consistent with Republic Act No. 8550, otherwise known as the
“Philippine Fisheries Code of 1998,” as amended, the State shall uphold the
preferential rights of municipal fisherfolk in municipal waters and ensure their
meaningful participation in fisheries governance and management.
       Towards these ends, the State shall recognize, protect, and promote the
rights and welfare of municipal small-scale and subsistence fisherfolk as primary
contributors to food security, livelihood generation, and coastal resource
stewardship.

      Section 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following
terms are hereby defined:
      a. Municipal Fisherfolk refers to individuals engaged in fishing and related
          livelihood activities within municipal waters, as defined under Republic
          Act No. 8550, as amended.
      b. Subsistence Fisherfolk refers to fisherfolk whose primary purpose of
          fishing is for household consumption, with limited surplus for trade.
      c. Small-Scale Fisherfolk refers to fisherfolk operating with limited
          capital, using traditional or labor-intensive fishing methods and small
          fishing vessels.
      d. Municipal Waters refer to inland and coastal waters within the
          jurisdiction of local government units, as defined under existing laws.
      e. Fisheries Support Services refers to services including access to credit,
          insurance, extension services, post-harvest facilities, training, and
          market linkages.

                               ARTICLE II
                          RIGHTS OF FISHERFOLK

      Section 4. Right to Preferential Access to Municipal Waters. – Municipal
small-scale and subsistence fisherfolk shall have preferential rights in the use and

                                                                                  5
management of municipal waters in accordance with Republic Act No. 8550, as
amended, and relevant local ordinances.

       Section 5. Right to Livelihood and Economic Security. – The State shall
ensure the protection of fisherfolk from displacement, resource depletion, and
economic shocks. In coordination with concerned agencies, the State shall provide
appropriate livelihood support, including temporary income or livelihood
assistance during closed fishing seasons, natural disasters, and other emergencies,
in accordance with existing social protection programs and applicable laws.

       Section 6. Right to Fair Trade and Market Access. – The State shall
promote fair trade practices and ensure that fisherfolk are protected from
exploitative pricing and abusive intermediaries. For this purpose, the Department
of Agriculture (DA), through the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources
(BFAR), in coordination with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and
local government units, shall facilitate direct market access, improve value chain
participation, and strengthen fisherfolk cooperatives.

       Section 7. Right to Credit, Insurance, and Financial Services. –
Municipal small-scale and subsistence fisherfolk shall be ensured access to
affordable credit, fisheries insurance, and disaster risk financing through existing
government financial institutions and programs, including but not limited to those
administered by the Land Bank of the Philippines, the Development Bank of the
Philippines, and the Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation, pursuant to existing
laws, rules, and regulations.

        Section 8. Right to Social Protection and Basic Services. – Fisherfolk shall
be covered by social protection programs, including health insurance under
Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the Universal Health Care Act,
social welfare programs under Republic Act No. 11310, otherwise known as the
Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) Act, and other relevant government
initiatives.

      Section 9. Right to Participation in Governance. – Municipal small-scale
and subsistence fisherfolk shall have the right to participate in decision-making
processes affecting fisheries resources, including representation in Fisheries and

                                                                                  6
Aquatic Resources Management Councils (FARMCs), pursuant to Republic Act
No. 8550, as amended.

      Section 10. Right to Sustainable Fisheries and Environmental
Protection. – The State shall protect municipal waters from illegal, unreported,
and unregulated fishing, environmental degradation, and encroachment by
commercial fishing operators and impose corresponding penalties, in accordance
with existing fisheries and environmental law.

                    ARTICLE III
  GOVERNMENT SUPPORT AND INSTITUTIONAL MECHANISMS

      Section 11. National Fisherfolk Registry. – The BFAR shall establish,
maintain, and regularly update a unified national fisherfolk registry to ensure
proper identification and targeting of beneficiaries of government programs and
services.
      It shall also coordinate with the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT) and other relevant agencies to integrate and
maximize existing systems such as the Fisherfolk Registration System (FishR),
Boat and Gear Registration (BoatR), and the Registry System for Basic Sectors in
Agriculture (RSBSA), for purposes of interoperability and efficient data
management.

       Section 12. Fisheries Support Services. – The State shall strengthen the
provision of fisheries support services, including post-harvest facilities, cold
storage systems, training, extension services, and climate-resilient fishing
technologies, in coordination with relevant agencies and local government units.
       The BFAR, in coordination with the DICT and other relevant agencies,
shall promote the use of digital systems and platforms to improve access of
fisherfolk to government services, information, and market opportunities.
       The BFAR, in coordination with the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD) and other relevant agencies, shall ensure that appropriate
assistance mechanisms are in place to support fisherfolk during emergencies,
including fuel price shocks, natural disasters, and other disruptions affecting
fisheries operations, consistent with existing laws and programs.
       The BFAR shall further develop and institutionalize sustainable, long-term
support programs for fisherfolk, building on existing initiatives.
                                                                               7
      Section 13. Infrastructure Development. – The national government, in
coordination with local government units, shall prioritize the establishment and
improvement of fisheries infrastructure, including fish ports, landing sites, cold
chain facilities, and processing centers, subject to existing infrastructure and
development programs.

      Section 14. Inter-Agency Coordination. – The BFAR shall serve as the
lead implementing agency of this Act. It shall coordinate with the DA, DTI,
DSWD, Department of Budget and Management, and local government units to
ensure the effective implementation of this Act.

                     ARTICLE IV
 PROTECTION, ORGANIZATIONAL DEVELOPMENT, AND ACCESS
                    TO REMEDIES

       Section 15. Protection against Harassment and Illegal Displacement. –
Municipal small-scale and subsistence fisherfolk shall be protected against
unlawful eviction, harassment, intimidation, or arbitrary displacement from
municipal waters, foreshore areas, and coastal lands traditionally used for their
livelihood, subject to existing laws, environmental regulations, and lawful
enforcement measures. Concerned national government agencies and LGUs shall
ensure that all enforcement actions affecting fisherfolk are conducted in
accordance with due process and applicable laws.

      Section 16. Protection against Exploitation. – The State shall regulate and
prevent unfair trade practices, monopolistic control, and exploitative
arrangements in the fisheries sector, consistent with existing trade and
competition laws.

       Section 17. Strengthened Fisherfolk Organizations. – The State shall
support the formation and strengthening of fisherfolk organizations, associations,
and cooperatives through capacity-building, financial assistance, and technical
support, in coordination with the Cooperative Development Authority (CDA) and
other relevant government agencies.



                                                                                8
        Section 18. Legal Assistance and Access to Remedies. – The State shall
facilitate access of municipal small-scale and subsistence fisherfolk to
appropriate legal assistance and administrative remedies in cases involving
unlawful fishing encroachment, illegal resource exploitation, labor-related
abuses, or violations of rights recognized under this Act, subject to existing laws,
rules, and regulations.

       Section 19. Capacity-Building and Leadership Development. – Relevant
government agencies, in coordination with local government units, state
universities and colleges, and accredited organizations, shall conduct capacity-
building, leadership development, and organizational management programs for
fisherfolk organizations and cooperatives to strengthen community participation,
sustainable fisheries management, and local enterprise development.

                                ARTICLE V
                             FINAL PROVISIONS

       Section 20. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

      Section 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DA, through the BFAR, in coordination
with concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations.

      Section 22. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      Section 23. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances,
decrees, rules, and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of
this Act are deemed amended, modified, or repealed accordingly.

       Section 24. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
