                              AN ACT
 DECLARING IT A POLICY OF THE STATE TO PROVIDE UNIVERSAL ACCESS
  TO RELIABLE, AFFORDABLE, AND ENVIRONMENTALLY SUSTAINABLE
  ELECTRICITY, ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE PROGRAM FOR THE
   SOLAR ELECTRIFICATION OF BARANGAYS THAT REMAIN OFF-GRID,
   PROVIDING A PACKAGE OF FISCAL AND NON-FISCAL INCENTIVES TO
   ENCOURAGE THE ACTIVE PARTICIPATION OF THE PRIVATE SECTOR,
    APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Energy insecurity in the Philippines is no longer a distant or abstract risk—it is
a present and pressing reality that directly affects the daily lives of millions of Filipinos.
While significant progress has been made in electrification, a number of barangays,
particularly those in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, remain without
access to reliable and affordable electricity. For these communities, development is
not merely delayed—it is effectively denied.

       The urgency of this measure is further magnified by recent economic
conditions. The country remains heavily dependent on imported petroleum, exposing
it to global price volatility. The recent surge in international oil prices, driven by
geopolitical tensions, has resulted in consecutive increases in domestic fuel prices. At
the same time, the depreciation of the Philippine peso has amplified the cost of
imported fuel, compounding the burden on power generation, transportation, and basic
goods. These factors are now feeding directly into inflation, eroding purchasing power
and placing additional strain on Filipino households.
        Nowhere is this more acutely felt than in off-grid communities. In these areas,
electricity is often generated using diesel, making power costs highly sensitive to fuel
price movements and currency fluctuations. As global oil prices rise and the peso
weakens, electricity becomes more expensive, less reliable, and increasingly
inaccessible. This creates a cycle of disadvantage—where the most vulnerable
communities pay the highest price for the least reliable service.

       This proposed measure responds to this urgent reality by advancing a decisive
and forward-looking solution: the accelerated electrification of off-grid barangays
through solar energy. Solar power offers a stable, indigenous, and sustainable
alternative to imported fuel. It insulates communities from global oil shocks, reduces
long-term electricity costs, and provides a reliable foundation for economic activity,
education, healthcare, and local enterprise.

       Through the establishment of a comprehensive Solar Barangay Electrification
Program, this bill sets a clear national target of achieving full electrification of all off-
grid barangays within ten (10) years. It recognizes that government alone cannot carry
this burden and therefore mobilizes private sector participation through well-structured
incentives, streamlined processes, and viable public-private partnership models. In
doing so, it unlocks capital, innovation, and efficiency at a scale necessary to meet
this national objective.

       This measure is not merely about electrification—it is about equity. It is about
ensuring that no Filipino is left behind simply because of geography. It is about
transforming energy from a barrier into a bridge for development. And most
importantly, it is about asserting that in the face of rising global uncertainty, the
Philippines must invest in solutions that are resilient, sustainable, and within our
control.

       In a time when fuel prices are volatile, the peso is under pressure, and inflation
threatens the stability of every household, the shift toward renewable, decentralized
energy is not optional — it is imperative.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                              AN ACT
 DECLARING IT A POLICY OF THE STATE TO PROVIDE UNIVERSAL ACCESS
  TO RELIABLE, AFFORDABLE, AND ENVIRONMENTALLY SUSTAINABLE
  ELECTRICITY, ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE PROGRAM FOR THE
   SOLAR ELECTRIFICATION OF BARANGAYS THAT REMAIN OFF-GRID,
   PROVIDING A PACKAGE OF FISCAL AND NON-FISCAL INCENTIVES TO
   ENCOURAGE THE ACTIVE PARTICIPATION OF THE PRIVATE SECTOR,
    APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section. 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known and cited as the “Solar
Electrification of Off-Grid Barangays Act.”

        Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and advance the right of all Filipinos to access affordable, reliable, and clean
electricity. It is further declared that achieving full electrification, especially in
barangays that are not connected to the main power grid, is a matter of national
priority. The State shall promote the accelerated development and utilization of
renewable energy resources, most notably solar power, to fulfill this objective. It is also
the policy of the State to encourage, support, and incentivize the active participation
of private enterprises, cooperatives, and organizations in the development, financing,
construction, operation, and maintenance of off-grid renewable energy systems.
These policies are in line with the Constitution’s mandate to promote the welfare of the
people, foster economic development, and ensure environmental sustainability for the
benefit of present and future generations.
        Sec. 3. Objectives. This Act shall pursue the following objectives:
        (a) To achieve the complete electrification of one hundred percent (100%) of all
barangays in the Philippines that remain off-grid within a period of ten (10) years from
the date this Act takes effect;
        (b) To facilitate and encourage the mobilization of private investments in off-
grid electrification projects through public-private partnerships and other contractual
arrangements;
        (c) To reduce the dependence of remote and underserved communities on
electricity generation using imported or fossil-based fuels, thereby lowering
greenhouse gas emissions and environmental impacts; and
        (d) To help fulfill the country’s commitments under the United Nations
Sustainable Development Goals, particularly Goal 7 on Affordable and Clean Energy,
and the Philippines’ obligations under the Paris Agreement on Climate Change.

       Sec. 4. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
have the meanings ascribed to them below:
       (a) Off-Grid Barangay refers to any barangay or community that is not presently
connected to the main transmission and distribution network of any electric utility or
cooperative.
       (b) Solar Energy System shall refer to a facility, plant, or installation that
generates electricity using solar photovoltaic panels and associated equipment such
as inverters, battery storage units, charge controllers, wiring, and support structures.
       (c) Private Sector Provider shall refer to any duly qualified private enterprise,
cooperative, association, or non-governmental organization that engages in the
design, financing, installation, operation, or maintenance of solar energy systems
intended to electrify off-grid barangays under this Act.
       (d) Public-Private Partnership (PPP) shall refer to any contractual arrangement
as defined under Republic Act No. 6957, as amended by Republic Act No. 7718, and
other relevant laws and regulations, wherein the private sector assumes substantial
financial, technical, and operational risks in undertaking an infrastructure or
development project.
       (e) Department shall refer to the Department of Energy (DOE).

       Sec. 5. Creation of the Solar Barangay Electrification Program. There is
hereby established a Solar Barangay Electrification Program (SBEP) which shall serve
as the national framework for carrying out the objectives of this Act. The Department
of Energy shall be primarily responsible for formulating, implementing, supervising,
and monitoring this Program.

        In the exercise of this mandate, the Department of Energy shall:
        (a) Develop and promulgate appropriate technical standards and guidelines for
the design, installation, operation, and maintenance of solar energy systems deployed
in off-grid barangays, ensuring that such systems are safe, reliable, and suitable for
the local conditions in each community;
       (b) Establish clear and transparent criteria for identifying and prioritizing the
barangays to be electrified under the Program, giving particular attention to
communities with the greatest number of households without access to electricity, high
poverty incidence, or geographic isolation;
       (c) Coordinate with the National Electrification Administration, the Department
of the Interior and Local Government, electric cooperatives, local government units,
and other relevant government agencies to ensure effective planning, implementation,
and monitoring of projects;
       (d) Set annual performance benchmarks and targets to track progress toward
achieving full electrification within the ten-year period mandated by this Act; and
       (e) Submit an annual report to Congress detailing the implementation status,
accomplishments, challenges encountered, and recommendations for improvement.

         Sec. 6. Private Sector Participation and Contractual Arrangements. The
Department of Energy, in coordination with relevant agencies, shall promote and
facilitate the participation of qualified private sector providers through various
contractual arrangements, including but not limited to Build-Operate-Transfer, Build-
Own-Operate, and other Public-Private Partnership modalities.
The selection of private sector partners shall be conducted through transparent and
competitive processes, except in cases where unsolicited proposals are submitted and
subsequently subjected to a competitive challenge procedure as required by law.

       All contracts and agreements entered into under this Act shall be subject to the
applicable regulations of the Energy Regulatory Commission, including those
governing tariffs, quality of service, consumer protection, and reporting requirements.

       Sec. 7. Fiscal Incentives. To encourage private investments and participation
in the electrification of off-grid barangays, the following fiscal incentives shall be
granted to qualified developers and operators:
       (a) An Income Tax Holiday of ten (10) years, during which the net income
derived from qualified solar electrification projects shall be exempt from corporate
income tax;
       (b) Exemption from the payment of customs duties and value-added tax on the
importation and local purchase of capital equipment, machinery, spare parts, and
materials directly and exclusively used in the construction, installation, operation, and
maintenance of solar energy systems, for a period of ten (10) years;
       (c) Entitlement to enhanced deductions, including a deduction equivalent to two
hundred percent (200%) of expenses incurred for training programs and capacity-
building activities for employees hired from the local community, as well as research
and development activities related to renewable energy technologies applicable to off-
grid electrification;
       (d) The right to carry over any net operating loss incurred during the first three
(3) years of commercial operations as a deduction from taxable income for the next
seven (7) consecutive taxable years immediately following the year of such loss;
       (e) Enhanced deductibility of donations, wherein any donation of fully installed
and operational solar energy systems to qualified public entities such as barangay
governments, public schools, or public health centers shall be deductible from the
donor’s taxable income at an amount equivalent to one hundred fifty percent (150%)
of the value of the donated system; and
       (f) Eligibility for registration as a priority investment project with the Board of
Investments, granting the investor access to all other applicable incentives provided
under the Omnibus Investments Code and the Strategic Investment Priority Plan.
These incentives shall be subject to the oversight of the Fiscal Incentives Review
Board and compliance with performance obligations and reporting requirements.

        Sec. 8. Non-Fiscal Incentives and Facilitative Measures. To complement the
fiscal incentives provided in the preceding Sec., the following non-fiscal incentives and
support measures shall be made available to qualified projects:
        (a) Fast-track processing of all necessary permits, licenses, and approvals, with
all concerned government agencies and local government units required to act on
complete applications within a period not exceeding thirty (30) working days;
        (b) Free and timely access to government data and resource maps necessary
for project assessment, planning, and design, including data on unserved barangays,
solar irradiance, and demographic profiles;
        (c) Priority access to credit facilities, concessional loans, guarantees, and
blended finance instruments provided by government financial institutions, including
the Development Bank of the Philippines and the Land Bank of the Philippines, as well
as grants and financing from development partners and climate funds;
        (d) The express authority for qualified operators of solar microgrids or mini-grids
to sell any surplus electricity generated beyond the needs of the served barangay to
the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market or to other qualified buyers, subject to applicable
regulations issued by the Energy Regulatory Commission and the Department of
Energy; and
        (e) Preferential access to government-supported capacity-building programs,
technical assistance, and recognition schemes, including awards for exemplary
performance and innovation in off-grid electrification.

       Sec. 9. Funding Mechanisms. The implementation of this Act shall be
financed through a combination of private investments and public funds, including but
not limited to:
       (a) Private capital mobilized through Public-Private Partnership arrangements;
       (b) Appropriations under the General Appropriations Act for the administrative,
regulatory, technical, and monitoring functions of the Department of Energy and other
implementing agencies;
       (c) Grants, donations, and contributions from domestic and international
sources, in accordance with existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules; and
      (d) The possible reallocation or utilization of the Universal Charge for
Missionary Electrification, subject to the approval of the Energy Regulatory
Commission, to support the transition from diesel-based generation to renewable
energy systems.

        Sec. 10. Lead Implementing Agency. The Department of Energy shall serve
as the lead implementing agency for this Act and shall coordinate with the National
Electrification Administration, the Department of Trade and Industry, the Department
of the Interior and Local Government, the Board of Investments, the National Power
Corporation, the Energy Regulatory Commission, the Department of Finance, and
other relevant agencies and government instrumentalities.

       Sec. 11. Appropriations. The funds necessary to commence implementation
of this Act shall be sourced from the current appropriations of the Department of
Energy and other relevant agencies. Thereafter, the funding necessary to sustain the
Program shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       Sec. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. The Department of Energy,
in consultation with other concerned agencies, shall issue the implementing rules and
regulations necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act within ninety (90) days
from its effectivity.

       Sec. 13. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

         Sec. 14. Separability Clause. If any provision or part of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

        Sec. 15. Effectivity Clause. This Act shall take effect immediately after its
publication in at least two (2) national newspapers of general circulation or in the
Official Gazette, whichever comes earlier.
