                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

     At least one in every three Filipinos die from non-communicable diseases
(NCDs), such as coronary heart diseases (CHDs), cerebrovascular disease, diabetes
mellitus, and hypertensive disease. CHDs account for 17.1% of the total registered
deaths and remain to be the leading cause of death among Filipinos.1

     The country's lack of effective labelling system makes Filipinos more
vulnerable to NCDs due to increased consumption of unhealthy food products.
Nutrition information labels on food packaging are complex and difficult for many
consumers to understand, particularly those of lower socioeconomic status.2 To
protect health and instill health consciousness, legislation mandating front-of-
package warning labels (FOPWL) is needed. FOPWL will help Filipinos make
informed food choices and avoid unhealthy products, especially for those at risk of
NCDs.3

       Hence, this representation seeks the passage of this measure.

                                AN ACT
          REQUIRING FRONT-OF-PACKAGE WARNING LABELS FOR
        PREPACKAGED FOOD AND NON-ALCOHOLIC BEVERAGES TO
         GUIDE FILIPINOS IN AVOIDING UNHEALTHY FOOD AND TO
                PROMOTE CHILDREN’S RIGHT TO HEALTH

     Congress assembled:

 3
 4       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Front-of-Package
 5   Warning Label Act.”
 6
 7         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and
 8   promote the right to health of all Filipinos, instill health consciousness among them
 9   and to defend the right of children to proper care and nutrition. It is likewise the
10   policy of the state to protect consumers from trade malpractices and from
11   substandard or hazardous products. Towards these ends, the State recognizes the
12   need to enable Filipinos to make informed food choices and avoid food that are high
13   in fats, sodium, and sugar through easy-to-read warning labels on prepackaged food.
14
15        SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act seeks to:
16
17        a.   Guide the population in making informed food purchases and discourage
18             people from consuming food with fat, sodium, and sugar content in excess
19             of the prescribed maximum thresholds;
20
21        b.   Reduce the risk of overweight and obesity; and
22
 1        c.   Prevent deaths due to non-communicable diseases caused by dietary risk
 2             factors.

 4         SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act, the following terms
 5   shall be defined as follows:
 6
 7        a.   Breastmilk Substitutes means any food being marketed or otherwise
 8             represented as a partial or total replacement for breastmilk, whether or not
 9             suitable for that purpose;
10
11        b.   Calories refer to the unit of measurement used to determine the amount of
12             energy that a food provides;
13
14        c.   Complementary Nutritional Information may state, suggest, or imply that
15             a food or beverage possesses particular nutritional properties, especially,
16             but not limited to its energy value, protein, fat, carbohydrates, dietary fiber
17             content, critical nutrients, vitamins, minerals, and even assertions of
18             fortification;
19
20        d.   Food refers to any substance or product, whether processed, partially
21             processed, or unprocessed that is intended for human consumption. It
22             includes drinks, chewing gum, water and other substances that were used
23             as an ingredient or a component in the manufacture, preparation, or
24             treatment of food, such as oils and fats, whether sold alone or incorporated
25             in processed food and/or prepackaged food;
26
27        e.   Front-of-Package Warning Label (FOPWL) refers to the supplementary
28             nutrition information on the main face of the packaging of prepackaged
29             food to inform consumers when a product contains excessive amounts of
30             nutrients of concern;
31
32        f.   Main face refers to the part of the packaging where the sales name and the
33             brand or logo, if any, are recorded in their most relevant forms;
34
35        g.   Micro, small, and medium enterprise (MSME) refers to food businesses as
36             defined within the classification of industries by the Department of Trade
37             and Industry;
38
39        h.   Nutrient Profile Model refers to a tool used to classify or rank foods
40             according to their nutritional composition for reasons related to the
41             prevention of disease and promotion of health;
42
43        i.   Nutrients refer to any chemical substance normally consumed as a
44             component of a food that: 1) provides energy; and/or 2) is necessary for,
45             or contributes to the growth, development, and maintenance of health and
1             life; and/or 3) the absence of which will cause characteristic chemical or
2             physiological changes to occur;
3
4        j.   Nutrients of concern refer to those nutrients that when ingested above the
5             maximum values are considered as risk factors associated with NCDs;
6             these include but are not limited to sugars, sodium, saturated fats, total
7             fats, and calories;
8
 9       k.   Prepackaged food refer to processed food prepared in advance and placed
10            in a container, labelled and ready for sale or distribution, or for catering
11            purposes;
12
13       l.   Saturated fatty acid (SFA) refers to fatty acids that contain the maximum
14            number of hydrogens possible, and no carbon-carbon double bonds.
15            Saturated fatty acids are classified as short chain (1-5 carbons), medium
16            chain (6-12 carbons), and long chain (13-21 carbons) and very long chain
17            (contains 22 and more carbons) according to the carbon chain length;
18
19       m.   Sodium refers to a chemical element that exists naturally in food,
20            associated with other molecular residues or atoms as an ionic bond,
21            forming chemical salts. It is of great importance since it helps to maintain
22            the water and base acid balance of any organism, constituting its most
23            common compound, sodium chloride or what is usually called table salt.
24            Likewise, we find other sodium salts that are of industrial origin, such as
25            monosodium glutamate (flavor enhancer); sodium benzoate, sodium
26            acetate, sodium bicarbonate (acidity regulators and preservatives); sodium
27            hydroxide, sodium carbonate (acidity regulator); sodium erythorbate
28            (antioxidant), among others. One gram of 'table salt' contains 400mg of
29            sodium (1 gram of sodium is equivalent to 2.5 grams of table salt);
30
31       n.   Sugar refers to sucrose, fructose, or glucose obtained from the industrial
32            processing of sugar cane, sugar beet, hard yellow corn, and their mixtures,
33            among others, in the form of a crystallized solid product or syrup used as
34            an ingredient in food products;
35
36       o.   Total fat refers to the total amount of fat in a food product. This includes
37            the saturated fat, trans-fat, monounsaturated, and polyunsaturated fat in a
38            food product;
39
40        SEC. 5. Scope and Application. - This Act shall apply to all food business
41   operators of prepackaged food produced, manufactured, imported, distributed,
42   supplied, and advertised or marketed in the territory of the Philippines.

 1             ARTICLE II. FRONT-OF-PACKAGE WARNING LABELS
 2
 3
 4         SEC. 6. Mandatory Front-of-Package Warning Labels. – No person or food
 5   business operator shall sell, manufacture, import, or distribute, prepackaged food
 6   and non-alcoholic beverages without a front-of-package warning label prescribed in
 7   this Act, when the final composition of the product contains nutrients of concern that
 8   exceed the maximum values prescribed under Section 8 of this Act.
 9
10        SEC. 7. Form, Size, Placement, and Characteristics of the Warning Labels. –
11   When the final composition of the pre-packaged food product contains critical
12   nutrients or calories in excess of the maximum values under Section 8 of this Act,
13   the packaging shall display a warning label as prescribed by the Food and Drug
14   Authority (FDA), in accordance with the following guidelines:
15
16        a.   The packaging must include one warning label for each nutrient in excess
17             of prescribed values, namely:
18
19             ●    “HIGH IN SUGAR”;
20             ●    “HIGH IN SODIUM”;
21             ●    “HIGH IN SATURATED FAT”;
22             ●    “HIGH IN TOTAL FAT”;
23
24        b.   If the product's calories is in excess of the prescribed values the package
25             must include the following warning label:
26
27             ●    “HIGH IN CALORIES”
28
29        c.   If the product contains sweeteners, the package must include the following
30             warning label:
31
32             ●    “CONTAINS SWEETENERS, NOT RECOMMENDED FOR
33                  CHILDREN”
34
35        d.   The warning label shall have the form of black octagons with a white edge
36             and capital letters;
37
38        e.   The size of each warning label shall never be less than five percent (5%)
39             of the area of the main face of the packaging;
40
41        f.   The warning labels shall form part of the packaging and their full visibility
42             and integrity must not be susceptible to being damaged, concealed,
43             obstructed or changed by any package design, feature, or mechanism, or
44             covered by any other markings;
45
1         g.   It must be located on the top right edge of the product’s main face. If the
2              shape of the product is round or cylindrical, it must be on the top center of
3              the main face. For products in which the main face of the packaging is
4              equal to or less than ten (10) square centimeters, it will contain a single
5              octagon that will show inside of it how many nutrients of concern above
6              the thresholds the product has. The warning label shall be 15% of the front
7              of the package.
8
 9        h.   If the product has more than one warning label, they must be placed
10             adjacent to each other on the product’s main face.
11
12        i.   For packages with multiple products, the outer packaging, as well as each
13             individual package inside must also include the warning label.
14
15        The provisions established in this Section are applied in addition to the existing
16   rules and regulations governing the labeling of prepackaged food distributed in the
17   Philippines. In addition, the FDA is encouraged to release guidelines pertaining to
18   the placement of products at the point of sale.
19
20        SEC. 8.       Maximum Values. – The maximum values of the nutrients of
21   concern shall be based on the nutrient profile model developed by the Department
22   of Health. Pending the development of a local NPM, the Pan American Health
23   Organization Nutrient Profile Model shall be used as the basis for such maximum
24   values.
25
26        The DOH may coordinate with the Department of Science and Technology –
27   Food and Nutrition Research Institute (DOST-FNRI) in developing an NPM for the
28   Philippines.
29
30         SEC. 9. Exemptions. – Prepackaged food for special therapeutic dietary uses
31   that are formulated for a particular physiological condition or as partial replacement
32   for meals, and culinary ingredients which typically consist of only one nutrient in
33   high purity (cooking oils, table sugars, common table salt, etc.) shall follow their
34   respective labeling requirements and shall be exempted from the obligation to
35   incorporate a warning label. Breastmilk substitutes shall likewise be exempted from
36   this Act and shall follow the guidelines promulgated by Executive Order No. 51,
37   series of 1986, its implementing rules and regulations and other pertinent
38   regulations.
39
40         SEC. 10. Prohibitions on misleading packaging and other promotional
41   features. – Prepackaged food, as well as non-alcoholic beverages, required to have
42   a warning label under Section 6 of this Act shall not have any element of feature
43   that:
44
45        a.   Provide complementary nutritional information;
46
1         b.   Logotypes or phrases stating the sponsorship or endorsements of scientific
2              societies, civil associations or other relevant actors; and
3
 4        c.   Other health and nutrition claims.

 7                             ARTICLE III. ENFORCEMENT
 8
 9
10        SEC. 11. Certificate of Product Registration. – The FDA shall not issue a
11   Certificate of Product Registration (CPR) or renew an existing CPR to any food
12   manufacturer, distributor, and importer whose product does not comply with
13   Sections 6, 7, and 10 of this Act.
14
15        SEC. 12. Inspection Powers and Record-Keeping. – The FDA, through its
16   authorized agents, shall have the power to inspect the premises and records of food
17   manufacturers, to determine compliance with this Act. The FDA shall issue
18   inspection procedures and guidelines on record-keeping.
19
20         SEC. 13. Enforcement for Traditional Markets and Food Service
21   Establishments. – Local government units (LGUs), through an appropriate issuance
22   and in coordination with the FDA and Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), shall
23   establish a mechanism to enforce the provisions of this Act on prepackaged food
24   produced and marketed in traditional markets and food service establishments within
25   their jurisdiction and shall impose penalties for violations thereof.
26
27        SEC. 14. Consumer Complaints. – The FDA may commence an investigation
28   motu proprio, upon petition, or upon letter-complaint from any consumer for
29   violations of this Act. Upon finding by the FDA of prima facie violation of any
30   provisions of this Act, it may motu proprio or upon a verified complaint, commence
31   formal administrative action against the food business operator or any person who
32   appears responsible.
33
34       The procedure in the commencement and processing of consumer complaints
35   mentioned in this Section shall follow the existing rules and regulations of the FDA.

 3        SEC. 20. Consumer Information, Education, and Communication. – Health and
 4   nutrition literacy including the importance of nutrition labeling, proper usage and
 5   rationale of the use of FOPWL and other food product labels, and the risks of
 6   overconsuming foods that are high in fats, sodium, and sugar shall be disseminated
 7   and promoted through a nationwide information, education, and communication
 8   campaign. The DOH shall spearhead the campaign, in cooperation with other
 9   concerned government agencies and LGUs. The DOH shall coordinate with the
10   Department of Education to conduct child-specific educational campaigns on
11   nutrition labeling in Philippine schools.
12
13       SEC. 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within sixty (60) days
14   from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH through the Health Promotion Bureau
15   (HPB), together with other relevant government agencies and stakeholders shall
16   develop the IRR for this Act. Non-issuance of the IRR shall not prevent the
17   implementation of this Act upon its effectivity.
18
19       SEC. 22. Transitory Provisions. – The provisions set forth in this Act shall be
20   complied with within a term not exceeding six (6) months from the effectivity date.
21
22        SEC. 23. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The DOH, through the FDA, shall
23   report annually or as necessary, to the President and the Congressional Committees
24   on Health, Agriculture and Food, and Trade and Industry on the implementation of
25   this Act.
26
27         SEC. 24. Appropriations. The initial amount necessary for the implementation
28   of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriation of all concerned
29   agencies. Thereafter, concerned agencies shall allocate such funds that may be
30   necessary for the continuous implementation of this Act and shall include such sums
31   in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA) under the respective budgets of all
32   concerned agencies.
33
34        SEC. 25. Conflict of Interest. – Pursuant to the fundamental objective of this
35   Act to advance public health, the implementation and enforcement of this Act and
36   the development of related policies shall promote multi-sectoral coordination while
37   safeguarding against potential conflict of interest.
38
39        SEC. 26. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is held
40   invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall
41   remain in full force and effect.
42
43        SEC. 27. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, and rules
44   and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
45   repealed or amended accordingly.
