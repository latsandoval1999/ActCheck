                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The rapid expansion of digital platforms and social media has transformed
the way people communicate, access information, and participate in public
discourse. While these technologies have strengthened democratic engagement
and expanded access to information, they have also enabled the widespread
dissemination of disinformation, misinformation, malinformation,             and
coordinated inauthentic behavior that undermine public trust, distort
democratic processes, threaten public safety, and erode institutional credibility.

       In recent years, online disinformation campaigns, troll farms, fake
accounts, bot networks, and deceptive digital practices have increasingly been
used to manipulate public opinion, spread fraudulent content, interfere in civic
discourse, and exploit the vulnerabilities of digital communication systems. The
absence of sufficient transparency and accountability mechanisms among digital
platforms has further aggravated these harms.

      At the same time, any effort to address harmful online conduct must
remain firmly consistent with the constitutional guarantees of freedom of speech,
freedom of the press, and the free flow of information. Regulation must therefore
focus not on suppressing legitimate expression, criticism, dissent, journalism,
or political advocacy, but on promoting transparency, accountability, ethical
standards, and informed digital participation.

      This proposed measure seeks to establish a balanced and rights-based
framework for digital platform accountability by requiring transparency in online
advertising, coordinated inauthentic behavior takedowns, recommender
systems, and platform moderation practices. It also provides safeguards for
users through notice-and-action mechanisms, statements of reasons for
moderation decisions, and accessible dispute resolution systems.

      This bill further recognizes the role of a multi-sectoral and independent
Digital Media Standards Coalition (DMSC) that shall promote ethical standards,
support fact-checking initiatives, and encourage responsible digital conduct
without exercising censorship powers or infringing upon editorial independence.

      In addition, this measure promotes media and digital literacy through
educational institutions and strengthens government transparency through
open and verifiable public information systems.

      Ultimately, this bill seeks to protect democratic discourse and strengthen
public trust in digital communications while upholding the fundamental
freedoms guaranteed under the Constitution.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           “AN ACT
      STRENGTHENING TRANSPARENCY, ACCOUNTABILITY, AND
      DIGITAL   LITERACY  TO   COMBAT    DISINFORMATION,
      MISINFORMATION, MALINFORMATION, AND COORDINATED
      INAUTHENTIC   BEHAVIOR   ONLINE,  RECOGNIZING   AN
      INDEPENDENT MULTI-SECTORAL ETHICAL BODY, WITHOUT
      INFRINGING ON FREEDOM OF SPEECH AND THE PRESS, AND
      FOR OTHER PURPOSES”

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
  in Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Truth and
Integrity in Digital Communications Act of 2026.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to uphold
the constitutional guarantees of free speech, free press, and the free flow of
information, while protecting the people from deceptive, manipulative, or
fraudulent practices online. The State shall promote transparency,
accountability, and digital literacy, and shall regulate conduct and systems in
addressing the harms of disinformation, misinformation, malinformation, and
coordinated inauthentic behavior.

      Governance of digital platforms shall be a shared responsibility among
government, industry, and civil society, ensuring that ethical standards are
enforced without censorship or political interference.

      Nothing in this Act shall be interpreted to prohibit or restrict satire,
parody, artistic expression, academic discourse, legitimate political advocacy,
opinion, criticism, or good-faith journalistic activity protected under the
Constitution.
       SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act, the
following terms are defined as follows:

     a. Disinformation refers to false information deliberately created or
        disseminated with the intent to mislead or cause harm.

     b. Misinformation refers to false information shared without intent
        to mislead or cause harm.

     c. Malinformation refers to accurate information unlawfully
        disclosed or maliciously manipulated in a manner that causes
        demonstrable harm, including violations of privacy, public safety,
        or security, by taking such information out of context or using it
        deceptively.

     d. Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior (CIB) refers to the organized use
        of fake accounts, impersonation, bots, or deceptive networks to
        manipulate public discourse, spread falsehoods, or interfere with
        democratic processes.

     e. Digital Platforms refer to online services such as social media
        networks, search engines, and content-sharing platforms that
        reach or influence a significant number of users in the
        Philippines. The Department of Information and Communications
        Technology (DICT) shall establish objective user, reach, revenue,
        or risk-based thresholds for determining platform coverage under
        this Act.

     f. Digital Media Standards Coalition (DMSC) refers to a multi-
        sectoral, non-governmental, non-partisan, and self-governing
        body composed of associations of content creators, advertisers,
        media institutions, fact-checkers, academe, and civil society,
        recognized under this Act as an independent ethical authority.

     g. Directive refers to a formal advisory, resolution, or
        recommendation issued by the DMSC pursuant to content
        disputes or ethical reviews.

     h. Sanction refers to any non-criminal, industry-standard,
        reputational, or remedial measure such as public notices,
        content-labeling advisories, or delisting from ethical registries.

     i. Harm refers to demonstrable injury to public safety, electoral
        integrity, public health, national security, privacy rights,
        consumer protection, or lawful democratic processes.
j. Very Large Online Platform in the Philippines (VLP-PH) refers to a
   digital platform designated by the DICT as reaching at least ten
   percent (10%) of the national population in average monthly active
   users, or such threshold and methodology as the DICT may
   publish annually.

k. Notice-and-Action Mechanism refers to a standardized electronic
   system operated by platforms that enables users to submit
   notices of allegedly illegal content and requires platforms to act
   upon and communicate the result of such notices.

l. Statement of Reasons (SoR) refers to a written notification provided
   by a platform to an affected user explaining a moderation decision.
   The statement must include:(a) the action taken, (b) the legal or
   policy basis, (c) the facts relied upon, (d) whether automated
   tools were used, and (e) available remedies or redress.

m. Certified Out-of-Court Dispute Settlement (ODR) body refers to an
   independent institution accredited by the DICT to provide
   accessible, impartial, and non-binding dispute resolution services
   for user appeals of platform decisions.

n. Ad Repository refers to a searchable, publicly accessible database
   maintained by platforms that includes information on all paid
   advertisements, including the content, sponsor identity, payor,
   targeting criteria, expenditure, and dates of display.

o. Recommender System refers to an automated system used by
   platforms to determine the ranking, ordering, or prioritization of
   information displayed to users, based on profiling, engagement,
   or other algorithmic criteria.

p. Non-Profiled Feed refers to a content display option offered by
   platforms that does not rely on profiling or user-specific data, but
   uses chronological or context- based ordering

q. Dark Patterns refer to deceptive, manipulative, or coercive
   interface designs that materially distort or impair a user’s ability
   to make free, informed, and autonomous choices, such as
   misleading consent flows, hidden settings, or default opt-ins.

r. Vetted Researchers refer to academics, journalists, or civil society
   organizations accredited by the DICT or the National Privacy
   Commission (NPC) to access platform data for the purpose of
   studying systemic risks, subject to strict privacy, cybersecurity,
        and confidentiality safeguards.

     s. Risk Assessment refers to a structured evaluation conducted
        annually by VLP-PH to identify, measure, and document systemic
        risks related to disinformation, manipulation, civic discourse,
        protection of minors, and election integrity.

     t. Independent Audit refers to an assessment carried out by an
        external, qualified, and independent auditor to evaluate a
        platform’s compliance with its obligations under this Act,
        including transparency, risk assessment, and mitigation
        measures.

     u. Compliance Officer refers to a senior employee of a platform, based
        in the Philippines, designated to ensure compliance with this Act
        and act as the contact point for regulators, the DMSC, and
        stakeholders.

      SECTION 4. Platform Obligations. – All digital platforms operating in
the Philippines shall:

     a. Establish and maintain a legal entity or permanent
        representative office in the Philippines for regulatory and ethical
        accountability;

     b. Disclose publicly and in real time sponsored content, political
        advertisements, and paid campaigns, and significant takedowns
        of networks or accounts engaged in CIB;

     c. Submit annual transparency reports to the DICT, detailing
        actions taken against CIB, bot networks, and impersonation;

     d. Provide anonymized and aggregate data necessary for oversight,
        consistent with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173), the
        Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012 (RA 10175), and
        internationally accepted cybersecurity standards; any access
        beyond aggregate/anonymized data shall require DICT pre-
        approval and, where personal or content data are involved,
        judicial authorization;

     e. Maintain an accessible Notice-and-Action mechanism for users
        to report allegedly illegal content;

     f. Provide a Statement of Reasons (SoR) to affected users for any
        restriction, suspension, or removal of content or accounts,
        stating the decision taken, the legal or policy basis, the facts
         relied upon, whether automation was used, and available
         redress; anonymized SoRs shall be transmitted to a DICT
         Transparency Portal within twenty-four (24) hours;

      g. Provide users with an internal complaint-handling system and
         access to certified out-of-court dispute settlement (ODR) bodies
         under the DICT for unresolved disputes;

      h. Maintain a public ad repository that is searchable and contains,
         at minimum, the ad creative, sponsor, payor, targeting criteria,
         spend, and dates of display;

      i. Ensure that advertisements are not targeted at minors and are
         not targeted using sensitive personal information as defined by
         RA 10173;

      j. Provide transparency on recommender systems and offer users a
         clearly accessible option to view content feeds not based on
         profiling; and

      k. Refrain from deploying manipulative interface designs (“dark
         patterns”) that materially distort or impair user autonomy,
         decision-making, or choice.

      l. Platforms shall publish periodic transparency reports disclosing
         the volume and nature of government requests for content
         restriction, user data access, or account actions, subject to lawful
         confidentiality requirements.

       SECTION 5. Platform Obligations of Very Large Online Platforms in
the Philippines (VLP-PH). – VLP-PHs designated by DICT, in addition to Section
4 of this Act shall:

      a. Conduct and publish annual systemic risk assessments
         addressing, at minimum, disinformation, manipulation, civic
         discourse, protection of minors, and election integrity;

      b. Publish and implement risk-mitigation plans;

      c. Undergo an independent annual audit, filing full results with
         DICT and a public summary;

      d. Appoint a senior compliance officer responsible for obligations
         under this Act; and

      e. Provide vetted researchers, accredited by the NPC or DICT, with
         secured access to data necessary for studying systemic risks,
         subject to strict privacy and cybersecurity safeguards.

       SECTION 6. Data Access and Technical Cooperation. – In connection
with Section 5(c) and 5(e), platforms shall provide reasonable technical
cooperation, including access to application programming interfaces (APIs),
metadata, and transparency dashboards, to support oversight and audits,
subject to privacy and cybersecurity safeguards.

       SECTION 7. Penalties for Non-Compliance by Platforms – After due
notice and hearing, any platform that, without lawful excuse, fails or refuses to
comply with obligations under Sections 4, 5, and 6, shall be subjected to the
following penalties:

      a. First offense – written warning and compliance order from the
         DICT; and

      b. Second and subsequent offenses – penalty of not less than one
         million pesos (₱1,000,000) but not more than ten million pesos
         (₱10,000,000), depending on gravity.

      For VLP-PHs, the DICT may additionally impose an administrative
      penalty of up to six percent (6%) of Philippine annual gross revenue
      for grave or repeated violations.

      The DICT shall publish an annual             list   of   non-compliant
      platforms for public transparency.

       SECTION 8. Prohibited Conduct and Penalties for Bad Actors. – The
following acts are unlawful for natural persons and juridical entities:

         a. Operating or financing coordinated inauthentic behavior,
            including troll farms, bot networks, or fake accounts intended
            to deceive the public;

         b. Fraudulent misrepresentation of identity for the purpose of
            spreading disinformation or manipulating online discourse
            through deceptive or coordinated inauthentic means; and

         c. The sale or purchase of inauthentic engagement (likes, shares,
            followers, comments) through automated or deceptive means.
        Any person found guilty of violating this Section shall be subjected to
  the following penalties:

         a. Natural Persons — fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos
            (₱100,000) to One Million Pesos (₱1,000,000) and/or
            community service of at least six (6) months, at the discretion
            of the court; and

         b. Juridical entities — fine of Five Hundred Thousand Pesos
            (₱500,000) to Ten million pesos (₱10,000,000) and possible
            suspension of licenses or permits for repeat offenses.

      SECTION 9. Transition Period. – Platforms shall have a two-year
transition period from the effectivity of this Act to fully comply with reporting, ad
repository, and systemic-risk audit obligations. The DICT may phase
requirements according to platform size and risk.

       SECTION 10. Promotion of Media and Digital Literacy. – The
Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), in partnership with civil society, shall integrate media and digital
literacy into curricula at all levels. Such programs shall emphasize critical
thinking, fact-checking, responsible online behavior, and verification of
information and images. Teacher training and assessment systems shall be
developed to ensure proper implementation.

      SECTION 11. Government Communication Standards. – All
government agencies shall maintain open data portals containing verifiable
public information. They shall also correct erroneous or misleading public
statements within a reasonable time upon discovery, indicating the nature of the
error and the corrected information.

      SECTION 12. Digital Media Standards Coalition (DMSC). – The DMSC
is hereby recognized as the independent ethical authority for digital media
standards, modeled after the Ad Standards Council (ASC) and the Kapisanan
ng mga Brodkaster ng Pilipinas (KBP).

      SECTION 13. Functions of the DMSC. – The DMSC shall:

          a. Accredit independent, non-partisan fact-checking
             organizations;

         b. Issue ethical directives, advisories, and resolutions for
            platforms to consider in good faith;

         c. Impose non-criminal sanctions such as public notices, ethical
            labeling, or delisting from ethical registries;
           d. Maintain a transparent appeals mechanism where platforms,
              content creators, or users may contest advisories, resolved
              within thirty (30) days;

           e. Collaborate with international fact-checking and oversight
              institutions; and

           f. Receive from DICT the anonymized Statements of Reasons
              (SoRs) transmitted by platforms and publish annual reports
              analyzing systemic trends and platform responses.

         SECTION 14. Obligation of Platforms to Engage. – Digital platforms
shall:

           a. Give due consideration to DMSC advisories; and

           b. Issue a reasoned written explanation within thirty (30) days if
              they decline to adopt an advisory.

      Failure to engage in good faith with the DMSC, including ignoring
advisories or failing to provide a written explanation, constitutes a violation of
this Act and shall be penalized under Section 7.

      Nothing in this Act shall make DMSC advisories legally binding content
decisions. Platforms retain editorial discretion under their community
standards. The DMSC’s role remains advisory, ethical, and reputational. The
DMSC shall not exercise sovereign regulatory powers.


     SECTION 15. Enforcement. – The DICT shall oversee platform
compliance with the obligations set out in Sections 4, 5, and 6, and maintain the
DICT Transparency Portal.

      The Department of Justice (DOJ), through the National Bureau of
Investigation Cybercrime Division (NBI-CD), shall investigate and prosecute
prohibited conduct under Section 8 and other applicable laws.

     The DICT, DOJ, and DMSC shall coordinate through a Digital Integrity
Stakeholders Council to monitor implementation, evaluate emerging risks, and
recommend policy updates.

      During election periods, the DICT, the DMSC, and digital platforms shall
coordinate with the Commission on Elections to address coordinated inauthentic
behavior and election-related disinformation consistent with constitutional
guarantees of free expression and electoral integrity.
       SECTION 16. Funding and Independence. – The DMSC shall not receive
direct government appropriations. It may secure project-based grants from non-
partisan sources, which must be disclosed annually in audited transparency
reports, in order to safeguard its independence.


      SECTION 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DICT, in consultation with the DOJ,
DepEd, CHED, the DMSC, the NPC, academe, civil society, and independent
media, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations.

       The rules shall include templates for statements of reasons, the structure
of the ad repository, requirements for notice-and-action and out-of-court dispute
settlement, methodology for designating VLP-PH, protocols for vetted
researchers, and cybersecurity safeguards.

      Section 18. Congressional Oversight Review. - Within three (3) years
from the effectivity of this Act, Congress shall conduct a review of its
implementation, impact on constitutional rights, effectiveness against
coordinated disinformation, and necessity for amendments

       SECTION 19. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full
force and effect.

      SECTION 20. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, rules, or regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly

      SECTION 21. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two newspapers of general
circulation.


      Approved.
