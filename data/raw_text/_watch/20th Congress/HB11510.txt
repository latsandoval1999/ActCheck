                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

In today’s hyperconnected world, the internet has become both a gateway to opportunity
and a source of risk. The Philippines, with 90.8 million social media users as of January
2025—representing 78% of the population and averaging 3 hours and 32 minutes daily
online use (DataReportal, We Are Social, Meltwater – Digital 2025 Report), ranks among
the most active nations globally. This level of engagement underscores the urgent need
for structured education on internet safety and media literacy.

While young Filipinos are often described as “digital natives,” technical proficiency alone
does not equate to digital literacy. Exposure to cyberbullying, misinformation, predatory
grooming, and privacy breaches continues to rise, threatening both individual well-being
and national resilience.

This measure seeks to institutionalize an Internet Safety Education Program (ISEP) in all
elementary and secondary schools. The program will equip teachers with professional
training, empower students through peer-driven initiatives, and engage parents and
communities in building a “360-degree” safety net. By embedding internet safety and
media literacy into the curriculum, the bill ensures that the next generation is not only
connected, but protected, informed, and responsible.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT ESTABLISHING A SAFETY INTERNET EDUCATION PROGRAM IN THE
      CURRICULUM OF ALL ELEMENTARY AND SECONDARY SCHOOLS

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Internet Safety Education Act."

SEC. 2. Internet Safety Education Program (ISEP). - The Department of Education
(DepEd) shall, within six (6) months after the effectivity of this Act, formulate, develop and
implement an ISEP. The ISEP shall be a research-based program that encourages safe,
ethical and responsible use of the Internet, promotes an informed and critical
understanding of the Internet, and educates children and communities about how to
prevent or respond to problems or dangers related to the Internet or new media.

SEC. 3. Implementation. - In order to ensure the efficiency and effectivity of the ISEP, the
DepEd shall:
  (a) Identify, develop and implement educational technology, multimedia and
      interactive applications, online resources and lesson plans for ISEP;
  (b) Provide professional training to elementary and secondary teachers,
      administrators and other staff on internet safety and New Media literacy. For the
      purposes of this Act, New Media is defined as emerging, digital, computerized or
      network-informed communication technologies that often have interactive
      capacities. This includes email, instant messaging, text messaging, websites,
      blogs, interactive gaming, social media, cellphones and mobile devices;
  (c) Develop online risk prevention programs for children;
  (d) Train and support peer-driven internet safety education initiatives;
   (e) Coordinate and fund research initiatives that investigate online risks to promote
       awareness of online risks to children and internet safety education;
   (f) Develop and implement public education campaigns to promote awareness of
       online risks to children and internet safety education;
   (g) Educate parents about teaching their children to use the internet and new media
       safely, responsibly and ethically, and help parents identify and protect their
       children from risks relating to use of the internet and new media; and
   (h) The Secretary shall, within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act,
       promulgate rules and regulations, including those of disciplinary nature, to carry
       out and enforce the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 4. Sec. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The Department of Education (DepEd) shall
submit an annual report to Congress on the implementation and effectiveness of the
Internet Safety Education Program (ISEP). The report shall include, but not be limited to,
measurable indicators such as:

   (a) Number of schools implementing ISEP;
   (b) Teacher and administrator training coverage;
   (c) Reported cases of cyberbullying and online exploitation;
   (d) Student literacy and awareness levels on internet safety;
   (e) Parental engagement and participation in internet safety education; and
   (f) Recommendations for program improvement.

SEC. 5. Appropriations. - The initial amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this
Act shall be charged against the current budget of the DepEd. Thereafter, the amount
necessary for the continuous implementation of this program shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 6. Separability Clause. - If any provision or section of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions and sections not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

SEC. 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders or issuances
or any part thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

SEC. 8. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
