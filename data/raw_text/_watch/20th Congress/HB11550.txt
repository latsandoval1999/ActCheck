EXPLANATORY NOTE

There are over 2.5 million Filipinos living with disabilities. While the
country has programs and projects for PWDs, that is not enough to fully care
for PWDs. They would need assistive care in their homes and their daily
activities.

Across the country, parents, spouses, siblings, relatives, and other
family members devote countless hours each day to providing physical
assistance, medical care, rehabilitation, supervision, and advocacy for their
loved ones with disabilities. Their work is indispensable, yet it remains largely
unpaid, unrecognized, and unsupported.

While Republic Act No. 7277, or the Magna Carta for Disabled Persons,
affirms the rights and welfare of persons with disabilities, there remains no
comprehensive law that recognizes and supports the equally vital role of their
caregivers. Many caregivers are compelled to reduce their working hours,
forego employment opportunities, or leave the workforce altogether to
provide full-time care. Consequently, they experience financial hardship,
physical exhaustion, emotional stress, social isolation, and increased
vulnerability to mental health concerns. Amidst these, caregivers provide
unpaid work 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

While initiatives such as the Department of Social Welfare and
Development's Therapy for Fee (TheraFee) Project, which provided cash-for-

===== [page 2] =====
work assistance to family and community care workers who support persons
with disabilities, represent a significant step forward, there remains a need for
a comprehensive and sustainable framework that addresses the broader needs
of caregivers of persons with disabilities. Financial assistance alone is not
sufficient.

This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive
support system for caregivers of persons with disabilities. It provides
qualified primary caregivers with a monthly caregiver assistance allowance,
free skills training and certification, access to livelihood and employment
support, mental health and wellness services, respite care, and priority
consideration in appropriate government programs.

Empowering caregivers is likewise an investment in persons with
disabilities. When caregivers are adequately supported, they are better
equipped to provide consistent and quality care, facilitate rehabilitation and
development, promote independence, and help persons with disabilities
participate more fully in society.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

KE TH T. GATCHALIAN

HOUSE BILL No. 1195590

AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE WELFARE, SOCIAL PROTECTION, TRAINING,
AND OTHER SUPPORT MECHANISMS FOR CAREGIVERS OF PERSONS
WITH DISABILITIES (PWDs), AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the
“Mapagkalinga Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to recognize the invaluable contribution of caregivers in promoting the well-
being, development, rehabilitation, and inclusion of persons with disabilities. The
State shall establish programs and mechanisms that support caregivers through
financial assistance, skills development, social protection, mental health services,
and other forms of assistance necessary to improve their quality of life and
caregiving capacity.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:
(a) Caregiver refers to a parent, guardian, spouse, sibling, relative,

foster parent, or designated individual who provides regular and

(b) Primary Caregiver refers to an individual who assumes the principal
and regular responsibility for providing daily care, assistance,

SEC. 4. Caregiver Certification. — Qualified caregivers shall be issued a
certification by the concerned Local Government Unit, Department of Social
Welfare and Development (DSWD), or National Council on Disability Affairs
(NCDA) upon submission of the necessary documentary requirements.

This certification shall serve as proof of eligibility for benefits and
privileges under this Act.

SEC. 5. Free Skills Training and Certification. — Registered caregivers
shall be entitled to free enrollment in skills training, livelihood development,
entrepreneurship, digital literacy, vocational, and technical education programs
conducted by government agencies, including Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority (TESDA), state universities and colleges, local
universities and colleges, and other government training institutions.

Assessment, certification, accreditation, and other related fees required

under such programs shall likewise be waived.

SEC. 6. Caregiver Compensation and Assistance Program. — Registered
primary caregivers, or duly certified individuals who have assumed the principal
responsibility for the daily care and support of persons with disabilities, shall be
entitled to a monthly caregiver assistance allowance of Two Thousand Pesos
(P2,000.00): Provided, That only one (1) registered primary caregiver per person
with disability shall be entitled to receive the monthly caregiver assistance
allowance under this Act: Provided, further, That the monthly caregiver assistance
allowance provided under this Act shall be separate and distinct from any benefits,
pensions, subsidies, or financial assistance granted to the person with disability
under existing laws and programs.

SEC. 7. Employment Support and Flexible Work Arrangements. —
Government agencies and private employers are encouraged to adopt flexible work
arrangements, modified work schedules, and other reasonable accommodations for
employees who serve as primary caregivers of persons with disabilities.

SEC. 8. Mental Health and Weliness Services. ~ The Department of
Health (DOH), in coordination with local government units and the National
Center for Mental Health (NCMH), shall ensure the availability of counseling
services, mental health interventions, support groups, and caregiver wellness
programs designed to address caregiver stress, burnout, and psychological well-
being.

SEC. 9. National Registry of Caregivers. - The DSWD, through the
NCDA, shall establish and maintain a National Registry of Caregivers of Persons
with Disabilities for policy development, program implementation, and service
delivery purposes.

SEC. 10. Documentary Requirements. — To avail of the benefits under this
Act, a caregiver shall submit:

(a) A valid PWD Identification Card of the person with disability;

SEC. 11. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Department of Social Welfare and Development. Thereafter, such sums as may
be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Social Welfare and Development
serving as the lead implementing agency of this Act, in coordination with the
Secretary of Health, Secretary of Labor and Employment, Director General of
TESDA, Executive Director of NCDA, Local Government Units, and other
relevant agencies as well as organizations of persons with disabilities, caregivers’
groups, and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 13. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 15. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its

publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
