                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to establish a National Nurse Mentorship and Training
Program that mobilizes the expertise of returning Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW)
nurses to strengthen the competencies of locally practicing nurses, enhance nursing
education and clinical training, and improve the quality of healthcare services
throughout the country.

       The Philippines remains one of the world’s leading sources of nurses,
supplying healthcare professionals to countries facing workforce shortages.
According to the World Health Organization (WHO)1, the global shortage of health
and care workers is projected to reach approximately 11 million by 2030, driving
sustained international demand for Filipino nurses. While overseas employment
provides better compensation and professional advancement, it also contributes to
persistent shortages of experienced nurses in Philippine healthcare facilities,
particularly in government hospitals and underserved communities. This continuing
migration has placed additional pressure on the country’s health system and
underscores the need to maximize the value of the nursing workforce that returns
home.

       Returning OFW nurses possess advanced clinical competencies, specialized
training, and extensive experience acquired from highly developed healthcare
systems. Many have worked in specialized fields such as critical care, emergency
medicine, oncology, geriatrics, maternal and child health, mental health, and
infection prevention. They also bring practical knowledge on modern healthcare
technologies, patient safety systems, interdisciplinary collaboration, and
evidence-based nursing practices. Despite the significant value of these
competencies, the country has yet to institutionalize a mechanism that
systematically facilitates their transfer to the local nursing workforce.

        This measure addresses that gap through a structured mentorship and skills

professional development, continuing education, and capacity building across both

       The bill also recognizes mentorship as a professional contribution deserving
institutional support. It grants Continuing Professional Development (CPD) credits
to participating nurses while providing appropriate incentives to accredited

government-supported postgraduate education and specialized training. These
measures encourage participation, promote professional excellence, and reinforce
the value of knowledge sharing within the nursing profession.

        Beyond workforce development, the proposed measure transforms overseas
nursing experience into a strategic national resource. Rather than allowing
internationally acquired expertise to remain untapped, the bill promotes its
reintegration into the Philippine healthcare system, strengthens institutional
capacity, raises standards of patient care, and supports the long-term development
of a resilient and globally competitive nursing profession.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
PROVIDING OPPORTUNITIES FOR RETURNING OVERSEAS FILIPINO
WORKER NURSES TO TRAIN LOCAL NURSES, ESTABLISHING A
NATIONAL NURSE MENTORSHIP AND TRAINING PROGRAM,
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Nurse
Mentorship and Training Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
recognize the invaluable contribution of Filipino nurses working overseas and to
harness their acquired knowledge, skills, and experience for national development.
To this end, the State shall provide opportunities for returning overseas Filipino
worker (OFW) nurses to contribute to the upgrading of nursing education, hospital
training, and health service delivery in the Philippines.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

          a. ﻿A returning OFW nurse refers to a licensed Filipino nurse who has
       worked abroad and is returning to the Philippines either temporarily or
       permanently; and

          b. ﻿Local nurse refers to any licensed nurse practicing in the Philippines,
       whether employed in public or private institutions, or newly graduated and
       undergoing hospital or community training.

      SEC. 4. Establishment of the National Nurse Mentorship and Training Program. –
There is hereby established a National Nurse Mentorship and Training Program
under the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Professional
Regulatory Board of Nursing (PRBON), Commission on Higher Education (CHED),
and the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW).



                                            1
      SEC. 5. Mentorship Assignments. – Returning OFW nurses may serve as
mentors in accredited hospitals, rural health units, community health centers, and
higher education institutions offering nursing programs.

       SEC. 6. Knowledge and Skills Transfer. – The training shall cover specialized
skills, updated international standards, advanced nursing practices, and
innovations in patient care acquired abroad.

       SEC. 7. Capacity Building. – Returning OFW nurses shall assist in training
local nurses for critical areas such as intensive care, emergency medicine, geriatrics,
maternal and child care, and public health.

       SEC. 8. Continuing Professional Development Credits. – Participation by both
returning OFW nurses and local nurses shall be credited as Continuing Professional
Development (CPD) units by the PRBON.

       SEC. 9. Incentives. – Returning OFW nurses who participate shall receive
appropriate honoraria, tax incentives, and eligibility for government scholarships or
grants for postgraduate studies.

      SEC. 10. Implementing Agencies. – The implementing agencies shall be as
follows:

          a.​ ﻿The DOH shall accredit hospitals and facilities as host training sites;

          b.​ ﻿The PRBON shall accredit returning OFW nurses as mentors/trainers;

        c.​ ﻿The CHED shall integrate mentorship programs into nursing curricula
      where feasible; and

         d.​ ﻿The DMW shall coordinate with foreign employers to facilitate return
      programs and encourage skills sharing upon reintegration.

      SEC. 11. Funding. – The amount necessary to carry out the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the DOH. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DOH, in coordination with
the PRC, CHED, and DMW, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations
for the effective implementation of this Act within ninety (90) days after its
effectivity.

       SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

      SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
