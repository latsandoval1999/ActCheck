                            EXPLANATORY NOTE


Wage theft in the form of unpaid overtime, holiday, and premium pay remains
widespread, particularly among contractual and informal-sector workers who fear
retaliation for filing complaints. Current penalties are too weak to deter habitual
violators, and workers often face lengthy adjudication before recovering what they
are legally owed.

This bill increases penalties for withholding overtime and holiday pay, creates a
summary claims process for amounts below a defined threshold, and prohibits
retaliatory dismissal of employees who assert their rights under this Act.

Respectfully submitted,

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. This Act shall be known as the "Overtime and Holiday Pay
Protection Act."
      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State affirms the right of every
worker to just and humane conditions of work, including fair compensation for
overtime and work rendered on rest days and holidays.
      SECTION 3. Summary Claims Process. — Claims for unpaid overtime,
holiday, or premium pay not exceeding Fifty Thousand Pesos (Php50,000.00) per
worker shall be resolved through a summary process before the Department of
Labor and Employment, with a decision to be rendered within thirty (30) days
from filing.
       SECTION 4. Anti-Retaliation Clause. — No employer shall dismiss,
demote, or otherwise discriminate against any employee for filing a claim,
testifying, or assisting in any proceeding under this Act. Any dismissal made
within ninety (90) days of such filing shall be presumed retaliatory and the burden
of proving a valid cause shall be on the employer.
      SECTION 5. Increased Penalties. — Employers found to have willfully
withheld overtime, holiday, or premium pay shall pay the unpaid amount plus a
penalty equivalent to twenty-five percent (25%) thereof, in addition to any fine
imposed under existing labor laws.




                                       Page 2
       SECTION 6. Habitual Violators. — Establishments found liable for
violations under this Act three (3) or more times within a five (5)-year period shall
be included in a public registry of habitual violators maintained by the
Department of Labor and Employment.
       SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The Department of
Labor and Employment shall issue implementing rules within sixty (60) days from
effectivity.
      SECTION 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in force.
      SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, and
issuances inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
      SECTION 10. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
