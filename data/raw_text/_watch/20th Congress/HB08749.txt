                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE


        The Philippines’ agricultural trade landscape in 2024 continues to reflect a strong
dependence on imports. Agricultural imports accounted for approximately 71.5 percent of total
agricultural trade, which was valued at around US$19.46 billion, while exports were only about
US$7.75 billion. This significant imbalance widened the domestic agricultural trade deficit to
nearly US$11.71 billion, highlighting the country’s continued reliance on foreign agricultural
products to meet domestic demand. Such dependence not only affects the country’s trade position
but also exposes local farmers and rural communities to vulnerabilities in global markets and price
fluctuations.

         Concurrently, the agricultural sector is experiencing a notable demographic shift. The
average age of Filipino farmers has declined from 57 years to approximately 49 to 50 years,
signaling that a new generation of younger individuals is entering the field of agriculture. While
this trend reflects a potential rejuvenation of the sector, these young farmers face considerable and
compounding challenges in securing sustainable livelihoods. They encounter limited access to
affordable credit, inadequate availability of land or insecure land tenure, high input and production
costs, and market conditions that often favor imported goods over local products. These constraints
create structural barriers that hinder productivity, reduce profitability, and discourage long-term
engagement in farming.

        Furthermore, technological gaps and the pressures of climate change pose additional threats
to agricultural productivity and resilience. Without targeted interventions and structural support,
many young farmers risk abandoning the sector altogether. Such a scenario could exacerbate food
insecurity, weaken rural economies, and undermine the sustainability of the nation’s agricultural
systems.

         Recognizing these challenges, this bill seeks to establish institutional mechanisms that will
prioritize and support young farmers in the formulation and implementation of national policies
and programs. Specifically, it proposes the creation of the Young Farmers Council and the National
Young Farmers Roadmap to ensure that the needs, perspectives, and rights of young farmers are
formally integrated into decision-making processes. The bill affirms the right of young farmers to
equal access to technology, affordable capital, secure farmland, and market opportunities, while

          AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF YOUNG FARMERS

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

1          SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “The Magna Carta of Young
2    Farmers.”

3            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to recognize
4    the economic, political, and socio-cultural conditions affecting young farmers and affirm their vital
5    role in nation-building, environmental sustainability, and the attainment of food security. It shall
6    formulate and implement plans, policies, programs, and mechanisms that will ensure the
7    empowerment, productivity, and competitiveness of young farmers in both local and international
8    markets

 9           The State acknowledges the role of young farmers as contributors to household income
10   and as key participants in the agricultural value chain, from production and processing to value-
11   adding and marketing. It further recognizes their role as advocates of environmental protection
12   and practitioners of sustainable farming.
13
14           The State likewise affirms the indispensable contribution of young farmers to national
15   food security, rural development, and the overall economy. Towards this end, the State
16   recognizes young farmers as a distinct basic sector with the right to participate in policy
17   formulation, planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of programs, projects, and
18   services that directly affect them.
19
20           Finally, the State shall establish and strengthen mechanisms for the promotion and
21   protection of the rights of young farmers, and ensure their full inclusion in development
22   processes.
23

25
26                   a) Agriculture refers to the prudent endeavor of cultivating plants and animals
27                   which includes the tillage of the soil, dairy, production, cultivation, growing, and
28                   harvesting of any agricultural, aquacultural, floricultural or horticultural

 7   b) Discrimination refers to any distinction, exclusion, or restriction which has the
 8   effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment, or exercise

10   human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural,

24   practitioners of sustainable farming;

26   d) Family Farming is a means of organizing agricultural, forestry, fisheries,
27   pastoral, and aquaculture production which is managed and operated by a family
28   and predominantly reliant on family labor, including both women and men;
29
30   e) Farm work refers to the land cultivation and tillage, dairying, production,
31   growing, harvesting, plowing, planting, application of fertilizers, and farm
32   supervision performed by an individual or a group of people;
33
34
35   f) Food Security refers to the condition in which all people, at all times, have
36   physical, social and economic access to sufficient safe and nutritious food that
37   meets their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life (as
38   defined by the United Nations Committee on World Food Security);
39
40   g) Lessee refers to a person who, by themselves, or with the aid available from
41   within their immediate farm household, cultivates the land, belonging to or lawfully
42   possessed by another, with the latter's consent, for purposes of agricultural
43   production, for a price certain in money or in produce or both. The term is
44   distinguished from a civil lessee as understood in the Civil Code of the Philippines;
45
46   h) Organic refers to the particular farming and processing system, described in the
47   standards and not in the classical chemical sense. The term "organic" is
48   synonymous in other languages with "biological" or "ecological". It is also a
49   labeling term that denoted products considered organic based on the Philippine
50   National Standards for organic agriculture;
51
52   i) Organic Agriculture refers to all agricultural systems that promote the

33          SEC. 4. State as Primary Duty-Bearer. – The State, as the primary duty bearer shall:
34
35                   a) Recognize, promote, and protect the rights of young farmers defined and
36                  guaranteed under this Act;
37
38                  b) Lead against the discrimination and coercion or violence, be physical, verbal,
39                  sexual, emotional, psychological, spiritual, or cultural in nature of young farmers;
40
41                  c) Endorse farm products produced by young farmers for economic development
42                  nationwide;
43
44                  d) Promote, protect and fulfill the rights of young farmers in all aspects including
45                  but not limited to their rights to food security, education and training, access to
46                  land, social justice, employment, health services, and participation.
47
48           The State shall take measures and establish mechanisms to promote the coherent and
49   integrated implementation and enforcement of this Act and related laws, policies, or other measures
50   to effectively recognize young farmers as a distinct basic sector, stop discrimination against young
51   farmers and farming in general, and advance their rights and responsibilities.

15          SEC. 11. Right to farmland. – The land rights of young farmers shall be prioritized. In
16   doing so, the State shall guarantee the following:
17
18                  a) Improve access to low-cost farmland. – Create more flexibility within
19                  CARP/CARPER to allow land trusts to protect farmland quickly and facilitate a
20                  new farmer. The DAR shall also ensure to protect the affordability of conserved
21                  lands and ensure that it remains in farming.
22
23                  b) Land Transfer Tax Exemptions. – Transactions including, but not limited to,
24                  purchase, inheritance, and donation of land, which is one (1) hectare and below, to
25                  young farmers, as defined under this Act, are hereby exempt from all taxes of
26                  whatever kind: Provided, however, That the beneficiary guarantees that the land
27                  will be kept and maintained by him or her for at least five (5) years.
28
29                  c) Land and Farm Planning Management. – The Department of Agriculture (DA),
30                  together with the LGUs and other related agencies, shall provide full assistance to
31                  young farmers, from the national scale down to community levels as regards in
32                  farm planning and management to ensure maximum utilization, production,
33                  profitability, and sustainability of their farms. The department shall utilize the
34                  existence of Farmer Field Schools and Agricultural Extension Training Centres as
35                  learning sites to ensure full-scale assistance to young farmers.

19           SEC. 13. Environmental Protection. – DA, in partnership with DENR and DOST shall
20   establish model farms that feature sustainable farming practices such as integrated and diverse
21   organic farming systems, climate-smart and resilient agriculture, organic urban farming systems,
22   and the like to serve as knowledge transfer and training institutions for young farmers and
23   agricultural workers and provide an avenue for the active participation of young farmers in
24   environmental protection and practice of sustainable farming.
25
26           SEC. 14. Recognition and Preservation of Cultural Identity and Indigenous Farming
27   Traditions. – The State shall respect, preserve and maintain knowledge, innovations, and practices
28   of indigenous and local communities of young farmers embodying traditional lifestyles relevant to
29   the conservation and sustainable use of biological diversity. These shall be considered in the
30   formulation and implementation of national policies and programs.
31
32           SEC. 15. Protection from Discrimination, Red-tagging, and Violence. – The State shall
33   ensure that all young farmers shall be protected from all forms of discrimination, red-tagging, and
34   violence as provided for in existing laws. They shall be ensured access to free legal services from
35   the Public Attorney's Office (PAO).
36
37            SEC. 16. Representation and Participation. – The State shall ensure young farmers'
38   involvement in the planning and design stages of agri-food development projects from
39   conceptualization to implementation. It shall also ensure the participation of young farmers in the
40   decision-making and policy-making processes at regional, national, and international levels
41   initiated by the government and private entities including but not limited to the National and Local
42   Agrarian Reform Councils, National Organic Agricultural Board, National Youth Commission,
43   and the National Anti-Poverty Commission to fully realize their roles as agents and partners for
44   development.
45
