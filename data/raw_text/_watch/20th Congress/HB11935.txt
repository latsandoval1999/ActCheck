                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 1 of Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that
the goals of the national economy are a more equitable distribution of
opportunities, income, and wealth, a sustained increase in the amount of goods
and services produced by the nation for the benefit of the people, and an
expanding productivity as the key to raising the quality of life for all, especially
the underprivileged. Complementing this mandate, Section 5 of Article XIII
recognizes the rights of farmers, farmworkers, cooperatives, and independent
farmers' organizations to participate in the planning, organization, and
management of agricultural development and directs the State to provide them
with support through appropriate technology and research, adequate financial,
production, marketing, and other support services. Likewise, Section 15 of
Article XIV mandates the State to conserve, promote, and popularize the nation's
historical and cultural heritage and resources, while Section 16 of Article II
recognizes the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology in accord
with the rhythm and harmony of nature.
       In fulfillment of these constitutional mandates, this measure seeks to
establish Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones (ATLZs) as designated areas where
agricultural production, farm-based tourism, cultural heritage preservation,
environmental sustainability, and community-based enterprises are integrated to
stimulate inclusive rural development. The proposed measure recognizes that
agriculture is not only the foundation of national food security but also a valuable
economic, educational, cultural, and tourism resource capable of generating
diversified livelihood opportunities for rural communities. Through the
establishment of Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones, the State can strengthen local
economies, create alternative sources of income for farmers and fisherfolk,
preserve agricultural traditions and cultural landscapes, encourage responsible
investments, and promote environmentally sustainable development.
       The enactment of the Farm Tourism Development Act of 2016 (Republic
Act No. 10816) marked a significant milestone in recognizing farm tourism as a
strategy for agricultural and rural development. The law established a national
policy for the promotion of farm tourism, the formulation of a Farm Tourism
Strategic Action Plan, and institutional mechanisms to encourage investment,
research, infrastructure development, and accreditation of farm tourism sites.
However, while Republic Act No. 10816 primarily focuses on the promotion and
regulation of individual farm tourism enterprises, it does not establish designated
agri-tourism development zones with integrated land-use planning, heritage
conservation, community enterprise development, and localized governance
mechanisms that allow entire farming communities to benefit collectively from
agriculture-tourism integration.
       The Philippine Development Plan (PDP) 2023–2028 identifies agriculture,
tourism, local economic development, and environmental sustainability as major
drivers of inclusive growth.1 It emphasizes the modernization of agriculture,
diversification of rural livelihoods, promotion of sustainable tourism,
strengthening of local enterprises, expansion of infrastructure, digital
transformation, and adoption of place-based development strategies to generate
quality employment, reduce rural poverty, and improve regional competitiveness.
       Supporting these policy directions, the Philippine Institute for
Development Studies (PIDS) found that community-based agroecotourism
strengthens rural livelihoods by empowering local communities to manage
tourism initiatives while preserving biodiversity, indigenous knowledge,
traditional agricultural practices, and cultural heritage.2 The study concludes that

       Recognizing these policy opportunities, this proposed legislation
institutionalizes a comprehensive national framework for the establishment and
development of Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones throughout the country. It
establishes national zoning standards, heritage conservation measures, Local
Government Unit implementation mechanisms, environmental safeguards,
infrastructure support, and fiscal and non-fiscal incentives for community-based
agri-tourism enterprises, farmers' cooperatives, indigenous peoples, and rural
micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs). The measure likewise
strengthens coordination among national government agencies, local government
units, academic institutions, civil society organizations, and the private sector to
ensure the sustainable planning, management, and promotion of agri-tourism as
a catalyst for countryside development.
       The establishment of Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones will diversify rural
economies, increase farm incomes, generate employment opportunities,
strengthen local entrepreneurship, preserve agricultural heritage and cultural
landscapes, promote environmental stewardship, and reinforce food security
through sustainable agricultural practices. By integrating agriculture, tourism,
culture, and community enterprise within a unified policy framework, the
proposed measure seeks to transform rural communities into resilient,
competitive, and inclusive centers of sustainable development.
      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Agri-Tourism
Livelihood Zones Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to promote inclusive and sustainable rural development by integrating
agriculture, tourism, environmental conservation, and cultural heritage into a
unified development strategy. The State recognizes that agriculture serves not
only as the foundation of food security but also as a valuable economic,
educational, cultural, and tourism resource capable of generating diversified
livelihood opportunities for rural communities.
      Pursuant to this policy, the State shall establish Agri-Tourism Livelihood
Zones (ATLZs) that strengthen local economies through farm-based tourism,
preserve agricultural heritage and indigenous knowledge systems, encourage
sustainable agricultural production, support community-based enterprises, create
employment opportunities, attract responsible investments, and enhance the
competitiveness of Philippine agri-tourism while ensuring the protection of
agricultural lands, natural resources, and cultural landscapes.

      SEC. 3. Objectives - This Act shall pursue the following objectives:

      (a) Establish designated Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones throughout the
      country;

      (b) Promote sustainable farm-based tourism as an alternative source of
      income for farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives, indigenous peoples, and rural
      communities;

      (c) Preserve traditional farming systems, cultural landscapes, agricultural
      heritage, indigenous knowledge, local cuisine, and rural craftsmanship;

      (d) Encourage community-based tourism enterprises that generate
      employment and entrepreneurship opportunities;

      (e) Develop integrated agricultural value chains through tourism,
      agribusiness, food processing, and local marketing;

      (f) Strengthen local government participation in agri-tourism planning and
      management;

      (g) Promote climate-resilient and environmentally sustainable agricultural
      practices;

(h) Encourage investments in agri-tourism infrastructure and rural
enterprises; and

(i) Improve the quality of life and economic resilience of rural communities
through inclusive countryside development.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:

(a) Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zone (ATLZ) refers to a designated
geographical area officially recognized by the Department of Tourism and
the Department of Agriculture where agriculture, tourism, cultural
heritage, environmental conservation, and rural enterprises are integrated
for sustainable local economic development.

(b) Agri-Tourism Enterprise refers to any business or livelihood activity
operating within an ATLZ that combines agricultural production with
tourism, educational, recreational, or cultural experiences.

(c) Community-Based Agri-Tourism Enterprise refers to an enterprise
owned, managed, or operated by farmers, fisherfolk, cooperatives,
indigenous peoples, rural organizations, or local communities.

(d) Farm-Based Tourism refers to tourism activities conducted in farms or
agricultural communities that provide educational, recreational, cultural, or
experiential activities while maintaining the agricultural character of the
land.

(e) Heritage Livelihood refers to traditional income-generating activities
rooted in farming, food production, handicrafts, indigenous practices, local
cuisine, cultural traditions, and agricultural knowledge passed from one
generation to another.

      (f) Cultural Landscape refers to agricultural lands and rural communities
      possessing significant historical, environmental, cultural, or traditional
      value.

      SEC. 5. National Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones Framework. - The
Department of Tourism (DOT), in coordination with the Department of
Agriculture (DA), Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of the
Interior and Local Government (DILG), Department of Environment and Natural
Resources (DENR), National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA),
National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), Department of Public
Works and Highways (DPWH), Technical Education and Skills Development
Authority (TESDA), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and concerned
Local Government Units (LGUs), shall formulate a National Agri-Tourism
Livelihood Zones Framework to guide the establishment, development,
regulation, and promotion of ATLZs throughout the country.

      The Framework shall be reviewed and updated every six (6) years.

      SEC. 6. Designation of Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones. - The DOT and
the DA shall jointly identify and designate Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones based
on the following criteria:

      (a) Presence of productive agricultural lands;

      (b) Existing or potential tourism attractions;

      (c) Significant agricultural heritage or cultural landscapes;

      (d) Active participation of farmers' organizations or cooperatives;

      (e) Environmental sustainability;

      (f) Accessibility and infrastructure potential;
                                                                               4
      (g) Local Government Unit commitment; and

      (h) Community participation and support.

      SEC. 7. Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zone Development Plan. - Each
designated ATLZ shall prepare a comprehensive Development Plan integrating:

      (a) Agricultural development;

      (b) Tourism promotion;

      (c) Heritage conservation;

      (d) Infrastructure improvement;

      (e) Climate adaptation and disaster resilience;

      (f) Environmental protection;

      (g) Investment promotion;

      (h) Livelihood generation;

      (i) Marketing and branding; and

      (j) Community participation.

      SEC. 8. Heritage Conservation Measures. - Every ATLZ shall implement
programs to preserve and promote:

      (a) Traditional farming systems;

      (b) Indigenous agricultural knowledge;

      (c) Agricultural heritage sites;

      (d) Local culinary traditions;
                                                                          5
      (e) Native crops and livestock breeds;

      (f) Traditional crafts;

      (g) Rural architecture; and

      (h) Cultural festivals related to agriculture.

      The NCCA, NCIP, DA, and DOT shall jointly formulate national standards
for heritage conservation within Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones.

      SEC. 9. Community-Based Agri-Tourism Enterprises. - The State shall
prioritize the development of community-based enterprises operated by:

      (a) Farmers' cooperatives;

      (b) Fisherfolk associations;

      (c) Indigenous Cultural Communities;

      (d) Women's organizations;

      (e) Youth organizations;

      (f) Rural MSMEs; and

      (g) Social enterprises.

      Such enterprises shall receive technical assistance, training, business
development support, market promotion, and access to government financing
programs.

      SEC. 10. Incentives. - Qualified enterprises operating within designated
Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones may enjoy, subject to existing laws and
regulations:

                                                                             6
      (a) Priority access to government credit facilities;

      (b) Technical and marketing assistance;

      (c) Skills development and entrepreneurship training;

      (d) Product development assistance;

      (e) Access to shared service facilities;

      (f) Priority inclusion in tourism promotion campaigns;

      (g) Assistance in digital marketing and e-commerce; and

      (h) Other incentives as may be provided under existing investment,
      tourism, agriculture, and MSME laws.

      SEC. 11. Infrastructure Development. - The National Government shall
prioritize the development of:

      (a) Farm-to-market roads;

      (b) Farm tourism access roads;

      (c) Water supply systems;

      (d) Renewable energy facilities;

      (e) Broadband connectivity;

      (f) Public sanitation facilities;

      (g) Waste management systems;

      (h) Visitor centers;

      (i) Farmers' markets; and
                                                                           7
      (j) Common processing facilities.

      SEC. 12. Capacity Building. - The DA, DOT, TESDA, CHED, DTI, CDA,
and State Universities and Colleges shall jointly conduct capacity-building
programs covering sustainable agriculture, tourism management, digital
entrepreneurship,    hospitality,   financial   literacy,   food   safety,   cultural
interpretation, and climate-resilient farming practices.

      SEC. 13. Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones Council. - There is hereby
created the National Agri-Tourism Livelihood Zones Council, chaired by the
Secretary of Tourism and co-chaired by the Secretary of Agriculture, with
representatives from the DTI, DILG, DENR, DPWH, TESDA, CHED, NCCA,
NCIP, CDA, and representatives from farmers, cooperatives, indigenous peoples,
tourism associations, and rural enterprises.

      The Council shall formulate policies, coordinate implementation, monitor
compliance, evaluate performance, and submit an annual report to Congress.

      SEC. 14. Funding. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
implementing agencies. Thereafter, the amount necessary shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.

      SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within one hundred
twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOT and the DA, in
coordination with the concerned agencies, shall promulgate the Implementing
Rules and Regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

      SEC. 16. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.
