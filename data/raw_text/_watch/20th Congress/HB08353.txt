                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article XII, Section 2 of the 1987 Constitution provides that, “[t]he State shall
protect the nation’s marine wealth in its archipelagic waters, territorial sea, and
exclusive economic zone, and reserve its use and enjoyment exclusively to Filipino
citizens.”

        The Philippines is an archipelago composed of 7,641 islands, based on the
latest data of the National Mapping and Resource Information Authority (NAMRIA),
with a coastline of over 36,000 kilometers, ranking among the top countries for
coastline length. Many coastal communities are dependent on fisheries and other
aquatic resources for their food and livelihood. However, the country’s resources are
in dire condition due to anthropogenic activities that adversely impact coastal habitats,
notably mangroves.

       Over 50% of the Philippines’ mangroves have been lost to fishpond conversion,
improper mangrove planting practices, weak enforcement, pollution, and aggressive
coastal development projects. At present, there exist statutory provisions prohibiting
the cutting or conversion of mangroves, and several orders and circulars issued by
relevant agencies seeking to manage, reforest, and revert abandoned fishponds to its
original mangrove state.

        However, due to the fragmented framework across various government
agencies, many mandates and requirements are either scientifically unsound, lacking
realistic basis, or unimplementable because of the institutional limitations of the
government agencies involved. The lack of a national policy on mangroves and the

                                           1
absence of a comprehensive framework pose a great challenge and have caused
existing mangrove protection and rehabilitation efforts to remain ineffective and
unsuccessful.

       This proposed National Coastal Greenbelt Bill seeks to provide a legal
framework for the designation, restoration, and sustainable management of local
communities of coastal greenbelt zones nationwide. Specifically, the bill defines the
policy goals and objectives for the management of mangrove resources in the country,
thus enabling agencies to address intersecting issues and work towards a common
goal. Primarily, the bill seeks to require the establishment of coastal greenbelt zones,
which are mangroves and beach forests stretching at least 100 meters in width,
designed to prevent coastal erosion and mitigate the adverse impacts of natural
coastal hazards on human lives and property.

       In the 20th Congress, similar bills were filed by Senators Bam Aquino, Jinggoy
Estrada, Risa Hontiveros, and Loren Legarda, and Representatives Jose Manuel Alba,
Rufus Rodriguez, Maximo Rodriguez, Stephen Tan, Lani Mercado-Revilla, Bryan
Revilla, Ramon Jolo Revilla, Aniela Tolentino, Raymond Mendoza, Christopherson
Yap, Tsuyoshi Horibata, Miguel Luis Villafuerte, Vincenzo Villafuerte, and Terry Ridon.
This bill adopts several provisions from the aforementioned bills and was developed
in consultation with OCEANA Philippines.

       To shield coastal communities, protect habitats, and set the stage towards
rebuilding our fisheries, early approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

28               atmosphere, and which is in addition to natural climate variability
29               observed over comparable time periods;
30
31            c. Coastal Greenbelt Zone refers to at least a 100-meter-wide strip of
32               natural or planted coastal vegetation, primarily of mangrove and beach
33               forest species, extending from the seaward edge of mangroves (middle
34               intertidal zone) towards land, or extending from the seaward edge of
35               beach forest (high tide line) towards land, in cases where mangroves are
36               absent. Its function is to absorb wave energy from the sea towards land,
37               prevent coastal erosion, and mitigate the adverse impacts of natural
38               coastal hazards on human lives and property;

44               kilometer from the shoreline at high tide to include mangrove swamps,

16          SEC. 5. Designated Areas for Coastal Greenbelt Zone. – Areas designated for
17   Coastal Greenbelt Zones shall cover natural, rural, and urban areas with mangrove or
18   mangrove areas, AUU fishponds turned over to the DENR, site-specific stretches of
19   vegetation in areas requiring the reversion to mangroves through natural regeneration
20   or replanting with locally appropriate species, and beach forests, subject to prior
21   vested rights. All applicable biophysical, socioeconomic, and geological assessments
22   shall be conducted prior to the designation of coastal greenbelts. Upon determination
23   by the DENR of a particular area as a designated area for coastal greenbelt under
24   Sec. 4(c) of this Act, CLUPs and zoning ordinances shall be updated accordingly. AUU
25   fishponds turned over to the DENR shall be prioritized in the designation of coastal
26   greenbelts, and their corresponding reversion to the category of public lands as
27   mangrove reforestation sites shall be completed within the year of identification as
28   such, following the guidelines endorsed by the National Technical Working Group
29   pursuant to Section 9 below.

31         SEC. 14. Prohibited Acts. – The following shall constitute prohibited acts
32   punishable under this Act, without prejudice to accrued liability for acts and omissions
33   punishable under existing laws:
34
35             a) Any person who unjustly obstructs the LGU, DENR, or DA through
36                BFAR, or PCSD in the Province of Palawan, in the removal of illegal
37                structures in the process of reversion of AUU fishponds, shall be liable
38                to the agency for an administrative fine of Fifty thousand pesos
39                (P50,000.00) per day that the agency is prevented from performing its
40                function: Provided, that UDHA, when applicable, shall be adhered to and
41                shall take precedence over this Act in the removal of illegal structures:
42                Provided, further, that the acts to obstruct the removal of illegal
43                structures in accordance with UDHA shall be exempt from liability.

31          SEC. 16. Joint Congressional Oversight Committee. – There is hereby created
32   a Joint Congressional Oversight Committee (JCOC) to monitor the implementation of
33   this Act and to review the implementing rules and regulations promulgated by the NCC
34   for a period not exceeding five (5) years from the effectivity of this Act. The Committee
35   shall be composed of five (5) Senators and five (5) Representatives, with at least one
36   (1) member of the Minority from both Houses, to be appointed by the Senate President
37   and the Speaker of the House of Representatives, respectively. The Oversight
38   Committee shall be co-chaired by the Chairpersons of the Senate Committee on
39   Environment, Natural Resources, and Climate Change and the House of
40   Representatives Committee on Climate Change. The secretariat of the JCOC shall be
41   drawn from existing personnel of the Senate Committee on Environment, Natural
42   Resources, and Climate Change, and the House of Representatives Committee on
43   Climate Change.

 6          SEC. 18. Appropriations and Other Fund Sources. – The amount necessary for
 7   the initial implementation of the Act shall be charged against the current year's
 8   appropriations of the departments or agencies concerned. Thereafter, such sums as
 9   may be necessary for the continuing implementation of this Act shall be sourced from
10   and included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
11
12         The LGUs concerned may provide the necessary funds for the purpose, in their
13   respective annual budgets.
14
15            The NCC, the LGUs and their LTACs, and the national government agencies
16   may source local and international grants, bequests, donations, and other sustainable
17   financing mechanisms in support of the implementation of this Act. LGUs to be
18   prioritized in the allocation of these funds shall include sixth- up to third-class
19   municipalities and LGUs with existing LCGMAPs and programs and activities in
20   accordance with this Act.
21
22          The Land Bank of the Philippines (LBP), the Development Bank of the
23   Philippines (DBP), the People's Credit and Finance Corporation (PCFC), and other
24   relevant government financial institutions shall formulate and identify loan and
25   financing mechanisms that shall be made available to support local coastal greenbelt
26   management programs, including alternative livelihood projects for small-scale
27   fisherfolk and cooperatives.
28
29          SEC. 19. Tax Exemption. – All grants, bequests, endowments, donations, and
30   contributions made to the NCC, the local development councils, the LGUs, the DA, the
31   DENR, and the PCSD to be used actually, directly, and exclusively for the
32   implementation of their mandates under this Act shall be exempt from donor’s tax, and
33   the same shall be considered as allowable deductions from gross income for purposes
34   of computing the taxable income of the donor in accordance with the provisions of the
35   National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.
36
37          SEC. 20. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
38   or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
39   and effect.
40
41         SEC. 21. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
42   presidential proclamations, letters of instruction, rules and regulations, or parts thereof
43   inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or amended
44   accordingly.
