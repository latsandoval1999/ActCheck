                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Underage drinking puts children and communities at risk. Alcohol use at a young
age is linked to health problems, poor school performance, violence, accidents, and
a higher chance of addiction later in life. These harms do not affect the child alone,
they strain families, schools, hospitals, and law enforcement, and lead to injuries
and tragedies that could have been prevented.

Although there are existing laws and local rules, many minors can still buy or
receive alcohol from stores, bars, parties, or even through online delivery. This bill
responds to that gap by creating a clear national policy that focuses responsibility
where it belongs: on adults and businesses that sell, serve, or provide alcohol to
minors. At the same time, it follows child-protection laws by making sure minors
are not treated as criminals, but are guided toward counseling, education, and
family or community support.

The Anti-Underage Drinking Act protects children by stopping easy access to
alcohol and holding violators accountable, while offering help, not punishment, to
minors involved. By combining stronger enforcement, age checks, online
safeguards, and prevention education, the bill promotes safer communities and
healthier youth. It sends a clear message: protecting children comes irst, and
preventing underage drinking is a shared responsibility of families, businesses,
and government.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the
undersigned respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                             AN ACT
PREVENTING AND CONTROLLING UNDERAGE ACCESS TO ALCOHOLIC
BEVERAGES, PROHIBITING THE SALE, SERVICE, AND FURNISHING THEREOF
TO PERSONS BELOW EIGHTEEN (18) YEARS OF AGE, PROVIDING
INTERVENTION MEASURES FOR MINORS, AND PENALIZING VIOLATIONS BY
INDIVIDUALS AND ESTABLISHMENTS

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Anti-Underage Drinking
Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect the
physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being of the youth. The State
shall prevent and control the access of persons below eighteen (18) years of age
to alcoholic beverages, hold accountable the adults and establishments that sell,
serve, furnish, or allow access to alcohol by minors, and ensure appropriate
intervention, counseling, and support measures for minors and their parents or
guardians.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms
are de ined as:

   (a) “Child or Minor ” refers to any person below eighteen (18) years of age;

   (b) “Alcohol” refers to ethyl alcohol or ethanol and all dilutions, mixtures, and
       preparations thereof, and includes spirits and distilled liquors such as
       whisky, brandy, rum, gin, tequila, vodka, and similar products; as well as

                                                                           2|Page
      fermented liquors and traditional alcoholic drinks including, but not
      limited to, tuba, basi, tapuy, and lambanog.

   (c) “Alcohol by volume (ABV)” refers to the percentage of pure alcohol
       contained in a beverage, expressed as alcohol content per one hundred
       milliliters (100 mL) of the beverage.;

   (d) “Alcoholic beverages” refer to beverages with an alcoholic content
       exceeding one and two-tenths percent (1.2%) ABV;

   (e) “Establishment” refers to any place or premises, whether ixed or mobile,
       permanent or temporary, that is licensed, authorized, or otherwise engaged
       in the sale, service, distribution, dispensing, or provision of alcoholic
       beverages, including bars, restaurants, hotels, resorts, convenience stores,
       groceries, supermarkets, sari-sari stores, event venues, and similar
       locations.

   (f) “Furnish” refers to sell, serve, give, deliver, provide, make available, or
       otherwise supply alcoholic beverages, whether for a price or for free.

SECTION 4. Prohibited Acts. — The following acts are prohibited:

   (a) Selling, serving, furnishing, delivering, or otherwise providing alcoholic
       beverages to a child or minor;

   (b) Purchasing, procuring, or ordering alcoholic beverages on behalf of, or for
       the purpose of furnishing to, a child or minor;

   (c) Allowing, permitting, or tolerating the possession or consumption of
       alcoholic beverages by a child or minor within the premises of an
       establishment;

   (d) Requiring, inducing, or coercing a child or minor to buy, procure, deliver,
       sell, serve, or otherwise handle alcoholic beverages;

   (e) Any parent, legal guardian, or person exercising parental authority who
       knowingly and deliberately furnishes alcoholic beverages to a child or
       minor, or knowingly and deliberately allows a child or minor to consume
       alcoholic beverages, except where the acts constitute neglect, abuse, or
       exploitation punishable under other laws, in which case such other laws
       shall apply.

SECTION 5. Age Veri ication and Compliance Duties of Establishments. —

                                                                       3|Page
(a) Age Veri ication. — Establishments and their owners, managers, employees,
    or agents shall require valid proof of age prior to selling or serving alcoholic
    beverages where the buyer or recipient appears to be below twenty-one (21)
    years of age, or such other reasonable threshold as may be set in the IRR, to
    ensure compliance.

(b) Mandatory Signage. — All establishments selling or serving alcoholic
    beverages shall prominently display notices stating that sale, service, or
    furnishing of alcoholic beverages to persons below eighteen (18) years of age
    is prohibited. The speci ications for size and placement shall be provided in
    the IRR.

(c) Self-Service Controls. — Establishments operating on a self-service basis shall
    ensure that alcoholic beverages are physically separated from non-alcoholic
    beverages and subject to staff-controlled release.

(d) Online Sales and Delivery. — Entities selling or delivering alcoholic beverages
    through e-commerce platforms or delivery services shall implement age-
    gating measures, including age declaration at point of sale and age veri ication
    upon delivery.

SECTION 6. Intervention Measures for Minors. — A child or minor found in
possession of or consuming alcoholic beverages shall not be subjected to criminal
prosecution under this Act. Instead:

   (a) The Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), in
       coordination with the Department of Health (DOH) and the local
       government unit (LGU), shall implement appropriate intervention
       programs, including counseling of the child or minor and the child’s parent
       or guardian, and referral to community-based services where needed.

   (b) The child or minor may be required to undergo community-based
       education or service activities designed for prevention and rehabilitation,
       subject to safeguards under applicable child welfare and juvenile justice
       policies.



       The foregoing is without prejudice to the iling of appropriate cases against
       liable adults or establishments under this Act or other applicable laws.

SECTION 7. Penalties for Adults and Establishments. —


                                                                        4|Page
(a) Any person who violates Section 4(a) or 4(b) of this Act shall be punished
    by imprisonment of not less than one (1) month and one (1) day but not
    more than six (6) months, or a ine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos
    (P50,000.00) but not more than Two hundred thousand pesos
    (P200,000.00), or both, at the discretion of the court.

(b) Any owner, president, manager, or person in charge of an establishment
    who violates Section 4(c) of this Act, or who permits or tolerates such
    violation in the establishment, shall be punished by imprisonment of not
    less than one (1) month and one (1) day but not more than six (6) months,
    or a ine of not less than One hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00) but
    not more than Three hundred thousand pesos (P300,000.00), or both, at
    the discretion of the court.

(c) Administrative Sanctions on Establishments. — In addition to criminal
    liability, the appropriate LGU licensing of ice or regulatory authority may,
    after due notice and hearing, impose the following administrative penalties
    for violations of this Act:

   (1) First offense — ine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos (P50,000.00)
       but not more than One hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00), and
       suspension of the license/permit to sell or serve alcohol for one (1)
       month;

   (2) Second offense — ine of not less than One hundred thousand pesos
       (P100,000.00) but not more than Two hundred thousand pesos
       (P200,000.00), and suspension for three (3) months;

   (3) Third offense — ine of not less than Two hundred thousand pesos
       (P200,000.00) but not more than Three hundred thousand pesos
       (P300,000.00), and suspension for six (6) months; and

   (4) Fourth and succeeding offenses — revocation of the license/permit to
       sell or serve alcohol and, where warranted, revocation of the
       establishment’s permit to operate.



(d) If the violation is committed by a business entity, the owner, president,
    manager, or most senior of icer, as applicable, shall be held liable,
    consistent with existing penalty structures for sales to minors. (See
    analogous liability pattern in tobacco regulation.)

SECTION 8. Advertising, Labeling, and Consumer Information. —

   (a) Labeling. — The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination
       with the DOH, shall ensure that packaged alcoholic beverages prominently
       display ABV and such health and safety warnings as may be prescribed in
       the IRR.

   (b) Warnings in Promotions. — Alcohol advertising and promotions shall carry
       legible warnings, in Filipino and English, regarding health risks and the
       prohibition against sale or furnishing to persons below eighteen (18) years
       of age, as may be prescribed in the IRR.

SECTION 9. Information and Education. —

   (a) The DOH, DepEd, and DILG shall conduct sustained public information
       campaigns on the risks associated with alcohol use, with speci ic modules
       for minors, parents, and establishments.

   (b) DepEd shall integrate age-appropriate instruction on alcohol harm
       prevention into relevant learning areas, consistent with curriculum
       standards.

SECTION 10. Inter-Agency Coordination; Enforcement. —

   (a) Primary enforcement at the local level shall be undertaken by LGUs through
       their licensing of ices and local law enforcement units, in coordination with
       the DILG.

   (b) The DSWD shall lead intervention services for minors under Section 6.

   (c) The DOH shall set clinical referral pathways for counseling, screening, and
       treatment for alcohol use problems, especially for minors, in coordination
       with LGUs.

SECTION 11. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary to implement this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOH, DSWD, DepEd,
DILG, and concerned agencies, and thereafter included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, DSWD, DTI, DepEd, DILG, and concerned
agencies shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations. The IRR shall

include standards on age veri ication, signage speci ications, online sale
safeguards, enforcement protocols, and referral mechanisms for minors.

SECTION 13. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, rules and
regulations, and issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 15. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
