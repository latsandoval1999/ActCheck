HOUSE BILL NO. 11548

EXPLANATORY NOTE

This bill seeks to grant unrecognized illegitimate children the right to
have and use a middle name and the surname of either parent.

Article 176 of the Family Code of the Philippines originally provided
that illegitimate children shall use the surname of their mother and be under
her sole parental authority. However, on February 24, 2004, Republic Act No.
9255 was enacted amending the said article to allow the illegitimate children,
upon recognition by the father, to use the father’s surname. As a result, the
recognized illegitimate child may now use the surname of the mother as
middle name and the father’s surname as the last name.

However, illegitimate children whose parentage has not been formally
acknowledged or recognized may be deprived of the opportunity to have and
use a middle name. The absence of a middle name often serves as a visible
marker of illegitimacy, which unfortunately perpetuates social stigma, invites
discrimination, and undermines the inherent dignity of the child.

This proposed measure allows an illegitimate child whose father has
not acknowledged or recognized the child to use the mother’s middle name as
the child’s middle name, followed by the mother’s surname. The mother may
exercise this right by executing a Sworn Affidavit formally manifesting her
choice of naming convention, subject to existing laws, civil registry rules, and
the best interests of the child.

===== [page 2] =====
Conversely, in cases where the mother has not acknowledged or
recognized the child, the father may likewise exercise such right through a
Sworn Affidavit, allowing the child to use the father’s middle name as the
child’s middle name, followed by the father’s surname.

The requirement of a Sworn Affidavit provides a formal mechanism
through which the mother or father may manifest their choice of naming
convention on behalf of the child. This mechanism is intended to provide the
civil registry authorities with a clear documentary basis for the registration or
annotation of the child’s middle name and surname, while ensuring that such
exercise remains subject to existing laws, civil registry rules, and the best
interests of the child.

This approach is analogous in principle to the legal mechanism under
Republic Act No. 9255, wherein an illegitimate father may execute the
appropriate affidavit to allow the child to use his surname. In the same spirit,
the mother should likewise be permitted to formally establish the child’s
middie name and surname through a Sworn Affidavit, particularly in cases
where the father has not acknowledged or recognized the child.

The Supreme Court explained in re: Petition for Change of Name of
Julian Lin Carulasan Wang, G.R. No. 159966, 30 March 2005, middle names
identify maternal lineage and help distinguish individuals who may share the
same given and family names. The absence or restriction of a middle name,
therefore, has direct consequences on a child’s recognized identity, lineage,
and dignity.

This proposed measure ensures that unrecognized illegitimate children
are legally acknowledged and given equal protection under the law. By
allowing the use of the mother’s middle name or the father’s middle name as
the child’s middle name, the proposed measure affirms the child’s right to
identity, familial heritage, and legal recognition, thereby mitigating stigma
and reinforcing the dignity of every child.

More importantly, the measure is pro-poor and promotes equal access
to justice. It provides a practical and accessible means for children to obtain
legal recognition without requiring families, particularly those who lack
financial means, to undergo costly and protracted court proceedings merely to
secure a right that should be available to them by law. By removing this

===== [page 3] =====
financial and procedural barrier, the measure ensures that legal recognition is
not a privilege available only to those who can afford litigation, but a right
accessible to every child regardless of economic status.

In this way, the proposed measure advances not only the child’s right to
identity and family affiliation, but also the State’s fundamental commitment
to equality, social justice, and the protection of the dignity and welfare of
every child.

In view of the foregoing, an immediate apprgval is earnestly sought.

AN ACT
GRANTING UNRECOGNIZED ILLEGITIMATE CHILDREN THE RIGHT
TO HAVE AND USE A MIDDLE NAME AND SURNAME

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Child Identity

and Name Recognition Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote
and protect the dignity and rights of every Filipino child, especially the
unrecognized illegitimate child, including the right to identity, heritage, and legal
status. Toward this end, the State shall implement measures to ensure the full legal
and social recognition of unrecognized illegitimate children by granting them the
right to have and use a middle name.

SEC. 3. Right to Middle Name and Surname. — The right to have and use
a middle name and surname is hereby granted to an unrecognized illegitimate
child. The mother or father may exercise such right by executing a Sworn

Affidavit formally manifesting their choice for the child to use the mother’s or

a. The affidavit of recognition must be accompanied by valid proof of
