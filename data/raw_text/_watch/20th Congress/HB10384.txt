                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Language is the fundamental medium through which we communicate,
interact, and forge a common national identity. The Philippines, being an archipelagic
nation, is home to many distinct languages and dialects spoken throughout the country.
While regions may differ in language, our common Filipino identity and our
commitment to the country remains undivided.

        The 1987 Philippine Constitution recognizes the people's right to information,
particularly on matters of public concern. Language also serves as an important tool
for instruction on public services, for transparency in government actions, and for
accountability in holding institutions answerable to the citizens they serve. However,
this right can only be meaningfully exercised when the information is communicated
in a language the people understand.

       Despite this Constitutional guarantee, numerous government documents
remain inaccessible due to overly technical, formal, or otherwise complicated
language. This makes it difficult for the public to understand and take part in
government processes and public affairs, leaving many unable to access important
information and basic public services.

        This bill seeks to address this communication gap by mandating all
government offices, including Government-Owned and Controlled Corporations
(GOCCs), to employ plain language or appropriate local language translations or
dialect in all communications intended for the public.

       It likewise aims to ensure universal accessibility of government information,
promote transparency in public transactions, and enable the citizenry to make
informed decisions regarding matters of public concern.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                          AN ACT
      PROMOTING ACCESSIBLE GOVERNMENT COMMUNICATION
AND TRANSPARENCY BY REQUIRING THE USE OF PLAIN LANGUAGE
   AND LOCAL LANGUAGE TRANSLATION OR DIALECT IN ALL
  GOVERNMENT ISSUED PUBLIC DOCUMENTS, ADVISORIES, AND
      OTHER SIMILAR DOCUMENTS INTENDED FOR PUBLIC
                     DISSEMINATION
       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:
Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “ Accessible Government
Communications Through Plain Language Act of 2026”

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
uphold the Constitutional mandate recognizing the vital role of communication in
nation building and affirming the fundamental right of the people to information on
matters of public concerns.

The State further recognizes that transparency in governance is essential to public
trust and accountability, and that this transparency can be meaningfully achieved
when government information is communicated in a manner that is clear, accessible,
and comprehensible to the general public.

Section 3. Definition of Terms. - The following terms, for the purposes of this Act,
shall be defined as follows:

    a) “Dialect” refers to a regional variety of language distinguished by features of
       vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation from other regional varieties and
       constituting together with them a single language.

    b) “Government agencies” refers to all government departments, bureaus,
       divisions, attached agencies, and government-owned and controlled
       corporations.

    c) “Language” refers to a system of conventional spoken, manual (signed), or
       written symbols by means of which human beings, as members of a social
       group and participants in its culture, express themselves.


                                           2
    d) Plain Language” also known as “Plain Writing” refers to writing and
       preparations thereof that are clear, concise, well-organized, and follow other
       best practices appropriate to the subject or filed and intended audience or
       constituents.

Section 4. Plain Language in Public Service- All government agencies, local
government units, and government-owned and controlled-corporations (GOCCs) are
hereby mandated to use plain language in Filipino, English, and / or regional
languages or dialects in all their communications and in the implementation of their
basic functions, mandates, and services. For this purposes, the Civil Service
Commission (CSC) is hereby tasked with the duty of monitoring and ensuring the
implementation of this plain language requirements. For GOCCs, the Governance
Commission for GOCCs (GCG) shall be the agency tasked to perform this duty.

Section 5. Scope of Application.- The use of plain language and translation shall be
made, including, but not limited to, the following documents:

    1) Citizen’s Charter
    2) Advisories;
    3) Notices;
    4) Announcements;
    5) Forms and other papers for the application, request, or enjoyment of a public
       service, right, privilege, benefit, license, clearance, permit, etc.;
    6) Documents detailing and explaining government programs, activity, or
       projects;
    7) Any other document that the CSC or the GCG as the case may be, in
       consultation with the agency or office concerned, deems as important to be
       included in this Act’s Coverage.

Section 6. Capacity Building.- The CSC and GCG shall regularly conduct training,
seminars, and other similar capacity-building activities for the offices and agencies
covered by this Act. For plain language in Filipino and translation in Filipino and
other regional dialects, the Komisyon ng Wikang Filipino (KWF) shall be tapped to
facilitate the necessary capacity-building activities.

Section 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations.- Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the CSC and the GCG, in coordination with the KWF, the
Philippine Information Agency, and other relevant government agencies and
stakeholders including civil society organizations and people’s organizations, shall
issue the necessary rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

Section 8. Separability Clause.- If any part or provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in full
force and effect.

Section 9. Repealing Clause.- All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees or
issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules and regulations contrary
to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.
