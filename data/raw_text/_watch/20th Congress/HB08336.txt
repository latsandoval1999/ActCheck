         AN ACT GRANTING MENSTRUAL LEAVE TO FEMALE EMPLOYEES,
         PROVIDING BENEFITS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Women’s labor force participation rate in the Philippines is at 53.4 percent.1
While substantial progress has been achieved in promoting women’s rights in the
workplace through the passage of various laws, women still encounter unique
challenges in the work place.

       One of these challenges is the experience of monthly menstrual pain, or
dysmenorrhea. According to the National Library of Medicine, dysmenorrhea is defined
as pain during the menstrual cycle, usually located in the lower abdomen and may
radiate to the inner thighs and back. It is a common gynecologic problem and can
negatively impact a women’s life. Symptoms may include nausea, bloating, diarrhea,
constipation, vomiting, indigestion, irritability, headache, lower back pain, tiredness,
and dizziness. In approximately 16 to 29 percent of women, dysmenorrhea is
associated with substantial impairment in daily functioning, and up to 12 percent of
monthly school and work activities may be missed due to absenteeism caused by
dysmenorrhea. 2

      In the Philippines, studies reveal that as many as 90 percent of Filipino women
have experienced period pain, and among those who are employed, 89 percent report

             AN ACT GRANTING MENSTRUAL LEAVE TO FEMALE EMPLOYEES,
             PROVIDING BENEFITS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1           SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Menstrual Leave
 2   Act”.
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of P olicy. The State shall protect working women by
 5   providing safe and healthful working conditions, taking into account their maternal
 6   functions, and such facilities and opportunities that will enhance their welfare and
 7   enable them to realize their full potential in the service of the nation. To this end, the
 8   State shall ensure that the unique health needs of women are recognized in the
 9   workplace and that female employees are provided with menstrual leave benefits to
10   safeguard their health, productivity and dignity.
11
12          SEC. 3. Coverage. This Act shall cover all female employees, except pregnant
13   and menopausal women in the public and private sectors, regardless of employment
14   status, who experience menstrual discomfort or health conditions associated with
15   menstruation.
16
17           Employers granting a menstrual leave or its equivalent are not covered by this
18   act.
19
20          SEC. 4. M enstrual Leave Benefits. Female employees shall be entitled to
21   two (2) days of menstrual leave with pay per month, to address menstrual discomfort
22   or health conditions associated with menstruation. Such leave shall be non-cumulative
23   and non-convertible to cash.
 1
 2         Employers shall not require medical certification unless the employee avails of
 3   more than two (2) consecutive days of menstrual leave.
 4
 5         SEC. 5. Non-Dim inution of Benefits. Nothing in this Act shall be construed
 6   to reduce any existing benefits of any form granted under existing laws, decrees,
 7   executive, orders, or any contract, agreement or policy between employer and
 8   employee.
 9
10          SEC. 6. Non-Discrim ination for availm ent of m enstrual leave. No
11   employee shall be subjected to any discrimination, harassment, retaliation, or adverse
12   action for availing of menstrual leave.
13
14         SEC. 7. Security of Tenure. Any female employee who avails of the
15   menstrual leave benefits provided under this Act shall be entitled to security of tenure.
16   The availment of such benefit shall not constitute a just or authorized cause for
17   termination of employment, non-renewal of contract, demotion, reduction of wages
18   or benefits, or any form of discrimination, retaliation, or adverse employment action.
19
20          SEC. 8. P enalty. Any person, corporation, trust, firm, partnership, association
21   or entity found violating this Act or the rules and regulations promulgated thereunder
22   shall be punished by a fine not exceeding Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (PHP
23   200,000.00).
24
25          The proceeds of fines collected under this Act shall be remitted to a special
26   fund dedicated to programs advancing women’s health, promoting workplace equity,
27   and strengthening labor rights education.
28
29          SEC. 9. I m plem enting R ules and R egulations. The Department of Labor
30   and Employment, Civil Service Commission, Department of Health, Philippine
31   Commission on Women in consultation with women’s health advocates and
32   organizations, civil society organizations and other relevant stakeholders shall within
33   thirty (30) days from the effectivity of this Act, issue such rules and regulations
34   necessary for the proper implementation of the provisions of this Act.
35
36          SEC. 10. Separability Clause. - If, for any reason, any part or provision of
37   this Act is held unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining parts or provisions not
38   affected will continue in full force and effect.
39
40         SEC. 11. R epealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
41   proclamations, rules and regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof,
1   inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
2   accordingly.
3
4          SEC. 12. Effectivity. - This Act takes effect fifteen (15) days after its
5   publication in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
