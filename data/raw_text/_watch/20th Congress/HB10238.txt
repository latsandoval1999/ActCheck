                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines stands at a critical juncture in aligning its regulatory framework
with evolving global standards on animal welfare, scientific innovation, and consumer
protection. While Republic Act (“R.A.”) No. 8485 (1998), otherwise known as the Animal
Welfare Act of 1998, as amended, provides a general framework for the humane
treatment of animals, it does not expressly prohibit the use of animals in cosmetic testing,
an area that has increasingly drawn ethical, scientific, and economic scrutiny.

       Cosmetic testing on animals involves subjecting them to procedures that may
cause pain, distress, or lasting harm, often for products that are non-essential to human
health. Advances in science have rendered such practices largely unnecessary. Today,
validated alternative methods, ranging from in vitro testing to computational models,
offer more reliable, human-relevant, and cost-efficient means of ensuring product safety.1
The continued reliance on animal testing for cosmetics is therefore no longer justified on
either scientific or ethical grounds.

      Globally, a decisive shift has taken place. The European Union has implemented
a comprehensive ban on animal testing for cosmetics and the marketing of animal-tested

        In the Philippines, the absence of a clear legal prohibition creates regulatory
ambiguity and disadvantages local manufacturers in accessing international markets that
require cruelty-free certification. Consumers, both domestic and global, are becoming
more conscious of ethical sourcing and production practices. A national policy that
decisively addresses cosmetic animal testing will therefore enhance consumer
confidence, strengthen export competitiveness, and encourage the domestic industry to
transition toward modern, science-based testing approaches.

       This proposed measure seeks to fill the existing legal gap by prohibiting cosmetic
testing on animals within the country, as well as the manufacture, importation, and sale
of cosmetic products that rely on such testing. It likewise provides a clear regulatory
framework for enforcement, allows narrowly defined exceptions when strictly necessary
for public health and safety, and promotes the development and adoption of validated
alternative methods consistent with international standards such as those recognized by
the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD).

       Importantly, the bill adopts a balanced and pragmatic approach by providing a
reasonable transition period for industry compliance, recognizing the need for
adjustment while ensuring that reform is both effective and sustainable. The long-term
benefits, such as reduced testing costs, improved scientific outcomes, enhanced global
market access, and strengthened ethical standards, clearly outweigh the short-term
adjustments required.

      This measure affirms the Philippines’ commitment to humane and responsible
innovation. It aligns national policy with global best practices, supports the

AN ACT PROHIBITING THE USE OF ANIMAL TESTING FOR COSMETIC
    PURPOSES, REGULATING THE MANUFACTURE, IMPORTATION,
    DISTRIBUTION, AND SALE OF COSMETICS IN RELATION THERETO,
    PROMOTING THE USE OF ALTERNATIVE METHODS, AND FOR OTHER
    PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Cruelty-
Free Cosmetics Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness
among them, ensure the humane treatment of animals, and regulate products that may
affect public health and safety, consistent with Section 15, Article II of the 1987
Constitution and Republic Act (“R.A.”) No. 8485 (1998), otherwise known as the “Animal
Welfare Act of 1998,” as amended.

      Towards this end, the State shall prohibit unnecessary animal testing for cosmetic
purposes, regulate the manufacture, importation, distribution, and sale of cosmetic
products in accordance with public health and safety standards, and promote the
development and use of scientifically valid alternative methods that do not involve the
use of animals.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
be defined as follows:

          (a) “Alternative method” refers to a non-animal test method or strategy that is
              scientifically valid and accepted, adopted, or recognized by the Food and
              Drug Administration (“FDA”), including methods reflected in relevant
              OECD guidelines, ASEAN cosmetic standards, or other internationally
              accepted validation standards.

          (b) “Animal testing” refers to any in vivo procedure carried out on a living non-
              human vertebrate for the purpose of developing or assessing the safety,
              efficacy, or regulatory compliance of a cosmetic product or cosmetic
              ingredient;

          (c) “Cosmetic” refers to any substance or mixture intended to be placed in
              contact with the external parts of the human body, teeth, or the mucous
              membranes of the oral cavity, with a view exclusively or mainly to cleaning
              them, perfuming them, changing their appearance, correcting body odors,
              protecting them, or keeping them in good condition, consistent with the
              laws, rules, and regulations administered by the FDA;

          (d) “Laboratory animal” refers to any live non-human vertebrate used in
              experimentation or testing;

          (e) “Manufacturer, importer, distributor, or trader” refers to any natural or
              juridical person engaged in the manufacture, importation, distribution,
              supply, sale, offer for sale, or placing on the market of cosmetic products in
              the Philippines.

      SEC. 4. Prohibition on Animal Testing for Cosmetics. – The following acts are
hereby prohibited:

   (a) Conducting or causing the conduct of animal testing within the Philippines for
       cosmetic purposes;
   (b) Manufacturing, importing, distributing, selling, offering for sale, or otherwise
       placing on the Philippine market any cosmetic product where the responsible
       person knowingly relied on animal testing conducted after the effectivity of this
       Act for cosmetic purposes, where an accepted alternative method was available at
       the time such testing was conducted;

   (c) Commissioning, authorizing, or knowingly causing a third party to conduct
       animal testing for cosmetic products or cosmetic ingredients intended to be placed
       on the Philippine market, except as provided under this Act.

       Animal testing conducted for non-cosmetic purposes pursuant to other applicable
laws shall not fall within the prohibition under this Act, provided that such testing is not
undertaken to circumvent the provisions herein.

       SEC. 5. Exceptions. – The FDA, in coordination with the Bureau of Animal
Industry and subject to R.A. 8485, as amended, may allow, on a case-to-case basis, the
limited use of animal testing only upon a determination, supported by substantial
evidence, that:

   (a) A specific cosmetic product or ingredient poses a demonstrated and serious risk
       to human health;

   (b) No scientifically valid and accepted alternative method is available to address the
       identified safety concern; and

   (c) The use of animal testing is strictly necessary and proportionate to address such
       risk.

      Any authorization under this section shall be prior, written, narrowly tailored,
time-bound, and subject to strict documentation and regulatory oversight.

      SEC. 6. Regulatory Authority and Coordination. – The FDA shall be the primary
agency responsible for the implementation and enforcement of this Act with respect to
cosmetic products and establishments.

       The Bureau of Animal Industry and the Committee on Animal Welfare shall assist
in matters relating to animal use, humane treatment, and laboratory oversight.
       The FDA shall integrate compliance with this Act into existing regulatory systems,
including licensing, product notification, inspection, and post-market surveillance
mechanisms.

       SEC. 7. Proof of Compliance. – Manufacturers, importers, distributors, and traders
shall maintain records sufficient to demonstrate compliance with this Act and shall
submit such documentation as may be required by the FDA, including sworn
declarations, technical dossiers, and certifications relating to the use or non-use of animal
testing.

       SEC. 8. Promotion of Alternative Testing Methods. – The State shall promote the
development, validation, and adoption of alternative testing methods by supporting
research and innovation, recognizing internationally accepted validation standards, and
encouraging collaboration among government agencies, academic institutions, and the
private sector.

       SEC. 9. Penalties. – Any person who violates any provision of this Act or its
implementing rules and regulations shall, upon conviction, be subject to the following
penalties:

   (a) First offense – A fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) but not
       more than Two Hundred Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱250,000.00);
   (b) Second offense – A fine of not less than Two Hundred Fifty Thousand Pesos
       (₱250,000.00) but not more than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00), and
       suspension of the appropriate license, permit, or authorization issued by the FDA;
   (c) Third and subsequent offenses – A fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand
       Pesos (₱500,000.00) but not more than One Million Pesos (₱1,000,000.00),
       cancellation or revocation of the appropriate license, permit, or authorization, and
       imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but not more than two (2) years, at
       the discretion of the court.

       In addition to the foregoing penalties, the FDA may impose appropriate
administrative sanctions, including product recall, seizure, market withdrawal, and
blacklisting of non-compliant entities.

      If the offender is a juridical person, the directors, officers, or responsible
employees who ordered, authorized, knowingly permitted, or through gross negligence
allowed the commission of the violation shall be held liable, without prejudice to the civil
and administrative liability of the juridical entity.
      .
       SEC. 10. Transition Period. – All covered persons shall be given a period of two
(2) years from the effectivity of this Act within which to comply with its provisions.
During such periods, the FDA shall issue the necessary guidelines, compliance
mechanisms, and transitional rules.

       SEC. 11. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
charged against the current appropriations of the FDA under the Department of Health
and the Bureau of Animal Industry under the Department of Agriculture for the initial
year of implementation. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       In allocating funds, priority shall be given to strengthening regulatory
enforcement, inspection and monitoring systems, development and validation of
alternative testing methods, capacity-building of regulatory personnel, and the
establishment of compliance verification mechanisms.

        SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the FDA, in coordination with relevant agencies, shall promulgate
the necessary implementing rules and regulations.

      SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder shall not be affected thereby.

       SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
