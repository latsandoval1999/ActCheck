                            AN ACT
   AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11036, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
  “MENTAL HEALTH ACT,” BY STRENGTHENING WORKPLACE MENTAL
 HEALTH PROMOTION AND PROTECTION, PROVIDING FOR WORKPLACE
      MENTAL HEALTH PROGRAMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                            EXPLANATORY NOTE
      Republic Act No. 11036, or the “Mental Health Act,” affirms the State’s
commitment to promote and protect the mental health of all Filipinos. However,
the law requires further strengthening to ensure that mental health promotion
and protection are effectively institutionalized in workplace settings, where
individuals spend a significant portion of their time and are exposed to various
psychosocial risks.

      This measure seeks to amend the law by clearly defining key concepts
necessary for workplace implementation, including “Workplace Mental Health
Program” and “Mental Health Focal Person,” to ensure accountability and proper
coordination within organizations.

      The bill introduces explicit obligations on employers, both in the public
and private sectors, to adopt and implement workplace mental health policies
and programs. These programs are required to include mental health promotion,
stigma reduction, access to referral mechanisms, confidentiality safeguards,
non-discrimination measures, early identification and intervention systems,
reasonable accommodations, and capacity-building for supervisors and
designated personnel. It also mandates the designation of a focal person to
oversee implementation and compliance.
      To protect the rights of workers, the measure establishes clear standards
on confidentiality and data protection, ensuring that any information related to
an employee’s mental health condition or treatment is handled with strict privacy
safeguards and in accordance with existing laws, including the Data Privacy Act
of 2012.

      The bill further provides for fair and supportive return-to-work and
rehabilitation procedures for employees recovering from mental health
conditions, including reasonable accommodations and reintegration measures,
while prohibiting discrimination on the basis of mental health history.

     Finally, the measure delineates the roles of key government agencies. The
Department of Health is tasked to lead in policy formulation and technical
guidance, while the Department of Labor and Employment and the Civil Service
Commission are mandated to ensure compliance in their respective jurisdictions.
Other relevant agencies are likewise encouraged to integrate mental health
promotion initiatives within their systems.

      By establishing clear standards, responsibilities, and safeguards, this
measure aims to create healthier, more supportive, and rights-based work
environments across all sectors.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
   AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11036, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
  “MENTAL HEALTH ACT,” BY STRENGTHENING WORKPLACE MENTAL
 HEALTH PROMOTION AND PROTECTION, PROVIDING FOR WORKPLACE
      MENTAL HEALTH PROGRAMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Workplace Mental
Health Amendment Act.”
SECTION 2. Section 4 of Republic Act No. 11036 is hereby amended to include
the following definitions: after the existing definition of “Mental Health
Professional”:
            n. “Workplace Mental Health Program” refers to a set of policies,
            procedures, services, and interventions adopted and implemented
            by an employer to promote mental health and psychological well-
            being, prevent and address psychosocial risks, provide appropriate
            support and referral mechanisms, and protect workers from
            discrimination, harassment, violence, and retaliation on the basis of
            an actual or perceived mental-health condition.
            o. “Mental Health Focal Person” refers to the designated officer or
            employee responsible for coordinating workplace mental health
            initiatives, referrals, and related compliance requirements under
            this Act.
           The succeeding definitions under Section 4 of Republic Act No.
      11036 shall be correspondingly renumbered.
SECTION 3. A new section to be designated as Section 25-A is hereby inserted
after Section 25 of Republic Act No. 11036, to read as follows:
            “SEC. 25-A. Employer Obligations on Workplace Mental Health. — All
     employers, whether in the public or private sector, shall adopt and
     implement a workplace mental health policy and program appropriate to
     the size, nature, and capacity of the organization.
           Such policy and program shall include, at a minimum:
           a. A written mental health policy that is disseminated to all
           employees and reviewed periodically;
           b. Measures to promote mental health awareness, reduce stigma,
           and encourage help-seeking behavior;
           c. A referral mechanism to licensed mental health professionals,
           facilities, or service providers;
           d. Confidential procedures for the handling of employee mental
           health information;
           e. A non-discrimination and anti-retaliation policy;
           f. A mechanism for early identification and intervention in cases of
           mental health distress;
           g. Procedures for reasonable workplace accommodations and
           return-to-work support, as appropriate;
           h. Training of supervisors and designated personnel on mental
           health literacy, psychological first aid, referral protocols, and lawful
           accommodation practices;
           i. A designated mental health focal person or equivalent point of
           contact within the organization.
                 The Department of Health, in coordination with the
           Department of Labor and Employment, Civil Service Commission,
           and such other agencies as may be necessary, shall issue the
           implementing guidelines for the minimum standards under this
           section.”
SECTION 4. A new section, to be designated as Section 25-B, is hereby inserted
after Section 25-A of Republic Act No. 11036, to read as follows:
                “SEC. 25-B. Confidentiality and Data Protection in the
           Workplace. — Any information relating to the mental health
           condition, treatment, counseling, or referral of an employee shall be
           treated as confidential.
                 No such information shall be disclosed, accessed, or used
           except:
           a. With the written consent of the employee concerned;
           b. When disclosure is required by law;
           c. When necessary to prevent an imminent and serious harm,
           subject to applicable legal and ethical standards; or
           d. As otherwise allowed under existing privacy and data protection
           laws and regulations.
                 Employers shall ensure that workplace records, referral
           documents, and related communications are protected from
           unauthorized disclosure and are managed in accordance with the
           Data Privacy Act of 2012 and other applicable laws.”
SECTION 5. A new section, to be designated as Section 25-C, is hereby inserted
after Section 25-B of Republic Act No. 11036, to read as follows:
                  “SEC. 25-C. Return-to-Work and Rehabilitation Support. —
           Employers shall adopt fair and supportive return-to-work
           procedures for employees recovering from mental health conditions
           or related leave.
                  Such procedures may include phased reintegration,
           temporary workload adjustments, check-ins with designated
           personnel, and other reasonable accommodations, provided that
           these shall not be construed as reducing the employee’s statutory
           rights or benefits.
                No employee shall be denied employment, demoted,
           suspended, discharged, or otherwise discriminated against solely on
           account of the employee’s history of mental health treatment, except
           as may be permitted under existing law and due process.”
SECTION 6. A new section, to be designated as Section 25-D, is hereby inserted
after Section 25-C of Republic Act No. 11036, to read as follows:
                 “SEC. 25-D. Role of Government Agencies. — The Department
           of Health shall lead the formulation of policies, standards, and
           technical guidance on workplace mental health.
                   The Department of Labor and Employment shall ensure
            compliance with workplace mental health obligations in the private
            sector, and the Civil Service Commission shall issue corresponding
            rules for government personnel.
                  The Department of Education, Commission on Higher
            Education, and Technical Education and Skills Development
            Authority may likewise integrate mental health promotion and
            awareness initiatives in their respective systems, consistent with
            this Act.”
SECTION 7. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations
of the concerned agencies, and thereafter such sums as may be necessary for its
continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.
SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health, in coordination
with the Department of Labor and Employment, Civil Service Commission,
National Privacy Commission, and other relevant agencies, shall promulgate the
necessary implementing rules and regulations.
SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder thereof not affected shall remain in
full force and effect.
SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, rules, and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
SECTION 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
