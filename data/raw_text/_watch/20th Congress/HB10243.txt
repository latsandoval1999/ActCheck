                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State affirms its commitment to ensuring equitable access to quality healthcare
services for all Filipinos. However, there remains a significant shortage of rehabilitation
and allied health professionals, particularly occupational therapists, physical therapists,
and speech-language therapists, across the country, especially in underserved and rural
areas. This gap limits access to essential services for persons with disabilities, children
with developmental needs, the elderly, and patients recovering from illness or injury.

       The demand for rehabilitation services continues to grow due to population aging,
rising incidence of non-communicable diseases, and increasing awareness of
developmental and speech-related conditions. Yet, the supply of qualified therapists
remains insufficient and unevenly distributed, with most professionals concentrated in
urban centers. As a result, many public hospitals, schools, and community-based facilities
lack the necessary personnel to deliver these critical services.

       This proposed measure seeks to address this gap by establishing a Scholarship and
Return Service Program for students pursuing degrees in Occupational Therapy, Physical
Therapy, and Speech-Language Pathology. The program provides comprehensive
financial support to qualified students and, in return, requires them to render service in
public institutions and priority areas after licensure.

        By linking education support with mandatory return service, the measure ensures
that public investment in human capital directly translates into improved access to
healthcare services. It also promotes a more equitable distribution of professionals by
prioritizing deployment in underserved communities, where the need is greatest.
Furthermore, the bill supports the long-term development of the country’s healthcare
workforce by encouraging more students to pursue careers in rehabilitation and allied
health. It reduces financial barriers to education while providing a clear pathway to
employment and public service.
       This measure strengthens the healthcare system by addressing both the supply
and distribution of essential health professionals. It ensures that Filipinos, regardless of
location or socio-economic status, have access to rehabilitation services necessary for
improved quality of life.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

       AN ACT ESTABLISHING A SCHOLARSHIP AND RETURN SERVICE
       PROGRAM FOR STUDENTS PURSUING A BACHELOR’S DEGREE IN
       OCCUPATIONAL THERAPY, PHYSICAL THERAPY, AND SPEECH AND
       LANGUAGE THERAPY, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Therapists Para sa
Bayan Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote the right to health by ensuring the availability, accessibility, and equitable
distribution of qualified rehabilitation and allied health professionals throughout the
country, particularly in underserved and disadvantaged areas.

        Towards this end, the State shall strengthen the development of the healthcare
workforce by providing accessible educational opportunities and establishing a
Scholarship and Return Service Program for students pursuing degrees in Occupational
Therapy, Physical Therapy, and Speech-Language Pathology. In return, beneficiaries
shall render service in public health institutions, schools, and community-based facilities
to address critical gaps in rehabilitation and allied health services.


       SEC. 3. Scholarship and Return Service Program. — There is hereby established a
Scholarship and Return Service Program (SRSP) for deserving students who intend to
pursue a Bachelor of Science in Occupational Therapy, Bachelor of Science in Physical
Therapy, or Bachelor of Science in Speech-Language Pathology in state universities and
colleges (SUCs) or in partner private higher education institutions (HEIs) in regions
where no SUC offers the said programs. The SRSP shall provide the following benefits:
           a) Free tuition and other school fees, including but not limited to: registration,
              library, and laboratory fees;

           b) Allowance for prescribed books, uniforms, and medical supplies;

           c) Transportation and living allowance, subject to guidelines to be issued;
              and,

           d) Allowances for licensure review and board examination fees.

        SEC. 4. Qualifications and Requirements. — To qualify for the SRSP, an applicant
must:

           a) Be a Filipino citizen residing in the Philippines;

           b) Be a graduate of a senior high school program or equivalent, or an
              undergraduate in good standing pursuing BS in Occupational Therapy,
              Physical Therapy, or Speech-Language Pathology;

           c) Have passed the entrance examination and complied with the admission
              requirements of the SUC or private HEI; and,

           d) Not be a current holder of any similar scholarship or government study
              grant.

        SEC. 5. Conditions of the Scholarship. — Qualified students under the SRSP shall:

   a) Sign a scholarship agreement with the Commission on Higher Education (CHED),
      which shall administer the program;

   b) Carry the full academic load prescribed per semester by the curriculum of the SUC
      or private HEI;

   c) Complete the Occupational Therapy, Physical Therapy, or Speech-Language
      Pathology degree within the prescribed period, unless granted a valid leave of
      absence for justifiable reasons;

   d) Take the licensure examination for Occupational Therapy, Physical Therapy, or
      Speech-Language Pathology within one (1) year of graduation;

   e) Render return service under Section 7 of this Act.
       SEC. 6. Disqualification and Repayment. — A scholar shall refund the full cost of
the scholarship and all related benefits if:

   a) The scholar fails to complete the course within the prescribed period without valid
      cause;

   b) The scholar commits acts of misconduct or violations of the agreement as
      determined by the administering agency or the HEI;

   c) The scholar fails or refuses to comply with the mandatory return service.

        SEC. 7. Mandatory Return Service. — Within one (1) year from obtaining a
professional license, the scholar shall render at least two (2) years of return service in any
of the following:

   a) Government hospitals, rural health units, public rehabilitation centers, or facilities
      operated by the Department of Health (DOH), Department of Social Welfare and
      Development (DSWD), Deрartment of Education (DepEd), or local government
      units;

   b) Special education or geriatric institutions where Occupational Therapy, Physical
      Therapy, or Speech-Language Therapy services are needed;

   c) Other health facilities or community-based programs identified by CHED and
      DOH as service-priority areas.

       Scholars may be appointed to such positions with corresponding salaries and
benefits during their return service. Those who are not appointed within one (1) year
may either:

   a) Reimburse the full cost of the scholarship; or

   b) Undertake an alternative compliance program to be formulated by CHED in
      consultation with relevant agencies.

       To this end the DOH, DSWD, DepEd, and LGUs shall prioritize the hiring of
Occupational Therapists, Physical Therapists, and Speech-Language Therapists with an
active Return Service Agreement over similarly qualified candidates.

        SEC. 8. Allocation and Target Areas. – CHED, shall determine the number of
scholars and geographic allocation based on national and regional demand for
Occupational Therapists, Physical Therapists, and Speech-Language Therapists
prioritizing underserved and disadvantaged areas.
       SEC. 9. Monitoring and Compliance. – CHED shall monitor scholars' academic
progress and return service compliance in coordination with SUCs, HEIs, DOH, and
other concerned agencies. Any violation shall be subject to sanctions under Section 6.

       SEC. 10. Sanctions for Non-Compliance. – Any licensed therapist who received
benefits under the SRSP but fails or refuses to render the mandatory return service shall
be required to pay twice the amount of scholarship and benefits received, unless justified
by force majeure or other valid grounds beyond the scholar’s control.

      SEC. 11. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act under the budget of
CHED.

        SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, CHED, in consultation with DOH and PRC, shall issue the
implementing rules and regulations.

        SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

      SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances, decrees, rules,
and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby
amended, modified, or repealed accordingly.

      SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
