        This bill therefore establishes a Disease Surveillance and Epidemic Response

 Bureau. It provides for the modernization of surveillance technology, laboratories,
 regional and local capabilities, personnel, research, public information, and
 preparedness mechanisms. It likewise requires existing resources to be inventoried
 and serviceable systems to be harmonized before new acquisitions are made, thereby
 strengthening Republic Act No. 11332 without creating duplicative agencies,

        A modern, coordinated, and adequately supported surveillance system is

 public health threats. By strengthening existing institutions and providing a
 continuing framework for modernization, this measure will enable the government
 to respond more promptly and effectively while protecting public health and national
 security.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the D               o         o

      Section 2. Declaration of Policy.        Pursuant to Section 15, Article II of the
1987 Constitution, which provides that the state shall protect and promote the right

with Republic Act No. 11332, otherwise known as Mandatory Reporting of
Notifiable Diseases and Health Events of Public Health Concern Act, which
recognizes the disease surveillance and response systems of the Department of
Health and its local counterparts as the first line of defense to epidemics and health
events of public health concern that pose risk to public health and security, the State
shall modernize the existing disease surveillance and epidemic response system.


      Toward this end, the State shall strengthen its technology, personnel,
laboratories, coordination, and response capabilities. Such modernization shall
improve the systems established under Republic Act No. 11332 and shall not create
duplicative agencies, databases, or reporting requirements.


      Section 3. Disease Surveillance and Epidemic Response Modernization
Program.     The Department of Health, through the Epidemiology Bureau, shall
establish and implement a Disease Surveillance and Epidemic Response
Modernization Program, strengthening and integrating Republic Act No. 11332.


      Section 4. Objectives.     The Disease Surveillance and Epidemic Response
Modernization Program shall have the following objectives:


          a. To upgrade physical resources and operational techniques through
             acquisition and development of state-of-the-art instruments, equipment,
             facilities, and systems to enhance monitoring of disease, epidemic
             response, and sharing of disease-surveillance and epidemic response
             information and analysis;
          b. To strengthen research on emerging and re-emerging diseases and
             epidemic response, and promote international cooperation and
             information sharing on public health threats.
        c. To integrate human, animal, environmental, laboratory, community,
           genomic, and border-health surveillance;
        d. To strengthen the Epidemiology Bureau and Epidemiology and
           Surveillance Units, particularly in remote and low-capacity areas; and
        e. To develop and retain trained disease-surveillance and epidemic-
           response personnel.
        f. To enhance public information, education and advocacy on hygiene,
           disease prevention, disease monitoring, and epidemic response.
        g. To provide a more competitive base pay and other benefits for its
           personnel in order to encourage their dedication to service and
           fulfillment of their duties.


     Section 5. Components of Modernization.        The Disease Surveillance and
Epidemic Response Modernization Program shall include:

        a. Surveillance Technology and Systems.            This shall entail the
           acquisition or upgrading of modern instruments, equipment, facilities,
           and interoperable electronic reporting systems, including real-time and
           offline-capable reporting where technically feasible. Existing resources
           shall first be inventoried, with obsolete resources phased out and
           serviceable resources harmonized and integrated, where practicable,
           before new acquisitions are made.
        b. Integrated Surveillance.        This shall entail the progressive
           harmonization and linkage of existing human, animal, environmental,
           laboratory, community, genomic, and border-health surveillance
            systems through common data standards and secure data-sharing
            arrangements;
         c. Regional and Local Capabilities.        This shall entail strengthening the
            Epidemiology Bureau and Epidemiology and Surveillance Units
            through     appropriate      technical,       equipment,     connectivity,
            communications, transportation, and logistical support, with priority
            given to geographically isolated and low-capacity areas;
         d. Human Resource Development.             This shall entail the recruitment,
            development, and retention of qualified personnel through continuing
            local and international training and competitive compensation and
            benefits, subject to applicable laws;
         e. Research and International Cooperation.                This shall entail
            strengthening research on emerging and re-emerging diseases and
            epidemic response and promoting international cooperation and lawful
            information sharing on public health threats;
         f. Public Information and Education.          This shall entail information,
            education, and advocacy programs on hygiene, disease prevention,
            surveillance, early reporting, and epidemic response; and
         g. Preparedness and Evaluation.       This shall entail regular simulation
            exercises, performance assessments, and after-action reviews to
            identify gaps and improve epidemic preparedness and response.



      Section 6. Formulation and Submission of the Disease Surveillance and
Epidemic Response Modernization Program.            The Department of Health through
the Director of the Epidemiology Bureau and in coordination with other concerned
agencies and local government units shall formulate and oversee the implementation
of the Disease Surveillance and Epidemic Response Modernization Program based
on the policy objectives provided in this Act.


      Section 7. Period of Implementation.       The Modernization Program shall be
implemented for an initial period of three (3) years: Provided, That payments for
multiyear contractual obligations may extend beyond such period: Provided, further,
that the Department of Health shall continuously modernize and update its
capabilities in accordance with scientific developments, applicable World Health
Organization guidance, and international best practices.


      Section 8. Progress Report. The Director of the Epidemiology Bureau shall
prepare an annual report on the implementation of the Modernization Program and
submit the same, through the Secretary of Health, to the President and the
appropriate committees on health of the Senate and the House of Representatives
not later than June 30 of the succeeding year.
      The report shall include the projects undertaken, funds utilized, performance
results, remaining gaps, and corrective measures adopted.


      Section 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations.           Within one hundred
twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health, in
consultation with concerned government agencies, local government units, and
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its
effective implementation.
      Section 10. Appropriations.          The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
Department of Health. Thereafter, such amount as may be necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


      Section 11. Separability Clause.      If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.


      Section 12. Repealing Clause.        All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, presidential proclamations, rules and regulations or parts thereof contrary to
or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


      Section 13. Effectivity.   This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
