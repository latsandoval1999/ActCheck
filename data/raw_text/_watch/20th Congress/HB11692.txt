EXPLANATORY NOTE

This bill seeks to establish a National Smart and Climate-Adaptive Public
Infrastructure Technology Program to promote the development and adoption of
smart, sustainable, low-carbon and climate-adaptive technologies in national and
public infrastructure projects.

Given its geographic location and archipelagic character, the Philippines is
extremely exposed and highly vulnerable to the impacts of climate change and
climate-related hazards. Its communities and public infrastructure face recurring
risks from extreme weather events and changing environmental conditions that
need for infrastructure systems that are resilient, adaptive, and capable of
sustaining essential public services.

At the same time, technological innovation is creating new possibilities for
building infrastructure that is more responsive, efficient, durable, and
environmentally sustainable. Advances in digital systems, renewable energy,
intelligent monitoring, resilient materials, automation, artificial intelligence, and
data-driven management can significantly improve how infrastructure is
designed, delivered, operated, and maintained.

===== [page 2] =====
Article XIV, Section 10 of the 1987 Constitution provides that “Science
and technology are essential for national development and progress. The State
shall give priority to research and development, invention, innovation, and their
utilization; and to science and technology education, training, and services. xxx.”
Therefore, it is both a development imperative and a constitutional duty for the
government to integrate smart and climate-adaptive technologies into all public

infrastructure.

This proposed bill establishes the Smart and Climate-Adaptive Public
Infrastructure Technology Program that provides a national policy and standards
for public infrastructures. The primordial goal of the program is to provide a
national framework for the development, assessment, adoption, deployment,
monitoring, and continuous improvement of smart and climate-adaptive
technologies in public infrastructure and to complement existing national policies
and programs on green infrastructure, nature-based solutions, climate change
adaptation, disaster risk reduction, sustainable development, digital
transformation, and public infrastructure development.

The bill also aims to promote the use of digital technologies, including
digital twin technologies, real-time monitoring and geographic information
systems, predictive analytics, artificial intelligence, internet-of things (IoT)
sensor networks, remote sensing, and climate modeling, in public infrastructure
planning and management.

For the proper implementation of the Program, a National Smart
Infrastructure Technology Task Force is created as the lead agency to coordinate
planning, funding, and implementation across agencies and local government
units.

Through this measure, public infrastructure will not only respond to the
challenges of today but will be designed and equipped to withstand the
environmental, technological, and societal demands of the future. The passage
of this measure will ensure that investment in public infrastructure will result in
assets that are safer and more efficient that will accelerate the country’s transition
to a digital and climate-resilient economy.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

(a) Climate-adaptive public infrastructure refers to public infrastructure
planned, designed, constructed, operated, maintained, or rehabilitated to
anticipate, withstand, absorb, adapt to, and recover from climate-related hazards
and changing environmental conditions while maintaining essential services;

(b) Climate-adaptive public infrastructure technology refers to
engineering, digital, environmental, material, energy, and other technological
solutions that enhance the resilience, efficiency, sustainability, monitoring, or
adaptive capacity of public infrastructure;

(c) Smart energy-integrated infrastructure refers to public infrastructure
incorporating renewable energy generation, building-integrated photovoltaics,
energy storage, energy-efficient systems, energy management platforms, or smart
microgrids; and

(d) Critical public infrastructure refers to facilities and systems the
disruption, failure, or destruction of which would significantly affect public

safety, essential services, economic activity, disaster response, national security,

SEC. 11. Sustainable and Low-Carbon Construction Materials. —
Government agencies undertaking public infrastructure projects shall consider
the use of sustainable and low-carbon construction materials that meet applicable
standards and technical specifications.

Priority shall be given, where technically appropriate, to materials that:

(a) Reduce embodied carbon;

(b) Utilize recycled or renewable inputs;

(c) Improve durability and lifecycle performance;

(d) Reduce dependence on imported materials;

(e) Utilize locally available resources;

(f) Support Philippine manufacturing and innovation; and

(g) Demonstrate measurable environmental and economic benefits.

The use of alternative materials shall not compromise structural safety,
public health, durability, performance, or applicable engineering standards.

SEC. 16. Technology Certification and Qualification. — The appropriate
government agencies shall establish a transparent and science-based system for
the testing, validation, certification, accreditation, and qualification of smart and
climate-adaptive infrastructure technologies, materials, systems, and service
providers.

Certification shall be based on objective technical, environmental, safety,
performance, lifecycle, and economic criteria.

No certification requirement under this Act shall be used to unfairly restrict
competition or favor a particular technology, manufacturer, contractor, or
supplier.

recommended as appropriate under the National Smart Infrastructure Technology
Standards.

SEC. 23. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred
eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Public Works
and Highways shall, in coordination with the Secretaries of DOST, DICT, DENR,
and other concerned agencies, promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for
the effective implementation of this Act.

The implementing rules and regulations shall provide for appropriate
transition periods, technical thresholds, exemptions, technology assessment
procedures, standards, certification mechanisms, financing procedures, and
monitoring requirements.

SEC. 24. Transitory Provision. — Existing public infrastructure projects
that are already under construction or have substantially completed detailed
engineering design upon the effectivity of this Act shall not be required to
undergo redesign solely for purposes of this Act, unless the implementing agency
determines that the incorporation of appropriate technologies is technically
feasible and financially justified.

New projects and projects undergoing major rehabilitation, expansion, or
substantial redesign shall comply with this Act to the extent provided by the
implementing rules and regulations.
