                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines continues to face significant learning challenges. In the
latest available PISA 2022 results, Filipino 15-year-old students ranked 76th
out of 81 countries in reading, 75th in mathematics, and 79th in science.
Only 24 percent reached the minimum reading proficiency level, while only
16 percent reached the minimum mathematics proficiency level and 23
percent reached the minimum science proficiency level.1

       The World Bank’s 2024 Learning Poverty Brief likewise reported that 91
percent of Filipino children of late-primary age were not proficient in reading,
adjusted for those out of school. Approximately 90 percent of students also
failed to achieve minimum reading proficiency by the end of primary school.

      These results underscore the need to strengthen the capacity and well-
being of public school teachers, who play a central role in improving student
learning outcomes. While reforms on compensation, training, and
professional development are important, ensuring that teachers have reliable
access to quality health care is equally necessary.

      Although the Universal Health Care Act has expanded health-insurance
coverage, gaps in access and implementation remain. This bill therefore seeks
to provide public school teachers with Healthcare Maintenance Organization

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Health Care for
Public School Teachers Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policies. – The State shall ensure our citizens’
access to quality and affordable health care. In particular, it shall provide
public school teachers with access to comprehensive, quality, cost-effective,
preventive, and rehabilitative health services.

SECTION 3. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all public school teachers under
the Department of Education.

SECTION 4. Healthcare Maintenance. The Department of Education shall,
through competitive bidding which may be done nationally or regionally,
procure a contract with a Healthcare Maintenance Organization which is duly
recognized and accepted by hospitals all over the country.

SECTION 5. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the immediate
implementation of the provisions of this Act shall be included in the General
Appropriations Act for the year following the approval of this Act.

SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Secretary of the
Department of Education shall formulate the Implementing Rules and
Regulations (IRR) for the effective implementation of this Act within thirty (30)
days from the effectivity of this Act.
SECTION 7. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act
shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected
thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. – All provisions of laws, orders, decrees,
including rules and regulations inconsistent herewith are hereby repealed
and/or modified accordingly.

SECTION 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
