INCLUSIVE EDUCATION”

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Republic Act No. 11650 (R.A No. 11650), otherwise known as the “Inclusive Education
Act of 2022”, institutionalizes inclusion and services for learners with disabilities in support of
inclusive education, including the establishment of Inclusive Learning Resource Centers (ILRCs)
in every city and municipality. The law likewise recognizes the importance of ensuring learner
transition from school to post-school activities, including vocational training and employment,
through coordination among relevant government agencies.

        In particular, R.A No. 11650 provides that the Department of Labor and Employment
(DOLE) shall coordinate with the Department of Education (DepEd) in developing a training
program for the learners with disabilities’ transition from school to work; that the Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) shall provide technical and vocational
training when necessary; and that DOLE, TESDA, and the Public Employment Service Office
(PESO) shall facilitate job placements and employability enhancement support.

       The establishment of Inclusive Learning Resource Centers in every city and municipality
which were intended as support hubs, also remain unfulfilled. While the DepEd strives to address
the gaps in school learning resource facilities, training for Special Needs Education teachers, and
resource deficits, the government must also ensure that transitional programs for LWDs already at
the Grade 12 level are available.

       Learners with disabilities or special needs can still be functional and can be trained to
perform tasks if they are given enough support and with the appropriate facilities to help them
cope.
        At present, TESDA and the National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA) are already
working together to provide free skills training for persons with disabilities nationwide. The skills
training programs are under the Training for Work Scholarship Program (TSWP), so that the
Persons with Disabilities could be given the National Certificate (NC) necessary for job application
here and abroad.
        This proposed legislation shall focus on providing transitional skills training programs for
Grade 12 learners with disabilities and shall extend the duration of the trainings for up to two (2)
years, in consideration of their diverse capacities to learn.
       Now more than ever is the right time to include persons with special needs in government
programs where they could be fully supported, given adequate attention to facilitate and boost the
development of their capabilities to be functional and vital part in nation building.
       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

24   Section 4. Transitional Training Programs for Grade 12 Learners with Disabilities. – TESDA
25   shall provide transitional technical-vocational training programs for Grade 12 LWDs to support
26   their transition from school to work, consistent with R.A No. 11650. Such programs shall be
     competency-based and disability-responsive and may be delivered through TESDA-registered
     technical vocational institutes, TESDA training centers, and other modalities consistent with
     TESDA rules.

            In implementing this Act, TESDA shall coordinate with Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE), Department of Education (DepEd), the Inclusive Learning Resource
Centers (ILRCs), and Public Employment Service Office (PESO) to ensure that training is aligned
with job coaching, employability enhancement support, and job placement facilitation, consistent
with R.A No. 11650.
        Given the diverse capabilities of learners with disabilities, the skills training shall extend
up to two (2) years to ensure that learners can perform tasks. After completion of the said skills
training, learners shall be issued National Certificate (NC) or other relevant TESDA certification
which is necessary for employment.

Section 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of
this Act, the TESDA, the National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA), in coordination with
relevant government agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations to effectively carry out the provisions of this Act.

Section 6. Appropriations. – Such sums as may be necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act
shall be included in the annual budget of TESDA. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to prohibit
the utilization of other lawful funding sources and inter-agency support mechanisms consistent
with R.A No. 11650, including local supplementation where authorized by law.

Section 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

Section 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof,
which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

Section 9. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
