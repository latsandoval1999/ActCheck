                      AN ACT
PROVIDING GOVERNMENT SERVICE INSURANCE SYSTEM
COVERAGE FOR BARANGAY OFFICIALS, AUTHORIZING THE
PAYMENT OF CONTRIBUTIONS FROM THEIR SALARIES OR
HONORARIA AND BARANGAY FUNDS, PROVIDING A CASH VALUE
BENEFIT UPON SEPARATION BEFORE COMPLETION OF THE
MINIMUM SERVICE REQUIREMENT FOR RETIREMENT, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Barangay officials perform essential public functions at the level of
government closest to the people. They maintain public order, help settle
disputes, coordinate emergency response, implement government programs, and
attend to the daily needs of their communities. Their responsibilities frequently
extend beyond regular working hours and require sustained personal
commitment.

      Despite these responsibilities, many barangay officials complete their
service without dependable financial protection. Their compensation is generally
paid through honoraria, and their tenure is limited by election laws or the duration
of their appointments.

Republic Act No. 8291, otherwise known as the “Government Service Insurance
System Act of 1997,” includes barangay and sanggunian officials in its definition
of employees or members. However, its definition of compensation excludes
honoraria. An express statutory provision is therefore necessary to ensure that
regular barangay honoraria serve as a valid basis for GSIS contributions and
benefits.

      Under the five-year term framework and applicable term limits, barangay
service alone may be insufficient to satisfy the fifteen-year service requirement
for GSIS retirement. Officials who leave government service before meeting that
requirement should nevertheless receive meaningful financial protection arising
from their contributions and qualified service.

      This bill establishes express GSIS coverage for the punong barangay,
members of the sangguniang barangay, including the Sangguniang Kabataan
chairperson serving as an ex officio member, the barangay secretary, and the
barangay treasurer. Their regular salaries or honoraria shall constitute covered
compensation.

The employer contribution shall be charged against barangay funds. The member
contribution may be deducted from the official’s salary or honorarium or
subsidized, in whole or in part, through a duly enacted barangay appropriation
ordinance. Other government units may provide financial assistance, particularly
to barangays with limited fiscal capacity.

The measure establishes an immediate cash value benefit upon actual separation
where the official’s total creditable service remains below the minimum


                                                                                   2
retirement requirement. Payment shall not require the official to reach sixty years
of age.

        An official with fewer than three years of creditable service may receive
accumulated member contributions attributable to coverage under this Act, with
credited interest. An official with at least three years but fewer than the minimum
retirement service requirement may receive the higher of that amount or the
applicable statutory separation benefit.

        Officials may instead preserve their contributions and service credits for
future qualified government service. The bill requires informed election,
transparent computations, timely claims processing, safeguards against duplicate
recovery, and an actuarially supported mechanism for restoring service credits
after reemployment.

Coverage and benefits shall be determined by actual qualified service. The
statutory duration of an elective term shall not automatically confer insurance
credits for periods unsupported by contributions or otherwise unrecognized under
law.

The proposal recognizes the value of barangay service while maintaining
responsible public financial management and protecting the sustainability of the
GSIS.



        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

                      AN ACT
PROVIDING GOVERNMENT SERVICE INSURANCE SYSTEM
COVERAGE FOR BARANGAY OFFICIALS, AUTHORIZING THE
PAYMENT OF CONTRIBUTIONS FROM THEIR SALARIES OR
HONORARIA AND BARANGAY FUNDS, PROVIDING A CASH VALUE
BENEFIT UPON SEPARATION BEFORE COMPLETION OF THE
MINIMUM SERVICE REQUIREMENT FOR RETIREMENT, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Barangay Officials’
GSIS Coverage and Cash Value Benefit Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to recognize the
public service rendered by barangay officials and provide accessible, adequate,
and financially sustainable social insurance protection during and after their
incumbency.
      The State recognizes that the five-year elective term and applicable term
limits may result in barangay officials leaving government service before
completing the minimum service requirement for GSIS retirement.

      The State shall therefore provide an immediate cash value benefit upon
actual separation for officials whose total creditable service remains below that
requirement, while preserving their right to retain service credits for future
qualified government employment.

      Implementation shall observe sound actuarial principles, transparent
budgeting, timely remittance, informed member choice, and accountability in the
use of public funds.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   a. Covered barangay official refers to the duly elected or appointed punong
      barangay; regular member of the sangguniang barangay; Sangguniang
      Kabataan chairperson serving as an ex officio member thereof; barangay
      secretary; or barangay treasurer.

   An official lawfully assuming office through succession or appointment to a
vacancy shall be covered for the period of actual service.

   b. Covered compensation refers to the regular basic monthly salary or
      honorarium lawfully payable for the performance of the duties of the
      covered office. It excludes bonuses, expense reimbursements, per diems,
      and allowances not integrated into basic compensation by law.

   Where honoraria depend on attendance at sessions, the amount actually earned
for the month shall constitute the contribution base.



                                                                                2
   c. Member contribution refers to the contribution attributable to the official,
      whether paid through compensation deductions, direct payment lawfully
      authorized, or a government subsidy permitted under this Act.
   d. Employer contribution refers to the contribution payable by the barangay
      in its capacity as employer.
   e. Creditable service refers to government service recognized by the
      Government Service Insurance System, hereinafter referred to as the GSIS,
      for which contributions have been paid or are legally creditable.
   f. Separation refers to cessation of government service through expiration
      of term, non-reelection, resignation, termination of appointment, removal,
      or another lawful cause, without immediate continuation in another GSIS-
      covered position.
   g. Cash value benefit refers to the special separation benefit established
      under this Act.
   h. Immediate continuation refers to assumption of another GSIS-covered
      position without an intervening cessation of government service, as
      determined from official service records.
   i. Five-year term refers to the statutory term of elected barangay officials
      under Republic Act No. 12232, as amended by Republic Act No. 12326. It
      shall not be interpreted as an automatic grant of five years of creditable
      GSIS service or as an extension of the tenure of an appointed official.

SEC. 4. Coverage and Membership. — Notwithstanding the exclusion of
honoraria under Section 2(i) of Republic Act No. 8291, covered compensation
under this Act shall constitute compensation for GSIS membership, contribution,
and benefit purposes.

      Membership shall be compulsory during actual incumbency, subject to
Republic Act No. 8291 except as expressly modified herein. Coverage shall

                                                                                 3
commence upon lawful assumption of office or the effectivity of this Act,
whichever is later.

      Coverage shall continue throughout lawful service in a covered position.
A change in electoral mandate, appointment, position, or government employer
shall not interrupt membership where the official immediately continues in GSIS-
covered service.

      The expiration of an elective term or appointment shall not constitute
separation for purposes of the cash value benefit where the official immediately
continues in a GSIS-covered position.

      The barangay shall be the employer for registration, employer
contributions, reporting, and remittance.

      An elected official who has reached or exceeds the compulsory retirement
age applicable to ordinary government employees shall not be excluded solely on
that ground while lawfully serving an elective term. Contribution assessment and
benefits shall follow actuarially supported rules consistent with this Act. Existing
rules governing pensions during reemployment shall apply.

      Coverage of the barangay secretary and barangay treasurer shall follow
their actual lawful appointments and service. This Act shall not confer a
guaranteed five-year appointment upon either official.

      Membership shall not extend an elective term or appointment, alter
statutory succession, confer permanent employment status, or modify applicable
term limits.

      Persons serving only as volunteers, barangay tanods, members of the
lupong tagapamayapa, or other community workers shall not acquire coverage
solely by reason of this Act, without prejudice to another applicable law.
                                                                                   4
SEC. 5. Covered Compensation and Contribution Assessment. — Contributions
shall be assessed using the compensation lawfully earned for the applicable
month and the GSIS contribution rates authorized by law.

      For an official serving only part of a month, contributions shall be based
on compensation actually earned, with corresponding service credit determined
under uniform GSIS rules.

      A lawful adjustment in compensation shall be reflected from its authorized
effective date. Retroactive adjustments shall be accompanied by payroll records,
the authorizing issuance, and payment of the corresponding contribution
deficiencies.

      The same remuneration shall not be assessed twice merely because it is
paid through more than one government funding source.

      Where an official concurrently occupies another GSIS-covered position,
the GSIS shall reconcile employer reports and assess contributions under
applicable rules on concurrent service. Overlapping periods shall not be counted
as additional calendar years of service.

      An unauthorized compensation increase shall not create an enforceable
entitlement to a higher benefit. Any adjustment following a final audit or
administrative determination shall preserve benefits attributable to lawful
compensation and contributions.

      The GSIS shall provide written contribution schedules and accessible
computation guidance to barangays.




                                                                               5
SEC. 6. Sources and Payment of Contributions. — The member contribution
shall ordinarily be deducted from the official’s salary or honorarium. The
employer contribution shall be charged against the barangay general fund and
shall not be deducted from the official’s compensation.

      Through an appropriation ordinance, the sangguniang barangay may
subsidize all or part of the member contribution, in addition to paying the
employer contribution.

      The subsidy shall apply uniformly using the same percentage of the
required member contribution for covered officials. It shall be supported by
available funds, comply with applicable expenditure limitations, and be
separately identified in the budget and accounting records.

      An ordinance granting or adjusting a subsidy shall operate prospectively
and shall state its percentage, funding source, and period of application. A subsidy
already earned for a funded period shall not be withdrawn selectively from an
individual official.

      A province, city, or municipality may provide contribution assistance
through a lawful appropriation and agreement specifying beneficiaries, amounts,
reporting duties, and remittance arrangements.

      National assistance may be provided through the General Appropriations
Act. Contributions shall not be charged against funds reserved for another
purpose unless expressly authorized by law.

      A subsidized member contribution shall be credited as the official’s
member contribution. The subsidy itself shall not increase covered compensation
or generate an additional service credit.



                                                                                   6
SEC. 7. Budgeting and Fiscal Accountability. — Every barangay shall include
its required employer contributions in its annual budget and, where necessary, in
a supplemental budget supported by lawfully available funds.

      The barangay treasurer shall maintain a contribution schedule identifying
covered officials, compensation, member deductions, subsidies, employer
contributions, and remittance dates.

      Employer contributions and authorized subsidies shall be accounted for
under applicable government accounting rules. Nothing in this Act shall create a
blanket exemption from personal services limitations or authorize the diversion
of restricted appropriations.

      Where available appropriations are insufficient, the barangay shall
promptly pursue lawful budget adjustments or assistance from another
government unit. Funding insufficiency shall not authorize the transfer of the
employer contribution to the official.

      Each barangay shall prepare an indicative contribution financing schedule
covering the remaining period of lawful incumbency of its covered officials. The
schedule shall identify projected employer contributions, authorized member
subsidies, and anticipated funding sources.

      The financing schedule shall be updated annually to reflect changes in
incumbency, lawful compensation, contribution rates, and fiscal capacity. It shall
guide annual appropriations and shall not constitute authority to spend without a
valid appropriation.

      A contribution subsidy shall continue only for the period supported by its
appropriation and authorizing ordinance. Continued incumbency shall not



                                                                                 7
dispense with annual budgeting requirements or permit withdrawal of a subsidy
already earned for a funded period.

       A change in barangay administration shall not extinguish outstanding
contribution obligations attributable to prior service.

       The Department of Budget and Management and the Department of the
Interior and Local Government shall provide model budget entries and guidance
for barangays with limited administrative capacity.

       Contribution assistance received from another government unit shall be
reconciled with the barangay’s obligations to prevent duplicate payment. Any
unused assistance shall be treated according to the governing appropriation and
assistance agreement.

SEC. 8. Registration, Reporting, and Remittance. — A barangay shall register
a newly covered official within thirty (30) days from assumption of office and
report changes in compensation, position, or service status within thirty (30) days
from the change.

       Existing officials shall be registered within the transitional period
prescribed under this Act.

       Contributions shall be remitted within the period prescribed by Republic
Act No. 8291. Each remittance shall identify the individual member and
contribution month.

       Unallocated payments shall be promptly reconciled.

       The GSIS shall provide electronic and assisted registration and remittance
facilities.   Where connectivity is unavailable, a documented alternative
submission procedure shall be provided.

                                                                                  8
      Before a change in barangay administration, the outgoing responsible
officials shall prepare a certified statement of contributions deducted, paid,
outstanding, or awaiting reconciliation. Copies shall be furnished to the incoming
administration and the GSIS.

      Administrative turnover shall not suspend the barangay’s continuing
obligation to remit contributions.

      The city or municipal government may provide technical assistance or
facilitate consolidated submission through a written arrangement, without
relieving the barangay of its legal obligations.

SEC. 9. Protection Against Nonremittance and Record Errors. — A
contribution deducted from an official’s compensation shall not be required to be
paid again by that official.

      Upon verification through payroll records, official receipts, or other
reliable evidence, the GSIS shall provisionally recognize the corresponding
member contribution and service, subject to final reconciliation.

      Nonpayment of the employer share shall remain an obligation of the
barangay. The GSIS shall pursue collection, interest, and applicable remedies
without shifting that obligation to the member.

      The GSIS shall establish a procedure for correcting omitted service,
misallocated contributions, duplicate deductions, and inaccurate compensation
entries. A member shall receive written notice of the disposition and the
supporting computation.

      An undisputed portion of a claim shall not be withheld solely because
another portion requires reconciliation, where separate payment can be made
without duplicate recovery.
                                                                                 9
      Responsibility for nonremittance, false reporting, or unlawful deductions
shall be determined under Republic Act No. 8291 and other applicable laws, with
due process. The innocent member’s entitlement shall not depend on the
conclusion of proceedings against the responsible officer.

SEC. 10. Benefits and Crediting of Service. — Covered officials shall receive
life insurance, retirement, separation, disability, survivorship, and other
applicable GSIS benefits, subject to the conditions governing each benefit and
the modifications established herein.

      Qualified barangay service shall be accumulated with other recognized
government service, whether rendered continuously or at different times and
under different employers.

      Creditable service shall be determined from actual qualified service and
the corresponding contributions paid or legally creditable. The statutory duration
of an elective term shall not automatically determine the service credited to a
member.

      The limited hours or session-based nature of an official’s duties shall not
alone defeat crediting of lawful incumbency supported by covered compensation
and contributions. The GSIS shall prescribe uniform rules for partial months and
periods without compensable service.

      A change in position, appointment, or government employer shall not
extinguish preserved service credits. An intervening period without creditable
service shall not itself be counted toward the minimum retirement requirement.

      Lawful service through succession, appointment to a vacancy, or an
authorized transitional arrangement shall be credited according to its actual
duration and applicable contribution requirements.

                                                                                10
      The computation of electoral terms shall remain separate from the
computation of GSIS service. Any statutory treatment of an incumbent as having
completed an electoral term shall not create insurance credits for previously
uncovered periods.

      Periods already used for a paid separation or retirement benefit shall not be
counted again unless validly restored under this Act or another applicable law.

      Portability and totalization rights under existing law shall remain available.
Before payment of a cash value benefit, the GSIS shall explain any applicable
portability option supported by verified records.

      Coverage under this Act shall not dispense with the separate age and other
qualifying conditions for retirement.

SEC. 11. Entitlement to the Cash Value Benefit. — Notwithstanding Sections
11(a) and 13-A of Republic Act No. 8291, a covered official who actually
separates from government service with fewer than fifteen (15) years of total
creditable service, or the minimum service requirement subsequently prescribed
by law, may elect an immediate cash value benefit.

      Separation may arise from expiration of term, non-reelection, resignation,
termination of appointment, removal, or another lawful cause, provided the
official does not immediately continue in a GSIS-covered position.

      Payment shall not require attainment of sixty (60) years of age or
completion of three (3) years of service.

      Eligibility shall be determined from total verified creditable government
service, including recognized service outside the barangay.



                                                                                  11
      Completion of an elective term shall not independently create entitlement
where the member continues in covered government service. Conversely,
completion of a full term shall not be required where the member lawfully
separates after a shorter period.

      An unresolved accusation, administrative complaint, or pending case shall
not, by itself, extinguish entitlement. Any forfeiture or withholding shall have an
express legal basis or be supported by an enforceable order.

      An official meeting the minimum retirement service requirement shall
receive the benefits applicable under existing law, subject to the corresponding
age and other conditions.

SEC. 12. Computation of the Cash Value Benefit. — The cash value benefit
shall be computed as follows:

   a. For fewer than three (3) years of total creditable service: accumulated
      member contributions attributable to service covered by this Act, including
      subsidized member contributions, plus credited interest.
   b. For at least three (3) years but fewer than fifteen (15) years of total
      creditable service: the higher of the amount under paragraph (a) or the
      separation benefit computed under Section 11(a) of Republic Act No.
      8291, payable upon separation without the ordinary age-based deferment.

   If the statutory minimum retirement service requirement changes, the
corresponding upper eligibility threshold shall change accordingly.

   The statutory separation component shall be computed using the applicable
average monthly compensation and actual creditable service, subject to the
minimum benefit prescribed by law. It shall not be based solely on compensation
received in the final month of office.

                                                                                 12
   Partial periods shall be treated under uniform GSIS rules. An incomplete
period shall not be rounded up to a full five-year term.

   Interest on accumulated member contributions shall accrue from the date each
contribution is credited until payment. A member electing preservation shall
thereafter be governed by the accounting and benefit rules applicable to preserved
membership.

   The GSIS Board shall prescribe a uniform, nonnegative interest rate supported
by actuarial recommendations. Applicable rates, periods, and computations shall
be disclosed to members.

   Amounts already refunded or used in a previously paid benefit shall be
excluded.

   Employer contributions shall remain within the applicable GSIS funds and
shall not be automatically refundable. The cash value benefit shall be financed as
an insurance benefit under this Act.

SEC. 13. Informed Election and Preservation of Credits. — Before payment,
the GSIS shall give the member a written statement showing the cash value
benefit, proposed deductions, net payment, service credits to be extinguished, and
the consequences of preserving or withdrawing those credits.

      Before an anticipated separation, the GSIS shall make available a benefit
statement explaining the member’s verified service, estimated cash value benefit,
and preservation option.

      The statement shall explain that immediate continuation in a GSIS-covered
position preserves membership and does not permit an intervening cash
withdrawal based solely on the expiration of a term or appointment.


                                                                                13
      A member choosing preservation may combine retained service credits
with future qualified government service, subject to applicable law.

      The GSIS shall not represent that preservation guarantees a future pension.
Retirement shall remain subject to completion of the applicable service, age, and
other statutory requirements.

      Election shall be made through a signed or authenticated declaration. It
shall be voluntary and shall not be required as a condition for receiving
compensation, clearance, or another unrelated barangay benefit.

      Failure to elect payment shall result in preservation of service credits. It
shall not constitute abandonment or waiver of a later claim.

      An application may be withdrawn before payment. Return to GSIS-
covered service before payment shall result in preservation of the corresponding
service and withdrawal of the cash benefit application.

      The GSIS shall offer reasonable assistance to members who cannot readily
access digital facilities or understand the computation.

      A barangay official or employee shall not receive a fee, commission, or
other consideration for facilitating an election or application.

SEC. 14. Claims Processing and Payment. — Applications shall require only
documents reasonably necessary to establish identity, lawful service, separation,
contributions, and payment instructions.

      Documents already held by the GSIS or verifiable through official
government records shall not routinely be required from the claimant again.




                                                                               14
         The GSIS shall acknowledge an application and communicate identified
deficiencies within fifteen (15) working days. Notices shall specify the missing
information and acceptable means of compliance.

         A complete and verified claim shall be paid within sixty (60) days. A delay
shall be explained in writing, identifying the unresolved issue and the action
required to resolve it.

         Payment shall be made directly to the member through an authorized
channel. No intermediary shall be required.

         Only deductions expressly authorized by law may be imposed. The
member shall receive an itemized statement identifying the legal basis and
amount of each deduction.

         An adverse determination shall state its factual and legal grounds and the
procedure for reconsideration or appeal.

         If the member dies before payment, any accrued amount legally payable
shall be released to the person entitled under applicable GSIS and succession
rules.

SEC. 15. Prevention of Duplicate Recovery and Restoration of Credits. —
Service used to compute a paid cash value benefit shall not support another
separation or retirement benefit for the same service unless restored.

         For payment based on accumulated member contributions, only the
barangay service corresponding to those contributions shall be extinguished.

For payment based on the statutory separation formula, all service included in
that computation shall be extinguished.



                                                                                  15
      The GSIS shall identify the affected periods in a statement furnished upon
payment and retain an auditable record of the transaction.

      Upon reentry into GSIS-covered service, the member may apply to restore
the extinguished credits by repaying the cash value benefit and actuarially
determined interest. Rules may permit installment payment under a written
agreement.

      Restoration shall become effective only upon satisfaction of the prescribed
repayment requirements. A partial repayment shall not restore all extinguished
service unless a proportionate restoration method is expressly authorized.

      A cash value payment shall not extinguish a separately accrued life
insurance or other benefit arising from a distinct insured contingency, subject to
applicable prohibitions against duplicate recovery.

SEC. 16. Transitional Coverage and Existing Rights. — Officials serving upon
effectivity shall be registered within ninety (90) days from effectivity of the
implementing rules.

      Contributions shall accrue from effectivity of this Act. Delayed registration
shall not shorten the coverage period, and the corresponding contribution
obligations shall be reconciled.

      Prior service shall be credited only insofar as recognized under existing
law or supported by required contributions under a legally authorized
arrangement.

      This Act shall not authorize the retroactive purchase of previously
uncovered service without express legal authority and payment of the actuarially
required amounts.


                                                                                 16
      Validly accrued benefits, existing service credits, and lawful contribution
payments shall be preserved.

      Claims arising from separation before effectivity shall remain governed by
the law applicable when the entitlement arose, unless another law expressly
provides otherwise.

      Lawful continued incumbency under applicable transitional provisions
shall remain eligible for coverage from the effectivity of this Act.

      The statutory treatment of an incumbent’s electoral term shall not create
contributions or service credits for previously uncovered periods. An extension
or adjustment of an election schedule shall not independently authorize
retroactive purchase of service.

      Officials lawfully succeeding to a vacancy shall receive credit for actual
qualified service and shall not automatically receive credit for the unserved
portion of their predecessor’s tenure.

      The GSIS and the Department of the Interior and Local Government shall
conduct an initial registration and information campaign and provide barangays
with standardized forms and implementation guidance.

SEC. 17. Actuarial Sustainability and Appropriations. — Benefits shall be
administered by the GSIS and paid from its applicable funds.

      Before issuance of the implementing rules, the GSIS shall assess the
liabilities arising from expanded coverage, older entrants, accelerated separation
payments, credited interest, and restoration of service.

      The actuarial assessment shall account for the five-year elective term,
applicable term limits, appointed and partial-period service, transitional

                                                                                17
incumbency, prior government service, and anticipated separation claims. It shall
assess the financial effects of immediate cash payments and the proportion of
members likely to leave service before attaining retirement eligibility.

       Any additional contribution specifically required to finance this Act shall
be actuarially justified, publicly disclosed, and charged to the employer under
lawful GSIS ratesetting authority.

       The assessment shall not authorize reduction of benefits expressly
guaranteed herein.

       The GSIS shall separately monitor contributions and benefits attributable
to this Act and periodically review their effect on fund sustainability.

       Amounts necessary for national implementation and authorized assistance
shall be included in the General Appropriations Act. National assistance
guidelines shall prioritize demonstrated fiscal need and shall identify objective
eligibility and allocation criteria.

       A barangay’s receipt of assistance shall not authorize duplicate funding of
the same contribution liability.

SEC. 18. Member Information, Data Protection, and Oversight. — Each
covered official shall have access to an individual statement of compensation,
contributions, service credits, and available benefits, issued at least annually.

       Records shall be processed and disclosed in accordance with data privacy
laws. Assistance arrangements shall specify safeguards for the transmission of
member information.




                                                                                    18
      The GSIS and the Department of the Interior and Local Government shall
submit an annual report to Congress covering registration, contributions, arrears,
subsidies, benefits paid, processing times, complaints, and actuarial findings.

      Reports shall present aggregate information without unnecessarily
disclosing personal member data.

      The Commission on Audit shall exercise its constitutional and statutory
audit authority over public expenditures under this Act.

SEC. 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the GSIS, in coordination with the Department of the Interior and
Local Government and the Department of Budget and Management, and in
consultation with the Commission on Audit and representatives of barangay
officials, shall promulgate implementing rules.

      The rules shall address membership, compensation assessment, budgeting,
subsidies, remittance, service crediting, benefit computation, informed election,
claims, restoration, and coordination with portability laws.

      The rules shall provide procedures for continuous coverage, actual
separation, immediate transfer to another covered government position,
preservation of credits, and payment of the cash value benefit.

      Procedures shall accommodate barangays with limited staffing and
connectivity and shall include assisted alternatives to digital submission.

      The rules shall distinguish electoral term reckoning from actual creditable
insurance service.




                                                                                  19
      The rules shall not impose additional qualifying years, an age-based
waiting requirement, or other conditions inconsistent with the cash benefit
guaranteed herein.

      Nothing in the rules shall modify applicable term limits, extend lawful
incumbency, or alter the tenure of appointed barangay officials.

SEC. 20. Suppletory Application. — Republic Act No. 8291 and applicable
social insurance laws shall apply insofar as consistent with this Act.

Existing benefits under Section 393 of Republic Act No. 7160 shall continue
according to their respective conditions. Coverage herein shall not replace those
benefits unless another law expressly requires such replacement.

      Republic Act No. 12232, as amended by Republic Act No. 12326, shall
govern elective terms, term limitations, elections, and applicable transitional
arrangements. Nothing in this Act shall amend those electoral rules.

      Accumulation or preservation of GSIS service credits shall not confer
eligibility to remain in office beyond the limits prescribed by law.

SEC. 21. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected shall remain in
full force and effect.

SEC. 22. Repealing Clause. — Sections 2(i), 3, 10, 11(a), and 13-A of Republic
Act No. 8291, and other laws, rules, regulations, and issuances inconsistent with
this Act, are hereby amended or modified to the extent of such inconsistency.

SEC. 23. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
