                             AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE GRANT OF EMERGENCY LEAVE TO EMPLOYEES IN
THE PUBLIC AND PRIVATE SECTORS DIRECTLY AFFECTED BY CALAMITIES

                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE
        The Philippines is no stranger to calamities. The country experiences an average of twenty (20)
typhoons annually, in addition to recurring floods, earthquakes, and other natural disasters. Although
Filipinos have demonstrated remarkable resilience in the face of these events, workers who are directly
affected by calamities should be afforded sufficient time to attend to their safety, recover from damages
and losses, and address urgent personal and family concerns without the added burden of reporting for
work.

        Hence, this proposed bill seeks to institutionalize the grant of five (5) days of emergency leave to
employees in both the public and private sectors who are directly affected by calamities. It aims to provide
workers with a reasonable period within which to recover from the effects of a disaster, attend to their basic
needs and restore their homes and livelihoods, without fear of losing their employment or suffering further
financial hardship.

        It is also consistent with the existing government policies and issuances recognizing the need to
protect employees affected by natural disasters and similar occurrences. These include Civil Service
Commission Memorandum Circular No. 2, Series of 2012 entitled Special Emergency Leave to
Government Employees Affected by Natural Disasters, Memorandum Circular No. 16, Series of 2012 or
the Supplemental Guidelines in the Grant of Special Emergency Leave and Labor Advisory No. 17, Series
of 2022 on the Suspension of Work in the Private Sector by reason of Weather Disturbances and Similar
Occurrences issued by the Department of Labor and Employment.

         This legislative measure is likewise proposed in furtherance of the constitutional duty of the State
to afford full protection to labor and promote their welfare. It is a positive stance to ensuring that Filipinos
are able to recover from calamities despite continuing threats these pose to their livelihood, property,
financial resources, and, in some cases, the lives of their loved ones.

        In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

           Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1   Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as “Emergency Leave for Employees Affected
 2   by Calamities Act.”
 3
 4   Section 2. Declaration of Policies. – It is the policy of the State to protect the rights of workers
 5   and promote their welfare.
 6
 7          The State recognizes that employees in the public and private sectors experience similar
 8   hardships during calamities and may require time to recover from injury, illness, displacement,
 9   property damage, transportation disruption, and other consequences of such events.
10
11          To this end, the State shall institutionalize a minimum of five (5) paid working days of
12   emergency leave per calendar year for qualified employees directly affected by calamities. The
13   emergency leave provided under this Act shall be without prejudice to more favorable benefits
14   granted under existing laws, rules, regulations, collective bargaining agreements, employment
15   contracts, company policies, or established company practices.
16
17   Section 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall mean, as
18   follows:
19
 1          a. “Calamity” refers to a natural or human-induced disaster, crisis, or other occurrence
 2             involving damage, loss, suffering or disruption of livelihood, infrastructures and
 3             normal way of life;
 4
 5          b.    “Emergency leave” refers to a five (5) paid working days of leave per calendar year
 6               granted to employees directly affected by calamities. The emergency leave shall be
 7               non-cumulative and non-commutable.
 8
 9          c. “Directly affected employee” refers to an employee whose place of residence,
10             workplace, route to or from work, health, immediate family, or primary household
11             facilities has been directly affected by a calamity, as provided under this Act and its
12             implementing rules and regulations.
13
14   Section 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to the following:
15
16          a. Employees in the private sector, subject to the eligibility requirements under this Act;
17             and
18
19          b. Civilian and uniformed personnel in the government service, including employees of
20             national government agencies, local government units, government-owned or -
21             controlled corporations, and state universities and colleges, unless their special law
22             provides a benefit that is equal to or more favorable than the benefit granted under this
23             Act.
24
25   The implementing rules and regulations shall provide appropriate rules for part-time, probationary,
26   fixed-term, project, seasonal, agency-deployed, and similarly situated employees.
27
28   Section 5. Eligibility. – An employee who has rendered at least six (6) months of service, whether
29   continuous or cumulative, as may be defined in the implementing rules and regulations, shall be
30   eligible to avail of the emergency leave under this Act.
31
32   An employee who has rendered less than six (6) months of service may be granted emergency
33   leave on a pro-rated basis if so provided in the implementing rules and regulations or under a more
34   favorable employer policy.
35
36   The six-month service requirement shall not prevent an employee from being granted emergency
37   leave under a more favorable law, collective bargaining agreement, employment contract,
38   company policy, or established company practice.
39
 1   Section 6. When the Leave May Be Availed Of. – A qualified employee may avail of emergency
 2   leave when the employee’s place of residence, workplace, or route to and from work is directly
 3   affected by:
 4
 5      (a) a state of calamity declared by the President or the appropriate local sanggunian pursuant
 6          to Section 16 of Republic Act No. 10121, otherwise known as the “Philippine Disaster Risk
 7          Reduction and Management Act of 2010”; or
 8
 9      (b) a calamity or emergency verified by the appropriate national government agency, local
10          government unit, or other competent authority, even in the absence of a formal declaration
11          of a state of calamity.
12
13   The emergency leave may be availed of for five (5) consecutive working days or on a staggered
14   basis within thirty (30) calendar days from the occurrence of the calamity or from the date the
15   employee was directly affected, whichever is later.
16
17   The emergency leave shall be available only to the extent of the employee’s unused emergency-
18   leave entitlement for the calendar year.
19
20   Section 7. Grounds for Availment. – A qualified employee may avail of the emergency leave upon
21   the existence of any one or more of the following circumstances:
22
23          (a) The employee is stranded in an affected area or cannot safely travel to or from work
24              because transportation is unavailable, roads are closed, or infrastructure has been
25              damaged;
26
27          (b) The employee sustained an injury, illness, or medical emergency caused by the
28              calamity;
29
30          (c) The employee must provide essential care or assistance to an immediate family member
31              directly affected by the calamity; or
32
33          (d) The employee must undertake urgent repair, cleaning, or restoration of the employee’s
34              primary residence or household facilities damaged by the calamity.
35
36   Section 8. Notice, Verification and Validation. – The employee shall notify the head of office or
37   employer of the intention to avail of emergency leave as soon as reasonably practicable. Notice
38   may be given through any available means of communication during or immediately after the
39   calamity.
 1
 2   The head of office or employer shall be responsible for determining the employee’s eligibility and
 3   approving the emergency leave upon verification that the employee was directly affected by the
 4   calamity. Verification may include, but shall not be limited to:
 5
 6       1. Proof that the employee’s place of residence is located in an affected area;
 7
 8       2. A declaration of calamity or other similar official notice issued by the proper government
 9          agency;
10
11       3. A medical certificate stating that the illness or injury of the employee or the employee’s
12          immediate family was sustained as a result of the calamity;
13
14       4. Proof concerning road conditions and the availability of transportation during the calamity;
15
16       5. Proof of road closures or transportation disruption caused by the calamity along the
17          employees’ route to and from work; and
18
19       6. Such other proof as may reasonably assist the head of office or employer in determining
20          the employee’s eligibility to avail of emergency leave.
21
22   The absence of a document that could not reasonably be obtained during the calamity shall not by
23   itself justify denial of the leave. The employee may submit the required proof within a reasonable
24   period after returning to work.
25
26   Section 9. Suspension or Cancellation of Work. – An official suspension or cancellation of work
27   due to a calamity shall not be charged against the employee’s emergency leave and shall not, by
28   itself, prevent the employee from subsequently availing of emergency leave when the conditions
29   under this Act exist.
30
31   Nothing in this Act shall diminish the employee protections and rules applicable to the suspension
32   of work during calamities, including the protection of employees who cannot safely report for
33   work because of imminent danger.
34
35   Section 10. Pay During Emergency Leave.— For employees in the private sector, emergency leave
36   shall be paid at the employee’s applicable daily wage for each approved working day of leave,
37   subject to the implementing rules and regulations.
38
 1   For government employees, the emergency leave shall be with pay and shall not be deducted from
 2   regular leave credits, subject to applicable budgeting, accounting, auditing, and civil-service rules.
 3
 4   Section 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
 5   of this Act, the Civil Service Commission, the Department of Labor and Employment, and the
 6   Department of Budget and Management, in consultation with representatives of employees,
 7   employers, government offices, and local government units, shall promulgate the implementing
 8   rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.
 9
10   Section 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
11   invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
12
13   Section 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof,
14   which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
15
16   Section 14. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
17   the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
