                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The enactment of the law guaranteeing universal access to quality tertiary education
represented a significant step toward making higher education more accessible to every Filipino.
However, expanding access to colleges and universities is only part of the challenge. For many
graduates, the transition from education to stable and meaningful employment remains difficult and
uncertain.

        Each year, thousands of Filipinos enter the workforce with academic qualifications but
encounter difficulty securing jobs that correspond to their education and training. Meanwhile,
industries continue to face challenges in finding workers who possess the competencies required by
available positions. This continuing disconnect between what education provides and what the labor
market demands diminishes the value of our investments in human capital and limits the economic
opportunities available to our young people.

        The Education-to-Employment Pathways Act of 2025 seeks to address this gap by
strengthening the link between education and employment. It advances a system in which learners
are provided with the information, guidance, skills, and institutional support necessary to move from
the classroom into decent, productive, and sustainable work.

To achieve this objective, the measure establishes key mechanisms that will strengthen the
education-to-employment pathway:

   1. Establishment of Job Placement Offices (JPOs). - Job Placement Offices shall be established
      in all public senior high schools, state universities and colleges (SUCs), and local universities
      and colleges (LUCs). These offices shall assist learners in making informed career decisions
      and transitioning into the workforce through career guidance, job matching, employer
      linkages, soft skills development, and entrepreneurship support.
   2. Creation of the Philippine Labor Market Information System (PhilLMIS). - A
      comprehensive and integrated labor market information system shall be established under
      the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE). The PhilLMIS shall function as the
      government's central platform for labor market intelligence, occupational forecasting, skills
      mapping, and evidence-based policy development. Its information shall support decision-
      making in education, workforce development, employment, and economic planning.
   3. Strengthening of the Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF) and institutionalization of
      the Philippine Skills Framework (PSF). - The bill reinforces the PQF as the national
      reference for classifying and recognizing qualifications while formalizing the PSF as a
       framework for identifying job-specific competencies. Together, these frameworks shall
       support curriculum alignment, certification, skills recognition, and more effective job
       matching. They shall be digitally integrated, regularly maintained in coordination with
       industry stakeholders, and promoted for nationwide adoption.

        Taken as a whole, these measures seek to ensure that education does not end with the
attainment of a diploma but leads to concrete opportunities for employment, professional growth,
dignity, and economic mobility.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1                                                 TITLE I

 2                                     PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS

 3         Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "School-to-Employment Program
 4   (STEP) Act."

 5          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to promote full, productive, and
 6   decent employment for all Filipinos, especially the youth. The State shall strengthen institutional
 7   mechanisms that align education and training with labor market needs, address jobs-skills mismatch,
 8   and facilitate smooth school-to-work transitions through multi-sectoral collaboration among the
 9   government, industry, and the academe.

10                                                 TITLE II

11                              JOB PLACEMENT OFFICES IN SCHOOLS

12           Sec. 3. Establishment of School-Based Job Placement Offices. - All public senior high
13   schools, state universities and colleges (SUCs), and local universities and colleges (LUCs) shall
14   establish and maintain a Job Placement Office (JPO) to provide employment facilitation, career
15   guidance, and industry linkage services to students and graduates.

16           Sec. 4. Composition. - Each JPO shall be provided with the necessary career services staff
17   to effectively carry out its mandate. The number and composition of staff shall be based on:

18      a) The student population of the school,

19      b) The diversity of tracks or programs offered, and

20      c) The scale of employer and institutional partnerships maintained.

21          Provided, That schools with large enrollments or complex placement needs may establish a
22   Job Placement and Career Services Unit, headed by the JPO, and composed of designated guidance
23   counselors, administrative support staff, and industry liaison officers, subject to available funding
24   and guidelines of DepEd and CHED.
25   Each region shall have a JPO Regional Coordinator, designated by the Department of Education
26   Regional Offices, to oversee the coordination, monitoring, and development of JPOs within the
27   region.

28          Sec. 5. Core Functions of the Job Placement Office. - JPOs shall serve as institutional hubs
29   for career planning, labor market information dissemination, and linkages to internship,
30   apprenticeship, entrepreneurship, and employment opportunities. Each JPO shall perform the
31   following functions:

32      a) Facilitate career guidance and job placement services;

33      b) Forge linkages with industry partners to develop internships, apprenticeship programs, and
34         employment pipelines;

35      c) Coordinate with the Public Employment Service Offices (PESOs), the Department of Labor
36         and Employment (DOLE), the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
37         (TESDA), and other relevant agencies on employment facilitation and program alignment;

38      d) Collaborate with Sector Skills Councils and industry boards to align training programs with
39         labor market needs;

40      e) Coordinate certification and assessment for trainees and graduates aligned with industry
41         standards;

42      f) Conduct labor market information dissemination and career coaching to guide students and
43         reduce job search costs;

44      g) Track and report post-graduation outcomes for institutional and policy feedback;

45      h) Maintain and update a database of employer partners, opportunities, and graduate outcomes;

46      i) Participate in planning and labor assessments led by the Local Industry-Academe Council
47         (LIAC);

48      j) Submit regular reports to the LIAC, DOLE, and relevant agencies.

49           Sec. 6. Area-Based Labor Market Reviews. - Each JPO shall facilitate an annual review of
50   local labor market demand, in coordination with DOLE, PESOs, and regional industry bodies. This
51   process shall:

52      a) Identify high-demand jobs by province or region;

53      b) Map these to existing academic and technical tracks offered by the institution;

54      c) Recommend curriculum or program enhancements to improve graduate employability and
55         relevance.

56           Sec. 7. Employer Partnership Repository. - Each JPO shall maintain access to a shared
57   repository of vetted employer partners, coordinated with the Schools Division Office, TESDA, and
58   local PESOs. The repository shall:

59      a) Document defined job roles, OJT protocols, and hiring qualifications;

60      b) Include employer feedback on graduate readiness and skills alignment;

61      c) Enable rural or under-resourced schools to connect with broader employment networks.

62           Sec. 8. Embedded Soft Skills Training Modules. - JPOs shall implement standardized
63   employability or soft skills training as part of the pre-placement process.
64   This training shall:

65      a) Cover workplace competencies including communication, teamwork, problem-solving, and
66         time management;
 67      b) Be informed by employer feedback and local labor market reviews;

 68      c) Be delivered through self-paced digital modules, in-class sessions, or blended learning,
 69         based on institutional capacity.

 70           Sec. 9. Digital Career Advisory and Guidance Tools. - JPOs shall adopt digital or AI-
 71   enabled career advisory platforms to support students in career planning, job search, and skills
 72   development. These tools will complement in-person services provided by Career Advocates and
 73   help bridge gaps in schools with limited career counseling capacity.

 74           Sec. 10. Optional Youth Entrepreneurship Track. - JPOs may implement an
 75   entrepreneurship bootcamp program for students interested in self-employment or microenterprise
 76   after graduation. The program shall:

 77      a) Be co-delivered with LGUs, NGOs, or accredited private partners;

 78      b) Include training in business ideation, budgeting, market analysis, and cooperative
 79         development;

 80      c) Facilitate referrals to MSME support services, funding programs, and incubator networks.

 81           Sec. 11. Learner Outcomes Tracking Platform. - Each JPO shall maintain a digital platform
 82   for tracking learner outcomes, to be managed by the Career Advocate or designated staff. The
 83   platform shall:

 84      a) Track graduate employment status, time to placement, income levels, and field-of-study
 85         alignment;

 86      b) Generate automated tracer studies and analytics for reporting to DepEd, CHED, and relevant
 87         agencies;

 88      c) Support curriculum refinement by identifying patterns of success and mismatch.

 89                                               TITLE III

 90        LABOR MARKET-ALIGNED CURRICULUM AND PROGRAM DEVELOPMENT

 91            Sec. 12. Senior High School Curriculum Responsiveness. - The Department of Education
 92   (DepEd) shall strengthen the labor market responsiveness of the senior high school curriculum by
 93   developing and implementing a job-ready curriculum that emphasizes work immersion and practical
 94   skills training.

 95   To ensure that senior high school graduates are better prepared for employment, DepEd, in
 96   coordination with the National Industry-Academe Council (NIAC) and relevant Local Industry-
 97   Academe Councils (LIACs), shall:

 98      a) Regularly review and enhance senior high school tracks and specializations based on labor
 99         market trends and graduate employability outcomes, with inputs from Job Placement Offices
100         (JPOs) and local employer networks;
101      b) Increase the duration, relevance, and quality of work immersion programs by expanding
102         partnerships with industry and ensuring alignment with in-demand job roles;
103      c) Provide school-based job simulation opportunities in cases where external immersion
104         placements are limited or infeasible, especially in rural or under-resourced areas; and
105      d) Integrate foundational employability skills and digital literacy across all tracks, informed by
106         feedback from employers and labor market assessments.

107          Sec. 13. Higher Education Program Responsiveness. - SUCs, LUCs, and private HEIs shall
108   review and have the authority to adjust their course and program offerings based on current and
109   emerging labor market trends and opportunities, in order to address jobs-skills mismatch and
110   enhance the employability of graduates.
111   SUCs and LUCs may consider:

112      a) Phasing in or expanding programs aligned with high-demand occupations and priority
113         industry sectors in the local or national labor market;

114      b) Phasing out or restructuring programs with consistently low graduate employability or
115         relevance;

116      c) Integrating employability and digital skills into existing curricula based on employer
117         feedback and labor market signals.

118          Provided, That any adjustments shall be based on regular labor market assessments and
119   outcome tracking data generated by the Job Placement Offices (JPOs), in coordination with the
120   Local Industry-Academe Council (LIAC). These assessments shall include analyses of graduate
121   employment outcomes, skills demand, and employer feedback.

122                                               TITLE IV

123                           NATIONAL INDUSTRY-ACADEME COUNCIL

124          Sec. 14. Creation of the National Industry-Academe Council (NIAC). - The National
125   Industry-Academe Council (NIAC) is hereby institutionalized to serve as the national coordinating
126   body for industry-academe linkage, education-employment alignment, and workforce development.

127          Sec. 15. Composition of the NIAC. - The NIAC shall be made up of representatives from
128   the government, academe, and industry, as follows:

129      a) Government representatives shall include a Commissioner of the Commission on Higher
130         Education (CHED); a Deputy Director-General of the Technical Education and Skills
131         Development Authority (TESDA); an Undersecretary of the Department of Education
132         (DepEd); an Undersecretary of the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE); an
133         Undersecretary of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST); an Undersecretary
134         of the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI); a Commissioner of the Civil Service
135         Commission (CSC); and a Commissioner of the Professional Regulation Commission
136         (PRC).

137      b) Representatives of the academe shall be composed of the presidents of the federations of
138         public and private HEIs, public and private TVIs, and accreditation bodies of higher and
139         technical-vocational educational institutions, which shall include the following: Philippine
140         Association of State Universities and Colleges; Association of Local Colleges and
141         Universities; Coordinating Council of Private Educational Associations; Technical-
142         Vocational Schools and Associations of the Philippines; National Network of Quality
143         Assurance Agencies; and the Federation of Accrediting Agencies of the Philippines.

144      c) Representatives of the industry sector shall be composed of the presidents of the following
145         business and industry federations: Philippine Chamber of Commerce and Industry; Joint
146         Foreign Chambers of the Philippines; Employers Confederation of the Philippines;
147         Semiconductor and Electronics Industries in the Philippines Foundation, Incorporated;
148         Information Technology and Business Process Association of the Philippines; Philippine
149         Business for Education; Management Association of the Philippines; Philippine Business for
150         Social Progress; and the People Management Association of the Philippines.

151   The NIAC shall be headed by DepEd Undersecretary as Chairperson, and the Commissioner of
152   CHED and Deputy Director-General of the TESDA shall be its Co-Chairpersons. The members of
153   the Council, except for the ex officio members, shall receive per diem for every meeting attended in
154   accordance with existing rules and regulations. The Council shall be attached to the DepEd for
155   administrative and budgetary purposes.
156   The DepEd shall provide a secretariat to the NIAC to be manned by the organic personnel of the
157   DepEd and may be augmented by the employees from the member-agencies of the Council through
158   detail or secondment.

159         Sec. 16. Powers and Functions of the NIAC. - The NIAC shall exercise the following
160   powers and functions:

161      a) Monitor labor market trends and the incidence of unemployment, underemployment, and
162         jobs-skills mismatch;

163      b) Formulate national policies and plans to improve labor market alignment;

164      c) Conduct reviews of curricula, training programs, and educational standards vis-à-vis labor
165         market needs;

166      d) Benchmark student competencies and qualifications against national and international
167         frameworks (e.g., PQF, ASEAN QRF);

168      e) Maintain an inventory of job specifications and skill requirements across industries;

169      f) Recommend policy measures to the President, Congress, and national government agencies;

170      g) Coordinate with agencies for data, expertise, and technical inputs;

171      h) Hold regular quarterly meetings; and

172      i) Perform such other functions as may be necessary.

173                                                TITLE V

174                            LOCAL INDUSTRY-ACADEME COUNCILS

175           Sec. 17. Establishment of Local Industry-Academe Councils (LIACs). - A Local Industry-
176   Academe Council (LIAC) shall be established in every province and highly urbanized city (HUC)
177   in the country. The LIAC shall serve as the local counterpart of the NIAC and shall be responsible
178   for aligning education and training programs with the labor market needs of the locality.

179          Sec. 18. Composition of the LIACs. - Each LIAC shall be composed of the following
180   representatives within the province or HUC:

181      a) Government:

182          i. Schools Division Superintendent of the Department of Education (DepEd)

183          ii. Regional or Field Officer of the Commission on Higher Education (CHED)

184          iii. Provincial or City Director of the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
185          (TESDA)

186          iv .Provincial or City Director of the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE)

187          v. Provincial or City Director of the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI)

188          vi. A representative of the Provincial Governor or City Mayor

189      b) Academe
190         vii. Representatives from State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) and Local
191         Universities   and     Colleges     (LUCs) within the   province   or  HUC
192         viii. Representatives from private Higher Education Institutions (HEIS) and
193         Technical-Vocational Institutions (TVIs)

194      c) Industry
195          ix. Representatives from local chambers of commerce and business Associations
196          x. Key employers in priority or emerging sectors within the province or HUC

197      d) Labor and Civil Society

198          xi. Representatives from local labor groups, workers' associations, and youth or community-
199   based organizations

200   The LIAC may invite additional resource persons, including representatives from public
201   employment service offices (PESOs), relevant NGOs, and sectoral skills councils, as necessary.

202   Each LIAC shall be chaired by the DepEd Schools Division Superintendent. The DepEd Schools
203   Division shall provide Secretariat support to the LIAC.

204   The LIAC shall meet at least once every quarter and may form technical working groups (TWGs)
205   as needed.

206         Sec. 19. Powers and Functions of the LIACs. - The LIAC shall exercise the following
207   powers and functions:

208              a) Localize and implement NIAC policies and workforce strategies;

209              b) Monitor local labor market trends and forecast skills demand;

210              c) Align curricula and training programs with local labor market needs;

211              d) Facilitate partnerships among industry, academe, and government;

212              e) Recommend policy and program adjustments to national agencies and the NIAC;

213              f) Support enterprise-based training, faculty immersion, and research collaboration;

214              g) Provide coordination and guidance to institutional JPOs, including in career
215                 guidance, labor market alignment, and employer linkages;

216              h) Submit regular reports to the NIAC on regional developments and challenges; and

217              i) Perform other tasks as may be necessary to achieve the objectives of this Act.

218          Sec. 20. Organization of the LIACs. - Each LIAC shall be chaired by the DepEd Regional
219   Director. A Vice Chair shall be elected from among the non-government members. The DepEd
220   Regional Office shall provide Secretariat support to serve as the LIAC Secretariat.

221   The LIAC shall meet at least once every quarter and may form technical working groups (TWGs)
222   as needed.

223                                                TITLE VI

224                     LABOR MARKET INTELLIGENCE AND DATA SYSTEMS

225            Sec. 21. Establishment of the Philippine Labor Market Information System (PhilLMIS). -
226   There is hereby established a comprehensive and integrated Philippine Labor Market Information
227   System (PhilLMIS) to be housed within the DOLE in close coordination with the Philippine
228   Statistics Authority (PSA), DepEd, CHED, TESDA, DTI, and other relevant agencies and
229   stakeholders.

230           Sec. 22. Objectives. - PhilLMIS shall serve as the central labor market intelligence platform
231   of the government and shall have the following objectives:

232      a) Collect, analyze, and disseminate real-time and forward-looking labor market data, including
233         supply-demand trends, skills gaps, wage levels, employment projections, and emerging
234         industries;
235      b) Identify critical skills shortages and surpluses across sectors and geographic areas;
236      c) Provide actionable insights for curriculum design, program offerings, and training alignment
237         in education institutions and training centers;
238      d) Support career guidance and job matching services for jobseekers, including students and
239         graduates;
240      e) Inform government workforce development policies, education planning, and investment
241         promotion strategies;
242      f) Empower employers and enterprises with reliable labor data to support hiring, reskilling, and
243         training decisions.

244          Sec. 23. Data Sources. - PhilLMIS shall integrate data from, but not limited to, the following
245   sources.

246      a) Government Agencies:

247          i. PSA (e.g., Labor Force Survey, census, business and industry statistics)

248          ii. DOLE (e.g., PESO data, job vacancy registries, displacement reports)

249          iii. TESDA (e.g., TVET enrollments, assessments, certification results)

250          iv. CHED and DepEd (e.g., enrollment and graduation data by course and track)

251          v. DTI and Board of Investments (e.g., industry performance data, investment pledges)

252      b) Private Sector:

253          i. Online job portals and employment platforms

254          ii. Industry associations and employer surveys

255          iii. Skills councils and private recruitment firms, with due consideration of data privacy

256      c) International Sources:

257          i. Regional and global labor market outlooks, benchmarks, and skills frameworks

258          Sec. 24. Key Functions. - PhilLMIS shall perform the following functions:

259      a) Develop and enforce common data standards and protocols to ensure interoperability and
260         accuracy across data sources;

261      b) Conduct periodic skills gap analyses and occupational forecasting to anticipate future
262         workforce needs;

263      c) Establish a public-facing digital portal offering accessible labor market information, career
264         guidance tools, and training program directories;

265      d) Commission and publish targeted research on critical labor issues, including informal
266         employment, green jobs, and automation;

267      e) Operate an early warning system to flag potential job disruptions or mass layoffs by sector
268         or region.

269           Sec. 25. Governance and Collaboration - A Labor Market Intelligence Council shall be
270   established, co-chaired by DOLE and NEDA, with members from PSA, DepEd, CHED, TESDA,
271   DTI, employer organizations, labor unions, and academic institutions.
272   This Council shall:

273      a) Provide strategic direction and oversight to PhilLMIS

274      b) Validate data frameworks and tools in consultation with stakeholders
275      c) Ensure alignment with the Philippine Qualifications Framework and national development
276         plans

277          Sec. 26. Capacity Building - DOLE and partner agencies shall allocate resources and
278   undertake capacity-building activities to strengthen labor market data analytics and forecasting
279   capabilities across all implementing units.

280          Sec. 27. Data Privacy and Security: - All data collection, processing, and sharing under
281   PhilLMIS shall comply with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA No. 10173) and other applicable
282   laws, with appropriate safeguards for personal and sensitive information.

283                                               TITLE VII

284                NATIONAL FRAMEWORKS FOR SKILLS AND QUALIFICATIONS

285           Sec. 28. Reinforcement and Promotion of the Philippine Qualifications Framework
286   (PQF). - The Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF), as established by Executive Order No.
287   83, s. 2012, is hereby reinforced as the national system for classifying qualifications according to
288   defined levels based on knowledge, skills, and values.

289      a) All educational and training institutions, including those supervised by the DepEd, CHED,
290         and TESDA, shall ensure that their programs and curricula are aligned with the relevant PQF
291         levels and descriptors, and that learning outcomes are clearly defined in accordance with
292         these standards.

293      b) The PQF National Coordinating Council (PQF-NCC) shall be strengthened with enhanced
294         mandates      and      resources      to    actively      promote,        monitor, and
295         ensure consistent implementation of the PQF across all sectors and institutions.

296      c) Mechanisms for the Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) and recognition of non-formal and
297         informal learning shall be standardized and streamlined across all PQF levels, allowing
298         individuals to earn formal qualifications based on verified skills, competencies, and
299         experience.

300         Sec. 29. Establishment and Institutionalization of the Philippine Skills Framework (PSF).
301   - The Philippine Skills Framework (PSF) is hereby established and institutionalized as a
302   complementary national reference tool outlining the specific technical, transversal, and behavioral
303   competencies required across various industries and occupations.

304      a) The PSF shall be developed, maintained, and regularly updated by TESDA, in close
305         collaboration with DOLE, DTI, CHED, DepEd, industry associations, and professional
306         regulatory boards.

307      b) The PSF shall serve the following purposes:

308          i. Curriculum Development - Guide the design and revision of curricula and training
309          programs in DepEd, CHED, TESDA, and other training institutions based on industry needs;

310          ii. Skills Assessment and Certification - Provide the basis for competency-based assessment,
311          credentialing, and national certifications;

312          iii. Career Guidance - Inform students and jobseekers about qualifications and competencies
313          required for various career pathways;

314          iv. Job Matching - Improve the accuracy of employment matching by linking job roles with
315          verified skills;

316          v. Talent Development - Enable employers to identify workforce skill gaps and design
317          appropriate training programs;
318          vi. International Benchmarking - Facilitate the alignment and mutual recognition of skills
319          and qualifications with international partners.

320           Sec. 30. Digital Integration and Interoperability. - The PQF and PSF shall be digitally
321   integrated to allow for seamless navigation between qualifications and their corresponding skills
322   sets. A centralized digital platform shall be developed and maintained to:

323              a) Host up-to-date information on the PQF and PSF,

324              b) Provide access to training providers, certified assessment centers, and competency
325                 standards, and

326              c) Serve as a registry of certified individuals and qualifications.

327   This platform shall be interoperable with the Philippine Labor Market Information System
328   (PhilLMIS) to support real-time skills tracking and planning.

329          Sec. 31. Industry-Led Development of the PSF. - Industry Sector Skills Councils

330   (SSCs) shall be formally established under TESDA and empowered to lead the development and
331   periodic updating of the PSF for their respective sectors.

332   The development of the PSF shall be evidence-based and data-driven, utilizing labor market
333   intelligence from PhilLMIS, national employment projections, and consultations with employers,
334   workers, educators, and other stakeholders.

335   Sec. 32. Promotion and Awareness - A sustained national campaign shall be

336   launched to promote widespread awareness and adoption of the PQF and PSF among learners,
337   educators, guidance counselors, employers, government agencies, and the general public.

338   DepEd, CHED, TESDA, and DOLE shall develop coordinated strategies for integration into school
339   guidance programs, job placement services, and public employment services.

340           Sec. 33. International Recognition of Philippine Qualifications and Skills. - The
341   Department of Foreign Affairs (DA), in coordination with DOLE, CHED, and TESDA, shall pursue
342   bilateral and multilateral agreements to support the international recognition of Philippine
343   qualifications and skills frameworks, particularly in countries with significant overseas Filipino
344   worker (OFW) populations.

345   These agreements shall be designed to:

346      a) Strengthen labor mobility and protect Filipino workers abroad;

347      b) Ensure mutual recognition of skills and certifications;

348      c) Align local qualifications with international benchmarks and standards.

349                                             TITLE VIII
350                                         FINAL PROVISIONS

353   partnerships and donor support to enhance JPO, NIAC and LIAC functions.

354           Sec. 35. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
355   of this Act, the DepEd, in coordination with CHED, DOLE, TESDA, DTI, and DILG, shall
356   promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations (IRR).

357           Sec. 36. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
358   invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.
359           Sec. 37. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules and
360   regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

361           Sec. 38. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
362   Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
