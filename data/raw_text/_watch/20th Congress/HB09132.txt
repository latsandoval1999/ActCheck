                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This proposed measure seeks to create the National Education Coordinating
Council that shall primarily be tasked with institutionalizing a permanent and high-
level mechanism for policy integration, strategic alignment and continuous monitoring
across the three pillars of the Philippine education system.

       The Philippine education sector currently operates under a trifocalized structure
where the Department of Education (DepEd), the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED) and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA)
manage their respective sub-sectors independently. While this division of labor was
designed to provide specialized focus, the lack of a formal and binding coordination
framework has led to significant systemic fragmentation. This disconnect manifests in
persistent gaps in the curriculum, misaligned teacher training programs and a
widening mismatch between the skills produced by our educational institutions and
the actual requirements of the modern labor market.

       The findings of the Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM
II) reveal that the country is grappling with a severe learning crisis that requires more
than just incremental changes within individual agencies. It demands a unified national
strategy that ensures a seamless transition for learners as they move from basic
education to higher studies or the workforce.

      Through the creation of the National Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE)
under the Office of the President, this piece of legislation elevates education to a
primary national security and economic priority. This structure ensures that reforms
are not merely departmental but are part of a cohesive National Education and
Workforce Development Plan that remains consistent across different administrations.

      This measure also addresses the administrative inefficiencies that currently
plague the sector by directing the harmonization of data systems and the
rationalization of overlapping inter-agency bodies. It empowers the Council to
streamline resources, optimize the power of the purse through coordinated budget
requests and ensure that every educational investment is directed toward a singular
vision of Filipino excellence.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
     CREATING THE NATIONAL COORDINATING COUNCIL FOR EDUCATION,
     DEFINING  ITS   MANDATE,   POWERS, AND   FUNCTIONS,  AND
     APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

           Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Coordinating
 2   Council for Education (NCCE) Act.”
 3
 4          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and to
 6   take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all. The State recognizes
 7   the need to address the learning crisis and ensure the seamlessness of the education
 8   system. To this end, the State shall establish a permanent mechanism to ensure
 9   strong, effective, and continuous coordination among the agencies involved in
10   education and workforce development, ensuring that reforms and priorities remain
11   consistent regardless of changes in national administration.
12
13          Sec. 3. Creation of the National Coordinating Council for Education. – There is
14   hereby created under the Office of the President an inter-agency body to be known
15   as the National Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE), hereinafter referred to as
16   the Council, which shall serve as the primary mechanism for directing, coordinating,
17   and monitoring education and workforce development reforms across the Executive
18   Branch.
19
20          The Council shall operate under the supervision of the Office of the President
21   and shall ensure alignment of agency policies, plans, programs, and budgets with the
22   National Education and Workforce Development Plan, and other subsequent midterm
23   and long-term plans on education and workforce development.
24
25         Sec. 4. Composition of the Council. – The President of the Philippines shall serve
26   as Chairperson of the Council. The President may designate a Cabinet Secretary as
27   Vice Chairperson to oversee day-to-day coordination, with the following as members:

38          Sec. 6. Technical Working Groups. – To support the work of the Council, the
39   body may create Technical Working Groups in identified priority areas. Said Groups
40   shall be composed of senior officials from member agencies, with at least the level of
41   Assistant Secretary or Director IV of the relevant offices handling these concerns.
42
43         Further, the Council may invite or enjoin relevant heads of departments and
44   agencies to join the Council and/or Groups, as necessary. This includes other executive
45   agencies, heads of industry and educational associations and other organizations, as
46   well as representatives of relevant sectoral groups, to ensure overall efficiency and
47   effectiveness.

 1        Sec. 7. Meetings. – Regular meetings of the Council shall be held at least once
 2   every quarter. Special meetings may be convened as often as deemed necessary by the
 3   Chairperson or a majority of the members. A quorum shall consist of four (4) members
 4   currently holding office: Provided, That the Vice Chairperson is among those present to
 5   constitute a valid meeting.
 6
 7         Sec. 8. The Council Secretariat. – The Secretariat shall serve as the central
 8   technical, analytical, and program management arm of the Council, and shall be
 9   responsible for policy analysis, program coordination, monitoring and evaluation, data
10   integration, and the provision of strategic and administrative support necessary for the
11   effective discharge of the Council’s mandate. It shall be headed by an Executive Director
12   with the rank of Undersecretary, who shall be appointed by the President.
13
14          The Secretariat may be staffed by a core complement of permanent personnel
15   and augmented, as necessary, by technical experts, seconded personnel from member
16   and other government agencies, and contracted specialists, subject to existing civil
17   service, budgeting, procurement, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.
18
19          Sec. 9. Rationalization and Convergence of Inter-Agency Bodies. – To promote
20   policy coherence and administrative efficiency, the Council shall serve as the primary
21   inter-agency platform for matters relating to education and workforce development.
22
23          Accordingly, existing inter-agency councils, committees, and similar bodies with
24   substantially similar mandates or memberships shall, to the extent practicable,
25   conduct their coordination, deliberations, and decision-making through the Council or
26   its Technical Working Groups.
27
28          Meetings convened by the Council or its duly authorized Technical Working
29   Groups shall be deemed compliance with the meeting and coordination requirements
30   of such bodies, subject to applicable laws, rules, and regulations.
31
32          Sec. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
33   of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Office of the
34   President. Thereafter, the amount necessary for its continuous implementation shall
35   be included in the budget proposal of the Office of the President in the annual General
36   Appropriations Act, subject to existing budgeting, accounting and auditing rules and
37   regulations.
