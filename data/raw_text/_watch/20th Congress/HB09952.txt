                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to declare a climate change emergency and establish a
comprehensive Climate Change Resiliency and Adaptability Program to strengthen
the country’s capacity to mitigate, prepare for, and adapt to the adverse effects of
climate change. The measure institutionalizes a whole-of-government and
whole-of-society approach to climate resilience by strengthening climate education,
risk assessment, ecosystem protection and ecocentric innovation.

        The Philippines remains among the countries most vulnerable to the impacts
of climate change. Stronger typhoons, prolonged droughts, rising sea levels, extreme
heat, and increasingly unpredictable weather patterns continue to threaten lives,
livelihoods, food security, water resources, public health, and critical infrastructure.
While existing laws have established important frameworks for climate action and
disaster risk reduction, the growing frequency and intensity of climate-related
events underscore the need for stronger coordination, greater investment in
resilience-building measures, enhanced public awareness, and more robust
adaptation strategies.

        This measure mandates climate risk assessments, promotes open data-sharing
systems, integrates climate resilience education across all levels of learning, and
prioritizes nature-based solutions and climate-resilient infrastructure as
cornerstones of the national response.

       Climate change is no longer a distant threat but a present reality affecting
millions of Filipinos. The costs of inaction continue to rise with every disaster, every
displaced family, and every community left vulnerable to environmental
degradation. The State must act decisively to strengthen resilience, safeguard future
generations, and protect the nation’s development gains.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
DECLARING A CLIMATE CHANGE EMERGENCY AND ENHANCING
RESILIENCY AND ADAPTABILITY TO THE EFFECTS OF CLIMATE CHANGE

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Climate Change
Resilience Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology. The
State also acknowledges that extreme weather conditions have devastated and may
continue to devastate parts of the country which have radically affected food
production and supply, water availability, public health, infrastructure, and
economy.

       The State further recognizes that demographics, settlements and
communities, energy and industry, environment and waste, and transportation are
elements that can influence and can be influenced by climate change policies.
Towards this end, it is imperative to enhance resilience and adaptation to the effects
of climate change, and to declare an environment and climate emergency for the
protection and preservation of people’s lives, livelihoods, and the environment.

       SEC. 3. Declaration of a Climate Change Emergency. – It is hereby declared that
the country is in the state of climate emergency and the government acknowledges
that global warming exists. The declaration stresses the need for the government to
devise measures to stop human-caused global warming, to limit its effects, and to
mobilize funds for climate mitigation efforts.

       SEC. 4. Climate Change Resiliency and Adaptability to the Effects of Climate
Change. – There is hereby created a Climate Change Resiliency and Adaptability
Program, hereinafter referred to as the “Program”, that shall develop and implement
solutions to adapt and mitigate the adverse impacts of climate change. The Program
shall have the following components:

                                           1
    a.​ Open sharing of data amongst the stakeholders. – All data sets related
to climate change and disaster efforts generated by the national government
agencies, local government units (LGUs), civil society organizations (CSOs),
academe, and the private sector shall be shared through a digital platform
that is readily accessible to all stakeholders;​

    b.​ Probabilistic Climate Risk Assessments. – Probabilistic Climate Risk
Assessments (PCRA) shall consider all possible scenarios, likelihood and
inherent uncertainties, associated impacts of climate change, and the climate
change projections in extremes in consultation with collective strengthening
for community awareness on natural disasters agencies. The PCRA shall
generate the needed localized baseline information on climate risk faced by
vulnerable communities and their support system for potential
climate-related hazards;​

    c.​ Multi-level national educational plan on climate resilience. – There
shall be a knowledge and competency building education agenda on climate
change from primary to tertiary levels of the education system, including the
country's research and development program, producing citizens with
progressive conceptual and operational knowledge and skills to build
resilient communities, sectors, and nation.

       Children and youth shall be provided with climate education and
green skills, necessary for their adaptation to and preparation for the effects
of climate change: Provided, That they shall be included in all climate-related
decision-making: Provided, further, That the youth shall be included in all
national, regional, and international climate negotiations and decisions;

   d.​ Link between health and climate change. – The link between health
and climate change shall promote, account for, and monitor the health
co-benefits from climate interventions which shall be reflected in
decision-making at all levels of governance.

       The right to a safe, clean, healthy, and sustainable environment shall
also be recognized: Provided, That policies and processes to safeguard this
right shall be adopted: Provided, further, That to strengthen the science of
health and climate change, it is necessary to close knowledge gaps and build
capacity to reach on the health impacts of climate change and health
co-benefits of climate actions;

    e.​ Critical services. – Increasing investment in climate change adaptation
and resilience in critical services, such as water, sanitation and hygiene
systems, health, and education, shall be supported in order to protect the
children, communities, and the most vulnerable from the worst impacts of
climate change; and

    f.​ Ecosystem protection strategies. – The following ecosystem protection
strategies shall be pursued:

               6.​ Assistance to LGUs by the relevant national government
            agencies in the conduct of vulnerability assessments, including their
            Local Climate Change Action Plan.​

      SEC. 5. Roles of Department Agencies. – Government agencies shall
immediately and efficiently coordinate and lead in taking practicable measures to
preserve and protect the environment, as follows:

         a.​ ﻿The Climate Change Commission (CCC) shall coordinate, monitor, and
      evaluate programs and action plans related to climate change based on the
      National Climate Change Action Plan;​

         b.​ ﻿The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council
      (NDRRMC) shall monitor the integration of climate change resilience and
      adaptation in the national development plans and create an enabling
      environment that shall promote multi-stakeholder participation of concerned
      departments, agencies, LGUs, organizations, and other stakeholders;​

          c.​ ﻿The Philippine Atmospheric Geophysical, and Astronomical Services
      Administration (PAGASA) shall conduct research and development on the
      science of climate and changing weather patterns worldwide, and provide

   d.​ ﻿The Department of Education (DepEd) shall integrate climate change
education under the Science Curriculum from Grade 2 to Senior High School
level, in both private and public educational institutions, to raise and increase
the awareness of children about the impacts and effects of climate change;​

    e.​ The Commissioner on Higher Education (CHED) shall integrate
climate change education under the General Science subjects of the secondary
and tertiary levels, in both private and public educational institutions, to raise
and increase the awareness of children and youth about the impacts and
effects of climate change;​

   f.​ ﻿The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) shall
develop, formulate, and recommend climate change strategies, programs, and
policies especially on physical vulnerability;​

    g.​ ﻿The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall
coordinate with LGUs to address vulnerability and impacts of climate change
in regions, provinces, cities, and municipalities;​

    h.​ ﻿The Department of Science and Technology (DOST) shall conduct
research and development on the effects of climate change and pathways to
climate disasters, including determining vulnerability and assessing climate
risks of natural ecosystem; develop technologies to enhance climate change
adaptation; facilitate the formulation of climate change policies on science,
technology, and innovations (STI) and related technical assistance; and
monitor and assess the impacts of provided technical assistance and/or
intervention;​

    i.​ The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall ensure that
employers promote a healthy and safe environment for the employees which
is sustainable and environment-friendly;​

   j.​ The Department of Information and Technology (DICT) shall provide
cost-free means for public information dissemination on climate change,
vulnerabilities, risks, relevant laws, and protocols specifically those which
need immediate dissemination;​

   k.​ The Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev)
shall provide the feasibility studies and the determination of how climate
change policies can help in the economic development of the country;​

   l.​ The Department of Agriculture (DA) shall institutionalize a
comprehensive program to strengthen the resilience of the country's food
system;​

       SEC. 6. Multi-Sectoral Approach. – Concerned government agencies and LGUs
shall allocate adequate funds from their annual appropriations for the formulation,
development, and implementation of their respective climate change programs and
plans, in accordance with Republic Act No. 9279, otherwise known as the “Climate
Change Act of 2009”.

       SEC. 7. Corporate Social Responsibility of Private Entities. – Projects undertaken
by private entities under this Act shall be considered as part of their Corporate
Social Responsibility Program.

       SEC. 8. Climate Change Resiliency and Adaptability Summit. – There shall be a
Climate Change Resiliency and Adaptability Summit to be held on the second week
of September every year. The Summit shall be spearheaded by the CCC and shall be
participated in by all stakeholders mentioned in Section 5 hereof. The Summit shall
aim to collaborate all the efforts of the concerned agencies in environment
preservation and protection.

      SEC. 9. Reportorial Requirement. – All agencies mentioned in Section 5 of this
Act shall submit an annual report on the current status of the country’s resiliency

declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full

hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
