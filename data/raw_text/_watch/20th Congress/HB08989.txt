                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The “Last Mile Schools Act” seeks to address the systemic educational gaps in
the country’s most vulnerable and underserved areas by reinforcing the State policy
to protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education. It specifically targets
geographically isolated, disadvantaged, and conflict-affected areas (GIDCA) where
access to basic education remains severely limited.

        To ensure the effectiveness and sustainability of education interventions, the
bill provides for inter-agency collaboration to deliver complementary basic services,
including: a) access roads through the Department of Public Works and Highways, to
enable safe and efficient travel to and from schools; b) electrification through the
Department of Energy and the National Electrification Administration, to power modern
learning facilities; and c) ICT infrastructure through the Department of Information and
Communications Technology, to bridge the digital divide.

        The bill explicitly protects the rights of indigenous cultural communities and
ensures that the establishment and operations of Last Mile Schools respect their
cultural traditions and comply with the Indigenous Peoples’ Rights Act of 1997
(Republic Act No. 8371). For this purpose, one of the criteria for the identification of
Last Mile Schools is that at least seventy-five percent (75%) of the student population
that is 75 shall be composed of indigenous peoples.

       The bill further mandates that Department of Education to design and
implement context-specific programs for Last Mile Schools, including specialized
training for teachers and school heads, appropriate staffing standards, and a distinct
formula for maintenance and other operating expenses (MOOE) that reflects the
higher cost of delivering education in remote and hard-to-reach areas. DepEd is also
mandated to ensure that the learning outcomes are not obscured by national averages
and that specific gaps are systematically identified and addressed.

      Recognizing the urgency of providing adequate, accessible, and culturally
responsive education to learners in these areas, the bill provides for a time-bound five

necessary school facilities and support systems.

    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                 AN ACT
     ESTABLISHING PUBLIC BASIC EDUCATION SCHOOLS IN GEOGRAPHICALLY
     ISOLATED AND DISADVANTAGED AND CONFLICT-AFFECTED AREAS,
     PROVIDING ACCESS ROADS LEADING TO LAST MILE SCHOOLS, AND
     APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Last Mile Schools
 2   Act."
 3
 4        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to protect
 5   and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and to take
 6   appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all.
 7
 8        The State recognizes and promotes the rights of Indigenous Cultural
 9   Communities within the framework of national unity and development. To this end, the
10   State shall recognize the right of indigenous families and communities to retain shared
11   responsibility for the upbringing, training, education, and well-being of their children,
12   consistent with the rights of the child.
13
14          The State shall improve the quality and access to basic education for children in
15   far-flung, isolated, disadvantaged, and conflict-affected areas, as well as those in Last
16   Mile Schools, by investing in school infrastructure, access roads, and programs
17   responsible for their unique contexts.
18
19           SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
20
21           (a)   Basic Education refers to the intended basic learning needs that provide
22                 the foundation on which subsequent learning can be based. It includes
23                 kindergarten, elementary and secondary education as well as alternative
24                 learning systems for out-of-school learners and those with special needs;


                                                 3
 1        (b)   Basic Education School refers to learning institutions accredited by the
 2              Department of Education (DepEd) to deliver services addressing the
 3              intended basic learning needs of students;
 4        (c)   Geographic Isolated and Disadvantaged and Conflict-Affected Areas
 5              (GIDCA) refer to schools or communities situated in remote or marginalized
 6              locations, with limited access to essential services and infrastructure, or
 7              impacted by conflict or insecurity;
 8        (d)   Indigenous Cultural Communities (ICCs)/Indigenous Peoples (IPs)
 9              refer to the same definition stated in Section 3h, Chapter 2 of Republic Act
10              No. 8371, or The Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997, which describes
11              it as "a group of people or homogenous societies identified by self-
12              ascription and ascription by others, who have continuously lived as
13              organized community on communally bounded and defined territory, and
14              who have, under claims of ownership since time immemorial, occupied,
15              possessed and utilized such territories, sharing common bonds of language,
16              customs, traditions and other distinctive cultural traits, or who have, through
17              resistance to political, social and cultural inroads of colonization, non-
18              indigenous religions and cultures, became historically differentiated from
19              the majority of Filipinos. ICCs/IPs shall likewise include peoples who are
20              regarded as indigenous on account of their descent from the populations
21              which inhabited the country, at the time of conquest or colonization, or at
22              the time of inroads of non-indigenous religions and cultures, or the
23              establishment of present state boundaries, who retain some or all of their
24              own social, economic, cultural and political institutions, but who may have
25              been displaced from their traditional domains or who may have resettled
26              outside their ancestral domains;" and
27        (e)   Last Mile Schools refer to public basic education schools with gaps in
28              resources and facilities, which are located in GIDCA.
29
30        SEC. 4. Establishment of Last Mile Schools. – The Last Mile Schools are
31   hereby established, under the administration of the DepEd, to cater to the basic
32   education of learners throughout the country's GIDCA.
33
34        The establishment of Last Mile Schools in locations situated by the ICCs shall be
35   undertaken in consideration of the rights of the IPs, in accordance with Republic Act
36   No. 8371.
37
38        SEC. 5. Access to Education. – The DepEd shall collaborate with concerned
39   national government agencies for the delivery of the infrastructures necessary to
40   ensure access to education in the Last Mile Schools, namely:
41        (a) The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), for the
42             construction of access roads leading to the location of Last Mile Schools;
43        (b) Department of Energy (DOE) and National Electrification Administration
44             (NEA), for the installation of electricity; and
45        (c) Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), for the
46             installation of information and communications technology.
47
48        SEC. 6. Criteria. – The indicators used in identifying a public school to be
49   considered a Last Mile School may include any of the following:
50

 1        (a)   Having fewer than four (4) classrooms;
 2        (b)   With makeshift or nonstandard rooms;
 3        (c)   Absence of or irregular electricity;
 4        (d)   Have not been allocated funds for repairs or new construction projects in
 5              the last four (4) years unless destroyed;
 6        (e)   With travel distance of more than one hour from the center of a city or
 7              municipality, or with difficulty of terrain or of such nature as to be considered
 8              having difficulty of access to existing educational institutions;
 9        (f)   Having multigrade classes or rooms;
10        (g)   With less than five (5) teachers;
11        (h)   Having a student population of less than one hundred (100) learners;
12        (i)   With more than seventy-five percent (75%) IP learners; and
13        (j)   Having such other circumstances that make it difficult for learners to access
14              school facilities.
15
16        SEC 7. Tailored Programs for Last Mile Schools. – The DepEd shall formulate
17   and implement programs responsive to the unique context of Last Mile Schools,
18   teachers, and learners, including but not limited to:
19
20        (a)   Learning delivery and intervention mechanisms;
21        (b)   School staffing standards, given the unique needs of learners in Last Mile
22              Schools;
23        (c)   Formula for school maintenance and other operating expenses, in
24              consultation with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM);
25        (d)   Learning tools and equipment;
26        (e)   Learner support services;
27        (f)   Relevant pre-service teacher education curriculum and assessment with
28              the Teacher Education Council (TEC), the Commission on Higher
29              Education (CHED), and the Professional Regulation Commission;
30        (g)   Relevant in-service training, through the National Educators Academy of
31              the Philippines (NEAP); and
32        (h)   Teacher, school head, and school evaluations, in coordination with the Civil
33              Service Commission.
34
35         SEC 8. Implementation. – The school building program for Last Mile Schools
36   shall be established and completed within a period of five (5) years after the effectivity
37   of this Act.
38
39        To facilitate synchronicity, harmony and complementarity of plans, resources,
40   and efforts of both the national government and the local government units (LGUs)

42   implement the programs, projects, and activities that address the needs of the Last
43   Mile Schools, in coordination and collaboration with the National Commission on
44   Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), DPWH, DICT, DOE, NEA, Department of Social Welfare
