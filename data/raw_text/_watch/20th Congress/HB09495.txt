                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Philippine coffee and cacao industries possess significant potential to
contribute to rural development, employment generation, value-added
agriculture, and export growth. Many upland and highland areas in the country
are well-suited for coffee and cacao production, yet farmers continue to face
challenges such as limited access to quality planting materials, inadequate post-
harvest facilities, weak market linkages, and insufficient technical support.

      Despite increasing global demand for premium coffee and cacao products,
many local farmers and small enterprises are unable to maximize economic
opportunities due to low productivity and limited access to processing and
marketing support. Strengthening these industries will not only improve farmer
incomes but also promote sustainable agricultural development and countryside
enterprise growth.

       This bill seeks to institutionalize government support for the coffee and
cacao sectors through the establishment of regional nurseries for quality
seedlings, provision of marketing grants and processing support, strengthening of
  buyer linkages, and expanded technical and capacity-building assistance for
  farmers, cooperatives, and micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs). By
  enhancing productivity, processing, and market access, this measure aims to
  develop globally competitive Philippine coffee and cacao industries while
  creating sustainable livelihood opportunities in rural communities.

       In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

                              CHAPTER 1
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Coffee and Cacao
Development Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote the sustainable development and global competitiveness of the
Philippine coffee and cacao industries as drivers of rural development,
employment generation, environmental sustainability, value-added agriculture,
and export growth.
      The State shall support the development of these sectors through the
provision of quality planting materials, research and development, financing
assistance, processing support, marketing programs, and stronger farm-to-market
connectivity, while promoting climate-resilient and agroforestry-based farming
systems.


SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
hereby defined:


a) “Coffee and Cacao Farmer” refers to any individual, cooperative, association,
or juridical entity engaged in the cultivation and production of coffee or cacao;
b) “MSMEs” refers to micro, small, and medium enterprises as defined under
existing laws;
c) “Shared Service Facility (SSF)” refers to common service facilities,
machinery, equipment, or processing centers shared by farmer groups,
cooperatives, associations, or MSMEs for production and post-harvest activities;
d) “Post-Harvest Facilities” refers to equipment and infrastructure used for
drying, fermentation, roasting, storage, packaging, grading, and processing of
coffee and cacao products;
e) “Sustainable Farming” refers to agricultural practices that protect natural
resources, improve productivity, promote biodiversity, and ensure long-term
environmental and economic viability;
f) “Value-Added Processing” refers to activities that enhance the quality,
marketability, or commercial value of coffee and cacao products through
roasting, fermentation, chocolate-making, packaging, branding, and related
processes.


SEC. 4. Objectives. – This Act aims to:

                                                                                    2
   a) Promote sustainable and climate-resilient farming systems for coffee and
      cacao, particularly in upland and highland areas suitable for cultivation;
   b) Enhance productivity, quality, and competitiveness of locally produced
      coffee and cacao products;
   c) Strengthen local processing capacities and value-added enterprises;
   d) Improve market access and establish direct linkages between farmers,
      processors, traders, exporters, and institutional buyers;
   e) Expand access to financing, insurance, and technical assistance for coffee
      and cacao stakeholders;
   f) Encourage research, innovation, and technology adoption in coffee and
      cacao production and processing; and
   g) Generate rural employment opportunities and increase farmer incomes.


SEC. 5. Establishment of Coffee and Cacao Nurseries. –


(a) The Department of Agriculture (DA), through its High Value Crops
Development Program (HVCDP), in partnership with Local Government Units
(LGUs), State Universities and Colleges           (SUCs), accredited     farmers’
cooperatives, and private sector stakeholders, shall establish and maintain
regional and provincial nurseries for high-quality coffee and cacao seedlings.


(b) These nurseries shall:
   1. Produce certified planting materials of recommended and climate-resilient
      coffee and cacao varieties;
   2. Provide seedlings to smallholder farmers at subsidized rates or under cost-
      sharing arrangements;
   3. Conduct trainings on nursery management, farm establishment, and
      sustainable cultivation practices; and

                                                                                   3
   4. Support replanting and rehabilitation programs in areas affected by pests,
      diseases, or natural calamities.


   Priority shall be given to smallholder farmers, agrarian reform beneficiaries,
indigenous peoples, and farmer cooperatives.


SEC. 6. Research and Development. – The DA, in coordination with the
Agricultural Training Institute (ATI), SUCs, research institutions, and industry
stakeholders, shall support research and development programs for the coffee and
cacao industries, including:
   a) Development of high-yielding, disease-resistant, and climate-resilient
       varieties;
   b) Soil health and sustainable nutrient management;
   c) Pest and disease prevention and control;
   d) Post-harvest innovation and quality enhancement technologies;
   e) Product diversification and value-added processing; and
   f) Digital traceability and quality certification systems.


SEC. 7. Marketing Grants and Local Processing Incentives. – (a) The DA shall
establish a Coffee and Cacao Marketing Grant Fund to support:
   1. Development of shared service facilities (SSF) and post-harvest processing
      centers;
   2. Participation in domestic and international trade fairs, exhibits, and
      business missions;
   3. Branding, packaging, quality certification, and traceability initiatives,
      including organic and fair-trade certification;
   4. Development of specialty coffee, artisanal chocolate, and other value-
      added products; and

                                                                                4
   5. Promotion       of    direct   farmer-to-market        and   farmer-to-exporter
         arrangements.


(b) Preference shall be given to farmer cooperatives, associations, and MSMEs
engaged in local processing and direct marketing.


SEC. 8. Buyer Linkages and Market Development Program. – The DA, through
the Agribusiness and Marketing Assistance Service (AMAS), shall establish a
Coffee and Cacao Buyer Linkages and Market Development Program which
shall:
   a) Organize regular buyer-seller forums, trade fairs, business matching
         activities, and investment promotion initiatives;
   b) Develop and maintain an online platform to facilitate transactions, market
         information, and product traceability;
   c) Coordinate with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), export
         promotion agencies, and international market networks to identify and
         develop new domestic and export markets;
   d) Promote fair trade practices and transparent pricing mechanisms; and
   e) Encourage institutional procurement of locally produced coffee and cacao
         products.


SEC. 9. Capacity Building and Technical Assistance. – The DA shall coordinate
with the ATI, SUCs, LGUs, and relevant industry stakeholders to provide
continuous capacity-building and technical assistance programs on:
   a) Sustainable and climate-resilient cultivation practices;
   b) Agroforestry and biodiversity conservation approaches;
   c) Farm management and entrepreneurship;
   d) Post-harvest quality control and standards compliance;

                                                                                    5
   e) Value-added product development and marketing; and
   f) Digital marketing and e-commerce strategies.


SEC. 10. Access to Credit and Financing. – Government financial institutions,
including the Land Bank of the Philippines, the Development Bank of the
Philippines, and the Agricultural Credit Policy Council (ACPC), shall develop
accessible financing programs and credit windows for coffee and cacao farmers,
cooperatives, associations, and MSMEs.


Such financing programs may include concessional loans, credit guarantees,
startup financing, equipment financing, and rehabilitation assistance.


SEC. 11. Crop Insurance Coverage. – The Philippine Crop Insurance
Corporation (PCIC) shall prioritize eligible coffee and cacao farmers for crop
insurance coverage against losses arising from natural calamities, pests, diseases,
and climate-related risks.


SEC. 12. Roles of Local Government Units. – LGUs shall assist in the
implementation of this Act by:
   a) Identifying priority coffee and cacao production areas;
   b) Providing extension and technical support services;
   c) Assisting in the organization of farmer cooperatives and associations;
   d) Supporting local market development initiatives; and
   e) Integrating coffee and cacao development programs into local agricultural
      development plans.


SEC. 13. Annual Report to Congress. – The DA shall submit an annual report
to Congress on the implementation of this Act, including information on:

                                                                                  6
   a) Production and productivity statistics;
   b) Number of farmer beneficiaries and supported enterprises;
   c) Nursery and processing facility outputs;
   d) Marketing grants and financing assistance provided;
   e) Export performance and market development initiatives; and
   f) Recommendations for policy and program improvements.


SEC. 14. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department
of Agriculture. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act.


LGUs may allocate additional funds from their respective local budgets for the
implementation of coffee and cacao development programs.


SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DA, in consultation with the DTI, Department of
Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), Department of Science and
Technology (DOST), SUCs, farmer organizations, cooperatives, and industry
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.


SEC. 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.
