                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

Republic Act No. 9263, otherwise known as the “Bureau of Fire Protection and Bureau of Jail
Management and Penology Professionalization Act of 2004,” was enacted to professionalize
the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) and the Bureau of Jail Management and Penology (BJMP),
strengthen their organizational structures, and promote the professional development and
welfare of their personnel. 1 More than two decades after its enactment, however, the
operational responsibilities, organizational requirements, and manpower needs of both
agencies have substantially evolved, warranting a reassessment of their existing rank
classification and compensation structure.

The growing demands placed upon the BFP and BJMP underscore the need for adequate
manpower and a rank structure commensurate with the responsibilities of their personnel. In
July 2026, the BJMP added 2,226 newly appointed jail officers, bringing its reported workforce
to 28,963 personnel, as the Bureau continues efforts to address its manpower requirements and
move toward a target workforce of 46,984.2 Meanwhile, the BFP has reported 39,289

These manpower requirements must be considered alongside the increasingly complex
functions performed by both agencies. The BFP is responsible not only for fire suppression but
also for fire prevention, investigation, emergency response, rescue, and disaster-risk reduction,
while the BJMP is tasked with the safe and humane custody, management, and rehabilitation
of persons deprived of liberty (PDLs). The adequacy of personnel and the effectiveness of their
command structures are therefore essential to public safety.

The urgency of strengthening BFP manpower is further demonstrated by the reported increase
in fire incidents. During the first quarter of 2026, the BFP recorded 4,092 fire incidents, a 28%
increase from the 3,184 incidents recorded during the same period in the previous year. Despite
the increase in incidents, reported fire-related fatalities and property damage declined during
the period.4 The continuing volume and complexity of emergency incidents demonstrate the
need for sufficient personnel who are properly trained, organized, and compensated according
to the demands of their functions.

The measure seeks to establish a clear and uniform rank and salary structure for BFP and BJMP
personnel, from Fire/Jail Officer to Fire/Jail General. Salaries will reflect the duties and
responsibilities of each rank and will be reasonably aligned with other uniformed services.
Promotions and reclassifications will remain based on merit and existing qualification and
plantilla requirements. During the transition, incumbent personnel shall retain their existing
rank, appointment status, tenure, and basic pay. No personnel shall be demoted, separated, or
deprived of security of tenure solely because of the new rank classification. The BFP and
BJMP, in coordination with the DILG, DBM, and CSC, shall develop a transition plan to guide
the orderly implementation of the new system.

The reform will help build a more professional and motivated BFP and BJMP workforce. Fair
ranks and appropriate compensation will improve morale and support career growth. It will
also help retain qualified personnel and strengthen their ability to perform their duties. By
investing in these essential uniformed services, the State can better protect communities and
ensure public safety.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

        SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “BFP and BJMP Rank
Classification and Compensation Reform Act.”

      SECTION 2. Amendment of Section 5 of Republic Act No. 9263. Section 5 of
Republic Act No. 9263 is hereby amended to read as follows:

        Section 5. Appointment of Uniformed Personnel to the BJMP – The appointment
of the BJMP shall be effected in the following manner:

       a) Fire/Jail Officer Fire/Jail to Fire/ Jail Executive Master Sergeant – Appointed by
the Regional Director for Fire Protection and Regional Director for Jail Management and
Penology for the regional office uniformed personnel or by the respective Chief of the Fire
Bureau and Chief of the Jail Bureau for the national headquarters office uniformed
personnel, and attested by the Civil Service Commission (CSC);

      b) Fire/ Jail Lieutenant Colonel – Appointed by the respective Chief of the Jail
Bureau, as recommended by their immediate superiors, and attested by the CSC;

       c) Fire/Jail Colonel – Appointed by the Secretary of the DILG upon
recommendation of the respective Chief of the Fire Bureau and Chief of the Jail Bureau,
with the proper attestation of the CSC; and
      d) Fire/ Jail Lieutenant General to Fire/ Jail General – Appointed by the President
of the Philippines upon recommendation of the Secretary DILG, with the proper
endorsement by the Chairman of the CSC.”

       SECTION 4. Transitional and Incumbency Protection. — All incumbent
uniformed personnel of the BFP and the BJMP shall retain their existing rank,
appointment status, tenure, and basic pay pending the full implementation of the revised
rank classification and salary-grade structure under this Act.

       Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the BFP and
the BJMP shall, in coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the Civil
Service Commission (CSC), submit to Congress and the DBM for approval a transition and
implementation plan containing:

   1. The exact mapping and equivalency of existing ranks and positions to the revised
      rank classifications;
   2. The authorized plantilla positions and corresponding salary grades;
   3. The revised qualification standards and eligibility requirements for each rank;
   4. The rules governing incumbent personnel who do not immediately meet the
      revised qualification standards;
   5. The schedule and timetable for the filling of authorized positions and the
      implementation of salary adjustments; and
   6. The projected personnel-services and retirement-benefit costs.

       No incumbent shall be separated, demoted, or deprived of security of tenure solely
by reason of the transition to the revised rank classification, except for lawful cause and
after due process.

       SECTION 5. Qualification Standards and Career Progression. — The
appointment, promotion, and lateral entry of uniformed personnel under the revised
rank classification shall be governed by the qualification standards, eligibility
requirements, performance evaluations, career progression rules, and selection
procedures prescribed by law and by rules duly issued by the CSC, DILG, BFP, BJMP, and
DBM within their respective mandates.
The adoption of a revised rank title or salary-grade allocation shall not dispense with any
qualification, eligibility, training, time-in-grade, experience, performance rating,
examination, or other requirement prescribed by law or by valid implementing rules.
      SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DILG, in coordination with the DBM, CSC, BFP, and
BJMP, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
implementation.
The Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) shall explicitly cover:
   1. The rank equivalency and mapping matrix;
   2. Procedures for appointment, promotion, lateral entry, and career development;
   3. The protection of incumbents during the transition period;
   4. The approval, creation, and funding of necessary plantilla positions;
   5. The phased schedule and manner of salary-grade implementation; and
   6. Guidelines ensuring non-diminution of salary, rank, retirement rights, and other
      accrued benefits.
       SECTION 7. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year's available
appropriations of the BFP and the BJMP. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for
the continued implementation and maintenance of this Act shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act (GAA).
No salary adjustment, benefit enhancement, or retirement-pay increase provided under
this Act shall take effect or be disbursed unless the corresponding budgetary allocation
has been authorized and appropriated by law.
      SECTION 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is held
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions or parts not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.
        SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, decrees,
administrative orders, rules, regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with or contrary
to the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
       SECTION 10. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
