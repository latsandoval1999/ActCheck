                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE​

      Republic Act No. 10963, the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion
(TRAIN) Act, exempted the first Two Hundred Fifty Thousand Pesos (PhP
250,000.00) of annual taxable income of individual taxpayers beginning 1
January 2018.1 That threshold has not been adjusted since and inflation
has not stopped. Based on the Consumer Price Index of the Philippine
Statistics Authority (2018=100), which stood at 135.4 in June 2026,2 the
goods and services that cost PhP 250,000.00 in 2018 now cost
approximately PhP 338,500.00. Stated inversely, the present exemption
retains only about PhP 184,600.00 of its original purchasing power, an
erosion of roughly twenty-six percent (26%). Inflation quietly increases their
tax burden through automatic withholding while their actual purchasing
power stays the same.
       In line with national policy priorities to grant individual tax relief, this
bill raises the annual tax-exempt income threshold to Three Hundred Fifty
Thousand Pesos (PhP 350,000.00). Estimates from the Department of
Finance project that the measure will benefit at least 3.13 million workers,
lifting approximately 1.2 million earners out of the income tax net entirely
and increasing the total tax-exempt workforce to roughly 6.3 million.3​
       This bill fully adopts the Three Hundred Fifty Thousand Peso (PhP
350,000.00) tax-exempt threshold. By recomputing every bracket base from
this new starting point, the schedule delivers a uniform Fifteen Thousand
Peso (PhP 15,000.00) tax reduction to all taxpayers above the exempt limit.
This design intentionally prevents a notch discontinuity at the threshold,
ensuring marginal income is not penalized and tax relief is extended across
all income bands.

       To prevent inflation from eroding these gains over time, Section 5
introduces a mandatory indexation mechanism beginning in the taxable
year 2030. Every three (3) years, the Secretary of Finance shall adjust all
income thresholds, fixed bracket tax amounts, and the tax-exempt benefit
ceiling upward using official Philippine Statistics Authority inflation data,
with downward adjustments expressly prohibited.​
​
​      Anchoring each cycle to the three (3) calendar years immediately
preceding publication ensures the adjustments rely on final, auditable data
without leaving gaps in inflation coverage. This mechanism satisfies the
completeness and sufficient-standard tests governing the valid delegation of
legislative authority under Abakada Guro Party List v. Ermita 4 because it
leaves the Executive with the purely ministerial function of factual
ascertainment and arithmetical computation.​
​
​    In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is respectfully and
urgently sought.

                           AN ACT
  RAISING THE TAX-EXEMPT INCOME THRESHOLD FOR INDIVIDUAL
    TAXPAYERS AND INSTITUTING A MECHANISM FOR PERIODIC
INFLATION INDEXATION, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTIONS 24
  AND 32 OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS
              AMENDED, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:
     SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Income
Tax Exemption Adjustment and Indexation Act.."
      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — Pursuant to Section 28(1), Article VI
of the Constitution, which mandates that the rule of taxation shall be
uniform and equitable and that Congress shall evolve a progressive system
of taxation, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect the
purchasing power of Filipino workers and their families, as guaranteed
under Section 3, Article XIII and Section 1, Article XII of the Constitution.
      Toward this end, the State shall increase the tax-exempt income
threshold of individual taxpayers, periodically and automatically adjust
income tax thresholds and ceilings to reflect changes in the consumer price
index without eroding real income through bracket creep, and strengthen
tax administration, compliance, and enforcement so that tax relief is
balanced with fiscal stability and revenue integrity.

       SEC. 8. Revision of Withholding Tax Tables. — The Secretary of
Finance, upon the recommendation of the Commissioner of Internal
Revenue, shall revise the withholding tax tables to conform to the schedule
under Section 24(A)(2)(a) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as
amended by this Act, not later than 1 December 2026, and thereafter not
later than 1 December of the year immediately preceding the effectivity of
any adjustment made under Section 24(A)(2)(d) thereof.​
​
​     SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance, upon the
recommendation of the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, shall promulgate
the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation:
Provided, That the non-issuance of implementing rules and regulations shall
not preclude the provisions of this Act from taking effect within the periods
herein prescribed.​
​
​      SEC. 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.​
​
​     SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are
hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.​
​
​     SEC. 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation. Provided, That the revised income tax schedules, thresholds,
and tax-exempt benefit ceilings shall take effect on 1 January 2027.​
