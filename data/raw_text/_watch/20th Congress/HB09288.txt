PEOPLES (NCIP), AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Many Indigenous Cultural Communities and Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) still
struggle to access justice. Courts are often far from their communities, legal processes
can be confusing, and language and cultural differences make it harder for them to be
heard. There is also frequent confusion about when to use their own customary laws,
go to the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), or ile cases in regular
courts. Because of this, disputes are delayed, cases are iled in the wrong place, and
the rights of ICCs/IPs are not fully protected.

The law already recognizes the right of ICCs/IPs to use their own justice systems and
traditions in resolving disputes. It also gives the NCIP authority over certain cases,
especially those involving members of the same community, after they have irst tried
to settle the matter using their customary practices. However, these rules are not
always clearly followed in practice, leading to confusion, repeated cases, and long
delays.

This bill aims to make justice more accessible and easier to understand for ICCs/IPs.
It strengthens coordination between customary justice systems, the NCIP, and regular

courts to ensure that cases are handled in the proper forum. It also proposes the
creation of Indigenous Peoples Justice Desks and special court schedules in areas with
large IP populations to provide guidance, reduce delays, and make the legal process
more responsive to their culture and needs.

By improving coordination and respecting indigenous traditions while upholding
national law, this measure helps ensure faster, fairer, and more meaningful justice for
ICCs/IPs. It promotes a system that they can trust and participate in.

respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Indigenous Peoples Access
to Justice Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to increase access
to justice for Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) by
strengthening culturally responsive dispute resolution, ensuring effective
coordination with customary justice systems, and improving the capacity of the
regular courts to hear and decide cases involving ICCs/IPs consistent with existing
laws and jurisprudence.

SECTION 3. Indigenous Peoples Justice Desks and Special Calendars. —

   (a) Creation. There is hereby established an Indigenous Peoples Justice Desk
       (IPJD) in the Regional Trial Courts (RTCs) and irst-level courts
       (MeTC/MTC/MCTC) in provinces and cities with signi icant ICC/IP

                                                                           3|Page
       populations, as may be identi ied by the Supreme Court in consultation with
       the NCIP.

   (b) Functions. The IPJD shall:

       1. Facilitate court user assistance and language/access needs for ICC/IP
          litigants;
       2. Coordinate referrals, where appropriate and allowed by law, to customary
          dispute resolution mechanisms and/or NCIP processes; and
       3. Assist in case management for cases involving ICCs/IPs through special
          calendars and monitoring to reduce delay.

   (c) Special Calendars. The Supreme Court may designate Special IP
       Calendars for eligible cases, to promote continuity of hearings and speedier
       disposition, without creating a separate court.

SECTION 4. Coordination with NCIP; Jurisdictional Guideposts. —

   (a) Consistency with jurisprudence. In determining whether a dispute should
       proceed before the NCIP or the regular courts, courts and prosecutors shall be
       guided by the Supreme Court’s rulings that NCIP jurisdiction is generally
       triggered only when the dispute arises between or among parties belonging to
       the same ICC/IP and where customary remedies have been exhausted, and that
       disputes involving parties from different ICCs/IPs or involving a non-ICC/IP
       fall under the jurisdiction of the regular courts.

   (b) No shield for crimes. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to divest the
       regular courts of criminal jurisdiction or to allow customary settlements to bar
       prosecution for crimes under national law, consistent with jurisprudence.

SECTION 5. Recognition of Tribal Courts in Autonomous Regions (Non-
derogation). —

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish or impair the authority to establish
or operate tribal courts as provided under existing organic acts, including:
     The Cordillera tribal court system established under RA 6766 (1989); and
     The tribal courts recognized in the Muslim Mindanao organic framework
       under RA 9054 (2001) and RA 6734 (1989).

SECTION 6. Rule-making; Training; Administrative Issuances. —

   (a) Supreme Court rules. Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the
       effectivity of this Act, the Supreme Court shall issue such rules and
       administrative issuances as may be necessary to implement this Act, including
       criteria for designating IPJDs and Special IP Calendars, consistent with existing
       procedural rules.

   (b) Capacity building. The Supreme Court, in coordination with the Philippine
       Judicial Academy (PHILJA) and the NCIP, shall develop continuing education
       modules on IP rights, customary law appreciation where applicable, and
       culturally responsive proceedings.

SECTION 7. Appropriation. — The amounts necessary to implement this Act shall
be charged against the current appropriations of the Judiciary and concerned
agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).

SECTION 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, other provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 10. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
