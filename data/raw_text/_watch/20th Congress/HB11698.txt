EXPLANATORY NOTE

The fisheries sector is of great importance to the livelihoods, food security and economic
development of the coastal communities in Zamboanga City; for many municipal and small-scale
fisherfolk fishing is not only a means of earning a living but a way of life which supports their
families and helps to supply local markets with cheap fish and other aquatic products.

Even though the sector is important, many fisherfolk still come up against ongoing
difficulties such as the high price of fishing equipment and supplies, limited access to ice and cold
storage, insufficient post-harvest infrastructure, and a reliance on intermediaries who usually set
the prices at which their catch is sold. Such problems could lead to large post-harvest losses, lower
incomes, and ongoing economic vulnerability among fishing families.

The lack of sufficient, easily accessible ice-making plants, cold storage facilities, and
suitable fish-handling facilities also affects the quality and market value of fishery products. If
proper preservation and storage are not available, fisherfolk might be forced to sell their catch right
away, even if the current market prices are bad. As a result, their ability to negotiate is reduced
and they are unable to get the full economic value from their daily catch.

On the other hand, although fisherfolk cooperatives and associations can assist their
members in pooling their resources, lowering operating costs, and gaining access to larger markets,
many of them need further technical, financial, and institutional support if they are to become
sustainable and competitive businesses. By strengthening these organizations, fisherfolk will be
able to take a more active part in the fisheries value chain and ensure better economic opportunities
for their communities.

Because of these concerns, the Zamboanga City Fisheries Livelihood and Market Access
Act proposes the creation of a comprehensive fisheries livelihood and market access scheme which
will give targeted aid to eligible fisherfolk, set up accessible ice plants and cold storage facilities,
strengthen the fisherfolk cooperatives, and enable direct market links to be established between
local fish producers and legitimate buyers.

The measure in question also supports fisheries processing and value addition, provides
technical and entrepreneurial training, improves market information, and establishes transparent
trading arrangements. As a result, fisherfolk will be better positioned to reduce post-harvest losses,
improve product quality, increase their share of the market value, and diversify their income
sources.

===== [page 2] =====
The Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources will implement the proposed program in
cooperation with the Zamboanga City Government and other relevant government agencies. The
aim of this collaborative method is to bring national fisheries development plans in line with the
real needs of local fishing communities and at the same time ensure compliance with the present
fisheries, environmental, procurement, and public financial management laws.

The measure in question also takes into account the importance of sustainable fisheries
management and will provide assistance to promote legal fishing methods, the responsible use of
marine resources, and the long-term viability of communities that depend on fishing. Special
attention should be paid to low-income fishing households, qualified small-scale fisherfolk,
fisherfolk cooperatives, and women who have fishing-related livelihoods.

The government could aid in the creation of a more inclusive and resilient fisheries
economy in Zamboanga City by funding productive equipment, post-harvest infrastructure,
cooperative businesses, and direct market access. The Act in question is meant not only to offer
immediate help with livelihoods but also to lay the groundwork for sustainable income generation,
the development of local enterprises, and better food security.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE ZAMBOANGA CITY FISHERIES LIVELIHOOD AND MARKET
ACCESS PROGRAM, PROVIDING GOVERNMENT ASSISTANCE FOR FISHING
EQUIPMENT, ICE PLANTS, COLD STORAGE FACILITIES, FISHERFOLK
COOPERATIVES, AND DIRECT MARKET ACCESS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Zamboanga City Fisheries

Livelihood and Market Access Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
and promote the welfare of Filipino fisherfolk, strengthen the fisheries sector, ensure food security,

expand livelihood opportunities, and promote equitable access to markets.

Toward this end, the State shall support the sustainable development of fisheries
livelihoods in Zamboanga City through the provision of appropriate fishing equipment, accessible
ice production and cold storage facilities, strengthened fisherfolk cooperatives, improved post-

harvest infrastructure, and direct market linkages.

The State shall likewise encourage responsible fisheries management, community-based
enterprise development, the participation of women in fisheries-related livelihoods, and the

equitable distribution of economic opportunities among fishing communities.

SEC. 3. Objectives. — This Act shall have the following objectives:

(a) Improve the productivity, safety, and income of municipal and small-scale fisherfolk in

Zamboanga City;

(b) Reduce post-harvest losses through accessible ice plants, cold storage, fish handling

facilities, and cold-chain services;

(c) Strengthen fisherfolk cooperatives and associations as viable enterprises for collective

purchasing, processing, storage, and marketing;

(d) Establish direct and transparent market linkages between fisherfolk and buyers,
including public markets, restaurants, hotels, supermarkets, institutional purchasers,

processors, and consumers;

(e) Improve access to livelihood assistance, enterprise development, technical training, and

appropriate financing programs;
(f) Promote value-added fisheries products and local employment; and

(g) Ensure that all assistance is consistent with sustainable fisheries management,

environmental protection, and applicable laws.

SEC. 4. Establishment of the Program. ~ There is hereby established the Zamboanga City

Fisheries Livelihood and Market Access Program, hereinafter referred to as the Program.

The Program shall be implemented by the Department of Agriculture (DA), through the

Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR), in coordination with the Zamboanga City
Government and other concerned national government agencies, local government units, and

qualified fisherfolk organizations.

The Program shall consist of the following components:
(a) Fishing Equipment and Safety Assistance;
(b) Ice Plant and Cold Storage Development;

(c) Fisherfolk Cooperative Development;

SEC. 5. Fishing Equipment and Safety Assistance. — The BFAR, in coordination with the

City Government, shall develop an assistance program for qualified municipal and small-scale

fisherfolk.
Assistance may include:
(a) Lawful fishing gear, nets, hooks, lines, traps, and related fishing implements;

(b) Personal flotation devices, life jackets, first-aid kits, and emergency communication

equipment;

(c) Insulated fish containers, food-grade storage boxes, weighing scales, and fish handling

equipment;
(d) Appropriate fish processing and value-adding equipment; and

(e) Other necessary fishing and livelihood equipment identified through community needs

assessments.

Assistance may be provided through grants, in-kind distribution, shared-use facilities,

equipment lending arrangements, or other lawful mechanisms.

The implementing agencies shall prioritize qualified fisherfolk with demonstrated
livelihood needs, particularly low-income households and communities with limited access to

productive equipment.

No equipment shall be provided for destructive fishing methods or activities prohibited by

applicable law.

SEC. 8. Direct Market Access and Fisheries Marketing Support. — The implementing

agencies, in partnership with the City Government, shall develop a direct market access system for

qualified fisherfolk and their organizations.

For this purpose, the Program may:

(a) Facilitate supply arrangements between fisherfolk cooperatives and public markets,
restaurants, hotels, supermarkets, processors, institutional buyers, and other legitimate

purchasers;

(b) Establish or support cooperative-managed fish trading centers and community-based

selling outlets;

(c) Facilitate participation in trade fairs, community markets, and other lawful marketing
activities;

(d) Develop a fisheries market information system providing information on prevailing

prices, buyer requirements, and market opportunities;

(e) Support digital ordering, electronic payment systems, and other appropriate marketing

(f) Assist fisherfolk organizations in complying with applicable product quality, food

(g) Promote transparent weighing, clear trading terms, timely payment, and voluntary

The Program shall encourage direct and collective marketing without imposing an

SEC, 9. Fisheries Processing and Value Addition. — The Program shall promote the
development of small and medium-scale fisheries enterprises through technical assistance,

training, and appropriate equipment support.

Eligible activities may include fish drying, smoking, deboning, filleting, packaging,

freezing, preparation of value-added products, and other lawful processing activities.

Women-led enterprises, community-based processing groups, and cooperatives shall be

encouraged to participate, subject to applicable eligibility requirements.

Processing facilities supported under this Act shall comply with applicable sanitation, food

safety, environmental, and business registration requirements.

SEC. 10. Training and Technical Assistance. — The BFAR, in coordination with the DA,
the City Government, the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, and other

appropriate agencies, shall provide training and technical assistance in:
(a) Responsible fishing practices and fisheries regulations;
(b) Fish handling, preservation, sanitation, and quality control;
(c) Cold storage and post-harvest management;
(d) Cooperative management and financial literacy;
(e) Entrepreneurship, processing, packaging, and value addition;
(f) Digital marketing and buyer negotiations; and

(g) Climate resilience, disaster preparedness, and sustainable livelihood diversification.

SEC. 11. Eligibility and Prioritization. — Beneficiaries shall be selected based on

transparent, objective, and publicly disclosed criteria.

Priority shall be given to:
(a) Qualified municipal and small-scale fisherfolk operating in Zamboanga City;

(b) Low-income fishing households and communities with limited access to livelihood

assistance;

(c) Registered fisherfolk cooperatives and associations with viable community-based

projects;
(d) Women engaged in fishing, processing, trading, and other fisheries-related livelihoods;
(e) Qualified beneficiaries affected by disasters or significant livelihood disruptions; and

(f) Applicants demonstrating compliance with applicable fisheries laws and responsible

resource management practices.

The implementing agencies shall coordinate with the City Government and concerned

barangays to verify eligibility and prevent duplication of assistance.

SEC. 12. Implementation and Interagency Coordination. - Within ninety (90) days from

the effectivity of this Act, the DA, through the BFAR, shall coordinate with the City Government

and other concerned agencies to prepare the initial implementation plan.

The plan shall include:
(a) A fisheries livelihood and infrastructure needs assessment;

(b) An inventory of existing ice plants, cold storage facilities, fish landing sites, and market

infrastructure;
(c) Identification of priority fishing communities and beneficiaries,
(d) A phased implementation schedule;

(e) A procurement, operations, and maintenance framework;

SEC. 13. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting. - The BFAR, in coordination with the

City Government, shall monitor and evaluate the implementation of the Program.

An annual report shall be submitted to the appropriate committees of the House of

Representatives and the Senate, containing information on:
(a) The number of beneficiaries assisted;
(b) Equipment distributed and facilities established or rehabilitated;
(c) Cooperatives supported and market linkages developed;
(d) Program expenditures and implementation status;

(e) Changes in post-harvest losses and beneficiary income, where reliable data are

available; and
(f) Implementation issues and recommendations for improvement.

The Program shall be subject to applicable government auditing, procurement, and

transparency requirements.

SEC. 14. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against available appropriations of the DA and BFAR, subject to applicable

budgeting and accounting laws.

Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary for the continued implementation of the

Program shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

The DA and BFAR may also pursue lawful funding partnerships, grants, and technical

assistance from other government agencies and development partners.

SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DA, through the BFAR, in consultation with the City Government, the
Department of Budget and Management, the Cooperative Development Authority, and
representatives of fisherfolk organizations, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary

for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 16. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or

invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive issuances, rules, and regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly, to the extent of such

inconsistency.

SEC. 18. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in

the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation, as required by law.
