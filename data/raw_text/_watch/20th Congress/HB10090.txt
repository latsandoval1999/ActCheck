                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to establish a Barangay Nutrition Worker position in every
barangay in the country. It institutionalizes a dedicated personnel for nutrition
promotion, disease prevention, and child development, and shall strengthen the
delivery of community-based nutrition services.

       The Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II)
reported that the stunting rate among children under five (5) years old has
reached twenty-five percent (25%). This represents one (1) in four (4) children in
the country. This is the first increase in a decade and classified malnutrition and
stunting as a high public health concern. These conditions impair physical
growth, cognitive development, educational attainment while imposing
long-term social and economic costs.

       Despite the enactment of Presidential Decree No. 1569, entitled
“Strengthening the Barangay Nutrition Program by Providing for a Barangay Nutrition
Scholar in Every Barangay, Providing Funds Therefor, and For Other Purposes”, its
reliance on a purely volunteer framework with token incentives has proven
insufficient against the current economic landscape and increasing public health
challenges of the country. Nearly five (5) decades after its implementation, the
evolving gravity of our nutritional crisis demands the transition to a volunteer
system into a professional and properly compensated workforce.

       Although nutrition programs exist at the national and local level, their
implementation is often impeded by the absence of personnel exclusively tasked
to oversee nutrition interventions in the community. This bill requires every
barangay to appoint a qualified Barangay Nutrition Worker and establishes
standards for recruitment, training, supervision, benefits and incentives. It also
ensures that each community has trained personnel dedicated to
nutrition-related services and interventions.
      Strengthening the nutrition workforce at the barangay level is an
investment to public health and human capital. Placing a barangay nutrition
worker in every barangay shall improve access to essential nutrition services,
support healthier communities, and contribute to the reduction of malnutrition
nationwide.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                         AN ACT
CREATING THE POSITION OF BARANGAY NUTRITION WORKER IN
EVERY BARANGAY, PROVIDING INCENTIVES THERETO, AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Barangay Nutrition
Worker Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby the declared policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
consciousness among them. The State recognizes that proper nutrition is
fundamental to human development and poverty reduction, and shall
recommend policies to address threats of malnutrition and stunting. Towards
this end, there shall be appointed a Barangay Nutrition Worker in every
barangay to ensure that essential nutrition services are delivered efficiently at the
local level.

      SEC. 3. Barangay Nutrition Worker. – The Barangay Nutrition Worker shall
be appointed by the Mayor, with the concurrence of the majority of all
Sangguniang Bayan or Sangguniang Panlungsod Members, and the Sangguniang
Barangay concerned. The Barangay Nutrition Worker shall be responsible for
delivering nutrition services and other related activities such as community
health, backyard food production, environmental sanitation, culture,
supplemental feeding, and responsible parenthood to the barangay.

       SEC. 4. Services. – The Barangay Nutrition Worker, while undergoing
training under the Barangay Nutrition Program, shall serve the barangay on a
voluntary basis for at least six (6) months. Thereafter, the NNC shall assess his or
her performance and make the necessary recommendation for his or her
appointment.

      SEC. 5. Qualifications. – The Barangay Nutrition Worker shall possess the
following qualifications:

         a.​ ﻿A Filipino citizen and a bona fide resident of the barangay for at
      least one (1) year;

         b.​ Able to communicate effectively in the local language of the
      community;

         c.​ ﻿At least eighteen (18) years old, but not more than sixty (60) years
      old;

         d.​ At least a graduate of senior high school, or its equivalent under the
      Alternative Learning, as provided under Republic Act No. 11510,
      otherwise known as the “Alternative Learning System Act”; and

         e.​ Mentally and physically fit to perform the duties of the position, as
      certified by a licensed government physician.

      SEC. 6. Recruitment. – The Barangay Nutrition Worker shall be recruited
from within the barangay and shall be tested on the basis of mental ability and
work aptitude.

       SEC. 7. Training. – Before actual barangay service, the Barangay Nutrition
Worker shall undergo appropriate and sufficient training on selected topics, such
as nutrition, health, food production, and environmental sanitation, based on
defined task expectations to be conducted by a provincial training team in
coordination with the municipal and barangay nutrition committee. This training
shall be supplemented by a practicum to be conducted in the barangay where the
worker is assigned, and such activity shall be in coordination with the local
training team.

​      SEC. 8. Benefits. – The Barangay Nutrition Worker shall be entitled to a
training stipend, meal, and travel allowance of at least Three hundred pesos
(Php 300.00) per day. During the period of volunteer service prior to his or her
appointment, the Barangay Nutrition Worker shall be entitled to an allowance of
Five thousand pesos (Php 5,000.00) every month.

      The Barangay Nutrition Worker shall be granted a civil service eligibility
equivalent to second grade after the completion of at least two (2) years of
continuous and satisfactory service in the barangay.

      SEC. 9. Incentives. – To further enhance the nutrition program, the NNC, in
coordination with the Commission on Higher Education, shall formulate a
scholarship program where incumbent and deserving barangay nutrition

workers shall be given the opportunity to avail of a scholarship grant in any state
college or university an pursue a course relevant to his or her present position.

      SEC. 10. Nutrition Committees. – The organization of local nutrition
committees and the implementation of their respective nutrition program shall
be the responsibility of the provincial/city nutrition committee headed by the
Governor/ Mayor as Chairman, and a municipal nutrition committee headed by
the Mayor as Chairman.

       SEC. 11. Local Training Team. – The local training team of the municipality
or city shall be composed of two (2) members chosen from among the members
of the municipal or city nutrition committee. This team shall follow up the
activities of the trained Barangay Nutrition Worker.

       SEC. 12. Role of the Mayor. – The Municipal or City Mayor shall initiate
activities to implement the nutrition program within the municipality or city and
organize the local training teams, and may recommend the appropriation by the
municipality or city of a local counterpart in the form of additional allowance for
the Barangay Nutrition Worker.

       SEC. 13. Barangay Nutrition Workers Staff Secretariat. – There shall be a staff
secretariat in every province or city which shall monitor the program, identify its
problems, and recommend possible solutions thereto.

       SEC. 14. Role of the National Nutrition Council. – The NNC shall formulate
rules and regulations, plans and programs within the limits set forth in this Act,
and allocate the funds to the various provinces and/or agencies for disbursement
in accordance with the financial plan of the Barangay Nutrition Program.

       SEC. 15. Donations. – For purposes of this Program, the NNC and local
government units are authorized to receive grants, donations, gifts, or bequests
from various government agencies and government corporations which shall be
tax exempt.

       SEC. 16. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act is hereby appropriated from any available funds in
the National Treasury or from the Special Activities Fund, and authorized to be
released. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

       LGUs are likewise authorized to appropriate such amount necessary for

shall appropriate as part of its annual budget for personal services the necessary
amount of Five thousand pesos (Php 5,000.00) per month, per Barangay
Nutrition Worker: Provided, further, That third- to fifth-class municipalities shall
