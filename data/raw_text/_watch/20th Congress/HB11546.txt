                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution explicitly guarantees the right of the people to health and
mandates the State to make essential goods, health, and other social services
available to all at an affordable cost. However, a recurring issue in healthcare
facilities across the country involves patients who, despite having been
medically cleared for discharge, remain confined due to administrative delays
and institutional processes beyond their control.

These administrative delays include, but are not limited to, protracted
discharge clearance workflows, delayed medical coding and billing
preparation, unverified health insurance or PhilHealth coverage claims,
delayed processing of government financial assistance (such as Malasakit
Centers or DSWD medical aid), and system or software downtime.

Under current practices, patients are frequently billed for extra days of room
stay, nursing services, and administrative room fees accrued during these
forced waiting periods. It is inherently unjust to financially penalize patients
and their families for delays caused solely by institutional inefficiency, system
failures, or third-party administrative processing.

This bill seeks to address this injustice by prohibiting hospitals and medical
facilities from imposing any room, board, nursing, or daily administrative
charges on medically cleared patients whose physical discharge is delayed
solely due to administrative reasons beyond their control. By enacting this
measure, healthcare institutions will be incentivized to streamline their
discharge protocols, ensuring fair treatment for patients while protecting
families from unconscionable medical expenses.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT PROHIBITING HOSPITALS AND MEDICAL FACILITIES FROM
IMPOSING ROOM, BOARD, AND DAILY ADMINISTRATIVE CHARGES ON
PATIENTS CLEARED FOR DISCHARGE BUT REMAIN CONFINED DUE TO
     ADMINISTRATIVE REASONS BEYOND THEIR CONTROL, AND
        PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Fair Hospital
Discharge and Anti-Administrative Penalty Act."


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect patients
and their families against unnecessary, unfair, and unconscionable healthcare
expenses. While recognizing the operational requirements of healthcare
facilities, the State shall ensure that patients are not subjected to additional
financial burdens resulting from administrative inefficiencies, procedural
delays, or third-party processing issues beyond their control.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   1. Medical Clearance for Discharge - refers to the official written order
      issued and signed by the attending physician certifying that the patient
      is medically fit to leave the hospital or medical facility.

   2. Administrative Reasons        - refer to any process, workflow, or
      circumstance not related to the patient’s medical condition or clinical
      care that prevents the patient from physically leaving the facility,
      including but not limited to:

            Delays in the preparation, auditing, or release of the final itemized
            bill or discharge documents;
            Delays in the verification, computation, or processing of
            PhilHealth, Private Health Insurance (HMO), or social safety net
            benefits;

            Delays in the approval or release of financial assistance from
            government agencies (e.g., DSWD, PCSO, Malasakit Centers, LGU
            assistance);

            Hospital IT/billing system outages,         technical   failures,   or
            administrative staffing shortages; and

            Delays caused by internal institutional clearances or disputes
            between the facility and third-party payers.

   3. Hospital Charges - refer to daily room rates, board fees, nursing care
      fees, equipment rental charges, and administrative overhead fees
      assessed per day or hour of stay.

SEC. 4. Prohibition on Administrative Charges. —

   1. Once an attending physician issues a written Medical Clearance for
      Discharge, no hospital, medical clinic, or healthcare facility shall assess,
      impose, or collect room, board, nursing, or daily administrative charges
      for any time accrued after the issuance of said clearance, provided that
      the delay in physical discharge is attributable to Administrative Reasons
      beyond the patient's control.

   2. The exact time and date of the medical discharge order must be clearly
      stamped and recorded in the patient’s official medical and billing record.

   3. Charges for necessary medical treatment, medicines, or emergency care
      rendered after discharge clearance due to a sudden change in the
      patient's medical condition shall be exempt from this prohibition.

SEC. 5. Maximum Turnaround Time for Discharge Billing. — Hospitals and
healthcare facilities shall establish a streamlined discharge billing protocol.
The final billing statement and discharge documents must be completed within
four (4) hours from the time the attending physician issues the official
discharge order. Any confinement extending beyond this period due to pending
administrative processes shall automatically fall under the prohibition
specified in Section 4.

SEC. 6. Penalties. — Any hospital officer, administrator, or employee who
violates the provisions of this Act shall be subject to the following penalties:

   1. First Offense: A fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) to One Hundred
      Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00).
   2. Second Offense: A fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00) to
      Three Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱300,000.00).

   3. Subsequent Offenses: A fine of Five Hundred Thousand Pesos
      (₱500,000.00) and/or suspension or revocation of the hospital’s
      operational license by the Department of Health (DOH).

SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in consultation
with the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), associations of
private and public hospitals, and patient advocacy groups, shall promulgate
the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this
Act.

SEC. 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, administrative orders,
rules, and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 10. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
