AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON THE PAYMENT OF
LOANS, MORTGAGES, AND OTHER CREDIT OBLIGATIONS DURING A
DECLARED STATE OF CALAMITY, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines is frequently affected by typhoons, floods, earthquakes,
volcanic eruptions, pandemics, and other natural or human-induced hazards.
These calamities may result in the loss of lives and property, displacement of
families, interruption of livelihoods, and temporary closure of businesses. For
many individuals and households, the resulting loss of income significantly
affects their ability to meet existing loan and credit obligations.

During a declared state of calamity, affected borrowers may be compelled
to prioritize food, shelter, medical needs, relocation, and the repair or
reconstruction of damaged property. Requiring the immediate payment of loans
during this period may further aggravate their financial hardship and expose
them to penalties, additional interest, collection efforts, and possible legal action.
Republic Act No. 10121, or the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Act of 2010, recognizes the need for a comprehensive and
coordinated system of disaster prevention, preparedness, response, and
recovery. Consistent with this policy, the State must adopt measures that protect
affected communities and enable them to recover without being placed under
additional and avoidable financial distress.

This bill seeks to establish a temporary moratorium on the payment and
enforcement of loan and other credit obligations of natural persons residing in
areas under a declared state of calamity. The proposed moratorium shall apply
for a period not exceeding one hundred twenty (120) days from the declaration
of the state of calamity, or until the declaration is lifted, whichever occurs earlier.

The measure does not condone or extinguish the borrower’s obligation.
Instead, it temporarily defers payment and allows the accumulated liability to be
settled through not less than six (6) equal monthly installments without
penalties or interest arising from the deferment. Borrowers who are able and

willing to continue making payments may voluntarily do so during the
moratorium period.

By providing temporary financial relief during calamities, this measure will
help affected individuals and families preserve their limited resources for
essential needs, prevent the further deterioration of household finances, and
support their recovery and rehabilitation. It will also promote fairness and social
protection while preserving the underlying creditor-borrower relationship.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

House Bill No. 11574

INTRODUCED BY REP. PATRICK MICHAEL “PM” D. VARGAS

AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON THE PAYMENT OF
LOANS, MORTGAGES, AND OTHER CREDIT OBLIGATIONS DURING A
DECLARED STATE OF CALAMITY, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Calamity Payment
Moratorium on Loans and Credit Obligations Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to promote the general welfare, public safety, health, and economic security of
the Filipino people in times of calamity, natural or man-made. In line with this

declared policy, the State recognizes the need to provide adequate relief and
protection to Filipinos in emergency or crisis situations.

SECTION 3. Scope. This Act shall cover all natural persons whose residence is
located in barangays, municipalities, cities, provinces, or regions under a State
of Calamity, and who have loan obligations in the said affected locality.

SECTION 4. Declaration of State of Calamity. The declaration by the
President of the Philippines, upon the recommendation of the National Disaster
Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC), or the Sangguniang
Bayan/ Panlungsod, upon the recommendation of the Municipality/City Mayor,
or the Sanggunian Panlalawigan, upon the recommendation of the Provincial
Governor, as the case may be, of a state of calamity in accordance with Section
16 of Republic Act No. 10121 otherwise known as the “Philippine Disaster Risk


===== [page 4] =====
Reduction Management Act of 2010”; Republic Act No. 11332, otherwise known
as the “Mandatory Reporting of Notifiable Diseases and Health Events of Public
Health Concern Act”; and other relevant laws, rules, and regulations, and the
lifting thereof shall be the basis for the effectivity of the measure provided under
this Act.

SECTION 5. Moratorium on the First One Hundred Twenty Days of a State
of Calamity. - There shall be a moratorium on all payment obligations for no
more than one hundred twenty (120) days following the date of declaration of a
state of calamity. Lending companies, or public or private creditors, are
prohibited from exacting payment during the entire duration of the moratorium.
Provided, that no penalties or interests shall be collected on the deferred
payments made pursuant to this Act. Provided further, that nothing in this
section shall prevent voluntary payment by customers. Provided, finally, that the
moratorium shall be lifted simultaneously when the state of calamity is lifted
within one hundred twenty days from the date of its declaration or earlier.

SECTION 6. Installment Payment for the Deferred Liability. - Any amount
due and demandable during the moratorium period of one hundred twenty days
following the declaration of a state of calamity under Section 5 of this Act shall
be payable in not less than six (6) equal monthly installments, without interest,
which shall accrue a month after the cessation of a state of calamity.

SECTION 7. Penalty. - Lending companies, or public or private creditors found
in violation of this law shall be punishable with a fine of not less than Fifty
Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) and not more than One Hundred Thousand Pesos
(P100,000.00) per infraction.

SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
rules and regulations or parts thereof which are in conflict or inconsistent with
the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. In the event that any provision of this Act is

declared unconstitutional, the validity of the other provisions shall not be
affected by such declaration.

SECTION 10. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
