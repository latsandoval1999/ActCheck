                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Institutions occupy a position of trust and authority. Schools educate students,
employers supervise employees, athletic organizations train athletes, government agencies
exercise public authority, and numerous other institutions direct, train, supervise, and protect
individuals under their care. The effective performance of these functions necessarily requires
the exercise of discipline, supervision, leadership, and accountability.

        Although Philippine law penalizes various offenses, including homicide and physical
injuries under the Revised Penal Code (Act No. 3815), coercion and threats under the same
Code, sexual offenses under Republic Act No. 8353 (The Anti-Rape Law of 1997), as
amended, and Republic Act No. 11648, hazing under Republic Act No. 11053 (Anti-Hazing
Act of 2018), bullying in educational institutions under Republic Act No. 10627
(Anti-Bullying Act of 2013), sexual harassment under Republic Act No. 7877 (Anti-Sexual
Harassment Act of 1995) and Republic Act No. 11313 (Safe Spaces Act), and unsafe working
conditions under Republic Act No. 11058 (An Act Strengthening Compliance with
Occupational Safety and Health Standards), these laws principally define and penalize
specific unlawful acts or regulate particular sectors.

         However, no general law presently establishes uniform statutory obligations
applicable across institutions to ensure the timely receipt, reporting, preservation of evidence,
investigation, documentation, and appropriate institutional response to allegations of serious
misconduct. As a result, institutional responses often vary significantly, resulting in delayed
investigations, inadequate protection for complainants and witnesses, loss or destruction of
evidence, inconsistent accountability measures, and diminished public confidence in
institutional governance.

         This bill seeks to fill that legislative gap by establishing minimum standards of
institutional accountability applicable to both public and private institutions. It imposes
affirmative duties upon covered institutions to receive and investigate reports of serious
institutional misconduct, preserve relevant evidence, protect complainants, victims,
witnesses, and whistleblowers from retaliation, implement appropriate interim protective
measures, maintain accountability mechanisms, and cooperate with competent authorities
whenever required by law.

        This measure does not create new crimes for conduct already punishable under
existing laws, nor does it diminish the authority of institutions to impose lawful and
reasonable disciplinary, supervisory, academic, athletic, military, employment, or training
measures. Rather, it establishes a uniform governance framework governing how institutions
are expected to prevent, receive, investigate, document, and respond to serious misconduct
occurring within their respective jurisdictions.

        By strengthening institutional accountability while preserving legitimate institutional
authority, this measure seeks to promote transparency, integrity, public trust, and the
protection of every person under the care or supervision of a covered institution.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING A UNIFORM FRAMEWORK FOR INSTITUTIONAL
ACCOUNTABILITY IN THE PREVENTION OF, RESPONSE TO, AND
INVESTIGATION OF REPORTABLE INSTITUTIONAL MISCONDUCT,
PRESCRIBING THE DUTIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF COVERED
INSTITUTIONS, PROVIDING PROTECTION FOR COMPLAINANTS, VICTIMS,
WITNESSES, AND WHISTLEBLOWERS, PRESCRIBING PENALTIES FOR
INSTITUTIONAL VIOLATIONS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Institutional Accountability
and Protection Act."

         Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - t is hereby declared the policy of the State to uphold
and protect the dignity, life, health, safety, security, and well-being of all persons participating
in, employed by, enrolled in, undergoing training under, or otherwise subject to the authority,
supervision, or control of institutions. The State recognizes that institutions perform essential
educational, governmental, commercial, athletic, military, professional, religious, civic,
charitable, and other legitimate functions that necessarily require the exercise of authority,
supervision, discipline, leadership, and training. Such authority is indispensable and shall be
respected and preserved. Towards this end, the State shall establish a uniform framework of
institutional accountability by requiring covered institutions to adopt effective systems for the
prevention, reporting, documentation, preservation of evidence, investigation, resolution, and
monitoring of Reportable Institutional Misconduct; protect complainants, victims, witnesses,
and whistleblowers from retaliation; and ensure that institutional authority is exercised in a
manner consistent with law, accountability, transparency, due process, and respect for human
dignity. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish or impair the authority of institutions
to impose lawful, reasonable, proportionate, and good-faith disciplinary, supervisory,
academic, athletic, military, employment, or training measures necessary to accomplish their
legitimate objectives, provided that such authority is exercised consistently with this Act and
other applicable laws.

       Section 3. Objectives. - This Act seeks to:

       (a) establish a uniform statutory framework governing institutional accountability in
responding to Reportable Institutional Misconduct;
        (b) prescribe the minimum duties and responsibilities of covered institutions in
receiving, documenting, investigating, resolving, and monitoring reports of institutional
misconduct;

       (c) protect complainants, victims, witnesses, and whistleblowers against retaliation;

         (d) require the preservation of evidence and the maintenance of appropriate
institutional records;

        (e) promote transparency, accountability, and due process in institutional
investigations;

       (f) strengthen public confidence in institutional governance; and

        (g) complement existing criminal, civil, labor, administrative, educational, and special
laws by regulating institutional responses to misconduct without diminishing liabilities
arising under such laws.

       Section 4. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

        (a) “Reportable Institutional Misconduct” refers to any allegation or report of conduct
occurring within or in connection with a Covered Institution which, if true, may constitute: (i)
a criminal offense; (ii) a violation of law or regulation; (iii) serious misconduct; (iv) gross
abuse of authority; (v) sexual misconduct; (vi) physical violence; (vii) hazing or
hazing-related conduct; (viii) bullying; (ix) retaliation against a complainant or witness; or (x)
any other act which the Covered Institution is required by law or its own policies to
investigate.

        (b) “Covered Institution” refers to any public or private entity, organization,
establishment,    association,  school,   college,   university, government        agency,
government-owned or controlled corporation, local government unit, business establishment,
athletic organization, professional training institution, religious organization, civic
organization, volunteer organization, or other organized body exercising authority or
supervision over Covered Persons.

        (c) “Covered Person” refers to any student, employee, applicant, trainee, intern,
apprentice, resident, athlete, volunteer, member, participant, or other individual subject to the
authority or supervision of a Covered Institution.

       (d) “Institutional Protection Officer” refers to the officer designated under this Act to
oversee institutional compliance, receive complaints, coordinate investigations, and ensure
compliance with statutory duties.

        (e) “Retaliation” refers to any adverse action taken against a person because that
person reported, intended to report, opposed, refused to participate in, or cooperated in the
investigation of Reportable Institutional Misconduct.

         Section 5. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all public and private Covered
Institutions and to all Covered Persons, regardless of the nature of their relationship with the
institution. This Act shall govern the institutional response to any Reportable Institutional
Misconduct that:

       (a)​    occurs within the premises of a Covered Institution;

       (b)​    occurs during any activity, program, event, training, internship, practicum,
competition, meeting, travel, outreach, field activity, or other undertaking sponsored,
authorized, organized, supervised, or officially recognized by a Covered Institution;

        (c)​   occurs through the use of information and communications technologies,
social media platforms, electronic communications, or other digital means where such
conduct arises out of or is reasonably connected with the activities, operations, functions, or
relationships within a Covered Institution; or

        (d)​    otherwise arises from, is connected with, or substantially affects the
operations, functions, members, employees, students, trainees, athletes, volunteers,
participants, or other Covered Persons of a Covered Institution.

        The applicability of this Act shall not be affected by the existence of concurrent
criminal, civil, administrative, labor, educational, or disciplinary proceedings arising from the
same act or omission. Compliance with the duties and obligations imposed under this Act
shall be without prejudice to any liability or remedy available under existing laws.

       Section 6. Duty of Institutional Accountability – Every Covered Institution shall
exercise reasonable diligence in preventing, detecting, documenting, investigating, and
responding to Reportable Institutional Misconduct occurring within or in connection with its
operations, activities, programs, or functions. For this purpose, every Covered Institution
shall:

       (a) adopt and implement written policies and procedures consistent with this Act;

      (b) establish accessible, confidential, and secure mechanisms for the receipt of reports
and complaints;

         (c) receive and act upon all reports of Reportable Institutional Misconduct promptly,
fairly, impartially, and in good faith;

        (d) undertake appropriate measures to preserve relevant evidence and records upon
receipt of a report or complaint;

        (e) protect complainants, victims, witnesses, and other persons participating in an
investigation from retaliation, intimidation, harassment, or undue interference;

        (f) conduct or cause the conduct of a timely, impartial, and appropriately documented
investigation in accordance with this Act and other applicable laws;

        (g) implement such interim measures as may be reasonably necessary to protect
persons, preserve evidence, prevent further harm, or maintain the integrity of the
investigation;
         (h) take appropriate administrative, disciplinary, corrective, or remedial action
whenever warranted by the results of an investigation, without prejudice to any criminal,
civil, or other liability arising under existing laws;

       (i) cooperate in good faith with law enforcement agencies, regulatory authorities, or
other government agencies having jurisdiction over the subject matter of the report or
complaint; and

       (j) maintain records of reports, investigations, actions taken, and resolutions in
accordance with this Act, applicable laws, and data privacy regulations.

       The duties imposed under this Act are mandatory and shall be exercised
independently of, and without prejudice to, any criminal, civil, administrative, labor,
educational, or disciplinary proceedings arising from the same act or omission.

        Section 7. Institutional Accountability Policy - Every Covered Institution shall
adopt, implement, disseminate, and periodically review a written Institutional Accountability
Policy consistent with the provisions of this Act. At a minimum, the Institutional
Accountability Policy shall provide for:

       (a) the designation of the office or officer responsible for receiving reports of
Reportable Institutional Misconduct;

       (b) accessible, confidential, and secure procedures for reporting complaints;

        (c) procedures governing the receipt, documentation, evaluation, investigation, and
resolution of reports;

       (d) measures for the immediate protection of complainants, victims, witnesses, and
other persons who may be at risk of retaliation or further harm;

       (e) procedures for the preservation, collection, custody, and documentation of
evidence;

        (f) standards ensuring that investigations are conducted promptly, fairly, impartially,
and in accordance with due process;

       (g) measures to protect the confidentiality of complainants, victims, witnesses, and
records, subject to applicable laws and the requirements of due process;

       (h) safeguards prohibiting retaliation against complainants, victims, witnesses, or any
person who, in good faith, reports or participates in proceedings under this Act;

        (i) procedures for referring matters to law enforcement agencies, regulatory bodies, or
other competent authorities whenever required by law;

       (j) administrative or disciplinary measures that may be imposed upon persons found
responsible for violations of institutional rules or policies, without prejudice to any liability
under existing laws;
        (k) regular education, orientation, and training programs for officers, employees,
students, members, trainees, athletes, volunteers, and other Covered Persons regarding the
rights, duties, and responsibilities established under this Act; and

       (l) such other measures as may be necessary to ensure effective compliance with this
Act and its implementing rules and regulations.

       Every Covered Institution shall make its Institutional Accountability Policy readily
accessible to all Covered Persons through appropriate means, including publication in
employee or student handbooks, institutional manuals, official websites, electronic
communication platforms, or other effective modes of dissemination.

        Section 8. Designation of Responsible Office or Officer - Every Covered Institution
shall designate an existing office or appoint a Responsible Officer who shall be accountable
for the implementation and oversight of the requirements of this Act. The Responsible Office
or Responsible Officer shall:

       (a) receive reports and complaints involving Reportable Institutional Misconduct;

        (b) ensure the prompt documentation and appropriate referral of reports and
complaints in accordance with this Act and the institution's Institutional Accountability
Policy;

       (c) monitor compliance with the procedures and safeguards prescribed under this Act;

        (d) coordinate, where appropriate, with the persons or offices authorized to conduct
investigations;

       (e) oversee the implementation of interim protective measures authorized under this
Act or under institutional policies;

       (f) ensure the preservation of records and evidence relating to reported incidents;

       (g) maintain records of reports received and actions taken, subject to applicable laws
on confidentiality, records management, and data privacy;

        (h) coordinate, whenever necessary, with law enforcement agencies, regulatory
authorities, or other competent government agencies; and

       (i) perform such other functions as may be necessary to ensure compliance with this
Act and its implementing rules and regulations.

         A Covered Institution may designate an existing office or officer to discharge the
functions provided under this Section. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to require the
creation of a new plantilla position, office, or organizational unit solely for purposes of
compliance with this Act. The designation of a Responsible Office or Responsible Officer
shall not relieve the head of the Covered Institution or any other person exercising
institutional authority from any duty or liability imposed by this Act or by other applicable
laws.
        Section 9. Reporting Mechanisms - Every Covered Institution shall establish and
maintain accessible, reliable, confidential, and secure mechanisms for the receipt of reports
and complaints involving Reportable Institutional Misconduct. The reporting mechanisms
shall, at a minimum:

       (a) permit reports to be made in writing, electronically, verbally, or through any other
reasonable means accessible to Covered Persons;

       (b) allow reports to be made by the complainant, the victim, a witness, a parent or
legal guardian, an authorized representative, or any person who has reasonable grounds to
believe that Reportable Institutional Misconduct has occurred or is likely to occur;

       (c) ensure that reports are received promptly and documented in a manner sufficient to
preserve their substance and material details;

      (d) protect, to the extent permitted by law, the identity and personal information of
complainants, victims, witnesses, and other persons providing information;

       (e) acknowledge receipt of reports within a reasonable period and inform the reporting
party of the applicable procedures under the institution's Institutional Accountability Policy,
whenever practicable;

        (f) be readily accessible to all Covered Persons and made known through appropriate
policies, manuals, electronic platforms, websites, bulletin boards, orientations, or other
effective means of communication; and

       (g) be administered in a manner that is fair, impartial, and consistent with the
requirements of this Act and other applicable laws.

       No Covered Institution shall refuse to receive or document a report solely because it is
informal, anonymous, or is not made using a prescribed form or format. However, the
anonymity or informality of a report may be considered in determining the appropriate course
of action, the extent of investigation, and the weight to be accorded to the information
received.

        Nothing in this Section shall preclude a Covered Institution from requiring the
execution of a sworn complaint or supporting documents where necessary for the institution
of formal disciplinary proceedings, provided that the absence thereof shall not justify the
outright refusal to receive, record, or make an appropriate preliminary assessment of the
report.

         Section 10. Receipt of Reports - Every Covered Institution shall promptly receive and
appropriately document every report of Reportable Institutional Misconduct. Upon receipt of
a report, the Responsible Office or Responsible Officer shall conduct a preliminary
assessment for the sole purpose of determining the appropriate institutional response under
this Act. Such preliminary assessment shall not constitute a determination of liability nor the
commencement of formal disciplinary proceedings. No report shall be refused solely because
it was submitted anonymously, electronically, informally, or without supporting evidence.
The institution may require such additional information as may reasonably be necessary to
facilitate further proceedings in accordance with its Institutional Accountability Policy.
        Section 11. Institutional Investigation - Every Covered Institution shall, upon
determining that a report involves Reportable Institutional Misconduct, conduct or cause the
conduct of an impartial, objective, and good-faith investigation consistent with its
Institutional Accountability Policy, the requirements of due process, and other applicable
laws. The investigation shall be undertaken by the appropriate office, committee, disciplinary
body, or other person authorized under the institution's rules. The Responsible Office or
Responsible Officer designated under this Act may coordinate or oversee compliance with
this Act but shall not be required to conduct the investigation where another office or body is
vested with such authority under law or institutional policy. In conducting an investigation,
the Covered Institution shall exercise reasonable diligence to ascertain the relevant facts,
preserve the integrity of the proceedings, safeguard the rights of all persons concerned, and
ensure that complainants, respondents, victims, and witnesses are treated fairly and with due
regard for their dignity and legal rights.

        Nothing in this Act shall be construed to supplant or interfere with investigative
procedures prescribed by law, collective bargaining agreements, civil service rules, academic
regulations, military regulations, professional disciplinary rules, or other special laws,
provided that such procedures are not inconsistent with the minimum standards established
under this Act.

        Section 12. Preservation of Evidence - Upon receipt of a report involving Reportable
Institutional Misconduct, every Covered Institution shall exercise reasonable diligence to
preserve all evidence, records, documents, data, communications, recordings, and other
materials that are relevant or reasonably likely to be relevant to the proper investigation or
resolution of the matter. No officer, employee, member, or person exercising Institutional
Authority shall knowingly destroy, alter, conceal, suppress, falsify, or otherwise interfere with
the preservation or integrity of evidence relating to any Reportable Institutional Misconduct.

        Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize the unlawful collection, disclosure,
or processing of personal information or privileged communications, nor to diminish the
rights of any person under existing laws relating to data privacy, confidentiality, privilege, or
due process.

        Section. 13. Protection Against Retaliation - No Covered Institution, nor any person
exercising Institutional Authority, shall directly or indirectly retaliate against any
complainant, victim, witness, reporting person, or any individual who, in good faith, reports,
discloses, opposes, refuses to participate in, cooperates with, or provides information in
relation to Reportable Institutional Misconduct.

        Retaliation under this Act includes any adverse action, whether actual or threatened,
that is intended to penalize or deter a person from exercising rights under this Act or from
participating in any institutional or official proceeding relating to Reportable Institutional
Misconduct.

        A Covered Institution shall take reasonable measures to prevent retaliation and, where
appropriate, adopt interim measures necessary to protect complainants, victims, witnesses,
and other persons participating in an investigation, consistent with due process and the rights
of all parties.
        Any act of retaliation committed in violation of this Section shall constitute a separate
violation of this Act, without prejudice to any criminal, civil, administrative, labor,
educational, or disciplinary liability arising under existing laws.

         Section. 14. Corrective and Remedial Measures - Upon completion of an
institutional investigation, every Covered Institution shall take such lawful, appropriate, and
proportionate corrective or remedial measures as may be warranted by the facts established
and the applicable laws, rules, regulations, or institutional policies.

        Corrective or remedial measures may include the imposition of disciplinary sanctions,
the adoption of preventive measures, the modification of institutional policies or procedures,
the provision of appropriate assistance to affected persons, the implementation of additional
safeguards, or such other actions as may be reasonably necessary to prevent the recurrence of
similar incidents.

         Nothing in this Act shall be construed to require a Covered Institution to impose
disciplinary sanctions where the evidence does not reasonably establish a violation of law or
institutional policy. Likewise, nothing herein shall preclude the institution from referring the
matter to the appropriate government agency, regulatory body, or law enforcement authority
whenever required by law.

       The corrective or remedial measures undertaken pursuant to this Section shall be
without prejudice to any criminal, civil, administrative, labor, educational, or other
proceedings arising from the same act or omission.

        Section 15. Institutional Obstruction - No Covered Institution, nor any person
exercising Institutional Authority, shall knowingly and willfully obstruct the implementation
of this Act or materially interfere with the institutional response to Reportable Institutional
Misconduct.

        Institutional Obstruction exists when a Covered Institution or any person exercising
Institutional Authority knowingly and willfully commits any act or omission that materially
impairs the receipt, documentation, preservation of evidence, investigation, resolution, or
reporting of Reportable Institutional Misconduct required under this Act.

       Liability under this Section shall be independent of, and without prejudice to, any
criminal, civil, administrative, labor, educational, or other liability arising under existing
laws. The absence of a finding of liability against any respondent in the underlying complaint
shall not, by itself, preclude liability under this Section where the elements of Institutional
Obstruction are otherwise established.

        Nothing in this Section shall be construed to prohibit the lawful exercise of
constitutional rights, the observance of due process, the protection of privileged or
confidential information as provided by law, or the good-faith performance of official duties.

        Section 16. Enforcement - The provisions of this Act shall be enforced by the
appropriate government agency or regulatory authority having jurisdiction over the Covered
Institution. Such agency or authority shall exercise its existing powers under applicable laws
to ensure compliance with this Act and may issue such orders, directives, or other lawful
issuances as may be necessary for its effective implementation.
       Nothing in this Act shall be construed to create a new regulatory agency or to
diminish, enlarge, or otherwise affect the existing jurisdiction or regulatory authority of any
government agency.

         Section 17. Administrative Liability - A Covered Institution, or any person exercising
Institutional Authority, who willfully or through gross neglect fails to comply with any
material duty imposed under this Act shall be subject to the appropriate administrative
sanctions authorized under the institution's charter, enabling law, applicable civil service,
labor, educational, professional, or other special laws, without prejudice to any criminal
liability arising under this Act or other applicable laws.

       Where the Covered Institution is subject to the supervision of a government agency or
regulatory authority, such authority may impose the administrative sanctions authorized
under its enabling law, after due notice and opportunity to be heard.

        Section 18. Criminal Liability - Any person exercising Institutional Authority who
knowingly and willfully commits Institutional Obstruction, as defined under Section 15 of
this Act, shall, upon conviction, suffer the penalty of imprisonment of prisión correccional in
its medium period to prisión mayor in its minimum period, or a fine ranging from Five
Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00) to Two Million Pesos (₱2,000,000.00), or both, at
the discretion of the court.

        Where the offender is a public officer, the penalty shall be imposed in its maximum
period, without prejudice to perpetual or temporary disqualification from holding public
office where authorized under existing laws.

       The prosecution of offenses under this Section shall be without prejudice to
prosecution under the Revised Penal Code or any other special law.

        Section. 19. Relation to Existing Laws - Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
repeal, amend, diminish, or otherwise impair any right, remedy, duty, liability, or penalty
arising under the Revised Penal Code, the Civil Code of the Philippines, the Labor Code of
the Philippines, the Anti-Hazing Act of 2018, the Anti-Bullying Act of 2013, the Safe Spaces
Act, the Occupational Safety and Health Law, or any other applicable law.

       Compliance with the duties imposed under this Act shall be without prejudice to any
criminal, civil, administrative, labor, educational, or disciplinary proceedings arising from the
same act or omission.

       Section. 20. Implementing Rules and Regulations - Within one hundred twenty (120)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the appropriate government agencies, in consultation
with stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

        Section. 21. Appropriations - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the agencies concerned.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included
in the annual General Appropriations Act.
         Section. 22. Separability Clause - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be
in full force and effect.

       Section 23. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
