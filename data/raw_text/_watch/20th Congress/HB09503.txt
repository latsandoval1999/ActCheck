                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines possesses a rich and diverse cultural heritage reflected in its
traditional crafts, artisanal industries, indigenous knowledge systems, and
community-based cultural practices. Across the country, generations of Filipino
artisans have preserved unique traditions in weaving, pottery, embroidery,
woodcarving, metalwork, basketry, and other heritage crafts that embody the
identity, history, and creativity of local communities.

       Despite their cultural and economic significance, many artisans and heritage
industries continue to face serious challenges, including declining transmission of
traditional skills, limited market access, lack of financing and technological support,
imitation and unauthorized commercial use of cultural designs, and the gradual loss
of indigenous and community-based knowledge systems. Globalization, changing
consumer trends, and the aging population of master artisans further threaten the
continuity and sustainability of Philippine heritage industries.


                                                                                       1
       Thus, this bill seeks to establish a comprehensive national framework for the
 preservation, development, promotion, and modernization of Philippine handicrafts
 and heritage-based industries. The measure recognizes that cultural heritage is not
 only a matter of historical preservation, but also a vital driver of inclusive economic
 growth, rural livelihood generation, tourism development, creative industry
 expansion, and national cultural identity.

        The bill institutionalizes a Local Handicrafts and Heritage Promotion Program
 under the leadership of the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination
 with the National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA), the Technical
 Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), the Department of Tourism
 (DOT), the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL), Local
 Government Units (LGUs), and other stakeholders. The measure likewise
 strengthens the protection of traditional knowledge, cultural expressions, and
 indigenous designs against unauthorized exploitation and cultural misappropriation.
 It establishes a system of certification and cultural authenticity labeling to promote
 genuine Philippine heritage products, enhance consumer confidence, strengthen
 export competitiveness, and safeguard the integrity of culturally significant crafts
 and traditions.

       By supporting both cultural preservation and sustainable enterprise
 development, the State affirms its commitment to protecting the country’s heritage
 while creating meaningful economic opportunities for present and future
 generations.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


Section 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Local Handicrafts and
Heritage Industries Promotion Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
preserve, protect, promote, and develop Filipino cultural heritage and traditional




                                                                                1
industries as integral components of national identity, sustainable development, and
inclusive economic growth.


The State recognizes the invaluable contribution of Filipino artisans, craft
communities, and indigenous and local knowledge systems in sustaining the
country’s intangible cultural heritage, promoting creative industries, and generating
livelihood opportunities in rural and urban communities.


The State shall ensure the transmission of traditional skills, techniques, and cultural
expressions to future generations by supporting education, apprenticeship, and
community-based training programs, while safeguarding the integrity and
authenticity of culturally significant designs and practices.


The State shall likewise promote the development of sustainable and globally
competitive heritage-based enterprises by improving access to technology,
financing, markets, and innovation support, without compromising cultural
authenticity and community ownership of traditional knowledge.


Consistent with the principles of inclusive growth and cultural sovereignty, the State
shall strengthen collaboration among national government agencies, Local
Government Units (LGUs), civil society organizations, and cultural communities in
the protection, development, and promotion of Philippine handicrafts and heritage
industries.


SEC. 3. Objectives. - This Act aims to:




                                                                                     2
   1. Provide capacity building and technical support to local artisans, weaving
      communities, and heritage industries;
   2. Expand local and global market access for Filipino handicrafts and traditional
      products;
   3. Support innovation and sustainability within heritage-based industries;
   4. Strengthen inter-agency collaboration for the preservation of cultural heritage
      skills and designs.


SEC. 4. Scope. - This Act shall cover:
   a. Individual artisans and craftspersons;
   b. Organized artisan groups, cooperatives, and micro, small, and medium
      enterprises (MSMEs) engaged in handicrafts, traditional textiles, and heritage
      products;
   c. Communities recognized for intangible cultural heritage practices such as
      weaving, pottery, woodcarving, metalwork, embroidery, and related crafts.


SEC. 5. Definition of Terms. –


   a. Heritage Industries refer to economic activities based on traditional
      knowledge, cultural practices, and indigenous or local craftsmanship,
      including but not limited to weaving, pottery, carving, embroidery, metalwork,
      and other similar artisanal production;
   b. Artisan refers to an individual skilled in traditional or contemporary craft
      production using manual, semi-manual, or heritage-based techniques;




                                                                                   3
   c. Intangible Cultural Heritage refers to practices, representations, expressions,
      knowledge, and skills transmitted from generation to generation as defined
      under applicable cultural heritage laws and international conventions;
   d. Cultural Communities refer to groups recognized as custodians of traditional
      knowledge, including indigenous cultural communities and local craft-based
      communities;
   e. Heritage Products refer to handicrafts and goods derived from traditional
      techniques, motifs, or cultural expressions;
   f. Common Service Facilities (CSFs) refer to shared production facilities,
      equipment, and services that support artisans and MSMEs in improving
      productivity and product quality.


SEC. 6. Implementing Agencies and Inter-Agency Coordination. - The
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) shall be the lead implementing agency of
this Act and shall be primarily responsible for the overall coordination,
implementation, and management of the Local Handicrafts and Heritage Industries
Promotion Program. In the exercise of its functions, the DTI shall coordinate with
the following agencies:


   1. The National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA), for the
      protection, documentation, and safeguarding of cultural heritage, traditional
      knowledge, and cultural authenticity of heritage products;
   2. The Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), for the
      development and implementation of skills training, apprenticeship programs,
      certification, and capacity-building initiatives for artisans and craft
      communities;


                                                                                   4
   3. The Department of Tourism (DOT), for the integration of heritage crafts into
      tourism circuits, cultural destinations, and promotional campaigns;
   4. The Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL), for the
      protection of traditional knowledge, designs, and intellectual property rights
      of artisans and cultural communities;
   5. Local Government Units (LGUs), for local implementation, community
      coordination, enterprise support, and facilitation of access to facilities and
      beneficiaries;
   6. State Universities and Colleges (SUCs), academic institutions, and research
      organizations, for research, design innovation, documentation, and product
      development support; and
   7. Civil society organizations, artisan associations, cooperatives, and private
      sector partners, for program delivery, market access, and sustainability
      support.


The DTI may create an inter-agency steering committee or technical working group
(TWG) to ensure coordinated implementation, policy alignment, and efficient
delivery of program components across participating agencies.


The respective roles of participating agencies shall be without prejudice to their
mandates under existing laws, and shall be exercised in coordination with the DTI
as lead implementing agency.


SEC. 7. Local Handicrafts and Heritage Promotion Program. - The Department
of Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination with the National Commission for
Culture and the Arts (NCCA), Technical Education and Skills Development


                                                                                   5
Authority (TESDA), and local government units (LGUs), shall establish a Local
Handicrafts and Heritage Promotion Program (LHHP Program) with the following
components:


    a. Capacity building on product design, quality standards, digital marketing,
       and financial literacy;
    b. Establishment of Common Service Facilities (CSFs) and equipment sharing
       centers;
    c. Mentoring and incubation programs to support new artisan entrepreneurs;
    d. Integration of heritage crafts in tourism circuits and local festivals;
    e. Documentation and safeguarding of traditional techniques, motifs, and
       processes.


SEC. 8. Protection of Cultural Heritage and Traditional Knowledge - The State
shall ensure that traditional designs, motifs, techniques, and cultural expressions of
indigenous and local communities are protected from unauthorized commercial
exploitation and cultural misappropriation.


The NCCA, in coordination with the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines
(IPOPHL), shall develop guidelines for the documentation, registration, and
protection of culturally significant designs and traditional knowledge systems.


Commercial use of culturally sensitive designs shall require, where applicable, free,
prior, and informed consent (FPIC) or appropriate community authorization in
accordance with existing laws.




                                                                                    6
SEC. 9. Certification and Cultural Authenticity Labeling. - The Department of
Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination with the National Commission for Culture
and the Arts (NCCA), the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL),
Local Government Units (LGUs), artisan associations, cooperatives, and relevant
cultural communities, shall develop and implement a system of certification and
cultural authenticity labeling for heritage products covered under this Act. The
certification and labeling system shall aim to:


       1. Promote the authenticity, quality, and cultural integrity of Philippine
          handicrafts and heritage products;
       2. Protect traditional designs, motifs, techniques, and cultural expressions
          from imitation, unauthorized reproduction, and deceptive commercial
          practices;
       3. Strengthen consumer awareness and market confidence in locally
          produced heritage products;
       4. Enhance the competitiveness and market value of authentic Philippine
          handicrafts in domestic and international markets; and
       5. Support community-based livelihoods and recognition of cultural
          knowledge bearers.


The certification system may include, where appropriate:


       1. Geographic origin identification or place-based cultural labeling;
       2. Verification of traditional production methods or cultural significance;
       3. Product traceability and authenticity verification mechanisms;




                                                                                     7
       4. Quality assurance and product standards consistent with cultural
           preservation objectives; and
       5. Official seals, marks, or designations identifying certified heritage
           products.


The DTI and NCCA, in consultation with concerned cultural communities and
stakeholders, shall establish guidelines and criteria for certification, including
procedures for application, evaluation, renewal, suspension, or revocation of
certification.


In cases involving indigenous cultural communities or culturally sensitive traditional
knowledge, certification and commercial use shall be subject to applicable laws on
free, prior, and informed consent (FPIC) and other community authorization
requirements.


The DTI, in coordination with the DOT and PTICs, shall promote certified
Philippine heritage products in local and international trade fairs, tourism programs,
digital platforms, and export promotion initiatives.


The unauthorized use, misrepresentation, falsification, or commercial misuse of
official certification marks or cultural authenticity labels established under this Act
shall be subject to penalties and sanctions as may be provided under the
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) and applicable laws.


SEC. 10. Fiscal Incentives and Financial Support. – Registered enterprises,
cooperatives, and associations under the Local Handicrafts and Heritage Promotion


                                                                                     8
Program shall be entitled to priority access to existing government MSME support
programs, including financing, training, trade promotion, and market development
assistance.


Subject to applicable laws and approval of the appropriate authorities, qualified
heritage-based enterprises may avail of incentives under existing laws governing
investment promotion, MSME development, and creative industries.


The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination with the Department
of Finance (DOF), Board of Investments (BOI), and other relevant agencies, shall
develop a certification and qualification system for heritage enterprises eligible for
fiscal and non-fiscal incentives.


Government Financial Institutions (GFIs) shall extend concessional financing
programs, credit guarantees, and matching grants to qualified artisans, cooperatives,
and MSMEs engaged in heritage industries.


SEC. 11. Market Development and Promotion. - The DTI, in partnership with the
Department of Tourism (DOT) and Philippine Trade and Investment Centers
(PTICs), shall:


          a. Promote local handicrafts and heritage products in domestic and
              international trade fairs;
          b. Develop an online National Heritage Crafts Portal to market products
              globally;




                                                                                    9
          c. Collaborate with designers, schools, and creative industries to innovate
             while preserving cultural authenticity.


SEC. 12. Heritage Skills Preservation, Apprenticeship, and Youth Engagement.
- The National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA), in coordination with
the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), the
Department of Education (DepEd), Local Government Units (LGUs), and other
relevant agencies, shall establish a comprehensive Heritage Skills Preservation and
Transmission Program to ensure the continuity of traditional Filipino crafts and
cultural practices.


The Program shall include structured apprenticeship and mentorship systems that
formally match master artisans with youth apprentices for the transmission of
traditional skills, techniques, and cultural knowledge, subject to accreditation
guidelines to be issued by the implementing agencies.


Master artisans and cultural knowledge bearers participating in the Program shall be
entitled to appropriate incentives, including stipends, honoraria, training subsidies,
or other forms of support, in recognition of their role in preserving intangible cultural
heritage and transferring specialized skills.


TESDA, in coordination with the NCCA, shall develop competency standards,
certification pathways, and training modules for heritage-based skills, ensuring that
traditional crafts are recognized within national technical and vocational education
frameworks without compromising cultural authenticity.




                                                                                      10
The DepEd, in coordination with the NCCA and TESDA, shall integrate heritage
crafts, traditional knowledge, and cultural industries awareness into relevant basic
education curricula and school-based cultural programs, where appropriate.


The NCCA shall identify and document endangered or at-risk traditional skills and
practices, and prioritize these for urgent safeguarding, transmission, and community-
based revitalization programs.


The DTI and partner agencies shall facilitate the linkage of trained apprentices and
graduates of the Program to livelihood opportunities, enterprise development
programs, cooperatives, and market access initiatives under the Local Handicrafts
and Heritage Industries Promotion Act.


SEC. 13. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting. – The Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI), as lead implementing agency, shall establish and implement a
comprehensive monitoring and evaluation (M&E) system for all programs, projects,
and activities under this Act.


The M&E system shall assess the implementation and impact of the Local
Handicrafts and Heritage Industries Promotion Program, including but not limited
to the following indicators:


   1. Number of artisans, craft groups, cooperatives, and MSMEs assisted under
      the Program;
   2. Changes in income levels and economic conditions of participating artisans
      and communities;


                                                                                  11
   3. Volume and value of domestic and export sales of heritage products;
   4. Number of trainees, apprentices, and beneficiaries of skills development and
      capacity-building programs;
   5. Number of Common Service Facilities (CSFs) established, upgraded, or
      utilized;
   6. Degree of market access expansion, including participation in trade fairs,
      online platforms, and export markets;
   7. Extent of preservation, transmission, and continued practice of traditional
      skills and techniques; and
   8. Compliance of participating enterprises with program standards, including
      cultural authenticity and product quality requirements.


All participating agencies and Local Government Units (LGUs) shall submit
periodic reports to the DTI in accordance with guidelines to be issued in the
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), including data on program
implementation, expenditures, outputs, and outcomes.


The DTI shall submit an annual consolidated report to the President of the
Philippines, the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the Congress
of the Philippines, detailing the status of implementation, performance outcomes,
challenges encountered, and policy recommendations.


The DTI shall maintain a centralized national database of artisans, heritage
enterprises, cooperatives, and assisted communities, which shall serve as the primary
reference for planning, targeting, monitoring, and policy development.




                                                                                  12
The Commission on Audit (COA) shall have access to all relevant records and
reports for audit and verification purposes in accordance with existing laws and
regulations.


Sec. 14. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DTI and
participating agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included
in the annual General Appropriations Act.


Sec. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DTI, in consultation with the NCCA, TESDA, DOT,
LGUs, and representatives from artisan groups and heritage industries, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations.


Sec. 16. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.


Sec. 17. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.


Sec. 18. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
