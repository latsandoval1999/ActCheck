          This proposed measure establishes three distinct tax amnesty programs: the Estate Tax
Amnesty, General Tax Amnesty, and Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies and Other Taxes, each
designed to address specific categories of outstanding tax obligations while providing
taxpayers with a structured and simplified mechanism for settlement. Through these amnesty
programs, qualified taxpayers will be given an opportunity to resolve their existing tax
liabilities, reduce the penalties and other burdens associated with delinquency, and return to
the formal tax system. At the same time, the measure seeks to improve tax compliance and
enable the government to recover revenues from previously unpaid or unresolved tax
obligations.

        Despite ongoing efforts to strengthen the country’s tax system, the Philippines
continues to face challenges in raising its tax effort relative to its ASEAN neighbors. In 2023,
the country recorded a tax effort of 17.9%, below the ASEAN average of 19.6%. One factor
contributing to this gap is persistent tax evasion and weak tax compliance. According to Dr.
Rosario Manasan of the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS), tax evasion may
take the form of underreporting income, overstating expenses, using fictitious receipts and
sales invoices, and failing to declare actual sales, among other practices. These activities
reduce the amount of revenue that the government is able to collect and undermine the
effectiveness of the tax system.

        Recognizing these challenges, the government has pursued a series of tax reforms
aimed at broadening the tax base, improving revenue collection, and making tax
administration more efficient. A major reform was the Tax Reform for Acceleration and
Inclusion (TRAIN) Law, or Republic Act No. 10963, enacted in 2017. This was followed by the
Corporate Recovery and Tax Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act, or Republic Act No.
11534, which introduced changes to corporate taxation and rationalized tax incentives. More
recently, the government enacted the CREATE MORE Act, or Republic Act No. 12066, which
further amended the tax incentives framework and sought to enhance the country’s
investment environment.




                                                                                                 1
       Alongside these legislative reforms, the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and other
government agencies have implemented enforcement initiatives such as Oplan Kandado and
the Run After Tax Evaders (RATE) program to address tax violations and improve compliance.

        But despite these reforms, the government continues to face challenges in tax
administration. In this context, the granting of a tax amnesty may serve as a complementary
measure to existing tax reforms by providing qualified taxpayers an opportunity to settle past
obligations and begin with a clean slate, complying with their tax responsibilities under the
current tax system. It is also the intent of this bill to adopt a comprehensive tax reform
program to simplify the tax compliance requirements. The complexity of the current tax
system makes it difficult for the small business owners, entrepreneurs, and other income
earners to comply with the requirements of the law for the payment of taxes, thereby resulting
in an inadvertent failure to file tax returns and pay the corresponding correct taxes due
thereon.

       Through these efforts, the bill seeks to improve compliance, broaden the tax base, and
strengthen revenue collection by encouraging taxpayers to settle their outstanding obligations
and participate in the formal economy.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                        TITLE I
                                PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS


       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Tax Amnesty Act of 2026”.



       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to

protect and enhance revenue administration and collection that will make the country’s tax

system more equitable, and to simplify the tax compliance requirements. Towards this end,

the State shall:



       (a) Provide a one-time opportunity to settle estate tax obligations through a tax

amnesty program that will give reasonable tax relief;



       (b) Broaden the tax base by offering a general tax amnesty for unpaid internal

revenue taxes that would help cleanse, organize, and improve the Bureau of Internal

Revenue’s database; and

                                                                                             3
       (c) Enhance revenue collection by providing tax amnesty on delinquencies and other

taxes in order to minimize administrative cost in pursuing tax cases and clearing tax accounts

of taxpayers.


                                             TITLE II
                                      ESTATE TAX AMNESTY

       SECTION 3. Coverage. - There is hereby authorized and granted a tax amnesty,

hereinafter referred to as Estate Tax Amnesty, which shall cover the estate of decedents who

died on or before December 31, 2025, with or without assessments duly issued, whether

disputed or final, whose estate taxes have remained unpaid.



      SECTION 4. Rate and Base of the Amnesty Tax. - Estates availing of the Estate Tax

Amnesty shall pay an amnesty tax at the rate of six percent (6%) based on the decedent’s net

undeclared taxable estate at the time of death or Five Thousand Pesos (P5,000.00), whichever

is higher: Provided, That if an estate tax return was previously filed with the BIR, the estate

tax rate of six percent (6%) shall be based on net undeclared estate.



      The provisions of the NIRC, as amended, or the applicable estate tax laws prevailing at

the time of death of the decedent, on valuation, manner of computation, and other related

matters shall apply in determining the net undeclared taxable estate.

for the availment of the estate tax amnesty shall be limited to the following:

        (3)     For "claims against the estate" arising from contract of loan, notarized

                promissory note, if applicable;

       (a)     The executor, administrator, heir, or beneficiary availing of the Estate Tax

Amnesty shall, until June 30, 2028, file with the Revenue District Office a sworn Estate Tax

Amnesty Return along with the complete mandatory requirements under Section 7;



       (b)     Within five (5) working days from the date of submission of Estate Tax Amnesty

       (c)     The executor, administrator, heir, or beneficiary shall present the Acceptance

                                            TITLE III

                                    GENERAL TAX AMNESTY


        SECTION 7. Coverage. - There is hereby authorized and granted a tax amnesty,

hereinafter called General Tax Amnesty, which, shall cover: (I) income tax, capital gains tax,

value-added tax, other percentage taxes, excise tax and documentary stamp tax, collected by

the Bureau of Internal Revenue, and (II) Value-Added Tax, Excise Taxes, and documentary

stamp taxes collected by the Bureau of Customs, including the applicable civil penalties and

interest, as of the end of taxable year 2023 and prior years, with or without assessments duly

issued therefor, that have remained unpaid: Provided, that the general tax amnesty shall

exclude estate tax, donor’s tax, and withholding tax, which shall be covered under separate

titles of this law.

       (a)     Based on the taxpayer’s total assets as of the end of taxable year 2023, as

declared in the statement of total assets, two percent (2%); or



       (b)     Based on the taxpayer’s total net worth as of the end of taxable year 2023, as

declared in the statement of assets, liabilities, and networth:



               (1) Individual (whether resident or nonresident citizens, including resident or

       nonresident aliens), Trusts and Estates - Five percent (5%) or One Hundred Thousand

       Pesos (Php 100,000), whichever is higher.



               (2) Corporations


                      (i) With subscribed capital of above Fifty Million Pesos (Php

               50,000,000) - Five percent (5%) or One Million Three Hundred Pesos

               (Php1,300,000), whichever is higher;


                      (ii) With subscribed capital of above Twenty Million Pesos (Php

               20,000,000) up to Fifty Million Pesos (Php 50,000,000) - Five percent (5%) or

               Six Hundred and Fifty Thousand (Php 650,000), whichever is higher.



                      (iii) With subscribed capital of Five Million (Php 5,000,000) up to

               Twenty Million (Php 20,000,000) - Five percent (5%) or Three Hundred and

                                      TITLE IV
                   TAX AMNESTY ON DELINQUENCIES AND OTHER TAXES


       SECTION 13. Coverage. – There is hereby authorized and granted a tax amnesty herein

called the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies, which shall cover all national internal revenue taxes

such as but not limited to income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, donor’s tax, value-

added tax, other percentage tax, excise tax and documentary stamp tax, including value-

added tax and excise taxes collected by the Bureau of Customs, as of the end of taxable year

2023 and prior years.


     For purposes of this Act, the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies and other taxes may be

availed on the following instances:



      (a)    Delinquencies and assessments, which have become final and executory,
                                                                                           16
including delinquent tax account, where the application for compromise is requested on the

basis of: (1) doubtful validity of the assessment; or (2) financial incapacity of the taxpayer, but

the same was denied by the Regional Evaluation Board or the National Evaluation Board, as

the case may be, on or before the Implementing Rules and Regulations take effect;



      (b)    Pending criminal cases with the Department of Justice or the courts for tax

evasion and other criminal offenses under Chapter II of Title X of the National Internal

Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, with or without assessments duly issued;



      (c)     Tax cases subject of final and executory judgment by the courts on or before the

Implementing Rules and Regulations take effect; and



      (d)     Withholding tax agents who withheld taxes but failed to remit the same to the

Bureau of Internal Revenue.



     SECTION 14. Rates and Base of the Amnesty Tax. – Any person may enjoy the immunities

and privileges of the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies and pay the following tax amnesty rates:



     (a)       Delinquencies and assessments which have become final and executory:



             (I)       forty percent (40%) of the basic tax assessed for delinquencies and

                       assessments corresponding to taxable years ending 2018 to 2022;



             (II) twenty percent (20%) of the basic tax assessed for delinquencies and

       assessments corresponding to taxable years ending 2013 to 2017;

                                                                                                 17
             (III) ten percent (10%) of the basic tax assessed for delinquencies and

       assessments corresponding to taxable years ending 2008 to 2012; and



             (IV) five percent (5%) of the basic tax assessed for delinquencies and

       assessments corresponding to taxable years ending 2007 and prior years.



     (b)        Tax cases subject of final and executory judgment by the courts:


             (I) forty percent (40%) of the basic tax assessed for tax cases corresponding to

       taxable years ending 2018 to 2022;



             (II) twenty percent (20%) of the basic tax assessed for tax cases corresponding

       to taxable years ending 2013 to 2017;



             (III) ten percent (10%) of the basic tax assessed for tax cases corresponding to

       taxable years ending 2008 to 2012; and



             (IV) five percent (5%) of the basic tax assessed for tax cases corresponding to

       taxable years ending 2007 and prior years.



     (c)        Subject of pending criminal cases with criminal information filed with the

Department of Justice or the courts for tax evasion and other criminal offenses under Chapter

II of Title X of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, with assessments

duly issued and otherwise excluded in Titles II and III hereof – Sixty percent (60%) of the basic

tax assessed;
                                                                                               18
     (d)       Withholding agents who withheld taxes but failed to remit the same to the

Bureau of Internal Revenue – One hundred percent (100%) of the basic tax assessed.



       SECTION 15. Availment of the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies; When and Where to File

and Pay. - Any person, natural or juridical, who wishes to avail of the Tax Amnesty on

Delinquencies shall, until June 30, 2028, file with the Bureau of Internal Revenue’s Revenue

District Office or Large Taxpayer District Offices or Large Taxpayer Assistance Division, as the

case may be, which has jurisdiction over the residence or principal place of business of the

taxpayer a sworn Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies Return accompanied by a Certification of

Delinquency. The payment of the amnesty tax shall be made at the time the return is filed.



     Provided, That the Revenue District Officer shall issue and endorse an acceptance

payment form, in such form as may be prescribed in the Implementing Rules and Regulations

of this Act for the authorized agent bank, or in the absence thereof, the revenue collection

agent or municipal treasurer concerned, to accept the amnesty tax payment.



     Provided, further, That the availment of the tax amnesty on delinquencies and the

issuance of the corresponding acceptance payment form do not imply any admission of

criminal, civil or administrative liability on the part of the availing taxpayer.

                                         TITLE V
                                IMMUNITIES AND PRIVILEGES


       SECTION 16. Immunities and Privileges. – Taxpayers availing of the amnesties that

have fully complied with all the conditions set forth in this Act and upon full payment of the

amnesty tax, shall be entitled to the following immunities and privileges:



     (a)       With respect to the years covered by the tax amnesty, the taxpayer shall be

immune from the payment of taxes, as well as additions thereto, and from all appurtenant

civil, criminal, and administrative cases and penalties under the National Internal Revenue

Code of 1997, as amended;



     (b)       Any information or data contained in, derived from or provided by a taxpayer

in the tax amnesty return, Statement of Total Assets or Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and

Networth, as the case may be, and appurtenant documents shall be confidential in nature

and shall not be used in any investigation or prosecution before any judicial, quasi-judicial and

administrative bodies. However, the taxpayer may use this as a defense, whenever

appropriate, in cases brought against him or her;



     (c)       The books of accounts and other records of the taxpayer for the years covered

by the tax amnesty availed of shall not be examined by the Bureau of Internal Revenue:

Provided, That the Commissioner of Internal Revenue may authorize in writing the

examination of the said books of accounts and other records to verify the validity or

correctness of a claim for any tax refund, tax credit (other than refund or credit of taxes

withheld on wages), tax incentives, and/or exemptions under existing laws;




                                                                                              20
      (d)       The availment of any amnesty does not imply admission of criminal, civil, or

administrative liability.



      (e)       Pending criminal, civil, and/or administrative cases for violations under the

National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, shall be terminated; and



      (f)       Any notice of levy, attachment and/or warrants of garnishment issued against

the taxpayer shall be set aside pursuant to a lifting of notice of levy/garnishment duly issued

by the Bureau of Internal Revenue or its authorized representative.



        Provided, that the availment of any amnesty, and the issuance of any certification by

the BIR pursuant thereto, shall not be construed as: a determination, recognition, or

adjudication of ownership, heirship, succession, or any right or interest in any property, and

shall not prejudice or otherwise affect any opposing claims, disputes, or judicial or

administrative proceedings involving such property; and creating, restoring, increasing, or

otherwise entitling the taxpayer to any refund, tax credit, net operating loss carry-over, excess

input value-added tax (VAT) carry-over, or other similar tax benefit or incentive for the taxable

year covered by the general tax amnesty.



        SECTION 17. Unlawful Divulgence of Tax Amnesty Return and Appurtenant

Documents. - It shall be unlawful for any person having knowledge of the Tax Amnesty Return

and appurtenant documents, to disclose any information relative thereto, and any violation

hereof shall be penalized a fine of One hundred fifty thousand pesos (PhP 150,000) and

imprisonment of not less than six (6) years but not more than ten (10) years: Provided, That

if the offender is an officer or employee of the Bureau of Internal Revenue or any government

                                                                                              21
entity, the penalties under Section 270 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as

amended, shall apply: Provided, further, That the offender shall likewise suffer an additional

penalty of perpetual disqualification to hold public office.



       SECTION 18. Report to Oversight Committee. – The Commissioner shall submit to the

Oversight Committee referred to in Section 290 of the National Internal Revenue Code of

1997, as amended, through the Chairpersons of the Committee on Ways and Means of the

Senate of the Philippines and House of Representatives, an annual detailed report on the

implementation of this Act.



       SECTION 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Secretary of Finance shall, in

coordination with the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, promulgate and publish the

necessary rules and regulations within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act.



       SECTION 20. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is subsequently declared

       SECTION 21. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, implementing

rules and regulations, issuances, or any part thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this

Act are deemed repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.



       SECTION 22. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete

publication in the Official Gazette or in at least one (1) newspaper of general circulation.
