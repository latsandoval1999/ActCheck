                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippine higher education system has expanded significantly, yet persistent challenges remain in
access, quality, institutional capacity, and the alignment of graduates' competencies with the needs of the
labor market. The Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II) reported that only 34.89%
of Filipinos participate in higher education, below the ASEAN average of 41.10%. It likewise identified
continuing concerns on graduate underemployment and skills mismatch, including the finding that
approximately 24% of specialized graduates work in industries unrelated to their field of study. 1

These challenges demonstrate the need to strengthen higher education not only as a pathway to employment,
but also as a driver of research, innovation, and national development. As industries and technologies
continue to change, higher education institutions must be given greater capacity to develop programs
responsive to emerging skills and regional development needs. The Philippine Statistics Authority's Jobs and
Skills Survey similarly recognizes the need to better understand skills gaps, technological changes, and
emerging labor market requirements.2

The proposed measure also strengthens pathways between higher education and technical-vocational
education and training through ladderized education, micro-credentials, associate degrees, diplomas, and
other flexible learning pathways. These mechanisms can provide learners and workers with alternative routes
toward employment, further education, and professional advancement while supporting the implementation
of the Philippine Qualifications Framework.

Ultimately, this aims to ensure that higher education institutions are not merely producers of credentials, but
active contributors to a skilled workforce, a stronger research ecosystem, and inclusive national development.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

18   SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:
19      a. Applied Bachelor's Degree - refers to a generally four-year degree program that combines
20         academic learning with a substantial proportion of practical or on-the-job experience,
21         targeted for specific career paths, instead of academic or research-focused outcomes;
22      b. Associate Degree - refers to post-secondary qualifications attained after completion of the K
23         to 12 basic education program, normally requiring a minimum of two years of study. It
24         equips learners with specialized skills for gainful employment in related professions, while
25         also providing the academic preparation necessary for progression to baccalaureate
26         programs;
27      c. Bachelor's Degree Program - refers to an undergraduate university degree that requires the
28         completion of a secondary-level education and involves four (4) years of full-time study for
29         most programs, consisting of general education courses and specialized subjectsm focused
30         on a major degree;
31      d. Commission En Banc - refers to the policy-making body of the Commission on Higher
32         Education composed of the Chairperson and Commissioners exercising their power and
33         functions under the mandates prescribed by this Act;
34      e. Diploma - refers to a credential awarded after completing a compressed and technically-
35         focused program, specializing in a particular trade or vocational skill, and corresponds to a
36         Level 5 qualification in the PQF framework;
37   f. Differentiated Autonomy - refers to a system of granting increasing levels of self-governance
38      to higher education institutions (HEIs) that consistently meet national standards on quality,
39      accountability, and relevance, allowing such HEIs to be subject to streamlined approvals,
40      and to receive differentiated supports and incentives;
41   g. Doctoral Degree - refers to the highest academic degree, earned by conducting significant
42      research and producing original work, typically in a Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) or a
43      practice-based professional doctorate;
44   h. Foreign Higher Education Institutions - refer to a foreign-owned and duly recognized higher
45      education institutions abiding by a prescribed system of education duly registered in a
46      country other than the Philippines;
47   i. Higher Education Institution (HEI) - refers to an educational institution, whether private or
48      public undertaking operations of higher education programs recognized by the Commission
49      on Higher Education, and whose primary offering is higher education, vis-a-vis their basic
50      education and TVET courses and enrolment;
51   j. Local Universities and Colleges - refer to public HEIs established and financially supported
52      by their respective local governments;
53   k. Ladderized Education - refers to the harmonization of all education and training mechanisms
54      that allow students and workers to progress between technical-vocational and higher
55      education programs, or vice- versa. It opens opportunities for career and educational
56      advancement to students and workers. It creates a seamless and borderless education and
57      training system that will allow transfers in terms of flexible entry and exit between technical-
58      vocational and higher education programs in the post-secondary school educational system;
59   l. Masteral Degree Program - refers to a postgraduate academic program that provides
60      advanced and specialized knowledge and skills beyond a bachelor's degree;
61   m. Micro-Credentials - refers to industry-recognized and/or qualification-related skill-specific
62      training and certification shorter in coverage compared to a full qualification or TESDA-
63      prescribed cluster units of competency wherein the curriculum of a micro- credential course
64      does not require the inclusion of basic and common competencies;
65   n. Non-degree program - refers to a postsecondary short-term certificate course program that
66      does not correspond to a specific PQF qualification grade, but may form parts thereof in the
67      case of stackable credentials;
68   o. Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF) - refers to the national policy that describes the
69      levels of educational qualifications and sets standards for learning outcomes across all
70      education and training sectors in the Philippines, namely basic education, technical-
71      vocational education and training (TVET), higher education, and post-graduate scale of
72      completion;
73   p. Polytechnic College or Technological College - refers to a college that offers the following:
74         i.    at least three (3) or more programs that grant or award NC levels 4-5 in TESDA's
75               National Competency Assessment scale, that provide pathways towards bachelor's
76               degree programs offered by the same institution;
77        ii.    three (3) or more ladderized and/or applied bachelor's degree programs, and
78       iii.    highly-specialized or technical industry on-the-job training for all of its offered
79               programs;
80   q. Polytechnic Institute or Technological Institute - refers to post- secondary education
81      institutions with a focused and technical industry-driven curricula, that offer at least three
82      (3) or more programs that grant or award National Certificates (NC) to qualified recipients,
83      with a corresponding diploma or similar credential and an established on-the-job training
84      program;
85   r. Polytechnic University or Technological University refers to a university that offers the
86      following:
87         i.    All of the cited qualifications for a polytechnic or technological college;
88        ii.    Three (3) or more masteral or doctoral programs in applied fields including but not
89               limited to highly technical or technologically- focused professions such as
90               engineering or other applied sciences; and
91       iii.    an expansive and direct linkage of its degree programs to practical engagements with
92               applicable industries;
93   s. State Universities and Colleges - refer to public HEIs with independent and separate
94      governing boards created and established by law;
95   t. Technical-Vocational Education and Training (TVET) - refers to the education or training
96      process where it involves, in addition to general education, the study of technologies and
97      related sciences and acquisition of practical skills relating to occupations in various sectors
98      of economic life and social life, comprises formal (organized programs as part of the school
99      system) and non-formal (organized classes outside the school system) approaches;
100      u. Tertiary Education - refers to formal post-secondary programs offered by degree-granting
101         colleges, universities, and diplomas offered by TVIs. It shall encompass both higher
102         education and TVET;
103      v. TVET Institute or TVI -refer to postsecondary education institutions that offer short-term
104         nondegree, diploma or certificate technical-vocational education and training programs or
105         courses; and
106      w. Typology - an organized system for classifying and categorizing institutions, students,
107         educational programs, or even teaching strategies based on shared characteristics and traits
108         to facilitate analysis, comparison, and understanding of the complexities within the higher
109         education system.

180   SEC. 9. Powers and Functions of the Commission. - The Commission shall exercise the following
181   powers and functions:
182   I. Developmental Powers and Functions:
183      a. Formulate, recommend, and implement development plans, policies, priorities, and
184         programs on higher education and research consistent with national and regional
185         development goals, as well as local and global human resource requirements;
186      b. Collaborate closely with DepEd and TESDA to ensure that higher education directions and
187         priorities are coherent with, respond to, and support, the needs and developments in basic
188         education and technical vocational education and training;
189      c. Work with TESDA and the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) to ensure that
190         policies and programs relating to the the development of professionals are aligned and
191         responsive to the needs of respective professions as well as national development;
192      d. Broaden access to quality higher education through programs and services, particularly for
193         the disadvantaged students, such as indigenous peoples, poor and deserving students, and
194         students from depressed areas, while guaranteeing systems that ensure equitable access for
195         the most underprivileged students as beneficiaries, and establishing policies that support
196         their completion and graduation from their respective programs;
197      e. Formulate differentiated levels of regulation, resourcing, excellence, and developmental
198         supports cognizant of the typology of tertiary education institutions in the country;
199      f. Direct the development and reform of higher education towards world class standards,
200         harmonization with the ASEAN education plan, and international conventions and
201         agreements;
202      g. Work towards broad recognition of Philippine higher educationqualifications and
203         credentials globally, with a special focus on countries that receive many of our college
204         graduates;
205      h. Support the development of non-traditional higher education programs and delivery modes,
206         including but not limited to distance education pursuant to Republic Act No. 10650
207         otherwise known as the Open Distance Learning Act; ladderized education in accordance
208         with Republic Act No. 10647, otherwise known as the Ladderized Education Act of 2014;
209         and the Expanded Tertiary Education Equivalency and Accreditation Program (ETEEAP) as
210           institutionalized under Republic Act No. 12124, together with applicable executive orders
211           and issuances, while ensuring inclusiveness of pathways from basic education;
212      i.   Fulfill the mandates of CHED under Republic Act No. 11448, otherwise known as the
213           Transnational Higher Education Act, including the establishment of transnational higher
214           education programs, and the internationalization of higher education in the country;
215      j.   Support the implementation of the Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF) for higher
216           education, pursuant to Republic Act No. 10968, in coordination with DepEd, TESDA, and
217           other relevant agencies;
218      k.   Develop and implement standards and systems on reclassification, promotion, and
219           professional development of personnel in state universities and colleges (SUCs), in
220           coordination with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and the Civil Service
221           Commission (CSC);
222      l.   Formulate and promulgate rules, regulations, policies, priorities, and programs necessary to
223           effectively carry out the purposes of this Act; and perform such other functions as may be
224           necessary for its effective operations and for the continued enhancement, growth, and
225           development of higher education.
226   II. Oversight and Regulatory Powers and Functions:
227      a. Exercise quasi-judicial powers in the pursuit of the Commission's mandate, subject to the
228         provisions of Section 13 of this Act, as well as the following related powers:
229            i.    Establishment and enforcement of systems to ensure program and institutional
230                  compliance with CHED policies, standards, and guidelines, and;
231           ii.    Imposition of appropriate sanctions, after due process, on non-compliant programs
232                  or institutions;
233      b. Review, evaluate, and recommend to the DBM, based on higher education sector priorities,
234         the budgets of SUCs, and coordinate with the DBM on guidelines for the use of their income;
235      c. Set guidelines for reasonable increases in tuition and other fees in private HEIs, and approve,
236         disapprove, or modify applications for such increases, ensuring equitable access to education
237         and financial sustainability of institutions;
238      d. Set standards, policies, and guidelines for the creation, conversion, or elevation of higher
239         education institutions, subject to budgetary limitations and rationalization of institutional
240         distribution across regions;
241      e. Provide directions and common standards for accrediting agencies, as well as support the
242         development of accreditation bodies and the participation of HEIs therein as a means of
243         upgrading higher education quality;
244      f. Conduct mediation, conciliation, or settlement proceedings, and exercise appellate
245         jurisdiction over disputes involving HEIs, students, teachers, and staff on academic matters;
246         and
247      g. Institutionalize and assess a system of differentiated autonomy for HEIs, enabling qualified
248         institutions to enjoy streamlined supervision while ensuring quality assurance.

405   SEC. 20. Guarantee of Religious Freedom. - No higher education student, faculty, or other
406   members of the academic community shall be prohibited from exercising their right to religious
407   freedom in the HEI or any of its campuses.
408   SEC. 21. Quality Assurance. - The Commission shall ensure that all higher education institutions
409   are responsible for the quality of their programs and have an established system of internal quality
410   assurance. HEIs are also expected to have their programs externally reviewed at least every five (5)
411   years to keep them abreast with developments in the field. The Commission shall provide incentives
412   to institutions of higher learning, public or private, whose programs are externally accredited or
413   assessed by reputable local or international external quality assurance agencies or whose needs are
414   for such accreditation or assessment.
415   SEC. 22. Tax Exemptions. - Any donation, contribution, bequest, and grant which may be made to
416   the Commission shall constitute as allowable deduction from the income of the donor for income
417   tax purposes and shall be exempt from donor's tax, subject to such conditions as provided under the
418   National Internal Revenue Code, as amended.
419   SEC. 23. Authority. - The Commission shall exercise such authority as may be deemed necessary
420   within its premises or areas of operation to effectively carry out its powers and functions and to
421   attain its objectives: Provided, That the Commission may seek the assistance of other government
422   agencies for the proper implementation of this Act.
423   SEC. 24. Periodic Review. - The Commission shall undertake a periodic assessment of its
424   accomplishment, review and update the roadmap of Philippine Higher Education every six (6) years.
425   The updated roadmap shall be submitted to Congress and the Office of the President for the
426   formulation of new policies necessary for the continuous modernization of higher education.
427   SEC. 25. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the implementation of this Act shall be
428   charged to the current year's appropriations of the Commission on Higher Education. Thereafter,
429   such amount necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual
430   General Appropriations Act.
431   SEC. 26. Transitory Provisions. - The incumbent chairperson and commissioners shall serve as
432   chairperson and commissioners respectively under the terms for which they have been appointed
433   without need of new appointments. The incumbent executive director shall likewise serve as
434   executive director without need of a new appointment.
435   All officers and personnel of the Commission shall continue to perform their duties and
436   responsibilities and receive their corresponding salaries and benefits. The approval of this Act shall
437   not cause any demotion in rank or diminution of salary, benefits and other privileges of the
438   incumbent personnel of the Commission.
439   SEC. 27. Repealing Clause. - Republic Act No. 7722, otherwise known as the "Higher Education
440   Act of 1994," is hereby repealed. All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules and
441   regulations, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
442   repealed, amended or modified accordingly.
443   SEC. 28. Separability Clause. - If any part or provision of this Act is held unconstitutional or
444   invalid, the other provisions or parts hereof which are not affected thereby shall continue to be in
445   full force and effect.
446   SEC. 29. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
447   Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
