                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The government has a fundamental responsibility to deliver public services that are
efficient, accessible, secure, and responsive to the needs of the citizens. In the digital age, it
is essential for the government to design, build, operate, and continuously improve digital
systems, applications, and platforms that support public administration and service delivery.

       Despite recent progress, the Philippines currently lags behind many regional and
global peers in digital government performance. According to the United Nations E-
Government Development Index (EGDI), the Philippines ranks 73rd globally, trailing countries
such as Estonia (2nd), Singapore (3rd), and South Korea(4th).1 This gap reflects structural
weaknesses in the country’s digital governance framework, particularly in the integration and
execution of digital government services.

         The recent enactment of the E-Governance Act established a national framework for
electronic governance and designated the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT) as the lead policy agency. However, it did not create a permanent
institution responsible for the actual creation, development, operation, and maintenance of
government digital products, platforms, and shared infrastructure.

       Digital initiatives across government agencies remain fragmented and often
duplicative. Agencies frequently develop and procure systems independently, leading to
inconsistent standards, cybersecurity risks, vendor dependence, and inefficient use of public
funds. At present, there is no single entity responsible for digital engineering and product
development across government.

        Government digital systems perform sovereign functions, process sensitive personal
and state data, and support the delivery of critical public services. Continued reliance on
private vendors for their development and maintenance exposes the government to risks such
as vendor lock-in, rising long-term costs, loss of institutional knowledge, continuity failures,
and reduced accountability. Without a permanent internal capability for digital engineering and
product development, the government faces difficulty in managing, securing, and improving
its own digital assets.

      Global best practices indicate that successful digital states maintain a clear separation
between ICT policy and regulatory functions and the operational delivery of digital government

services. Although the DICT, pursuant to Republic Act No. 10844, effectively manages ICT
infrastructure, cybersecurity standards, and sector regulation, the rapidly evolving digital
landscape presents challenges.

        The DICT was primarily designed as a sectoral department responsible for ICT policy,
planning, infrastructure development, cybersecurity, and industry regulation. It was not
specifically structured to function as a centralized digital service delivery organization
responsible for designing, building, and operating digital systems across the entire
government.

        E-government is not primarily a technology or infrastructure issue but a governance
and administrative reform challenge. It requires redesigning processes, integrating agency
systems, harmonizing legal mandates, and improving service delivery across the entire
bureaucracy. These functions fall outside the DICT’s core statutory design. Moreover, the
DICT operates as a line department with a vertical structure, while effective e-government
requires horizontal, whole-of-government coordination and implementation. DICT cannot
compel other departments, constitutional bodies, or local government units to reengineer
workflows or integrate systems without risking institutional overreach. In addition, the E-
Government Unified Project Management Office (Egov-UPMO) established under the E-
Governance Act remains a project-based and transitional mechanism, leading to fragmented
portals, duplicated systems, and weak platform ownership. While it plays an important
coordinating role, its temporary nature limits its ability to serve as a permanent institutional
home for national digital platforms.

         To address these gaps, this measure proposes the establishment of the GovTech
Philippines Corporation (GTPC) as a Government-Owned or Controlled Corporation (GOCC)
under the Office of the President. The Corporation shall serve as the government’s central
digital engineering, product development, and service delivery authority responsible for
designing, building, operating, and continuously improving government digital products,
platforms, and infrastructure, including national digital platforms like eGov.ph. It shall function
strictly as an operational and technical entity, without regulatory or quasi-judicial powers.

       To ensure operational flexibility while maintaining accountability, the proposed GTPC
shall be governed by Republic Act No. 10149. It shall be subject to the oversight of the
Governance Commission for GOCCs and to audit by the Commission on Audit. Additionally,
to ensure strong data protection and uphold privacy-by-design principles, the National Privacy
Commission will serve as an ex officio member of the Board.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

    AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE GOVTECH PHILIPPINES CORPORATION AS A
 GOVERNMENT-OWNED OR CONTROLLED CORPORATION, DEFINING ITS POWERS
     AND FUNCTIONS AS THE CENTRAL DIGITAL ENGINEERING, PRODUCT
  DEVELOPMENT, AND SERVICE DELIVERY AUTHORITY OF THE GOVERNMENT,
   ENHANCING THE PHILIPPINE E-GOVERNANCE AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “GovTech Philippines and E-
Governance Integration Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

   (a) Implement and empower a whole-of-government (WOG) mandate to digital
       governance, consistent with the E-Governance Act, ensuring that government digital
       systems and services are interoperable, secure, reliable, accessible, and citizen-
       centric and digital transformation is treated as a governance and administrative reform
       challenge, rather than a mere technical upgrade;

   (b) Institutionalize a “Government-as-a-Platform” (GaaP) model with centralized digital
       engineering, product development, and service delivery capability within government
       to create, build, operate, maintain, and continuously improve digital products,
       platforms, and infrastructure including integrated digital systems, allowing the shift
       from siloed agency systems to a unified national digital ecosystem where common
       services are built once and used by all;

   (c) Address fragmentation, duplication, inefficiency, and vendor dependence in
       government digital systems by adopting shared platforms, a common infrastructure,
       and standardized digital products and services, at the same adopting the "Once-Only"
       Data Principle through an auditable framework where citizens and businesses provide
       data to the government only once, with the State ensuring seamless and lawful sharing
       among authorized entities;

   (d) Ensure transparency, accountability, fiscal responsibility, and performance-based
       management in the delivery of digital government services, consistent with laws
       governing government-owned or controlled corporations;
   (e) Strengthen executive coordination and national oversight of digital government
       initiatives by attaching the GovTech Philippines Corporation to the Office of the
       President;

   (f) Support the mandates of the Department of Information and Communications
       Technology and other relevant agencies under existing laws through a dedicated
       operational, engineering, and product delivery arm;

   (g) Advance inclusive, secure, and technology-enabled public services aligned with
       national development goals and the public interest;

   (h) Attract, develop, and retain skilled professionals in digital and technology fields in line
       with Section 6(h) of Republic Act 12254, also known as the E-Governance Act of 2025.
       To achieve this, it will implement a compensation and benefits framework that is
       competitive with the prevailing rates and benefits offered in the private sector. This
       framework will adhere to principles of merit, transparency, and public accountability;

   (i) Harness artificial intelligence or AI, data-driven systems, and smart digital technologies
       as strategic enablers of efficient, transparent and responsive governance, while
       ensuring that such technologies are human-centered, accountable, secure, and
       consistent with law and public interest;

   (j) Strengthen public trust in government through efficiency, interoperability and
       innovation;

   (k) Prioritize “Sovereign Digital Capability” by reducing long-term costs and security risks
       by developing internal, permanent digital engineering expertise, ending indefinite
       reliance on private vendors for core sovereign functions; and

   (l) Adopt agile and human-centered designs by mandating that all government digital
       products be built using iterative, user-centric methodologies that prioritize the citizen's
       experience over bureaucratic convenience.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall mean:

   (a) Application Programming Interface (API) – a set of protocols and technical standards
       that enable different software applications or systems to securely communicate and
       exchange data;

   (b) Central Digital Engineering, Product Development, and Service Delivery Authority –
       the institutional role of the GovTech Philippines Corporation as the government entity
       responsible for the creation, design, development, operation, maintenance, and
       continuous improvement of shared digital products, platforms, and infrastructure;

   (c) Data Exchange Mechanism – a secure, standardized, and auditable framework that
       enables lawful sharing of data among authorized entities, consistent with applicable
       laws on data privacy, cybersecurity, and access to information;

   (d) Digital Platform – an integrated set of digital systems, applications, and services that
       enable electronic transactions, service delivery, data exchange, and interaction among
       government agencies, citizens, businesses, and other stakeholders;

   (e) E-Governance – refers to the use of ICT by the government to provide public services
       in a more friendly, convenient, affordable, efficient and transparent manner. Further, it
       is the application of ICT for delivering government services through integration of
    various stand-alone systems, platforms, and applications between Government-to-
    Citizens (G2C), Government-to-Business (G2B), and Government-to-Government
    (G2G) services. It is often linked to back-office processes and interactions within the
    entire government framework;

(f) E-Government – refers to the use of ICT by the government to enhance access to and
    delivery of government services for an efficient, responsive, ethical, accountable, and
    transparent government;

(g) eGov.ph – the official national government digital platform or portal designated for
    integrated access to government digital services and applications;

(h) Government Application – any software application, system, or digital service
    developed, owned, managed, operated, or used by a government agency or
    government-owned or controlled corporation in the performance of its functions;

(i) Government-as-a-Platform (GaaP) – refers to a service-oriented architectural model
    where the Corporation provides a common core of shared digital infrastructure,
    standards, and reusable software components. This model enables government
    agencies to "plug into" a unified ecosystem to deliver integrated services, rather than
    building independent, siloed systems. It is characterized by the use of shared digital
    identity, unified payment gateways, secure data exchange, and common cloud hosting;

(j) Government Cloud or GovCloud – the shared cloud computing infrastructure and
    services used by government agencies for hosting applications, systems, and data in
    a secure, scalable, and resilient environment;

(k) GovTech Venture Sandbox – refers to a controlled and secure operational environment
    managed by the Corporation that allows for the testing and validation of innovative
    technologies, business models, or software solutions from the private sector or
    academe. It provides a "safe harbor" for experimentation with emerging technologies
    (such as Artificial Intelligence or Blockchain) under technical supervision before they
    are considered for government-wide procurement or deployment;

(l) Interoperability – the ability of different government information systems, applications,
    and platforms to communicate, exchange data, and use information effectively,
    securely, and lawfully;

(m) Legacy System – an existing government information system or application that is
    outdated, inefficient, costly to maintain, or incompatible with current digital standards or
    interoperability requirements;

(n) “Once-Only” Data Principle – refers to the digital governance strategy where citizens
    and businesses provide standard information to the government only once. The
    Corporation provides the technical framework to ensure this data is securely and
    lawfully shared among authorized agencies, eliminating the need for redundant data
    submissions;

(o) Philippine Government Tech Stack (PGTS) – refers to a modular suite of central
    software components, including but not limited to, national digital identity integration,
    secure payment gateways, and standardized API layers, which are developed and
    maintained by the Corporation as mandatory shared services for all government
    entities;
   (p) Private Sector Entity – any natural or juridical person not forming part of the national
       government, local government units, or government-owned or controlled corporations;

   (q) Service-Fee or Cost-Recovery Basis – a pricing arrangement whereby fees are
       charged to recover reasonable costs of developing, operating, maintaining, and
       improving digital platforms or services, subject to applicable laws;

   (r) Step-in-Rights – refers to the extraordinary technical and managerial authority of the
       Corporation to intervene in the development, operation, or rehabilitation of a
       government digital project or system. These rights are triggered when a project is
       deemed critical to national interest, security, or essential public services and is found
       to be failing, compromised by a cybersecurity breach, or suffering from significant
       contractual or technical default that threatens service continuity; and

   (s) Whole-of-Government (WOG) Approach – a coordinated method of governance
       whereby government agencies work collaboratively across institutional boundaries to
       deliver integrated, efficient, and citizen-centric digital services.

SECTION 4. Rules of Construction. – This Act shall be liberally construed to promote
efficient, secure, interoperable, and citizen-centric digital government services. In case of
doubt, the provisions of this Act shall be interpreted in a manner consistent with the
Constitution, the E-Governance Act, Republic Act No. 10149, and other applicable laws.

SECTION 5. Technology-Neutrality. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed to mandate or
limit the use of any specific technology, platform, software, architecture, or vendor. The
GovTech Philippines Corporation shall adopt technology-neutral, standards-based, and future-
ready solutions, subject to applicable laws and governance rules.

SECTION 6. Creation of the GovTech Philippines Corporation. – There is hereby created
a government-owned or controlled corporation to be known as the GovTech Philippines
Corporation (GTPC), hereinafter referred to as the Corporation, which shall be attached to the
Office of the President for policy coordination and strategic oversight. The Corporation shall
possess corporate personality, perpetual succession, and all powers necessary to carry out
its mandate in accordance with this Act and existing laws.

SECTION 7. General Mandate and Functions. – The Corporation shall have the mandate to
conceive, design, build, develop, deploy, operate, maintain, and continuously improve
government digital products, platforms, and infrastructure, and shall:

   (a) Create, design, and build digital products and applications for government use;

   (b) Manage, operate, integrate, maintain, and enhance government digital platforms,
       including eGov.ph;

   (c) Build, own, and operate shared digital infrastructure, including government cloud
       platforms, middleware, APIs, authentication, and interoperability services;

   (d) Develop and operate secure and lawful data exchange mechanisms, digital identity,
       authentication and authorization systems subject to law;

   (e) Ensure reliability, cybersecurity, accessibility, resilience, and continuity of digital
       products and infrastructure;

   (f) Provide end-to-end digital engineering and product services to government entities
       and, subject to safeguards under this Act, to private sector entities;
    (g) Offer digital products and infrastructure services on a service-fee or cost-recovery
        basis;

    (h) Rationalize, modernize, migrate, consolidate, or retire legacy systems;

    (i) Own and manage intellectual property arising from government digital products,
        subject to applicable laws;

    (j) Adopt modern, user-centered, and iterative digital product development and
        operations practices and continuously improve smart government capabilities through
        innovation, system modernization and integration of emerging technologies, subject to
        limitation imposed by law;

    (k) Develop and operate government-owned artificial intelligence systems, including
        decision support tools, automation platforms, predictive analytics, and intelligent
        service systems, for use by government agencies in the performance of their official
        functions;

    (l) Design, embed, and integrate artificial and data-driven technologies in government
        digital platforms and infrastructure to improve efficiency, responsiveness, accessibility,
        and evidence-based decision making;

   (m) Serve as technical integrator of PhilSys for secure, consent-based digital authentication
       for government services, without creating new biometric databases; and

    (n) Perform such other acts and functions as may be necessary to carry out the purposes
        of this Act.

SECTION 8. Specific Operational Powers. – In addition to the powers and mandate as
provided in the preceding provisions, GovTech Philippines Corporation shall have the following
operational authority:

    (a) Serve as the Primary Technical Evaluator for all high-value government ICT
        procurements to ensure alignment with the Philippine Government Tech Stack;

    (b) Operate a GovTech Venture Sandbox to facilitate the adoption of emerging
        technologies from the private sector;

    (c) Implement Step-In Rights to provide technical rescue for mission-critical systems at
        risk of failure; and

    (d) Act as the National Repository for Government Source Code, ensuring that all software
        built with public funds remains the property of the State and is reusable across
        agencies.

SECTION 9. Technical Step-In Rights. – Upon the recommendation of the Board and
approval by the Office of the President, the Corporation shall have the authority to intervene
in the management and operation of any government digital project or system that is deemed
critical to national security, public health, or essential public services, and is found to be failing
or at high risk of failure. The Corporation shall provide the necessary technical expertise to
rehabilitate, migrate, or integrate said system into the National Tech Stack.
SECTION 10. Smart Nation Infrastructure Program. – As the primary authority for digital
transformation and to ensure digital resilience and fiscal efficiency, GovTech Philippines
Corporation envisions a Smart Nation Program that shall:

   (a) Establish the Philippine Government Tech Stack (PGTS) to develop and maintain a
       mandatory, modular suite of shared software components, including national digital
       identity, secure payments, and a unified communications gateway, to enable the
       "Once-Only" Data Principle;

   (b) Set and enforce government-wide technical architecture and interoperability
       standards to ensure all agency systems can communicate seamlessly through secure
       Application Programming Interfaces (APIs);

   (c) Develop and propose agile digital procurement and specialized, iterative procurement
       frameworks for digital services to replace traditional, rigid software acquisition models
       subject to existing laws and regulations;

   (d) Serve as the active cybersecurity defense and threat-hunting arm for all government
       digital platforms, complementing the policy functions of the DICT; and

   (e) Lead the deployment and management of a common sensor and Internet of Things
       (IoT) platform to provide data-driven insights for public safety, transport, and
       environmental monitoring.

SECTION 11. The Board of Directors. – The powers and functions of the Corporation shall
be vested in a Board of Directors composed of seven (7) members, all of whom shall be
appointed by the President of the Philippines. The Board shall be composed of:

   (a) The Secretary of the Department of Information and Communications Technology
       (DICT) or his or her duly authorized representative – Ex Officio Member;

   (b) The Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST) or his or her duly
       authorized representative – Ex Officio Member;

   (c) The Commissioner of the National Privacy Commission (NPC) or his or her duly
       authorized representative – Ex Officio Member;

   (d) One (1) appointive member who is a member of the Philippine Bar, with at least ten
       (10) years of practice of law, preferably in the field of technology law, digital law, data
       privacy, cybersecurity, intellectual property, or corporate governance, who shall
       likewise serve as the Corporate Secretary of the Corporation; and

   (e) Three (3) appointive members from the private sector or academe with recognized
       competence and experience in information technology, digital systems, cybersecurity,
       data governance, digital infrastructure, or public sector innovation.

       The appointive members shall serve for a term of five (5) years, with staggered initial
terms and hold-over capacity, and shall comply with the fit-and-proper standards of the
Governance Commission for Government-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GCG) pursuant
to Republic Act No. 10149.

SECTION 12. President and Chief Executive Officer (CEO). – The Corporation shall have
a President and Chief Executive Officer (CEO) who shall be appointed by the President of the
Philippines. No person shall be appointed President and CEO unless he or she:
   (a) Is a citizen of the Philippines;

   (b) Has at least ten (10) years of experience as an information technology practitioner or
       in a closely related field;

   (c) Has a proven track record of leadership and performance in managing significant
       digital systems, platforms, or technology-driven organizations; and

   (d) meets the fit-and-proper standards prescribed by the Governance Commission for
       Government-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GCG).

        The President and CEO shall serve for a term of five (5) years, and may be reappointed
for the same term, unless earlier removed for cause in accordance with law.

SECTION 13. Capitalization and Sources of Funds. – The Corporation shall have an
authorized capital stock of Fifty Billion Pesos (₱50,000,000,000.00), fully subscribed by the
National Government, subject to appropriation by Congress. The funds of the Corporation
shall be sourced from: (a) appropriations authorized in the General Appropriations Act; (b)
lawfully realigned funds, subject to applicable laws and regulations; and (c) income and
revenues generated from services provided to government agencies and, subject to
safeguards, to private sector entities. Nothing in this Act shall be construed as an automatic
appropriation of public funds. All funds of the Corporation shall be subject to audit by the
Commission on Audit (COA) in accordance with existing auditing rules.

SECTION 14. Authority to Provide Services to the Private Sector. – The Corporation may
provide digital products, platforms, infrastructure, engineering, and related technology
services to private sector entities, provided that:

   (a) Such services are ancillary to and supportive of its public-sector mandate;

   (b) Public service delivery is not compromised;

   (c) Terms and conditions are transparent, non-discriminatory, and compliant with
       competition laws; and

   (d) Revenues derived therefrom are used exclusively to support and sustain government
       digital products, platforms, and services.

SECTION 15. Revenue Generation and Financial Sustainability. – The Corporation is
authorized by this Act to operate on a service-fee or cost-recovery basis. This ensures the
long-term stewardship, continuity, and evolution of national digital platforms through various
revenue generation schemes, including but not limited to the following:

   (a) Service Fees for Government Entities – The Corporation is permitted to charge
       reasonable fees to government agencies, local government units, and other
       government-owned and controlled corporations (GOCCs) for the use of shared digital
       infrastructure, including GovCloud, managed APIs, and the national payment gateway.

   (b) Private Sector Integration Fees – Subject to strict data privacy safeguards and
       oversight from the National Privacy Commission (NPC) and the provisions of the Data
       Privacy Act, the Corporation may charge private sector entities for access to
       government-owned digital products or infrastructure services, such as secure
       authentication and data exchange interfaces.
   (c) Ancillary Revenue – Additional revenues may be generated from technical
       consultancy, digital engineering services, and the management of intellectual property
       arising from government digital products.

   (d) Reinvestment of Earnings – All income and revenues generated shall be used
       exclusively to support, maintain, and continuously improve government digital products
       and shared infrastructure.

   (e) Fiscal Transparency – All fees and pricing models shall be transparent, non-
       discriminatory, and subject to the review of the Governance Commission for GOCCs
       (GCG) to ensure they remain in the public interest.

SECTION 16. Investment of Funds. – The Corporation shall have the authority to invest its
generated income and such portions of the Corporation's funds that are not needed to meet
current operational requirements. To ensure the liquidity and safety of the funds, the
Corporation shall invest with the care, foresight, and prudence that a "prudent man" would
exercise in the management of his own affairs. The Corporation may invest in the following:

   (a) Government Securities – In bonds, securities, promissory notes, or other evidence of
       indebtedness of the Government of the Philippines, or in bonds and securities where
       the payment of principal and interest is guaranteed by the National Government.

   (b) High-Grade Deposits – In interest-bearing deposits and self-liquidating certificates of
       deposit with any government-owned bank or any domestic bank designated as a
       systemically important financial institution.

   (c) Strategic Digital Assets – In the acquisition or development of intellectual property,
       proprietary software, and critical digital infrastructure that provides a long-term
       technical return on investment for the State.

   (d) Digital Transformation Projects – In joint-venture agreements or public-private
       partnerships specifically aimed at scaling the National Tech Stack, provided that the
       Corporation retains control over the sovereign data and core architecture.

   (e) Corporate Bonds – In bonds or other evidence of indebtedness of any stable
       corporation with a track record of profitability for at least the last five (5) years and with
       a high credit rating from a reputable agency.

        All income, dividends, and interests earned from these investments shall be credited
to the Corporation's Digital Sustainability Fund and shall be used exclusively for the
maintenance, upgrading, and continuous innovation of government digital services and
infrastructure.

SECTION 17. Abolition of the E-Government Unified Project Management Office (Egov-
UPMO) under Section 7 of the E-Governance Act. –

   (a) The functions, mandate, powers, responsibilities, programs, projects, personnel, and
       records of the E-Government Unified Management Project Office (Egov-UPMO),
       created under the E-Governance Act, are hereby transferred to and absorbed by the
       GovTech Philippines Corporation.

   (b) GovTech Philippines Corporation shall succeed to and assume all operational,
       technical, and project management functions of Egov-UPMO, including but not limited
       to the planning, development, integration, operation, maintenance, and lifecycle
       management of government digital platforms, shared services, and e-government
       systems.

   (c) All ongoing projects, systems, platforms, and initiatives previously undertaken,
       coordinated, or supervised by Egov-UPMO shall continue without interruption and shall
       be deemed absorbed as projects of GovTech Philippines Corporation, ensuring
       permanent ownership and continuity of national platforms like eGov.ph.

   (d) All references to Egov-UPMO in existing laws, executive orders, rules and regulations,
       administrative issuances, and government instruments shall be deemed references to
       GovTech Philippines Corporation.

   (e) The transfer and absorption provided under this section shall be without prejudice to
       the policy-setting, standard-issuing, and oversight functions of the E-Governance
       Authority created under the E-Governance Act, which shall remain distinct from the
       execution and operational role of GovTech Philippines Corporation.

SECTION 18. GOCC Governance and Oversight. – The GovTech Philippines Corporation
shall be subject to Republic Act No. 10149, otherwise known as the “GOCC Governance Act
of 2011,” and all applicable rules, regulations, and circulars issued by the Governance
Commission for Government-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GCG). The Corporation shall
likewise be subject to audit by the Commission on Audit (COA) and such other accountability
mechanisms as may be provided by law.

SECTION 19. Coordination and Non-Duplication. – The Corporation shall coordinate with
the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), the Department of
Science and Technology (DOST), the National Privacy Commission (NPC), and other
concerned agencies to ensure functional complementarity, avoid duplication, and promote the
efficient delivery of digital government services.

       The Corporation, upon approval of the Board, may designate specific digital services
such as Cloud and Identity, Payments as "Mandatory Shared Services," which all government
agencies must adopt to prevent redundant spending

SECTION 20. Market Competitiveness and Benefits. – Notwithstanding Republic Act No.
6758, as amended, and all laws, rules, and regulations regarding compensation standards—
including the Salary Standardization Law and related issuances—this section covers the
creation, reclassification, and rationalization of both plantilla and non-plantilla positions,
including project-based, fixed-term, and contract engagements for highly specialized
expertise. All actions will be in accordance with applicable civil service, procurement, and
auditing laws.

SECTION 21. Technical Specialist Track. – To attract top-tier engineering talent essential
for a GovTech model, the GovTech Philippines Corporation shall create a dual-career path,
ensuring technical experts, including software architects, cybersecurity engineers, data
scientists, and other identified specialists, receive compensation and benefits that are
competitive with the private sector, regardless of their administrative or managerial status. As
such, the Corporation shall implement and establish a:

  (a) Performance-Based Incentives based on technical milestones and service delivery
      performance, rather than seniority; and

  (b) National Digital Academy that operates as a training arm to continuously upskill
      government employees in agile methodologies, data science, and user-experience
      (UX) design.
SECTION 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Office of the President, in coordination with the Governance
Commission for Government-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GCG), the Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT), the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), the Commission on Audit (COA), and other relevant agencies, shall
promulgate the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act. No provision of the IRR shall expand, diminish, or contradict the
provisions of this Act.

SECTION 23. Annual Report. – The Corporation shall submit an annual report to the
President of the Philippines and to Congress, detailing its financial condition, operations,
performance, and the status of government digital products, platforms, and infrastructure
under its management.

SECTION 24. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 25. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and regulations
or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 26. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.


Approved,
