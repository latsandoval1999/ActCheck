                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

The travel tax is an added charge on airline tickets that was created many years
ago to raise money for tourism, education, and cultural programs. While it was
meant to support public goals, it has become an extra burden on travelers and a
complicated system to collect and manage. Today, there are better and more
transparent ways to fund these programs without charging passengers an
additional tax every time they travel.

This bill removes the travel tax entirely and shifts the funding of affected programs
to the national budget, where they can be openly reviewed and approved by
Congress each year. Instead of relying on an automatic charge hidden in ticket
prices, support for tourism, higher education, and culture will come from clear and
direct appropriations under the General Appropriations Act. This makes
government spending easier to track, fairer, and more accountable to the public.

To protect passengers, the bill clearly states that no travel tax may be collected for
trips on or after the law takes effect. If any passenger is wrongly charged, the bill
requires a proper refund process so people get their money back. In short, this
measure lightens the cost of travel, modernizes public financing, and ensures that
important programs continue to receive support, this time in a way that is open,
fair, and easier for everyone to understand.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the
undersigned respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                            AN ACT
ABOLISHING THE TRAVEL TAX, PROVIDING FOR THE REFUND OF TRAVEL
TAXES COLLECTED FOR TRAVEL ON OR AFTER THE EFFECTIVITY OF THIS
ACT, AND PROVIDING TRANSITIONAL FUNDING FOR PROGRAMS
PREVIOUSLY FUNDED FROM TRAVEL TAX PROCEEDS

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Travel Tax Abolition
Act of 2026.”

SECTION. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to remove the
travel tax burden on passengers while ensuring the continuity of public programs
previously funded from travel tax proceeds through appropriations in the General
Appropriations Act (GAA), consistent with the Constitution and existing budget
laws.

SECTION 3. Abolition of the Travel Tax. —

   (a) The travel tax imposed under Presidential Decree No. 1183, as amended,
       and all other laws, decrees, rules, and regulations establishing, increasing,
       amending, or otherwise implementing the travel tax, are hereby abolished.

   (b) Section 73 of Republic Act No. 9593, otherwise known as the “Tourism Act
       of 2009”, and all related provisions on the collection and allocation of travel
       tax proceeds that are inconsistent with the abolition under this Act are
       hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 4. Prohibition on Collection; Cut-off Rule. —
   (a) No government agency, government instrumentality, government-owned
       or -controlled corporation, or private entity acting as collecting agent or
       intermediary shall collect travel tax for travel scheduled on or after the
       effectivity of this Act.

   (b) Any travel tax amount collected, withheld, charged, or embedded in ticket
       charges for travel scheduled on or after the effectivity of this Act shall be
       unlawful and shall be refunded in accordance with Section 5 hereof.

SECTION 5. Refund of Travel Tax Collected for Travel on or After Effectivity.
—

   (a) Coverage. — Refund shall apply to travel tax payments collected for travel
       scheduled on or after the effectivity date of this Act, regardless of the date
       of ticket purchase.

   (b) Payor/Responsible Party. — The entity that collected the travel tax from
       the passenger (including airlines, travel agents, online booking platforms,
       or other intermediaries) shall process and release the refund to the
       passenger within thirty (30) days from

       (i)    the passenger’s submission of a refund request and proof of
              payment, or
       (ii)   the effectivity of this Act, whichever is later.

   (c) Reimbursement Chain. — The collecting entity may, in accordance with
       the implementing rules, recover the refunded amounts from the
       government entity to which such collections were remitted, or from any
       settlement mechanism established in the IRR, subject to accounting and
       auditing rules.

   (d) Non-waiver. — Any stipulation, ticket condition, or contract term waiving
       the passenger’s right to the refund provided herein shall be void.

   (e) Administrative Liability. — Without prejudice to existing laws, failure or
       refusal to refund covered travel tax amounts within the period provided
       herein, without lawful justification, shall subject responsible officers to
       appropriate administrative sanctions under applicable civil service and
       regulatory rules.

SECTION 6. Transitional Budgeting and Alternative Funding. —


                                                                           3 | Page
   (a) Consistent with the policy recognized under Republic Act No. 9593 that the
       National Government shall look for alternative funding sources for
       programs funded by travel tax in the event of a phase out, Congress shall
       appropriate, through the GAA, amounts sufficient to support the continuity
       of programs previously funded from travel tax proceeds.

   (b) For this purpose, the following shall be included as distinct line items in
       the GAA, subject to existing budget preparation, authorization, and
       execution rules:

       (1) Tourism Infrastructure and Enterprise Zone Authority (TIEZA) — for
           tourism development and tourism infrastructure projects previously
           supported from travel tax proceeds;
       (2) Commission on Higher Education (CHED) — for tourism-related
           programs funded through the Higher Education Development Fund
           component supported by travel tax proceeds; and
       (3) National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA) — for programs
           previously supported from the NCCA share in travel tax proceeds.

   (c) Nothing in this Act shall be construed as authority for any off-GAA transfer,
       cross-border transfer, or augmentation outside the limits allowed by the
       Constitution and jurisprudence.

SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Tourism Infrastructure and Enterprise Zone
Authority (TIEZA), in coordination with the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), the Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB), the Department of
Transportation (DOTr), and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations to ensure:

   (a) immediate cessation of collection;
   (b) an efficient refund process; and
   (c) orderly transition to GAA-based funding. The IRR shall take effect fifteen
       (15) days after publication in a newspaper of general circulation.

SECTION 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
