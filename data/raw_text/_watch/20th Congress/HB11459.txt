    PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9136, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “ELECTRIC
                 POWER INDUSTRY REFORM ACT OF 2001"

                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines now has the highest residential electricity rates in Southeast Asia,
surpassing Singapore.1 As of June 2026, the average residential electricity rate was at
an average of P12.43 per kilowatt hour.2 With volatile global conditions affecting fuel
prices and supply, Filipino households may have to pay even more for electricity.3

Faced with these issues, Filipinos are turning to alternative sources of energy, particularly
solar power. A Pulse Asia survey showed that 93% of Filipinos believe that affordable
rooftop solar panels are necessary due to the rising demand and prices of electricity.4
There is also strong public support for policies that make renewable energy more
accessible, thereby lowering consumers’ electricity expenses.5 In fact, rooftop
installations of solar panels have nearly doubled in the country since early 2025.6

However, our current laws and regulatory framework has yet to fully adapt to the
increasing ability of ordinary consumers to generate and store electricity for their own use.
Republic Act No. 9136, or the “Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001,” was enacted
at a time when electricity generation was largely undertaken by power generation
companies operating large-scale facilities and selling electricity to the grid. Since then,
advances in technology have made small-scale electricity generation more accessible to
households and end-users.

This measure therefore seeks to promote the installation of behind-the-meter electricity
generation and storage, to help Filipinos reduce their electricity expenses and improve
the country’s overall energy security. Behind-the-meter refers to energy systems installed
on the consumer’s side of the utility meter, including solar panels, home batteries, heat
pumps, and EV chargers.7 This is distinguished from front-of-meter systems which are
located on the utility side for large-scale generation and storage and supply to the power
grid.8

This proposed bill aims to reflect this distinction in regulation. As such, end-users who
generate and store electricity for their own use should not be subjected to the same
regulatory requirements imposed upon generation companies and commercial power
producers. Further, systems using equipment certified under standards recognized by the
Energy Regulatory Commission to prevent electricity from flowing into the distribution grid
would no longer need a certificate of compliance or prior ERC authorization. Finally, the
Department of Energy is tasked to promote the widespread adoption of behind-the-meter
electricity generation and energy storage systems and to coordinate the removal of
national and local administrative barriers to their installation. With this measure, we hope
to ease the burden on ordinary Filipinos.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is respectfully sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Sariling Kuryente Act.”

SEC. 2. Generation Sector. — Section 6 of Republic Act No. 9136 is hereby amended
to read as follows:

      SEC. 6. Generation Sector. — Generation of electric power, a business
      affected with public interest, shall be competitive and open.

      Upon the effectivity of this Act, any new generation company shall, before it
      operates, secure from the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) a certificate
      of compliance pursuant to the standards set forth in this Act, as well as health,
      safety and environmental clearances from the appropriate government
      agencies under existing laws.

      AN END-USER THAT INSTALLS AND OPERATES A BEHIND-THE-METER
      ELECTRICITY GENERATION OR ENERGY STORAGE SYSTEM FOR ITS
      OWN USE SHALL NOT, BY REASON THEREOF, BE CONSIDERED A
      GENERATION COMPANY. A SYSTEM USING EQUIPMENT CERTIFIED
      UNDER STANDARDS RECOGNIZED BY THE ERC TO PREVENT THE
      EXPORT OF ELECTRICITY TO THE DISTRIBUTION SYSTEM SHALL NOT
      REQUIRE A CERTIFICATE OF COMPLIANCE OR OTHER PRIOR
      AUTHORIZATION FROM THE ERC.

SEC. 3. Functions of Distribution Utilities. — Section 23 of Republic Act No. 9136 is
hereby amended to read as follows:

      SEC. 23. Functions of Distribution Utilities. — A distribution utility shall have
      the obligation to provide distribution services and connections to its system for
      any end-user within its franchise area consistent with the Distribution Code.

      Any entity engaged therein shall provide open and non-discriminatory access
      to its distribution system to all users. Any distribution utility shall be entitled to
      impose and collect distribution wheeling charges and connection fees from
      such end-users as approved by the ERC. A distribution utility shall have the
      obligation to supply electricity in the least-cost manner to its captive market,
      subject to the collection of retail rate duly approved by the ERC.

      A BEHIND-THE-METER ELECTRICITY GENERATION OR ENERGY
      STORAGE SYSTEM USING EQUIPMENT CERTIFIED UNDER
      STANDARDS RECOGNIZED BY THE ERC TO PREVENT THE EXPORT OF
      ELECTRICITY TO THE DISTRIBUTION SYSTEM SHALL NOT REQUIRE
      THE PRIOR APPROVAL OF, OR ANY AGREEMENT, STUDY, METER
      REPLACEMENT, OR CHARGE IMPOSED BY, THE DISTRIBUTION
      UTILITY. THE END-USER MAY BE REQUIRED ONLY TO GIVE NOTICE OF
      THE INSTALLATION.

      AN APPLICATION FOR THE INTERCONNECTION OF A SYSTEM
      CAPABLE OF EXPORTING ELECTRICITY TO THE DISTRIBUTION
      SYSTEM SHALL BE ACTED UPON WITHIN TEN (10) WORKING DAYS
      FROM RECEIPT OF THE COMPLETE REQUIREMENTS PRESCRIBED BY
      THE ERC. UPON THE LAPSE OF SUCH PERIOD WITHOUT ACTION, THE
      APPLICATION SHALL BE DEEMED APPROVED.

      NO DISTRIBUTION UTILITY MAY IMPOSE ANY REQUIREMENT,
      PROCEDURE, STUDY, EQUIPMENT SPECIFICATION, OR CHARGE NOT
      AUTHORIZED UNDER UNIFORM RULES PROMULGATED BY THE ERC.

SEC. 5. Protection Against Unreasonable Restrictions. — No homeowners’
association, subdivision developer, property manager, or similar entity shall prohibit the
installation of a behind-the-meter electricity generation or energy storage system on
property owned or exclusively controlled by an end-user. Reasonable requirements may
be imposed only for structural safety, public safety, or the protection of common property.
For this purpose, the Department of Energy shall develop and publish model rules that
such entities may adopt.

SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Energy, in consultation with the Energy
Regulatory Commission and other relevant government agencies and stakeholders, shall
promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

SEC. 7. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 9. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
