26                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
27
28           The Philippines is an agricultural country, with the livelihood of the majority of
29   its population directly dependent on farming. Farming generally includes, through both
30   backyard and commercial operations, the raising and production of farm animals such
31   as cattle, carabao, goat, hog, chicken and duck.
32
33          From 2001 to 2013, according to the Bureau of Agricultural Statistics (BAS), the
34   country’s average annual production of carabao was at 137.68 metric thousand tons,
35   while cattle was at 250.98 thousand metric tons. For the same period, average annual
36   production of hog was at 1,826.65 thousand metric tons, goat was at 76.13 thousand
37   metric tons, chicken was at 1,285.40 thousand metric tons, and duck was at 43.24
38   thousand metric tons.
39
40          In 2024, according to data from the Philippine Statistics Authority, the country’s
41   production of carabao was at 129.76 thousand metric tons, while cattle was at 233.33
42   thousand metric tons. Production of hog was at 1,708.79 thousand metric tons, goat
43   was at 67.43 thousand metric tons, chicken was at 2,080.25 thousand metric tons, and
44   duck was at 26.96 thousand metric tons.

54           Most animal raising and production farms are found in rural or far-flung areas in
55   the country where there is, unfortunately, despite the consistently high rate of livestock
56   and poultry production, a pervasive dearth of veterinary services. This can be largely
57   attributable to the fact that the law, particularly Republic Act No. 7160 or The Local
58   Government Code of 1991, specifies that only provincial and city governments are
59   mandatorily required to appoint a veterinarian officer, leaving a great number of
60   municipalities for the most part deprived of access to much-needed public veterinary
61   support and assistance.
62
63          Animal raisers, especially those engaged in backyard operations and are poor,
64   usually cannot afford the services of private veterinarians. They are often made to wait
65   for the provincial or city veterinarian to visit their area, a very rare occasion, in fact,
66   which comes at no certain time.
67
68           Backyard animal raisers are, thus, practically left to themselves in treating animal
69   diseases. They employ traditional forms and ways that do not necessarily guarantee
70   either a quick fix or the prevention of such diseases from being transmitted to other
71   animals or, worse, to people. There are obviously animal diseases, such as cat scratch
72   disease, mad cow disease, and brucellosis, among others, that need the immediate
73   attention and professional expertise of duly licensed veterinarians.
74
75          Moreover, failure to properly treat animal diseases naturally jeopardizes the
76   supply of livestock and poultry products in the market, to the detriment of the public
77   and the economy.
78

                                                                                               2
79          Thus, this Bill, filed previously in the 15th, 16th and 17th congresses by former
80   Anakpawis representatives Rafael Mariano, Fernando Hicap, and Ariel Casilao
81   respectively, is here being refiled with the intent of amending certain provisions of
82   Republic Act No. 7160 or the Local Government Code of 1991 in order to prescribe the
83   mandatory appointment of veterinarian officers not only in provincial and city
84   governments but also in municipal units.
85
86         This legislative measure seeks to decisively address the proliferation of
87   contagious and harmful animal diseases in the countryside and the immediate delivery
88   of public veterinary services to far-flung villages where the raising and production of
89   farm animals is concentrated.
90
91          This measure also aims to expand certain functions and duties of a veterinarian
92   officer in order to more adequately and regularly serve the veterinary needs of remote
93   communities.
94
95          In view of the foregoing, the early passage of this Bill is urgently sought.
