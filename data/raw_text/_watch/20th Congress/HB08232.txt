                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE


        In 2023, the Department of Science and Technology - Food and Nutrition Research
Institute (DOST-FNRI) conducted a survey to determine whether Filipinos check their food labels
before buying. The survey revealed that only 19.6 percent (%) or about 1 in 10 of Pinoy consumers
read food product labels, while only 16.7% of them only check the expiration date and nutrition
facts before buying.1


        Compounding this issue, Filipino children face excessive exposure to television food
advertisements during school and non-school days, where content analyses from various countries
show unhealthy options like sugary and sodium-rich snacks, drinks, and fast food dominating
promotions.2 Evidently, food marketing tends to aim at children creating a toxic food environment
where the cheapest and most visible products on store shelves are often the least nutritious. Time
- and budget-constrained parents thus rely on misleading labels, giving food companies undue
influence over children's diets rather than health experts.3


        It is the declared policy of the State to protect and promote the people’s right to health.
The pervasive marketing of unhealthy foods to children demands regulatory measures to curb their

3              SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to protect and promote
4    the right to health of all Filipinos and instill health consciousness among them. It is likewise the
5    policy of the State to protect and promote the right of every child to the enjoyment of the highest

14                                            ARTICLE II.
15                          FRONT-OF-PACKAGE WARNING LABELS
16          SECTION 5. Mandatory front-of-package warning labels (FOPWL).- No person shall
17   sell, manufacture, import, export, or distribute prepackaged food without a FOPWL as prescribed
18   in this Act, when the final composition of the product contains nutrients of concern that exceed
19   the maximum values prescribed under Section 8 of this Act.


20          SECTION 6. Form, size, placement, and characteristics of the warning label.- When the
21   final composition of the prepackaged food product contains critical nutrients or calories in excess
22   of the maximum values under Section 7 of this Act, the packaging shall display a warning label as
23   prescribed by the FDA, in accordance with the following guidelines:


24      a) The packaging must include one warning label for each nutrient of concern in excess of
25          prescribed values, namely:
26                  “HIGH IN SUGAR”;
27                  “HIGH IN SODIUM”;
28                  “HIGH IN SATURATED FAT”;
29                  “HIGH IN TOTAL FAT”;
30       b) If the product’s calories is in excess of the prescribed values, the package must include
31          the following warning label:


                                                                                                        7
1                   i. “HIGH IN CALORIES”;


2        c) If the product contains sweeteners, the package must include the following warning label:
3                   i. “CONTAINS             SWEETENERS,              NOT RECOMMENDED
4                   FOR CHILDREN”;


5        d) The warning label for (a) and (b) of this section shall have the form of black octagons with
6        a white edge and capital letters;


7        e) The warning label for (c) of this section shall have the form of a black rectangle with a
8        white edge and with capital letters;


9        f) The size of each warning label shall never be less than five percent (5%) of the total area of
10       the main face of the packaging. For products in which the main face of the packaging is equal
11       to or less than ten (10) square centimeters, it will contain a single octagon that will show inside
12       of it how many nutrients of concern above the thresholds the product has. The warning label
13       shall be fifteen (15%) of the total area of the front of the package;


14       g) The warning labels shall form part of the packaging and their full visibility and integrity
15       must not be susceptible to being damaged, concealed, obstructed or changed by any package
16       design, feature, or mechanism, or covered by any other markings;


17       h) The warning label must be located on the top right edge of the product’s main face. If the
18       shape of the product is round or cylindrical, it must be on the top center of the main face;


19       i) If the product has more than one warning label, they must be placed adjacent to each other
20       on the product’s main face; and


21       j) For packages with multiple products, the outer packaging, as well as each individual package
22       inside must also include the warning label.


23          The provisions established in this Section shall be applied in addition to the existing rules
24   and regulations governing the labeling of prepackaged food distributed in the Philippines.

1              SECTION 7. Maximum values of nutrients of concern for prepackaged food.- The
2    Department of Health (DOH) shall develop or adopt a nutrient profile model (NPM) to determine
3    the maximum values of energy, sugar, sodium, saturated fat, total fat, and other nutrients for
4    prepackaged food. Pending the development of a local NPM, a conflict-free, evidence-based NPM
5    recommended by the World Health Organization shall be used as the basis for such maximum
6    values.


7              SECTION 8. Prohibition against misleading claims, other promotional features in the
8    packaging.- It is absolutely prohibited for all prepackaged food required to have a warning label
9    under Section 6 of this Act to contain:
10             a. Complementary nutritional information
11             b. Logotypes or phrases stating the sponsorship or endorsements of scientific societies or
12                civil associations or other relevant actors
13             c. Any other health and nutrition claim; and
14             d. Any advertisement or promotional feature that qualifies as child-directed marketing as
15                defined in this Act.


16             SECTION 9. Misbranding.- A violation under Sections 5, 6, or 8 of this Act shall render
17   the product misbranded under Section 11 of Republic Act No. 3720 as amended by Republic Act
18   no. 9711.


19             SECTION 10. Prohibition against concealment or obstruction of warning labels.-
20   Prepackaged food products required to have a warning label under Section shall be displayed with
21   the warning labels in full view for the consumers in retail stores. The concealment or obstruction
22   of the warning labels in retail stores, whether partial or whole, is prohibited.


23             Any concealment or obstruction of view of warning labels herein shall be considered as a
24   deceptive act or practice under Republic Act No. 7394 or the Consumer Act of the Philippines
25   and is considered a prohibited act therein.


26             SECTION 11. Exemptions.- Prepackaged food for special therapeutic dietary uses that
27   are formulated for a particular physiological condition or as partial replacement for meals, and
28   culinary ingredients which typically consist of only one nutrient in high purity (such as cooking
29   oils, table sugars, common table salt, etc.) shall follow their   respective labeling requirements

1    and shall be exempted from the obligation to incorporate FOPWL. Breastmilk substitutes shall
2    likewise be exempted from this Act and shall follow the guidelines promulgated by Executive
3    Order No. 51, series of 1986 (the Philippine Milk Code), its implementing rules and regulations
4    and other pertinent regulations.

           a) Seizure and       condemnation, destruction,      recall, and/or appropriate

               Section 9 of this Act, or items that constitute prohibited marketing under Sections
               8(d), 12 or 13 of this Act;
           b) The issuance of a Cease and Desist Order (CDO) which shall specify that the trader
               or retailer and/or their agents shall cease and desist from concealment or
               obstruction of view of warning labels under Section 10 of this Act and that they
               shall be required to submit a report of compliance therewith within a reasonable
               time;
           c) A submission of a voluntary assurance of discontinuance of concealment or
               obstruction of warning labels under Section 10 of this Act;
           d) Issuance of take-down orders against prohibited advertising, promotion, and
               sponsorship content under Sections 12 or 13 of this Act;
           e) Suspension, revocation, or cancellation of applicable licenses, registrations, and
               permits to operate for business establishments and operators or drivers of public
               utility vehicles; including the submission of a recommendation to the LGU
               concerned, through proper channels, for the cancellation or suspension of licenses,
               registrations, and permits;
           f) The withholding of any permit, license, authority, or registration pending from the
               appropriate agency or entity;
           g) Disapproval of an application for the issuance or renewal of a Certificate of

17   reports required under this Act shall be made available to the public primarily via the DOH website
18   and other means to readily access such information.

23   The DOH is also authorized to demand accountability from such agencies or LGUs for failure to
24   fulfill its duties under this Act, without prejudice to other remedies or penalties and sanctions that
25   are available or may be imposed by law for such delay or non-compliance.
