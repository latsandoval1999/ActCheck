EXPLANATORY NOTE

The right to suffrage or the right to vote is embodied in three instruments: Universal
Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR); the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights;
and in the 1987 Philippine Constitution. The State has an important and substantial interest in
seeing to it that the conduct of elections be honest, orderly, and peaceful. Throughout history,
countless individuals fought for the right to vote. People from many backgrounds endured
immense hardships and discrimination to secure this fundamental right. By exercising your right
to vote, you honor their struggle and show gratitude for the progress made towards a more
equitable and inclusive society.1

To adapt to modern technological advancements, this bill seeks to amend Republic Act
No. 8189, otherwise known as "The Voter's Registration Act of 1996", by modernizing the
registration process through the institution of an online and automated registration system
alongside traditional in-person filing, thereby creating a more accessible, clean, complete,
permanent, and updated list of voters.

Overall, this proposed legislative measure seeks to streamline data management, inter-
agency cooperation, and record preservation. It also seeks to introduce crucial legal safeguards
and procedural updates to protect the integrity of the electoral roll. Through these proposed
amendments, this bill seeks to safeguard democratic participation while modernizing electoral
administration in the Philippines.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Commission on Elections, in consultation with the Department of
Information and Communications Technology, Department of Science and Technology,
Department of the Interior and Local Government, National Commission for Senior Citizens, and
other stakeholders shall promulgate the rules and regulations for the effective implementation
of this Act.

SEC. 17. Separability Clause. — If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall remain in
force and effect.

SEC. 18. Repealing Clause. — All other laws, acts, presidential decrees, executive orders,
issuances, presidential proclamations, rules and regulations or parts thereof, which are contrary
to and inconsistent with any provision of this Act, are hereby repealed, amended or modified

accordingly.

SEC. 19. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
