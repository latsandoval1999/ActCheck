14                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE
15
16   Article XIV, Section 1 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution explicitly mandates that the State
17   "shall protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, and
18   shall take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all." Furthermore, Article
19   II, Section 13 underscores that the State "recognizes the vital role of the youth in nation-
20   building and shall promote and protect their physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and
21   social well-being."

22   Implicit in these constitutional mandates is the obligation of the State to maintain learning
23   environments where students, educators, and school workers can exercise their
24   constitutional right to education free from physical harm, fear, and violence.

25   However, our educational institutions—long revered as safe sanctuaries for learning and
26   development—are increasingly being shattered by an unprecedented surge of violence. In
27   recent months, a terrifying pattern of physical attacks, knife stabbings, and deadly shootings
28   inside and immediately around school premises across the country has laid bare severe gaps
29   in campus security, child protection, and early intervention mechanisms:

30         June 16, 2026 (General Trias, Cavite): A Grade 8 student armed with a kitchen
31          knife entered a Grade 5 classroom at Bethel Academy and attacked seven young
32          pupils without provocation, leaving two needing emergency surgery.
33         June 19, 2026 (Cavite City): A Senior High School student was repeatedly stabbed
34          in the arm and back during a violent altercation inside Cavite National High School.
35         June 22, 2026 (Tacloban City): Two minors armed with firearms opened fire at San
36          Jose National High School, killing three students and injuring 20 others.
37         June 23, 2026 (San Carlos City, Negros Occidental): A 17-year-old Grade 10
38          student was stabbed outside Julio Ledesma National High School due to a personal
39          grudge.
40         July 1, 2026 (Zamboanga City & Bacolod City): Stabbing incidents erupted
41          involving students at Cabaluay National High School in Zamboanga and near Sum-
42          ag National High School in Bacolod.
43         July 31, 2026 (Las Piñas City): A 13-year-old Grade 9 student was fatally stabbed
44          in the chest by a 14-year-old classmate right outside a classroom during recess at Las
45          Piñas National High School.
46         August 18, 2026 (Zamboanga City): A tragic shooting incident inside the Junior
47          High School premises of Ateneo de Zamboanga University left two dead.
48         September 18, 2026 (Banga, South Cotabato): A horrific campus shooting at
49          Banga National High School claimed 3 lives and wounded 8 others.

50   Between June and September 2026 alone, at least eight major violent incidents involving
51   bladed weapons and firearms have resulted in 9 fatalities and over 40 injuries among
52   learners. This alarming wave of campus violence—compounded by bullying, mental health
53    crises, and the exposure of vulnerable youth to online radicalization and extremism—
54    demands an immediate, structured, and comprehensive legislative intervention.

55    This bill seeks to institutionalize a Comprehensive School Safety Framework. By
56    adopting a whole-of-society and all-hazards approach, the measure strengthens institutional
57    response mechanisms across prevention, preparedness, and recovery. Furthermore, it
58    mandates seamless coordination among the Department of Education (DepEd), local
59    government units (LGUs), law enforcement agencies, and community stakeholders to
60    enforce strict access controls, mental health support, and child protection policies.

 61   In light of the constitutional duty to safeguard the youth and preserve the right to a secure,
 62   quality education, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

        AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING A COMPREHENSIVE SCHOOL SAFETY
       FRAMEWORK, ENSURING THE SAFETY AND WELL-BEING OF LEARNERS
       AND SCHOOL PERSONNEL, AND PROMOTING A SAFE AND SUPPORTIVE
                           SCHOOL COMMUNITY

121         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
122   Congress assembled:

123          SECTION 1. Short Title.
124          This Act shall be known as the “Comprehensive School Safety Act.”
125

126          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy.
127          It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect and promote the right of all
128   learners and school personnel to an enabling, safe, healthy, and supportive learning and
129   working environment where all learners and school personnel are protected and secured
130   from any form of abuse, violence, or harm.
131

132          The State likewise recognizes that health promotion and healthy learning
133   environments are integral to school safety, and shall therefore promote conditions, policies,
134   practices, and environments that prevent harm, support physical, mental, and social well-
135   being, and enable learners and school personnel to thrive.
136

137   Towards this end, the State shall:
138      a) Adopt a comprehensive school safety framework that fosters a whole-of-society and
139          all-hazards approach in the development, implementation, monitoring, and
140          evaluation of school safety policies, programs and plans;
141      b) Institutionalize an integrated school safety system for basic education institutions
142          that ensures prevention from, response to, and recovery from threats and incidents
143          relating to school safety.
144      c) Strengthen coordination among the DepEd, national government agencies, LGUs,
145          and other community stakeholders in the implementation of school safety measures;
146      d) Ensure the provision of adequate human resources, infrastructure, equipment,
147          technology, financial support, and other resources necessary for the effective
148          implementation of this Act; and
149      e) Promote learner-centered, fully inclusive, evidence-based, and developmentally
150          appropriate approaches in the design and implementation of school safety programs
151          and interventions.
152

153          SECTION 3. Scope and Coverage.
154          This Act shall apply to all public and private basic education institutions, hereinafter
155   referred to as “schools,” including elementary and secondary schools and other basic
156   education institutions as may be covered by the Department of Education (DepEd).
157

158          The provisions of this Act shall apply to all learners, school personnel, and other
159   members of the school community and shall encompass measures for the prevention of,
160   response to, and recovery from hazards and threats to their safety and well-being within
161   school premises and in other venues related to school activities, whether conducted on or
162   off campus, including those conducted through or involving digital platforms and
163   technologies.
164

165          Acts such as violence, child abuse, neglect, exploitation, bullying, discrimination,
166   harassment, gender-based violence, corporal punishment, and retaliation committed by
167   students or school personnel against students or school personnel are prohibited.
168

169          For purposes of this Act, school safety encompasses the protection and promotion of
170   the physical, mental, emotional, psychosocial, and digital safety and well-being of learners,
171   school personnel, and other members of the school community, as well as the prevention
172   and appropriate management of violence, abuse, bullying, discrimination, harassment,
173   exploitation, emergencies, disasters, and other threats to their safety and well-being.
174

175                                             TITLE II
176

177                    SCHOOL SAFETY FRAMEWORK AND POLICIES
178                      CHAPTER I. SCHOOL SAFETY FRAMEWORK
179

180          SECTION 4. Establishment of the National Comprehensive School Safety
181   Framework.
182          The Department of Education (DepEd), in coordination with the Department of
183   Health (DOH), the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), the
184   Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the Philippine National Police
185   (PNP), Local Government Units (LGUs), National Youth Commission (NYC), and other
186   relevant government stakeholders shall establish, maintain, and periodically update a
187   National Comprehensive School Safety Framework, hereinafter referred to as the
188   “Framework,” to guide the implementation of this Act in all schools.
189

190          The Framework shall, at the minimum, prescribe national standards for the
191   prevention of, response to, and recovery from threats and incidents relating to school safety,
192   including:
193

194      a) The development, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of School Safety
195          Plans and School Safety Handbooks;
196      b) School safety personnel, staffing guidelines, competency standards, and capacity-
197          building;
198      c) School safety protocols, infrastructure, facilities, equipment, and security measures;
199      d) Learner protection, violence prevention, and anti-bullying mechanisms;
200      e) Digital safety, responsible technology use, and cybersecurity;
201      f) Learner well-being, mental health promotion, counseling services, and psychosocial
202          support;
203      g) Emergency preparedness, disaster risk reduction and management, crisis response,
204          recovery, and business continuity;
205      h) Periodic engagement with parents and other community stakeholders;
206      i) Meaningful participation and empowerment of learners and the youth in matters
207          affecting school safety and well-being;
208      j) Reporting school safety concerns such as those involving school bullying and other
209          acts and forms of violence;
210      k) Detection of health and safety concerns, as well as assessment, referral, case
211          management, and inter-agency coordination to address health and safety concerns
212          and mitigate risk;
213      l) Monitoring, evaluation, reporting, and continuous improvement; and
214      m) Such other standards as may be necessary to effectively implement this Act.
215

216          The Framework shall likewise harmonize and integrate existing laws, policies, and
217   mechanisms on school safety, protection, health and well-being into a unified system.
218

219          The DepEd, in consultation with other stakeholders, shall develop and periodically
220   update policies, templates, manuals, protocols, reporting forms, training modules,
221   assessment tools, and other implementation resources necessary to assist schools in
222   complying with this Act.
223          The Framework shall be reviewed at least once every two years to ensure that it
224   remains responsive to new and emerging challenges in school safety.
225

226          SECTION 5. Establishment of School Safety Plans. – Every school shall develop
227   and annually update a School Safety Plan that localizes and operationalizes the Framework
228   to the school’s unique needs, risks, and resources, emphasizing the safety, protection, and
229   well-being of learners, as well as school personnel.
230

231          The School Safety Plan shall be harmonized with other school plans related to safety,
232   school improvement, Disaster Risk Reduction and Management (DRRM), child protection,
233   and healthy learning institutions, among others.
234

235          The Plan shall include:
236      a) School safety standards, including standards for prevention, response, and recovery;
237      b) Protocols and manuals for emergencies, including hotlines for reporting, designated
238          hospitals, police stations, safety zones, evacuation areas, referrals for mental health
239          needs, and a fixed-timeline referral pathway for serious threats and abuse;
240      c) Clear roles and responsibilities of members of the school community;
241      d) Protective safety measures, including security infrastructure and equipment;
242      e) Policies on safe, responsible, developmentally appropriate, and healthy use of digital
243          technologies, including gadgets, social media, gaming, artificial intelligence, and
244          other emerging technologies, as well as measures addressing digital safety,
245          cybersecurity, and digital wellness, on and off campus;
246      f) Clear mechanisms for recovery, such as intervention and reintegration plans for
247          learners and personnel affected, coordination referrals to government agencies, and
248          post-incident reviews;
249      g) Guidelines and protocols for off-campus activities;
250      h) Community engagement and partnership strategies to strengthen school safety,
251          including coordination with parents and guardians, LGUs, community organizations,
252          health and mental health service providers, security and emergency response groups,
253          and other relevant stakeholders; and
254      i) Monitoring and evaluation mechanisms.
255

256          The School Safety Plan shall be developed in consultation with the school
257   community, including school personnel, learners, parents or guardians, and other relevant
258   stakeholders.
259

260         The School Head shall be responsible for overseeing the implementation of the
261   School Safety Plan within the school. The School Head shall submit the Plan to the Schools
262   Division Office (SDO) for review and approval at the beginning of each school year. The
263   SDO shall ensure that the Plan is aligned with the Framework and complies with the
264   minimum standards set forth in this Act. Upon approval, the Plan shall be presented to the
265   Local School Board to facilitate coordination with the local government and inform the
266   development and updating of relevant local plans and programs.
267

268         SECTION 6. School Safety Handbook.
269         Every school shall prepare, maintain, and regularly update a School Safety
270   Handbook, as part of the school’s Learner Handbook, to communicate essential school
271   safety information to learners, parents or guardians, school personnel, visitors, and other
272   members of the school community.
273

274         The School Safety Handbook shall present, in a clear, concise, accessible, child-
275   sensitive and user-friendly manner, the school safety policies, procedures, emergency
276   information, and learner support services that members of the school community need to
277   know in order to promote safety and respond appropriately during emergencies and other
278   school safety situations. It shall be made available in Filipino and prevailing regional
279   languages.
280

281         At a minimum, the School Safety Handbook shall contain:
282      a) The school's commitment to safety and learner well-being;
283      b) The School Code of Conduct and key school safety policies for learners and school
284         personnel;
285      c) Procedures for reporting bullying, violence, abuse, discrimination, harassment,
286         emergencies, and other school safety concerns;
287      d) Emergency procedures for fire, earthquakes, severe weather, lockdowns, medical
288         emergencies, evacuations, and other applicable situations;
289      e) Emergency contact information for school officials, emergency responders,
290         designated hospitals, and relevant government agencies;
291      f) School maps showing evacuation routes, emergency exits, assembly and evacuation
292         areas, clinics, School Care Centers, guidance offices, nearest hospitals, and other
293         important locations;
294      g) Visitor management procedures and campus access guidelines;
295      h) Learner support services, including counseling, psychosocial support, referral
296         services, and School Care Centers;
297      i) Digital safety guidelines and responsible technology use;
298      j) Such other practical school safety information as may be prescribed by the DepEd;
299          and
300      k) Digital wellness and responsible technology use, including developmentally
301          appropriate use of digital devices, social media, online gaming, artificial intelligence
302          and other emerging technologies; healthy digital habits; prevention of digital harms;
303          and pathways for help-seeking and referral when technology use adversely affects
304          learner health, development, education, relationships, or daily functioning.
305

306          The School Safety Handbook shall be made readily available in printed and
307   electronic formats, as appropriate, and shall be disseminated to learners, parents or
308   guardians, school personnel, and other members of the school community at the beginning
309   of every school year and whenever significant revisions are made.
310

311          Schools shall also conduct an annual orientation on the School Safety Handbook to
312   ensure that members of the school community understand their rights, responsibilities, and
313   the procedures for preventing and responding to safety-related concerns.
314

315          The School Safety Handbook shall be periodically reviewed in consultation with
316   learners, parents or guardians, school personnel, and other relevant stakeholders.
317

318                        CHAPTER II. SCHOOL SAFETY POLICIES
319

320          SECTION 7. Policy on Safe School Culture.
321          Every school shall foster a safe, inclusive, respectful, and supportive school culture
322   that cultivates socioemotional learning, empathy, peer connectedness, and the preservation
323   of human dignity, in accordance with applicable DepEd and DOH standards on the social
324   and physical environment of schools. Schools shall promote positive relationships, mutual
325   respect, responsibility, resilience, and meaningful participation among all members of the
326   school community through preventive, developmentally appropriate, and evidence-based
327   policies, programs, and practices that strengthen positive behavior, respectful conflict
328   resolution, and a shared commitment to maintaining a safe learning environment.
329

330          SECTION 8. Policy Against Violence and Harm.
331          Every school shall adopt and enforce a policy against all forms of violence and harm,
332   including physical violence, child abuse, exploitation, bullying, discrimination, harassment,
333   sexual and gender-based violence, corporal punishment, retaliation, and other acts that
334   threaten the safety, dignity, or well-being of learners and school personnel, whether
335   occurring in person or through digital or other means. Violence or harmful conduct
336   perpetrated by school personnel against learners shall be subject to applicable child
337   protection laws and appropriate administrative, disciplinary, and legal measures. For
338   incidents involving learners, schools shall ensure that violence and harmful conduct are
339   promptly addressed and shall implement interventions that are fair, proportionate,
340   developmentally appropriate, trauma-informed, and restorative, where appropriate, with due
341   regard to the safety and rights of all persons involved.
342

343          SECTION 9. Anti-Bullying and Learner Protection Policy.
344          Pursuant to Republic Act No. 10627, or the Anti-Bullying Act of 2013, every school
345   shall adopt and implement a comprehensive Anti-Bullying and Learner Protection Policy
346   that promotes the safety, dignity, rights, and well-being of all learners and ensures the
347   prevention, early identification, prompt reporting, timely intervention, and appropriate
348   resolution of incidents involving bullying, cyberbullying, harassment, including
349   technology-facilitated harassment and abuse, violence, child abuse, neglect, exploitation,
350   discrimination, impersonation, non-consensual sharing of personal or intimate content,
351   retaliation, and other acts that threaten the safety or welfare of learners. The Policy shall
352   provide for prompt mental health and psychosocial assessment, support, and appropriate
353   intervention for learners affected by bullying, including both the learner who has been
354   subject to bullying and the learner who has engaged in bullying behavior, in coordination
355   with their respective families and licensed professionals, as appropriate.
356

357          SECTION 10. School Code of Conduct.
358          The School Safety Handbook shall include a School Code of Conduct that promotes
359   a culture of respect, responsibility, integrity, inclusivity, and accountability among members
360   of the school community. The Code shall clearly define unacceptable acts, corresponding
361   disciplinary and restorative interventions, and the rights and responsibilities of all members
362   of the school community. In implementing the Code, schools shall ensure that disciplinary
363   measures are fair, proportionate, developmentally appropriate, non-discriminatory, and
364   restorative in nature, with due regard for the best interests of the child, due process, and the
365   rights of school personnel.
366

367          SECTION 11. Learner Participation and Voice.
368          Every school shall recognize and promote the right of learners to participate
369   meaningfully in matters affecting their safety, well-being, and school life, consistent with
370   their evolving capacities and the best interests of the child.
371
372          Schools      shall     establish   age-appropriate,   inclusive,   and   learner-responsive
373   mechanisms through which learners may express their views, report safety concerns,
374   contribute to school safety initiatives, and participate in the development, implementation,
375   monitoring, and evaluation of school safety policies and programs. Schools shall provide
376   opportunities for the student council and other recognized learner organizations to
377   participate in school safety initiatives and such processes.
378

379   Learner participation shall be safe, respectful, and free from discrimination, retaliation, or
380   intimidation.
381

382          SECTION 12. Consistency with Existing Laws and Policies.
383          The policies, programs, standards, and procedures adopted and implemented
384   pursuant to this Act shall be consistent with existing laws, rules and regulations, and
385   applicable policies of the DepEd and other relevant agencies on child protection, anti-
386   bullying, positive discipline, mental health, gender-based violence, safe spaces, learner
387   welfare, and other matters relating to the safety, dignity, rights, and well-being of learners
388   and school personnel.
389                                                 TITLE III
390                                  SCHOOL SAFETY GOVERNANCE
391

392          SECTION 13. School Safety Governance.
393          Every school shall establish a comprehensive school safety governance system to
394   ensure the effective implementation of this Act and to promote a safe, secure, inclusive,
395   supportive, and resilient learning environment. This may utilize or expand on existing
396   school governance mechanisms, with clear designation of school safety functions and
397   responsibilities.
398

399          Every school shall organize or designate an appropriate school safety governance
400   mechanism composed of school personnel responsible for learner protection, counseling
401   and psychosocial support, school safety and security, health services, disaster risk reduction
402   and management, and other functions as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
403

404          SECTION 14. Key School Safety Personnel.
405          Every school shall ensure the availability of qualified school personnel necessary to
406   effectively implement this Act.
407

408          The DepEd            shall prescribe    staffing standards,    competency    requirements,
409   qualifications, deployment guidelines, training, and continuing professional development
410   requirements for key school safety personnel. Schools shall progressively strengthen the
411   staffing of key personnel in public schools, consistent with national staffing standards and
412   subject to the availability of funds. Key school safety personnel may be designated from
413   among qualified existing school personnel.
414

415      a) School Head. – The School Head shall serve as the overall lead for the
416         implementation of this Act at the school level, and shall:
417             1. Oversee the implementation of the School Safety Plan;
418             2. Ensure compliance with national school safety standards;
419             3. Provide leadership in fostering a safe, inclusive, and supportive school
420                environment;
421             4. Allocate school resources to support school safety initiatives;
422             5. Ensure coordination among school personnel, parents, LGUs, concerned
423                agencies, and relevant stakeholders;
424             6. Oversee incident management and post-incident recovery; and
425             7. Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
426

427      b) School Counselor. – Pursuant to Republic Act No. 12080, or the Basic Education
428         Mental Health and Well-Being Promotion Act, the School Counselor shall serve as
429         the lead professional responsible for guidance, counseling, learner mental health
430         promotion, and psychosocial support, and shall:
431             1. Provide counseling services;
432             2. Conduct risk and psychosocial assessments;
433             3. Lead mental health promotion initiatives;
434             4. Coordinate referrals to appropriate service providers;
435             5. Support    case   management      involving   learners   requiring   specialized
436                intervention;
437             6. Provide technical guidance to school personnel on learner well-being; and
438             7. Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the Department.
439

440      c) School Counselor Associates. – School Counselor Associates shall assist the School
441         Counselor in implementing learner support programs and shall facilitate access to
442         appropriate guidance and psychosocial services.
443

444                In cases where a School Counselor is not available, School Counselor
445         Associates may serve as the lead professional for the delivery of school-based mental
446         health services, while ensuring appropriate referrals to mental health professionals,
447         pursuant to Republic Act No. 12080.
448

449   d) Learner Formation Officer. – Consistent with Republic Act No. 10627, the Learner
450      Formation Officer shall serve as the primary focal person for learner formation and
451      the first responder to reports of bullying and other learner-related incidents. The
452      Learner Formation Officer shall:
453         1. Receive, document, and facilitate the appropriate initial response to reports of
454            bullying, peer violence, harassment, and other incidents involving learners;
455         2. Ensure that reported incidents are promptly referred to the School Head,
456            guidance or counseling personnel, School Care Center, Child Protection
457            Committee, or other appropriate authorities, as warranted;
458         3. Coordinate the implementation of preventive and formative programs that
459            promote safe school culture;
460         4. Support the implementation of learner-led and peer-based initiatives,
461            including peer mentoring, buddy systems, bystander and upstander programs,
462            and other learner formation activities;
463         5. Promote learner awareness of their rights and responsibilities, the School
464            Code of Conduct, reporting mechanisms, and available protection and support
465            services;
466         6. Monitor patterns and recurring concerns involving bullying, peer violence,
467            and learner behavior, and recommend appropriate preventive interventions;
468         7. Regularly monitor actions taken on referrals of incidents of bullying, peer
469            violence and other learner-related incidents; and
470         8. Coordinate with classroom advisers, guidance or counseling personnel,
471            parents or guardians, and other appropriate school personnel in addressing
472            learner safety and formation concerns.
473

474   e) School Safety Officer. – Every school shall designate a School Safety Officer who
475      shall serve as the principal coordinator for the implementation of school safety
476      programs under this Act. The School Safety Officer shall:
477         1. Coordinate implementation of the School Safety Plan;
478         2. Monitor compliance with school safety standards;
479         3. Coordinate emergency preparedness activities;
480         4. Oversee school safety inspections;
481         5. Manage a non-threatening reporting system within the school;
482         6. Coordinate reporting and documentation;
483         7. Facilitate coordination with government agencies and emergency responders;
484         8. Support school safety training and drills; and
485         9. Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
486      f) School Health Personnel. – School nurses, school physicians, school dentists, and
487          other qualified school health personnel shall provide health services and support
488          school health and wellness programs consistent with existing laws and DepEd
489          policies. They shall:
490              1. Provide appropriate school health services;
491              2. Participate in emergency preparedness and response;
492              3. Support mental health and psychosocial interventions;
493              4. Assist in health promotion and disease prevention activities;
494              5. Coordinate referrals to health facilities;
495              6. Coordinate and report to the LGUs cases of disease outbreaks in the school;
496                    and
497              7. Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
498

499          School health personnel should be trained to recognize physical or somatic
500   complaints, including recurrent headaches, abdominal pain, sleep difficulties, as well as
501   tendencies for violence and self-harm, as possible manifestations of bullying, anxiety, or
502   abuse, identify signs of psychological distress, and facilitate referral to the School
503   Counselor, other qualified school personnel, or appropriate health or mental health
504   professionals.
505

506          Where qualified school health personnel are not available on-site, schools shall
507   establish linkages with health and mental health professionals and facilities, including,
508   where relevant, through teleconsultation or other consultative arrangements with DOH-
509   retained or accredited facilities.
510

511      g) Security Personnel. – Security guards and other authorized security personnel shall
512          help maintain a safe and secure school environment. They shall:
513              1. Regulate access to school premises;
514              2. Implement visitor management procedures;
515              3. Assist in emergency response;
516              4. Report security incidents;
517              5. Support the implementation of campus safety measures; and
518              6. Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
519

520          Schools shall have adequate security guards or trained security personnel, taking into
521   consideration school size, enrollment, operating hours, location, and security risk.
522
523          In accordance with applicable DILG issuances, LGUs may extend auxiliary support
524   through the deployment of barangay tanods or local law enforcement to secure the vicinity
525   and immediate public perimeters of schools, subject to local risk assessment, capacity, and
526   the availability of local funds, including the Special Education Fund (SEF), as may be
527   authorized under applicable laws.
528

529      h) Classroom Adviser. – The Classroom Adviser shall promote learner well-being
530          within the classroom and serve as the primary point of contact for learners and
531          parents on routine learner welfare concerns. The Classroom Adviser shall:
532             1. Monitor learner well-being and classroom climate;
533             2. Be trained in the identification of signs of psychological distress such as self-
534                 harm or violent behavior;
535             3. Identify learners requiring additional support based on indicators such as, but
536                 not limited to, chronic or sudden absence, repeated tardiness, withdrawal, and
537                 abrupt decline in participation or performance, among others;
538             4. Coordinate with the School Counselor and other school personnel on
539                 appropriate interventions;
540             5. Maintain regular and periodic communication with parents or guardians
541                 regarding learner welfare;
542             6. Promote positive discipline and learner participation; and
543             7. Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
544

545          In recognition of their critical role in safeguarding the safety and well-being of
546   learners, every Classroom Adviser shall be provided with the commensurate teaching loads
547   dedicated exclusively to learner safety and well-being responsibilities, including learner
548   monitoring, parent engagement, case documentation, home visitation, when necessary,
549   participation in case conferences, implementation of interventions, and coordination with
550   other school personnel for student safety.
551

552          The DepEd shall ensure that the corresponding teaching load reduction, staffing
553   requirements, and funding necessary to implement this provision are reflected in its staffing
554   standards and annual appropriations.
555

556      i) Student Leaders. – Student leaders, including officers of the student council and
557          recognized learner organizations, shall support the promotion of a safe, inclusive,
558          and respectful school environment. They shall:
559             1. Assist in raising awareness of school safety policies and reporting
560                 mechanisms;
561             2. Promote positive behavior, peer support, and responsible bystander action;

564             4. Be trained to recognize peers experiencing safety or psychosocial concerns,

694            CHAPTER I. School Safety Infrastructure and Protective Measures
695

696         SECTION 17. Safety Infrastructure and Equipment.
697         Schools shall be equipped with appropriate safety infrastructure and security
698   equipment, including, as applicable:
699      a) Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV). - CCTV cameras shall be installed in common
700         areas, entrances and exits, perimeter areas, and other locations identified through the
701         school's risk assessment. CCTV shall not be installed in areas where there is a
702         reasonable expectation of privacy. The collection, storage, access, use, and disposal
703         of CCTV footage shall comply with applicable data privacy laws and regulations and
704         shall be used solely for investigating documented bullying or security incidents.
705      b) Screening Equipment. – Schools shall have access to appropriate screening
706         equipment, including metal detectors, where warranted by the school's risk profile.
707         Screening shall be conducted by authorized and appropriately trained personnel in a
708         reasonable, proportionate, non-discriminatory, and child-sensitive manner.
709      c) Visitor Management System. – Schools shall establish appropriate systems to
710         regulate and monitor visitor entry into and movement within school premises,
711         including visitor identification, registration, verification, and access control.
712      d) Security Personnel and Protective Presence. – Schools shall ensure appropriate
713         security and protective presence based on their size, enrollment, location, and risk
714         profile. This may include licensed security guards and coordinated support from the
715         Philippine National Police (PNP), barangay tanods, safety officers, community
716         volunteers, and other authorized personnel.
717      e) Perimeter and Access Security. – Schools shall maintain appropriate physical
718         measures to secure school boundaries and regulate access, including, as appropriate,
719         gates, fencing, lighting, locks, barriers, and other protective infrastructure.
720

721         SECTION 18. Emergency, Health, and Life-Safety Equipment.
722         Schools shall be equipped with appropriate emergency, health, and life-safety
723   equipment, including, as applicable:
724      a) first aid kits and essential emergency medical supplies;
725      b) fire extinguishers and other required fire safety equipment;
726      c) emergency lighting, flashlights, batteries, emergency power supplies, and other
727         disaster preparedness supplies;
728      d) rescue and evacuation equipment appropriate to identified hazards; and
729      e) emergency communication and alert systems, including public address systems,
730         alarms, emergency notification systems, two-way radios, and other equipment.
731

732         SECTION 19. Preventive Safety Measures.
733         Every school shall implement preventive safety measures appropriate to its
734   circumstances to reduce risks, strengthen preparedness, and promote a safe and secure
735   learning environment. At a minimum, schools shall implement:
736      a) Risk Mapping and Assessment. – Regularly identify physical, social, behavioral,
737         environmental, and digital risks, including locations and circumstances that may
738         increase the likelihood of violence, bullying, abuse, harassment, accidents,
739         emergencies, digital harm, harmful or unsafe technology use, or other threats to
740         learner safety, health, development, and well-being. Findings shall inform the School
741         Safety Plan and preventive interventions.
742      b) Campus Security Monitoring. – Maintain appropriate monitoring measures,
743         including security patrols, monitoring of entrances and exits, observation of common
744         and high-risk areas, and review of security camera footage where available,
745         consistent with the school's risk profile and applicable privacy and child protection
746         laws.
747      c) Visitor Screening. – Establish procedures for visitor identification, registration,
748          verification, issuance of passes where appropriate, and monitoring of visitor access,
749          while ensuring access for emergency responders and other authorized persons.
750      d) Mandatory Screening and Inspection. – Conduct mandatory screening of persons
751          and belongings, including bag inspections or use of metal detectors, where
752          authorized and warranted by the school's risk assessment and applicable DepEd
753          standards. Screening shall be conducted by authorized personnel in a respectful, non-
754          discriminatory, child-sensitive, and developmentally appropriate manner.
755      e) Confiscation and Disposition of Prohibited Items. – Establish procedures for the
756          identification, confiscation, documentation, safekeeping, turnover, and lawful
757          disposition of prohibited items, including appropriate due process, notification of
758          parents or guardians when necessary, and coordination with authorities when
759          required by law.
760      f) Emergency Drills. – Regularly conduct developmentally appropriate, trauma-
761          informed, and inclusive drills for fires, earthquakes, severe weather, medical
762          emergencies, active attacks, other security incidents, and other relevant hazards.
763          Such drills shall be designed to prepare learners and school personnel to respond
764          appropriately to emergencies while minimizing unnecessary fear or distress and shall
765          be conducted with appropriate advance notice and in consultation with relevant
766          professionals.
767      g) Safety Orientation and Awareness. – Provide developmentally appropriate and role-
768          appropriate safety orientation for learners, school personnel, parents and guardians,
769          and other members of the school community. These orientations shall cover the
770          National Comprehensive School Safety Framework and the School Safety Plan,
771          protocols for prevention from, response to, and recovery from school safety threats
772          and incidents, digital safety, and responsible technology use.
773      h) Safety Audits and Preventive Maintenance. – Conduct an annual School Safety Audit
774          and regular inspections of infrastructure, facilities, equipment, emergency systems,
775          and other safety measures, and implement corrective and preventive measures based
776          on identified hazards and deficiencies. Findings shall inform the School Safety Plan
777          and resource priorities.
778

779          SECTION 20. General Standards for School Safety Measures.
780          All school safety policies, infrastructure, equipment, and measures under this
781   Chapter shall be appropriate to the school's size, enrollment, location, risk profile, specific
782   needs, and subject to the availability of funds.
783
784           The DepEd, in coordination with relevant national government agencies and other
785   stakeholders, shall establish national standards and operational guidelines for the provision,
786   implementation, use, maintenance, and monitoring of infrastructure and equipment under
787   this Chapter, including, where applicable, their installation, inspection, replacement, and
788   data management and disposal.
789

790           SECTION 21. Nature- and Climate-Related School Safety Protocols.
791           The DepEd, in coordination with the DILG, DOST, DOH, DENR, DPWH and other
792   relevant agencies, shall establish and periodically update national policies for the
793   assessment, management, risk-reduction and recovery from nature- and climate-related
794   hazards affecting schools. These standards shall cover extreme heat conditions, tropical
795   cyclones, flooding, earthquakes, air quality, and other related hazards that may endanger the
796   safety of learners and school personnel.
797

798           The policy shall include a systemic plan for the construction of disaster-resilient
799   infrastructure, and assessment and prioritization for retrofitting or replacement of unsafe
800   building structures, taking into consideration the natural- and climate-related hazards of the
801   area.

804

805           SECTION 22. Safety During Off-Campus Activities.
806           Schools conducting authorized off-campus activities shall adopt and implement
807   measures to ensure the safety, security, health, and well-being of learners and school
808   personnel participating in such activities. Schools shall take reasonable and appropriate
809   measures to prevent foreseeable harm to learners and school personnel throughout the
810   activity.
811

812           Prior to any authorized off-campus activity, the school shall conduct an appropriate
813   risk assessment and prepare an activity-specific safety plan, which shall include learner
814   supervision arrangements and designated personnel-in-charge, transportation safety
815   measures, emergency communication and response procedures, emergency contact
816   information for participating learners, and such other safety measures as may be prescribed
817   by DepEd.
818

819           Schools shall also ensure full disclosure to parents or guardians of information
820   regarding the activity, including the objectives of the itinerary, transport arrangements,
821   accommodations, identified risks, emergency procedures, and contact information of
822   responsible personnel. Schools shall likewise ensure that reasonable safety equipment is
823   made available during the activity, and that appropriate informed consent is secured in
824   accordance with applicable laws and DepEd policies.
825

826          The DepEd shall prescribe minimum operational standards governing educational
827   tours, work immersion, athletic competitions, cultural activities, community engagement
828   activities, and other authorized off-campus activities.

830                CHAPTER III. Digital Safety and Responsible Technology Use
831

832          SEC. 23. Digital Safety and Responsible Technology Use.
833          Schools shall adopt and implement comprehensive policies and programs to ensure
834   that the use of digital technologies in teaching, learning, communication, and school
835   operations is safe, responsible, ethical, age-appropriate, and consistent with the best
836   interests and developmental needs of learners.
837

838          Such policies and programs shall promote digital wellness and responsible
839   technology use, including developmentally appropriate use of digital devices, social media,
840   gaming, artificial intelligence, and other emerging technologies.
841

842          Schools shall establish measures to protect learners and school personnel from
843   cyberbullying, online abuse, cybersecurity risks, and other forms of digital harm. Such
844   measures shall include, as appropriate, the prevention and early identification of digital-
845   related harm and harmful or unsafe technology use, appropriate intervention and referral
846   mechanisms, media and information literacy programs, orientation programs for parents and
847   guardians, procedures for reporting and responding to digital safety incidents, and other
848   digital safety measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
849

850          Schools shall prohibit the use of mobile devices and other electronic gadgets by
851   learners within school premises during class hours, except for learning-related activities,
852   health and well-being-related activities, and purposes related to safety, emergency response,
853   or risk management, including in response to a perceived threat or danger, and during field
854   trips and other school-related activities conducted outside school premises.
855

856          Implementation of this Section shall be consistent with applicable laws on child
857   protection, data privacy, cybersecurity, and online safety.

860                           CHAPTER IV. LEARNER WELL-BEING
861

862          SECTION 24. Learner Well-Being and Psychosocial Support.
863          Schools shall establish and implement a comprehensive, coordinated, and age-
864   appropriate system for promoting learner health and well-being, providing mental health
865   services, and ensuring psychosocial support, consistent with the principles of prevention,
866   early identification, appropriate referral, inclusion, and protection of learners.
867

868      a) Health and Well-Being Promotion. – Schools shall promote the physical, mental,
869          emotional, and social health and well-being of learners through healthy learning
870          environments, health-promoting policies and practices, health education and skills
871          development, and measures that address the conditions and determinants that
872          influence learner health and well-being. Preventive health promotion and the
873          development of healthy learning environments shall be implemented as integral
874          components of school safety and shall be guided by applicable policies of the DepEd
875          and the DOH.
876      b) Mental Health Services. – Schools shall facilitate access to appropriate mental health
877          services, including prevention, early identification, assessment, treatment, referral,
878          and other professional interventions, in accordance with Republic Act No. 12080 and
879          other applicable laws, rules, standards, and guidelines. Professional mental health
880          services shall be provided by appropriately qualified and authorized mental health
881          professionals within their respective scopes of practice.
882      c) Psychosocial Support. – Schools shall provide or facilitate appropriate psychosocial
883          support to learners experiencing stress, distress, crisis, trauma, loss, violence, abuse,
884          displacement, disasters, or other circumstances affecting their well-being. Such
885          support may include psychological first aid, supportive and responsive school-based
886          interventions, peer and social support, referral and linkage to appropriate services,
887          and other non-clinical measures that strengthen learners’ coping, resilience,
888          connectedness, and sense of safety and belonging. Psychosocial support shall
889          complement, and shall not substitute for, professional mental health services when
890          such services are required.

892                                              TITLE V
893                                            RESPONSE
894

895          SECTION 25. Incident Reporting, Assessment and Immediate Response.
896   Every school shall establish and maintain responsive mechanisms and protocols for the
897   reporting, assessment, protection interventions, and immediate response to school safety
898   concerns involving learners, school personnel, and other members of the school community.
899   Such mechanisms shall form part of the School Safety Plan and shall be implemented in
900   accordance with this Act and applicable laws.
901

902          Schools shall ensure that the implementation of this Section is guided by the
903   principles of child protection and the best interests of the child, confidentiality and respect
904   for privacy, timely intervention, trauma-informed and developmentally appropriate
905   practices, due process, coordinated inter-agency response, and survivor-centered, rights-
906   based, and restorative approaches, where appropriate.
907

908          Schools shall ensure the availability of multiple reporting channels, which may
909   include in-person reporting, anonymous reporting systems, digital reporting platforms,
910   designated reporting officers, emergency hotlines, and such other reporting mechanisms as
911   may be prescribed by the DepEd.
912

913          Upon receipt of a report, the school shall:
914      a) Immediately record the report;
915      b) Conduct an initial risk assessment;
916      c) Determine whether immediate protective measures are necessary;
917      d) Notify the School Head and appropriate school personnel;
918      e) Notify parents or guardians, where appropriate;
919      f) Activate emergency response procedures when necessary;
920      g) Coordinate with the PNP, LGU, and other relevant local authorities in cases
921          involving violence, imminent threats to safety or security, and other emergencies;
922      h) Document actions taken; and
923      i) Determine whether referral or case management should be initiated.
924

925          Where the initial risk assessment identifies indicators of suicide risk, self-harm,
926   active psychosis, or imminent danger to self or others, the school shall immediately activate
927   emergency protocols and refer the learner for same-day psychiatric evaluation, in
928   coordination with parents or guardians and the nearest DOH-accredited mental health
929   facility or service provider.
930

931          Every school shall ensure that all members of the school community are informed of
932   the reporting mechanisms, available support services, and emergency procedures
933   established under this Act. Schools shall likewise ensure that reporting mechanisms are
934   accessible to learners with disabilities and other learners requiring additional support.
935
936         Every school shall maintain updated emergency contact information and establish
937   procedures for the immediate notification of parents or guardians, emergency responders,
938   LGUs, and other appropriate authorities during emergencies and critical incidents.
939

940         SECTION 26. Referral, Case Management, and Coordination.
941         Every school shall establish a formal case management and referral system for
942   learners requiring continuing intervention and support.
943

944   The system shall provide for:
945      a) Designation of case managers, where appropriate;
946      b) Multidisciplinary case conferences involving relevant school personnel;
947      c) Counseling and psychosocial interventions;
948      d) The establishment of a tiered referral pathway to hospitals, mental health and well-
949         being offices, health professionals, psychologists, psychiatrists, social workers, child
950         protection authorities, law enforcement agencies, or other service providers,
951         consistent with Republic Act No. 12080 and other applicable laws and policies;
952      e) Development of individualized intervention plans, where appropriate;
953      f) Regular monitoring and follow-up;
954      g) Coordination with parents or guardians;
955      h) Documentation and secure case records; and
956      i) Safe reintegration into the school community.
957

958         The DepEd shall establish standardized case management forms, referral protocols,
959   documentation procedures, and inter-agency coordination mechanisms to promote
960   consistency across schools.
961

962         Schools shall coordinate with the DSWD, the DOJ, the PNP, and LGUs for the
963   proper handling of cases, including children in conflict with the law, and for the provision
964   of medical, legal, and psychosocial support.

967                                          RECOVERY
968

969         SECTION 27. School Recovery.
970         Schools shall establish and implement recovery and reintegration measures for
971   learners, school personnel, and other affected members of the school community.
972
 973          Recovery measures shall aim to restore safety, promote healing, minimize disruption
 974   to learning, strengthen resilience, and facilitate the safe return of affected persons to school.
 975

 976          Schools shall, at a minimum:
 977      a) Provide timely counseling, psychosocial support, psychological first aid, and other
 978          appropriate interventions;
 979      b) Develop individualized intervention and reintegration plans for learners or school
 980          personnel requiring continuing support, where appropriate;
 981      c) Implement academic accommodations and learning continuity measures for affected
 982          learners;
 983      d) Engage parents or guardians in planning and supporting the recovery of affected
 984          learners;
 985      e) Coordinate referrals to appropriate government agencies, health professionals, and
 986          community-based service providers;
 987      f) Monitor the progress of affected learners and school personnel following
 988          reintegration;
 989      g) Implement measures to prevent retaliation, stigmatization, discrimination, or re-
 990          traumatization; and
 991      h) Conduct post-incident reviews to identify lessons learned and improve school safety
 992          systems.
 993

 994          Recovery and reintegration measures shall be developmentally appropriate, trauma-
 995   informed, culturally responsive, and consistent with the best interests of the child.
 996

 997          SECTION 28. National Support for School Recovery and Continuous
 998   Improvement.
 999          The DepEd shall establish a National School Recovery and Support Program to
1000   strengthen the capacity of schools to respond to and recover from incidents affecting school
1001   safety and learner well-being. This shall be integrated into the School Safety Plan.
1002

1003          The Program shall include:
1004      a) Technical assistance to affected schools;
1005      b) Deployment of specialized psychosocial support teams, where appropriate;
1006      c) Development of standardized recovery and reintegration protocols;
1007      d) Capacity-building for school personnel on post-incident recovery;
1008      e) Technical assistance for learning continuity and academic recovery;
1009      f) Collection and analysis of post-incident data and lessons learned;
1010      g) Dissemination of good practices and model recovery interventions; and
1011      h) Such other support measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
1012

1013          The DepEd shall coordinate with the DOH, the DSWD, LGUs, and other appropriate
1014   agencies to strengthen community-based recovery and reintegration services.
1015

1016          Subject to the availability of appropriations, the DepEd shall prioritize technical
1017   assistance and specialized support for schools significantly affected by major emergencies,
1018   disasters, violence, or other critical incidents.
1019

1020          The DepEd may organize or designate regional or division-level multidisciplinary
1021   support teams to provide technical assistance, psychosocial support, crisis intervention, and
1022   other specialized services to schools affected by major incidents, emergencies, or disasters.
1023   These shall include or have rapid access to a psychiatrist, alongside psychologists and social
1024   workers, for post-incident deployment as needed.
