                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The welfare of animals is a matter of public interest that directly affects
community health, safety, and social responsibility. While existing laws such as
Republic Act No. 8485, as amended, and Republic Act No. 9482 provide
frameworks for animal protection and rabies prevention, access to affordable and
reliable veterinary services remains uneven across the country. Many local
government units lack adequate facilities, equipment, and personnel to deliver
essential animal health services.

       This measure seeks to institutionalize the establishment of a public
veterinary clinic in every city and municipality, under the supervision of the
respective local government units and in coordination with the Department of
Agriculture – Bureau of Animal Industry. These clinics shall provide basic
veterinary services, including vaccination, spay and neuter programs, treatment
of common animal illnesses, and support for zoonotic disease control.

         By strengthening local veterinary infrastructure, this bill promotes
responsible pet ownership, advances animal welfare, enhances rabies prevention
efforts, and supports the broader goal of safeguarding public health. It likewise
ensures alignment with existing laws while respecting the principles of local
autonomy under Republic Act No. 7160.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            “AN ACT
   ESTABLISHING A PUBLIC VETERINARY CLINIC IN EVERY CITY AND
   MUNICIPALITY IN THE COUNTRY, PROVIDING FOR THEIR
   OPERATION AND MAINTENANCE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
   THEREFOR”

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Animal Medical
Center Act."

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared a policy of the State
to protect and promote the welfare of every animal, recognize their intrinsic
value, and ensure that their basic health and safety needs are adequately met.
Towards this end, the State shall establish and strengthen accessible and
affordable animal health and veterinary services in all local government units
nationwide, thereby contributing to public health, responsible pet ownership,
and a humane society.

      This Act shall supplement and strengthen the implementation of Republic
Act No. 8485, as amended by Republic Act No. 10631, and other related animal
health and welfare laws, and shall be implemented in harmony with existing
statutes governing animal protection and public health.

        SECTION 3. Establishment. There shall be established, operated, and
continuously maintained a public veterinary clinic in every city and municipality
in the country. These clinics shall be under the direct control and supervision of
the respective city or municipal local government unit (LGU), in consultation and
coordination with the Department of Agriculture - Bureau of Animal Industry
(DA-BAI).
        The public veterinary clinics established under this Act shall serve as the
primary local facilities for the implementation of relevant animal health and
rabies prevention programs pursuant to Republic Act No. 9482 and other related
laws and shall not be construed to diminish existing mandates of local
government units under said Act.

       SECTION 4. Provision of Facilities and Equipment. The concerned city or
municipal LGU shall provide an adequate, accessible, and suitable lot or space
where the public veterinary clinic shall be constructed or established. The clinics
shall be equipped with essential machines, equipment, instruments, and
supplies necessary for providing comprehensive basic veterinary services.

       SECTION 5. Functions and Services. The public veterinary clinics shall
perform functions and provide services including, but not limited to:

      (a) Providing routine vaccinations (e.g., anti-rabies, distemper) and
          deworming services;
      (b) Conducting spay and neuter programs for pet population control;
      (c) Offering basic veterinary consultations, diagnoses, and medical
      treatments for common animal illnesses;
      (d) Performing minor surgical procedures;
      (e) Assisting in the control and prevention of zoonotic diseases in
      coordination with local health offices;
      (f) Conducting animal welfare awareness and responsible pet ownership
      campaigns;
      (g) Maintaining a database of registered pets and vaccinated animals
      within the LGU's jurisdiction subject to applicable data privacy laws and
      regulations;
      (h) Providing basic emergency care for animals; and
      (i) Coordinating with local animal welfare organizations and law
      enforcement for animal rescue and cruelty cases.

      SECTION 6. Organizational Structure and Staffing. The local chief
executive shall appoint a qualified Chief Veterinarian to head the public
veterinary clinic, who shall have the necessary professional qualifications and
experience as prescribed by the Civil Service Commission and the Professional
Regulation Commission and must be a duly licensed veterinarian in good
standing in accordance with Republic Act No. 9268. The Chief Veterinarian
shall render full-time service and be responsible for the clinic's overall
administration and operation. The local chief executive shall also appoint the
necessary veterinary medical, technical, and administrative personnel and staff,
proportionate to the needs of the LGU and in accordance with relevant civil
service laws, rules, and regulations.
      SECTION 7. Design, Policies, Terms and Conditions. The LGU shall prepare
the design and layout of the clinic structure in consultation with the DA-BAI.
The LGU shall also formulate and implement appropriate local policies,
guidelines, fees (if any, for non-indigent cases, and subject to LGU ordinances),
and terms and conditions relative to the efficient operation and maintenance of
the public veterinary clinic, ensuring accessibility and affordability of services.

      The exercise of authority by the LGU under this Section shall remain
subject to existing national standards prescribed by the Department of
Agriculture and consistent with applicable public health regulations issued by
the Department of Health, particularly in matters involving zoonotic disease
prevention and control.

       SECTION      8.    Funding.   For     the    initial  establishment     and
construction/renovation of public veterinary clinics, an initial appropriation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act for the ensuing fiscal
year following the approval of this Act. This initial funding shall be allocated as
a subsidy from the National Government, through the Department of
Agriculture, to cities and municipalities in meeting the capital outlay
requirements for these facilities.

         The National Government shall prioritize the provision of financial
assistance to ensure that no local government unit is unduly burdened in the
initial establishment of the required facilities under this Act.

        The city or municipal local government unit shall primarily be
responsible for providing the necessary funds for the continuous operation,
maintenance, and personnel services of their respective public veterinary clinics.
Such funds shall be included in their annual regular appropriations. The
Department of Agriculture may provide supplementary financial assistance or
technical support for specific programs (e.g., mass vaccination, spay/neuter
drives) as part of its regular mandate.

        Implementation of this Act shall be subject to the fiscal capacity of the
local government units concerned and consistent with the principles of local
fiscal autonomy under Republic Act No. 7160.

       Nothing in this Act shall be construed as an unfunded mandate imposed
upon local government units.

       SECTION 9. Monitoring and Evaluation. The DA-BAI, in coordination with
the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and relevant
professional organizations, shall establish a robust system for monitoring and
evaluating the establishment, operation, and effectiveness of the public
veterinary clinics. This system shall ensure compliance with national standards,
promote best practices, and facilitate continuous improvement of animal health
services nationwide.

      Monitoring mechanisms established under this Act shall complement
and not duplicate existing regulatory and supervisory functions of the
Department of Agriculture under Republic Act No. 8485, as amended.

       SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90)
days after the approval of this Act, the DA-BAI, in coordination with the
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and such other relevant
agencies and professional organizations, shall prepare and issue the necessary
rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

       SECTION 11. Separability Clause. If any portion or provisions of this Act
are declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining portions or provisions
not otherwise affected shall remain valid and subsisting.

        SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, memoranda, circulars, and other issuances, or parts thereof, which are
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

        SECTION 13. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
