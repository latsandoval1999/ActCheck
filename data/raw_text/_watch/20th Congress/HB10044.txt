                             AN ACT

                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        According to the Philippine Statistics Authority’s 2026 Factsheet on Women and
Men, 3,343 cases of violence against girls were recorded in 2025, representing 54.6%
of all documented cases of violence against children for that year 1.

       As of 2022, 19,228 women have been documented as survivors of violence2.
These figures, however, capture only what has been reported, and only within
particular demographics. Survivors across other gender identities continue to face
violent acts and systemic discrimination that perpetuate harm 3, yet remain
inadequately recognized under existing law. Compounding this, only approximately
15% of Filipinos live in areas covered by local ordinances that prohibit discrimination
based on sexual orientation and gender identity, leaving a vast majority without even
baseline legal protection4.

      Ideally, when victims are subjected to abuse or at risk of being abused, they
must have a safe place to go to. Having immediate access to a secure environment is

       Currently, the challenge lies not in the lack of designated shelter facilities to
cater to these victims, but rather that these facilities are limited in number and
geographic distribution, leaving many provinces, municipalities, and remote areas
without dedicated shelter infrastructure.

        The Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) currently
operates shelter facilities like the Haven for Women, which offer temporary shelter and
protective custody for women ages 18-59 years old who are victims of involuntary or
forced prostitution, illegal recruitment, battered/abused women, victims of sexual
abuse, women in detention, women victims of armed conflicts and others 5 and Home
for Girls, for girl children aged 7 to below 18 years old 6, but these facilities are still too
few to provide sufficient coverage nationwide. At present, DWSD operates and
manages about seventy-seven (77) Centers and Residential Care Facilities (CRCF)
nationwide7. Of these facilities, eight (8) are dedicated as Havens for Women, eight
(8) serve as Homes for Girls, seven (7) function as combined facilities serving both
demographics, and none of which particularly cover survivor populations on persons
of diverse sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, and sex
characteristics (SOGIESC)8.

       Thus, this proposed measure.

       This measure aims to protect survivors of domestic, sexual, and gender-based
violence by establishing inclusive halfway houses in every city and province. It ensures
immediate, non-discriminatory protective custody and emergency shelter.
Furthermore, the initiative mandates trauma-informed, child-sensitive care,
strengthens local government capacity to deliver integrated services, and upholds
survivors' rights to safety, dignity, and justice.

       The establishment of a nationwide halfway house system represents a more
inclusive and rehabilitative approach to the State’s obligation to protect those who
have experienced or remain vulnerable to violence. As both a mechanism and a
framework for protection, recovery, and reintegration, it ensures that all survivors,
regardless of gender, age, or sexual orientation, receive the protective rehabilitation
and sustained support they are owed.

       This bill draws direct inspiration from Quezon City’s Bahay Kanlungan, the first-
ever LGU-run shelter providing comprehensive medical, psychological, police, and
legal assistance to women, children, and LGBTQ+ victim-survivors of gender-based
violence. Recognizing the immense success of this local initiative, this bill seeks to
enact a formal law that replicates and institutionalizes this protective model across the
entire country.

       Early approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

 1          SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Bahay Kanlungan
 2   Act of 2026”
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   uphold the dignity, equality, bodily autonomy, safety, and human rights of all persons,
 6   and to protect survivors of violence, abuse, exploitation, and coercion through
 7   accessible, survivor-centered, gender-responsive, child-sensitive, disability-inclusive,
 8   culturally appropriate, and trauma-informed services.
 9
10          The State recognizes that violence may be experienced by women, men,
11   children, older persons, persons with disabilities, and persons of diverse sexual
12   orientation, gender identity, gender expression, and sex characteristics (SOGIESC),
13   and that barriers to reporting, protection, and recovery are often compounded by
14   poverty, discrimination, stigma, geography, disability, age, and unequal access to
15   services.
16
17         Toward this end, the State shall establish and maintain inclusive halfway
18   houses and related support systems that provide temporary shelter, protective
19   custody, medical care, psychosocial services, legal assistance, safety planning, and
20   pathways to reintegration and long-term recovery for survivors of gender-based

 4         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:
 5
 6         (a) Child refers to any person below eighteen (18) years of age, or a person
 7         eighteen (18) years or older who is unable to fully take care of or protect
 8         themselves from abuse, neglect, cruelty, exploitation, or discrimination because
 9         of a physical, mental, intellectual, psychosocial, or similar condition.
10
11         (b) Family Violence refers to violence or abuse committed by a family member,
12         household member, caregiver, former spouse, current or former intimate
13         partner, dating partner, co-parent, or any other person similarly situated,
14         including physical, sexual, psychological, emotional, verbal, digital, or economic
15         abuse.
16
17         (c) Gender-Based Violence refers to acts or threats of physical, sexual,
18         psychological, verbal, emotional, digital, or economic harm or suffering directed
19         against a person on the basis of sex, gender, sexual orientation, gender identity,
20         gender expression, sex characteristics, or perceived nonconformity with social
21         gender norms, including coercion, threats, harassment, intimidation, stalking,
22         deprivation of liberty, and other analogous acts.
23
24         (d) Halfway House refers to a government-operated or government-accredited
25         facility that provides temporary shelter, protective custody, and integrated
26         support services to survivors. Such services shall include, as appropriate:
27         residential care, food and clothing, medical and mental health services, trauma-
28         informed psychosocial support, crisis intervention, case management, legal
29         assistance, child protection services, education support, livelihood and skills
30         training, transportation, security and safety planning, family assessment, where
31         appropriate, referral services, and aftercare.
32
33         (e) Protective Custody refers to temporary care and protection extended to a
34         survivor in order to secure their safety, recovery, privacy, and access to
35         services, subject to applicable laws, the best interests of the child, and the
36         informed participation of the survivor whenever possible.
37
38         (f) Qualified Guardian or Caregiver refers to a fit and proper person, family
39         member, accredited foster carer, institution, or other legally authorized
40         custodian found by the appropriate authority or social worker to be capable of
41         ensuring the safety and welfare of the survivor, consistent with existing laws.
42
43         (g) Sexual Violence refers to rape, sexual assault, acts of lasciviousness,
44         sexual harassment, exploitation, incest, online sexual abuse or exploitation,

14         SEC. 4. Creation of Halfway Houses for Survivors — The Department of Social
15   Welfare and Development (DSWD) shall procure, build, establish, operate, manage,
16   maintain, or accredit at least one (1) Inclusive Halfway House in every province in the
17   country, geographically accessible to the municipalities therein.
18
19           Every highly urbanized city, independent component city, and component city
20   shall likewise establish, operate, maintain, or accredit at least one (1) Inclusive
21   Halfway House within its jurisdiction.
22
23           These Halfway Houses shall accommodate survivors in need of immediate
24   relief, protection, recovery, stabilization, reintegration, or referral, without
25   discrimination on the basis of sex, gender, age, disability, civil status, religion,
26   ethnicity, language, socioeconomic status, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender
27   expression, sex characteristics, or other analogous status.
28
29          Temporary accommodation may be provided for a period not exceeding ninety
30   (90) days, unless extension is justified by the survivor’s safety needs, ongoing case
31   management, court processes, medical condition, child-protection concerns, or other
32   meritorious circumstances as may be provided in the implementing rules and
33   regulations.
34
35          SEC. 5. Admission, Custody, Referral, and Discharge. – Admission to a
36   Halfway House may be initiated through walk-in requests, referrals from barangays,
37   local social welfare offices, hospitals, law enforcement agencies, schools, courts,
38   women and children protection desks, anti-trafficking or child protection units,
39   community organizations, hotlines, or other authorized sources.
40
41          Adult survivors with full decision-making capacity shall, to the fullest extent
42   possible, participate in decisions affecting their shelter, services, safety planning,
43   referral, and discharge.
