                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Road safety is a fundamental right, yet the current legal framework under Republic Act
No. 10586, otherwise known as the "Anti-Drunk and Drugged Driving Act of 2013," has revealed
critical procedural loopholes that allow intoxicated drivers to evade justice.
      The tragic death of young entrepreneur Kingston Ralph Cheng in Cebu City on February 8,
2026, highlighted a systemic "hiding tactic." By avoiding immediate police contact, suspects allow
the alcohol or drugs in their system to dissipate naturally, rendering chemical tests—the primary
evidence required by the current law—ineffective. This gap essentially shields hit-and-run
behavior and penalizes the victims of road tragedies.
      Firstly, this bill redefines "driving under the influence of alcohol (DUIA)" as a driver with
blood alcohol concentration (BAC) after a breath analyzer test, of above 0.00%, versus the present
IRR of RA 10586 which defines DUIA for drivers of private vehicles as drivers with BAC of
above 0.05%. It is important to note that the same IRR already provides for DUIA for drivers of
motorcycles and public utility vehicles (PUVs) as drivers with BAC of above 0.0%. This bill also
proposes that the mandatory alcohol and drug tests be administered within a two hour window
from the time of the accident. Moreover, refusal to undergo the mandatory test will amount to a
prima facie presumption that the driver was DUI. This bill also defines a "hit and run incident"
which shall constitute as an aggravating circumstance to the final penalties given to DUI offenders.
      In terms of penalties, this bill proposes an increase in fines for all types of violations, from
(a) DUI without injury, to (b) DUI with injury and (c) DUI resulting in death. This bill also
proposes to lengthen periods for imprisonment for DUI without injury or death to six months (from
current three months imprisonment), as an increased preventive measure. For DUI resulting in


                                                  1
death or homicide, this bill proposes to increase the penalty to reclusion perpetua (from reclusion
temporal per Art. 249 of the Revised Penal Code).
     Furthermore, this bill proposes the establishment and institutionalization of a dedicated
community public reporting channel via the 911 national emergency hotline and/or an official
social media platform account for the purpose of reporting DUI incidents. This shall encourage
the prevention of DUI accidents, timely reporting of accidents and also providing secondary
evidence from the public to ensure that DUI violators are brought justice.
     Finally, this bill proposes that the Philippine Information Agency (PIA) in coordination with
the LTO conduct a continuous educational and informational campaign via social media on the
dangers, consequences and penalties for DUI.
     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SEC. 6. Community Reporting and Citizen Intervention. - A new section is inserted to
read as follows:
         "SEC. 7-A. Community Reporting and Citizen Intervention. – To ensure the
         timely apprehension of impaired drivers and the collection of forensic evidence
         within the prescribed window, the following reporting framework is hereby
         established:
         (a) Dedicated Public Reporting Channels. – The Department of Transportation
         (DOTr) and the Land Transportation Office (LTO), in coordination with the
         Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), shall establish a
         centralized 'Road Safety Emergency Hotline' to be integrated with the National
         Emergency Hotline 911 and/or the establishment of an official Road Safety
         Facebook Page managed by the LTO for the immediate reporting of suspected
         DUI incidents, including hit and run incidents.
         (b) Admissibility of Citizen-Provided Evidence. – Photos, CCTV footage,
         dashcam footage, and video recordings of erratic driving or accidents captured
         and submitted by citizens through official channels shall be admissible as
         secondary evidence to establish probable cause for an immediate stop or to
         support a DUI charge in cases where a chemical test was delayed due to the
         suspect’s flight.
         (c) Good Samaritan Immunity. – Any citizen who, in good faith, reports a
         suspected impaired driver to the authorities shall be immune from civil or
         criminal liability arising from such report, provided that the report is not proven to
         be malicious or intentionally false. The identity of the reporter shall remain
         confidential unless their testimony is indispensable for the prosecution of the case.




                                           7
        (d) Mandatory Police Response. – Upon receipt of a verified report through the
        official reporting channels, the nearest law enforcement unit or traffic enforcer is
        mandated to intercept the reported vehicle immediately. Failure of an officer to
        act on a verifiable report without valid justification shall constitute Dereliction of
        Duty under existing administrative laws."
