                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

Mental health is a fundamental component of overall well-being, directly
influencing a child’s capacity to learn, build healthy relationships, and
navigate life’s challenges. In recent years, the urgency of addressing mental
health issues among Filipino youth has escalated significantly. Rates of
anxiety, depression, and self-harm among primary and secondary school
students have reached alarming levels, further exacerbated by modern social
pressures, economic difficulties, and the aftermath of global disruptions.

Despite the enactment of Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the
"Philippine Mental Health Act," and Republic Act No. 11206, or the "Career
Guidance and Counseling Act," a severe gap remains in the actual delivery of
mental health services within the basic education system. Currently, the
public school system faces a catastrophic shortage of registered guidance
counselors (RGCs), primarily due to low salary grades and non-competitive
compensation structures. Consequently, thousands of elementary and high
schools across the country operate without a single mental health professional
on site.

This bill seeks to address these critical vulnerabilities by establishing a
comprehensive Mental Health Program in all public and private elementary
and high schools nationwide and creating a dedicated mental health personnel
positions with competitive compensation packages (adjusting salary grades for
Guidance Counselors and creating Mental Health Associate positions) to
recruit and retain qualified professionals.

Prioritizing the mental health of our learners is an urgent social imperative. By
building a resilient, supportive, and accessible mental health infrastructure
within our schools, we safeguard the future of our nation.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT STRENGTHENING MENTAL HEALTH SERVICES IN ALL PUBLIC
     AND PRIVATE ELEMENTARY AND HIGH SCHOOLS NATIONWIDE,
        CREATING THE POSITIONS OF SCHOOL MENTAL HEALTH
    PROFESSIONALS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR
                             OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Basic Education
Mental Health Service Enhancement Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being of the
youth. The State recognizes the vital role of mental health in holistic student
development and shall guarantee the provision of accessible, timely, and
quality school-based mental health services in all basic education institutions
to prevent harm, violence, and psychological distress.

SEC. 3. Objectives. — This Act aims to:

(a) Establish comprehensive and age-appropriate school-based mental health
programs in all elementary and high schools;

(b) Address the critical shortage of school guidance counselors and mental
health professionals through competitive compensation and revised
qualification standards;

(c) Integrate mental health awareness and education into the basic education
curriculum;

(d) Provide clear referral pathways, early threat identification, and immediate
crisis intervention mechanisms for students in distress; and

(e) Reduce mental health stigma within school communities.
SEC. 4. Comprehensive School Mental Health Program (CSMHP). — The
Department of Education (DepEd), in coordination with the Department of
Health (DOH) and the National Mental Health Council, shall formulate and
implement a Comprehensive School Mental Health Program (CSMHP) for all
elementary and high schools, incorporating:

   1. Prevention and Promotion: Mental health literacy integrated into class
      subjects, wellness activities, and anti-stigma campaigns.
   2. Early Intervention & Threat Assessment: Routine screening, peer-
      support network training, anti-bullying integration, and early
      identification protocols for at-risk students exhibiting behavioral
      distress or radicalization signs.
   3. Crisis Response & Emergency Intervention: Immediate counseling,
      psychological first aid, trauma response, and immediate referral systems
      for severe cases or violent threats.

SEC. 5. Creation of School Mental Health Positions and Revised Salary
Structure.

      (a) Salary Grade Upgrade: To attract and retain qualified professionals,
      the entry-level position of Guidance Counselor I (currently Salary Grade
      11) is hereby upgraded to Salary Grade 16. Higher Guidance Counselor
      positions shall be adjusted accordingly.

      (b) Mental Health Associates: To assist Guidance Counselors and bridge
      operational gaps, the position of School Mental Health Associate is
      hereby created under Salary Grade 13. Graduates of Psychology,
      Guidance and Counseling, Social Work, or related behavioral sciences
      shall be eligible for this position.

      (c) Staffing Ratios: The DepEd shall ensure that every public elementary
      and high school maintains a minimum ratio of one (1) Guidance
      Counselor or Mental Health Associate for every five hundred (500)
      students. Schools with large student populations shall establish a
      multidisciplinary School Mental Health Team.

SEC. 6. Establishment of School Care Centers. — Every public elementary and
high school shall establish a dedicated, safe, and confidential space to be
known as the "School Care Center." The Center shall serve as the primary hub
for counseling, mental health consultations, peer-group processing, and
psychological emergency response.

SEC. 7. Capacity Building for School Personnel. — DepEd shall mandate
continuous training in Psychological First Aid (PFA), suicide and violence
prevention, trauma-informed teaching, and early warning sign recognition for
all teaching and non-teaching personnel. Teachers shall act as first responders
in identifying distressed students but shall not replace certified professionals
in delivering clinical interventions.
SEC. 8. Parental and Community Engagement. — Schools shall conduct
mandatory orientation programs for parents and guardians to promote mental
health literacy at home, emphasize parental involvement, and establish family
support networks.

SEC. 9. Confidentiality. — All student records, counseling sessions, and
psychological evaluations shall be strictly confidential, subject to the
provisions of Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012) and Republic
Act No. 11036 (Mental Health Act), except in emergency cases involving
immediate risk of harm to the student or others.

SEC. 10. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the immediate
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA) under the budget of the Department of Education.

SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Education, in coordination
with the Department of Health, the Department of Budget and Management,
and relevant professional organizations, shall promulgate the necessary rules
and regulations for its effective implementation.

SEC. 12. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is held
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, administrative
orders, rules, and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 14. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
