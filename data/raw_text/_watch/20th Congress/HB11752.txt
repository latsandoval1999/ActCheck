                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        For generations, Filipino fisherfolk have depended on our seas for their livelihood. For
many Palaweño fisherfolk, the West Philippine Sea is not an abstract question of maps,
boundaries, or foreign policy. It is where they work, where they fish, and where they provide for
their families. Yet going out to sea has become increasingly difficult and dangerous. Filipino
fisherfolk continue to face water cannoning, blocking, dangerous maneuvers, and other forms of
interference by foreign vessels in the West Philippine Sea.

        In October 2026, Philippine authorities also encountered unauthorized foreign fishing
vessels in the Kalayaan Island Group, including a Chinese fishing vessel and smaller boats found
fishing without authorization within the territorial sea of Lawak Island, Palawan. Behind every
damaged fishing boat or destroyed net is a fisherfolk who may not be able to return to sea the
next day.

       For families who depend on the day's catch, the loss of a boat, engine, fishing gear, or
catch already obtained can mean losing their source of income. In more serious encounters, it is
not only their livelihood that is at risk, but their safety and their lives. Government programs
already provide livelihood, fisheries, and insurance assistance to fisherfolk.

        The Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation (PCIC), in particular, provides insurance
protection for fisherfolk and certain fishing assets. However, existing insurance policies may
exclude losses arising from invasion, acts of foreign enemies, hostilities, or similar events. A
fisherfolk may therefore have insurance and still find that damage caused by foreign maritime
interference is not covered.

         This bill seeks to address that gap. It directs the PCIC to develop special insurance and
indemnity coverage for qualified Filipino fisherfolk and fishing vessels operating within
Philippine maritime zones. Rather than create another government agency or a separate
compensation fund, the measure builds on the government's existing insurance system. The
Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) and the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR) will
assist in verifying incidents, while the PCIC will administer the coverage and process claims.

        The coverage is intended for actual and verifiable losses, including damage to fishing
boats, engines, gear and equipment, loss of catch already obtained, and reasonable rescue and
recovery expenses. Projected catch, expected profits, and other speculative losses are not
covered. Our fisherfolk should not have to bear these losses alone. They go to sea to work, not to
become casualties of a maritime dispute. If they suffer actual damage while lawfully earning a
living in Philippine waters, there should be a clear mechanism to help them recover and return to
sea.
        As the Philippines continues to assert and defend its rights in the West Philippine Sea, we
must also stand behind the Filipino fisherfolk who exercise those rights through their everyday
livelihood. Protecting our waters must also mean protecting the people who depend on them.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT ESTABLISHING SPECIAL INSURANCE AND INDEMNITY COVERAGE
    FOR FILIPINO FISHERFOLK AND FISHING VESSELS AGAINST LOSS OR
   DAMAGE ARISING FROM FOREIGN MARITIME INTERFERENCE WITHIN
        PHILIPPINE MARITIME ZONES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "West Philippine Sea
Fisherfolk Indemnity and Insurance Act.”

        SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect Filipino fisherfolk in the lawful pursuit of their livelihood within Philippine maritime
zones and to ensure that they are not left without adequate financial protection for actual loss or
damage arising from foreign maritime interference.

     Toward this end, the State shall ensure that appropriate insurance and indemnity coverage is
made available for actual and verifiable losses suffered by qualified Filipino fisherfolk and
fishing vessels as a direct result of foreign maritime interference, without prejudice to the rights
and remedies of the Republic of the Philippines under domestic and international law.

        SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
be defined as follows:

   (a) “Covered fisherfolk” refers to a Filipino fisherfolk duly registered under the Municipal
       Fisherfolk Registration System (FishR), or such other applicable national or local
       fisherfolk registration system, who is lawfully engaged in fishing or fishing-related
       activities within Philippine maritime zones, whether as an individual fisherfolk, fishing
       vessel owner or operator, or crew member aboard a covered fishing vessel;
   (b) “Covered fishing vessel” refers to a Philippine-registered fishing boat or vessel lawfully
       operating within Philippine maritime zones and used principally for fishing or
       fishing-related activities;
   (c) “Foreign maritime interference” refers to an act committed by, or reasonably attributable
       to, a foreign government vessel, foreign fishing vessel, maritime militia vessel, or other
       foreign vessel that directly causes loss or damage to a covered fisherfolk or covered
       fishing vessel while lawfully engaged in fishing or fishing-related activities within
       Philippine maritime zones. Such acts may include:
                (1) ramming or collision;
                (2) water cannoning;
                (3) blocking or other dangerous maneuvers that directly result in physical
                   damage;
                (4) seizure, confiscation, destruction, or loss of fishing gear, equipment, or catch;
                (5) forced grounding or other acts resulting in damage to a fishing vessel;
                (6) acts directly causing bodily injury, disability, or death; and
       ​       (7) other analogous acts as may be determined under the implementing rules and
                  regulations of this Act.

       The mere presence of a foreign vessel, issuance of a warning, or interruption or
       abandonment of a fishing voyage, without actual loss or damage covered under Section 5
       of this Act, shall not by itself give rise to a claim;

   (d)​“Insurance indemnity” refers to the amount payable by the Philippine Crop Insurance
        Corporation (PCIC) to an insured covered fisherfolk, fishing vessel owner or operator, or
        beneficiary for an actual and verified covered loss under the insurance coverage
        established pursuant to this Act;
   (e)​ “Philippine maritime zones” refers to the maritime zones over which the Philippines
        exercises sovereignty, sovereign rights, or jurisdiction pursuant to Republic Act No.
        12064, otherwise known as the “Philippine Maritime Zones Act,” and other applicable
        laws and international law; and
   (f)​ “Verified Foreign Maritime Interference Incident” refers to an incident falling within item
        (3) of this Section that has been verified by the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG), Bureau of
        Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR), or such other competent government authority
        designated under the implementing rules and regulations.

       SECTION 4. Special Foreign Maritime Interference Insurance and Indemnity
Coverage. – The Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation (PCIC), in coordination with the BFAR,
PCG, and other concerned government agencies, shall develop and make available appropriate
insurance coverage providing indemnity for covered fisherfolk and covered fishing vessels
against actual loss or damage directly arising from Verified Foreign Maritime Interference
Incidents.

​      Such coverage may be offered as a separate insurance product, special coverage, rider, or
extension of existing fisheries or fishing vessel insurance products, as the PCIC may determine.

​       The PCIC shall establish actuarially sound and reasonable premium rates, coverage
limits, deductibles, valuation standards, and other terms and conditions necessary for the
sustainability of the insurance coverage. The PCIC may adopt appropriate risk-sharing,
reinsurance, or other risk-transfer arrangements as may be necessary to ensure the financial
sustainability of the coverage established under this Act.

     Loss or damage shall not be denied coverage under this Act solely on the ground that the
incident involves foreign maritime interference, acts of foreign vessels, hostilities, or analogous
risks otherwise excluded from ordinary fisheries or fishing vessel insurance.

    Nothing in this Act shall be construed as establishing a separate government indemnity or
compensation fund. Any insurance indemnity payable under this Act shall arise only from
coverage issued or administered by the PCIC pursuant to this Act.

​      SECTION 5. Insurable Losses. – Subject to the coverage limits, deductibles, valuation
standards, and other reasonable conditions established by the PCIC, insurance under this Act
may provide indemnity for actual and verifiable losses directly arising from a Verified Foreign
Maritime Interference Incident, including:

   (a)​ actual damage to, or total loss of, a covered fishing vessel;
   (b)​actual loss of or damage to fishing gear, engines, navigation and communication
        equipment, safety equipment, and other equipment necessary for fishing operations;
   (c)​ actual loss or destruction of catch already obtained at the time of the incident, when
        reasonably documented or established;
   (d)​reasonable towing, salvage, rescue, and vessel recovery expenses necessarily incurred as
        a direct result of the incident; and
   (e)​ bodily injury, permanent disability, or death directly resulting from the incident, subject
        to such terms and conditions as may be prescribed by the PCIC and coordination with
        other applicable government insurance or compensation programs.

    No insurance indemnity shall be payable for projected catch, anticipated profits, speculative
income, or other consequential or indirect losses.

​       SECTION 6. Verification of Foreign Maritime Interference Incidents. – The PCG and
BFAR shall establish a simplified and coordinated mechanism for the reporting and verification
of incidents covered by this Act.

Verification may be based on any combination of competent evidence, including:
   (a)​ PCG, BFAR, or other official incident reports;
   (b)​photographs or video recordings;
   (c)​ geographic coordinates and vessel tracking or monitoring information;
   (d)​radio, distress, or other communications;
   (e)​ vessel identification information;
   (f)​ statements of the affected fisherfolk and other witnesses; and
   (g)​such other credible evidence as may reasonably establish the occurrence and
        circumstances of the incident.

​       The absence of a particular form of evidence, including automatic identification system
data or contemporaneous photographic or video evidence, shall not by itself defeat a claim when
the incident may be sufficiently established through other competent evidence.

​      Nothing in this Section shall require the PCG, BFAR, or PCIC to make a determination
as to the international responsibility or legal liability of a foreign State as a condition for
payment under this Act.

​       SECTION 7. Assessment, Payment, and Claims Assistance. – The PCIC shall assess
and process claims in accordance with fair, reasonable, and transparent valuation standards. In
determining the amount of insurance indemnity payable, the PCIC may consider repair or
replacement cost, depreciation where appropriate, prevailing market value, salvage value,
receipts or other proof of acquisition, standard valuation schedules for fishing equipment, and
other relevant evidence.

​      Claims procedures shall take into consideration the circumstances of municipal and
small-scale fisherfolk and shall, as far as practicable, minimize unnecessary documentary and
administrative requirements.
​
​      The PCIC and BFAR shall provide reasonable assistance to covered fisherfolk in filing
and documenting claims, particularly municipal and small-scale fisherfolk and those in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged coastal communities. Such assistance may be made
available through PCIC and BFAR field offices, concerned local government units, or other
accessible government service points.

​      SECTION 8. No double recovery. – An insured or beneficiary shall not receive
compensation more than once for the same actual loss. Benefits received from another
government insurance or compensation program for the same specific loss shall be disclosed to
the PCIC and taken into account in determining the amount payable under the insurance
coverage provided under this Act.

​      Nothing in this Section shall prohibit a covered fisherfolk from receiving assistance
intended for a different purpose or covering a different loss.

​      SECTION 9. Preservation of Government Claims and Remedies. – Payment of an
insurance indemnity under this Act shall not prejudice or affect any claim, position, or remedy
that the Republic of the Philippines may pursue against a responsible foreign State, entity, vessel,
or person arising from the same incident.

​       Nothing in this Act shall limit the authority of the Department of Foreign Affairs, Office
of the Solicitor General, Department of Justice, or other competent government agency to pursue
appropriate diplomatic, legal, or other remedies under Philippine law and international law.

​        SECTION 10. Inter-Agency Coordination. – For purposes of implementing this Act:
    (a)​ the PCIC shall develop and administer the insurance coverage, establish applicable
         insurance terms and valuation standards, assess covered losses, and process and pay valid
         claims;
    (b)​the BFAR shall assist in fisherfolk registration and validation, validation of fishing
         activities, and valuation of fishing gear and catch when necessary;
    (c)​ the PCG shall assist in incident reporting and verification and provide relevant maritime
         information consistent with law and national security requirements; and
    (d)​other concerned government agencies and local government units shall provide such
         assistance as may be necessary for the efficient implementation of this Act.

​     Nothing in this Act shall be construed as transferring or diminishing the existing
mandates of any government agency.

​        SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the PCIC, in coordination with the Department of Agriculture through the
BFAR, the PCG, the Department of Finance, and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the
rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.
​
​        The implementing rules and regulations shall provide, among others, standards for
eligibility, insurance coverage, verification, premiums, valuation, coverage limits, deductibles,
claims processing, appeals, prevention of fraudulent claims, and coordination with existing
insurance and assistance programs.
​
​        SECTION 12. Appropriations. – The implementation of this Act shall be undertaken
through the existing funds, resources, and insurance mechanisms available to the PCIC and other
concerned agencies, subject to applicable budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules and
regulations. Such additional amounts as may be necessary for the continued implementation of
this Act may be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

​      SECTION 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
and effect.

​       SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

​      SECTION 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
