                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State upholds its duty to protect the health and well-being of the Filipino people by
ensuring the establishment, preservation, and proper management of parks and open spaces.
These areas are not merely places of leisure but vital public resources that maintain ecological
balance, foster community identity, and contribute significantly to the overall quality of life.

        However, current statistics reveal a critical gap in access to such spaces. Only 24.24%
of the Philippines’ 42,046 barangays—approximately 10,191—had a public plaza or park as of
the 2020 Census, with Metro Manila at a mere 20.2%. Iconic sites like Rizal Park, which spans
58 hectares, and the Nayong Pilipino site, covering 45.9 hectares, cannot alone meet the
growing demand for recreation, green spaces, and tourism. Rizal Park, for instance, drew 5.7
million visitors in 2023, peaking at 121,000 visitors in a single day on January 1, 2024,
underscoring the vital role of parks in urban life. Meanwhile, the World Health Organization
recommends a minimum of 9 square meters of green space per capita, a standard far above
current availability in Manila.

        This proposed measure seeks to create the Philippine National Public Parks System
through the National and Local Public Parks Authority (NALPA), dissolving the existing
National Parks Development Committee (NPDC) and National Parks Foundation (NPF), while
transferring their assets to the new authority. The bill focuses on public recreational parks and
urban open spaces, distinct from the National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS),
which currently covers 244 sites totaling 7.76 million hectares or 15.4% of the country’s land
area.

        The institutionalization of this system will safeguard natural landscapes, cultural and
historical treasures, and ensure their sustainable use for the benefit of present and future
generations. Parks and open spaces will play an essential role in improving public health,
strengthening social cohesion, promoting tourism, conserving biodiversity, enhancing air
quality, capturing carbon, managing water resources, and mitigating the adverse effects of
climate change through natural cooling functions.

       The bill provides an initial allocation of ₱100 million to the NALPA Fund,
supplemented by retained income from park fees and concessions, ensuring sustainable
financing for operations and maintenance.

       By enacting this measure, the State affirms that parks and open spaces are indispensable
to sustainable development and commits to granting equitable access to these shared national
assets for all Filipinos.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

3           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine National Public
4    Parks System Act.”

5           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
6    and maintain the health and well-being of the Filipino people through the establishment and
7    preservation of parks and open spaces. To this end, the State recognizes parks and open spaces
8    as valuable assets, and as such:

 9                a) shall establish the Philippine National Public Parks System aimed to conserve
10                   the scenery, natural and historical objects, and to provide for their enjoyment
11                   in such manner and by such means as will leave them unimpaired for the
12                   enjoyment of future generations.
13
14                b) shall recognize that parks and open spaces contribute to human health and well-
15                being, social cohesion/identity, tourism, biodiversity, air quality, carbon
16                sequestration, water management, and cooling of the environment.
