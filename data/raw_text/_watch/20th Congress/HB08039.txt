                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE
      The youth are vital partners in nation-building and national
development.1 They remain the most potent force of the Filipino people towards
a better and more just future. Despite this, a significant portion of young
Filipinos are among the most vulnerable and least protected.

      The devastating numbers speak for themselves. 17.4% of Filipinos
between the ages of 5 to 24 –or nearly one out of five Filipino youth—are not in
school. 47.5% of young adults between the ages of 18 to 24 – or nearly half of
young Filipino adults – remain outside formal schooling.2

      In absolute terms, the numbers remain staggering. As of 2024, 7.8 million
Filipinos aged 5 to 24 years old were not in school, and 54.7% of them were

      Malinaw ang datos: Napakaraming kabataang Pilipino ang napag-

pangarap. Kung hindi natin sila kikilalanin bilang mga katuwang, mawawala
sa atin ang lakas, talino, at pag-asa ng kabataang Pilipino.

      The State must take decisive action to establish mechanisms that protect
and empower out-of-school children and youth, ensuring that they are provided
with a level playing field and a genuine fighting chance to improve their lives.
This is not merely a matter of social assistance — it is an issue of inclusion,
dignity, and justice.

      Hence, this Magna Carta of the Out-of-School Children and Youth, which
seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive framework to safeguard the rights,
welfare, and development of the out-of-school youth. The aim of this measure is
to substantially reduce, if not eliminate, the rate of out-of-school children and
youth in the Philippines. This will require a whole-of-government and whole-of-
society approach to ensure that no young Filipino is left behind.

       Specifically, this bill seeks to: (1) safeguard the rights and welfare of the
out-of-school children and youth; (2) establish a coordinating council to set
policies and provide special protection; (3) require national government agencies
and local government units (LGUs) to provide social service assistance and
support; (4) identify children at risk of becoming out-of-school youth and provide
the necessary government intervention; (5) provide an alternative learning
system and free technical and vocational education; (6) promote employment
and entrepreneurship; (7) require that not less than ten percent (10%) of all
scholarships under the Commission on Higher Education are set aside for out-

1    SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act will be known as the “Magna Carta for
2    Out-of-School Children and Youth.”

3                                       CHAPTER I
4                               GENERAL PROVISIONS

5    Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes its responsibility to
6    promote and protect the physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-
7    being of the Filipino youth, and to help the youth fulfill their vital role in nation-
8    building by ensuring that they have access to quality education. In addition, the
9    State adopts the following policies:
10      a. Out-of-school children and youth have the right to social protection and
1         opportunities for the full development of their potential;
2      b. The State must remove the systemic barriers that hinder equitable access
3         to education, training, and decent work opportunities;
4      c. The State must ensure that out-of-school children and youth below
5         eighteen (18) years of age are provided access to and are retained within
6         the formal or alternative education systems. At the same time, the State
7         recognizes the right of out-of-school youth aged eighteen (18) and above to
8         freely choose whether to pursue further education or engage in gainful
9         employment.

10   Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
11     a. Alternative Learning System (ALS) – refers to a parallel practical
12        learning system offering pathways equivalent to formal basic education.
13        It provides accessible and flexible basic education to out-of-school youth
14        and adults.
15     b. Children at Risk of Becoming Out-of-School Youth – refer to those
16        within schooling age below eighteen (18) years old who are at risk of
17        becoming out-of-school youth due to factors including but not limited to:
18           i) Family income below the poverty threshold;
19           ii) Solo parent family;
20           iii) Youth abandoned or neglected by their parents;
21           iv) Consistently absent and unenrolled youth for long periods of time,
22              especially pregnant girls and teenage parents; or
23           v) Former out-of-school youth.
24     c. Formal Education – refers to the systematic process of sequential
25        learning, corresponding to the elementary and secondary level of
26        schooling. At the end of each level, a certification is given to the learner to
27        advance to the next level.
1      d. Nonformal Education – refers to any organized or systematic
2         educational activity carried outside the framework of the formal education
3         system.
4      e. Out-of-School Child – refers to a person of official school-age of five (5)
5         to fifteen (15) years old who is not enrolled in and not attending any formal
6         basic education program or any equivalent nonformal education
7         recognized by the Government. This includes children who have never
8         enrolled or who have permanently or temporarily discontinued their
9         participation in recognized educational programs.
10     f. Out-of-School Youth – refers to a person aged fifteen (15) to thirty (30)
11        years old who:
12           i)    Is not gainfully and sustainably employed;
13           ii)   Has not been enrolled in an educational institution within the
14                 immediately preceding period of at least six (6) months; and
15           iii) Has not completed formal or nonformal secondary or technical-
16                 vocational education.
17        Employment is gainful and sustainable if maintained for at least six (6)
18        continuous months while earning at least the minimum wage.
19     g. Technical-Vocational Education and Training (TVET) – refers to
20        education and training for acquiring technical, scientific knowledge and
21        practical skills. It refers to degree programs, non-degree granting courses,
22        or associate degrees provided by authorized public or private training
23        institutions.

24                                    CHAPTER II
25                         RIGHTS AND EMPOWERMENT

26   Sec. 4. Right against Discrimination. – Out-of-school children and youth
27   have the right:
1      a. To be protected from discrimination on the basis of socioeconomic
2          standing, pregnancy, youth parenthood, and former status as out-of-
3          school youth;
4      b. To be admitted to formal and nonformal schools and learning centers
5          regardless of their personal circumstances; and
6      c. To be protected from discrimination based on sexual orientation,
7          gender identity and expression, sex characteristics, socioeconomic
8          class, age, ethnicity, religion, and disability.

9    Sec. 5. Right to Special Protection. – Out-of-school children and youth have
10   the right:

11       a. To be protected from sexual harassment, violence, exploitation, and
12          other forms of abuse they may experience due to their status;
13       b. To access government services and interventions to address their
14          immediate and developmental needs;
15       c. To be given priority in policies, programs, and projects in education,
16          employment, entrepreneurship, health, and social services, especially
17          for those who are economically disadvantaged or facing especially
18          difficult situations.

19   Sec. 6. Right to Recognition and Preservation of Cultural Identity and
20   Integrity. – Moro out-of-school children and youth and those from Indigenous
21   Cultural Communities have the right to practice, protect, and preserve their
22   own culture, traditions, and institutions. These rights must be respected and
23   considered in creating and implementing national policies and programs.

24   Sec. 7. Right To Decent Work. – Out-of-school youth are entitled to fair labor
25   standards, including:
1      a. Access to economic activities, local job opportunities, and strong
2         regulations to prevent forced or involuntary job loss;
3      b. Wages that meet the family living wage set by local wage boards; and
4      c. Opportunities for personal growth and social inclusion.

5                                     CHAPTER III
6      THE OUT-OF-SCHOOL CHILDREN AND YOUTH COORDINATING
7                                      COUNCIL

8    Sec. 8. Lead Agencies. – The Department of Education (DepEd) and the
9    Department    of   Labor   and    Employment    (DOLE)        will   oversee   the
10   implementation of this Act in coordination with the Out-of-School Children
11   and Youth Coordinating Council.

12   Sec. 9. Creation of the Out-of-School Children and Youth Coordinating
13   Council – To strengthen coordination among agencies, an Out-of-School
14   Children and Youth Coordinating Council (“Coordinating Council”) is hereby
15   created. The Coordinating Council must convene within three (3) months from
16   the effectivity of this Act to draft a manual of procedures, including meeting
17   rules and decision-making processes.

18   Sec. 10. Composition of the Out-of-School Youth Coordinating Council
19   – The Coordinating Council is composed of the following:

20     a. The DepEd and DOLE Secretaries as ex-officio Co-Chairpersons of the
21        Coordinating Council;
22     b. One (1) representative from each of the following agencies:
23         i. Commission on Higher Education (CHED);
24        ii. Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA);
25        iii. National Youth Commission (NYC);
1           iv. Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD);
2            v. Department of Health (DOH);
3           vi. Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev);
4           vii. Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);
5        viii. Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA); and
6           ix. Department of Trade and Industry (DTI).

7           The Department Secretaries or Chairpersons may designate permanent
8           and alternate authorized representatives to the Coordinating Council. The
9           representative must have a rank of Undersecretary.

10     c. Three (3) sectoral representatives from out-of-school youth organizations
11           recognized by the National Youth Commission (one each from Luzon,
12           Visayas, and Mindanao); and
13     d. Three (3) representatives from civil society and the private sector.

14   The Coordinating Council may include other government agencies as members
15   of the Coordinating Council upon approval of at least two-thirds (2/3) majority
16   of all the members thereof.

17   Sec.     11.   Powers       and   Functions    of   the   Out-of-School      Youth
18   Coordinating Council. – The Coordinating Council has the following powers
19   and functions:

20     a. Formulate a timebound and comprehensive plan to eliminate the cases of
21           out-of-school children and reduce the incidence of out-of-school youth to
22           less than five percent (5%) of the population aged fifteen (15) to thirty (30)
23           years old;
24     b. Coordinate and align existing and potential programs and policies to
25           enforce this Act;
1      c. Monitor and assess the implementation of this Act;
2      d. Lead regular nationwide information campaigns to raise awareness and
3         support for out-of-school youth;
4      e. Ensure that strategies specific for out-of-school youth are integrated in
5         the Philippine Youth Development Plan;
6      f. Create and implement capacity-building programs and activities for
7         Coordinating Council members to fulfill their mandates and functions;
8      g. Submit annual reports and recommendations to Congress;
9      h. Collate sufficient actionable data and quantifiable information to enact
10        and propose policies and programs for the out-of-school youth, through the
11        use of the Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS);
12     i. Oversee the expenditure of the budget, including securing grants,
13        donations, and other sources of funding;
14     j. Develop a quantifiable and definitive tracking system using the CBMS to
15        monitor the incidence and rate of the population of out-of-school youth;
16        and
17     k. Meet at least once every quarter to continuously improve the
18        implementation of this Act;

19   Sectoral, civil society, and private sector representatives will serve a three-
20   year term. Members of the Coordinating Council will receive per diem
21   allowances, as set by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM)
22   under existing rules.

14                                     CHAPTER V
15                                 FREE EDUCATION

16   Section. 17. Compulsory Basic Education. – Children from ages five (5) to
17   eighteen (18) years old must be enrolled in school. To this end, the DepEd must
18   adopt programs and interventions to identify, retrieve, and retain children and
19   youth at risk of dropping out. Out-of-school children and youth shall be provided
20   access to free basic education through either the formal or nonformal
21   educational institutions.

22   Sec. 18. Alternative Learning System. – The State must make the ALS free,
23   widely available, and accessible to out-of-school youth. Out-of-school youth shall
24   be prioritized in enrollment and completion of ALS. Their completion of the ALS
25   will be considered as completion of the equivalent level of formal education.
1    Sec. 19. TESDA Support to Out-of-School Youth. – TESDA is directed to
2    prioritize and offer free technical and vocational education services to out-of-
3    school youth. The ALS certification of completion shall be recognized for
4    admission into the programs of TESDA requiring secondary education
5    requirements.

6    Sec. 20. Promotion to Formal Tertiary Education. – After completing basic
7    education through either formal or nonformal institutions, the out-of-school
8    youth who want to pursue college or university studies will be endorsed by
9    DepEd or the LGUs to the CHED.

10   CHED must ensure that not less than ten percent (10%) of all scholarships are
11   set aside for out-of-school youth.

12   Sec. 21. Education Cost and Support. – DepEd, CHED, and TESDA must
13   provide ALS and technical and vocational education services to out-of-school
14   youth free of charge. They will provide materials, instruments, and tools that
15   the out-of-school youth may need while enrolled in ALS or a technical or
16   vocational course, and financial support to cover transportation and other
17   related costs for the duration of training.

18   The LGUs, SKs, and government agencies must ensure that out-of-school
19   children and youth are supported in their studies or training. This support may
20   include helping develop learning hubs, transportation, and learning materials.

21                                    CHAPTER VI
22       EMPLOYMENT AND ENTREPRENEURSHIP OPPORTUNITIES

23   Sec. 22. Employment. – Out-of-school youth aged eighteen (18) years old and
24   above have the option to either continue learning through formal or nonformal
25   educational institutions or engage in gainful employment. They shall be
1    provided employment opportunities to enable their transition as productive
2    members of society.

3    Sec. 23. Government Internship Program for Out-of-School Youth. – Out-
4    of-school youth will be given access to the Government Internship Program
5    (GIP). The DOLE must ensure that out-of-school youth are informed about the
6    availability and requirements of the GIP.

7    Sec. 24. Government-Issued Documents. – Out-of-school youth jobseekers
8    will not be charged any fees when applying for the following government-issued
9    documents:

10         a. Police clearance certificate;
11         b. National Bureau of Investigation clearance;
12         c. Barangay clearance;
13         d. Medical certificate from a public hospital;
14         e. Birth Certificate;
15         f. Marriage Certificate;
16         g. Transcript of academic records issued by state colleges and
17            universities;
18         h. Tax Identification Number (TIN); and
19         i. Unified Multi-Purpose ID (UMID) card.

20   Sec. 25. Entrepreneurial Facilitation. – The DTI will establish a
21   community-based method of instruction for entrepreneurial education for out-
22   of-school youth. In addition to this, the DTI must develop training programs to
23   support entrepreneurship among the out-of-school youth.

24   Sec. 26. Incentives for Employing Out-of-School Youth. – Private
25   companies that hire out-of-school youth are entitled to a tax incentive. The
1    private companies may subtract fifteen percent (15%) of the total salaries paid

5                                    CHAPTER VII
6                                     PENALTIES

7    Sec. 27. Penalties. – Any person who violates any provision of this Act will
8    suffer the following penalties upon conviction:

9          a. For the first violation, imprisonment of six (6) months to two (2) years
10            and a fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) to One Hundred
11            Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00);
12         b. For any subsequent violation, imprisonment of two (2) years to six (6)
13            years and a fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00) to Two
14            Hundred Thousand Pesos (P200,000.00);
15         c. If the offender is a corporation, organization, or any similar entity, the
16            officers directly involved in the violation or abuse shall be held liable;
17            and
18         d. If the offender is an alien, the person will be deported immediately
19            upon service of sentence without further proceedings.

20   Sec. 28. Falsification of Out-of-School Children and Youth Status. – Any
21   person who wrongfully declared themself as an out-of-school child or youth and
22   avails of the benefits and privileges under this law will suffer the following
23   penalties:

24         a. For the first violation, public censure;
25         b. For the second violation, a fine of Three Thousand Pesos (P3,000.00);
1                and
2             c. For the third or subsequent violation, a fine of not less than Ten
3                Thousand Pesos (P10,000.00) but not more than Fifty Thousand Pesos
4                (P50,000.00).

5    If the offender is a child, the provisions of Republic Act No. 9344, otherwise
6    known as the “Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006,” as amended, shall
7    apply.

8    Sec.     29.   Falsification    of   Out-of-School     Children    and    Youth
9    Certification. – Any person who issues an out-of-school child or youth
10   certification to a person who does not fall within the definition of an out-of-
11   school child or youth will suffer the following penalties:

12            a. For the first violation, a fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos
13               (P50,000.00) but not more than Seventy-Five Thousand Pesos
14               (P75,000.00);
15            b. For the second violation, temporary suspension from office for six (6)
16               months to one (1) year or a fine of not less than One Hundred
17               Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00) but not more than One Hundred Fifty
18               Thousand Pesos (P150,000.00) or both; and
19            c. For the third or subsequent violation, imprisonment of not less than
20               six (6) months but not more than two (2) years and a fine of not less
21               than Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (P200,000.00) but not more than
22               Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00).

23                                    CHAPTER VIII
24                                  FINAL PROVISIONS

25   Sec. 30. Appropriations. – This Act will be implemented within the
1    framework of the budget of the DepEd and DOLE as the lead agencies of the
2    Out-of-School Children and Youth Coordinating Council for the year it is
3    enacted into law. The amount necessary for the effective implementation of this
4    Act will be included in the budget of the member agencies of the Coordinating
5    Council in the annual General Appropriations Act.

6    Sec. 31. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within thirty (30) days
7    upon the effectivity of this Act, the member agencies of the Coordinating
8    Council are directed to promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
9    effective implementation of this Act. This will be done in consultation with
10   other stakeholders including out-of-school children and youth organizations
11   and NGOs working with out-of-school children and youth. Failure to
12   promulgate the rules and regulations will not prevent the implementation of
13   this Act upon its effectivity.

14   Sec. 32. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
15   unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder of this Act or any provision not
16   affected thereby will remain in full force and effect.

17   Sec. 33. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, issuances, rules and
18   regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
19   hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly;

20   Sec. 34. Effectivity. – This Act takes effect fifteen (15) days after its
21   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
