                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Behind every harvest that reaches our tables is a quiet army of workers
whom we rarely see: the agricultural technologists who walk the rice paddies
with farmers, the extension workers who teach new planting methods in the
barangays, the researchers in our state universities, and the technicians who
inspect our livestock and crops. They are the bridge between government
programs and the Filipino farmer. Without them, even the best-funded
agricultural program never leaves the office.

      Yet these workers are among the least protected in government and in
industry. Many serve for years under contract-of-service or job-order
arrangements, without security of tenure, without hazard pay even as they work
under the heat of the sun and in the middle of typhoons and animal disease
outbreaks, and without a clear career path. Many of them are assigned to our
poorest and most remote municipalities, where the need is greatest.

       The farmers they serve remain among the poorest Filipinos. Based on the
latest official poverty statistics of the Philippine Statistics Authority, released in
March 2025, poverty incidence in 2023 stood at 27.0 percent among farmers and
27.4 percent among fisherfolk, among the highest of all the basic sectors in the
country. The State has made important investments to change this, including
the extension of the Rice Competitiveness Enhancement Fund until 2031 under
Republic Act No. 12078. But machines, seeds, and credit will only go as far as
the people who bring them to the farm.

      This bill seeks to modernize Philippine agriculture by investing in its
people. It professionalizes agricultural development workers in public service
through proper position titles, a merit-based career system, and security of
tenure. It guarantees their right to organize, to due process, and to be consulted
in the policies that affect their work. It grants public-sector agricultural
development workers the allowances and benefits appropriate to the risks and
demands of their work, and it provides national government support to our
poorer local government units so that these benefits do not become an unfunded
burden on them.

       For us in the provinces, the agricultural development worker is not a
stranger. He or she is the kababayan who knocks on the farmer’s door, who stays
until the job is done, and who carries the hopes of our rural communities. It is
time that the State took care of them, as they take care of our farmers.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
    MODERNIZING THE PHILIPPINE AGRICULTURAL SECTOR BY
PROFESSIONALIZING AND PROTECTING AGRICULTURAL DEVELOPMENT
             WORKERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Agricultural
Modernization Act of 2026.”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – Pursuant to Article II, Section 21 and
Article XIII, Section 3 of the Constitution, the State shall promote comprehensive
rural development and afford full protection to labor. Toward this end, the State
recognizes that the modernization of the agricultural sector depends on a
competent, motivated, and adequately protected agricultural workforce. The
State shall therefore professionalize agricultural development workers, promote
and protect their rights and welfare, and ensure that they are able to deliver
efficient and effective services to farmers, fisherfolk, and rural communities.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Agricultural sector refers to the sector engaged in the cultivation of the
          soil, the planting of crops, the growing of fruit trees, the raising of
          livestock, poultry, or fish, including the harvesting and marketing of
          such farm products, and other farm activities and practices;
      (b) Agricultural development worker or ADW refers to any person engaged
          in agricultural development activities such as extension, research,
          training, technical, regulatory, and support services, whether in the
          public or private sector and regardless of employment status;
      (c) Public-sector ADW refers to an ADW employed by the Department of
          Agriculture (DA) and its attached agencies and bureaus, local
          government units (LGUs), state universities and colleges, and other
          government instrumentalities, including those engaged under contract-
          of-service or job-order arrangements; and
      (d) Private-sector ADW refers to an ADW employed by a private person,
          firm, cooperative, or institution.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. – The provisions of this Act shall be applicable to
all ADWs in the public and private sectors.

      SECTION 5. Employment Generation Support. – The DA and the
Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), in coordination with the
Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), the Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority (TESDA), and the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), shall undertake programs to generate employment opportunities for
ADWs, with priority given to deployment in underserved rural areas.

      SECTION 6. Professionalization of Public-Sector ADWs. – The DA, in
coordination with the Civil Service Commission (CSC) and the Department of
Budget and Management (DBM), shall ensure that public-sector ADWs are
conferred with appropriate position titles and salary grades, consistent with the
applicable salary standardization law, and with professional recognition
commensurate with their qualifications and functions.

       SECTION 7. Career Development and Merit Promotion. – The DA, in
consultation with the CSC, the PRC and the appropriate Professional Regulatory
Boards, and recognized national organizations of ADWs, shall prepare
standardized career and personnel development plans for public-sector ADWs.
Such plans shall include merit promotion, performance evaluation, in-service
training and scholarship grants, and job rotation opportunities.

       The DA, the CSC, and the DBM shall likewise formulate a program for the
progressive creation of permanent positions for public-sector ADWs who have
rendered at least three (3) years of satisfactory service under contract-of-service
or job-order arrangements, subject to civil service laws, rules, and regulations.

      SECTION 8. Transfer or Geographical Reassignment. – No public-sector
ADW shall be transferred or geographically reassigned without written notice
stating the reason therefor, served at least thirty (30) days before the effective
date of the transfer or reassignment: Provided, That the ADW concerned may
appeal the order before the CSC or other appropriate authority, and the transfer
or reassignment shall be held in abeyance pending the resolution of the appeal:
Provided, further, That no transfer or reassignment shall be made within three
(3) months before any local or national election: Provided, finally, That the
necessary expenses of the transfer or reassignment of the ADW and his or her
immediate family shall be borne by the government.

       SECTION 9. Security of Tenure. – No public-sector ADW holding a
permanent position shall be removed or suspended from the service except for
cause as provided by law and after due process, in accordance with civil service
laws, rules, and regulations. A public-sector ADW who is found by competent
authority to have been unjustly dismissed shall be entitled to reinstatement
without loss of seniority rights and to full back salaries from the time of dismissal
until actual reinstatement.

      SECTION 10. Code of Professional Conduct. – ADWs engaged in regulated
professions shall be guided by the codes of professional ethics promulgated by
the PRC and the appropriate Professional Regulatory Boards. The Secretary of
Agriculture, in consultation with the DOLE, the CSC, and concerned
stakeholders, shall adopt a Code of Professional Conduct for ADWs who are not
covered by a regulated profession.

      SECTION 11. Hours of Work. – The normal hours of work of a public-sector
ADW shall not exceed eight (8) hours a day or forty (40) hours a week. Hours of
work shall include all time during which the ADW is required to be on duty at
the prescribed workplace, and all time during which the ADW is required or
permitted to work in the field or in places other than the prescribed workplace.

      SECTION 12. Overtime Work. – Where the exigencies of the service so
require, a public-sector ADW may be required to render service beyond the
normal hours of work, including on rest days and holidays: Provided, That the
ADW shall be entitled to overtime pay or compensatory time-off in accordance
with existing laws, rules, and regulations.

      SECTION 13. Freedom from Interference or Coercion. – It shall be unlawful
for any person to commit any of the following acts against an ADW:

      (a) To require, as a condition of employment, that the ADW shall not join
          an association, organization, or union of ADWs;
      (b) To discriminate in regard to hiring, tenure, or any term or condition of
          employment in order to encourage or discourage membership in any
          association, organization, or union of ADWs;
      (c) To prevent an ADW from carrying out his or her duties and functions
          in his or her association, organization, or union;
      (d) To penalize an ADW for any lawful action performed as a member of
          any association, organization, or union of ADWs;
      (e) To harass or intimidate an ADW, or to prevent him or her from
          performing his or her duties and functions; or
      (f) To perform acts that diminish the independence of any association,
          organization, or union of ADWs in directing its own affairs.
       SECTION 14. Non-Discrimination. – No ADW shall be discriminated
against on the basis of age, sex, civil status, disability, religion, ethnicity, or
political belief in connection with the exercise of his or her profession or the
performance of his or her duties.

       SECTION 15. Rights in Administrative Proceedings. – In every
administrative proceeding against a public-sector ADW, he or she shall have the
right to:

      (a) Be informed, in writing, of the charges;
      (b) Full access to the evidence against him or her;
      (c) Defend himself or herself, with or without the assistance of counsel of
          his or her choice;
      (d) Adequate time to prepare his or her defense, which shall not be less
          than fifteen (15) days from receipt of the charges;
      (e) Confront and cross-examine witnesses, and to have compulsory
          process for the attendance of witnesses and the production of
          documents in his or her favor;
      (f) Appeal any adverse decision to the proper authorities; and
      (g) Reimbursement of reasonable expenses incurred in his or her defense,
          in case of exoneration or dismissal of the charges.

       SECTION 16. Right to Organize. – ADWs shall have the right to freely form,
join, or assist associations, organizations, or unions for purposes not contrary
to law, for the protection of their mutual interests, and to seek redress of
grievances through peaceful concerted activities, in accordance with the
Constitution and existing laws.

      SECTION 17. Consultation in Policymaking. – Organizations of ADWs shall
be consulted in the formulation of national and local policies, programs, and
professional standards on agriculture. The State shall ensure that ADWs are
adequately represented in the Philippine Council for Agriculture and Fisheries,
the provincial, city, and municipal Agricultural and Fishery Councils, local
development councils, and other policymaking and consultative bodies on
agriculture.

       SECTION 18. Performance-Based Incentives. – Public-sector ADWs shall
be entitled to performance-based incentives, which may include honoraria for
services rendered beyond their regular workload, incentive bonuses, and
performance bonuses for exceeding established targets, subject to the guidelines
of the DBM and existing laws, rules, and regulations.
      SECTION 19. Allowances and Benefits of Public-Sector ADWs. – In addition
to the compensation and benefits provided under existing laws, public-sector
ADWs shall be entitled to the following:

      (a) Hazard allowance, for ADWs whose work exposes them to occupational
          risks, such as exposure to pesticides and other hazardous substances,
          animal diseases, extreme weather, and remote or conflict-affected
          areas;
      (b) Subsistence allowance;
      (c) Transportation and travel allowance, for ADWs whose duties require
          regular field work;
      (d) Clothing allowance, including protective gear appropriate to field work;
      (e) Longevity pay equivalent to five percent (5%) of the monthly basic salary
          for every five (5) years of continuous and satisfactory service; and
      (f) Compensation for work-related injury, illness, or death, in accordance
          with existing laws.

       The rates and conditions for the grant of the foregoing allowances shall be
determined by the DA and the DBM in the implementing rules and regulations
of this Act, taking into account the applicable salary standardization law.

      SECTION 20. Support to Local Government Units. – The National
Government shall provide financial assistance to fourth, fifth, and sixth class
municipalities, and to other LGUs with limited fiscal capacity, to cover the
additional cost of the allowances and benefits granted to their ADWs under this
Act. The DA, the DBM, and the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG) shall jointly issue the guidelines for such assistance.

      SECTION 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DA, in coordination with the DOLE, the
CSC, the DBM, the DILG, the PRC and the appropriate Professional Regulatory
Boards, and recognized organizations of ADWs, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for the implementation of this Act.

      SECTION 22. Monitoring and Reporting. – The DA shall create an oversight
committee composed of representatives of the DA, the DOLE, the CSC, the DBM,
the PRC, and organizations of ADWs to monitor the implementation of this Act.
The oversight committee shall submit a semi-annual report on the
implementation of this Act to the appropriate committees of the Senate and the
House of Representatives.

       SECTION 23. Penalties. – Any person who violates Section 13 of this Act
shall, upon conviction, be punished with a fine of not less than Fifty thousand
pesos (₱50,000.00) but not more than Two hundred thousand pesos
(₱200,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but not more
than one (1) year, or both, at the discretion of the court. If the offender is a
juridical entity, the penalty shall be imposed upon its responsible officers.

      If the offender is a public officer or employee, the penalty shall be without
prejudice to his or her administrative liability under civil service laws, rules, and
regulations.

      SECTION 24. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations
of the DA. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SECTION 25. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts not affected
shall remain in full force and effect.

       SECTION 26. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules, and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 27. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

      Approved,
