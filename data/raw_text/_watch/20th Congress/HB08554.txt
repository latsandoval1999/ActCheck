    ESTABLISHING A FISH PORT IN THE MUNICIPALITY OF MAGALLANES,
     PROVINCE OF SORSOGON AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The enactment of the Philippine Fisheries Code of 1998 embodies the State’s
policy of extending full support to the fishery sector, particularly to municipal
fisherfolk, including women and youth, through appropriate technology and research,
adequate financial and production assistance, the establishment of post-harvest
facilities, marketing support, and other related services.

       Consistent with this state policy, this bill seeks to establish a fish port in the
Municipality of Magallanes, Province of Sorsogon. Magallanes is a coastal and agri-
fishery town, with 21 of its 34 barangays located along the coast. Many of its residents
rely on the sea as a primary source of food and livelihood. The municipality also
envisions itself as a leading agri-fishery and historico-tourism municipality in Sorsogon,
with development focused on agriculture, fisheries, livelihood, infrastructure, and
environmental protection.1

        The establishment of a fish port in Magallanes is therefore both timely and
necessary. The Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) has emphasized
that fishing ports and post-harvest facilities are critical to the full development of the
fisheries sector because fish are highly perishable and require strategically located
landing points where catch may be immediately sold, stored, processed, or shipped to
markets. PIDS further noted that municipal fishing ports in the country have
historically been inadequate, and that many lacked basic post-harvest facilities such
as warehouses and ice plants.2

       Moreover, this measure can advance broader development goals for Sorsogon.
It can support municipal fisherfolk, attract buyers and seafood traders, improve local
value-adding activities, and stimulate complementary enterprises in logistics, retail,
and tourism. It can also help institutionalize better fisheries management by
concentrating fish landings in a facility where sanitation, traceability, post-harvest
practices, and conservation-oriented regulation may be more effectively enforced. This
is especially important for Magallanes, where the bay is not only a source of livelihood
but a source of daily sustenance and hope for many families, making marine resources
central to the community’s survival and progress.

       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:


 1      SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Magallanes Fish Port
 2   Development Act”.
 3
 4      SEC. 2. Establishment of Fish Port. There shall be established, under the
 5   supervision of the Department of Agriculture, through the Philippine Fisheries
 6   Development Authority (PFDA), a fish landing port in the Barangay of Tagas,
 7   Municipality of Magallanes, Province of Sorsogon.
 8
 9       SEC. 3. Transfer of Management to the Local Government Unit. Within
10   two (2) years after the construction of the fish port, the PFDA, through a memorandum
11   of agreement, shall transfer its management to the local government unit concerned.
12   It shall implement a training and phasing-in program for local government personnel
13   on the management and operation of the fish port.
14
15      SEC. 4. Appropriations. The Secretary of Agriculture shall include in the
16   Department's programs the establishment of the fish port, the funding of which shall
17   be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. The local government unit
18   concerned shall set said funds from any available local revenue in an amount deemed
19   appropriate for the operationalization of the fish port
20
21       SEC. 5. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect immediately following the
22   completion of its publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
23   circulation.

     Approved,
