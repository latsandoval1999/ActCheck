EXPLANATORY NOTE

Extreme heat poses serious risks to public health, workplace safety, and the

livelihood of Filipino workers, particularly those engaged in agriculture, construction,
manufacturing, transportation, and other occupations that require prolonged exposure
to high temperatures. Prolonged heat exposure can cause dehydration, heat
exhaustion, heat stroke, and other conditions that threaten workers’ health and safety.

In Japan, as of June 1, 2025, a revised national regulation under the Industrial

Safety and Health Law requires employers to protect their workers from extreme heat
or face fines.’ Australia’s Safe Work Australia states that the employer must carry out
additional risk assessments and put in place heat risk control measures including work
plans so as to use the coolest times of the day, reduced activities involving physical
effort, carrying out work in safer locations, ensuring workers have access to cool fresh
water, air conditioning or fans (if possible), and shaded areas if they are working
outdoors. South Africa has the 1987 Environmental Regulations for Workplaces,?
amended in 2003 to include “Thermal Requirements”, covering both cold and hot work.

Emirates, Kuwait, Qatar, and Oman experiences intense heat throughout the period

Filipino workers should not have to choose between protecting their health and

prevent heat-related illnesses, including providing drinking water, adequate rest
periods, suitable recovery areas, and appropriate adjustments to work schedules
during periods of elevated heat risk. While Congress has already legislated and

focused on this is required given the extreme changes in climate in the past years.

This measure seeks to strengthen the integration of extreme-heat risks into
disaster preparedness and local response planning while establishing specific
occupational heat-stress prevention measures for covered workplaces. It also
provides for enforcement through the existing occupational safety and health
framework and protects workers from retaliation when reporting heat-related hazards
or seeking medical assistance.

The measure likewise emphasizes on employers’ responsibility to maintain safe
working conditions and the obligation of government agencies to strengthen public
preparedness and enforce existing worker protections.

Quezon City, Metro Manila

HOUSE BILLNO. 12041

introduced by Deputy Speaker David “Jay-Jay” C. Suarez

AN ACT PROVIDING FOR EXTREME HEAT PREPAREDNESS AND MITIGATION
IN WORKPLACE, STRENGTHENING OCCUPATIONAL HEAT-STRESS
PROTECTION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. -— This Act shall be known as _ the
"Extreme Heat Preparedness and Worker Protection Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is the policy of the State to protect the
life, health, and safety of workers and the public against the hazards of extreme heat;
strengthen local preparedness and response; and ensure safe and healthful working
conditions, particularly for workers engaged in outdoor, agricultural, construction,
manufacturing, and other heat-exposed occupations.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

(a) Extreme Heat Condition refers to conditions identified through official
Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services
Administration (PAGASA) heat index advisories or other relevant official
assessment that indicate elevated risks to human health.

(b) Occupational Heat Stress refers to the physiological strain arising from
exposure to heat, taking into account environmental conditions, physical
exertion, ventilation, radiant heat, clothing, and personal protective
equipment.

===== [page 4] =====
(c) Covered Workplace refers to a workplace covered by Republic Act No.
11058, otherwise known as the Occupational Safety and Health Standards
Law, and its implementing rules and regulations.

(d) Danger Category Heat Index refers to a heat index ranging from 42°C to
51°C as declared by PAGASA, which poses high risks of heat cramps, heat
exhaustion, and heat stroke with continued exposure.

(e) Extreme Danger Category Heat Index refers to a heat index of 52°C and
above as declared by PAGASA, wherein heat stroke is imminent.

(f) Vulnerable Workers refers to pregnant workers, senior citizens, persons
with disabilities (PWDs), and workers with pre-existing medical conditions
aggravated by thermal stress.

SECTION 4. Integration of Extreme Heat into Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Plans. — Extreme heat shall be recognized as a climate-related hazard
for purposes of disaster risk reduction and management planning under Republic Act
No. 10121, otherwise known as the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Act of 2010.

The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC),
PAGASA, and other concerned agencies shall incorporate appropriate extreme-heat
preparedness, monitoring, and mitigation measures into their respective plans and
advisories.

SECTION 5. Local Heat Preparedness and Response. — Local government
units (LGUs) shall incorporate appropriate heat preparedness and response measures
into their local disaster risk reduction and management plans, taking into account local
conditions and available resources.

Such measures may include public cooling and hydration stations, shaded rest
areas, heat-related public advisories, emergency medical response, and assistance
to vulnerable communities. Eligible activities may be funded from the Local Disaster
Risk Reduction and Management Fund (LDRRMF) and other lawful sources, subject
to approved plans, applicable appropriations, and budgeting, accounting, and auditing
rules. The Quick Response Fund shall be used only in accordance with its governing
legal requirements and authorized purposes.

SECTION 6. Mandatory Occupational Heat-Stress Prevention Measures. -
Employers in covered workplaces shall assess and address occupational heat risks
and implement appropriate preventive measures. During periods of elevated heat risk,
employers shall:

(a) Provide workers with an adequate and continuous supply of free, potable
drinking water near their work areas;

===== [page 5] =====
(b) Provide shaded, ventilated, or appropriately cooled recovery areas and
reasonable rest periods. For continuous outdoor or non-air-conditioned
work during a PAGASA Danger or Extreme Danger heat index advisory,
workers shall receive a paid recovery period of at least fifteen (15) minutes
for every two (2) hours of continuous work, separate from meal periods,
subject to any more protective applicable standard;

(c) Adjust work schedules or shift strenuous tasks to cooler hours where
practicable. During a declared Danger Category Heat Index, employers
shall, subject to applicable labor standards, suspend strenuous outdoor
work between 12:00 P.M. and 3:00 P.M., or implement flexible work
arrangements including telecommuting, without diminution of wages,
salaries, or benefits;

(d) Provide suitable protective equipment and clothing appropriate to the work
and heat conditions;

(e) Conduct heat-stress information and training activities, including recognition
of symptoms, first aid, and the implementation of a peer-monitoring or
“buddy system” for early detection of heat-related illnesses; and

(f) Establish procedures for responding to heat-related emergencies, including
prompt access to first aid and appropriate medical care.

The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall prescribe
supplementary standards based on the nature of the work, exposure risks, and
available scientific and occupational health guidance. Compliance with a heat index
threshold alone shall not relieve an employer of the duty to address other identified
workplace heat hazards.

SECTION 7. Compensability and Protection of Vulnerable Workers. —
Heat-related illnesses, including but not limited to heat stroke, heat exhaustion, and
severe dehydration, arising out of or in the course of employment shall be recognized
as compensable occupational injuries or illnesses. Affected workers shall be entitled
to benefits under existing labor, social security, and employees’ compensation laws.

Employers shall provide enhanced protection measures for Vulnerable
Workers, which may include reasonable work modifications, reduced heat exposure,
or reassignment to safer duties where practicable. No Vulnerable Worker shall be
compelled to perform strenuous work under dangerous heat conditions.

SECTION 8. Worker Protection and Enforcement. — No worker shall be
dismissed, discriminated against, or subjected to retaliation for reporting a heat-related
hazard, seeking medical assistance for a suspected heat-related illness, or exercising
rights protected under applicable occupational safety and health laws. The DOLE shall
monitor compliance with this Act through its existing inspection and enforcement
mechanisms. Any violation shall be subject to applicable administrative sanctions and
penalties under Republic Act. No. 11058 and other relevant laws. Nothing in this Act
shall diminish any existing worker protection or remedy under law.

===== [page 6] =====
SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), in
coordination with the Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG), the
National Disaster Risk and Reduction Management Council (NDRRMC), the
Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical, and Astronomical Services Administration
(PAGASA), the Department of Health (DOH), and other concerned agencies, shall
promulgate the rules necessary for its implementation.

SECTION 10. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the immediate
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the available appropriations
concerned agencies. Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary shall be included
in the annual General Appropriations Act, subject to the usual budgetary process.
Local Government Units shall implement their responsibilities through their lawful
funding sources and approved local budgets.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue
in full force and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, administrative
orders, rules, and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
