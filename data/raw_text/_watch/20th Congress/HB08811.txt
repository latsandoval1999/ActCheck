                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Breastmilk is considered the ideal food for infants because it contains antibodies that help
protect children against illnesses. Breastfed children perform better on intelligence tests, are less
likely to be overweight or obese and less prone to diabetes later in life.1

        However, based on UNICEF’s data, less than half (48%) of all newborns globally, are put
to the breast within an hour of birth, leaving many newborns without timely initiation of
breastfeeding and early mother-infant contact.2

        In the Philippines, Republic Act No. 7600, otherwise known as the “Rooming-In and
Breast-Feeding Act of 1992,” adopted rooming-in and breastfeeding as a national policy and seeks
to enable mother-infant bonding immediately after birth. Republic Act No. 10028, otherwise
known as the “Expanded Breastfeeding Promotion Act of 2009,” amended Republic Act No. 7600
and mandates the establishment of lactation stations in all health and non-health facilities,
establishments, or institutions, subject to exemptions where the establishment of lactation stations
is not feasible or necessary due to the peculiar circumstances of the workplace or public place.

        Republic Act No. 10028 further provides that health institutions adopting rooming-in and
breastfeeding shall provide equipment, facilities, and supplies for breastmilk collection, storage,

        Several malls and NAIA terminals, as well as local government units such as Angeles City,
Bacolod City, Naga City, Kalinga, Calapan City, Baras (Rizal), and Luna (La Union), among
others, have established breastfeeding or lactation stations in compliance with Republic Act No.
10028.

       Congress has likewise required lactation stations in other public-facing facilities. Under
Republic Act No. 11311, land transport terminals, stations, stops, rest areas, and RORO terminals
must establish at least one (1) lactation station separate from sanitary facilities, compliant with
Republic Act No. 7600 as amended by Republic Act No. 10028 and its rules and regulations, with
random ocular inspections conducted in coordination with the DOH.

      Breastfeeding also helps mothers recover after childbirth, supports postpartum weight loss,
and may lower the risk of breast or ovarian cancer.

        Despite existing laws and initiatives, breastfeeding support remains uneven at the primary
care level. Rural Health Units (RHUs) and Barangay Health Stations/Centers (BHS/BHC), as
frontline community health facilities, should be equipped with private, clean, and accessible
lactation stations that support breastfeeding and milk expression in accordance with DOH
standards.

        This proposed legislation aims to strengthen the implementation of Republic Act No. 7600,
as amended by Republic Act No. 10028, by mandating the establishment of lactation
(breastfeeding) stations and DOH-standard human milk bank services) in RHUs and BHS/BHCs.

        This measure will help improve access to breastfeeding-friendly spaces and appropriate
lactation support at the community level, and will help ensure the availability of safe, pasteurized
donor human milk where clinically indicated and in accordance with DOH standards, particularly
when lactating mothers are medically unable to breastfeed, temporarily unable to provide sufficient
milk, or are experiencing breastfeeding challenges such as nipple soreness, improper latching, and
engorgement.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

1          Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “RHU and Barangay Lactation
2   (Breastfeeding) Stations and Human Milk Banks Act”

3           Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared a policy of the state to protect,
4   promote, and support breastfeeding and safe infant nutrition by ensuring availability of private,
5   clean, and accessible lactation (breastfeeding) stations in Rural Health Units (RHUs) and Barangay
6   Health Stations/Centers (BHS/BHCs), and by establishing standards-based, Department of Health
7   (DOH) accredited human milk banks to support vulnerable infants where clinically indicated.

8           Consistent with Republic Act No. 7600, as amended by Republic Act No. 10028, the DOH
9   shall define the standards for facilities, equipment, and supplies for breastmilk collection, storage,
 1   and utilization, and shall issue standards for the safe collection, screening, processing,
 2   pasteurization, storage, and distribution of donor human milk.

 3          Section 3. Establishment of Lactation (Breastfeeding) Stations. –                Lactation
 4   (Breastfeeding) stations shall be established by all local government units in Rural Health Units
 5   (RHUs) and Barangay Health Centers (BHS/BHCs) to provide private, clean, and accessible areas
 6   conducive for nursing mothers to feed their children and to express milk.

 7           Consistent with Republic Act No. 10028, lactation stations shall be adequately provided
 8   with the necessary equipment and facilities, such as: lavatory for hand-washing, unless there is an
 9   easily-accessible lavatory nearby; refrigeration or appropriate cooling facilities for storing
10   expressed breastmilk; electrical outlets for breast pumps; a small table; comfortable seats; and such
11   other items, the standards of which shall be defined by the DOH. The lactation station shall not be
12   located in the toilet. LGUs may provide additional suitable amenities (e.g., nursing pillows),
13   subject to DOH standards.

14           Section 4. Establishment of Human Milk Banks. – the Local Government Units shall
15   establish human milk banks in RHUs and BHS/BHCs: Provided, That no human milk bank shall
16   operate unless accredited or licensed by the DOH in accordance with standards issued pursuant to
17   Republic Act No. 7600, as amended by Republic Act No. 10028.
