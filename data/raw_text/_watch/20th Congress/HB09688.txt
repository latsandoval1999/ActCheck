                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Constitution unequivocally recognizes the vital role of the people in
governance and mandates the State to uphold transparency, accountability, social
justice, environmental protection, and the participation of citizens in matters affecting
their lives and welfare. Article II, Section 23 declares that the State shall “encourage
non-governmental, community-based, or sectoral organizations that promote the welfare of the
nation,” while Article XIII, Section 16 further provides that the “right of the people and
their organizations to effective and reasonable participation at all levels of social, political, and
economic decision-making shall not be abridged.” Complementing these constitutional
guarantees are the State policies under Article II promoting full public accountability,
the protection of labor, and the preservation of a balanced and healthful ecology for
present and future generations.

      These constitutional principles are especially significant in the governance and
administration of the Freeport Area of Bataan (FAB), which has emerged as one of the
country’s major industrial, logistics, and investment hubs under Republic Act No. 9728,
otherwise known as the “Freeport Area of Bataan (FAB) Act of 2009,” as amended by
Republic Act No. 11453. Through these laws, the Authority of the Freeport Area of
Bataan (AFAB) has been vested with broad regulatory, administrative, developmental,
and quasi-governmental powers intended to accelerate economic growth, attract
investments, and strengthen industrial development in the Province of Bataan.




                                                                                           Page 1 of 13
       While these expanded powers are intended to advance national development,
they likewise heighten the need for stronger democratic safeguards, meaningful
community participation, and institutional accountability. Large-scale industrial and
infrastructure projects within economic zones often generate profound social,
environmental, and economic consequences for workers, local communities, fisherfolk,
informal settlers, and Indigenous Peoples. In the absence of effective transparency and
participatory mechanisms, development projects may lead to displacement, labor
exploitation, environmental degradation, social conflict, and erosion of public trust.
Worse, communities directly affected by such projects are frequently excluded from
decision-making processes despite bearing the long-term consequences of these
developments.

       At present, there remains no comprehensive statutory mechanism within the FAB
governance framework that institutionalizes the participation of civil society
organizations, affected communities, labor groups, and other stakeholders in project
review, monitoring, and grievance redress. Existing laws primarily focus on expanding
the administrative and regulatory powers of the AFAB without establishing
corresponding participatory structures capable of ensuring that development remains
equitable, transparent, and socially accountable. Consequently, consultations are often
discretionary, monitoring mechanisms are fragmented, and grievance systems remain
inaccessible or inadequate for ordinary citizens and workers.

       This measure seeks to address these longstanding gaps by institutionalizing
participatory governance, transparency mechanisms, and community accountability
systems within the FAB. Central to this reform is the recognition that people’s
participation is not merely symbolic consultation but an essential component of
democratic governance and sustainable economic development. When communities are
given meaningful opportunities to participate in decisions affecting their livelihoods,
land, environment, and welfare, development becomes more stable, inclusive, and
legitimate.

        Ultimately, this measure recognizes that genuine development cannot be
measured solely by investment inflows or infrastructure expansion. Economic growth
that excludes workers, disregards community welfare, weakens environmental
protections, or silences public participation cannot be considered sustainable or just.
Development must be people-centered, transparent, and accountable to those whose
lives are directly affected by it.

       By institutionalizing participatory governance mechanisms within the FAB, this
bill seeks to balance economic development with social justice, environmental
                                                                              Page 2 of 13
stewardship, labor protection, and democratic accountability. It affirms the
constitutional principle that governance must remain accountable to the people and that
those most affected by development must never be excluded from decisions shaping
their future.

      In solidarity with the working class, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
