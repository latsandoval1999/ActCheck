                            AN ACT
STRENGTHENING       THE   ANTI-RABIES  PROGRAM    THROUGH
MANDATORY MICROCHIPPING OF PET DOGS AND CATS, AMENDING FOR
THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9482, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
“ANTI-RABIES ACT OF 2007”

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

This bill seeks to strengthen the country’s anti-rabies program under Republic Act No.
9482 or the “Anti-Rabies Act of 2007”, by requiring the mandatory microchipping, in
addition to mandatory registration, of pet cats and dogs, in order to ensure that stray
dogs and cats are easily identi ied and returned to their owners as soon as they are
caught to avoid possible infection from infected stray animals in the community.

While stray dogs are usually the main source of rabies infestation in a community, cats
are proposed to be included for mandatory registration and microchipping for the
simple reason that house cats that are left to roam around the community are
vulnerable to being attacked by another animals that are possibly infected with rabies.

Unlike other progressive countries, the Philippines has remained to have one of the
highest numbers of rabies cases in the world. The Department of Health (DOH) has
reported that around 200 or 300 Filipinos die of rabies every year. One of the major
reasons for this high rabies incidence in the country is the low compliance with the
mandatory registration of dogs provided in RA 9482, hence, the dif iculty on the part
of government employees to return stray dogs to their rightful owners.
