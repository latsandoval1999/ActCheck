                                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 1 of Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that
the goals of the national economy include a more equitable distribution of
opportunities, income, and wealth and an expanding productivity as the key to
raising the quality of life, especially for the underprivileged. Consistent with these
objectives, mining communities should be equipped with sustainable economic
opportunities beyond mining, particularly because mining operations are finite.
Philippine research shows that Social Development and Management Programs
(SDMPs) support enterprise development and other community interventions,
while economic diversification strengthens the resilience of mining-dependent
communities1

       Recent Philippine research further emphasizes the importance of livelihood
diversification and sustainable enterprise development in mining-affected
communities. A study in Garcia-Hernandez, Bohol highlighted the need for
alternative livelihood opportunities that protect community livelihoods while

 promoting environmental sustainability.2 International research likewise finds
 that mine closure can cause significant socioeconomic disruption and emphasizes
 advance planning, skills development, community participation, and economic
 diversification as essential to sustainable mining transitions.3

        Recognizing these concerns, this measure seeks to strengthen the existing
 SDMP framework by requiring that not less than twenty percent (20%) of the
 total annual amount required to be allocated for the SDMP be earmarked for
 qualified alternative livelihood and enterprise development programs and
 projects in mining host and neighboring communities. The allocation may support
 agricultural and agro-industrial enterprises, community-based businesses,
 cooperatives, skills development, entrepreneurship, MSME development,
 financing, market linkages, and other livelihood-transition activities. The
 allocation shall form part of the existing SDMP obligation and shall not diminish
 other mandatory SDMP requirements.

        The measure further requires community participation and separate
 monitoring, accounting, and reporting of the earmarked funds to promote
 transparency, accountability, and sustainability. By directing a dedicated portion
 of existing SDMP resources toward economic diversification while mining
 operations remain active, the measure seeks to reduce excessive dependence on
 mining and strengthen the capacity of communities to sustain alternative sources
 of income. This approach is consistent with Philippine and international research
 emphasizing economic diversification, livelihood development, and early
 preparation for mine closure and post-mining transitions

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
 sought.

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.         It is hereby declared the policy of the

long-term self-reliance of mining host and neighboring communities. To this end,
the State shall strengthen the existing Social Development and Management

non-mining livelihood opportunities and alternative enterprises.

reduce excessive economic dependence on mining operations and enhance the
capacity of communities to sustain alternative sources of income beyond the life
of the mine.

      SEC. 5. Community Participation and Beneficiary Prioritization.                                                        The
planning and implementation of programs and projects funded under Sections 57
and 57-A of Republic Act No. 7942, as amended, shall be undertaken in
consultation with the concerned host and neighboring communities and, where
applicable,   in    accordance                      with          the       rights           of       Indigenous         Cultural
Communities/Indigenous Peoples under existing laws.


                                                                                                                                4
      Priority shall be given to qualified individuals, households, workers,
dependents, livelihood groups, cooperatives, associations, and community
enterprises whose sources of income are directly or indirectly affected by mining
operations or who require livelihood diversification or transition as a result of
mine development, suspension, closure, or rehabilitation.

      SEC. 6. Monitoring, Reporting, and Compliance.               The amount
earmarked pursuant to Section 57 of Republic Act No. 7942, as amended, shall
be separately identified in the approved five-year and annual Social Development
and Management Program.

      Every contractor, permit holder, or lessee covered by Section 57 shall
submit such financial and accomplishment reports as may be prescribed by the
Department of Environment and Natural Resources through the Mines and
Geosciences Bureau, including:

      (a) The total annual amount required to be allocated for the Social
      Development and Management Program;

      (b) The amount corresponding to the mandatory twenty percent (20%)
      allocation;

      (c) The programs, projects, and activities funded under Section 57-A;

      (d) The beneficiaries and communities served;

      (e) The amount programmed, disbursed, and utilized; and

      (f) The physical, financial, and sustainability accomplishments of such
      programs and projects.

      The Mines and Geosciences Bureau shall establish appropriate monitoring

      SEC. 7. Administrative Penalties.         Any contractor, permit holder, or
lessee covered by Section 57 of Republic Act No. 7942, as amended, who fails to
comply with the mandatory allocation under this Act, or who diverts, misuses, or
improperly utilizes funds earmarked thereunder, shall, upon a finding of
administrative liability and after observance of due process, be subject to the
following administrative penalties:

      (a) Failure to allocate the required amount.         An administrative fine
equivalent to twice (2) the amount of the deficiency or Five Hundred Thousand
Pesos (P500,000.00), whichever is higher, without prejudice to the obligation to
make up the deficiency;

      (b) Failure to properly implement an approved program or project without
justifiable cause.   An administrative fine equivalent to the amount improperly
withheld, unutilized, or unimplemented, but not less than Five Hundred Thousand
Pesos (P500,000.00), without prejudice to an order directing the proper
implementation or completion thereof;

      (c) Misuse, diversion, or unauthorized utilization of earmarked funds. An
administrative fine equivalent to twice (2) the amount misused, diverted, or
improperly utilized or One Million Pesos (P1,000,000.00), whichever is higher,
together with an order for restitution or restoration of the amount concerned; and

      (d) Repeated violation. In case of a second or subsequent violation within
five (5) years from the finality of an administrative decision for a prior violation,
the Department of Environment and Natural Resources, through the Mines and
Geosciences Bureau, may recommend the suspension or revocation of the

      SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations.          Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Environment and Natural
Resources, through the Mines and Geosciences Bureau, in coordination with the
Department of Trade and Industry, Department of Agriculture, Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority, Department of the Interior and
Local Government, concerned local government units, Indigenous Cultural
Communities/Indigenous Peoples, mining industry stakeholders, and other
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.

      The implementing rules and regulations shall provide, among others, for:

      (a) The manner of determining and computing the mandatory twenty
      percent (20%) allocation;

      (b) The qualifications of beneficiaries and criteria for eligible programs,
      projects, and activities;

      (c) Community consultation and participation procedures;

      (d) Financial management, documentation, monitoring, and reporting
      requirements;

      (e) Audit and verification mechanisms;

       (f) Procedures for investigating and adjudicating violations; and

       (g) Sustainability and livelihood-transition indicators.

       SEC. 9. Transition of Existing Social Development and Management
Programs.       All approved five-year and annual Social Development and
Management Programs existing upon the effectivity of this Act shall be reviewed
and, where necessary, adjusted to comply with the mandatory allocation
prescribed herein.

       Existing lawful alternative livelihood and enterprise development
programs and projects that satisfy the requirements of this Act may be credited
toward the twenty percent (20%) allocation, subject to the approval, monitoring,
accounting, and reporting requirements prescribed by the Department of
Environment and Natural Resources through the Mines and Geosciences Bureau.

       SEC. 10. Non-Diminution of Existing Obligations.           Nothing in this Act
shall be construed to reduce, remove, suspend, or otherwise diminish any existing
obligation of contractors, permit holders, or lessees under Republic Act No. 7942,
its implementing rules and regulations, environmental laws, mine rehabilitation
