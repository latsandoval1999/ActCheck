                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill aims to institutionalize a Magna Carta for Private Health
Workers, extending to health personnel in the private sector protections
comparable to those enjoyed by their counterparts in government service
under Republic Act (RA) No. 7305, otherwise known as the Magna Carta of
Public Health Workers. The measure finds constitutional anchor in Article II,
Sections 15 and 18, which mandate the government to protect public health
and uphold the welfare and rights of workers. Furthermore, Article XIII,
Sections 3, 11, and 14 of the 1987 Constitution, which respectively mandate
the State to afford full protection to labor, to ensure the delivery of quality
and affordable health services, and to promote equal work opportunities
regardless of sex, disability, or other status.

       The Philippine Government has continuously underscored the critical
roles of health workers in carrying out health programs and projects. The
Department of Budget and Management (DBM) issued DBM Budget Circular
2020-4 on July 17, 2020, which implemented the Supreme Court ruling to
upgrade entry-level government nurses (Nurse I) from Salary Grade 11 to
Salary Grade 1. Although this was a milestone for our public health workers,
this has resulted in a disparity of benefits and privileges between public
health workers and private health workers.

        The Magna Carta of Private Health Workers aims to level the playing
field between public and private health workers, and seeks to afford them the
benefits and safeguards they deserve. While it will substantially mirror the
Magna Carta of Public Health Workers, this proposed measure seeks to
implement a minimum wage rate for nurses and other health workers, subject
to the increases deemed appropriate by a tripartite body.
      This proposed measure seeks to:

         a) Align its labor standards provisions (probationary
            employment, hours of work, overtime, holiday and rest-day
            pay, wage-fixing, legal interest, and due process) with the Labor
            Code of the Philippines, as renumbered by Department of
            Advisory No. 01-15, and with prevailing Supreme Court
            jurisprudence;
         b) Reconcile the proposed statutory minimum wage with the
            wage-fixing machinery under Republic Act No. 6727 (Wage
            Rationalization Act) by treating it as a national floor without
            prejudice to more favorable Regional Tripartite Wages and
            Productivity Board (RTWPB) wage orders;
         c) Qualify the right to self-organization and to strike with the
            Secretary of Labor and Employment's power to assume
            jurisdiction over labor disputes in industries indispensable to
            the national interest under Article 278(g) of the Labor Code, and
            with the concomitant duty to maintain an unhampered skeletal
            workforce in hospitals and clinics, in order to safeguard
            patients' constitutional right to life and health; and

         d) Clarify that the funds to be appropriated under this Act are for
            the Department of Labor and Employment's administrative and
            enforcement functions, and not a government subsidy for
            private-sector wages and benefits, which remain the obligation
            of private employers.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this legislative
measure is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT INSTITUTING A MAGNA CARTA FOR PRIVATE HEALTH
 WORKERS, PROVIDING FUNDS FOR ITS IMPLEMENTATION AND
         ENFORCEMENT, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Title.- This Act shall be known as the "Magna Carta of
Private Health Workers."

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.- The State values the health of the
people above all else. Pursuant to Article II, Sections 15 and 18, and Article
XIII, Sections 3, 11, and 14 of the 1987 Constitution, the State shall protect and
promote the right to health of the people, afford full protection to labor,
ensure the delivery of quality and affordable health services, and promote
equal opportunities for private health workers regardless of sex, disability,
religion, or similar status. Towards this end, this Act aims to:

       a) Promote and improve the social and economic well-being of private
       health workers, their living and working conditions, and their terms of
       employment;

       b) Develop the skills and capabilities of private health workers so that
       they are more responsive to, and better equipped for, the delivery of
       health goods, services, and programs; and

       c) Achieve parity of treatment, to the extent reasonable and consistent
       with the nature of private enterprise, between private health workers
       and public health workers covered by Republic Act (RA) No. 7305 or
       the Magna Carta of Public Health Workers, without diminishing the
       latter's existing benefits.
      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms.- For purposes of this Act:

      a) Private Health Worker refers to any person who, for compensation,
      renders health or health-related work in a private health care
      institution, whether rank-and-file, supervisory, or managerial, and
      regardless of employment status: Provided, That the term excludes
      personnel already covered by RA No. 7305 and personnel whose
      functions are purely administrative, financial, or clerical and are not
      directly related to the delivery of health care services.

      b) Private Health Care Institution refers to a hospital, infirmary, health
      center, clinic, diagnostic or laboratory center, dialysis center, hospice,
      drug and alcohol rehabilitation center, or other health-related
      establishment that is privately owned or operated and duly licensed
      by the Department of Health (DOH).

      c) Transfer refers to a movement from one position to another of
      equivalent rank, level, or salary, without break in service.

      d) Geographical Reassignment or reassignment refers to a movement of a
      private health worker from one geographical work location to another.

      e) On-Call Status refers to a condition where a private health worker,
      while not physically required to be at the workplace, must remain
      reachable and able to report promptly to respond to an urgent or
      immediate need for health or medical assistance or relief work during
      emergencies, such that he/she cannot devote the time to purely
      personal pursuits.

      f) Hazard Pay refers to the additional compensation for private health
      workers who are exposed to hazardous working conditions, including
      but not limited to exposure to infectious or communicable diseases,
      radiation, or physical, chemical, or biological hazards inherent in their
      work.

       SEC. 4. Coverage.- This Act shall apply to all private health workers as
defined herein, whether Filipino or foreign nationals lawfully employed in
the Philippines and subject to existing immigration and alien employment
laws, and to all private health care institutions employing them, without
prejudice to more favorable terms and conditions of employment already
granted under existing company policy, collective bargaining agreements, or
contracts.

     SEC. 5. Recruitment and Qualification.- The recruitment policy and
minimum requirements for the selection and appointment of a private health
worker shall be developed by the concerned employer in accordance with the
policies and standards of the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE)
and applicable professional regulatory laws. Probationary employment shall
not exceed six (6) months from the date the private health worker started
working, pursuant to Article 296 of the Labor Code, unless a shorter period is
agreed upon by the parties or a different period is fixed by an apprenticeship
agreement: Provided, That the reasonable standards for regularization shall
be made known to the private health worker at the time of his/her
engagement, failing which he/she shall be deemed a regular employee.

       SEC. 6. Transfer or Geographical Reassignment of Private Health
Workers.- A private health worker shall not be transferred and/or reassigned
except when made in the interest of public service or of patient care, in which
case the health worker concerned shall be informed in writing of the reasons
therefor.

       If the private health worker believes that there is no justification for the
transfer and/or reassignment, he/she may appeal the matter to the DOLE,
which shall cause the implementation of the transfer or reassignment to be
held in abeyance pending resolution of the appeal, without prejudice to the
health worker's right to contest the transfer or reassignment as constructive
dismissal before the National Labor Relations Commission (NLRC). The
necessary and reasonable expenses of the transfer or reassignment, including
those of the employee's immediate family who relocate with him/her, shall
be paid by the employer.

       SEC. 7. Married Health Workers.- To the extent reasonable and
consistent with the prevailing working conditions of the private health care
institution, and without disrupting health care services, the employer shall
take steps to enable married couples who are both private health workers to
be employed or assigned in the same unit, section, department, or division of
the institution, giving primordial consideration to patient care and
operational requirements.

       SEC. 8. Right to Security of Tenure.- All private health workers shall
enjoy security of tenure in their employment as provided by law. Their
services shall not be terminated except for just or authorized causes under the
Labor Code, as amended, and only after compliance with statutory due
process. A private health worker found by final judgment or resolution to
have been unjustly dismissed shall be entitled to reinstatement without loss
of seniority rights, full back wages, and other benefits, computed from the
time compensation was withheld up to actual reinstatement, plus legal
interest at the rate prescribed by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) and
applicable Supreme Court jurisprudence.
       SEC. 9. Nature of Employment; Contracting and Subcontracting.-
When an employer contracts or subcontracts the performance of work that is
necessary or desirable to the operations of the hospital or health facility, the
arrangement shall be governed by the Labor Code and its implementing rules
on contracting and subcontracting, including the prohibition on labor-only
contracting. Workers supplied by a legitimate job contractor to perform such
work shall enjoy all the rights and benefits granted under this Act and other
applicable laws.

      Project-based private health workers are considered regular
employees for the duration of the project. Project-based employment refers to
employment in a project or undertaking that is temporary in nature and
whose completion or termination has been determined and made known to
the employee at the time of engagement.

       SEC. 10. Discrimination Prohibited.- No private health worker shall
be discriminated against on the basis of age, sex, gender identity or
expression, sexual orientation, civil status, pregnancy, disability, health status
(including HIV status, consistent with RA No. 11166 or the Philippine HIV
and AIDS Act), creed, religious or political belief, or ethnicity, in the exercise
of his/her profession or in any term or condition of employment.

       SEC. 11. No Understaffing or Overloading of Health Staff.- There shall
be no understaffing or overloading of private health workers. The ratio of
health staff to patient load shall be such as to reasonably ensure the sustained
delivery of quality health care at all times, without overworking the private
health worker or over-extending his/her duty and service. Health students,
trainees, and apprentices shall be engaged only for training and education
purposes and shall not perform tasks beyond their academic and practical
curriculum.

A substitute officer or employee shall be provided in place of an officer or
employee who is on leave for more than three (3) months.

The DOLE, in coordination with the DOH and in consultation with
professional and health workers' organizations or unions, shall define the
parameters for determining the existence of understaffing or overloading
through the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

       SEC. 12. Administrative Due Process.- No private health worker shall
be subjected to any disciplinary or administrative sanction except for just
cause and after due process, consisting at minimum of:

       a) a written notice specifying the ground(s) for the charge and giving
       the health worker reasonable opportunity to explain his/her side;
       b) an opportunity to be heard, with the assistance of a representative
       or counsel if he/she so desires; and

       c) a written notice of the decision, stating the facts and grounds relied
       upon.

Administrative charges are encouraged to be heard by a committee composed
of an ethics and/or grievance committee, a union representative where the
private health worker belongs, a representative of any existing employees'
association or union, and/or a representative of a national or provincial
health workers' organization. The committee shall submit its findings and
recommendations to the concerned administrative body within thirty (30)
days from termination of the hearings.

       SEC. 13. Duties and Obligations.- A private health worker shall
discharge his/her duty humanely, with conscience and dignity, and shall
perform his/her duty with utmost respect for life, irrespective of race, gender,
religion, nationality, political affiliation, social standing, or capacity to pay.

       SEC. 14. Regular Hours of Work.- The regular hours of work of any
private health worker shall not exceed eight (8) hours a day, five (5) days a
week, or a total of forty (40) hours a week, exclusive of time for meals. "Hours
worked" shall include: i) all time during which a private health worker is
required to be on duty or to be at a prescribed workplace; and (ii) all time
during which a private health worker is suffered or permitted to work:
Provided, That time spent on On-Call status, as defined in Section 3(e) hereof,
shall not be considered hours worked but shall entitle the private health
worker to On-Call pay equivalent to one hundred percent (100%) of his/her
applicable hourly wage for each hour of such status, without prejudice to full
compensation at applicable overtime rates for any time actually spent
rendering work while on-call.

        SEC. 15. Overtime Work.- Where the exigencies of the service so
require, such as force majeure, a national health threat, or imminent loss of
life, a private health worker may be required to render service beyond eight
(8) hours a day or on a rest day. In such cases, the private health worker shall
be paid additional compensation at rates not lower than those fixed under
Article 87 of the Labor Code and other applicable laws and issuances.

       SEC. 16. Work on Rest Days and Holidays.- A private health worker
made to work on his/her scheduled rest day, on a regular holiday, or on a
special (non-working) day shall be paid additional compensation at rates not
lower than those fixed under Articles 91 to 94 of the Labor Code and other
applicable laws. Where such work falls on the worker's scheduled rest day
and coincides with a holiday, the corresponding premium rates shall be
cumulative as provided by law.

      SEC. 17. Minimum Wages.- A minimum wage for private health
workers is hereby established, which shall be adequate to maintain the
minimum standard of living necessary for their health, efficiency, and general
well-being, taking into consideration the circumstances of their government
counterparts under the Magna Carta of Public Health Workers.

       Without prejudice to existing benefits and to any Wage Order issued
by the Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board (RTWPB) that fixes
a higher rate pursuant to RA No. 6727 or the Wage Rationalization Act, the
wages of private health workers for all actual hours and days worked shall
not be lower than Thirty-Five Thousand Pesos (Php35,000.00) entry monthly
salary for nurses, and Eight Hundred Fifty Pesos (Php850.00) per day for
other private health workers, multiplied by the actual number of days
worked in a month. The rates fixed in this Section constitute a nationwide
statutory floor and shall not be construed to reduce any higher wage rate
already being received or already fixed by a Wage Order in a given region.

        Wages shall be paid at least once every two (2) weeks or twice a month,
at intervals not exceeding sixteen (16) days, consistent with Article 103 of the
Labor Code. Private health workers already receiving wages higher than the
minimum set herein shall have their wages correspondingly adjusted upward
where required by law.

        The rates fixed in this Section shall be reviewed and, if warranted,
adjusted by the DOLE, through the National Wages and Productivity
Commission and the RTWPBs, at least once every two (2) years from the
effectivity of this Act, in order to maintain their adequacy.

       SEC. 18. Other Benefits.- Private health workers shall be entitled to all
benefits provided under the Labor Code and other applicable laws, rules, and
regulations, including but not limited to overtime pay, night-shift differential,
on-call pay, hazard pay, longevity pay, subsistence allowance, special risk
allowance, housing allowance, laundry allowance, remote-assignment
allowance, cash gifts, mental health leave as provided under Section 20
hereof, a free annual physical examination, and free hospitalization for work-
related injury or illness, at levels that are, to the extent reasonable for private
enterprise, comparable to those enumerated for public health workers under
RA No. 7305 or the Magna Carta of Public Health Workers.

      SEC. 19. Hazard Pay.- All private health care institutions shall grant
hazard pay, at their own cost as part of their operating expenses, to personnel
who physically report for work during a declared community quarantine or
public health emergency, or who, by the nature of their work, are subjected
to health hazards or occupational risks, regardless of their degree of exposure.

       Private health workers shall be entitled to a monthly hazard pay of at
least Ten Thousand Pesos (Php10,000.00), without prejudice to a higher
amount depending on existing exigencies or the nature of the work, as may
be fixed by the DOLE.

       SEC. 20. Mental Health Leave.- Consistent with the policy of the State
to value the mental health of workers under RA No. 11036, or the Mental
Health Act, and in keeping with the parity of treatment intended under
Section 2(c) hereof, every private health worker shall be entitled to a Mental
Health Leave of five (5) days with full pay in each calendar year, comparable
to the Wellness Leave granted to government officials and employees,
including public health workers, under Civil Service Commission (CSC)
Resolution No. 2501292 and its implementing guidelines.

      The Mental Health Leave may be availed of for mental health care,
consultation, or treatment, or for a general break from work necessary to
prevent or address work-related stress, burnout, or other mental health
concerns arising from the nature of health care work: Provided, That it may
be taken consecutively for a maximum of three (3) days at a time, or on
separate, non-consecutive days, within the calendar year.

        The Mental Health Leave shall be separate and distinct from the
vacation leave, sick leave, service incentive leave, and other leave benefits
already granted to the private health worker, and shall not be charged against
the latter. It shall be non-cumulative and non-commutable to cash, and shall
be forfeited if not availed of within the calendar year: Provided, That the
employer may adopt more liberal accumulation or commutation rules.

       Applications for Mental Health Leave shall be filed at least five (5) days
before the intended date of availment: Provided, That in urgent or emergency
cases, the application may be filed immediately upon the health worker's
return to work.

       All information relating to a private health worker's mental health
condition disclosed in connection with the availment of this leave shall be
treated with strict confidentiality, consistent with RA No. 10173 or the Data
Privacy Act of 2012, and shall not be used as a ground for any discriminatory
or adverse employment action.

       The DOLE, in coordination with the DOH, shall issue the guidelines
for the implementation of this Section, taking into account the operational
requirements of private health care institutions in ensuring continuity of
patient care.

       SEC. 21. Right to Self-Organization.- All private health workers have
the right to self-organize, and to form, join, or assist an employees' union for
purposes of collective bargaining and mutual aid and protection, consistent
with the Labor Code and Article XIII, Section 3 of the Constitution.

       SEC. 22. Right to Strike; Limitations in Health Care Settings.- Private
health workers and their unions have the right to strike and to engage in other
peaceful concerted activities in accordance with the Labor Code, in order to
defend and protect their rights and welfare and to seek redress of grievances.

       Considering that private health care institutions provide services
indispensable to the national interest, this right shall be exercised subject to
Article 278(g) of the Labor Code, under which the Secretary of Labor and
Employment may assume jurisdiction over, or certify to the NLRC for
compulsory arbitration, a labor dispute in the sector, with the effect of
automatically enjoining the intended or impending strike or lockout.

       In any labor dispute adversely affecting a private health care
institution, it shall be the duty of the striking union and/or the locking-out
employer to maintain an effective and unhampered skeletal workforce of
medical and other health personnel sufficient to ensure the proper and
adequate protection of the life and health of patients, especially emergency
and critical cases, for the duration of the strike or lockout, consistent with
Article 278(g) of the Labor Code.

       SEC. 23. Freedom from Interference or Coercion.- It shall be unlawful
for any person to commit any of the following acts of interference or coercion:

       a) Requiring, as a condition of employment, that a private health
       worker shall not join a health workers' organization or union, or shall
       relinquish membership therein;

       b) Discriminating in regard to hiring, tenure of employment, or any
       term or condition of employment, in order to encourage or discourage
       membership in any health workers' organization or union;

       c) Preventing a health worker from carrying out the duties of his/her
       position in the organization or union, or penalizing him/her for
       actions undertaken in such capacity;

       d) Interfering with the discharge of a health worker's functions when
       calculated to intimidate or prevent the performance of his/her duties
       and responsibilities;
      e) Requiring membership in any professional association, other than
      valid professional licensure or accreditation required by law under RA
      No. 10912 or the Professional Regulation Commission Modernization
      Act or a specific profession's regulatory law, as a condition for
      employment;

      f) Interfering in the establishment, functioning, or administration of a
      health workers' organization or union through acts designed to place
      it under the control of the employer or of a government authority; and

      g) Harassing or bullying a health worker in the workplace at any time
      and under any circumstance.

       SEC. 24. Consultation with Health Workers' Organizations.- In
formulating national policies governing the social security and welfare of
private health workers, the Secretary of Labor and Employment shall consult
professional and health workers' organizations or unions, as well as other
appropriate government agencies. For this purpose, a Tripartite Council shall
be established and made operational at the national, regional, and other
appropriate levels.

       SEC. 25. Health Human Resource Development.- The DOLE shall
conduct a periodic health human resource development and management
study covering, among others:

      a) the adequacy of facilities and supplies for quality health care;

      b) opportunities for health workers to grow professionally and
      experience dignity in their work; access to free legal assistance for
      work-related cases and mechanisms for democratic consultation in
      private health institutions;

      c) staffing patterns and standards of care, which shall be immediately
      and strictly enforced;

      d) means of enabling rank-and-file workers to access education for
      personal growth;

      e) the upgrading of working conditions, position reclassification, and
      salaries of private health workers to correct disparities vis-à-vis
      comparable professions;

      f) and an assessment of the national policy on the deployment of
      skilled health human resources abroad, with a view toward retaining
      and utilizing these resources for the country's needs.
       The employer shall ensure career opportunities for health workers to
grow professionally. A private health worker who completes a post-graduate
degree relevant to his/her work shall be entitled to a corresponding upgrade
in position or pay, granted not more frequently than once every two (2) years.

        SEC. 26. Implementing Rules and Regulations.- Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Labor and Employment,
after consultation with the DOH and other appropriate agencies, professional
regulatory boards, and health workers' organizations or unions, shall
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary to implement this Act,
including a Code of Conduct for Private Health Workers, which shall be
disseminated to all private health care institutions.

       SEC. 27. Prohibition Against Double Recovery of Benefits.- Whenever
other laws provide the same benefits covered by this Act, the private health
worker shall have the option to choose which benefits will be paid to
him/her: Provided, That if the benefits chosen are less than those provided
under this Act, he/she shall be paid only the difference.

       SEC. 28. Prohibition Against Elimination and/or Diminution of
Benefits.- Consistent with Article 100 of the Labor Code, nothing in this Act
shall be construed to eliminate or in any way diminish benefits being enjoyed
by private health workers at the time of its effectivity.

       SEC. 29. Prohibition Against Retaliation.- No private health care
institution shall discharge, terminate the employment of, reduce the
compensation of, or otherwise discriminate or take adverse action against any
private health worker for opposing any act or practice made unlawful by this
Act, for participating in proceedings related to this Act, for seeking to enforce
rights under this Act, or for otherwise asserting rights under this Act.

       SEC. 30. No Waiver of Rights.- Any waiver by a private health worker
of any provision of this Act, or of the rights or protections afforded under its
authority, is contrary to public policy and shall be void and unenforceable.

       SEC. 31. Suppletory Application.- In all matters not specifically
covered by this Act, the applicable provisions of the Labor Code of the
Philippines, as amended, and other relevant social legislation shall apply
suppletorily.

       SEC. 32. Appropriations.- The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act by the DOLE, including the conduct of studies,
monitoring, inspection, and the operation of the Tripartite Council under
Section 24 hereof, shall be charged against the current year's appropriations
of the DOLE. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
For clarity, this Section funds only the government's administrative and
enforcement functions under this Act; the wages, benefits, and other
monetary obligations of private health care institutions to private health
workers under this Act shall remain the responsibility of the private
employer.

       SEC. 33. Penal Provision.- Any person or private health care
institution that willfully interferes with, restrains, or coerces a private health
worker in the exercise of his/her rights under this Act, or otherwise violates
any provision of this Act, shall, after due notice and hearing, be liable for an
administrative fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos
(Php100,000.00) nor more than One Million Pesos (Php1,000,000.00) for each
violation, to be imposed by the DOLE, without prejudice to any appropriate
criminal action under the Labor Code and other applicable penal laws.

      If the offender is a public official, the court, upon conviction, may
impose the additional penalty of disqualification from public office.

       A private health worker who is a victim of a violation of this Act shall
be entitled to back wages and full payment of unpaid benefits, as well as
reimbursement of attorney's fees reasonably incurred in enforcing his/her
claim under this Act.

      SEC. 34. Coexistence with Other Available Reliefs.- Nothing in this
Act shall be construed to limit any private health worker's right to any relief
to which he/she may be entitled at law or in equity.

       SEC. 35. Separability Clause.- If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in force
and effect.

       SEC. 36. Repealing Clause.- All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, and their implementing rules and regulations, or parts thereof,
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

      SEC. 37. Effectivity.- This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
