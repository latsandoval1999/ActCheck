                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article 148 of the Family Code governs property relations between persons
who live together without the benefit of marriage where legal capacity to marry is
absent. Recent jurisprudence has clarified that protection of property rights under
Article 148 extends to cohabiting partners regardless of sex or gender, emphasizing
that the controlling standard is legal capacity to marry and not sex and gender
identities.

       Despite this clarification, the existing statutory language of Article 148
remains limited in defining what constitutes contribution and how courts may
fairly evaluate shared acquisition of property. The absence of statutory guidance
may lead to inconsistent interpretations, heightened evidentiary burdens, and
inequitable outcomes for long-term partners whose contributions may not always
be strictly financial.

      This bill seeks to strengthen the equitable spirit of Article 148 by:
         ● Clarifying the scope of cohabiting partners covered by the provision;
         ● Providing a modern and inclusive definition of contribution;
         ● Establishing rebuttable presumptions in long-term cohabitation; and
         ● Recognizing contemporary forms of proof consistent with evolving
             financial and social realities.

property rights arising from shared effort and mutual support receive clear
statutory protection consistent with recent jurisprudence and the principles of
equity and justice.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT STRENGTHENING ARTICLE 148 OF THE FAMILY CODE OF THE
   PHILIPPINES, TO ENSURE FAIR AND ENFORCEABLE PROPERTY
 RIGHTS IN COHABITATION, INCLUDING SAME-SEX PARTNERSHIPS

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippine Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Shared Property
Rights Protection Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote
fairness, equity, and stability in property relations arising from cohabitation not
governed by Article 147 of the Family Code. The State recognizes that property
may be acquired and preserved through diverse forms of contribution, including
unpaid labor and mutual support, and that such contributions deserve legal
protection regardless of the sex, sexual orientation, or gender identity or
expression of the partners, consistent with the Constitution and existing family
and property laws.

       SEC. 3. Clarification of Coverage under Article 148. — For purposes of
Article 148 of the Family Code, cohabiting partners shall include persons living
together, regardless of sex or gender, where a legal impediment to marriage exists.




                                                                                Page 3 of 5
      SEC. 4. Definition of Contribution. — Contribution to the acquisition of
property under Article 148 shall include, but shall not be limited to:

   a. Direct financial investment or payment;
   b. Management of household finances or shared resources;
   c. Caregiving, domestic work, and household administration that enable the
      acquisition or preservation of property;
   d. Professional, business, or economic support that contributes to the earning
      capacity of a partner; and
   e. Other forms of economic or non-economic participation that directly or
      indirectly facilitate acquisition.

       Such contributions may be proven through documentary, testimonial,
digital, or circumstantial evidence.

        SEC. 5. Rebuttable Presumption in Long-Term Cohabitation. — Where
cohabitation between partners covered by Article 148 has been continuous for at
least five (5) years, property acquired during such period shall be presumed to
have been obtained through equal contribution, unless proven otherwise by clear
and convincing evidence.

     SEC. 6. Evidentiary Guidelines. — In determining contribution and co-
ownership under Article 148, courts shall consider all relevant evidence, including
modern forms of proof, such as:
  a. Electronic fund transfers and digital financial records;
  b. Shared utility payments or recurring expenses;
  c. Written acknowledgments of ownership or contribution;
  d. Joint financial arrangements or liabilities; and
  e. Any other relevant indicia of shared economic activity.

       SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Justice shall formulate guidelines
to assist courts and stakeholders in the uniform application of this Act.

       SEC. 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, presidential proclamations, rules or regulations and other issuances, or
parts thereof, contrary to or inconsistent with any provision of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

        SEC. 10. Effectivity Clause. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
