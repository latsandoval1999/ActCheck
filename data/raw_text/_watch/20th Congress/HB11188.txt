                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The COVID-19 pandemic exposed the vulnerability of the Philippine healthcare system
to disruptions in global pharmaceutical supply chains. During the initial wave of the pandemic,
border closures, export restrictions, and logistical bottlenecks significantly constrained the
country’s ability to import essential medicines, placing public health at risk.
        While existing laws promote access to affordable medicines (e.g. Cheaper Medicines
Act), the current regulatory framework relies heavily on administrative issuances of the Food
and Drug Administration (FDA) to safeguard medicine quality and ensure supply continuity. At
the same time, domestic pharmaceutical manufacturers incur substantial fixed costs to comply
with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards, while import-dependent products may
enter the market with uneven quality oversight. This imbalance undermines medicine security,
public trust, and long-term industrial resilience.
        Local pharmaceutical manufacturing occupies a distinct and strategic role in the
national health system. Unlike purely distribution-based or import-based models, local
manufacturing directly supports medicine security by maintaining domestic production
capacity, enabling closer regulatory oversight, sustaining technical and skilled employment,
and strengthening the country’s ability to respond to public health emergencies and external
supply disruptions. For this reason, local pharmaceutical manufacturing should be recognized
not merely as an ordinary commercial activity, but as a strategic capability necessary to
safeguard public health and national resilience.
        This bill seeks to establish a statutory medicine security Framework that balances
access, availability, affordability, safety, and quality, while strengthening local pharmaceutical
manufacturing as a strategic component of national health security. It does not prohibit imports
nor confer automatic incentives. The bill also does not discriminate between multinational and
local industry players in the market. Instead, it ensures that all medicines, whether locally
produced or imported, are subject to equivalent, science-based quality standards.
        The measure aligns fully with the CREATE Act, the CREATE MORE Act, and the
Strategic Investment Priority Plan by recognizing pharmaceutical manufacturing as a strategic
investment activity subject to existing incentive regimes. It further strengthens regulatory
enforcement through statutory FDA-BOC coordination, risk-based traceability, and enhanced
oversight of online pharmaceutical sales.
        Ultimately, this bill aims to protect Filipino patients, promote fair competition, and
ensure that the availability of affordable medicines is never achieved at the expense of quality,
safety, and national resilience.
        In view of the foregoing, immediate approval of this measure is earnestly requested.

                               AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL MEDICINE SECURITY FRAMEWORK, STRENGTHENING
LOCAL PHARMACEUTICAL MANUFACTURING AS A STRATEGIC INVESTMENT,
ENSURING THE QUALITY, SAFETY, AND AFFORDABILITY OF MEDICINES, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Medicine Security Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right to health by ensuring medicine security, defined as the sustained
availability, accessibility, quality, safety, and affordability of medicines through resilient,
transparent, and well-regulated supply chains. In furtherance thereof, the State recognizes
local pharmaceutical manufacturing as an industry with a distinct contribution to strategic
investment and development necessary to safeguard public health, ensure supply continuity,
and strengthen the country’s preparedness for public health emergencies and external supply
disruptions.
        The State recognizes that excessive dependence on import-dependent
pharmaceutical supply chains exposes the country to significant public health risks during
pandemics, global emergencies, trade disruptions, and geopolitical shocks. Accordingly, the
State shall:
    (a) Strengthen local pharmaceutical manufacturing as a pillar of national health security,
        industrial development, and economic resilience;
    (b) Ensure that all medicines offered in the Philippine market, whether locally
        manufactured or imported, comply with uniform, science-based, and verifiable
        standards of quality, safety, and efficacy;
    (c) Promote fair competition, consumer choice, and transparency in the manufacture,
        importation, marketing, and distribution of medicines;
    (d) Encourage pharmaceutical investments in a manner fully consistent with Republic Act
        No. 11534, Republic Act No. 12066, the Strategic Investment Priority Plan (SIPP), and
        related investment laws;
    (e) Strengthen regulatory enforcement against counterfeit, substandard, falsified, and
        unregistered medicines, including those sold through digital and online platforms; and
    (f) Harmonize public health objectives with the State’s obligations under international
        trade agreements.

SECTION 3. Scope and Application. – This Act shall apply to all entities engaged in the
manufacture, importation, registration, distribution, marketing, sale, and dispensing of
pharmaceutical products in the Philippines, without prejudice to existing powers of the Food
and Drug Administration (FDA), the Department of Health (DOH), the Department of Trade
and Industry (DTI), and other relevant agencies.

SECTION 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

   (a) Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient or API refers to any substance or mixture of
       substances intended to be used in the manufacture of a medicine and which, when
       used in such manufacture, becomes an active ingredient of the medicine intended to
       furnish pharmacological activity or other direct effect in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation,
       treatment, or prevention of disease, or to affect the structure or function of the human
       body;

   (b) Advanced Manufacturing refers to the use of innovative, technology-enabled, or
       high-value pharmaceutical production methods, platforms, processes, facilities, or
       systems, including continuous manufacturing, biotechnology-based manufacturing,
       vaccine manufacturing, biologics manufacturing, sterile manufacturing, high-
       containment manufacturing, digital quality systems, process automation, and other
       technologies as may be identified by the Inter-Agency Committee on Medicine
       Security;

   (c) Certificate of Product Registration or CPR refers to the authorization issued by the
       FDA allowing the manufacture, importation, distribution, sale, offer for sale, or use of
       a specific medicine or pharmaceutical product in the Philippines, subject to applicable
       laws, rules, and regulations;

   (d) Critical Pharmaceutical Inputs refer to active pharmaceutical ingredients,
       intermediates, excipients, primary and secondary packaging materials, laboratory
       supplies, reference standards, reagents, production materials, quality testing
       materials, and other inputs necessary for the development, manufacture, testing,
       release, storage, or distribution of medicines, as may be identified by the DOH, FDA
       or the Inter-agency Committee on Medicine Security;

   (e) Essential Medicines refer to medicines identified by the DOH and FDA as significantly
       critical and absolutely necessary to addressing priority public health needs, including
       those which may be identified under the National Pharmaceutical Manufacturing and
       Resilience Strategy;

   (f) Equivalent Locally Manufactured Medicine refers to a locally manufactured
       medicine that is duly registered with the Food and Drug Administration and is
       comparable to an imported or other reference medicine in terms of active ingredient,
       strength, dosage form, route of administration, therapeutic use, quality, safety,
       efficacy, regulatory status, required delivery specifications, and, where applicable,
       therapeutic equivalence or interchangeability as determined under applicable laws,
       rules, and regulations;

   (g) Foreign Manufacturing Site refers to any facility located outside Philippine territory
       that is engaged in the manufacture, processing, formulation, filling, finishing,
       packaging, testing, release, or other production-related activity involving a medicine or
       pharmaceutical product intended for importation, distribution, sale, or use in the
       Philippines;

   (h) Good Manufacturing Practices or GMP refer to the standards, systems, and
       procedures for pharmaceutical manufacturing, quality assurance, quality control,
       sanitation, personnel qualification, facility management, documentation, validation,
       storage, and distribution, as adopted, recognized, or required to ensure that medicines
       are consistently produced and controlled according to applicable quality standards;

(m) Local Pharmaceutical Manufacturing refers to the manufacture, formulation,

(n) Locally Manufactured Medicine refers to a medicine or pharmaceutical product

    be determined under the implementing rules and regulations, is manufactured in the
    Philippines by a local pharmaceutical manufacturer in an FDA-licensed facility
    compliant with GMP;

(o) Medicine Security refers to the ability of the State to ensure the sustained availability,
    accessibility, affordability, quality, safety, efficacy, and rational use of medicines
    through resilient, transparent, competitive, and well-regulated pharmaceutical supply

    effective safeguards against supply disruptions, public health emergencies, and the

    medicines;

(p) Medicines or Pharmaceutical Products refer to drugs, medicines, biologicals,
    vaccines, herbal medicines, and other pharmaceutical products intended for human
    use, whether branded or generic, and whether locally manufactured or imported, which
    are subject to regulation by the FDA;

(q) Parallel Importation refers to the importation of patented or off-patent medicines
    without the authorization of the patent holder, subject to applicable quality, safety,
    efficacy, registration, customs, legal and regulatory requirements;

       projects and related activities that qualify under the Strategic Investment Priority Plan

is hereby established an Inter-Agency Committee on Medicine Security, which shall be the
primary coordinating body for the formulation, implementation, monitoring, and periodic review
of policies, programs, and measures under this Act.
        The Committee shall coordinate national government efforts relating to medicine
security, domestic pharmaceutical manufacturing capacity, supply resilience, quality
assurance, procurement alignment, investment facilitation, research and innovation, and
regulatory coordination. It shall ensure that the implementation of this Act is guided by a

SECTION 10. National Pharmaceutical Manufacturing and Resilience Strategy. – Within
twelve (12) months from the effectivity of this Act, the Inter-agency Committee on Medicine
Security shall formulate and approve a ten-year National Pharmaceutical Manufacturing and
Resilience Strategy.
        In the formulation of the Strategy, the Committee shall ensure coordination among its
member agencies and alignment with national public health and economic development
objectives.
        The Strategy shall:
    (a) Identify essential and priority medicines suitable for domestic manufacture;
    (b) Identify critical pharmaceutical inputs, including active pharmaceutical ingredients,
        requiring strategic supply security;
    (c) Establish phased development targets for formulation capacity, input security, and
        advanced manufacturing;
    (d) Prescribe measurable performance indicators and annual reporting requirements;
    (e) Integrate pharmaceutical supply resilience into public health and procurement
        planning; and
    (f) Be subject to review and updating every three (3) years.
        The Committee shall submit the Strategy for the approval of the President and all
subsequent updates shall be submitted to the President and to Congress.

SECTION 11. Phased Development of Domestic Pharmaceutical Manufacturing
Capacity. – Consistent with the National Pharmaceutical Manufacturing and Resilience
Strategy, the State shall pursue a phased approach to strengthen domestic pharmaceutical
manufacturing capacity, support medicine security, and reduce vulnerability to external supply
disruptions.
        The phased development framework shall include:

   (a) Essential Medicines Formulation Capacity – The State shall prioritize the domestic
       manufacture of finished dosage forms of essential medicines, particularly those
       identified by the Inter-agency Committee on Medicine Security as having high public
       health impact, significant procurement volume, or strategic importance during public
       health emergencies. Concerned agencies shall, within their respective mandates,
       support regulatory facilitation, technology transfer, workforce development, shared
       quality testing facilities, and other measures necessary to strengthen domestic
       formulation capacity.

   (b) Strategic Security for Critical Pharmaceutical Inputs – The State shall identify and
       support measures to improve the availability and supply security of critical
       pharmaceutical inputs, including active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs),
       intermediates, excipients, and other raw materials necessary for the production of
       essential medicines. Such measures may include incentives, public-private

        arrangements, regional cooperation, inventory rotation mechanisms, framework
        supply contracts, and other lawful supply assurance systems.

    (c) Research, Innovation, and Advanced Manufacturing – The State shall support
        pharmaceutical research, innovation, and advanced manufacturing, including
        vaccines, biologics, and other strategic pharmaceutical technologies. The Department
        of Science and Technology (DOST), in coordination with the DOH, FDA, DTI, BOI, and

SECTION 12. Local Pharmaceutical Manufacturing as a Strategic Investment. – Local
pharmaceutical manufacturing activities, including but not limited to formulation, processing,
filling, finishing, packaging, testing, active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) production, vaccine
manufacturing, herbal medicines, biologics, and other pharmaceutical or health-related
activities shall be recognized as strategic investment activities that support medicine security
and shall be covered by the Strategic Investment Priority Plan.
           Projects covering the foregoing activities shall be eligible for tax incentives, as well as
other fiscal and non-fiscal incentives under Republic Act No. 11534, Republic Act No. 12066,
and other applicable laws, rules and regulations, subject to the applicable qualifications,
performance-based conditions, and time-bound requirements administered by the appropriate
investment promotion agencies and the Fiscal Incentives Review Board.

SECTION 13. Green Lanes for Compliant Local Manufacturers. – In accordance with
Executive Order No. 18 (s. 2023), and consistent with the applicable Strategic Investment
Priority Plan and Tatak Pinoy initiatives, the FDA, the Bureau of Customs (BOC), and other
relevant agencies shall establish risk-based priority processing or green lanes for FDA-
licensed, GMP-compliant local pharmaceutical manufacturers, subject to objective eligibility
criteria to be prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations.
     Green lane privileges may include:

    (a) priority scheduling of GMP inspections;
    (b) expedited processing of initial and renewal applications for licenses and permits,
         including the License to Operate (LTO);
    (c) expedited processing of applications relating to essential medicines, including
         Certificate of Product Registration (CPR) and renewal, variations, and post-approval
         changes;
    (d) facilitated importation and customs clearance of manufacturing inputs, including APIs,
         excipients, packaging materials, and laboratory supplies, subject to risk-based
         controls; and
    (e) priority coordination for regulatory clearances required for medicine security
         investments.
         Eligibility for green lane privileges shall be provided to FDA-licensed local
pharmaceutical manufacturers with actual, verifiable pharmaceutical manufacturing
operations in the Philippines. Mere corporate registration, trading operations, distribution
activities, warehousing, relabeling, repacking, rebranding, or other activities that do not
constitute local pharmaceutical manufacturing under this Act shall not qualify an entity for
green lane privileges. The FDA may require documentary verification, facility inspection,
compliance history review, or such other risk-based validation measures as may be necessary
to determine eligibility for green lane privileges.

       Green lane privileges may be suspended or revoked for cause, including serious or

SECTION 14. Government Procurement Preference and Strategic Procurement
Measures for Locally Manufactured Medicines. – Consistent with Republic Act No. 12009,
otherwise known as the New Government Procurement Act, and other applicable
procurement, competition, and trade laws, government procuring entities shall, in the
procurement of medicines for public health programs, government hospitals, local government
health facilities, and other public healthcare institutions, give preference to locally
manufactured medicines where equivalent locally manufactured medicines are available,
subject to the following conditions:

   (a) The locally manufactured medicine is duly registered with the Food and Drug
       Administration and manufactured in an FDA-licensed facility compliant with Good
       Manufacturing Practices;

   (b) The locally manufactured medicine meets the required quality, safety, efficacy,
       therapeutic, technical, and delivery specifications of the procuring entity;

   (c) The locally manufactured medicine is available in sufficient commercial quantities and
       within the required delivery period; and

   (d) The procurement remains consistent with value-for-money principles, applicable
       procurement rules, competition laws, and the Philippines’ obligations under
       international agreements.

        For purposes of this Section, procurement evaluation shall not be based solely on the
lowest price, but may consider quality, clinical effectiveness, supply reliability, compliance
history, manufacturing track record, post-market performance, delivery reliability, and other
relevant criteria consistent with law.
        To support medicine security, domestic manufacturing capacity, and supply stability,
the DOH, PhilHealth, local government units, government hospitals, and other procuring
entities may adopt strategic procurement and market stabilization measures for essential
medicines and other priority medicines identified under the National Pharmaceutical
Manufacturing and Resilience Strategy, including:

   (a) Consolidated or pooled procurement to generate predictable demand and improve
       supply planning;

   (b) Multi-year or framework supply agreements, subject to appropriations law and
       applicable procurement rules;

   (c) Volume commitments or indicative demand forecasts for priority medicines where
       domestic manufacturing capacity is available;

   (d) Transparent price benchmarking and reference pricing mechanisms that consider
       quality, clinical effectiveness, supply reliability, and total value to the health system;

   (e) Direct delivery, regional delivery, cold chain, inventory management, and logistics
       requirements that may be considered in bid evaluation where relevant to medicine
       availability, shelf-life protection, and timely delivery to public health facilities;


                                                                                               10
   (f) Risk-based post-delivery quality verification, including random sampling, independent
       laboratory testing, batch verification, market audits, and other quality assurance
       measures for medicines procured for public health programs, government hospitals,
       local government health facilities, and other public healthcare institutions; and

   (g) Other lawful procurement arrangements that support stable supply, competitive
       pricing, and the development of domestic pharmaceutical manufacturing capacity.

        The DOH, in coordination with the Government Procurement Policy Board (GPPB),
PhilHealth, DTI, BOI, FDA, and other relevant agencies, shall issue guidelines to
operationalize this Section, including the standards for determining equivalence, availability,
quality, supply reliability, and value-for-money in the procurement of medicines.
        Nothing in this Section shall prohibit the procurement of imported medicines where no
locally manufactured equivalent is available, where the locally manufactured product fails to
meet the required quality, safety, efficacy, therapeutic, technical, delivery, or quantity
requirements, or where the application of domestic preference would be inconsistent with
applicable law or international obligations.

SECTION 15. Quality Assurance and GMP Linkage for Imported Medicines. – As a
condition for the approval or renewal of a CPR for an imported pharmaceutical product, the
applicant shall submit evidence, in form and substance acceptable to the FDA, that the foreign
manufacturing site complies with internationally recognized GMP standards, including
documentation capable of verification.
        The FDA shall implement a risk-based and proportionate assessment framework for
imported products, taking into account the dosage form, public health risk, history of quality
defects or recalls, compliance record of the applicant, and the reliability of the regulatory
authority having oversight over the manufacturing site. The FDA may require such additional
documentary submissions and verification measures as may be warranted by risk, in
accordance with internationally accepted practices.
        The FDA shall, through the implementing rules and regulations, identify categories of
pharmaceutical products that warrant heightened quality assurance review based on public
health risk, vulnerability to counterfeiting, history of quality defects, or other relevant factors,
and may prescribe additional documentary and verification requirements for such categories
consistent with internationally accepted practices.
        Nothing in this Section shall prohibit or restrict lawful parallel importation under
Republic Act No. 9502, provided that products introduced through parallel importation shall
remain subject to equivalent quality, safety, and efficacy requirements under this Act and other
applicable laws.

SECTION 16. Statutory FDA-BOC Pre-Clearance and Border Control. – The FDA and
BOC shall institutionalize an integrated pre-clearance and risk-profiling system for
pharmaceutical imports, consistent with their respective mandates under existing laws. The
system shall verify the validity of the importer’s License to Operate (LTO), the product’s CPR,
and, where applicable, the GMP status of the manufacturing site prior to customs release, and
shall authorize the holding, examination, and referral of shipments that fail verification or are
flagged as high-risk, in accordance with law.
        This Section shall be implemented in a manner that facilitates legitimate trade while
preventing the entry of counterfeit, substandard, falsified, or unregistered pharmaceutical
products.

SECTION 17. Traceability and Post-Market Surveillance. – The FDA shall develop and
implement a risk-based traceability or serialization system for designated essential and priority
medicines, prioritizing products with high public health impact or high risk of counterfeiting.



                                                                                                 11
Post-market surveillance activities shall be institutionalized, including random sampling,
adverse event monitoring, and market audits.
       The FDA may prioritize post-market surveillance for medicines and pharmaceutical
products that are high-risk, widely procured or dispensed, subject to repeated quality
complaints, sourced from manufacturers or importers with adverse compliance history, or
otherwise identified under the National Pharmaceutical Manufacturing and Resilience
Strategy.

SECTION 18. Regulation of Online and Digital Sale of Medicines. – The online sale or
distribution of medicines without proper FDA authorization is hereby prohibited. The FDA, in
coordination with the DTI and the Department of Information and Communications Technology
(DICT), shall establish a takedown mechanism requiring digital platforms and online
marketplaces to remove or disable access to listings of unregistered, counterfeit, or non-
compliant pharmaceutical products upon notice. Digital platforms shall be required to
cooperate in enforcement actions, without being deemed sellers of the regulated products.
        The FDA shall maintain, or cause to be maintained, an updated and electronically
accessible registry or verification system of authorized medicines and pharmaceutical
products, including relevant CPR information, for use by consumers, procuring entities,
healthcare institutions, digital platforms, online marketplaces, and other stakeholders. The
registry or verification system may be made available through the FDA website, electronic
verification tool, or other digital mechanism, subject to data privacy, cybersecurity,
confidentiality, and operational safeguards.
        Digital platforms and online marketplaces that allow the listing, sale, offer for sale,
advertising, facilitation, or distribution of medicines shall adopt reasonable verification, notice-
and-takedown, and cooperation mechanisms, consistent with guidelines to be issued by the
FDA, DTI, and DICT. Such mechanisms may include seller verification, product authorization
checks, removal or disabling of non-compliant listings upon notice, preservation of relevant
transaction information, and cooperation with lawful enforcement actions.
        Digital platforms and online marketplaces shall not be deemed manufacturers,
importers, distributors, sellers, or dispensers of medicines solely by reason of hosting,
indexing, advertising, facilitating, or processing listings or transactions involving regulated
products: Provided, that this shall not exempt any platform, marketplace, seller, or other
person from liability under applicable laws where it has actual knowledge of unlawful activity
and fails to act in accordance with lawful notice, or where it directly engages in the
unauthorized sale, distribution, importation, dispensing, or promotion of medicines.

SECTION 19. Transparency and Consumer Right to Choose. – All pharmaceutical
products shall clearly indicate the country of manufacture and the name of the importer or
manufacturer on labels, packaging, and online listings. The DTI and FDA may issue joint
regulations to promote fair substitution practices and ensure that consumers are informed
whether a product is locally manufactured or imported.
        Nothing in this Section shall mandate price controls or violate competition laws.

SECTION 20. Competition and Market Oversight. – The Philippine Competition
Commission (PCC) is encouraged to monitor market concentration, pricing behavior, and
potential abuse of dominance in the pharmaceutical retail and distribution sector, consistent
with existing competition laws.

SECTION 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred eighty (180)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the constituted Inter-Agency Committee on Medicine
Security, with the assistance of other implementing agencies under this Act, shall promulgate
the necessary implementing rules and regulations (IRR) to carry out the provisions of this Act.
        The IRR shall ensure effective, coordinated, and consistent implementation of this Act,
and shall be issued in accordance with existing laws, rules and regulations, and applicable
international commitments.

SECTION 22. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is held unconstitutional
or invalid, the remaining provisions shall not be affected and shall continue in full force and
effect.

SECTION 23. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, administrative rules, or
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 24. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
