                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill seeks to establish a Regional Rehabilitation Center for Youth (RRCY) in
every region of the country, under the direction of the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD), with an Intensive Juvenile Intervention and Support Center in
each RRCY. It amends Republic Act (RA) No. 9344, otherwise known as the “Juvenile
Justice and Welfare Act of 2006”, as amended by RA No. 10630, to strengthen the
rehabilitation and supervision of children exempt from criminal responsibility who
commit serious crimes.

       Despite the enactment of RA No. 9344, as amended, the increasing number of
cases involving children in conflict with the law provides an opportunity to strengthen
the existing rehabilitation system. The current framework places significant
responsibility on local government units (LGUs) for the establishment and operation of
Bahay Pag-Asa facilities which results in uneven capacity to provide secure and
specialized care.

        The bill transfers the primary responsibility for specialized residential
rehabilitation to the DSWD, and strengthens the protection and assistance afforded to
victims and their families. It likewise preserves the right of victims to recover
restitution, indemnity, damages, and other civil remedies notwithstanding the child’s
exemption from criminal liability.

       The protection of children and the protection of society are not competing
obligations. A just juvenile justice system gives children a genuine opportunity to
change while giving victims, families, and communities the assurance that serious
offenses will be met with meaningful intervention and appropriate action. This measure
gives the State the capacity to fulfill both responsibilities, because rehabilitation must be
a system capable of protecting lives, restoring families, and giving children the chance to
return to society as better and more responsible individuals.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

        SEC. 10. Transitory Provision – Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, in coordination with the Juvenile Justice and Welfare
Council (JJWC), the Regional Juvenile Justice and Welfare Committees (RJJWCs), the
Department of Budget and Management (DBM), the Department of the Interior and
Local Government (DILG), and other concerned agencies, shall formulate and
implement a transition plan for the establishment and full operation of the Regional
Rehabilitation Centers for Youth (RRCYs) and the Intensive Juvenile Intervention and
Support Centers (IJISCs) therein, in accordance with the implementing rules and
regulations of this Act.

       The transition plan shall include, among others, the establishment or designation
of RRCYs, the transfer of children covered under Sections 20-A and 20-B of RA No.
9344, otherwise known as the “Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006”, as amended, the
deployment of personnel, and the transition timeline for IJISC operations, as may be
prescribed in the implementing rules and regulations.

        Pending the establishment and full operation of the RRCYs, existing IISCs
operating within Bahay Pag-asa facilities shall continue to operate under the
supervision of the DSWD until the transfer of the children concerned to the appropriate
RRCYs is completed: Provided, That such transfer shall be subject to the prior authority
of the court, where required under this Act, and shall not interrupt the implementation
of the child's intervention and rehabilitation program.

        SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DSWD and JJWC shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for its effective implementation.

       SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or amended accordingly.

       SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
