                            EXPLANATORY NOTE
       In the most critical moments of human crisis, social workers serve as
the front line of the State’s social protection machinery. When a child is
rescued from abuse in the dead of night, a social worker is there to receive
them. In the wake of natural disasters, when communities are leveled, social
workers are among the first to respond on the ground and the last to leave.
From municipal halls sheltering victims of domestic violence to courtrooms
guiding children in conflict with the law, it is the social worker who listens,
evaluates, and acts when families have lost everything. They absorb the
profound weight of our society’s most vulnerable moments, carrying out this
critical mandate quietly and without reserve.
      The State has not carried its share of that weight in return. A Social
Welfare Officer I, a licensed professional, still enters government service at
Salary Grade 11, or PhP 31,705 a month in 2026, four grades below a Nurse
I, whose Salary Grade 15 floor the law guarantees.1 The Magna Carta for
Public Social Workers promised hazard pay nearly two decades ago, yet it
limited that pay to a list of postings, provided no funds to pay it, and left out
the many social workers hired under contracts of service and job orders,
who face the same responsibilities for less.2 When we ask social workers to
stand between the vulnerable and harm. The least the State can do is stand
behind them.
          In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

    AN ACT STRENGTHENING THE COMPENSATION, WELFARE, AND
 PROTECTION OF SOCIAL WORKERS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9433, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE MAGNA CARTA
 FOR PUBLIC SOCIAL WORKERS, AND ARTICLE 152 OF THE REVISED
         PENAL CODE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR
       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:
       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Social
Workers' Welfare and Protection Act."
       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall ensure that
social workers, however engaged, receive pay commensurate with their
qualifications and risks, and shall protect them from violence and
harassment in their duties.
     SECTION 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to registered social
workers under Republic Act No. 4373, as amended:
     (a) employed in government, including local government units (LGUs);
     (b) engaged by the government under a contract of service or job order
     (COS/JO), as provided in Section 6; and
     (c) employed in the private sector, as provided in Sections 7 to 10.
     Social workers in the Judiciary shall be covered as the Supreme Court
     may adopt.
      SECTION 4. Upgraded Salary Grades. – Section 12 of Republic Act
No. 9433 is hereby amended by adding the following paragraph:
      "The salary grades (SG) of Social Welfare Officer positions and
      equivalent social work positions in government shall be: Social
      Welfare Officer I, SG 15; II, SG 17; III, SG 19; IV, SG 23; and V,
      SG 25, at prevailing salary schedule rates. Incumbents shall be
      adjusted to the new SG at their present step. The difference shall
      be paid in two (2) equal annual tranches starting the fiscal year
      after effectivity. In LGUs, the rates shall apply at the percentage
      for the LGU's income class under Section 10 of Republic Act No.
      6758, as amended."

      SECTION 5. Hazard Allowance. – Section 15(a) of Republic Act
No. 9433 is hereby amended to read:
            "(a) Hazard Allowance. – Public social workers posted in
      the areas heretofore enumerated herein, or whose actual
      duties, wherever posted, involve direct case management of
      victims and perpetrators of abuse, violence, or exploitation,
      children in conflict with the law, court-related or forensic work,
      psychiatric or infectious disease settings, armed conflict areas,
      or disaster and emergency response, as certified by the head of
      office under DSWD-DBM rules, shall receive a hazard
      allowance of not less than twenty percent (20%) of their
      monthly basic salary."​
       SECTION 6. Contract-of-Service and Job-Order Social Workers. –
COS/JO social workers shall receive a monthly rate, pro-rated if less than
full-time, not lower than the salary of Social Welfare Officer I, Step 1, and a
hazard premium equal to the allowance under Section 5 when performing
the duties therein, without acquiring civil service status. Agencies that have
engaged COS/JO social workers for recurring functions for three (3) or more
consecutive years shall, within two (2) years, submit a staffing plan for such
functions to the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), or in LGUs
to the sanggunian, subject to qualification standards and appropriations.​
​
​     SECTION 7. Privately Employed Social Workers. – Private
employers shall provide each registered social worker with personal accident
insurance of not less than PhP 500,000.00 covering accidental death and
permanent disability, including fieldwork. Where a private agency delivers
services under government contract or funding, the hazard pay under
Section 5 for its social workers doing such duties shall be included therein.​
​
​     SECTION 8. Line-of-Duty Benefit. – For death or disability from
violence or accident in the actual performance of social work functions, the
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) shall pay PhP
500,000.00 to the heirs of the covered social worker in the order of the Civil
Code, PhP 250,000.00 for permanent total disability, and a proportionate
amount for permanent partial disability.​
​
​     SECTION 9. Essential Frontline Service Providers. – Registered
social workers are essential frontline service providers. In declared
calamities and emergencies, they shall have priority in protective equipment,
health care, and access to affected areas, subject to safety rules, and shall
receive any frontline special risk allowance whose conditions they meet.​
​
​      SECTION 10. Protection of Social Workers. – The last paragraph of
Article 152 of Act No. 3815, otherwise known as the "Revised Penal Code,"
as amended, is hereby further amended to read:
             "In applying the provisions of Articles 148 and 151 of
       this Code, teachers, professors, and persons charged with the

       supervision of public or duly recognized private schools,
       colleges and universities, lawyers, and registered social
       workers, in the actual performance of their professional duties
       or on the occasion of such performance, shall be deemed
       persons in authority."
       Government shall provide legal assistance to public and COS/JO
social workers sued for the lawful performance of their duties, reimbursable
upon final finding of bad faith.​
​
​     SECTION 11. Career Progression and Professional Development. –
Within one (1) year, the Social Work Management and Consultative Council
shall issue the career ladder required under Section 8 of Republic Act No.
9433. ​
​
​     SECTION 12. Social Workers Support Grant. – A DSWD Social
Workers Support Grant shall cover the incremental costs of Sections 4 and 5
at 100% for 4th–6th class LGUs and 50% for 2nd–3rd class LGUs, while 1st
class LGUs must self-fund. Beneficiary LGUs cannot cut existing social
worker compensation, and grant funds are exempt from RA 7160 personal
services caps but remain subject to standard audit rules.​
​
​     SECTION 13. Non-Diminution and Non-Duplication. – No existing
benefit shall be diminished.​
​
​     SECTION 14. Appropriations. – This shall be charged against the
Miscellaneous Personnel Benefits Fund of the General Appropriations Act
(GAA) for the year of implementation.​
​
​    SECTION 15. Registry – DSWD shall keep a registry of government
and COS/JO social workers, consistent with the Data Privacy Act of 2012.​
​
​     SECTION 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within
ninety (90) days, DSWD, DBM, the Civil Service Commission, and DOLE,
after consulting DILG, the PRC, and stakeholders, shall issue the
implementing rules.​
​
​     SECTION 17. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is
declared invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in force.​
​
​     SECTION 18. Repealing Clause. – Sections 12 and 15(a) of Republic
Act No. 9433 and Article 152 of the Revised Penal Code are amended as
provided herein; other inconsistent laws and issuances are modified
accordingly.​
​
​      SECTION 19.Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
​      Approved.
