                       AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A COMPREHENSIVE MENTAL HEALTH
AND PSYCHOSOCIAL SUPPORT SYSTEM FOR FILIPINO ATHLETES
AND PARA-ATHLETES, PROMOTING SAFE AND SUPPORTIVE
SPORTS ENVIRONMENTS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State recognizes the importance of promoting the mental health and
well-being of Filipino athletes and para-athletes as an integral component of
sports development. Athletes at all levels, from grassroots and school-based
participants to elite national representatives, face unique psychological pressures
arising from training demands, performance expectations, injuries, and even
career uncertainties. These challenges, if left unaddressed, may adversely affect
not only the athletic performance but also the long-term personal development of
the athletes.

       Pursuant to Section 19, Article XIV of the 1987 Philippine Constitution,
the State shall promote physical education and encourage sports programs, league
competitions, and amateur sports, including training for international
competitions. Complementarily, Section 15, Article II provides that the State
shall protect and promote the right to health of the people, including mental
health, while Section 13, Article II recognizes the vital role of the youth in nation-
building.
        While existing laws such as Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as
  the “Mental Health Act,” establish a general framework for mental health
  services, and Republic Act No. 12080, otherwise known as the “Basic Education
  Mental Health and Well-Being Promotion Act,” institutionalizes mental health
  programs in basic education, there remains a gap in the delivery of specialized
  and accessible mental health services tailored to the unique needs of athletes.
  Moreover, Republic Act No. 10699, otherwise known as the “National Athletes
  and Coaches Benefits and Incentives Act,” provides health-related benefits for
  national athletes but does not sufficiently address the systematic provision of
  mental health and psychosocial support within sports systems.

         At present, mental health services in sports remain fragmented, unevenly
  accessible, and often reactive rather than preventive. Grassroots and school-based
  athletes, in particular, have limited access to structured mental health support,
  despite being at a critical stage of development.

        This proposed measure seeks to address these gaps by institutionalizing a
  comprehensive mental health and psychosocial support system for athletes and
  para-athletes across all levels of participation. It provides for increased access to
  mental health services, integration of mental health mechanisms in sports
  programs, capacity-building for stakeholders, and strengthened inter-agency
  coordination, while ensuring confidentiality and data privacy.

        By prioritizing mental health as a core component of athlete development,
  the State affirms its commitment to fostering resilience, sustaining performance,
  and promoting the overall well-being of Filipino athletes and para-athletes.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
  sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Athlete Mental
Health and Welfare Act.”

       Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to:
       a. Promote the holistic well-being of Filipino athletes and para-athletes,
          recognizing mental health as integral to sports performance, athlete
          development, and long-term welfare;
       b. Recognize sports participation and athlete welfare as important
          components of youth development, nation-building, and social
          development;
       c. Ensure accessible, timely, appropriate, and athlete-centered mental
          health and psychosocial support across all levels of sports participation;
                                                                                  3
      d. Protect athletes and para-athletes from mental health risks arising from
         injury, overtraining, burnout, abuse, performance pressure, public
         scrutiny, and career transitions;
      e. Foster safe, respectful, inclusive, and supportive sporting environments
         that uphold the dignity, welfare, resilience, and holistic development of
         athletes and para-athletes; and,
      f. Strengthen institutional support systems within schools, communities,
         local government units, and national sports programs.

      Section 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following
terms are hereby defined:
      a. Athletes refer to individuals engaged in organized sports at the
          grassroots, school-based, amateur, or professional level, including those
          representing the Philippines in international competitions. Professional
          athletes may be covered by this Act insofar as applicable and consistent
          with existing league rules, contracts, and other relevant laws;
      b. Para-athletes refer to persons with disabilities, as defined under
          Republic Act No. 7277, otherwise known as the "Magna Carta for
          Disabled Persons," as amended, who are Filipino citizens, engaged in
          organized sports at the grassroots, school-based, amateur, or
          professional level, including those representing the Philippines in
          international competitions;
      c. Burnout refers to physical, emotional, and mental exhaustion caused by
          prolonged stress, excessive training demands, or sustained performance
          pressure;
      d. Career Transition Support refers to interventions and support
          mechanisms assisting athletes and para-athletes in adjusting to post-
          competition, post-injury, retirement, academic, occupational, or other
          major life transitions;
      e. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services (MHPSS) refer to
          services and interventions that protect or promote mental well-being,
          including mental health promotion, counseling, psychological services,
          psychosocial support, crisis intervention, referral systems, stress
          management, and post-competition or post-injury support;
      f. Sports Organizations refer to schools, athletic associations, local
          government units (LGUs), National Sports Associations (NSAs),

                                                                                 4
         national training bodies, sports clubs, and other entities involved in
         athlete training, competition, and development;
      g. Trauma-informed care refers to an approach that recognizes the impact
         of trauma, integrates such understanding into policies and practices, and
         promotes safe, supportive, and recovery-oriented sporting
         environments.

       Section 4. Establishment of the Athlete Mental Health System. – A
comprehensive mental health and psychosocial support system for athletes and
para-athletes is hereby established, covering all levels of sports participation,
including grassroots, school-based, and national training programs.
       The system shall adopt a preventive, developmental, trauma-informed, and
athlete-centered approach that promotes mental wellness, psychological
resilience, safe sporting environments, and holistic athlete development.
       It shall include programs, services, and interventions addressing mental
health concerns commonly experienced by athletes and para-athletes arising from
injury, overtraining, burnout, abuse, performance pressure, public scrutiny,
retirement, career transition, and other high-stress sporting environments.

       Section 5. Scope of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services
(MHPSS). – Athletes and para-athletes shall be provided access, whether directly
or through referral systems, to appropriate mental health and psychosocial support
services, including but not limited to:
          a) Mental health assessments and screening;
          b) Counseling and psychological services;
          c) Stress management and resilience-building programs;
          d) Crisis intervention and psychosocial support;
          e) Voluntary post-competition, post-injury, and career-transition
              support mechanisms;
          f) Mental health literacy and awareness activities;
          g) Peer support and referral mechanisms; and,
          h) Facilitation of access to specialized clinical interventions, when
              necessary and appropriate.
       Services under this Act shall, where applicable, be coordinated with
existing health benefits and support systems under Republic Act No. 10699,
otherwise known as the “National Athletes and Coaches Benefits and Incentives

                                                                                5
Act,” Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the “Mental Health Act”, and
other relevant laws.

      Section 6. Integration in Sports Programs. – All sports organizations,
schools, athletic associations, national sports associations, training institutions,
and relevant sports governing bodies, where practicable, shall integrate mental
health and psychosocial support mechanisms into their training and athlete
development programs.
      Such mechanisms shall include:
          a) Mental wellness promotion activities;
          b) Mental health awareness and literacy programs;
          c) Referral pathways to qualified mental health professionals;
          d) Preventive measures against burnout, overtraining, abuse, bullying,
             harassment, and harmful training practices; and,
          e) Policies promoting safe, respectful, inclusive, and athlete-centered
             sporting environments.

       Section 7. Mental Health Education, Safe Sports Environment, and
Protection Programs. – Sports organizations shall establish and implement
mental health education and safe sports environment programs for coaches,
trainers, sports administrators, educators, medical personnel, athlete support
personnel, and, where applicable, parents or guardians of minor athletes.
       Such programs shall include:
          a) Mental health literacy and awareness;
          b) Trauma-informed coaching and athlete engagement practices;
          c) Psychological first response and safe communication practices;
          d) Recognition of signs of mental distress and other mental health
             concerns affecting athletes and para-athletes;
          e) Prevention of physical, emotional, verbal, psychological, sexual, and
             other forms of abuse, harassment, bullying, exploitation, hazing, and
             harmful training practices; and,
          f) Promotion of supportive, non-discriminatory, and athlete-centered
             sporting environments.
       Sports organizations shall likewise establish accessible and confidential
reporting and referral mechanisms for athletes and para-athletes experiencing
mental health concerns, abuse, harassment, exploitation, retaliation, or other
harmful conduct.
                                                                                  6
       Sports organizations and relevant governing bodies are likewise
encouraged, where practicable, to conduct periodic psychosocial environment
assessments, athlete welfare surveys, or similar mechanisms to identify systemic
concerns affecting athlete well-being, safety, team culture, and mental health,
subject to applicable data privacy and confidentiality laws.
       The Department of Health, in coordination with the Philippine Sports
Commission and other relevant agencies, shall conduct information, education,
and awareness campaigns to reduce stigma, promote mental health awareness, and
encourage help-seeking behavior among athletes, coaches, sports personnel, and
other stakeholders.

       Section 8. Capacity-Building and Human Resource Development. – The
State shall promote the continuing professional development and specialized
training of mental health professionals, coaches, trainers, educators, sports
medicine practitioners, and athlete support personnel in sports psychology,
psychosocial support, trauma-informed care, and athlete mental wellness.
       The Philippine Sports Commission may, in coordination with the
Department of Health, academic institutions, sports organizations, and relevant
stakeholders, establish partnerships and training programs to expand the pool of
qualified personnel capable of providing athlete-centered mental health and
psychosocial support services.
       The PSC, in coordination with the DOH, shall regularize and fund capacity-
building programs, seminars, and training modules on sports psychology, mental
health first aid, and trauma-informed coaching for coaches, trainers, sports
administrators, and physical education teachers.
          a) Provincial and Grassroots Specialization Pipeline. – To address the
             acute shortage of specialized mental health professionals outside
             Metro Manila, the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), in
             collaboration with the PSC, the DOH, and State Universities and
             Colleges (SUCs), shall establish a scholarship and specialization
             support program.
                   Beneficiaries of this program shall be subject to a return-
             service agreement requiring them to serve as mental health
             professionals with training or specialization in sports psychology for
             local government units (LGUs), regional public schools, or
             grassroots sports hubs for a period equivalent to the duration of their
             scholarship.

         b) Tele-Behavioral Sports Network. – The PSC, in coordination with
            the Department of Information and Communications Technology
            (DICT), shall develop and maintain a secure, nationwide Tele-
            Behavioral Sports Network, subject to applicable data privacy,
            cybersecurity, and telemedicine regulations. This platform shall
            provide school-based, grassroots, and provincial athletes in remote
            or underserved areas with direct, confidential, and remote access to
            qualified mental health professionals with training or specialization
            in sports psychology.
         c) Equity in Resource Deployment. – Priority in resource deployment
            under this Act shall be given to underserved and geographically
            isolated areas.

      Section 9. Inter-Agency Coordination. – The Philippine Sports
Commission shall serve as the lead coordinating agency for the implementation
of this Act, in coordination with the Department of Health, Department of
Education, Commission on Higher Education, Philippine Olympic Committee,
Philippine Paralympic Committee, local government units, National Sports
Associations, and other relevant stakeholders.
      The Department of Health shall provide technical guidance on mental
health standards, service delivery, and integration with existing national mental
health programs.
      The Department of Education and the Commission on Higher Education
shall promote the integration of mental health and psychosocial support
mechanisms within school-based sports and student-athlete development
programs.

       Section 10. Confidentiality and Data Privacy. – All mental health records,
assessments, consultations, and related information of athletes and para-athletes
shall be treated with strict confidentiality. Mental health consultations conducted
pursuant to this Act shall, whenever practicable, be conducted in safe and private
environments.
       The collection, processing, storage, and sharing of such data shall comply
with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of
2012.”

       No information shall be disclosed without the informed consent of the
athlete or para-athlete, except in cases authorized by law or when necessary to
prevent serious and imminent harm.

      Section 11. Non-Discrimination and Right to Safe Sporting
Environments. – No athlete or para-athlete shall be subjected to unjust
discrimination solely on the basis of utilizing mental health and psychosocial
support services or having a diagnosed mental health condition, without prejudice
to legitimate competitive, medical, safety, or performance standards. Mental
health records shall not be used to unjustly deny athletic opportunities.
          a) Athlete Grievance Mechanism. – The PSC, in consultation with
             relevant stakeholders and mental health professionals, shall establish
             an independent, secure, and confidential Athlete Grievance Desk
             within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act. Any athlete
             who alleges discrimination under this Section may file a complaint,
             which the PSC shall act upon and resolve within a reasonable period,
             pursuant to the implementing rules and regulations.
          b) Administrative Penalties. – Any National Sports Association (NSA),
             school, sports club, or governing sports body found to have violated
             the non-discrimination mandate or failed to provide a confidential
             reporting channel as required under this Act shall, after due notice
             and hearing, face the following administrative penalties:
               i) First Offense: Written warning and a mandatory audit of
                    internal selection and welfare policies;
              ii) Second Offense: Suspension of financial assistance, training
                    allowances, or regular subsidies provided by the PSC for a
                    period of six (6) months; and,
             iii) Third and Subsequent Offenses: Temporary suspension or
                    permanent revocation of accreditation by the PSC, without
                    prejudice to civil, criminal, or other administrative liabilities
                    under existing laws: Provided, however, That penalties
                    imposed by the PSC under this Section shall be subject to
                    existing PSC accreditation powers under Republic Act No.
                    6847, otherwise known as the “Philippine Sports Commission
                    Act,” and related issuances.

       Section 12. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting. – The Philippine
Sports Commission, in coordination with implementing agencies, shall establish
a monitoring and evaluation system to assess the implementation and
effectiveness of programs under this Act.
       The monitoring and evaluation framework shall include indicators relating
to service accessibility, utilization rates, training activities conducted, referral
mechanisms established, psychosocial environment assessments or athlete
welfare feedback mechanisms conducted, and other relevant performance
measures.
       An annual report shall be submitted to Congress, through the House
Committee on Youth and Sports Development and the Senate Committee on
Sports, detailing program implementation, utilization of services, challenges
encountered, and recommendations for improvement.

      Section 13. Complementarity with Existing Laws. – This Act shall
complement existing laws, including Republic Act No. 11036, Republic Act No.
12080, otherwise known as the “Basic Education Mental Health and Well-Being
Promotion Act,” and Republic Act No. 10699, particularly in facilitating access
to and utilization of available health services.

      Section 14. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the immediate
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s
appropriations of the Philippine Sports Commission (PSC). Thereafter, such
sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).
      Provided, That to ensure the sustainability and continuity of the Athlete
Mental Health and Psychosocial Support System, a portion of the National Sports
Development Fund (NSDF), as managed by the PSC pursuant to Republic Act
No. 6847, may be utilized and prioritized for the implementation of the programs,
services, human resource development, and rural deployment mandated under
this Act.

      Section 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Philippine Sports Commission, in
coordination with relevant agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

      Section 16. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      Section 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances,
decrees, rules, and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of
this Act are deemed amended, modified, or repealed accordingly.

       Section 18. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
