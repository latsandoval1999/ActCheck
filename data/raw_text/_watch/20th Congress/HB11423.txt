                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines has established a significant record in advancing the Women, Peace, and
Security (WPS) agenda. In 2010, the Philippines adopted its first National Action Plan on
Women, Peace and Security (NAP-WPS), making it the first country in Asia to formulate and
adopt a national action plan pursuant to United Nations Security Council Resolution (UNSCR)
No. 1325 (2000).1 The country is now implementing its fourth-generation NAP-WPS, covering
the period 2023–2033, which provides for strengthened collaboration between government and
civil society, implementation at national and local levels, attention to emerging peace and
security concerns, and a monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning framework. 2

The Philippine experience demonstrates that women are not merely among those affected by
armed conflict. They have served as negotiators, mediators, community peacebuilders,
monitors, humanitarian actors, human rights defenders, and leaders in conflict prevention and
post-conflict recovery. The country’s peace processes provide concrete examples of Women’s
substantive participation. In the negotiations with the Moro Islamic Liberation Front, women
served on the Government negotiating panel and were among the signatories to the
Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro. Professor Miriam Coronel-Ferrer, who served
as chief negotiator of the Government of the Philippines, became the first woman to lead a
government negotiating panel in the signing of a major peace accord.3

The existing WPS framework, however, remains distributed among executive issuances,
national plans, administrative mechanisms, and existing laws. Executive Order No. 865, series
of 2010, created the National Steering Committee on Women, Peace and Security (NSCWPS)
to spearhead implementation of the NAP-WPS and expressly provided for partnership with
civil society organizations, particularly women and peace groups, in implementation,
monitoring, and evaluation. 4 Subsequent policies have integrated WPS programs into
government planning and Gender and Development (GAD) mechanisms, while the current
NAP-WPS provides a longer-term policy framework through 2033.5

This measure therefore seeks to establish a durable statutory framework for the WPS agenda
while preserving and strengthening existing institutions and programs. It seeks to ensure
continuity across changes in presidential administrations, strengthen inter-agency
coordination, provide for sustained and transparent financing, institutionalize civil society
participation, and establish mechanisms for legislative oversight and public accountability.

The proposed Act is anchored upon the Constitution, existing Philippine statutes, and the
country’s international commitments.

First, the Constitution recognizes the role of women in nation-building and guarantees equality
before the law. Article II, Section 14 of the 1987 Constitution provides that the State recognizes
the role of women in nation-building and shall ensure the fundamental equality before the law
of women and men. Article XIII, Section 14 further directs the State to protect working women
by providing safe and healthful working conditions and facilities and opportunities that will
enhance their welfare and enable them to realize their full potential in the service of the nation. 6
The Constitution likewise recognizes the right of the people and their organizations to effective
and reasonable participation at all levels of social, political, and economic decision-making.7

Second, Republic Act No. 9710, otherwise known as the “Magna Carta of Women,” already
provides a statutory foundation for Women’s participation in peace and security. Section 11
requires measures to accelerate Women’s participation and equitable representation in
decision-making and policy-making processes. Section 29 specifically directs that the peace
process take into account Women’s participation in discussions and decision-making,
including membership in peace panels; Women’s participation in the planning,
implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of rehabilitation and rebuilding in conflict-
affected areas; the protection of civilians with particular attention to women and girls; and the
recognition and support of Women’s roles in conflict prevention, management, resolution, and
peacemaking.8 The proposed measure builds upon, rather than displaces, these existing
statutory mandates.

framework established by the United Nations Security Council. UNSCR No. 1325 (2000)
recognizes the differential impact of armed conflict on women and girls and calls for Women’s
full participation in conflict prevention, peace processes, peacebuilding, and post-conflict
reconstruction. It has been followed by nine additional WPS resolutions: UNSCR Nos. 1820
(2008), 1888 (2009), 1889 (2009), 1960 (2010), 2106 (2013), 2122 (2013), 2242 (2015), 2467
(2019), and 2493 (2019).9 Collectively, these resolutions address Women’s participation and
leadership, protection from conflict-related sexual violence, prevention, accountability, and
gender-responsive approaches to peace and security.

The Philippines is also a State Party to the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of
Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW), having ratified the Convention in 1981. CEDAW
requires States Parties to take appropriate legislative and other measures to eliminate
discrimination against women and to ensure Women’s effective participation in public and
political life.10 The proposed Act accordingly provides a domestic institutional framework
consistent with the country's existing obligations under international human rights law.

Fourth, the measure incorporates the Youth, Peace and Security (YPS) agenda. UNSCR No.
2250 (2015) recognizes young people as important actors in preventing and resolving conflict
and sustaining peace, and identifies participation, protection, prevention, partnerships, and
disengagement and reintegration as principal areas for action. UNSCR Nos. 2419 (2018) and
2535 (2020) subsequently strengthened the call for meaningful youth participation and
representation in conflict prevention, peace processes, peacebuilding, and decision-making.11
The Philippines has demonstrated its commitment to the YPS agenda. On August 30, 2022, the
Philippines launched its National Action Plan on Youth, Peace and Security, covering the
period 2023–2033, becoming the first country in Asia and the fourth country globally to
develop and adopt a national action plan on YPS. The proposed framework therefore
recognizes young women, in particular, as participants and leaders in peacebuilding and
conflict prevention rather than solely as beneficiaries of protection programs.

Fifth, the measure respects the constitutional and statutory framework governing the
Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM). Republic Act No. 11054,
otherwise known as the “Organic Law for the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim
Mindanao,” recognizes meaningful self-governance within the framework of the Constitution
and national sovereignty and territorial integrity. It expressly provides for Women’s
participation in the Bangsamoro Government, including representation in decision-making and
policy-determining bodies, and requires the Bangsamoro Government to uphold and protect
the fundamental rights of women. 12 The proposed Act shall therefore be implemented in a

The statutory framework is likewise consistent with the country's broader climate and disaster-
risk governance architecture. Republic Act No. 9729, otherwise known as the “Climate Change
Act of 2009,” recognizes the particular vulnerability of poor communities, women, and
children to climate-related impacts and provides for participation by national and local
governments, civil society, local communities, and other stakeholders. 13 Republic Act No.
10121, otherwise known as the “Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of
2010,” requires gender-responsive disaster-risk measures, recognizes the participation of civil
society and communities, and expressly calls for the mainstreaming of disaster risk reduction
into peace processes and conflict-resolution approaches.14 These existing mandates provide a
legal basis for integrating WPS considerations into climate, disaster-risk reduction,
humanitarian, and community-resilience policies.

The measure further recognizes the importance of safeguarding persons and organizations
engaged in lawful peacebuilding and human rights work. Such protection shall be implemented
consistently with the Constitution, due process, freedom of expression and association, and
applicable laws. The Supreme Court has recognized that red-tagging, vilification, labeling, and
guilt by association may, depending on the circumstances and evidence, constitute threats to a
person's life, liberty, or security for purposes of the writ of amparo.15 The proposed framework
would accordingly establish appropriate institutional safeguards, documentation and referral
mechanisms, and accountability procedures for threats or reprisals against lawful
peacebuilding activities, without restricting legitimate law-enforcement or national-security
functions undertaken in accordance with law.

The measure also seeks to institutionalize the National Steering Committee on Women, Peace
and Security as a statutory, inter-agency mechanism, while retaining meaningful participation
by civil society and other relevant stakeholders. This builds upon Executive Order No. 865,
which already directs the NSCWPS to work in partnership with civil society organizations in
implementing, monitoring, and evaluating the NAP-WPS.16 A statutory framework would
provide greater continuity and clarify the respective responsibilities of national government
agencies, local government units, the BARMM, civil society organizations, Women’s
organizations, youth organizations, and other relevant stakeholders.

Finally, the measure provides for sustainable and coordinated financing of Women, Peace and
Security initiatives through existing government budgeting and financing mechanisms. Given
the intersectional nature of the WPS agenda, its implementation may be supported through
existing government programs, appropriations, and financing mechanisms, including, but not

The proposed Women, Peace and Security Act thus seeks to consolidate and strengthen existing
constitutional, statutory, administrative, and international commitments into a coherent
national framework. It provides for institutional continuity, meaningful participation,
protection, localization, youth engagement, civil society partnership, climate and disaster-risk
integration, financing, monitoring, and legislative oversight.

The objective is to ensure that Women’s participation in peace and security is treated as a
continuing component of national governance and peacebuilding policy; that young women
and men are recognized as partners in preventing and resolving conflict; that peacebuilding
organizations and communities may participate safely and meaningfully in public processes;
and that WPS commitments are implemented through sustained institutions, measurable
programs, and accountable public financing.

Thus, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                         ARTICLE I. GENERAL PROVISIONS

Section 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Women, Peace, and Security Act.”

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

The State recognizes that lasting peace, stability, and human security cannot be achieved
without the full, equal, and meaningful participation and leadership of women in the
prevention, management, and resolution of conflicts, peacebuilding, peacekeeping, transitional
justice, relief, recovery, and reconstruction processes.

Pursuant to Article II, Section 14 and Article XIII, Section 14 of the 1987 Constitution, the
State shall uphold the fundamental equality of women and men before the law and recognize
and value the role of women in nation-building. Consistent with the State’s obligations under
the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW),
United Nations Security Council Resolution (UNSCR) No. 1325 and subsequent resolutions
on Women, Peace and Security, and UNSCR Nos. 2250, 2419, and 2535 on Youth, Peace and
Security, the State hereby declares it a policy to:

   a. promote and ensure the full, equal, safe, and meaningful participation, representation,
      and leadership of women in decision-making and in all stages of peace and security
      processes at the national and local levels;

   b. prevent and eliminate all forms of violence, discrimination, exclusion, and other
      barriers that impede the participation and leadership of women and youth in peace and
      security processes;

   c. strengthen and institutionalize a gender-responsive and youth-responsive approach to
      conflict prevention, conflict resolution, peacebuilding, peacekeeping, relief, recovery,
      rehabilitation, and reconstruction;
   d. recognize and support the diverse roles, experiences, capacities, and contributions of
      women and youth as agents of peace, conflict prevention, social cohesion, and
      sustainable development;

   e. ensure that peace and security policies, programs, plans, and interventions are informed
      by the distinct needs, perspectives, and experiences of women and youth, particularly
      those affected by armed conflict, violence, displacement, and other crisis situations;

   f. strengthen coordination and cooperation among national government agencies, local
      government units, civil society organizations, Women’s organizations, youth
      organizations, Indigenous Peoples, the private sector, academe, and other relevant
      stakeholders in advancing the Women, Peace, and Security and Youth, Peace and
      Security agendas;

   g. promote the development of the capacity, leadership, participation, and representation
      of women and youth in peace and security institutions and mechanisms, particularly at
      the local and community levels;

   h. ensure adequate, sustainable, transparent, and gender- and youth-responsive allocation
      of resources for the implementation of the Women, Peace and Security and Youth,
      Peace and Security agendas; and

   i. establish and strengthen mechanisms for accountability, monitoring, evaluation, data
      collection, and reporting to ensure the effective implementation of policies and
      programs on Women, Peace and Security and Youth, Peace and Security.


Section 3. Objectives. This Act seeks to:

     a.    Institutionalize the Women, Peace, and Security (WPS) agenda as a statutory
           matter of law rather than executive policy alone, thereby guaranteeing its
           institutional continuity, sustained funding, and strategic coherence across political
           administrations;

     b.    Guarantee the meaningful, not merely token or ceremonial, participation and
           decision-making power of women, youth, indigenous peoples, persons with
           disabilities, internally displaced persons, and other marginalized groups in all
           stages of peace and security policy formulation, implementation, monitoring, and
           evaluation;

     c.    Synchronize the Women, Peace, and Security (WPS) and Youth, Peace, and
           Security (YPS) agendas (derived from United Nations Security Council
           Resolutions 1325, 2250, 2419, and 2535), recognizing young women as crucial
           bridge-builders between Women’s rights and youth movements;

     d.    Protect and expand civic space for women peacebuilders, mediators, and human
           rights defenders, prohibiting red-tagging, harassment, or intimidation, and creating
           safe environments for grassroots peace advocacy;

     e.    Ensure local ownership of National and Regional Action Plans on WPS,
           embedding peacebuilding mechanisms within local government units and conflict-
           affected communities;
     f.    Recognize women primarily as peacebuilders, negotiators, mediators, and leaders,
           rather than solely as passive victims of conflict; and

     g.    Adopt a whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach that integrates WPS
           across the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus, disaster risk reduction,
           climate security, and disarmament frameworks.


Section 4. Definition of Terms. For the purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
defined as follows:
    a. Women, Peace, and Security (WPS) Agenda refers to the body of international and
       national commitments originating from UNSCR 1325 (2000) and subsequent
       resolutions, promoting Women’s participation, protection, conflict prevention, and
       relief and recovery in conflict contexts.
   b. Youth, Peace, and Security (YPS) Agenda refers to the global framework anchored in
      UNSCR 2250, 2419, and 2535, recognizing youth as active partners in peacebuilding
      and decision-making.
   c. National Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security (NAP-WPS) refers to the
      comprehensive statutory strategy adopted by the Philippine government setting specific
      commitments, indicators, and funding arrangements for WPS implementation.
   d. Regional Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security (RAP-WPS) refers to a localized
      action plan adopted by an autonomous region, including the Bangsamoro Autonomous
      Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM), consistent with but tailored to the region’s
      unique conflict context and organic law.
   e. Meaningful Participation refers to engagement that (1) occurs at an early stage to
      influence outcomes, (2) is supported by adequate resources and information, (3)
      establishes documented mechanisms for input response, and (4) moves beyond mere
      attendance or token representation to actual decision-making influence.
   f. Intersectionality refers to the analytical framework recognizing that women, youth,
      indigenous peoples, and marginalized groups experience overlapping forms of
      exclusion based on gender, age, ethnicity, disability, and socioeconomic status.
   g. Shrinking Civic Space refers to any legal, administrative, or informal restriction—
      including red-tagging, surveillance, or harassment—that limits civil society's capacity
      to advocate, organize, or participate in peace governance.
   h. Local Ownership refers to the principle that WPS priorities, budgets, and indicators
      shall be determined primarily by national and regional institutions and domestic civil
      society, with external development partners playing a supporting role.
   i. Women Peacebuilders and Human Rights Defenders refer to individuals or CSOs
      engaged in conflict resolution, community mediation, human rights monitoring, and
      peace advocacy.
   j. Gender Analysis refers to the systematic examination of the differential impacts of
      conflict, arms proliferation, security policies, and climate threats on women, men, girls,
      and boys.


Section 5. National Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security.
The National Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security (NAP-WPS), first adopted by the
Philippines in 2010 and updated for 2023–2033, is hereby institutionalized with full statutory
authority. The NAP-WPS shall be renewed every six (6) years through a transparent,
participatory process led by the National Steering Committee on Women, Peace and Security
(NSC-WPS) in consultation with civil society organizations.
Each NAP-WPS cycle shall contain explicit objectives, measurable targets, disaggregated
indicators, implementation timelines, clear agency responsibilities, and financing framework
identifying applicable existing funding sources and mechanisms. YPS commitments shall be
fully integrated into the core body of the NAP-WPS rather than treated as a supplementary
annex.

Section 6. National Steering Committee on Women, Peace and Security.

The National Steering Committee on Women, Peace and Security (NSC-WPS) is hereby
established as a statutory inter-agency coordinating body attached to the Office of the
President.

   a. Composition. The NSC-WPS shall be chaired by the Office of the Presidential Adviser
      on Peace, Reconciliation and Unity (OPAPRU). Ex-officio members shall include the
      heads of: (1) Department of National Defense (DND); (2) Department of the Interior
      and Local Government (DILG); (3) Department of Social Welfare and Development
      (DSWD); (4) Department of Justice (DOJ); (5) Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA);
      (6) National Youth Commission (NYC); (7) Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP);
      (8) Philippine National Police (PNP); (9) Bangsamoro Women Commission (BWC);
      (10) Philippine Commission on Women (PCW) and (11) Bangsamoro Youth
      Commission (BYC).

   b. Civil Society and Youth Representation. The NSC-WPS shall include not less than five
      (5) representatives from Women’s peacebuilding CSOs and not less than three (3)
      representatives from youth-led peacebuilding organizations, selected through an open,
      participatory process. At least fifty percent (50%) of these civil society seats shall be
      held by young women.

   c. Core Functions. The NSC-WPS shall:
         i. Formulate, oversee, and evaluate the NAP-WPS;
        ii. Coordinate with regional and local governance bodies;
       iii. Establish the WPS-YPS Joint Coordination Mechanism;
       iv. Administer the Protection and Grievance Mechanism under Section 12;
         v.    Review and approve gender-responsive M&E indicators;
        vi.    Manage the Civil Society Advisory Council; and
       vii.    Submit annual reports to Congress.

Section 7. Regional and Local Action Plans.

Local Government Units (provinces, cities, municipalities, and barangays), particularly in
conflict-affected and conflict-vulnerable areas, shall formulate a Local Action Plans on WPS
(LAP-WPS).

The relationship between NAP-WPS, RAP-WPS, and LAP-WPS shall be governed by the
principle of Coordination, Not Subordination. LAPs shall be integrated directly into existing
Local Development Plans, Peace and Order Plans, and Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Plans rather than creating redundant parallel structures.

   a. Regional Action Plans. Autonomous regions, including BARMM, may adopt their own
      Regional Action Plans on Women, Peace and Security (RAP-WPS), reflecting region-
      specific conflict history, cultural and religious context, and governance arrangements.
      A RAP-WPS shall be developed in consultation with, but need not be subordinate in
      structure or content to, the NAP-WPS, in recognition of the autonomous region’s self-
      governance mandate under its organic law.

       Special measures shall guarantee the active leadership of Bangsamoro women, Moro
       women, Indigenous Peoples (non-Moro indigenous women), internally displaced
       women, and conflict-affected grassroots communities in all BARMM peace and
       security mechanisms.

   b. Local Action Plans. Provinces, cities, and municipalities shall adopt Local Action Plans
      on Women, Peace and Security, particularly in areas with a documented history of
      armed conflict, consistent with guidelines to be issued by the NSC-WPS.

   c. Coordination, Not Subordination. The relationship between the NAP-WPS, RAP-
      WPS, and any local action plans shall be one of coordination and mutual reinforcement,
      not hierarchical subordination, in recognition of the finding that uneven regional
      engagement and unequal access to decision-making spaces have historically weakened
      WPS implementation outside of the national level.


Section 8. Meaningful Participation and Leadership of Women. The State shall guarantee
Women's full, equal, and meaningful participation and leadership across all peace and security
decision-making spaces. This mandate covers: (a) Peace negotiations and peace agreements;
(b) Community mediation and dialogue; (c) Ceasefire monitoring and verification teams; (d)
Transitional justice and reconciliation bodies; (e) Local Peace and Order Councils; (f) Early
warning and conflict prevention mechanisms; (g) Disaster risk reduction and humanitarian
response; and (h) Security sector leadership and policy formulation.
Implementing agencies shall ensure that participation mechanisms provide women with
decision-making power, voting rights, access to classified security information, and financial
support, transitioning from numerical representation to substantive policy influence.

Section 9. WPS in Peace Processes, Conflict Prevention, and Peacebuilding.

Women peacebuilders shall be integrated across all stages of formal and informal peace
processes:

   a. Before Negotiations: In conflict prevention, early warning systems, cross-line
      community dialogue, and pre-negotiation consultations;
   b. During Negotiations: As principal negotiators, technical working group members,
      legal advisors, and formal consensus-builders; and
   c. After Agreements: In normalization, ceasefire implementation, arms management,
      transitional justice, and community-based rehabilitation.

All peace agreements negotiated by the National Government shall undergo mandatory gender
analysis prior to final signing to ensure gender-responsive provisions.

Section 10. Prevention of Violent Extremism. The State recognizes women as vital actors in
preventing and countering violent extremism (PVE). Government PVE strategies shall
incorporate gender-responsive frameworks that support women community leaders, educators,
and mothers in identifying early indicators of radicalization, fostering social cohesion, and
addressing the root causes of armed violence.

Section 11. Protection of Women and Girls in Conflict and Crisis. The State shall enforce
zero-tolerance policies against conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV), gender-based violence
(GBV), human trafficking, and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) committed during armed
conflict, civil unrest, or humanitarian emergencies. Specialized legal, medical, and
psychosocial support units shall be deployed in all conflict-affected LGUs.

Section 12. Protection of Women Peacebuilders and Human Rights Defenders.

   a. Prohibition on Stigmatization and Red-Tagging. It shall be unlawful for any public
      official, uniformed personnel, or government employee to label, accuse, or stigmatize
      any individual or organization engaged in peacebuilding, human rights advocacy, or
      WPS implementation as subversive or terroristic (‘red-tagging’) without due process
      and a final judicial determination.
   b. Rapid-Response Protection Measures. The NSC-WPS, in coordination with the
      Commission on Human Rights (CHR), shall establish a rapid-response Protection
      Mechanism providing emergency assistance to women peacebuilders facing threats.
      Protection measures shall include: (a) Immediate security assessments; (b) Emergency
      protective custody; (c) Legal aid and representation; (d) Psychosocial recovery
      services; (e) Digital security support; and (f) Temporary safe relocation.
   c. Grievance Mechanism and Non-Retaliation. An independent Grievance Mechanism
      shall receive and investigate complaints regarding civic space restrictions or threats
       against peacebuilders. Non-retaliation protections shall automatically apply to all
       complainants.

Section 13. WPS in the Security Sector.

The DND, AFP, PNP, DILG, and Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) shall fully integrate the WPS
agenda into their institutional doctrine, operational planning, education, and promotion criteria.

Mandatory continuing training on gender analysis, human rights, WPS/YPS frameworks, and
the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) shall be conducted across all military
and police academies and pre-deployment courses.

Section 14. Arms Control, Disarmament, and Prevention of Armed Violence.

The State shall adopt a gender-responsive approach to arms control, small arms and light
weapons (SALW) management, disarmament, and counter-trafficking:

   a. Inclusive Disarmament: Women peacebuilders shall participate in designing and
      implementing arms control strategies and community firearm reduction initiatives;
   b. DDR & Normalization: Gender perspectives and WPS guidelines shall be embedded in
      Disarmament, Demobilization, and Reintegration (DDR) and normalization programs;
      and
   c. Disaggregated Impact Data: Security agencies shall systematically collect and analyze
      sex- and age-disaggregated data regarding armed violence impacts.

Section 15. Peace Education.

The Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) shall integrate peace
education, non-violent conflict resolution, and WPS/YPS principles into standard primary,
secondary, tertiary, and non-formal learning curricula and teacher training modules.

Section 16. Young Women and Youth, Peace, and Security.

The NSC-WPS and the National Youth Commission (NYC) shall jointly establish a standing
WPS-YPS Joint Coordination Mechanism. This mechanism shall promote young Women’s
leadership, facilitate access to existing resources and funding mechanisms for youth peace
projects, and eliminate barriers preventing youth from shaping peace policy.

Section 17. WPS, Humanitarian Action, Disaster Risk Reduction, and Climate Security.

Implementing agencies shall operationalize the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus by
embedding WPS perspectives into disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM), climate
change adaptation, displacement management, and crisis recovery. Women community leaders
shall lead local disaster and emergency response committees.

Section 18. Civil Society Participation and Advisory Council.

There is hereby created a statutory Civil Society Advisory Council on WPS, composed of
grassroots women peacebuilders, indigenous women, youth leaders, and human rights
advocates from Luzon, Visayas, Mindanao, and BARMM. The Advisory Council shall
independently monitor WPS implementation, advise the NSC-WPS, and present community-
level policy recommendations.

Section 19. Local Ownership and Safeguards on Development Partner Engagement.

WPS priorities, strategic targets, and evaluation criteria shall be determined through nationally
led and participatory processes involving relevant government institutions and Philippine civil
society. Development partner assistance shall be aligned with nationally determined priorities
and applicable Philippine laws, policies, and procedures.. All funding terms and technical
assistance agreements shall be publicly disclosed to the NSC-WPS.

Section 20. Funding.

The funding requirements for the effective implementation of this Act shall be charged against
existing appropriations and available funds of the concerned national government agencies and
local government units, subject to existing budgeting, accounting, auditing, and procurement
laws, rules, and regulations.


Section 21. Data, Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning.

The NSC-WPS shall institute a robust M&E framework with disaggregated indicators tracking
qualitative outcomes, structural changes, and decision-making influence. An independent,
external evaluation of NAP-WPS shall be commissioned every six (6) years, featuring direct
consultations with grassroots peacebuilders.

Section 22. Annual Report to Congress.

The NSC-WPS shall submit a comprehensive report on or before March 30 of every year to
both Houses of Congress detailing progress against NAP-WPS targets, regional/local
implementation, budget utilization, protection incidents, and civic space indicators.

Section 23. Congressional Oversight.

A Joint Congressional Oversight Committee on WPS is hereby created, composed of five (5)
Senators and five (5) Representatives. Congress shall hold annual legislative oversight hearings
on WPS implementation to review budget efficiency, progress, and legislative gaps.
Section 24. Implementing Rules and Regulations.

Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the NSC-WPS, in joint consultation
with civil society peacebuilding organizations, shall promulgate the necessary Implementing
Rules and Regulations (IRR) for its effective enforcement.

Section 25. Separability Clause.

If any provision or part hereof is held invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or
the provisions not otherwise affected shall remain valid and subsisting.

Section 26. Repealing and Amending Clause.

Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive order, letter of instruction, administrative
order, rule, or regulation contrary to, or inconsistent with, the provisions of this Act is hereby
repealed, modified, or amended accordingly.

Section 27. Effectivity.

This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete publication in the Official Gazette
or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
