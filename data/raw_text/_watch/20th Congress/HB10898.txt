                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) are the backbone of
the Philippine economy. As of 2023, MSMEs account for 99.59% of all
business establishments in the country and generate the majority of private
sector employment. In CALABARZON, one of the nation's principal
economic engines, there are 189,662 MSMEs, representing 99.57% of all
establishments in the region and providing employment to nearly 956,000
workers. Batangas, as one of the region's leading industrial and
commercial provinces, owes much of its economic dynamism to thousands
of entrepreneurs engaged in manufacturing, agribusiness, logistics,
tourism, retail, and services.

       Yet the very enterprises that drive economic growth remain among
the most vulnerable to economic disruptions. Unlike large corporations with
greater capital reserves and market power, MSMEs are disproportionately
affected by inflation, volatile fuel prices, supply chain disruptions, rising
logistics costs, fluctuating interest rates, and other price shocks. A sudden
increase in the cost of electricity, transportation, imported inputs, or
financing can rapidly erode already thin profit margins, constrain
expansion, or even force business closures.
      Compounding this challenge is the fact that the statutory thresholds
defining micro, small, and medium enterprises under Republic Act No.
6977, as amended, have remained largely disconnected from changing
economic realities. Inflation and sustained increases in the cost of doing
business have significantly reduced the real value of these asset
thresholds. Consequently, many enterprises are prematurely reclassified
despite having no corresponding increase in their actual productive
capacity or financial strength, thereby losing access to programs
specifically intended to help them grow.

       This measure recognizes that a rapidly growing economy requires
equally responsive institutions. It strengthens the Magna Carta for MSMEs
by ensuring that enterprise classifications are periodically reviewed to
reflect prevailing economic conditions; expanding access to financing
through strengthened credit support and innovative lending mechanisms;
simplifying regulatory compliance; reducing the cost of doing business;
improving market access through government procurement and
commercial opportunities; promoting digitalization and financial inclusion;
strengthening local MSME development councils; and enhancing the
institutional capacity of the MSMED Council and the Small Business
Corporation to respond more effectively to the evolving needs of Filipino
entrepreneurs.

      This bill seeks to build enterprises that are not only competitive during
periods of economic expansion but also resilient in the face of inflation,
market volatility, natural disasters, and other external shocks. We implore
Congress to consider this measure to create an enabling environment
where local entrepreneurship can flourish, innovation can thrive, and
economic opportunities can spread and reach all communities across the
country.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is
earnestly sought.
