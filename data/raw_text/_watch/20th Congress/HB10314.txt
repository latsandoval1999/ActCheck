                             EXPLANATORY NOTE


The high cost of electricity in the Philippines remains a constant burden to
Filipino households and businesses, contributing significantly to the rising
cost of living and the overall cost of doing business in the country. This
situation has been exacerbated by the ongoing conflict in the middle east.
A major component of electricity pricing is the imposition of the Value Added
Tax (VAT) on the sale of electricity by generation, transmission, and
distribution companies, as well as electric cooperatives. Under the current
system, VAT is imposed on each stage of the power supply chain, from
generation, transmission, to distribution, resulting in compounded tax costs
that are ultimately passed on to consumers.


Another contributing factor to high electricity bills is the “system loss” charge,
which represents the difference between the electricity supplied to the
distribution system and the electricity actually delivered to end-users. These
losses arise from both technical inefficiencies and non-technical factors such
as pilferage. Despite not being directly consumed by households, this system
loss component is currently included in electricity billing and remains subject
to the Value-Added Tax (VAT).


The US State Department reported in its 2022 Investment Climate Statement,
that: “Poor infrastructure, high power costs, slow broadband connections,
regulatory inconsistencies, and corruption are major disincentives to
investment. The Philippines’ complex, slow, and sometimes corrupt judicial
system inhibits the timely and fair resolution of commercial disputes. Traffic
in major cities and congestion in the ports remain barriers to doing
business.”1


This bill seeks to amend Sections 108(A) and 109(1) of the National Internal
Revenue Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 9337, to exempt the sale of
electricity from VAT. By removing the VAT on electricity, we aim to provide
immediate relief to Filipino households and ease financial pressures on
businesses, particularly micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs).
Moreover, by removing VAT on system loss charge, we can address the
growing public concern over electricity pricing structures.


Lowering electricity costs will not only improve the purchasing power of
Filipino families but also enhance the competitiveness of local industries,
encourage job creation, and promote inclusive economic growth. This
measure forms part of a broader agenda to bring down the cost of living and
ensure that economic development is felt by all.


In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
