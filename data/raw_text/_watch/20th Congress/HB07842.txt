                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

                                AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE MUNICIPALITY OF SULTAN KUDARAT WOMEN AND CHILDREN
PROTECTION CENTER IN THE PROVINCE OF MAGUINDANAO DEL NORTE, ADOPTING
AN AGE-APPROPRIATE, GENDER-SENSITIVE, AND TRAUMA-INFORMED PROGRAM,
AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

In 2022, the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) conducted the National Demographic
and Health Survey, which showed that an alarming percentage of women aged 15-49
years old who have ever had a husband or intimate partner experienced emotional,
physical, or sexual violence committed by their current or most recent husband or
intimate partner.

The key findings tell a story of unimaginable violence against women in the following
ways:
   • Experience of violence: 13% of women aged 15–49 have experienced physical
       violence since age 15, and 4% have ever experienced sexual violence, including
       2% who have experienced sexual violence by someone who is not a husband or
       intimate partner.
   • Controlling behaviors: 9% of ever-married women and never-married women
       who have ever had an intimate partner report that their husband/intimate
       partner has exhibited at least three specified types of controlling behaviors.
   • Violence by current or most recent husband/intimate partner: 18% of women
       who have ever had an intimate partner have experienced physical, sexual, or
       emotional violence by their current or most recent husband/intimate partner,
       and 12% experienced such violence in the 12 months preceding the survey.
   • Trends in intimate partner violence by current or most recent husband/partner:
       The percentage of ever-married women who have experienced physical, sexual,
       or emotional violence committed by their current or most recent
       husband/partner has declined over time, from 29% in 2008 to 24% in 2017 and
       18% in 2022.
   •   Injuries due to intimate partner violence: 42% of ever-married/partnered
       women who experienced intimate partner physical or sexual violence in the 12
       months preceding the survey sustained an injury.
   •   Help seeking: 34% of women who have ever experienced physical or sexual
       violence have sought help to stop the violence.

Although recorded figures for the BARMM are low, there is a strong possibility that
many cases are not being reported to authorities for reasons that the study could have
diligently investigated.

Be that as it may, this representation believes that even if official records are high or
low, a functional women’s crisis center in the Province of Maguindanao del Norte
should be established now, with qualified and competent staff to manage women and
children – victim-survivors of violence and abuse from other towns and municipalities
who have limited access to justice, and discouraged from pursuing cases against their
perpetrators.

This bill seeks the establishment of a Women and Children Protection Center for the
inhabitants of the Province of Maguindanao del Norte, to be located in the Municipality
of Sultan Kudarat.

In view of the above, approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE MUNICIPALITY OF SULTAN KUDARAT WOMEN AND CHILDREN
PROTECTION CENTER IN THE PROVINCE OF MAGUINDANAO DEL NORTE, ADOPTING
AN AGE-APPROPRIATE, GENDER-SENSITIVE, AND TRAUMA-INFORMED PROGRAM,
AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

 SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Municipality of Sultan Kudarat
Women and Children Protection Center Act."

   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. The following State policies shall be observed at all
times:
   (a) The State recognizes the value of the dignity of every human person and
guarantees full respect for human rights and likewise recognizes the need to protect
women and children, as members of the vulnerable sector, from all forms of gender-
based violence;

  (b) The State recognizes and promotes the right to health of the people and shall
support the elimination of all forms of gender-based violence;

   (c) Pursuant to Article 19 of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, State
Parties shall take all appropriate measures to protect the child from all forms of physical
or mental violence, injury or abuse, neglect or negligent treatment, maltreatment or
exploitation including sexual abuse, while in the care of parent(s), legal guardian(s) or
any other person who has the care of the child.

  Towards this end, the State shall exert efforts to address gender-based violence
committed against women and children consistent with the fundamental freedoms
guaranteed under the 1987 Philippine Constitution, the Universal Declaration of
Human Rights, the Convention on The Rights of the Child, the Convention on the
Elimination of all forms of Discrimination Against Women, and other international
human rights instruments of which the Philippines is a party.

  SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. As used in this Act:

  a) "Child" refers to a person below eighteen (18) years of age or those over but are
     unable to fully take care of themselves or protect themselves from abuse, neglect,
     cruelty, exploitation, or discrimination because of a physical or mental disability
     or condition;
  b) "Child Abuse" refers to the maltreatment, whether habitual or not, of the child,
     which includes any of the following:
     1) Psychological and physical abuse, neglect, cruelty, sexual abuse, and
          emotional maltreatment;
     2) Any act by deeds or words which debases, degrades, or demeans the intrinsic
          worth and dignity of a child as a human being;
     3) Unreasonable deprivation of his basic needs for survival, such as food and
          shelter; or
     4) Failure to immediately give medical treatment to an injured child resulting in
          serious impairment of his growth and development, or in his permanent
          incapacity or death.
  c) "Online sexual abuse or exploitation of children (OSAEC)" refers to the use of ICT
     as a means to abuse and/or exploit children sexually, which includes cases in
     which offline child abuse and/or exploitation is combined with an online
     component. This can also include, but is not limited to, the production,
     dissemination, and possession of CSAEM; online grooming of children for sexual
     purposes; sexual extortion of children; sharing image-based sexual abuse;
     commercial sexual exploitation of children; exploitation of children through
     online prostitution; and live-streaming of sexual abuse, with or without the
     consent of the victim.
  d) "Violence against women" refers to any act or a series of acts committed by any
     person against a woman which result in or is likely to result in physical, sexual,
     psychological harm or suffering, or economic abuse, including threats of such
     acts, battery, assault, coercion, harassment, or arbitrary deprivation of liberty. It
     shall also include the definitions under Republic Act No. 9262.
  e) "Best Interest of the Child" refers to the totality of the circumstances and
     conditions which are most congenial to the survival, protection, and feelings of
     security of the child and most encouraging to the child's physical, psychological,
     and emotional development.

   SEC. 4. Women and Children Protection Center. The Women and Children Protection
Center shall be established in the Municipality of Sultan Kudarat, which shall respond
to the needs of women and children’s victims-survivors of violence and abuse in an age-
appropriate, gender-sensitive, and trauma-informed approach. It shall be composed of
a multidisciplinary team equipped to provide comprehensive medical and psycho-social
services to women and children victims of violence.
  SEC. 5. Services. The Center shall provide the following services as may be necessary:

  a) Medical services such as, but not limited to, routine check-up, screening, and
  treatment of Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STD), mental health screening, and
  development assessment;
  b) Forensic Services, such as but not limited to medico-legal evaluation,
  videotaped forensic interview, and forensic social work;
  c) Legal services, such as but not limited to legal counseling to the victim-survivor
  and their family, preparation of legal documents, assistance in the filing of cases or
  claims from the Department of Justice Board of Claims and Commission on Human
  Rights;
  d) Social services such as risk and security assessment of the victim-survivor and
  referral to agencies, non-government organizations, and civil society organizations
  for other support services necessary for the recovery and well-being of the victim-
  survivor.

   SEC. 6. Facilities. In order to provide a safe space for victims-survivors and to ensure
efficient and effective service, the Center shall have the following facilities:
   a) Reception or Intake Area;
   b) Counseling Room;
   c) Medical Examination Room;
   d) Interview/lnvestigation Room (with one-way mirror, video recorder,
   e) Database or Records Section;
   f) Child-friendly Room.

  SEC. 7. Personnel. The Center shall be operated by a multi-disciplinary team
composed of the following:
   1) Executive Director with a bachelor's degree, preferably with a medical license,
      at least four (4) years of work experience in Gender And Development (GAD) or
      related programs and advocacies relating to women's and children's rights,
      particularly women and children in crises;
   2) Social Welfare Officer with at least one (1) year of work experience in GAD or
      related programs and advocacies relating to women and children rights,
      particularly women and children in crises, and registered as a social worker;
   3) Two (2) Administrative Officers with bachelor's degree, at least one (1) year
      work experience in GAD or related programs and advocacies relating to women
      and children rights, particularly women and children in crises;

   The following professionals and consultants who will be employed or detailed in
the Center shall receive honoraria, allowances, and other such emoluments in
accordance with budgetary limitations prescribed by law:

    l) Psychologist or child psychologist — Registered Psychologist with at least two
       (2) years of experience in handling cases of violence against women and
       children;
    2) Medico-Legal Officer — Doctor of Medicine with one (1) year of experience in
       medico-legal;
    3) Police Officer to be detailed and recommended by the Philippine National
       Police;
    4) Legal Officer — Member of the Integrated Bar of the Philippines who specializes
        and has experience in handling cases of violence against women and children.

    SEC. 8. Operations. The Center shall be under the Provincial Health Office of
Maguindanao del Norte, which shall act as the lead agency for its operations, such that
the Center's budget shall be included in the Provincial Office's appropriation.

     SEC. 9. Role of the Police Officer. The full-time police officer detailed at the Center
is expected to handle the conduct of the investigation of complaints with utmost care
and sensitivity.

    Upon the receipt of the complaint, the police officer shall:
    a) Immediately refer the case to the prosecutor for inquest if the accused is
       detained; otherwise, the rules of court shall apply.
    b) Arrange for counseling and medical services for the victim-survivor;
    c) Assist the family in the preparation of documents and requirements in the filing
       of claims for compensation before the Department of Justice- Board of Claims
       and Commission on Human Rights if applicable;
    d) Prepare a written report of the actions taken.

    It shall be the duty of the police officer or the examining physician, who must be of
the same gender as the victim-survivor, to ensure that the only persons expressly
authorized by the victim-survivor shall be allowed inside the room where the
investigation or medical examination is being conducted.

    SEC. 10. Protocol in handling cases of violence against women and children. The
Center shall develop a comprehensive protocol in handling cases of violence against
women and children, taking into consideration the sensitive nature of the cases before
the Center.

     It shall adopt the provisions of Republic Act No. 8505 or the Rape Victim Assistance
and Protection Act of 1998, Republic Act No. 7610 or the Special Protection of Children
Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act, Republic Act No. 11930 or the Anti-
Online Sexual Abuse or Exploitation of Children and Anti-Child Sexual Abuse or
Exploitation Materials Act, Republic Act No 9208 or the Anti-Trafficking in Persons Act
of 2003, Republic Act No. 9344 or the Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act as amended, and
Administrative Matter (A.M.) No. 00-4-07-SC or the Examination of Child Witness Rule,
as far as practicable.

    The Center's personnel shall also be trained to recognize signs of abuse or violence
as well as mental health issues that arise in survivors of violence and shall employ a
compassionate and nonjudgmental approach when dealing with the victim-survivor
and their family.

    SEC. 11. Legal assistance. The Center shall create a legal support network
specializing in cases of violence against women and children. It shall include members
of the Integrated Bar of the Philippines, Legal Aid offices, non-government
organizations, and civil society organizations with an advocacy for the promotion and
protection of the rights of women and children.
   SEC. 12. Centralized Database on Violence Against Children and Women Cases. The
Center shall keep a database of all cases assisted and filed before it for proper
monitoring and reporting purposes only. The collection and processing of data shall
comply with Republic Act No. 10173 or the Data Privacy Act of 2012,

    SEC. 13. Confidentiality of Records. All records shall be considered privileged and
confidential. The records shall not be disclosed directly or indirectly to anyone, and all
public officers and employees, and public or private clinics or hospitals shall respect the
right to privacy of the victim.

    Any person who, without the consent of the victim-survivor, causes the publication
or broadcast of any information about the victim-survivor, their family, or the case, shall
be held liable to the contempt power of the court.

    SEC. 14. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the establishment and
operation of the Municipality of Sultan Kudarat Women and Children Protection Center
shall be charged against the budget of the DSWD for the following year's appropriation.

    Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of
this Act shall be included in the budget of the Provincial Social Welfare Office of
Maguindanao del Norte.

    In addition, the Center may accept donations, grants, and contributions from
various sources, in cash or in kind, for purposes relevant to its functions, subject to the
usual government accounting and auditing regulations.

    SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within (90) days upon the approval
of this Act, all concerned agencies shall formulate rules and regulations as may be
necessary. Provided that the failure of the concerned agencies to promulgate the said
rules and regulations shall not prevent the implementation of this Act upon its
effectivity.

    SEC. 16. Separability Clause. If any provision or part hereof is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain valid and
subsisting.

    SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation contrary to or
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed, modified or amended
accordingly.

     SEC. 18. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in any two (2) newspapers of general circulation in the
Philippines.
