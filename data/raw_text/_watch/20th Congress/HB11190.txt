                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

        A country's mobility system is highly indicative of a State's treatment of every
citizen's right to experience an improved quality of life and to participate meaningfully
in society. The barriers each citizen faces just to get from one place to another reveal
not only issues with the transportation system, but also issues concerning public
health, economic opportunity, environmental sustainability, and the rights of more
vulnerable persons.

       This is evident in the Philippines’ mobility system, which has been characterized
as a "carmageddon" for being extremely congested and almost dehumanizing to the
common Filipino.1 Metro Manila, for example, ranked as the most congested
metropolitan area in the world in 2023, causing immense economic losses estimated
at PHP 3.5 billion per day.2

      This crisis is attributed, among others, to a highly car-centric system.
Government agencies measure success by the number of roads they widen and the
expressways they build.      A 2015 technical report of the Japan International
Cooperation Agency (JICA) showed that 88 percent (88%) of households in Metro

        What seems to be forgotten is that the vast majority of Filipinos actually rely on
public transportation, accounting for approximately 70% of daily trips.4 For them, each
day they go to work is marked by a grueling and undignified ordeal consisting of
systemic shortages in public transport supply, endless terminal queues, and highly
unpredictable travel times.

       Urgent priority must therefore be given to addressing the myriad issues
underlying the country's mobility system. This bill seeks to do exactly this by mandating
the creation of the Congressional Commission on Mobility ("MobiComm"), a ten-
member bicameral body co-chaired by the Chairpersons of the Senate Committee on
Public Services and the House Committee on Transportation. The MobiComm will also
establish specialized standing committees to engage resource persons in addressing
issues concerning active mobility, public transport, road safety, and universal
accessibility. The MobiComm is structured to exist until 2031, with a mandate to submit
a mid-term report in 2028 and a final, comprehensive roadmap with key performance
indicators at the end of its term to guide future reforms.

        To ensure that its policy recommendations are evidence-based and
community-informed, the bill also creates a Technical Secretariat led by an Executive
Director and designates the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) as its
primary research arm. Lastly, a ten-member Mobility, Legislation and Policy Advisory
Council, which consists of experts from academe, transport cooperatives, LGUs, and
civil society, is also created to provide written inputs before any final report can be
adopted.

       Ultimately, this bill is but a first step, albeit a necessary one, to address the
longstanding issues that have been plaguing the country's mobility system for
decades.

 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   protect and promote the right of all persons to safe, accessible, inclusive, affordable,
 6   sustainable, and reliable mobility. Toward this end, the State adopts the following
 7   policies:
 8
 9          (a) Mobility policy shall reduce excessive and inequitable travel and waiting
10   times that constrain the people's access to work, education, healthcare, family life, and
11   civic participation.
12
13           (b) Roads, road-based transportation systems, and complementary transport
14   modes are essential public goods that directly affect human dignity, socio-economic
15   equality, inclusive growth, environmental sustainability, public health, and the
16   fulfillment of human rights. Mobility systems and the built environment shall be planned
17   to maximize accessibility, rather than merely to increase vehicle movement or road
18   capacity.

 1          (c) Mobility-related policies, plans, programs, and investments shall uphold the
 2   constitutional rights to life, health, a healthy environment, safety, equality, and
 3   development.
 4
 5         (d) Consistent with the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology,
 6   and recognizing the rights of nature, mobility systems shall incorporate and maximize
 7   nature-based solutions, including native trees and the restoration of urban forests, to
 8   address the extreme heat and rainfall intensified by the climate crisis.
 9
10         (e) The State shall pursue transit-oriented development that enhances
11   intermodal connectivity, first-mile and last-mile accessibility, universal design, and
12   seamless mobility across transport modes, and shall avoid land use and built-
13   environment patterns that create unnecessary travel distances and structural
14   dependence on private motor vehicles.
15
16          (f) The State adopts a people-centered hierarchy of road users, consistent with
17   national development policy, according the highest priority to pedestrians and cyclists,
18   followed by public transport and other transport modes. Active transport is a
19   fundamental component of the mobility system and requires safe, continuous, and
20   accessible infrastructure within the built environment.
21
22          (g) The State recognizes the complementary roles of national and local
23   governments, the public and private sectors, communities, and transport workers in
24   building a people-centered, safe, and sustainable mobility system.

26          SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
27
28          a) Active Transport – shall refer to the modes of transportation that require or
29             primarily involve human physical activity, including walking, cycling, and the
30             use of mobility assistive devices.
31
32          b) Built Environment – shall refer to all human-made or modified physical
33             components of communities, including land development patterns, transport
34             networks, public spaces, utilities, infrastructure, and other structures.
35
36          c) First-mile and last-mile access – shall refer to the accessibility of the initial
37             and final segments of a trip to and from public transport, encompassing the
38             ease with which commuters can reach transit stops and their destinations
39             from those stops.
40
41          d) Mobility – shall refer to the ability of persons, as enabled or constrained by
42             the built environment, transport systems, and public infrastructure planned,
43             designed, regulated, and managed by government, to move safely,
44             efficiently, affordably, inclusively, and sustainably, and to access

                                                 2
 1             opportunities, services, and destinations necessary for personal, social, and
 2             economic well-being.
 3
 4         e) Mobility System – shall refer to the framework of roads, streets, transport
 5            services, public spaces, land-use structures, policies, regulations, and
 6            institutional arrangements planned, designed, regulated, and managed by
 7            government, which together enable or constrain the ability of persons and
 8            goods to move and access destinations.
 9
10         f) Vulnerable Road Users (VRU) – shall refer to road users not in a car, bus,
11            or truck, generally considered to include pedestrians, motorcycle riders,
12            cyclists, children seven (7) years and under, the elderly, and users of
13            mobility devices.

15          SEC. 4. State Principles on Mobility. — The State recognizes that mobility
16   systems, public transport, active transport, roads, and the built environment shall be
17   planned, designed, constructed, regulated, and managed in accordance with the
18   following principles:
19
20      a) People-Centered. — Mobility policies, programs, and the design of roads and
21         the built environment shall prioritize the safe, equitable, and dignified movement
22         of persons, particularly vulnerable road users, including pedestrians, cyclists,
23         children, older persons, and persons with disabilities, while addressing
24         imbalances and inequities arising from car-oriented planning approaches and
25         allocation of public safety.
26
27      b) Multimodal and Integrated. — Mobility systems and the built environment shall
28         be designed within an integrated and balanced transport network comprising
29         public transport, active transport, freight and logistics, and private motor
30         vehicles.
31
32      c) Accessible and Inclusive. — The planning and design of mobility systems and
33         the built environment shall prioritize access of people to destinations, services,
34         and opportunities, rather than solely increasing vehicle movement or road
35         capacity. Mobility systems and the built environment shall be designed or
36         redesigned to eliminate physical, economic, and social barriers and ensure
37         universal accessibility and universal design, particularly for persons with
38         disabilities, older persons, children, and other vulnerable users, in roads,
39         streets, sidewalks, public spaces, terminals, vehicles, and related
40         infrastructure.
41
42      d) Sustainable and Climate-Resilient. — Mobility planning, infrastructure
43         investments, and the development of the built environment shall minimize


                                                3
 1         environmental impacts, promote resource efficiency, support cleaner transport
 2         technologies, and strengthen resilience to climate-related risks.
 3
 4      e) Safe and Health-Promoting. — Mobility systems and the built environment shall
 5         adopt a safety-first approach consistent with the Safe System framework, and
 6         be designed to reduce road crash fatalities and serious injuries, and promote
 7         public health through safe and supportive conditions for active transport,
 8         including walking and cycling.
 9
10      f) Green Infrastructure and Nature-Based Solutions. — Mobility infrastructure and
11         the built environment shall integrate green infrastructure and nature-based
12         solutions, including the protection and expansion of native tree cover, urban
13         forests, shade, permeable surfaces, and climate-responsive public spaces,
14         particularly in areas exposed to extreme heat and flooding.

24           SEC.15. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
