                           AN ACT

  SECTOR, PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Oral health is a fundamental component of an individual's overall health and
well-being. Despite its critical importance, access to oral healthcare remains
inadequate for many Filipinos, as dental care is too often treated as a privilege rather
than an essential public health service. This situation reflects longstanding gaps in
public health investment and the insufficient integration of dental professionals into
the public healthcare system.

       Data from the Department of Health's National Database of Human Resources
for Health indicate that only about 2,400 dentists are employed in the public sector.
As of 2026, this leaves one public dentist to serve an average of 53,000 people—far
exceeding the World Health Organization's recommended ratio of one dentist for every
7,500 individuals. This significant shortage underscores the urgent need to strengthen
the country's public oral healthcare workforce.

        A primary barrier is the severe wage disparity between government service and
private practice. An entry-level public dentist is locked into Salary Grade (SG) 13,
which translates to a mere PHP 34,421.00 compensation monthly. In contrast, the
private sector offers significantly higher income potential without the rigid bureaucratic
restrictions and stringent requirements inherent to the civil service.

       In alignment with the policy objectives initiated during the 20th Congress, this
Act mandates a comprehensive restructuring of the compensation framework for
dental health professionals across all public sectors, explicitly encompassing national
government agencies, government-owned or controlled corporations, state

universities and colleges, and local government units. To fortify the civil service by
incentivizing specialized dental education and ensuring the robust recruitment and
long-term retention of premier practitioners, the state-employed dental workforce
shall be reallocated to upgraded salary grades, specifically designating Salary Grade
17 for Dentist I, Salary Grade 20 for Dentist II, Salary Grade 23 for Dentist III, Salary
Grade 25 for Dentist IV, Salary Grade 27 for Dentist V, Salary Grade 29 for Dentist VI,
and Salary Grade 30 for Dentist VII.

       Ultimately, this measure seeks to fulfill the constitutional mandate to protect
and promote the right to health of the people, thereby fostering a deeply ingrained
health consciousness across the nation.

      The passage of this bill which is a counterpart measure of Senate Bill 493 is
earnestly requested.

 1          SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Public Dentist Salary
 2   Modification Act.”
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared a policy of the State to
 5   protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness
 6   among them. In line with this policy, and in recognition of the critical role of dentists
 7   in promoting oral health among Filipinos, and the need to encourage qualified health
 8   care professionals to join and remain in government service, the State shall ensure
 9   that the compensation received by public dentists remain commensurate to the
10   demands of the service, and that their social and economic well-being, living and
11   working conditions, and terms of employment are continuously promoted and
12   improved.
13
14          SEC. 3. Coverage. This Act shall cover all dentists employed in all agencies
15   and instrumentalities of government, including national government agencies,
16   government-owned-and-controlled corporations (GOCCs), state universities and
17   colleges, and local government units (LGUs), regardless of their status of employment.
