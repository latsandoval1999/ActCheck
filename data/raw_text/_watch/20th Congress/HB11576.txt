House Bill No, 11576

INTRODUCED BY REP. PATRICK MICHAEL “PM” D. VARGAS

AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR A MORATORIUM ON RENTAL PAYMENT AND EVICTION
DURING DISASTERS AND OTHER EMERGENCIES

EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines is highly vulnerable to natural hazards and other
emergencies that may result in the loss of livelihoods, interruption of economic
activities, damage to homes, and displacement of families. For low-income
households, informal workers, and other vulnerable sectors, these disruptions
may immediately affect their ability to pay rent and maintain stable housing.

Republic Act No. 10121, or the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Act of 2010, establishes a comprehensive framework for disaster
prevention, preparedness, response, and recovery. However, existing laws,
including Republic Act No. 9653, or the Rent Control Act of 2009, do not provide
a uniform and automatic mechanism for temporarily protecting tenants from
rental enforcement and eviction during declared disasters and emergencies.

This bill seeks to establish a time-bound moratorium on the enforcement
of rental payments and eviction proceedings involving covered residential units
during a declared state of calamity or emergency, including a reasonable period
after its termination. The measure does not condone the cancellation of rental
obligations; rather, it provides temporary relief by allowing deferred payments to
be settled through a reasonable installment arrangement without penalties or
interest arising from the covered period.

Subject to appropriate rules and eligibility requirements, similar relief may
be extended to qualified micro, small, and medium enterprises affected by the
closure or interruption of business operations. The bill likewise preserves
safeguards for lessors and property owners by allowing mutually agreed payment
arrangements and recognizing exceptions involving criminal acts or conduct that
threatens life, safety, or property.

By providing temporary housing and rental-payment security during
disasters and emergencies, this measure will help prevent displacement, protect
vulnerable households, support affected enterprises, and strengthen the
country’s disaster risk reduction and management framework.

Jac view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

TWENTIETH CONGRESS
Second Regular Session

House Bill No. 11576

INTRODUCED BY REP. PATRICK MICHAEL “PM” D. VARGAS

AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR A MORATORIUM ON RENTAL PAYMENT AND EVICTION
DURING DISASTERS AND OTHER EMERGENCIES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as “Rental Payment and
Eviction Moratorium during Disasters and Emergencies Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to promote the general welfare and provide adequate protection and timely
assistance to individuals, families, and communities affected by disasters and
other emergencies.

The State shall uphold housing security, prevent avoidable displacement, and
ensure that affected lessees and qualified micro, small, and medium enterprises
are given reasonable temporary relief from rental-payment enforcement and
eviction, without unduly impairing the legitimate rights and interests of lessors,
property owners, and other parties to a lease

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act, these terms are
defined as follows:

a. “Disaster” refers to a serious disruption of the functioning of a community
or a_ society involving widespread human, material, economic or
environmental losses and impacts, which exceeds the ability of the affected
community or society to cope using its own resources. Disasters are often
described as a result of the combination of the exposure to a hazard, the

===== [page 4] =====
conditions of vulnerability that are present, and insufficient capacity or
measures to reduce or cope with the potential negative consequences.
Disaster impacts may include loss of life, injury, disease and other
negative effects on human, physical, mental and social well-being, together

with damage to property, destruction of assets, loss of services, social and
economic disruption and environmental degradation.

. “Emergency” refers to an unforeseen or sudden occurrence, especially
danger, demanding immediate action.

. “State of Calamity’ refers to a condition involving mass casualty and/or
major damages to property, disruption of means of livelihoods, roads, and

normal way of life of people in the affected areas as a result of the
occurrence of natural or human-induced hazard.

“Rent” refers to the amount agreed upon by the lessor and lessee for the
use or occupancy of a residential unit, whether payable monthly or on

another periodic basis, including other contractual charges directly related
to such use or occupancy

“Residential unit” refers to an apartment, house, condominium unit,
dormitory, room, bedspace, or other building or portion thereof used
principally as a dwelling and offered for rent. It shall not include motels,

hotels, or similar establishments intended primarily for transient
accommodation.

A residential unit that is also used for a home-based industry, retail store,
or other lawful business activity shall remain covered if the owner and the

owner’s family actually reside therein and the premises are principally
used as a dwelling.

“Lessee” refers to a person who rents or occupies a residential unit under
a lease, whether written or oral.

. “Owner/Lessor’ refers to the owner of a residential unit or the owner’s duly

authorized administrator, representative, or agent who leases or offers the
unit for rent.

. “Sublessor’ refers to a lessee who, with authority from the lessor or as

permitted by law, leases all or part of the leased premises to another
person.

“Sublessee” refers to a person who occupies all or part of a leased premises
under a sublease.

===== [page 5] =====
“Covered period” refers to the period beginning on the date the declaration
of a state of calamity or emergency takes effect and ending thirty (30) days
after the termination or lifting of such declaration, unless a different period
is provided under this Act or its implementing rules and regulations.

SECTION 4. Scope of Application. - This Act shall cover disasters and other
kinds of emergencies which may be (1) national in scope, as stated in the

Declaration of a State of Calamity or State of National Emergency by the
President of the Philippines, or (2) provincial, city or municipal in scope, as

declared through a State of Calamity or Emergency by the appropriate local
government unit.

SECTION 5. Moratorium on Rental Payments. - During the covered period,
no lessor, sublessor, owner, administrator, or agent shall demand, enforce, or
commence an action solely for the collection of rent, fees, charges, or costs

directly related to the use or occupancy of a covered residential unit from a
covered lessee.

The obligation to pay rent shall not be extinguished, waived, or deemed satisfied
by reason of the moratorium. Deferred rental obligations shall become payable

after the expiration of the covered period and shall be settled without penalties,
surcharges, or interest arising solely from the deferment.

Provided, That if the covered period exceeds a total of sixty (60) days, the payment
of the deferred fees, charges and costs shall be spread out for a six-month period

of equal installments: Provided, further, That no penalties or interests shall be
collected on the payments made by virtue of this Act.

The benefit of this moratorium on rental payment may be applied to commercial
or office space rental of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs), as
defined in Republic Act (RA) No. 9501, otherwise known as the “Magna Carta for
MSMEs"’, as amended, at the discretion of the Secretary of the Department of
Trade and Industry (DTI), taking into consideration the fact of closure/shutting

down of businesses, community quarantine orders, or any other circumstance
that may hamper their operations.

Nothing in this Act shall prevent the contractual parties from mutually agreeing

on other forms of payment, relief, or assistance, or from voluntarily waiving the
benefit of the moratorium on rental payments.

SECTION 6. Moratorium on Eviction. - During the same period stated in
Section 5 of this Act, the lessor, sublessor or owner of a residential unit may not

make, or cause to be made, any eviction or similar legal proceeding to recover
possession of the residential unit from the lessee/sublessee regardless of cause,

except when a lessee/sublessee perpetrated a criminal act that threatens the

===== [page 6] =====
life, limb and property of the lessor, sublessor or owner or their immediate family,
or other tenants or staff of the property in which residential unit is located.

The benefit of this moratorium on eviction may be applied to commercial or office
space rental of MSMEs, as defined in RA 9501, as amended, at the discretion of
the DTI Secretary, taking into consideration of the fact of closure/shutting down

of businesses, community quarantine orders, or any other circumstance that
may hamper their operations.

SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Human Settlements and Urban
Development (DHSUD) and the Department of Trade and Industry, in
coordination with the Office of Civil Defense (OCD), the Department of the
Interior and Local Government (DILG), the Department of Social Welfare and
Development, relevant government agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate
the necessary rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, proclamations, rules and regulations, or any part thereof, which are

inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part of this Act, or the
application thereof to any person or circumstance, is held unconstitutional or
invalid, the remainder of this Act shall not be affected thereby.

SECTION 10. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days from its

publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.

Approved,
