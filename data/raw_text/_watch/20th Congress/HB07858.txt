                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to strengthen the policies pursuant Republic Act No. 11930,
otherwise known as the “Anti-Online Sexual Abuse or Exploitation of Children (OSAEC)
and Anti-Child Sexual Abuse or Exploitation Materials (CSAEM) Act”. Despite the
enactment of Republic Act No. 11930 in 2022, the digital landscape has been
continuously evolving with terrifying speed, outpacing existing legal frameworks.

       A 2023 report from the Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center
(CICC) shows that the Philippines is ranked second (2nd) among the countries with
the highest cases of OSAEC. Furthermore, the United Nations Children’s Fund
(UNICEF) data indicates that in 2022, two (2) out of ten (10), or approximately two
million Filipino children have experienced online exploitation1. Yet ninety seven
percent (97%) of these cases remain unreported due to the paralyzing fear of being
shamed, stigmatized, and becoming an invisible statistic.

       The emergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) has created a new
parameter for predators, where the likeness of an innocent child can be weaponized
without a physical act ever taking place. This proposed measure ensures that any
representation of a child engaged in sexual activity, whether captured by a camera or
generated through AI, is punished under our penal statutes. It also protects children
whose identifiable facial features are mapped onto synthetic content, granting them
and their guardians the legal standing to seek justice against those who create,
possess, or distribute these digital violations.

       This bill imposes a heightened duty of care upon internet intermediaries,
platforms, and payment service providers. These are now required to adopt
safeguards to prevent and disrupt the flow of exploitation materials, extending
penalties to those who manage the digital infrastructure hosting such content. It
sends a clear message that nobody is exempt from the responsibility of protecting
