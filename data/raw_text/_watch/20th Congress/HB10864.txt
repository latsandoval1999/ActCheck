                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Schools are meant to be one of the safest places for our children. This is where
they learn, make friends, form their character, and develop skills they will need later in
life. However, recent events in the country have shattered this expectation, turning our
learning institutions into places of fear and anxiety.

       We do not have to look far for an example. Just last year, in the 2nd District of
Parañaque, a Grade 8 student was fatally stabbed by a classmate inside Moonwalk
National High School. Initial reports revealed that the conflict may have stemmed from
a piece of makeup that was not lent by one student.1

       Since the opening of School Year 2026-2027, there have already been multiple
incidents of school-based violence, including two tragic school shootings in Tacloban–
which killed 3 students and injured more than 202 – and Zamboanga–which killed 2
students including the perpetrator who took his own life.3 These incidents do not just

        These alarming incidents demonstrate the need for a comprehensive measure
to ensure our children’s safety in school. This need is further supported by the findings
of the Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II), which noted that
bullying and aggression often remain unreported because learners fear retaliation or
do not know where or how to seek assistance5. Data from the Programme for
International Student Assessment or PISA likewise show that bullying remains a
significant concern among learners.6

       To help address these incidents of school-based violence, this proposed
measure seeks to establish, maintain, and periodically update a National
Comprehensive School Safety Framework, which will prescribe the standards for
school safety plans, school safety infrastructure and security measures, violence
prevention and anti-bullying mechanisms, learner well-being and mental health
promotion, and emergency preparedness. This measure also provides policies for a
safe school culture, zero-tolerance against violence, and anti-bullying.

        This bill also specifies and clarifies the responsibilities of school heads, school
counselors and counselor associates, learner formation officers, and school safety
officers. In addition, it seeks to promote the meaningful participation of parents,
guardians, and families in the improvement of school safety programs.

      It likewise prescribes safety infrastructure and equipment, such as CCTV
cameras in strategic areas, appropriate screening equipment, visitor management
systems, the presence of appropriate security personnel, and emergency equipment.
Further, in recognition of the prevalence of technology in children’s lives, this measure
also mandates schools to adopt policies and programs that promote safe, responsible
and age-appropriate use of digital technologies, including measures to protect learners
from cyberbullying. Finally, it requires schools to establish protocols for reporting,
assessment, interventions, and immediate response to school safety concerns.

       No child should enter a classroom in fear. With this measure, we hope to ensure
that our educational institutions are safe and remain conducive environments for
learning.

                                  TITLE I
                           GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Comprehensive School
Safety Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right of all learners and school personnel to an enabling and
supportive learning and working environment where all learners and school personnel
are protected and secured from any form of abuse, violence, or harm.

The State recognizes that school safety isa shared responsibility requiring the
coordinated participation of learners, parents or guardians, school personnel,
communities, local government units, national government agencies, civil society
organizations, and the private sector.

Towards this end, the State shall:
  (a) Institutionalize a comprehensive and integrated school safety system that
      promotes prevention, response, and recovery across all public and private
      basic education institutions;


                                          4
   (b) Ensure that every school adopts appropriate policies, standards, programs,
       infrastructure, personnel, and resources necessary to safeguard learners and
       school personnel from violence, abuse, bullying, discrimination, harassment,
       exploitation, emergencies, disasters, digital threats, and other risks that may
       affect their safety, well-being, and educational experience;
   (c) Promote learner-centered, inclusive, and evidence-based approaches in the
       design and implementation of school safety programs and interventions;
   (d) Strengthen coordination among the DepEd, national government agencies,
       LGUs, and other community stakeholders in the implementation of school
       safety measures; and
   (e) Ensure the provision of adequate human resources, infrastructure, equipment,
       technology, financial support, and other resources necessary for the effective
       implementation of this Act.

SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. — This Act shall apply to all public and private basic
education institutions, hereinafter referred to as “schools,” including elementary and
secondary schools and other basic education institutions as may be covered by the
Department of Education (DepEd).

The provisions of this Act shall apply to learners, school personnel, and other
members of the school community, and shall cover measures for the prevention of,
protection from, response to, and recovery from threats to their safety and well-being
within school premises and in connection with school-related activities, whether
conducted on or off campus, including activities conducted through or involving digital
platforms and technologies.

For purposes of this Act, school safety encompasses the protection and promotion of
the physical, emotional, psychosocial, and digital safety and well-being of learners,
school personnel, and other members of the school community, as well as the
prevention and appropriate management of violence, abuse, bullying, discrimination,
harassment, exploitation, emergencies, and other threats to their safety and well-
being.

                                TITLE II
                SCHOOL SAFETY FRAMEWORK AND POLICIES
                 CHAPTER I. SCHOOL SAFETY FRAMEWORK

SEC. 4. Establishment of the National Comprehensive School Safety
Framework. — The Department of Education (DepEd), in coordination with the
Department of Health (DOH), the Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD), the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the Philippine
National Police (PNP), Local Government Units (LGUs), and other relevant
government stakeholders shall establish, maintain, and periodically update a National



                                           5
Comprehensive School Safety Framework, hereinafter referred to as the
“Framework,” to guide the implementation of this Act in all schools.

The Framework shall, at the minimum, prescribe national standards for:
   (a) The preparation, implementation, periodic review, and evaluation of School
       Safety Plans and School Safety Handbooks;
   (b) School safety personnel, staffing guidelines, competency standards, and
       capacity-building;
   (c) School safety infrastructure, facilities, equipment, and security measures;
   (d) Learner protection, violence prevention, and anti-bullying mechanisms;
   (e) Digital safety, responsible technology use, and cybersecurity;
   (f) Learner well-being, mental health promotion, counseling services, and
       psychosocial support;
   (g) Emergency preparedness, disaster risk reduction and management, crisis
       response, recovery, and business continuity;
   (h) Reporting school safety concerns such as those involving school bullying and
       violence;
   (i) Assessment, referral, case management, and inter-agency coordination to
       address concerns and mitigate risk;
   (j) Monitoring, evaluation, reporting, and continuous improvement; and
   (k) Such other standards as may be necessary to effectively implement this Act.

The Framework shall likewise harmonize and integrate existing laws, policies, and
mechanisms on learner protection, anti-bullying, mental health, safe spaces, and
workplace safety into a unified system.

The DepEd shall develop and periodically update model policies, templates, manuals,
protocols, reporting forms, training modules, assessment tools, and other
implementation resources necessary to assist schools in complying with this Act.

It shall periodically review and update the Framework to reflect emerging risks,
evolving educational needs, technological developments, lessons learned from
implementation, and global best practices on school safety.

SEC 5. Establishment of School Safety Plans. — Every school shall develop and
annually update a School Safety Plan that contextualizes and operationalizes the
Framework to its unique needs, risks, and resources, emphasizing the safety,
protection, and well-being of learners, as well as school personnel. The Plan shall
include:

   (a) School safety standards, including standards for prevention, response, and
       recovery;




                                         6
   (b) Protocols and manuals for emergencies, including hotlines, designated
       hospitals, police stations, safety zones, evacuation areas, and a fixed-timeline
       referral pathway for serious threats and abuse;
   (c) Protective safety measures, including security infrastructure and equipment;
   (d) Policies on use of gadgets, social media, digital safety and cybersecurity, on
       and off campus;
   (e) Guidelines and protocols for off-campus activities;
   (f) Community engagement and partnership strategies;
   (g) Monitoring and evaluation mechanisms.

The School Head shall be responsible for overseeing the implementation of the School
Safety Plan within the school. School Heads shall submit their School Safety Plan to
the Schools Division Office (SDO) for review and approval at the beginning of each
school year. The SDO shall ensure that the plan is aligned with the Framework and
complies with the minimum standards set forth in this Act.

SEC. 6. School Safety Handbook. — Every school shall prepare, maintain, and
regularly update a School Safety Handbook, as part of the school’s Learner
Handbook, to communicate essential school safety information to learners, parents or
guardians, school personnel, visitors, and other members of the school community.

The School Safety Handbook shall present, in a clear, concise, accessible, and user-
friendly manner, the school safety policies, procedures, emergency information, and
learner support services that members of the school community need to know in order
to promote safety and respond appropriately during emergencies and other school
safety situations.

At a minimum, the School Safety Handbook shall contain:
   (a) The School's commitment to safety and learner well-being;
   (b) The School Code of Conduct and key school safety policies for learners and
       school personnel;
   (c) Procedures for reporting bullying, violence, abuse, discrimination, harassment,
       emergencies, and other school safety concerns;
   (d) Emergency procedures for fire, earthquakes, severe weather, lockdowns,
       medical emergencies, evacuations, and other applicable situations;
   (e) Emergency contact information for school officials, emergency responders,
       designated hospitals, and relevant government agencies;
   (f) School maps showing evacuation routes, emergency exits, assembly and
       evacuation areas, clinics, School Care Centers, guidance offices, nearest
       hospitals, and other important locations;
   (g) Visitor management procedures and campus access guidelines;
   (h) Learner support services, including counseling, psychosocial support, referral
       services, and School Care Centers;
   (i) Digital safety guidelines and responsible technology use; and

                                          7
   (j) Such other practical school safety information as may be prescribed by the
       DepEd.

The School Safety Handbook shall be made readily available in printed and electronic
formats, as appropriate, and shall be disseminated to learners, parents or
guardians, school personnel, and other members of the school community at the
beginning of every school year and whenever significant revisions are made.

Schools shall also conduct an annual orientation on the School Safety Handbook to
ensure that members of the school community understand their rights, responsibilities,
and the procedures for preventing and responding to safety-related concerns.

The School Safety Handbook shall be periodically reviewed in consultation with
learners, parents or guardians, school personnel, and other relevant stakeholders.

                    CHAPTER II. SCHOOL SAFETY POLICIES

SEC. 7. Policy on Safe School Culture. — Every school shall foster a safe, inclusive,
respectful, and supportive school culture. Schools shall promote positive
relationships, mutual respect, responsibility, resilience, and meaningful participation
among all members of the school community through preventive, developmentally
appropriate, and evidence-based policies, programs, and practices that strengthen
positive behavior, social and emotional learning, respectful conflict resolution, and a
shared commitment to maintaining a safe learning environment.

SEC. 8. Zero-Tolerance Policy Against Violence. — Every school shall adopt and
enforce a zero-tolerance policy against all forms of violence, child abuse, neglect,
exploitation, bullying, discrimination, harassment, gender-based violence, corporal
punishment, retaliation, and other acts that threaten the safety, dignity, or well-being
of learners and school personnel. Appropriate preventive, protective, restorative, and
disciplinary measures shall be implemented to prevent, address, and respond to such
acts.

SEC. 9. Anti-Bullying and Learner Protection Policy. – Every school shall adopt
and implement a comprehensive Anti-Bullying and Learner Protection Policy that
promotes the safety, dignity, rights, and well-being of all learners and ensures the
prevention, early identification, prompt reporting, timely intervention, and appropriate
resolution of incidents involving bullying, cyberbullying, violence, abuse, neglect,
exploitation, discrimination, harassment, retaliation, and other acts that threaten the
safety or welfare of learners.

SEC. 10. School Code of Conduct. – The School Safety Handbook shall include a
School Code of Conduct that promotes a culture of respect, responsibility, integrity,
inclusivity, and accountability among members of the school community. The Code

                                           8
shall clearly define unacceptable acts, corresponding disciplinary and restorative
interventions, and the rights and responsibilities of all members of the school
community. In implementing the Code, schools shall ensure that disciplinary
measures are fair, proportionate, developmentally appropriate, non-discriminatory,
and restorative in nature, with due regard for the best interests of the child, due
process, and the rights of school personnel.

SEC. 11. Learner Participation and Voice. — Every school shall recognize and
promote the right of learners to participate meaningfully in matters affecting their
safety, well-being, and school life, consistent with their evolving capacities and the
best interests of the child.

Schools shall establish age-appropriate, inclusive, and child-friendly mechanisms
through which learners may express their views, report safety concerns, contribute to
school safety initiatives, and participate in the development, implementation,
monitoring, and evaluation of school safety policies and programs.

Learner participation shall be voluntary, safe, respectful, and free from discrimination,
retaliation, or intimidation.

SEC. 12. Consistency with Existing Laws and Policies. — The policies, programs,
standards, and procedures adopted and implemented pursuant to this Act shall be
consistent with existing laws, rules and regulations, and applicable policies of the
DepEd on child protection, anti-bullying, positive discipline, mental health, gender-
based violence, safe spaces, learner welfare, and other matters relating to the safety,
dignity, rights, and well-being of learners and school personnel.

                                  TITLE III
                         SCHOOL SAFETY GOVERNANCE

SEC. 13. School Safety Governance. — Every school shall establish a
comprehensive school safety governance system to ensure the effective
implementation of this Act and to promote a safe, secure, inclusive, supportive, and
resilient learning environment.

Every school shall organize or designate an appropriate school safety governance
mechanism composed of school personnel responsible for learner protection,
counseling and psychosocial support, school safety and security, health services,
disaster risk reduction and management, and other functions as may be prescribed
by the DepEd.

SEC. 14. Key School Safety Personnel. — Every school shall ensure the availability
of qualified school personnel necessary to effectively implement this Act.



                                            9
The DepEd shall prescribe staffing standards, competency requirements,
qualifications, deployment guidelines, and continuing professional development
requirements for key school safety personnel. It shall progressively strengthen the
staffing of key school safety personnel in public schools, consistent with national
staffing standards and subject to the availability of appropriations.

   (a) School Head. — The School Head shall serve as the overall lead for the
       implementation of this Act at the school level, and shall:
          (i) Approve and oversee the implementation of the School Safety Plan;
         (ii) Ensure compliance with national school safety standards;
        (iii) Provide leadership in fostering a safe, inclusive, and supportive school

        (iv) Allocate school resources to support school safety initiatives;
         (v)  Ensure coordination among school personnel, parents, local

        (vi) Oversee incident management and post-incident recovery; and
       (vii) Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

   (b) School Counselor and School Counselor Associates. — Pursuant to Republic
       Act No. 12080, or the Basic Education Mental Health and Well-Being Promotion
       Act, the School Counselor shall serve as the lead professional responsible for
       guidance, counseling, learner mental health promotion, psychosocial support,
       and behavioral interventions and shall:
          (i) Provide counseling services;
         (ii) Conduct risk and psychosocial assessments;
        (iii) Lead mental health promotion initiatives;
        (iv) Coordinate referrals to appropriate service providers;
         (v)  Support case management involving learners requiring specialized
              intervention;
        (vi) Provide technical guidance to school personnel on learner well-being;
              and
       (vii) Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the
              Department.

   (c) School Counselor Associates shall assist the School Counselor in
       implementing learner support programs and shall help ensure the accessibility
       of guidance and psychosocial services.

   (d) Learner Formation Officer. — Consistent with Republic Act No. 10627, or the
       Anti-Bullying Act of 2013, the Learner Formation Officer shall serve as the
       primary focal person for learner formation and the first responder to reports of
       bullying and other learner-related incidents. The Learner Formation Officer
       shall:

     (i)   Receive, document, and facilitate the appropriate initial response to
           reports of bullying, peer violence, harassment, and other incidents
           involving learners;
    (ii)   Ensure that reported incidents are promptly referred to the School Head,
           guidance or counseling personnel, School Care Center, Child Protection
           Committee, or other appropriate authorities, as warranted;
   (iii)   Coordinate the implementation of preventive and formative programs

   (iv)    Support the implementation of learner-led and peer-based initiatives,

    (v)    Promote learner awareness of their rights and responsibilities, the

(e) School Safety Officer. — Every school shall designate a School Safety Officer

     (iii) Coordinate emergency preparedness activities;
     (iv) Oversee school safety inspections;
      (v)  Coordinate reporting and documentation;
     (vi) Facilitate coordination with government agencies and emergency
           responders;
    (vii) Support school safety training and drills; and
   (viii) Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
           Pending the creation of plantilla positions as may be authorized by law,
           the School Safety Officer may be designated from among qualified
           existing school personnel.

(f) School Health Personnel. — School nurses, school physicians, school dentists,
    and other qualified school health personnel shall provide health services and
    support school health and wellness programs consistent with existing laws and
    DepEd policies. They shall:
      (i) Provide appropriate school health services;
     (ii) Participate in emergency preparedness and response;
    (iii) Support mental health and psychosocial interventions;

                                       11
      (iv)   Assist in health promotion and disease prevention activities;
       (v)   Coordinate referrals to health facilities; and
      (vi)   Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

   (g) Security Personnel. — Security guards and other authorized security personnel
       shall help maintain a safe and secure school environment. They shall:
         (i) Regulate access to school premises;
        (ii) Implement visitor management procedures;
       (iii) Assist in emergency response;
       (iv) Report security incidents;
        (v)   Support the implementation of campus safety measures; and
       (vi) Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
              Schools shall be provided with adequate security guards or trained

   (h) Classroom Adviser. — The Classroom Adviser shall promote learner well-
       being within the classroom and serve as the primary point of contact for

          (i) Monitor learner well-being and classroom climate;
         (ii) Identify learners requiring additional support;
        (iii) Coordinate with the School Counselor and other school personnel on

        (iv) Maintain communication with parents or guardians regarding learner

         (v)  Promote positive discipline and learner participation; and
        (vi) Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

             In recognition of their critical role in safeguarding the safety and well-
             being of learners, every Classroom Adviser shall be provided with the

The DepEd shall ensure that the corresponding teaching load reduction, staffing
requirements, and funding necessary to implement this provision are reflected in its
staffing standards and annual appropriations.

(f) LGUs. — LGUs shall support implementation of school safety programs,
    participate in local emergency planning, strengthen community partnerships,

The DepEd may enter into partnerships with other government agencies, state

SEC. 16. Community Engagement. — Schools shall promote meaningful
participation among learners, parents or guardians, families, LGUs, community
organizations, civil society organizations, and other stakeholders in the planning,
implementation, monitoring, and continuous improvement of school safety programs.
Community participation shall complement and strengthen school-based efforts to
create safe, secure, inclusive, supportive, and resilient learning environments.

Schools shall establish appropriate mechanisms to ensure that stakeholders are
regularly informed, consulted, and engaged in school safety initiatives.

   (a) Parents and Guardians. — Parents and legal guardians shall be recognized as
       essential partners of schools in promoting the safety, well-being, and holistic
       development of learners. Parents and guardians shall be encouraged to
       support the implementation of school safety policies, reinforce positive
       behavior and learner well-being at home, promptly communicate safety
       concerns to the school, participate in school safety orientations, consultations,
       and emergency preparedness activities, and cooperate with school authorities
       in implementing interventions affecting their children.

      Schools shall establish mechanisms to ensure meaningful parent and guardian
      participation in the implementation of the National Comprehensive School
      Safety Framework through regular communication, consultation, and
      collaborative interventions.

   (b) Parents-Teachers Association (PTA). — The Parents-Teachers Association
       shall serve as the primary organized partner of the school in promoting school
       safety and learner well-being.

      The PTA shall participate in the development, implementation, and periodic
      review of the School Safety Plan, assist in promoting school safety awareness
      among parents and families, support school programs on school safety, assist
      the school in mobilizing community participation and volunteer support for
      school safety initiatives, and provide feedback on school safety concerns
      affecting learners and parents, subject to guidelines prescribed by the DepEd.

   (c) Child Protection Committee (CPC). — Every school shall establish a Child
       Protection Committee (CPC), composed of the School Head, School Safety

                                          14
      Officer, School Counselor, and designated representatives of teachers,
      parents, learners, and the community. The CPC shall support the school in the
      prevention of and response to child abuse, violence, exploitation,
      discrimination, bullying, and other child protection concerns. The CPC shall, as
      may be applicable, assist in the implementation of child protection policies and
      preventive programs; facilitate the appropriate handling, referral, and follow- up
      of child protection concerns; coordinate with relevant school personnel, parents
      or guardians, the Barangay Council for the Protection of Children (BCPC), the
      City or Municipal Social Welfare and Development Office (C/MSWDO), and
      other appropriate authorities; and recommend measures to strengthen the
      school’s child protection mechanisms.

      The establishment or functioning of a CPC shall not relieve the School Head or
      any other responsible school personnel of their duties and accountability under
      this Act and other applicable child protection laws, rules, and policies.

The DepEd shall encourage and facilitate community partnerships that strengthen the
implementation of this Act.

                                 TITLE IV
                              PREVENTION
      CHAPTER I. School Safety Infrastructure and Protective Measures

SEC. 17. Safety Infrastructure and Equipment. — Schools shall be equipped with
appropriate safety infrastructure and security equipment, including, as applicable:

   (a) Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV). - CCTV cameras shall be installed in
       strategic areas, particularly entrances and exits, perimeter areas, common
       areas,andotherlocationsidentifiedthroughtheschool'sriskassessment.            CCTV
       shall not be installed in areas where there is a reasonable expectation of
       privacy. The collection, storage, access, use, and disposal of CCTV footage
       shall comply with applicable data privacy laws and regulations.
   (b) Screening Equipment. — Schools shall have access to appropriate screening
       equipment, including handheld metal detectors, where warranted by the
       school's risk profile. Screening shall be conducted by authorized and
       appropriately trained personnel in a reasonable, proportionate, non-
       discriminatory, and child-sensitive manner.
   (c) Visitor Management System. — Schools shall establish appropriate systems to
       regulate and monitor visitor entry into and movement within school premises,
       including visitor identification, registration, verification, and access control.
   (d) Security Personnel and Protective Presence. — Schools shall ensure
       appropriate security and protective presence based on their size, enrollment,
       location, and risk profile. This may include licensed security guards and



                                           15
       coordinated support from the Philippine National Police (PNP), barangay
       tanods, safety officers, community volunteers, and other authorized personnel.
   (e) Perimeter and Access Security. — Schools shall maintain appropriate physical
       measures to secure school boundaries and regulate access, including, as
       appropriate, gates, fencing, lighting, locks, barriers, and other protective
       infrastructure.

SEC. 18. Emergency, Health, and Life-Safety Equipment. — Schools shall be
equipped with appropriate emergency, health, and life-safety equipment, including, as
applicable:

   (a) first aid kids and essential emergency medical supplies;
   (b) fire extinguishers and other required fire safety equipment;
   (c) emergency lighting, flashlights, batteries, emergency power supplies, and other
       disaster preparedness supplies;
   (d) rescue and evacuation equipment appropriate to identified hazards; and
   (e) emergency communication and alert systems, including public address
       systems, alarms, emergency notification systems, two-way radios, and other
       equipment.

SEC. 19. Preventive Safety Measures. — Every school shall implement preventive
safety measures appropriate to its circumstances to reduce risks, strengthen
preparedness, and promote a safe and secure learning environment. At a minimum,
schools shall implement:

   (a) Risk Mapping and Assessment. — Regularly identify physical, social,
       behavioral, environmental, and digital risks, including locations and
       circumstances that may increase the likelihood of violence, bullying, abuse,
       harassment, accidents, emergencies, or other threats. Findings shall inform the
       School Safety Plan and preventive interventions.
   (b) Campus Security Monitoring. — Maintain appropriate monitoring measures,
       including security patrols, monitoring of entrances and exits, observation of
       common and high-risk areas, and review of security camera footage where
       available, consistent with the school's risk profile and applicable privacy and
       child protection laws.
   (c) Visitor Screening. — Establish procedures for visitor identification, registration,
       verification, issuance of passes where appropriate, and monitoring of visitor
       access, while ensuring access for emergency responders and other authorized
       persons.
   (d) Reasonable Screening and Inspection. — Conduct screening of persons and
       belongings, including bag inspections or use of metal detectors, where
       authorized and warranted by the school's risk assessment and applicable
       DepEd standards. Screening shall be conducted by authorized personnel in a



                                           16
       respectful, non-discriminatory, child-sensitive, and developmentally
       appropriate manner.
   (e) Confiscation and Disposition of Prohibited Items. — Establish procedures for
       the identification, confiscation, documentation, safekeeping, turnover, and
       lawful disposition of prohibited items, including appropriate due process,
       notification of parents or guardians when necessary, and coordination with
       authorities when required by law.
   (f) Emergency Drills. — Regularly conduct age-appropriate, developmentally
       appropriate, and inclusive drills for fires, earthquakes, severe weather, medical
       emergencies, and other relevant hazards. Where appropriate, drills may
       address active threats and other security incidents, subject to DepEd standards
       and relevant protocols.
   (g) Safety Orientation and Awareness. — Provide age-appropriate and role-
       appropriate safety orientation for learners, school personnel, and, where
       appropriate, parents, guardians, and other members of the school community,
       including relevant safety policies, emergency procedures, reporting
       mechanisms, and proper use of safety equipment.
   (h) Safety Audits and Preventive Maintenance. — Conduct an annual School
       Safety Audit and regular inspections of infrastructure, facilities, equipment,
       emergency systems, and other safety measures, and implement corrective and
       preventive measures based on identified hazards and deficiencies. Findings
       shall inform the School Safety Plan and resource priorities.

SEC. 20. General Standards for School Safety Measures. — All school safety
policies, infrastructure, equipment, and measures under this Chapter shall be
appropriate to the school's size, enrollment, location, risk profile, available resources,
and specific needs.

The DepEd, in coordination with relevant national government agencies and other
stakeholders, shall establish national standards and operational guidelines for the
provision, implementation, use, maintenance, and monitoring of infrastructure and
equipment under this Chapter, including, where applicable, their installation,
inspection, replacement, and data management and disposal.

                CHAPTER II. Safety During Off-Campus Activities

SEC. 21. Safety During Off-Campus Activities. — Schools conducting authorized
off-campus activities shall adopt and implement measures to ensure the safety,
security, health, and well-being of learners and school personnel participating in such
activities.

Prior to any authorized off-campus activity, the school shall conduct an appropriate
risk assessment and prepare an activity-specific safety plan, which shall include
learner supervision arrangements and designated personnel-in-charge, transportation

                                           17
safety measures, emergency communication and response procedures, emergency
contact information for participating learners, and such other safety measures as may
be prescribed by DepEd.

Schools shall also ensure full disclosure to parents or guardians of information
regarding the activity, including the objectives of the itinerary, transport arrangements,
accommodations, identified risks, emergency procedures, and contact information of
responsible personnel. Schools shall likewise ensure that appropriate informed
consent is secured in accordance with applicable laws and DepEd policies.

The DepEd shall prescribe minimum operational standards governing educational
tours, work immersion, athletic competitions, cultural activities, community
engagement activities, and other authorized off-campus activities.

          CHAPTER III. Digital Safety and Responsible Technology Use

SEC. 22. Digital Safety and Responsible Technology Use. — Schools shall adopt
and implement comprehensive policies and programs that promote safe, responsible,
ethical, and age-appropriate use of digital technologies in support of teaching,
learning, communication, and school operations.

Schools shall establish measures to protect learners and school personnel from
cyberbullying, online abuse, cybersecurity risks, and other forms of harm. Such
measures may include media and information literacy programs, orientation programs
for parents and guardians, procedures for reporting and responding to digital safety
incidents, and other digital safety measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

School heads may regulate or prohibit the use of portable electronic devices during
instructional periods, subject to exceptions for academic purposes, accessibility
needs, emergencies, and other legitimate purposes consistent with applicable DepEd
policies.

Implementation of this Section shall be consistent with applicable laws on child
protection, data privacy, cybersecurity, and online safety.

                       CHAPTER IV. LEARNER WELL-BEING

SEC. 23. Learner Well-Being and Psychosocial Support. — Schools shall
establish and implement comprehensive learner well-being and psychosocial support
programs that promote positive mental health, resilience, social and emotional
development, and learner engagement. These programs shall include:

   (a) Socio-Emotional Learning (SEL). — The DepEd shall institutionalize an SEL
       program within schools. This shall be in coordination with the School Care

                                           18
       Center, pursuant to Republic Act No. 12080. The program may include
       orientations for teachers and parents or guardians on mental health and
       emotional check-ins, learning sessions during homeroom periods on basic SEL
       processes, and the provision of resources to support mental and emotional
       health, including hotlines.

   (b) Peer Mentoring and Buddy Systems. — Schools may establish age-
       appropriate peer mentoring and buddy systems to foster positive relationships,
       social connectedness, mutual respect, and a sense of belonging among
       learners. Such programs may include peer mentoring and support networks,
       buddy systems for learners with disabilities or other learners requiring
       additional support, and other initiatives that promote inclusion, leadership,
       empathy, and shared responsibility within the school community.

       Peer mentors and buddies shall receive appropriate orientation, training, and
       adult supervision. These programs shall complement, and not replace, the
       responsibilities of teachers, guidance personnel, school counselors, or other
       school authorities in ensuring learner safety, well-being, and access to
       appropriate interventions and referral services.

   (c) Bystander and Upstander Programs. — Schools shall implement
       developmentally appropriate programs that educate learners on the role of
       upstanders and empower them to safely intervene in or report bullying and
       violence. These programs shall emphasize empathy, courage, and collective
       responsibility.

                                TITLE V RESPONSE

SEC. 24. Incident Reporting, Assessment and Immediate Response. — Every
school shall establish and maintain responsive mechanisms and protocols for the
reporting, assessment, protection interventions, and immediate response to school
safety concerns involving learners, school personnel, and other members of the
school community. Such mechanisms shall form part of the School Safety Plan and
shall be implemented in accordance with this Act and applicable laws.

Schools shall ensure that the implementation of this Section is guided by the principles
of child protection and the best interests of the child, confidentiality and respect for
privacy, timely intervention, trauma-informed and developmentally appropriate
practices, due process, coordinated inter-agency response, and survivor- centered,
rights-based, and restorative approaches, where appropriate.
Schools shall ensure the availability of multiple reporting channels, which may include
in-person reporting, anonymous reporting systems, digital reporting platforms,
designated reporting officers, emergency hotlines, and such other reporting
mechanisms as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

                                          19
Upon receipt of a report, the school shall:
  (a) Immediately record the report;
  (b) Conduct an initial risk assessment;
  (c) Determine whether immediate protective measures are necessary;
  (d) Notify the School Head and appropriate school personnel;
  (e) Notify parents or guardians, where appropriate;
  (f) Activate emergency response procedures when necessary;
  (g) Document actions taken; and
  (h) Determine whether referral or case management should be initiated.

Where there is imminent danger to life, safety, or security, schools shall immediately
coordinate with emergency responders and appropriate government authorities.

Every school shall ensure that all members of the school community are informed of
the reporting mechanisms, available support services, and emergency procedures
established under this Act. Schools shall likewise ensure that reporting mechanisms
are accessible to learners with disabilities and other learners requiring additional
support.

Every school shall maintain updated emergency contact information and establish
procedures for the immediate notification of parents or guardians, emergency
responders, LGUs, and other appropriate authorities during emergencies and critical
incidents.

SEC. 25. Referral, Case Management, and Coordination. — Every school shall
establish a formal case management and referral system for learners requiring
continuing intervention and support.

The system shall provide for:
   (a) Designation of case managers, where appropriate;
   (b) Multidisciplinary case conferences involving relevant school personnel;
   (c) Counseling and psychosocial interventions;
   (d) Referral to hospitals, health professionals, psychologists, social workers, child
       protection authorities, law enforcement agencies, or other service providers;
   (e) Development of individualized intervention plans, where appropriate;
   (f) Regular monitoring and follow-up;
   (g) Coordination with parents or guardians;
   (h) Documentation and secure case records; and
   (i) Safe reintegration into the school community.

The DepEd shall establish standardized case management forms, referral
protocols, documentation procedures, and inter-agency coordination mechanisms to
promote consistency across schools.

                                          20
Schools shall coordinate with the DSWD, the DOJ, the PNP, and LGUs for the proper
handling of cases, including children in conflict with the law, and for the provision of
medical, legal, and psychosocial support.

                                  TITLE VI
                        RECOVERY AND REINTEGRATION

SEC. 26. Recovery and Reintegration. — Schools shall establish and implement
recovery and reintegration measures for learners, school personnel, and other
affected members of the school community.
Recovery measures shall aim to restore safety, promote healing, minimize disruption
to learning, strengthen resilience, and facilitate the safe return of affected persons to
school.

Schools shall, at a minimum:
   (a) Provide timely counseling, psychosocial support, psychological first aid, and
       other appropriate interventions;
   (b) Develop individualized intervention and reintegration plans for learners or
       school personnel requiring continuing support, where appropriate;
   (c) Implement academic accommodations and learning continuity measures for
       affected learners;
   (d) Engage parents or guardians in planning and supporting the recovery of
       affected learners;
   (e) Coordinate referrals to appropriate government agencies, health professionals,
       and community-based service providers;
   (f) Monitor the progress of affected learners and school personnel following
       reintegration;
   (g) Implement measures to prevent retaliation, stigmatization, discrimination, or re-
       traumatization; and
   (h) Conduct post-incident reviews to identify lessons learned and improve school
       safety systems.

Recovery and reintegration measures shall be developmentally appropriate,
trauma-informed, culturally responsive, and consistent with the best interests of the
child.

SEC. 27. National Support for School Recovery and Continuous Improvement.
— The DepEd shall establish a National School Recovery and Support Program to
strengthen the capacity of schools to respond to and recover from incidents affecting
school safety and learner well-being.

The Program shall include:
   (a) Technical assistance to affected schools;

                                           21
   (b) Deployment of specialized psychosocial support teams, where appropriate;
   (c) Development of standardized recovery and reintegration protocols;
   (d) Capacity-building for school personnel on post-incident recovery;
   (e) Technical assistance for learning continuity and academic recovery;
   (f) Collection and analysis of post-incident data and lessons learned;
   (g) Dissemination of good practices and model recovery interventions; and
   (h) Such other support measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

The DepEd shall coordinate with the DOH, the DSWD, LGUs, and other appropriate
agencies to strengthen community-based recovery and reintegration services.

Subject to the availability of appropriations, the DepEd shall prioritize technical
assistance and specialized support for schools significantly affected by major
emergencies, disasters, violence, or other critical incidents.

The DepEd may organize or designate regional or division-level multidisciplinary
support teams to provide technical assistance, psychosocial support, crisis
intervention, and other specialized services to schools affected by major incidents,
emergencies, or disasters.

                                 TITLE VII
                     PROTECTION OF SCHOOL PERSONNEL

SEC. 28. Protection and Support for School Personnel. — All schools shall ensure
that school personnel receive appropriate protection, support, and resources
necessary to safely and effectively perform their responsibilities under this Act.

Such measures shall include:
   (a) Access to counseling, psychosocial support, employee wellness, and mental
       health services;
   (b) Training and continuing professional development on school safety, learner
       protection, emergency preparedness, and crisis management;
   (c) Appropriate legal assistance and institutional support for school personnel
       acting in good faith in the performance of official duties;
   (d) Protection against retaliation, intimidation, harassment, or threats arising from
       the implementation of school safety measures;
   (e) Provision of appropriate personal protective equipment, emergency equipment,
       and occupational safety measures where necessary;
   (f) Access to post-incident debriefing and recovery support following critical
       incidents;
   (g) Recognition of school safety responsibilities in workload planning and
       administrative support; and
   (h) Such other measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.



                                          22
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish existing rights, benefits, and
protections available to school personnel under existing laws.

                              TITLE VIII
             MONITORING, EVALUATION, AND ACCOUNTABILITY

SEC. 29. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Accountability. —The DepEd shall establish
a comprehensive national monitoring and evaluation system to assess compliance
with this Act, measure the effectiveness of school safety programs, and support
evidence- based planning, policymaking, budgeting, and continuous improvement.

The monitoring and evaluation system shall include:
   (a) Monitoring of compliance with the National Comprehensive School Safety
       Framework and the minimum standards established under this Act;
   (b) Periodic review of School Safety Plans and School Safety Handbooks;
   (c) Assessment of school safety infrastructure, equipment, personnel, and
       services;
   (d) Monitoring of school safety incidents, trends, response times, recovery
       outcomes, and implementation of corrective actions;
   (e) Collection and analysis of data on learner well-being, school climate, violence
       prevention, bullying, digital safety, emergency preparedness, and other school
       safety indicators;
   (f) Monitoring of capacity-building activities and competency development of
       school personnel;
   (g) Periodic compliance assessments of public and private schools; and
   (h) Such other monitoring mechanisms as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

SEC. 30. National School Safety Information System. — The DepEd shall establish
and maintain a National School Safety Information System to support planning,
monitoring, research, policy development, and resource allocation under this Act.

The System shall collect, consolidate, analyze, and report information on:
   (a) Incidents involving violence, bullying, abuse, discrimination, harassment,
       emergencies, disasters, and other school safety concerns;
   (b) Learner well-being, school climate, and behavioral trends;
   (c) Implementation of school safety programs, infrastructure, equipment, and
       personnel;
   (d) Emergency preparedness and disaster resilience;
   (e) Digital safety and technology-related incidents;
   (f) Implementation of recovery and reintegration measures;
   (g) Capacity-building and professional development activities;
   (h) School safety performance indicators; and
   (i) Such other information as may be prescribed by the DepEd.



                                         23
Information collected under this Section shall be used to support evidence-based
policymaking, strategic planning, budget preparation, program evaluation, and
continuous improvement of school safety programs.

The collection, processing, storage, sharing, and publication of information under this
Section shall comply with applicable laws on child protection, confidentiality, and data
privacy.

SEC. 31. Annual School Safety Report. — Every school shall submit an Annual
School Safety Report to the DepEd, through the Schools Division Office, summarizing
the implementation of its School Safety Plan, including the status of school safety
implementation, significant incidents and trends, actions taken, implementation
challenges, resource needs, and such other information as may be prescribed by the
DepEd.

The Schools Division Office shall review the reports submitted by schools and provide
appropriate technical assistance, recommendations, and other support necessary to
address identified gaps and strengthen the implementation of School Safety Plans.

The DepEd shall prescribe the form, content, submission schedule, and reporting
procedures for the Annual School Safety Report.

SEC. 32. National School Safety Report to Congress. — The DepEd shall submit
a National School Safety Report to Congress every two years summarizing the
nationwide implementation of this Act and the National Comprehensive School Safety
Framework, including national school safety trends, significant findings,
implementation status, emerging challenges, and recommendations for policy,
programmatic, and budgetary improvements.

The National School Safety Report shall be made publicly available, subject to
applicable laws on child protection, confidentiality, and data privacy.

                            TITLE IX FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 33. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DepEd. Thereafter,
such sums as may be necessary for the full implementation of this Act shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 34. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The DepEd, in coordination with
the DOH, DSWD, DILG, and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations of this Act within sixty (60) days from its effectivity.




                                            24
SEC. 35. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder not otherwise affected shall remain valid and
subsisting.

SEC. 36. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, rules, regulations, or parts
thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 37. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
