                             AN ACT
PROHIBITING ALL PUBLIC TELECOMMUNICATIONS ENTITIES AND
INFORMATION AND COMMUNICATIONS TECHNOLOGY PROVIDERS FROM
IMPOSING AN EXPIRATION PERIOD ON THE VALIDITY OF PREPAID LOAD
                  CREDITS AND THEIR FORFEITURE

                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

       It hardly needs statistical proof to recognize that nearly every Filipino has, at
some point in their lives, maintained a prepaid mobile phone subscription, and most
continue to be subscribers of such services today.

        Before the broad availability of the internet, Filipinos were able to become a
collective, interconnected people largely through mobile phone subscriptions. In fact,
the mobile phone subscription industry remains one of the most pervasive in the
country.

       In the 2024 Annual Report of the National Telecommunications Office (NTC),1
the subscriber base for mobile prepaid users among the three (3) largest
telecommunications industry players was reported as follows: PLDT with 56,731,489
subscribers, Globe with 57,714,593 subscribers, and the newest entrant, DITO,
already reaching 28,227,421 subscribers. For all of these consumers, the current
regulatory framework permits telecommunication companies to enforce expiration
dates on prepaid load credits, resulting in the forced forfeiture of billions of pesos in
consumer assets annually.

        This regime originated in Joint Memorandum Circular No. 05-12-2017, issued
jointly by the National Telecommunications Commission (NTC), Department of

Information and Communications Technology (DICT), and Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI). The Joint Circular imposed a uniform one-year validity period for all
prepaid loads regardless of the amount. Under this policy, consumers who purchase
load in advance must either use their credits or forfeit them, a result contrary to basic
consumer expectations that money spent should remain spendable until used.

      That policy is unfair and inequitable, as it forces consumers to use up their data
or credit allowances prematurely to avoid forfeiting them to service providers.
Consequently, the unilateral expiration of privately owned prepaid credits constitutes
a clear injustice, particularly given that the mechanism places the consumer at a
severe structural disadvantage.

       Thus, this proposed measure.

       This bill prohibits all public telecommunications entities and providers from
imposing expiration periods on prepaid load credits purchased by active subscribers.
The bill makes unlawful three specific acts: (1) imposition of any expiration period on
unused prepaid load credits; (2) forfeiture of such credits on active accounts, except
for mobile number portability purposes; and (3) refusal to refund forfeited credits
without valid cause.

       To address legitimate operational concerns, the bill includes a narrowly tailored
provision for dormant accounts, those inactive for a minimum period of one (1) year.
Such accounts may be deducted ₱1 per day of inactivity until credits are fully
consumed, allowing carriers to reclaim dormant numbers for reallocation while
preserving the vast majority of consumer assets. Subscribers must receive notification
of these daily deductions.

        The telecommunications industry, as vital as its contribution is to the national
economy, must be enjoined in protecting consumer rights. In the hopes of maintaining
social justice, even every peso that avails us of the ability to communicate in this day
and age must be safeguarded. The State may then intervene when the common good
requires, when the general welfare demands, precisely as this legislation does,
safeguarding the assets of millions of Filipino consumers and supporting the digital
economy's expansion into underserved populations.

        In the 20th Congress, Senators Sherwin “Win” T. Gatchalian and Jinggoy
Estrada filed similar measures as SBN 1095 and SBN 382, respectively. This version
fully adopts Senate President Gatchalian’s SBN 1095.

       The prompt passing of this measure is respectfully urged.

 1           SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Prepaid Load Forever
 2   Act."

 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   recognize the vital role of information and communications technology in nation-
 6   building. It likewise recognizes the primary role of the private sector as an engine of
 7   economic growth and guarantees the right of individuals and private entities to own,
 8   establish, and operate economic enterprises.
 9
10           However, as the Constitution provides that the use of property bears a social
11   function, the right of private entities is subject to the duty of the State to intervene when
12   the common good so requires – including protecting the interest of consumers,
13   promoting their general welfare, and establishing standards of conduct of business
14   and industry. Towards this end, the State shall implement measures to protect the
15   consumer against unfair and unconscionable sales acts and practices, and provide
16   information and education to facilitate sound choice and proper exercise of consumer
17   rights.

 4         SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. - The following shall constitute prohibited acts of any
 5   PTE or ICT provider and are hereby declared to be unlawful:
 6
 7      a) Imposition of an expiration period on the validity of unused prepaid load credits,
 8         whether purchased via prepaid card or electronic load;
 9      b) Forfeiture of such prepaid load credits on an active prepaid account, save for
10         the purpose of availing of the mobile number portability service; and
11      c) Refusal to give a refund to any prepaid subscriber whose load credits were
12         forfeited without any valid cause.
13
14         In view of paragraph (b), a prepaid account that has become dormant shall have
15   one (1) peso deducted for every day of inactivity from its remaining load credits until
16   such credits are fully consumed. The prepaid subscriber must be notified of the
17   deduction made.
18
19          SEC 5. Penal Provision. - Any director, officer, employee, or agent of a PTE or
20   ICT provider providing prepaid telecommunications services who shall violate any of
21   the acts mentioned under Section 4 hereof shall, upon conviction, be subject to the
22   penalty of a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (PhP100,000.00) but
23   not exceeding One Million Pesos (PhP1,000,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than
24   two (2) years but not more than six (6) years, or both, upon the discretion of the court.
25
26       If the violation was committed by or in the interest of a juridical person duly
27   licensed to engage in business in the Philippines, the following penalties shall be
28   imposed on the juridical entity:
29
30      a) First offense – a fine of Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (PhP500,000.00);
31      b) Second offense – a fine of One Million Pesos (PhP1,000,000.00) plus
32         suspension of license to engage in business for a period of thirty (30) days; and
33      c) Third offense – a fine of Two Million Pesos (PhP2,000,000.00) plus immediate
34         revocation of license to engage in business.
