                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This proposed measure seeks to uphold the State’s constitutional duty to
defend and protect its citizens, particularly women and children, from violence and
abuse that destroy the very foundation of familial harmony. As a necessary
consequence of an abusive and traumatic marriage, this bill shifts the focus entirely
toward protecting the victim-survivor considering how there is absolutely no reason
for them to remain legally bound to a union that has turned violent.

        According to data gathered by the World Health Organization (WHO) in the
years 2000 to 2023, 25.8% of women (roughly 682 million), from the ages 15 to 49,
have suffered from physical or sexual intimate partner violence at least once in their
lifetime. Even with the legal safeguards under Republic Act (R.A.) No. 9262, otherwise
known as the "Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act of 2004", violence
against women appears to be one of the country’s most pervasive social problems that
rattles and disturbs familial relationships.

        This piece of legislation inserts a new Article into Executive Order No. 209,
otherwise known as the Family Code of the Philippines, explicitly mandating that when
a spouse is convicted by final judgment of any of the crimes of violence enumerated
under Section 5 of R.A. No. 9262, the marriage contracted by the offending spouse
shall be automatically dissolved. Furthermore, it mandates that the convicted
husband's entire share in the conjugal partnership or community property will be
forfeited in favor of his wife and children.

       The bill also inserts a new section into the Anti-Violence Against Women and
Their Children Act of 2004 that mandates the dissolution of a marriage upon the
conviction of the accused. The resulting dissolution of the marriage will legally carry
the exact same effects as a formal annulment under the Family Code of the Philippines.
       This measure does not attempt to diminish the sanctity of marriage, but rather
it recognizes that the country has the duty to protect the lives of its citizens by
providing these victims of abuse a way out, especially for those trapped in abusive
marital unions. In this way, the State actively prioritizes the safety, dignity and
recovery of the survivor over the preservation of an abusive partnership.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     AUTOMATIC GROUND FOR MARRIAGE DISSOLUTION, AMENDING FOR THE
     PURPOSE EXECUTIVE ORDER NO. 209, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
     FAMILY CODE OF THE PHILIPPINES, AND REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9262,
     OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE ANTI-VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND THEIR
     CHILDREN ACT OF 2004
