                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

Agriculture is facing unprecedented challenges. Rapid urbanization, land
degradation, climate change, and unpredictable weather patterns are
continuously shrinking arable land and threatening food security. Traditional
farming methods, while foundational, are becoming increasingly vulnerable to
these environmental pressures.

To safeguard food sovereignty and boost economic productivity, we must
transition toward climate-resilient, space-efficient, and technology-driven
agricultural practices. Aeroponics (growing plants in an air/mist environment),
hydroponics (growing plants in a nutrient-rich water solution), and aquaponics
(a symbiotic system combining aquaculture and hydroponics) represent the
frontier of modern sustainable farming.

These methods provide key advantages over traditional farming, using up to 90%
less water with closed-loop systems. They reduce the need for arable land, making
vertical and urban farming feasible. Closed environments lower risks of soil-borne
pests and diseases, minimizing chemical pesticide use. Additionally, they enable
continuous cultivation of high-value crops, vegetables, and fish, regardless of
weather conditions.

This Bill aims to establish a national program promoting aeroponics,
hydroponics, and aquaponics in rural and urban areas. By investing in these
technologies, offering incentives to agri-tech entrepreneurs, and creating
community farming hubs, it seeks to boost the income of fishers and farmers,
ensure a steady supply of high-value vegetables and fish, and promote climate-
smart agriculture.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT PROMOTING THE USE OF AEROPONICS, HYDROPONICS, AND
AQUAPONICS FOR THE PRODUCTION OF HIGH-VALUE FISH, CROPS, AND
VEGETABLES, PROVIDING INCENTIVES THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Modern Agri-
Technology and Soilless Farming Promotion Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to promote sustainable, resilient, and high-yielding agricultural practices to
ensure food security, optimize natural resources, and improve the livelihoods of
farmers and fishers. In pursuit of this, the State shall actively support the
research, development, commercialization, and widespread adoption of
innovative, land-independent agricultural systems, specifically aeroponics,
hydroponics, and aquaponics.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

      •   Aeroponics refers to a plant cultivation technique where roots are
          suspended in a closed or semi-closed environment and periodically
          sprayed with a nutrient-rich water mist.

      •   Hydroponics refers to the method of growing plants without soil, using
          mineral nutrient solutions in a water solvent or an inert medium like
          perlite, gravel, or coconut coir.

      •   Aquaponics refers to a sustainable food production system that
          combines aquaculture (raising aquatic animals) with hydroponics in a
          symbiotic, recirculating ecosystem.
      •   High-Value Fish and Crops refer to agricultural commodities that fetch
          higher market prices, have high nutritional value, or possess significant
          export potential, including but not limited to leafy greens, berries, vine
          crops, herbs, tilapia, catfish, and ornamental fish.

      SEC. 4. The National Modern Agri-Technology Program. – There is hereby
established a National Modern Agri-Technology Program (the "Program") under
the Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the Department of
Science and Technology (DOST) and the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI).

      The Program shall focus on:

   (a) Establishing modern research and development hubs for high-value
       soilless production.

   (b) Providing technical training and extension services to smallholder farmers,
       fisherfolk, and urban communities.

   (c) Creating standard blueprints for low-cost, locally fabricated aeroponic,
   hydroponic, and aquaponic setups.

      SEC. 5. Urban and Community Farming Integration. – The Department of
the Interior and Local Government (DILG), in cooperation with Local Government
Units (LGUs), shall integrate these modern farming practices into urban planning
and community livelihood programs. Idle public lands, rooftops of government
buildings, and community centers shall be evaluated for conversion into localized
aquaponic and hydroponic food hubs.

      SEC. 6. Incentives for Agri-Tech Entrepreneurs and Cooperatives. – To
accelerate private sector participation, registered individuals, cooperatives, and
enterprises utilizing certified aeroponic, hydroponic, or aquaponic systems for
commercial production shall be entitled to the following incentives:

   (a) Tax- and duty-free importation of specialized farming equipment, solar
       arrays, water filtration systems, and sensors not locally available.

   (b) Accelerated depreciation of plant equipment and infrastructure dedicated
       to soilless farming.

   (c) Preferential low-interest   loan   windows    from   government     financial
       institutions.
      SEC. 7. Research, Development, and Education. – The DOST, through its
research institutes and in partnership with State Universities and Colleges
(SUCs), shall prioritize research into nutrient solution optimization, renewable
energy integration for indoor farms, and native fish species compatibility in
aquaponic systems. The Department of Education (DepEd) shall incorporate
basic principles of soilless farming into secondary school agricultural curricula.

       SEC. 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement the
provisions of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations
of the Department of Agriculture. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for
its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

      SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Agriculture, in consultation with
the DOST, DTI, and private sector stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary
rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

       Sec. 10. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      Sec. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
presidential issuances, and other administrative rules and regulations or parts
thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

      Sec. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
