                           AN ACT
GRANTING AN AMNESTY ON UNPAID INTERNAL REVENUE TAXES
IMPOSED BY THE NATIONAL GOVERNMENT FOR TAXABLE YEAR 2026 AND
PRIOR YEARS WITH RESPECT TO ESTATE TAX, OTHER INTERNAL
REVENUE TAXES, AND TAX ON DELINQUENCIES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Filipino families, workers, entrepreneurs, and businesses are presently
confronting an unusually difficult economic environment.

        The continuing effects of elevated prices, higher energy and transportation
costs, disruptions in international trade and fuel markets, and slower economic
growth have placed considerable pressure on household finances and business
activity. Headline inflation remained elevated at 6.2 percent in July 2026, while
economic growth slowed to 2.3 percent in the second quarter of 2026. These conditions
are felt not merely in economic statistics, but in the everyday cost of food, electricity,
transportation, housing, education, and the operation of small and medium
enterprises.

       Government policy must therefore strike a careful balance. Revenue must
continue to be raised for essential public services and national development, but
taxation should not become an obstacle to the recovery and continued economic
participation of otherwise productive citizens and enterprises.

      This policy direction was expressly articulated by President Ferdinand R.
Marcos Jr. during his 2026 State of the Nation Address. Recognizing the burden borne
not only by low-income families but also by workers, the middle class, and small
businesses, the President called upon Congress to pursue tax relief measures that
promote economic growth, generate revenue, and advance equity. More particularly,



                                                                                         1
the President expressly called for an amnesty covering unpaid taxes, including income
tax, estate tax, donor's tax, value-added tax, and the penalties attached thereto.

       This bill is modeled upon previous tax amnesty laws, but updated with
additional safeguards against fraud and abuse in order to better attain the policy
objectives of the current administration. Among the concerns raised were the
following: the continued restrictions imposed by bank secrecy laws in cases of
suspected fraud, the absence of an adequate framework for the automatic exchange of
tax information, and insufficient mechanisms to verify the truthfulness of declarations
of assets and net worth.

       Certain provisions involving the previous attempts for different versions of
Estate Tax Amnesty were likewise changed. In particular, the issue over allowing
several successive unsettled estates to be discharged through a single tax computation
based only upon the last decedent, notwithstanding that estate tax attaches at each
taxable transfer. Additionally, provisions regarding conclusive presumption of
correctness, originally granted to Estate Tax Amnesty Returns, has been balanced with
a safeguard in order to prevent a situation wherein the Government is deprived of a
reasonable opportunity to verify ownership, valuation, succession, and other material
information.

       Congress subsequently enacted Republic Act Nos. 11569 and 11956,
progressively extending and simplifying the Estate Tax Amnesty. These amendments
recognized that an effective amnesty must be accessible to ordinary taxpayers. They
allowed easier filing, reduced documentary barriers, separated payment of estate
taxes from the later process of transferring property, and permitted installment
payments.

       This bill builds upon all of these legislative developments.

        First, it expands the coverage of the Estate Tax Amnesty to estates of decedents
who died on or before December 31, 2026, and grants taxpayers two (2) years from the
effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations within which to avail themselves
of the program.

       Second, it reinstates the General Tax Amnesty originally contemplated under
Republic Act No. 11213 and updates its coverage to taxable year 2026 and all prior
years. Taxpayers are likewise granted two (2) years from the effectivity of the
implementing rules and regulations within which to avail themselves of the amnesty.

         To encourage taxpayers to come forward promptly, the bill preserves the
principle of the original early-availment incentive. A taxpayer who avails during the
first six months receives a twenty percent reduction in the amnesty tax; those availing
during the succeeding six months receive a fifteen percent reduction; those availing
during the third six-month period receive a ten percent reduction; and those availing
during the final six months may still obtain the amnesty but without an additional
reduction.




                                                                                      2
     Third, the bill directly addresses the objections that prevented the General Tax
Amnesty from taking effect in 2019.

       Tax Amnesty Returns and declarations of assets and net worth will no longer
become conclusively and immediately unassailable upon filing. Instead, honest
taxpayers will enjoy a prima facie presumption of correctness, while the Bureau of
Internal Revenue is afforded a three-year period within which to conduct ordinary
verification. After this period, the amnesty becomes final and irrevocable, subject to
the taxpayer’s continuing liability for the full payment of the correct tax due if the
declarations are subsequently determined to contain an inaccuracy affecting the tax
due.

        Fraud, willful concealment, or material misrepresentation receives no
protection and may additionally give rise to revocation, civil penalties, and criminal
liability even if discovered beyond the three-year verification period.

       Where the amnesty itself was obtained through fraud, willful concealment, or
material misrepresentation, the special protection of the amnesty may be revoked.
Consistent with the long-established principle under the National Internal Revenue
Code applicable to fraudulent returns, the Government is given ten (10) years from
the discovery of the fraud within which to pursue the appropriate action.

       This distinction is essential. The bill forgives tax violations committed during
the periods covered by the amnesty; otherwise there would be no amnesty at all. But
it does not forgive a new and separate fraud committed for the purpose of
fraudulently obtaining the amnesty.

      Fourth, the bill requires every applicant for the General Tax Amnesty to execute
a waiver of the applicable bank secrecy laws for the entire three-year verification
period. Pursuant to such waiver, the Commissioner of Internal Revenue may, by
written order and without a prior finding of fraud or material misrepresentation,
inquire into the applicant’s financial accounts to verify the truthfulness, completeness,
and accuracy of the declarations made under the amnesty. Any inquiry shall remain
limited to verification of the amnesty and subject to strict confidentiality protections.

      The bill likewise expressly establishes authority for the receipt and automatic
exchange of tax and financial information pursuant to treaties and international
agreements binding upon the Philippines.

       Fifth, the bill strengthens the Government's capacity to detect fraudulent
amnesty applications by establishing a special informer's reward. A qualified
informer whose definite, sworn, previously unknown information actually results in
the recovery and collection of revenues arising from a fraudulent amnesty application
may receive ten percent (10%) of the amount actually recovered, subject to a ceiling of
Ten Million Pesos (P10,000,000.00). Appropriate safeguards are imposed to prevent
frivolous accusations, duplicate rewards, participation by accomplices, and misuse by
public officials.




                                                                                       3
       Sixth, the bill cures the objection involving successive unsettled estates.
Consolidated administrative processing may be permitted for convenience, but a
separate computation and payment must be made for every taxable estate and every
taxable transfer. Administrative convenience will therefore no longer operate to
extinguish taxes legally due from earlier transfers.

       Finally, the measure preserves strong confidentiality protections and due
process. Information voluntarily provided under the amnesty cannot generally be
used to prosecute the historical tax violation that Congress has chosen to forgive.
Exceptions are confined to verification of the amnesty itself, fraud committed in
obtaining it, and lawful international exchange of tax information. Wrongful
availment must be established through appropriate proceedings, with written notice
and an opportunity for the taxpayer to be heard.

      Tax amnesty must offer a genuine fresh start. If it is too restrictive, taxpayers
will not avail of it and dormant liabilities will remain uncollected. If it is too
permissive, it rewards concealment and undermines confidence in the tax system.

      This measure seeks the proper middle ground: meaningful relief for Filipinos
who wish to settle their obligations and return to full compliance with the tax laws,
paired with credible safeguards ensuring that the generosity of the State cannot itself
be exploited through fraud.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                                        TITLE I

                           PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Tax Amnesty Act of 2026.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
and enhance revenue administration and collection, and make the country's tax
system more equitable, by simplifying the tax compliance requirements. Towards this
end, the State shall:

(a) Provide a one-time opportunity to settle estate tax obligations through an estate
tax amnesty program that will give reasonable tax relief to estates with deficiency
estate taxes;

(b) Broaden the tax base by offering a General Tax Amnesty for all unpaid internal
revenue taxes that will help cleanse, organize, and improve the Bureau of Internal
Revenue's database;

      The General Tax Amnesty shall be implemented subject to appropriate
safeguards for the verification of declarations, limited inquiry into financial accounts
pursuant to the waiver required of applicants under this Act, exchange of information


                                                                                       5
for tax purposes, and such other measures as may be necessary to ensure the
truthfulness and completeness of declarations made under this Act;

(c) Enhance revenue collection by providing a tax amnesty on delinquencies that will
minimize administrative costs in pursuing tax cases and declog the dockets of the
Bureau of Internal Revenue and the courts; and

(d) Provide a more equitable tax system by adopting a comprehensive tax reform
program that will simplify the requirements on tax amnesties with the use of
simplified forms, and utilization of information technology in broadening the tax
base.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. As used in this Act:

(A) Basic tax assessed refers to the latest amount of tax assessment issued by the Bureau
of Internal Revenue against the taxpayer, exclusive of interest, penalties, and
surcharges;

(B) Net estate refers to the gross estate less all allowable deductions as provided in the
National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, or the applicable estate tax laws
prevailing at the time of death of the decedent;

(C) Net undeclared estate refers to the difference between the total net estate valued at
the time of death and the net estate previously declared with the Bureau of Internal
Revenue, if any;

(D) Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth refers to a declaration of the assets,
liabilities, and net worth as of December 31, 2026, as follows:

       (1) Assets within or without the Philippines, whether real or personal, tangible
       or intangible, whether or not used in trade or business: Provided, that property
       other than money shall be valued at the cost at which the property was
       acquired: Provided, further, that foreign currency assets and/or securities shall
       be valued at the rate of exchange prevailing as of the date of the Statement of
       Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth;

       (2) All existing liabilities which are legitimate and enforceable, secured or
       unsecured, whether or not incurred in trade or business; and

       (3) The net worth of the taxpayer, which shall be the difference between the
       total assets and total liabilities.

(E) Total asset refers to the amount of the aggregate assets whether within or without
the Philippines, real or personal, tangible or intangible, or ordinary or capital.

                            ESTATE TAX AMNESTY

SEC. 4. Coverage. — There is hereby authorized and granted a tax amnesty, hereinafter
called Estate Tax Amnesty, which shall cover the estate of decedents who died on or
before December 31, 2026, with or without assessments duly issued therefor, whose
estate taxes have remained unpaid or have accrued as of December 31, 2026: Provided,
however, that the Estate Tax Amnesty hereby authorized and granted shall not cover
instances enumerated under Section 9 hereof.

SEC. 5.Entitlement under Estate Tax Amnesty. — Except for instances covered by Section
9 hereof, the estate may enjoy the immunities and privileges of the Estate Tax Amnesty
and pay an estate amnesty tax at the rate of six percent (6%) based on the decedent's
total net estate at the time of death: Provided, that if an estate tax return was previously
filed with the Bureau of Internal Revenue, the estate tax rate of six percent (6%) shall
be based on net undeclared estate. The provisions of the National Internal Revenue
Code of 1997, as amended, or the applicable estate tax laws prevailing at the time of
death of the decedent, on valuation, manner of computation, and other related matters
shall apply suppletorily, at the time of the entitlement: Provided, further, that if the
allowable deductions applicable at the time of death of the decedent exceed the value
of the gross estate, the heirs, executors, or administrators may avail of the benefits of
tax amnesty under Title II of this Act, and pay the minimum estate amnesty tax of Five
thousand pesos (P5,000).

SEC. 6. Availment of the Estate Tax Amnesty; When and Where to File and Pay. — The

and Regulations issued pursuant to this Act, file, either electronically or manually,
with any authorized agent bank, revenue district office through the revenue collection
officer, or authorized tax software provider, a sworn Estate Tax Amnesty Return, in
such forms as may be prescribed in the Implementing Rules and Regulations.

        The payment of the amnesty tax shall be made either electronically or
manually, at the time the return is filed with any authorized agent bank, revenue
district office through the revenue collection officer, or authorized tax software
provider: Provided, that the appropriate Revenue District Officer shall issue and
endorse an Acceptance Payment Form, in such form as may be prescribed in the
Implementing Rules and Regulations of this Act for the authorized agent bank, or in
the absence thereof, the revenue collection agent or authorized tax software provider
concerned, to accept the tax amnesty payment;

the estate tax amnesty shall be limited to the following:

SEC. 7. Presumption of Correctness of the Estate Tax Amnesty Returns. —The Estate Tax
Amnesty Return shall, upon filing and full payment of the amount due, be prima
facie presumed true and correct, subject to verification by the Bureau of Internal
Revenue.

       The Bureau of Internal Revenue may, within three (3) years from the issuance
or deemed issuance of the Certificate of Availment, verify the ownership, succession,
valuation, deductions, and other material information contained in the Estate Tax
Amnesty Return and its supporting documents.

       Any notice of material discrepancy, proposed revocation, or other proceeding
arising from an ordinary verification under this Section shall be commenced within
such three (3)-year period.

      Upon expiration of the three (3)-year period without the commencement of any
such proceeding, the availment shall become final and irrevocable, except where the
availment was procured through fraud, willful concealment, or material


                                                                                        9
misrepresentation to reduce the amnesty tax or improperly obtain the benefits of the
Estate Tax Amnesty.

      Notwithstanding the three (3)-year verification period, any proceeding to
revoke an Estate Tax Amnesty procured through fraud, willful concealment, or
material misrepresentation, to assess or collect taxes becoming due by reason of such
revocation, or to impose applicable civil penalties may be commenced at any time
within ten (10) years after discovery of such fraud, willful concealment, or material
misrepresentation.

       The ten (10)-year period shall commence only upon discovery by the Bureau of
Internal Revenue of facts sufficient to constitute prima facie evidence of the fraud,
willful concealment, or material misrepresentation.

        For purposes of this Section, fraud, willful concealment, or material
misrepresentation shall refer to acts committed in the preparation, filing, or
procurement of the Estate Tax Amnesty for the purpose of reducing the amnesty tax
or improperly obtaining the benefits thereof. The mere historical failure to pay an
estate tax otherwise covered by the amnesty shall not, by itself, constitute fraud in the
availment of the amnesty.

       No revocation shall be made without written notice stating the factual and legal
basis therefor and affording the taxpayer, estate, heirs, executor, or administrator a
reasonable opportunity to submit evidence and be heard.

       The Acceptance Payment Form and the Estate Tax Amnesty Return shall be
submitted to the Revenue District Office after complete payment. The completion of
these requirements shall constitute compliance with the filing and payment
requirements of this Title, without prejudice to the verification and anti-fraud
provisions of this Section. A Certificate of Availment of the Estate Tax Amnesty shall
be issued by the Bureau of Internal Revenue within fifteen (15) calendar days from
submission to the Bureau of Internal Revenue of the Acceptance Payment Form and
the Estate Tax Amnesty Return. Otherwise, the duplicate copies of the Acceptance
Payment Form, stamped as received, and the Estate Tax Amnesty Return shall be
deemed as sufficient proof of availment.

       The issuance or deemed issuance of the Certificate of Availment shall be
without prejudice to the verification period and the fraud exception provided under
this Section.

SEC. 8. Immunities and Privileges. — Estates covered by the Estate Tax Amnesty, which
have fully complied with all the conditions set forth in this Act, including the payment
of the estate amnesty tax shall be immune from the payment of all estate taxes, as well
as any increments and additions thereto, arising from the failure to pay any and all
estate taxes for the period ending December 31, 2026 and prior years, and from all
appurtenant civil, criminal, and administrative cases and penalties under the National
Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.

SEC. 9. Exceptions. — The Estate Tax Amnesty under Title II of this Act shall not extend
to estate tax cases which shall have become final and executory and to properties
involved in cases pending in appropriate courts:

(A) Falling under the jurisdiction of the Presidential Commission on Good
Government;

(B) Involving unexplained or unlawfully acquired wealth under Republic Act No.
3019, otherwise known as the Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act, and Republic Act
No. 7080 or an Act Defining and Penalizing the Crime of Plunder;

(C) Involving violations of Republic Act No. 9160, otherwise known as the Anti-
Money Laundering Act, as amended;

(D) Involving tax evasion and other criminal offenses under Chapter II of Title X of
the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended; and

(E) Involving felonies of frauds, illegal exactions and transactions, and malversation
of public funds and property under Chapters III and IV of Title VII of the Revised
Penal Code.

                                       TITLE III

                             GENERAL TAX AMNESTY

SEC. 10. Coverage. — There is hereby authorized and granted a tax amnesty,
hereinafter called General Tax Amnesty, which shall cover all national internal
revenue taxes such as, but not limited to, income tax, withholding tax, capital gains
tax, donor's tax, value-added tax, other percentage taxes, excise tax and documentary
stamp tax collected by the Bureau of Internal Revenue, including value-added tax and
excise taxes collected by the Bureau of Customs for taxable year 2026 and prior years,
with or without assessments duly issued therefor, that have remained unpaid:
Provided, however, that the General Tax Amnesty hereby authorized and granted shall
not cover persons or cases enumerated under Section 19 and Title IV hereof.

SEC. 11. Entitlement under the General Tax Amnesty. — Except for the instances covered
in Section 19 hereof, any person, whether natural or juridical, may enjoy the


                                                                                      11
immunities and privileges of the General Tax Amnesty by paying, at the taxpayer's
option, an amnesty tax at:

(i) The rate of two percent (2%) based on the taxpayer's total assets as of December 31,
2026, as declared in the Statement of Total Assets; or

(ii) Based on the taxpayer's total net worth as of December 31, 2026, as declared in the
Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth filed pursuant to Section 12 hereof and
in accordance with the following schedule of amnesty tax rates and minimum
amnesty tax payments required:

       (a) Individuals (whether resident or nonresident citizens, including resident or
       nonresident aliens), Trusts, and Estates — five percent (5%) or Seventy-five
       thousand pesos (P75,000), whichever is higher;

       (b) Corporations:

              (1) With subscribed capital of above Fifty million pesos (P50,000,000) —
              five percent (5%) or One million pesos (P1,000,000), whichever is higher;

              (2) With subscribed capital of above Twenty million pesos (P20,000,000)
              up to Fifty million pesos (P50,000,000) — five percent (5%) or five
              hundred thousand pesos (P500,000), whichever is higher;

              (3) With subscribed capital of Five million pesos (P5,000,000) up to
              Twenty million pesos (P20,000,000) — five percent (5%) or Two hundred
              fifty thousand pesos (P250,000), whichever is higher;

              (4) With subscribed capital of below Five million pesos (P5,000,000) —
              five percent (5%) or One hundred thousand pesos (P100,000), whichever
              is higher;

       (c) Other juridical entities, including, but not limited to, cooperatives and
       foundations, that have become taxable as of December 31, 2026, — five percent
       (5%) or Seventy-five thousand pesos (P75,000), whichever is higher:

       Provided, that if the taxpayer opts to pay the amnesty tax based on total net
worth and the computed net worth is negative, the taxpayer may still avail of the
benefits of tax amnesty under this Title, and pay the minimum amnesty tax.

SEC. 12. Availment of the General Tax Amnesty; When and Where to File and Pay. — Any
person, natural or juridical, who wishes to avail of the General Tax Amnesty shall,
within two (2) years from the effectivity of the Implementing Rules and Regulations
issued pursuant to this Act, file with the appropriate office of the Bureau of Internal
Revenue, which has jurisdiction over the taxpayer, a sworn General Tax Amnesty
Return accompanied by a notarized Statement of Total Assets or notarized Statement
of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth, as the case may be, as of December 31, 2026,
together with a notarized waiver executed in accordance with Section 15 of this Act.
The payment of the amnesty tax shall be made at the time the return is filed:

SEC. 13. Contents of the Statement of Total Assets and Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and
Net Worth. —

(A) The Statement of Total Assets shall contain a declaration of the total assets as of
December 31, 2026, as follows:

       (1) Assets within or without the Philippines, whether real or personal, tangible
       or intangible, whether or not used in trade or business:

              (A) Real properties shall be accompanied by a description of their
              classification, exact location, and valued at acquisition cost, if acquired
              by purchase, or the zonal valuation or fair market value as shown in the
              schedule of values of the provincial, city or municipal assessors at the
              time of inheritance or donation, whichever is higher, if acquired through
              inheritance or donation;

              (B) Personal properties other than money, shall be accompanied by a
              specific description of the kind and number of assets (e.g. automobiles,
              shares of stock, etc.) Or other investments, indicating the acquisition cost
              less the accumulated depreciation or amortization, or the corresponding
              book value for shares of stock, in proper cases, if acquired by purchase,
              or the fair market price or value at the date of the Statement of Total
              Assets, if acquired through inheritance or donation;

              (C) Assets denominated in foreign currency shall be converted into the
              corresponding Philippine currency equivalent, at the rate of exchange
              prevailing as of the date of the Statement of Total Assets; and

              (D) Cash on hand and in bank in peso as of the date of the Statement of
              Total Assets, as well as cash on hand and in bank in foreign currency,
              converted to Philippine peso at the rate of exchange prevailing as of the
              date of the Statement of Total Assets.

(B) The Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth shall contain a true and
complete declaration of assets, liabilities, and net worth of the taxpayer as of
December 31, 2026, as follows:

       (1) Assets within or without the Philippines, whether real or personal, tangible
       or intangible, whether or not used in trade or business:

              (A) Real properties shall be accompanied by a description of their
              classification, exact location, and valued at acquisition cost, if acquired
              by purchase, or the zonal valuation or fair market value as shown in the



                                                                                         14
              schedule of values of the provincial, city or municipal assessors,
              whichever is higher, if acquired through inheritance or donation;

              (B) Personal properties other than money, shall be accompanied by a
              specific description of the kind and number of assets (e.g. automobiles,
              shares of stock, etc.) or other investments, indicating the acquisition cost
              less accumulated depreciation or amortization, or the corresponding
              book value for shares of stock, in proper cases, if acquired by purchase,
              or the fair market price or value at the date of the Statement of Assets,
              Liabilities, and Net Worth, if acquired through inheritance or donation;

              (C) Assets denominated in foreign currency shall be converted into the
              corresponding Philippine currency equivalent, at the rate of exchange
              prevailing as of the date of the Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net
              Worth; and

              (D) Cash on hand and in bank in peso as of the date of the Statement of
              Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth, as well as cash on hand and in bank
              in foreign currency, converted to Philippine peso at the rate of exchange
              prevailing as of the date of the Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net
              Worth.

       (2) All existing liabilities, which are legitimate and enforceable, secured or
       unsecured, whether or not incurred in trade or business, disclosing or
       indicating clearly the name and address of the creditor and the amount of the
       corresponding liability.

       (3) The total net worth of the taxpayer, which shall be the difference between
       the total assets and total liabilities.

SEC. 14. Presumption of Correctness of the Statement of Total Assets, and Statement of
Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth. — The Statement of Total Assets or the Statement of
Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth, filed at the option of the taxpayer, shall, upon filing
and full payment of the amount due, be prima facie presumed true and correct, subject
to verification by the Bureau of Internal Revenue.

       The Bureau of Internal Revenue may, within three (3) years from the issuance
or deemed issuance of the Certificate of Availment, verify the truthfulness,
completeness, and accuracy of the General Tax Amnesty Return, Statement of Total
Assets, or Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth, including the existence,
ownership, value, and completeness of the assets and liabilities declared therein,
through information lawfully available to the Bureau, third-party information,
information obtained pursuant to Section 15 hereof, and information received
pursuant to Section 26 of this act.

       Any notice of material discrepancy, proposed revocation, or other proceeding
arising from an ordinary verification under this Section shall be commenced within
such three (3)-year period.



                                                                                        15
        If, upon final determination after written notice and an opportunity to be
heard, the Statement of Total Assets or Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth
reflects an amount lower than the actual amount or otherwise contains any inaccuracy
affecting the tax due, the taxpayer shall be liable for the full payment of the correct tax
due for the taxable years or periods covered by the General Tax Amnesty, as
computed under the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, as if the
General Tax Amnesty had not been availed of. Such liability shall apply whether the
inaccuracy is discovered within or after the three (3)-year verification period and
regardless of whether fraud, willful concealment, or material misrepresentation is
established.

       No criminal liability shall arise from an inaccuracy alone. Criminal liability
shall attach only when fraud, willful concealment, or material misrepresentation
amounting to a willful attempt to evade or defeat tax is established beyond reasonable
doubt.

       Upon expiration of the three (3)-year period without the commencement of any
such proceeding, the availment of the General Tax Amnesty shall become final and
irrevocable, subject to the taxpayer's liability for the full payment of the correct tax
due under the preceding paragraphs, except where the availment was procured
through fraud, willful concealment, or material misrepresentation to reduce the
amnesty tax or improperly obtain the benefits of the General Tax Amnesty.

        The Acceptance Payment Form, and the General Tax Amnesty Return shall be
submitted to the Revenue District Office after complete payment. The completion of
these requirements shall constitute compliance with the filing and payment
requirements of this Title, subject to the verification and anti-fraud provisions of this
Act. A Certificate of Availment of the General Tax Amnesty shall be issued by the
Bureau of Internal Revenue within fifteen (15) calendar days from submission to the
Bureau of Internal Revenue of the Acceptance Payment Form and the General Tax
Amnesty Return. Otherwise, the duplicate copies, stamped as received, of the
Acceptance Payment Form, and the General Tax Amnesty Return shall be deemed as
sufficient proof of availment.

       The issuance or deemed issuance of a Certificate of Availment shall be without
prejudice to the verification period, the liability for the full payment of the correct tax
due, and the fraud exception provided under this Section.

SEC. 15. Waiver of Bank Secrecy Laws; Inquiry into Financial Accounts. — As a condition
for availing of the General Tax Amnesty, the applicant shall execute a notarized
waiver in favor of the Bureau of Internal Revenue authorizing the Commissioner of
Internal Revenue or the Commissioner's duly authorized representative to inquire
into and examine the applicant's bank deposits, investments, trust accounts, foreign
currency deposits, and other financial accounts, for the entire three (3)-year
verification period under Section 14 of this Act.

       The execution of the waiver shall constitute the applicant's voluntary
relinquishment, solely for purposes of verifying the General Tax Amnesty Return and
the declarations required under this Title, of the confidentiality protections under


                                                                                        16
Republic Act No. 1405, Republic Act No. 6426, and other laws, rules, or regulations
relating to the confidentiality of bank deposits and financial accounts.

       The Commissioner of Internal Revenue may, at any time during the three (3)-
year verification period, by written order, direct an inquiry into or examination of the
applicant's bank deposits, investments, trust accounts, foreign currency deposits, and
other financial accounts. No prior showing or finding of reasonable grounds, fraud,
willful concealment, or material misrepresentation shall be required, the applicant's
waiver constituting sufficient legal basis for the inquiry.

       Any inquiry under this Section shall be limited to information reasonably
necessary to verify the taxpayer's entitlement to the General Tax Amnesty and the
truthfulness, completeness, and accuracy of the declarations made thereunder.

      Information obtained pursuant to this Section shall remain confidential, shall
be used solely for the purposes authorized under this Act, and shall be subject to the
confidentiality requirements and penalties provided under the National Internal
Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, and this Act.

      The expiration of the waiver shall not prevent the use of information lawfully
obtained during the three (3)-year verification period in any proceeding authorized
under Section 16 of this Act.

       Failure to execute the required notarized waiver, or the execution thereof with
any reservation or qualification that materially limits its scope, shall render the
General Tax Amnesty Return incomplete and shall be a ground for denial of the
application.

SEC. 16. Wrongful Availment; Revocation; Prescriptive Period. — Notwithstanding the
three (3)-year verification period provided under Section 14 of this Act, where the
availment of the General Tax Amnesty was procured through fraud, willful
concealment, or material misrepresentation to reduce the amnesty tax or improperly
obtain the benefits of the General Tax Amnesty, the Certificate of Availment may be
revoked.

       Any proceeding to revoke such availment, assess or collect taxes becoming due
by reason of such revocation, or impose applicable civil penalties may be commenced
at any time within ten (10) years after the discovery of the fraud, willful concealment,
or material misrepresentation.

      The prescriptive period provided in the preceding paragraph shall commence
only upon discovery by the Bureau of Internal Revenue of facts sufficient to constitute
prima facie evidence of the fraud, willful concealment, or material misrepresentation.

      For purposes of this Section, fraud, willful concealment, or material
misrepresentation shall refer solely to such acts committed in the preparation, filing,
or procurement of the General Tax Amnesty or for the purpose of reducing the
amnesty tax due thereunder.




                                                                                     17
       Fraud, tax evasion, non-declaration of income, or other tax noncompliance
committed during taxable year 2026 or prior years which is otherwise validly covered
by the General Tax Amnesty shall not, by itself, constitute fraud in the availment of
the amnesty under this Section.

       The Bureau of Internal Revenue shall bear the burden of establishing fraud,
willful concealment, or material misrepresentation by clear and convincing evidence.

       No revocation shall be made without written notice to the taxpayer stating the
factual and legal basis therefor and affording the taxpayer a reasonable opportunity
to submit evidence and be heard.

        Upon final revocation, the immunities and privileges granted under Title III
shall cease to apply, and the taxpayer shall be liable for the underlying taxes, interests,
surcharges, and applicable penalties, without prejudice to prosecution for any offense
arising from the wrongful availment of the amnesty.

       Where the fraud, willful concealment, or material misrepresentation
constitutes a willful attempt to evade or defeat tax, the taxpayer shall, upon
conviction, be subject to the penalties prescribed under Section 254 of the National
Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, in addition to the payment of the correct
tax due and the applicable interests, surcharges, and civil penalties. Criminal liability
under this paragraph must be established beyond reasonable doubt.

SEC. 17. Informer's Reward for Wrongful Availment of Tax Amnesty. — Notwithstanding
Section 282 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, any person
who voluntarily provides definite and sworn information, not yet in the possession of
the Bureau of Internal Revenue, which directly leads to the discovery and final
determination of fraud, willful concealment, or material misrepresentation in the
availment of the Estate Tax Amnesty under Title II or the General Tax Amnesty under
this Title, and results in the actual recovery and collection of revenues, surcharges,
fees, fines, or penalties, shall be entitled to a reward equivalent to ten percent (10%) of
the amount actually recovered and collected by the Government, or Ten million pesos
(P10,000,000.00) per case, whichever is lower.

     No reward shall be granted unless:

     (A) The information was voluntarily provided, definite, material, and sworn;

     (B) The material information was not already in the possession of the Bureau of
     Internal Revenue and was not merely duplicative of an existing investigation or
     verification;

     (C) The information materially contributed to the discovery and final
     determination of the wrongful availment;

     (D) The information resulted in actual recovery and collection by the
     Government; and




                                                                                        18
     (E) The informer did not participate in, conspire in, or materially assist in the
     wrongful availment.

       No internal revenue official or employee, other public official or employee who
acquired the information by reason of official duties, or other person disqualified
under Section 282 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, shall
be entitled to the reward provided under this Section.

       The identity of the informer and any information that would reasonably lead
to the disclosure of such identity shall be confidential, except when disclosure is
required by a court of competent jurisdiction and is indispensable to the fair
disposition of a pending case.

      The reward shall be payable only after the amount upon which the reward is
based has been actually collected by the Government and the taxpayer's liability has
become final.

       An informer who qualifies for a reward under this Section shall not be entitled
to a duplicate reward under Section 282 of the National Internal Revenue Code of
1997, as amended, for the same information and the same recovery.

SEC. 18. Immunities and Privileges. — Those who avail of the General Tax Amnesty and
have fully complied with all the conditions set forth in this Act and upon payment of
the amnesty tax shall be entitled to the following immunities and privileges:

     (A) With respect to the years covered by the tax amnesty, the taxpayer shall be
     immune from the payment of taxes, as well as additions thereto, and from all
     appurtenant civil, criminal, and administrative cases and penalties under the
     National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, arising from the failure to
     pay any and all internal revenue taxes for taxable year 2026 and prior years and
     from such other investigations or suits insofar as they relate to the assets,
     liabilities, net worth and internal revenue taxes that are subject of the tax
     amnesty.

     The immunities provided under this paragraph shall not preclude verification of
     the taxpayer's entitlement to the amnesty pursuant to Sections 14, 15, and 16 of
     this Act, or the imposition and collection of the taxpayer's liability for the full
     payment of the correct tax due under Section 14 of this Act.

     (B) Any information or data contained in, derived from or provided by a
     taxpayer in the Tax Amnesty Return, Statement of Total Assets or Statement of
     Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth, as the case may be, and appurtenant
     documents shall be confidential in nature and shall not be used in any
     investigation or prosecution before any judicial, quasi-judicial, or administrative
     bodies, except to the extent necessary to verify compliance with this Title or to
     investigate or prosecute fraud, willful concealment, or material
     misrepresentation committed in the availment of the General Tax Amnesty.
     Information submitted under the amnesty shall not be used to prosecute the
     underlying tax noncompliance that is validly covered by the amnesty. However,


                                                                                     19
     the taxpayer may use this as a defense, whenever appropriate, in cases brought
     against the taxpayer.

          Nothing in this paragraph shall preclude the use of such information to
     determine, assess, and collect the correct tax due pursuant to Section 14 of this
     act.

     (C) The books of accounts and other records of the taxpayer for the years covered
     by the tax amnesty availed of shall not be examined by the Bureau of Internal
     Revenue, except to the extent reasonably necessary to verify the truthfulness,
     completeness, and accuracy of the taxpayer's General Tax Amnesty Return,
     Statement of Total Assets, or Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth, or
     to determine whether the availment was procured through fraud, willful
     concealment, or material misrepresentation: Provided, that the Commissioner of
     Internal Revenue may authorize in writing the examination of the said books of
     accounts and other records to verify the validity or correctness of a claim for any
     tax refund, tax credit (other than refund or credit of taxes withheld on wages),
     tax incentives, and/or exemptions under existing laws.

      All these immunities and privileges shall not apply when the taxpayer failed to
file a General Tax Amnesty Return and a Statement of Total Assets, or Statement of
Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth, as the case may be, or failed to execute the
notarized waiver required under Sections 12 and 15 of this Act.

     The immunities and privileges under this Title shall likewise not apply where
the availment has been finally determined, after notice and opportunity to be heard,
to have been procured through fraud, willful concealment, or material
misrepresentation.

      Upon full compliance with all the conditions set forth in this Title and payment
of the corresponding general amnesty tax, the tax amnesty granted under this Title
shall become final and irrevocable.

       The finality and irrevocability provided in the preceding paragraph shall be
subject to Sections 14, 15, and 16 of this Act, including the taxpayer's continuing
liability for the full payment of the correct tax due when an inaccuracy is discovered
within or after the three (3)-year verification period, and shall not apply to an
availment procured through fraud, willful concealment, or material
misrepresentation.

SEC. 19. Exceptions. — The General Tax Amnesty under this Act shall not extend to
the following:

     (A) Withholding tax agents who withheld taxes but failed to remit the same to
     the Bureau of Internal Revenue;

     (B) Taxpayers with cases pending in appropriate courts involving:




                                                                                     20
           (1) Those that fall under the jurisdiction of the Presidential Commission on
           Good Government;

           (2) Unexplained or unlawfully acquired wealth under Republic Act No.
           3019, otherwise known as the Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act, and
           Republic Act No. 7080 or an Act Defining and Penalizing the Crime of
           Plunder;

           (3) Violations of Republic Act No. 9160, otherwise known as the Anti-
           Money Laundering Act, as amended;

           (4) Tax evasion and other criminal offenses under Chapter II of Title X of
           the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended; and

           (5) Felonies of frauds, illegal exactions and transactions, and malversation
           of public funds and property under Chapters III and IV of Title VII of the
           Revised Penal Code;

     (C) Tax cases that have become final and executory; and

     (D) Delinquencies and assessments that have become final and executory;

     (E) Taxpayers whose availment of the General Tax Amnesty has been finally
     determined, after notice and opportunity to be heard, to have been procured
     through fraud, willful concealment, or material misrepresentation.

                                    TITLE IV

                   TAX AMNESTY ON DELINQUENCIES

SEC. 20. Coverage. — There is hereby authorized and granted a tax amnesty herein
called the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies, which shall cover all national internal
revenue taxes such as, but not limited to, income tax, withholding tax, capital gains
tax, donor’s tax, value-added tax, other percentage taxes, excise tax and documentary
stamp tax collected by the Bureau of Internal Revenue, including value-added tax and
excise taxes collected by the Bureau of Customs for taxable year 2026 and prior years.

        For purposes of this Title, Delinquencies shall mean the tax due on any return
to be filed, the tax due for which no return is required, or a deficiency tax, surcharge,
or interest thereon due and demandable as of the date appearing in the
Commissioner's notice and demand, as defined under Section 249(C) of the National
Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.

        For purposes of this Act, the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies may be availed of
in the following instances:

     (A) Delinquencies and assessments, which have become final and executory,
     including delinquent tax account, where the application for compromise has
     been requested on the basis of:


                                                                                      21
             (1) Doubtful validity of the assessment; or

             (2) Financial incapacity of the taxpayer, but the same was denied by the
             Regional Evaluation Board or the National Evaluation Board, as the case
             may be, on or before the Implementing Rules and Regulations take
             effect;

     (B) Pending criminal cases with the Department of Justice or the courts for tax
     evasion and other criminal offenses under Chapter II of Title X and Section 275
     of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, with or without
     assessments duly issued;

     (C) Tax cases subject of final and executory judgment by the courts on or before
     the Implementing Rules and Regulations take effect; and

     (D) Withholding tax agents who withheld taxes but failed to remit the same to
     the Bureau of Internal Revenue.

SEC. 21. Entitlement of Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies. — Any person may enjoy the
immunities and privileges of the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies and pay the
following tax amnesty rates:

      (A) Delinquencies and assessments which have become final and executory —
          40% of the basic tax assessed;

      (B) Tax cases subject of final and executory judgment by the courts — 50% of
          the basic tax assessed;

      (C) Pending criminal cases with criminal Information filed with the
          Department of Justice or the courts for tax evasion and other criminal
          offenses under Chapter II of Title X and Section 275 of the National Internal
          Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, with assessments duly issued and
          otherwise excluded in Titles II and III hereof — 60% of the basic tax
          assessed; and

      (D) Withholding agents who withheld taxes but failed to remit the same to the
          Bureau of Internal Revenue — 100% of the basic tax assessed.

SEC. 22. Availment of the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies; When and Where to File and Pay.
— Any person, natural or juridical, who wishes to avail of the Tax Amnesty on
Delinquencies shall, within two (2) years from the effectivity of the Implementing
Rules and Regulations of this Act, file with the appropriate office of the Bureau of
Internal Revenue, which has jurisdiction over the residence or principal place of
business of the taxpayer, a sworn Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies Return accompanied
by a Certification of Delinquency. The payment of the amnesty tax shall be made at
the time the Return is filed:




                                                                                    22
       Provided, that the Revenue District Officer shall issue and endorse an
Acceptance Payment Form, in such form as may be prescribed in the Implementing
Rules and Regulations of this Act authorizing the authorized agent bank, or in the
absence thereof, the revenue collection agent or municipal treasurer concerned, to
accept the amnesty tax payment:

       Provided, further, that the availment of the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies and
the issuance of the corresponding Acceptance Payment Form do not imply any
admission of criminal, civil or administrative liability on the part of the availing
taxpayer.

SEC. 23. Immunities and Privileges. — The tax delinquency of those who avail of the
Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies and have fully complied with all the conditions set
forth in this Act and upon payment of the amnesty tax shall be considered settled and
the criminal case under Section 21(C) and its corresponding civil or administrative
case, if applicable, be terminated, and the taxpayer shall be immune from all suits or
actions, including the payment of said delinquency or assessment, as well as additions
thereto, and from all appurtenant civil, criminal, and administrative cases, and
penalties under the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, as such
relate to the taxpayer's assets, liabilities, net worth, and internal revenue taxes that are
subject of the tax amnesty, and from such other investigations or suits insofar as they
relate to the assets, liabilities, net worth and internal revenue taxes that are subject of
the tax amnesty: Provided, that any notices of levy, attachments and/or warrants of
garnishment issued against the taxpayer shall be set aside pursuant to a lifting of
notice of levy/garnishment duly issued by the Bureau of Internal Revenue or its
authorized representative: Provided, further, that the Authority to Cancel Assessment
shall be issued by the Bureau of Internal Revenue in favor of the taxpayer availing of
the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies within fifteen (15) calendar days from submission
to the Bureau of Internal Revenue of the Acceptance Payment Form and the Tax
Amnesty on Delinquencies Return. Otherwise, the duplicate copies, stamped as
received, of the Acceptance Payment Form, and the Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies
Return shall be deemed as sufficient proof of availment: Provided, furthermore, that the
Tax Amnesty on Delinquencies Return and the Acceptance Payment Form shall be
submitted to the Revenue District Office after complete payment. The completion of
these requirements shall be deemed full compliance with the provisions of this Act.

       Upon full compliance with all the conditions set forth in this Title and payment
of the corresponding tax on delinquency, the tax amnesty granted under this Title
shall become final and irrevocable.

                                      TITLE V

  CONFIDENTIALITY AND NON-USE OF INFORMATION AND DATA,
  AND AMENDMENT TO THE STATEMENT OF TOTAL ASSETS AND
     STATEMENT OF ASSETS, LIABILITIES, AND NET WORTH




                                                                                         23
SEC. 24. Confidentiality and Non-use of Information and Data in the Statement of Total
Assets and Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth. — Any information or data
contained in, derived from or provided by a taxpayer in the Tax Amnesty Return,
Statement of Total Assets or Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth, as the
case may be, and appurtenant documents shall be confidential in nature and shall not
be used in any investigation or prosecution before any judicial, quasi-judicial, or
administrative body, except to the extent necessary for the verification of an availment
under Title II or Title III of this Act, for the determination of whether such availment
was procured through fraud, willful concealment, or material misrepresentation, or
for the exchange of information authorized under Section 26 of this Act.

       Nothing in this Section shall authorize the use of information voluntarily
disclosed under the General Tax Amnesty for the investigation or prosecution of the
underlying tax noncompliance validly covered by the amnesty.

Any statement of assets, liabilities, and net worth, financial statements, information
sheets, and any such other statements or disclosures that may have been previously
submitted by the taxpayer as required by existing laws are deemed to have been
amended by the Tax Amnesty Return and/or the Statement of Total Assets or
Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth, as the case may be, filed under this
Act and may be compared with the Tax Amnesty Return, Statement of Total Assets,
or Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth solely for purposes of verifying the
truthfulness and completeness of an availment under Title II or Title III or determining
whether such availment was procured through fraud, willful concealment, or material
misrepresentation: Provided, that such prior statements or disclosures and the
information contained therein shall otherwise remain subject to applicable
confidentiality laws.


                                    TITLE VI

                           GENERAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 25. Information Management System. — For purposes of enhancing revenue
administration, revenue collection and policy formulation, the Department of Finance,
in coordination with the Bureau of Internal Revenue, Land Registration Authority,
Department of Trade and Industry, Securities and Exchange Commission, Land
Transportation Office, and other agencies concerned, shall institute an Information
Management Program for the effective use of information declared or obtained from
the Tax Amnesty Returns and Statements of Total Assets or Statements of Assets,
Liabilities, and Net Worth, as the case may be, required to be filed under this Act.

      All the statements and returns required under this Act shall be filed and
processed separately from all other records of the Bureau of Internal Revenue in
accordance with the Implementing Rules and Regulations of this Act.

       If the data requirements consist of information found in the income tax return
of taxpayers, the requirements under Section 71 of the National Internal Revenue
Code of 1997, as amended, shall still be complied with. The Information Management


                                                                                     24
System shall also comply with the provisions of Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise
known as the "Data Privacy Act" and such other laws relating to confidentiality of
information.

SEC. 26. Automatic Exchange of Information for Tax Purposes. — The Commissioner of
Internal Revenue is hereby authorized to obtain, receive, process, use, and exchange
taxpayer and financial account information pursuant to tax treaties, tax information
exchange agreements, the Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax
Matters, and other international agreements or arrangements on tax matters to which
the Philippines is a party, whether upon request, automatically, or spontaneously,
subject to the terms and conditions thereof.

       Notwithstanding Republic Act No. 1405, Republic Act No. 6426, and other
laws, rules, or regulations relating to the confidentiality of bank deposits or financial
accounts, banks, financial institutions, and other reporting entities shall submit such
reportable financial account information as may be required for the implementation
of the automatic exchange of information for tax purposes in accordance with rules
and regulations to be issued by the Secretary of Finance, upon recommendation of the
Commissioner of Internal Revenue.

       The information obtained, received, processed, or exchanged pursuant to this
Section shall be used only for lawful tax purposes and shall be subject to the
confidentiality, data privacy, data security, and safeguard requirements provided
under applicable law and the relevant international agreement or arrangement.

SEC. 27. Disposition of Proceeds from the Tax Amnesty. — An amount equivalent to Five
hundred million pesos (P500,000,000) of the collection from the tax amnesty herein
granted shall accrue to the Department of Finance and shall be used exclusively for
purposes of establishing the tax database under Section 25 of this Act.

      Collections in excess of the amount provided in the preceding paragraph shall
accrue to the National Treasury and may be appropriated for social protection and
national infrastructure programs in accordance with the annual General
Appropriations Act.

                                       TITLE VII

                                 Offenses and Penalties

SEC. 28. Unlawful Divulgence of Tax Amnesty Return and Appurtenant Documents. — It
shall be unlawful for any person having knowledge of the Tax Amnesty Return and
appurtenant documents, to disclose any information relative thereto, except for a
disclosure expressly authorized under this Act for purposes of verification of an
availment, investigation or prosecution of wrongful availment, automatic or other
lawful exchange of tax information, or compliance with a lawful order of a court of
competent jurisdiction, and any violation hereof shall be penalized by a fine of One
hundred fifty thousand pesos (P150,000) and imprisonment of not less than six (6)
years but not more than ten (10) years: Provided, that if the offender is an officer or



                                                                                      25
employee of the Bureau of Internal Revenue or any government entity, the penalties
under Section 270 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, shall
apply: Provided, further, that the offender shall likewise suffer an additional penalty of
perpetual disqualification to hold public office.


                                       TITLE VIII
         CONGRESSIONAL OVERSIGHT COMMITTEE AND FINAL
                         PROVISIONS
SEC. 29. Report to Oversight Committee. — The Commissioner shall submit to the
Oversight Committee referred to in Section 290 of the National Internal Revenue Code
of 1997, as amended, through the Chairpersons of the Committee on Ways and Means
of the Senate of the Philippines and the House of Representatives, a detailed report on
the implementation of this Act within six (6) months after the two (2)-year period of
availment of the Estate Tax Amnesty and two (2)-year period of availment of the
General Tax Amnesty, and the applicable period of availment of the tax amnesty on
delinquencies.

SEC. 30. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The Secretary of Finance shall, in
coordination with the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, promulgate and publish the
necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act within
sixty (60) days from its effectivity.

       The failure of the Secretary of Finance to promulgate the said rules and
regulations shall not prevent the implementation of this Act upon its effectivity.

SEC. 31. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is subsequently declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 32. Repealing Clause. — All laws, acts, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 33. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least one (1) newspaper of general circulation.

     Approved.
