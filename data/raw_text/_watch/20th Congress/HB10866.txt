                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Republic Act No. 9003, or the “Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of
2000,” already requires households and establishments to segregate solid waste
at source under Section 21 of the law. More than two decades since its passage,
however, segregation-at-source often remains the weakest-enforced link in the
country’s solid waste management chain. Barangays, which are on the frontline
of collection, generally have no clear, standardized mechanism to refuse
uncollected, unsegregated waste, to inspect compliance, or to impose graduated,
due-process-compliant penalties on repeat violators.

       This absence of a workable enforcement mechanism at the barangay level
has allowed segregation-at-source to remain, in practice, optional in many
communities, undermining the entire downstream chain of materials recovery,
composting, and waste diversion that later legislation and local ordinances have
tried to strengthen.

       This bill amends Republic Act No. 9003 by inserting a new section, Section
22, that gives barangays a clear, standardized, and due-process-compliant
enforcement toolkit: (1) a designated Barangay Segregation Enforcement Team;
(2) authority to refuse collection of unsegregated waste; (3) a graduated schedule
of warnings and fines for households and establishments; (4) authority to
conduct spot inspections; and (5) a self-sustaining Barangay Solid Waste
Management Enforcement Fund sourced from the fines themselves, primarily for
information campaigns and segregation materials rather than punitive revenue-
raising.

      By giving barangays real, low-cost enforcement tools instead of merely
repeating the mandate to segregate, this measure aims to close the compliance
gap that has persisted since RA 9003 took effect. It is our view, that given the
precarious situation in which the country finds itself in vis-à-vis solid waste and
environmental waste-leakage, tackling segregation-at-source provides a solid
step towards the right direction. It is high time that we move on from the old
mantra of “tapat ko, linis ko,” and into a new adage: I will keep my community,
and therefore the country, clean.

      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is urgently sought.

   AN ACT STRENGTHENING THE ENFORCEMENT OF MANDATORY
  SEGREGATION-AT-SOURCE OF SOLID WASTES AT THE BARANGAY
   LEVEL, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9003,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “ECOLOGICAL SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT
            ACT OF 2000”, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Barangay
Segregation-At-Source Enforcement Act.”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to pursue a
systematic, comprehensive, and ecological solid waste management program
consistent with Republic Act No. 9003. Toward this end, the state recognizes
that segregation-at-source is the foundation of resource recovery, composting,
and waste diversion, and that this foundation can only be secured through a
clear, consistent, and enforced compliance mechanism at the barangay level,
where waste generation and collection typically occur.

      SECTION 3. Republic Act No. 9003 is hereby amended by adding a new
Section 22 after Section 21. – A new Section 22 is hereby inserted after Section
21 of Republic Act No. 9003, to read as follows:

      “Section 22. Barangay Enforcement of Mandatory Segregation-at-source.

            (a) Barangay Segregation Enforcement Team – Every Barangay Solid
                Waste Management Committee constituted under this Act shall
                designate, from among its members, barangay tanods, or other
                barangay-level officers, a Segregation Enforcement Team
                responsible for monitoring household and establishment
                compliance with the segregation-at-source requirement under
                Section 21 of this Act;
            (b) Non-Collection of Unsegregated Waste – Barangay or LGU-
                contracted collection crews shall not collect solid waste that is
                not segregated in accordance with Section 21 of this Act.
                Uncollected waste shall be left in place together with a notice of
                non-compliance indicating the violation and the steps for
                rectification; and
            (c) Graduated Enforcement – for households and micro-enterprises:
                   a. First Violation – Written warning and required attendance
                       in a barangay-conducted segregation orientation.
                   b. Section Violation – Fine of not less than one thousand
                       pesos (PHP 1,000.00); and
                   c. Third and Subsequent Violations – Fine of not less than
                       two thousand pesos (PHP 2,000.00) nor more than one
                       thousand pesos (PHP 1,000.00) for each violation.

                    For commercial and institutional establishments, the fines
                    under this subsection shall be five (5) times the amount
                    imposed on households.
            (d) Spot Inspections – The Segregation Enforcement Team may
                conduct random or scheduled inspections of households and
                establishments, including public markets, eateries, and
                commercial complexes, within the barangay to verify compliance.
            (e) Barangay Solid Waste Management Enforcement Fund – Seventy
                percent (70%) of all finds collected under this section shall accrue
                to a Barangay Solid Waste Management Enforcement Fund, to be
                used exclusively for information, education, and communication
                campaigns, segregation bins and materials, and incentives for
                compliant households. The remaining thirty percent (30%) shall
                accrue to the general fund of the barangay.
            (f) Due Process – No fine shall be imposed under this section without
                affording the alleged violator the opportunity to be heard before
                the Punong Barangay or the Lupong Tagapamayapa consistent
                with the Katarungang Pambarangay Law Under Republic Act No.
                7160.”

       SECTION 4. Relation to Existing Penal Provisions. – The fines imposed
under Section 21 of Republic Act No. 9003, as inserted by this Act, are
administrative in nature and shall be without prejudice to the criminal penalties
for prohibited acts under Section 48 and 49 of the same Act, where applicable.
