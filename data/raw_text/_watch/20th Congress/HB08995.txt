                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

     Access to culture, education, information, and public life is a fundamental
component of human dignity. Yet for millions of Filipinos who are blind, low vision,
deafblind, or living with other sensory impairments, visual media and public spaces
remain partially or entirely inaccessible.

     While Republic Act No. 7277 (Magna Carta for Persons with Disabilities), the
United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD), the
Marrakesh Treaty, and related accessibility laws affirm the right to equal participation
in cultural and educational life, the Philippines has yet to institutionalize a
comprehensive requirement for audio description in visual media and public
entertainment venues.

     Audio description is not an alteration of artistic content. It is a parallel narrative
layer that describes essential visual elements—actions, settings, facial expressions,
scene changes, and on-screen text—during natural pauses in dialogue. It allows
persons with visual impairments to understand and enjoy films, television programs,
theater productions, museums, tourist attractions, theme parks, and educational
multimedia on equal footing with sighted audiences.

      At present, access to audio description in the Philippines is inconsistent and
largely voluntary. Some producers and institutions have adopted accessibility features,
but there is no uniform legal standard requiring such inclusion. As a result, accessibility
depends on goodwill rather than enforceable rights.

     This bill seeks to establish audio description as a national accessibility standard
across movies, television programs, live performances, museums, tourist sites, theme
parks, educational videos, and advertising materials. It aligns the Philippines with
evolving global best practices in universal design and inclusive media production.

     The measure adopts a reasonable accommodation framework, recognizing that
accessibility must be integrated into production and distribution processes. It further
provides for technical standards to be formulated by appropriate government agencies

in accordance with international guidelines, including Web Content Accessibility
Guidelines (WCAG), and ensures coordination among cultural, communications, and
disability affairs institutions.

      Importantly, this bill recognizes that accessibility benefits extend beyond persons
with blindness or low vision. Audio description assists senior citizens, individuals within
the autism spectrum, persons with learning difficulties, and language learners.
Universal design strengthens inclusivity not just for a specific group, but for society at
large.

     The proposed law also provides reasonable exemptions in cases where
compliance would be economically burdensome, ensuring proportional
implementation while upholding the principle of accessibility as the norm rather than
the exception.

       In a nation that values family, culture, and shared public experiences, exclusion
from media and public spaces is not a minor inconvenience—it is a barrier to full
enjoyment of the benefits of citizenship. Accessibility must not be treated as charity. It
is a right.

     By institutionalizing audio description as a standard feature in public and cultural
content, this measure affirms the State’s commitment to inclusion, equality, and
human dignity.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                 AN ACT
     REQUIRING ALL MOVIES, VIDEOS, LIVE PERFORMANCES, MUSEUMS,
     TOURIST SPOTS AND THEME PARKS, EDUCATIONAL VIDEOS AND
     MULTIMEDIA PROJECTS TO HAVE AUDIO DESCRIPTION OPTION

         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1        SECTION 1. This Act shall be known as the "Philippine Audio Description Act."
 2
 3        SEC. 2. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the term:
 4
 5        a. Audio description (also referred to as "description", "video description or
 6           described video") shall mean to describe the process that consists of the
 7           adaptation of video or multimedia content through descriptions of images
 8           including actions, settings, facial expressions, costumes, scene changes,
 9           surroundings and on-screen texts through audio narration during periods in
10           which there is no dialogue and whose sole purpose is to allow people who
11           are unable to adequately see the video due to blindness, low vision, deaf
12           blindness and other sensory impairments to enjoy any television, films,
13           cultural shows or theatrical contents without barriers. However, it works in
14           tandem with the audio of the video, and it does not replace the video's original
15           narration & soundtrack.
16
17        b. Accessibility shall mean the practice of making information, activities, and/or
18           environments sensible, meaningful, inclusive and usable for persons with
19           blindness and visual impairments.
20
21        c. Accurate shall mean that there must be no errors in word selection,
22           pronunciation, diction or enunciation.
23
24        d. Appropriate shall mean that the material should consider the intended
25           audience, be objective and seek simplicity.

 6                 (2) The impact on the operation of the provider or producer;

 8                 (4) The type of operations of the provider or producer.
 9
10        f. Prioritized shall mean that a content is essential to the intended learning and
11           that enjoyment outcomes is of primary importance.
12
13        g. Reasonable Accommodation shall mean modifications or adjustments that
14           eliminate or minimize disability-related barriers to ensure equivalent access
15           to video contents for all people.
16

18           barriers, accessible to everyone, by considering accessibility to be integral to
19           the design process, benefitted not just the intended end users, but a much
20           larger number of people. Furthermore, integrating accessibility to the start of
21           the design process was proven to result in more aesthetically pleasing
22           outcomes at a lower cost and could be marketed to a greater percentage of
23           the population.

25        SEC. 3. Purpose of audio description. – The purpose of audio description is to
26   provide persons who are blind, low vision, deafblind and with other sensory impairment
27   the additional information and complete picture and context that are considered
28   essential to fully understanding, appreciating and enjoying the visual media they are
29   watching.

34              b. Deaf -Blind

36              d. Persons within Autism spectrum disorder
37              e. Persons with Learning Difficulties
38              f. Language Learners

40        SEC. 4. Requirement to provide audio description. – All franchise holders or
41   operators of television programs are required to provide audio description option in the
42   broadcast of their programs including newscast and news programs, pre-scripted
43   programs, sports and state related events.
44
45       Likewise, movie producers, live theater shows, video contents, tourist spots and
46   theme parks shall also be required to provide audio description.
47

39         SEC. 8. Incentives. – Producers who implement audio description shall be given
