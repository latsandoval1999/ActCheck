                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE


        Section 5, Article III of the 1987 Philippine Constitution guarantees the right to
religious freedom of every Filipino. Among the bundles of rights subsumed in this
constitutional guarantee is the right of a person to share, proclaim or disseminate his
or her religious belief within constitutional or lawful bounds. Thus, any act that hinders,
obstructs, prevents or threatens a person, natural or juridical, from sharing, preaching
or doing any act of propagating his or her religion or religious belief, without justifiable
cause, is a violation or impairment of this God-given and constitutionally- guaranteed
right.

        Faith propagators – regardless of the church or religious denomination they
come from – are messengers of peace and hope to needy people. In a survey
conducted in a recent year, it is found that 73% of Filipinos valued religion as “very
important” in their lives.1 Religion provides faith, hope, and a sense of purpose to
Filipinos which keep them resilient even in the midst of life challenges like poverty,
natural disasters, and personal struggles. Religion also provides the Filipino people
with sense of values and moral compass that help them remain anchored on God as
they navigate political and social issues and face the onslaught of modern ideologies.

        Unfortunately, probably due to prejudice, bias, hatred, religious fanaticism, or
money-motivated persecution, many missionaries or faith propagators have suffered
different forms of persecution, hardship and threat to their lives in their honest and
sincere quest to share their faith or religious belief to others. These include coercing
or threatening missionaries or faith propagators if they continue preaching their
religious belief; or disrupting or harassing religious gathering in order to sow fear and
intimidation with the aim of preventing such religious gathering from happening again.

        This proposed measure seeks to give substance to the Constitutional right to
freedom of religion by protecting and promoting the right of a person or a religious
group or organization to freely and safely propagate or share his or her faith to others.
By doing so, Filipinos are saved from being deprived of opportunities to hear the
messages of these religions which are the producer of faith in God in their lives. This
spiritual nourishment is very crucial in attaining the interest of the State of turning
Filipinos to good and productive citizens of the country even in the midst life hardships
and challenges.

        Further, the bill balances freedom with responsibility by providing exceptions.
In particular, the right to propagate religion cannot be invoked if an act may result to
physical harm, violence, or if it will endanger public safety, order, or health.
Propagating religion must also not be subversive, or contrary to good morals, a valid
work, business or school policy, and to the laws of the country. It can also be regulated
when done within a private establishment or dwelling, if it violates the fundamental
rights and freedoms of others, or if it involves a minor whose consent of the parent or
legal guardian has not been obtained.

        The bill also supersedes Article 132 and 133 of the Revised Renal Code by not
limiting to just public officers or employees the ones who can commit the offense, and
by providing for stiffer penalties.

      This measure is not about establishing a state religion nor giving special
treatment to any faith group; rather, it is about giving faith propagators or religious
groups a safe environment to share their faith and at the same time provide them with
legal protection and remedies in case of threats, obstruction, harassment or
persecution.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
PROTECTING THE RIGHT OF THE PEOPLE TO FREELY AND SAFELY
PROPAGATE THEIR RELIGION, GUARANTEED UNDER SECTION 5, ARTICLE III
OF THE 1987 CONSTITUTION

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

    SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Freedom to
Propagate Religion Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the declared policy of the State to protect
and uphold the fundamental and inalienable right of every person to religious freedom
as guaranteed under Section 5, Article Ill of the Constitution and other international
human rights instruments, including the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the
International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights and the Declaration on the
Elimination on All Forms of Intolerance and Discrimination Based on Religion and
Belief, to which the State is a party or that it adheres thereto.

      The right of every Filipino to religious freedom, which includes the right to safely
propagate religious beliefs, publicly or privately, through speech or actions, must
always be recognized, protected and promoted.

        Towards this end, the State must ensure that no person should be allowed to
hinder, obstruct, prevent or threaten a person, natural or juridical, from sharing,
preaching or doing any act of propagating his or her religion or religious belief, without
justifiable cause.

     SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

   a. Religious Belief refers to a particular belief or set of beliefs held by a particular
      religion, or by an individual person;




                                       Page 3 of 5
   b. Religious group refers to a church, religious entity, religious organization or
      group of persons who have formed together into a congregation for the worship
      of a Higher Authority according to the group's established doctrines and
      ceremonies whether or not the same is registered with the government. For
      purposes of this Act, the group does not need to be registered to be entitled to
      recognition as a religious group.

     SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. – Subject to limitations and exceptions provided under
Section 5, it shall be unlawful for any person to:

   a. Hinder, obstruct, disturb, or prevent a religious ceremony or manifestation
      either using force, threat or otherwise;

   b. Perform an act or acts notoriously offensive to the feelings of the faithful in a
      place devoted to religious worship or during the celebration of any religious
      ceremony;

   c. Hinder, obstruct, threaten or prevent a person or a group of persons from
      sharing, teaching, preaching or doing any act of propagating his or her religion
      or religious belief to other people; and

   d. Commit any other act that curtails or violates the right of a person to freely and
      safely propagate his or her religious belief, either publicly or privately.

      SEC. 5. Exceptions. – The rights granted by under this Act shall be subject to
the following limitations:

   a. When propagating religion may result in physical harm, violence, or endanger
      public safety, order or health;

   b. When propagating religion is contrary to good morals, or will violate the
      fundamental rights and freedoms of others;

   c. When the religious doctrine or teaching being propagated is subversive or
      contrary to the laws of the Republic of the Philippines;

   d. When propagating religion is contrary to a valid work, business or school policy;

   e. When propagating religion is conducted within a private establishment or
      dwelling owned, operated, or managed by a religious sect or one of its
      members; and

   f. When propagating religion involves a minor and the consent of the parent or
      legal guardian has not been obtained.

      SEC. 6. Penalty. – Any person who violates Section 4 of this Act shall suffer a
fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00) but not exceeding
Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than six
(6) years and one (1) day but not more than eight (8) years, or both, at the discretion
of the court.

     SEC. 7. Prohibition Against the Establishment of a Religion by the State. –
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to affect or diminish, in any way, the meaning
and intent of Section 5, Article III of the Constitution, which prohibits the State from
establishing any religion.

      SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
after the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of the Department of Justice, in upon
consultation with other concerned and relevant government agencies and
stakeholders, shall formulate the necessary implementing rules and regulations of this
Act.

      SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part hereof is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – Articles 132 and 133 of the Revised Penal Code
are hereby superseded by this Act. All laws, decrees, executive orders, memorandum
orders, memorandum circulars, administrative orders, ordinances, or parts thereof,
which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby deemed repealed or
modified accordingly.

       SEC. 11. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
