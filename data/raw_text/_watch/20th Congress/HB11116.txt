                            AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE FOR SOLO PARENTS
  PROGRAM (FASPP) OF THE DEPARTMENT OF SOCIAL WELFARE AND
   DEVELOPMENT (DSWD) AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Solo parents carry the responsibility of raising and providing for their
children without the full and regular support of another parent, making access
to reliable social protection particularly important for families with limited
resources. The Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) estimates
that there are approximately 14 million solo parents in the Philippines, and the
agency recognizes solo parents as a sector requiring social protection and
government assistance under the Expanded Solo Parents Welfare Act, or
Republic Act No. 11861.1 The scale of this sector underscores the need for
government programs that not only recognize solo parents' rights in law but also
provide practical assistance when families face immediate financial difficulty.

       The need for responsive assistance is particularly evident among low-
income households facing rising costs of basic necessities. In June 2026, food
inflation among the bottom 30% of income households reached 8.2 percent,
while the cost of housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels increased by 8.7
percent and transport costs increased by 15.6 percent year-on-year.2 These

likewise demonstrates the continuing demand for government safety-net
assistance: more than 8.2 million Filipinos in crisis received assistance from
AICS from January to November 2025, exceeding the program's target for the
year.3

       While existing government programs provide important support, there is a
need to institutionalize a dedicated and predictable mechanism through which
qualified solo parents can receive assistance for essential needs. The proposed
Financial Assistance for Solo Parents Program (FASPP) Act responds to this need
by establishing a statutory framework for assistance that may cover food,
medical expenses, education, livelihood, and cash relief, while allowing
complementary assistance through LGUs in the form of rice assistance. The Bill
also provides safeguards through eligibility requirements, social-worker
assessment, documentary requirements, and penalties for fraudulent or
improper claims, while ensuring that assistance may complement support from
LGUs, other government agencies, and non-government organizations. By
institutionalizing the FASPP and providing for its continuing appropriation, the
measure seeks to make government assistance more accessible, coordinated,
and responsive to the actual needs of solo parents and their dependents.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT

  PROGRAM (FASPP) OF THE DEPARTMENT OF SOCIAL WELFARE AND

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Solo Parents
Economic Support Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to recognize and protect solo parents and their dependents as a vulnerable sector
and to promote their welfare by ensuring access to timely, adequate, and
responsive social protection, financial assistance, and essential goods and
services necessary for food security, health, education, livelihood, and other
basic needs.

It is further declared the policy of the State to institutionalize a coordinated and
accessible system of financial assistance for qualified solo parents through the
Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), in partnership with
local government units (LGUs) and other relevant agencies and service providers,
particularly for solo parents whose limited incomes make them vulnerable to
poverty, economic shocks, inflation, illness, and other circumstances that
threaten the well-being and continued support of their dependents.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
   1. FASPP - refers to the Financial Assistance for Solo Parents Program
      implemented by the DSWD providing various forms of assistance to
      qualified solo parents.

   2. Solo Parent - refers to an individual who qualifies as a solo parent under
      Republic Act No. 11861 and its implementing rules and regulations.

   3. Beneficiary - refers to a solo parent whose monthly income does not exceed
      Fourteen Thousand Pesos (PhP 14,000.00), and is qualified to receive
      assistance under this Act, subject to assessment and submission of
      documentary requirements.

   4. Dependent - refers to a child or a dependent qualified under the Expanded
      Solo Parents Welfare Act.

   5. Certificate of Eligibility (CE) - refers to a document issued by the DSWD
      certifying that the solo parent is eligible to avail of assistance under the
      FASPP.

   6. General Intake Sheet (GIS) - refers to the form used by DSWD social
      workers to record relevant information on the solo parent and dependents.

   7. Social Case Study Report (SCSR) - refers to the document prepared by a
      professional social worker describing the socio-economic condition,
      assessment, and recommended interventions for the solo parent.

   8. Service Providers - refers to entities providing goods and services covered
      by the FASPP, including but not limited to hospitals, clinics, educational
      institutions, pharmacies, and other relevant providers.

   9. Guarantee Letter (GL) - refers to a document issued by the DSWD
      guaranteeing payment to service providers on behalf of the solo parent.

SECTION 4. Coverage and Modalities in Providing Assistance. Financial and
other assistance shall be provided through the DSWD and in partnership with
local government units (LGUs), subject to eligibility and availability of funds, as
follows:

   1. Implementation through the DSWD - Qualified solo parents who need
      financial assistance for goods and services may avail of:

         a. Food Assistance - this is an outright cash assistance provided to
            meet the need for food and other nutritional requirements for
            sustenance of the solo parent’s children;
b. Medical Assistance - this is an outright cash or Guarantee Letter
   provided to cover hospitalization expenses and professional fees,
   cost of medicines and other medical treatment or procedures, such
   as common laboratory tests, and diagnostic imaging for any illness
   or condition, including postpartum complications. This assistance
   does not extend to other healthcare expenses that are unrelated to
   medical treatment or aftercare;

c. Educational Assistance for dependents - refers to an outright cash
   assistance or Guarantee Letter provided to cover school-related
   expenses of the solo parent and dependent(s), such as tuition and
   miscellaneous fees, purchase of school supplies, uniforms, books,
   payment of transportation expenses, and other incidental costs
   necessary for the dependent’s continued attendance and completion
   of primary, secondary, or tertiary education, including technical-
   vocational education or skills training programs, subject to
   assessment by the DSWD social worker.

d. Livelihood Assistance - refers to an outright cash assistance or
   support package granted to a qualified solo parent for the purpose
   of initiating, sustaining, or expanding a small-scale livelihood or
   income-generating activity. This assistance may cover the
   procurement of necessary tools, raw materials, training costs, or
   other inputs directly related to the livelihood undertaking, as
   determined by the assessment and recommendation of the DSWD
   social worker.

e. Cash Relief Assistance - this is an outright cash assistance provided
   to qualified beneficiaries for reasons other than for food, medical or
   funeral expenses, which, upon the assessment of the DSWD social
   worker, may appear to be directly related to the effects of high
   inflation (i.e., difficulty in paying for house rental, electricity and
   water expenses, etc.).

   Cash assistance may be granted outright in amounts from Five
   Thousand Pesos (PhP 5,000.00) up to Fifteen Thousand Pesos (PhP
   15,000.00), subject to the DSWD’s assessment. For amounts
   exceeding Fifteen Thousand Pesos (PhP 15,000.00), the same shall
   be provided through a Guarantee Letter approved by the Secretary
   of DSWD or his duly authorized representative.

   The distribution of assistance is without prejudice to the adoption
   of digital payment solutions once the necessary infrastructure is in
   place.
   2. Implementation through Local Government Units (LGUs) - The financial
      assistance that will be provided to qualified beneficiaries through the LGUs
      shall be called rice assistance. For this purpose, the DSWD shall enter into
      a Memorandum of Agreement with the local government units to provide
      the details of the transfer of funds and the requirements for its liquidation,
      in accordance with the existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules
      and regulations.

      The amount of rice assistance that can be provided to qualified
      beneficiaries shall be equivalent to half of the cost of twenty-five (25)
      kilograms of rice based on the suggested retail price from the Department
      of Agriculture (DA). The amount of each transfer to the LGU shall not
      exceed Thirty Million Pesos (PhP 30,000,000.00).

      The financial assistance under this Act may complement any support
      provided by the LGU, government agencies, and non-government
      organizations. A beneficiary who has received rice assistance through the
      LGU may still qualify to receive other financial assistance from the DSWD
      subject to the requirements under this Act, and professional assessment
      of the DSWD social worker.

SECTION 5. Documentary Requirements. For purposes of assessment and
evaluation, the solo parent shall submit the following:

   1. Solo Parent Identification Card (SPIC). Provided, that, in the absence of an
      SPIC, the DSWD and/or the LGU shall assist the Solo Parent in securing
      the same; and

   2. Proof of residency such as barangay certificate, utility bill, or Voter’s ID.

SECTION 6. Penalties. The penalty of imprisonment of not less than one (1)
month but not more than one (1) year, or a fine of not less than Ten thousand
pesos (₱10,000.00) but not more than One hundred thousand pesos
(₱100,000.00) or both imprisonment and fine, at the discretion of the court shall
be imposed upon any person, whether or not acting in conspiracy with public
officials, who, by act or omission, shall:

   1. Submit falsified documents in order to avail of the benefits under this
      program;
   2. Cause or aid in the grant of financial support to persons who are not
      qualified beneficiaries of this program; or
   3. Cause or aid in the grant of financial support in excess of or below what is
      provided under this program.
A public official who commits or aids in the commission of any of the acts
provided herein shall be penalized with temporary disqualification to hold public
office. Administrative sanctions shall be imposed without prejudice to penalties
as may be applicable under existing laws.

SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within six (6) months from
the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of the DSWD, in consultation with
relevant agencies and stakeholders, shall formulate and promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations to effectively implement the provisions of this
Act.

SECTION 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s
appropriations of the DSWD. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. All laws, executive orders, proclamations, rules,
regulations, and other issuances or parts inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

SECTION 11. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days following its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.
