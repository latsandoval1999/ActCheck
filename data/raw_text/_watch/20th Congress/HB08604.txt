                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE


The Philippines is home to a wealth of marine life. With our rich marine and inland
resources, our country is among the major fish producing countries in the world. These
marine and inland resources serve as vital pillars of the national economy while playing
an indispensable role in ensuring food security for millions of Filipinos.

According to the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources’ “2022 Philippine Fisheries
Profile1” there were a total of 2,302,648 registered fisherfolk in our country with 51.0%
engaged in capture fishing which is the most common source of income among fisherfolk.

However, despite the richness of our marine life, data from the Philippine Statistics
Authority reveal a stark contradiction: in 2023, 27.4% of fisherfolk lived below poverty
line and remain to be one of the poorest sectors of our society2 with some earning as little
as P250 a day.3

Rising fuel prices and market volatility threaten the livelihoods of municipal fisherfolk,
who play a vital role in our economy and the food security of millions.

This proposed bill establishes an institutionalized fuel subsidy program in the amount of
two thousand five hundred pesos (₱2,500.00) monthly for each eligible beneficiary to
support the livelihood of our fisherfolk.

                            AN ACT
       PROVIDING FUEL SUBSIDY FOR MUNICIPAL FISHERFOLK,
    APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       SEC. 1. Short Title.- This Act shall be known as the “Bayanihang Pambangka
Act of 2026”

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State recognizes the important role of
municipal fisherfolk in ensuring the food security of our country. It is hereby declared
the policy of the State to provide support to the fishery sector and protect the rights and
welfare of municipal fisherfolk, in recognition of their sacrifices and contributions to the
nation, and in pursuit of the Constitutional mandate to promote social justice in all
phases of development.

       SEC. 3. The Bayanihang Pambangka Program.- There is hereby created the
Bayanihang Pambangka Program, hereinafter referred to as the Program which shall
grant a monthly fuel voucher amounting to not less than Two Thousand Five Hundred
Pesos (₱2,500.00) per month for each eligible beneficiary under this Act: Provided,
That the amount shall be adjusted annually to account for inflation, fuel prices, and
fiscal sustainability. The Program shall be implemented by the Department of
Agriculture (DA), through the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR).

        SEC. 4. Beneficiaries.- The beneficiaries of this Act shall be municipal
fisherfolk engaged in fishing within municipal waters, whether coastal or inland, who
own or operate motorized fishing vessels of three (3) gross tons or less, either
individually or through a fisherfolk organization, cooperative or association. Provided,
That such beneficiaries are duly listed in the Municipal Fisherfolk Registration System
or “FishR” of the DA, the National Program for Municipal Fishing Vessels and Gears
Registration or “BoatR” of the BFAR, or the boat registry system of the concerned local
government unit (LGU): Provided, finally, That municipal fisherfolk and fishing vessels
that are not yet registered under the aforementioned registry systems may still avail of

the benefits of this Act provided they register in any of the aforementioned registry
systems within the periods set by the DA, BFAR, or the LGU.

        SEC. 5. Convergence of Social Services.- Beneficiaries of this program shall
also be automatically enrolled and covered under the National Health Insurance program
of the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth). The Social Security System
(SSS) shall also provide micro-insurance mechanisms to beneficiaries of the program.

       SEC. 6. Non-Diminution of Benefits.- Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
reduce existing benefits granted under existing laws, decrees, executive orders, or any
contract, agreement, or policy between the employer and employee.

        SEC. 7. Appropriations.- The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the Department of
Agriculture. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continuation of this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within one hundred twenty
(120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DA together with BFAR and in
consultation with other relevant stakeholders including the Department of Energy (DOE),
Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG), as well as concerned stakeholders
especially municipal fisherfolk, cooperatives, and associations. Failure of the relevant
government agencies to promulgate the IRR within the period provided shall subject the
heads of these agencies to administrative penalties under applicable civil service laws.
Should the IRR contain provisions that are contrary to this Act, the heads of the
government agencies responsible for such provision, when done in bad faith or with
gross negligence, shall be held administratively liable.

       SEC. 9. Separability Clause.- If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
declared unconstitutional, the other provisions which are not affected thereby shall
remain valid and subsisting.

       SEC. 10. Repealing Clause.- All laws, executive order, presidential decrees,
presidential proclamations, letters of instruction, rules and regulations inconsistent with
the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

        SEC 11. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
