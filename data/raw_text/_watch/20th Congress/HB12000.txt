                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Every peso in the national budget comes from the Filipino people. It comes from the taxes
paid by workers who toil every day to provide for their families, from the earnings of small business
owners who struggle to keep their enterprises alive, and from the contributions of millions of ordinary
citizens who expect their government to put public resources to work for the common good.

        Behind every allocation is a Filipino family hoping for better healthcare, a student waiting for
a decent classroom, a farmer seeking support for his livelihood, a worker aspiring for better
opportunities, and a community longing for roads, bridges, and essential public services. The national
budget, therefore, is not merely a financial document containing figures and expenditures. It is a
reflection of whose needs are heard, whose concerns are prioritized, and whose lives are ultimately
improved by government.

        The 1987 Philippine Constitution recognizes the vital role of people's organizations in nation-
building and guarantees the right of the people and their organizations to effective and reasonable
participation at all levels of social, political, and economic decision-making. This constitutional
guarantee must extend meaningfully to the preparation and authorization of the Annual National
Budget, where decisions involving billions of pesos directly affect the lives and future of every
Filipino.

         Yet, despite existing consultation mechanisms, the participation of ordinary citizens and Civil
Society Organizations (CSOs) in the national budget process remains limited. The preparation and
deliberation of the national budget are largely undertaken through government and legislative
institutions, often leaving communities and the organizations that represent them with limited
opportunities to influence the allocation of public resources.

        It is not enough for the people to be informed of how their money is spent after decisions have
already been made. They must also be given a meaningful opportunity to express their needs, present
their recommendations, question priorities, and participate in determining where public funds should
be directed.
        Recent controversies involving the allocation and utilization of public funds have further
underscored the urgent need to strengthen transparency, accountability, and public oversight. When
resources intended for classrooms, hospitals, livelihood programs, infrastructure, and essential social
services are misallocated or improperly utilized, it is not merely the government that suffers. It is the
Filipino family that loses access to opportunities, the student whose education is compromised, the
patient who is deprived of adequate healthcare, and the community whose development is delayed.

         Safeguarding the national budget must therefore be a shared responsibility. Government
institutions must remain accountable for the proper management of public funds, while the people
must be empowered to participate in ensuring that these resources are directed toward legitimate,
necessary, and meaningful public priorities. This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize the
participation of CSOs throughout the preparation and authorization of the Annual National Budget,
from the formulation of agency budget proposals by National Government Agencies (NGAs) and
Government-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GOCCs) to the deliberations and authorization of
the national budget by Congress. Specifically, the measure establishes a clear and transparent
accreditation system and provides accredited CSOs with meaningful opportunities to participate in
budget consultations and hearings, submit position papers and alternative budget proposals, examine
relevant budget documents, and present recommendations concerning government programs and
expenditures.

         It likewise mandates the appropriate committees of both Houses of Congress to allocate a
hearing day for the presentation of the positions of accredited CSOs on the proposed national budget.
Through this mechanism, the voices of sectors and communities that are often underrepresented in
national policymaking may be heard and considered in the legislative process. Furthermore, the
measure strengthens public access to budget information by providing for the timely disclosure of
relevant documents, committee reports, and records of budget deliberations, including those of the
bicameral conference committee, subject to the constitutional powers, internal rules, and applicable
confidentiality requirements of Congress. It also establishes safeguards to ensure that accreditation is
administered fairly and that the participation of legitimate civil society organizations is not arbitrarily
restricted.

        The participation of CSOs must not be reduced to a mere formality or a requirement satisfied
through occasional consultations. It must become a meaningful institutional process through which
the people can contribute to budget priorities, examine government proposals, and help ensure that
public resources serve their intended purpose. Ultimately, the national budget belongs to the Filipino
people. Every allocation must carry with it the responsibility to serve the public interest, every
government expenditure must be open to scrutiny, and every decision involving public funds must be
guided by the needs and aspirations of the citizens who make these resources possible.

       A government that listens to its people is a government that recognizes that development
cannot be measured solely by the amount of money appropriated, but by the lives improved, the
opportunities created, and the communities uplifted through public spending. The people should not
merely be spectators in the preparation of the national budget. They are its rightful stakeholders, its
ultimate beneficiaries, and its most important guardians.

      In the pursuit of a more transparent, accountable, and participatory government, the
immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE PARTICIPATION OF CIVIL SOCIETY ORGANIZATIONS
 (CSOs) IN THE PREPARATION AND AUTHORIZATION PROCESS OF THE ANNUAL
NATIONAL BUDGET, PROVIDING EFFECTIVE MECHANISMS THEREFOR, AND FOR
                            OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Bantay Budget Act."

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to facilitate the
establishment of adequate consultation and feedback mechanisms to promote meaningful and
effective participation of Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) in the preparation and authorization of
the annual national budget in order to ensure that the annual appropriations will be an instrument of
development that is reflective of the national objectives, strategies and plans beneficial to the people.

        The State further recognizes that public participation is essential to ensuring transparency,
accountability, and responsiveness in the allocation and utilization of public funds. Accordingly,
CSOs shall be provided with meaningful opportunities to participate in the formulation, review,
deliberation, and monitoring of the national budget, consistent with the Constitution and the rules
and procedures of the respective government institutions.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are defined as
follows:

(a) Accreditation refers to the registration process of CSOs with the National Government Agencies
(NGAs) and Government-Owned or Controlled Corporations (GOCCs), in order to allow them to
participate in the preparation and authorization of the annual national budget;

(b) Accredited CSOs refers to CSOs that have fulfilled the requirements under Section 5 of this Act;

(c) Budget Call refers to the budget document issued by the Department of Budget and Management
(DBM) and sent to all NGAs and GOCCs, which contains the policy guidelines and procedures in
the preparation of the annual budget, including the budget preparation calendar for any given year.
The issuance of a Budget Call signals the beginning of the budget preparation phase;

(d) Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) include non-government organizations (NGOs), cause-
oriented groups, people's organizations, cooperatives, trade unions, professional associations, faith-
based organizations, media groups, Indigenous Peoples movements, foundations, and other citizen
groups formed primarily for social and economic development, which monitor government projects,
engage in policy discussions, and actively participate in collaborative activities with Government;

(e) Conflict of Interest refers to a condition which occurs when a CSO or any of its key officers are
involved in multiple interests which prevent an impartial or unbiased attitude towards their
participation in the national budget process;

(f) Constructive Engagement refers to a partnership between Government and counterpart CSOs
marked by sustained dialogue towards problem solving while respecting and retaining the focus on
assertion for reforms;

(g) Government-Owned or Controlled Corporation (GOCC) refers to any agency organized as a
stock or non-stock corporation, vested with functions relating to public needs whether governmental
or proprietary in nature, and owned by the Government of the Republic of the Philippines directly or
through its instrumentalities either wholly, or, where applicable as in the case of stock corporations,
to the extent of at least a majority of its outstanding capital stock;

(h) National Government Agency (NGA) refers to a unit of the National Government, including the
Judiciary, both Houses of Congress, and Constitutional Offices; and

(i) Participatory Mechanisms refer to the means by which CSOs can participate meaningfully and
effectively in the government process of formulating and authorizing an annual national budget that
is responsive to the needs of the different sectors in our society.

        SEC. 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all NGAs and GOCCs whose budget is included
in the Annual General Appropriations Act.

        All covered NGAs and GOCCs shall establish appropriate mechanisms to facilitate the
participation of accredited CSOs in their respective budget preparation processes, subject to their
respective mandates and applicable laws.

       The participation of CSOs in the budget process of the Senate and the House of
Representatives shall be governed by the respective rules and procedures of each House, consistent
with the provisions of this Act.

        SEC. 5. Accreditation. – The application for accreditation for the Senate, the House of
Representatives, the Judiciary, and the Constitutional Offices shall be filed at the Office of the
Secretary, the Secretary-General, the Supreme Court Clerk of Court, and the Office of the Chairman,
respectively. The application for accreditation for other NGAs and GOCCs may be filed at their
respective regional or central office.

        The application form shall be prescribed by each office and the application for accreditation
shall be accompanied by the following documents:

(a) Articles of Incorporation and By-Laws of the applicant CSO;

(b) Certificate of Registration and/or other such similar documents, issued by an appropriate
government agency tasked to register civil society organizations such as but not limited to, the
Securities and Exchange Commission, Bureau of Labor Relations, Department of Human
Settlements and Urban Development, Insurance Commission, and the Cooperative Development
Authority;
(c) The list of officers, directors, trustees, and members;

(d) Duly audited financial statements for the past three (3) years showing the assets and liabilities of
the organization;

(e) Full disclosure of the sources of funds; and

(f) Full disclosure of service contracts with other NGAs, CSOs, private corporations, other CSOs,
and other engagements which may result in conflict of interest.

        Upon approval of the application, the head of the concerned NGA or GOCC shall issue the
certificate of accreditation. This shall be valid for three (3) years following its issuance, except for
the certificate of accreditation issued by the Senate and the House of Representatives, which shall be
valid for the entire term of the Congress under which such certificate of accreditation was issued.

       No application for accreditation shall be disapproved, except upon due notice and hearing.
The decision of the head of the concerned NGA or GOCC shall be rendered within twenty (20)
working days from the time of the filing of the application. Failure to act on the application shall be
deemed an automatic approval of the application for accreditation of the concerned CSO, and the
issuance of a certificate of accreditation shall necessarily ensue.

       For purposes of this Act, the concerned NGA or GOCC shall acknowledge receipt of the
application and inform the applicant of any deficiency in its documentary requirements within a
reasonable period. An applicant shall be given a reasonable opportunity to comply with such
requirements.

        The accreditation process shall be transparent, accessible, and non-discriminatory. In
evaluating applications, the concerned agency shall consider the nature, size, organizational
structure, and actual operations of the applicant CSO, without prejudice to the documentary
requirements prescribed under this Section.

        SEC. 6. CSO Participation. – In the interest of transparency, CSO participation shall be a
component in the preparation and authorization of the annual national budget. All NGAs and GOCCs
shall be required to formulate and adopt guidelines outlining the mechanism for the accreditation and
active participation of the CSOs in the preparation and authorization of the annual national budget.
The DBM shall ensure that the NGA or GOCC has complied with the above-mentioned requirement.

        Accredited CSOs, through their duly authorized representatives, shall be allowed to
participate as resource persons in the budget hearings to be conducted by the appropriate Committees
of each House of Congress, provided that they comply with the guidelines to be issued by both
Houses of Congress.

       Notwithstanding their participation in the budget hearings to be conducted by the appropriate
Committees of each House of Congress as provided in the preceding paragraph, the Committee on
Appropriations of the House of Representatives and the Committee on Finance of the Senate shall
schedule one hearing day for the presentation of the positions of accredited CSOs on the budget.

       The DBM and all concerned NGAs and GOCCs shall establish procedures for receiving,
evaluating, and responding to position papers, alternative budget proposals, and recommendations
submitted by accredited CSOs during the budget preparation stage.
        The participation of CSOs shall cover the identification of sectoral needs, review of proposed
programs and projects, examination of budget allocations, and submission of recommendations on
the prioritization and utilization of public resources.

       SEC. 7. Privileges and Incentives. – An accredited CSO shall enjoy the following privileges
and incentives:

(a) To have similar participation rights accorded to NGOs in the Local Development Councils
pursuant to the provisions of Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the "Local Government
Code of 1991," in setting the direction of economic and social development, coordinating
development efforts within its territorial jurisdiction;

(b) To receive notices and participate as resource person through its duly authorized representatives
in any regular and consultative public meetings, hearings, conferences, and dialogues on matters
related to the preparation and authorization of the annual national budget;

(c) To be allowed to observe at the budget deliberations and at the bicameral conference, subject to
the applicable rules and procedures of the respective Houses of Congress;

(d) Access to copies of the budget proposals submitted by the NGAs or GOCCs to the DBM;

(e) To provide position papers to the DBM on the budget proposal of NGAs or GOCCs submitted
pursuant to the Budget Call;

(f) To submit its own alternative or proposed budget or position paper;

(g) Access to issuances such as, but not limited to, policy orders, circulars and memoranda that are
related to the budget;

(h) Access to the documents prepared by the DBM for submission to Congress by the President as a
basis for the General Appropriations Bill required under Article VII, Section 22 of the Constitution;

(i) To present written proposals on projects and activities of the government entities whose budget is
under deliberation; and

(j) Immediate access to stenographic notes and committee reports released by both Houses, including
the bicameral conference committee report. The bicameral conference committee meetings and all
other meetings related or associated thereto shall be recorded and be made accessible immediately to
promote transparency and to allow members of both Houses to easily determine how conflicting
provisions in the proposed national budget were reconciled or resolved prior to the submission
thereof for ratification by both Houses of Congress.

        The respective Houses of Congress shall establish appropriate procedures to facilitate the
timely publication and accessibility of relevant budget deliberation records, subject to their
constitutional powers, internal rules, and lawful restrictions on confidential information.

        Accredited CSOs shall likewise be allowed to submit written observations on significant
changes in budgetary allocations, proposed amendments, and realignments during the congressional
deliberation of the annual national budget.

      SEC. 8. Notice to CSOs. – Notice to CSOs may be sent through electronic mail, facsimile
machine, postal mail, and by posting at the website of the NGA or GOCC concerned. Any of the said
modes of sending notices shall be deemed compliance to the requirements under Section 7(a) of this
Act.

       The concerned NGAs and GOCCs shall maintain updated contact information of accredited
CSOs and provide reasonable advance notice of scheduled budget consultations, hearings,
conferences, and other activities relevant to the annual national budget.

        The notice shall indicate the subject matter, date, time, venue or online platform, applicable
participation requirements, and procedures for submitting position papers or other written
recommendations.

        SEC. 9. Conflict of Interest. – It is the responsibility of the CSO to inform the NGA or
GOCC of a conflict of interest in their work with the concerned agency, if and when such a situation
arises.

        However, if the NGA or GOCC deems that the CSO has a conflict of interest with the work
of such NGA or GOCC, the latter shall allow the applicant-CSO to explain and prove that the
conflict of interest does not exist.

        The existence of a conflict of interest shall not automatically disqualify a CSO from
participation. The concerned agency shall determine whether the conflict materially affects the
impartiality or credibility of the CSO's participation and shall provide the organization an
opportunity to explain the circumstances.

       SEC. 10. Cancellation of Accreditation. – Upon receipt of a verified complaint in writing,
which charges the holder of a certificate of accreditation of having procured their certificate of
accreditation by fraud or deceit, the head of the NGA or GOCC shall notify and serve a copy of the
complaint to the CSO concerned, who shall be given an opportunity to be heard. Inaction by the
NGA or GOCC on the said complaint within twenty (20) working days from the receipt of such
complaint shall be deemed a dismissal of the complaint against the CSO involved.

       Cancellation of accreditation may likewise be considered in cases of material
misrepresentation, submission of falsified documents, or substantial violation of the requirements
and obligations under this Act.

        No accreditation shall be cancelled without due notice, an opportunity to respond, and a
written decision stating the factual and legal grounds for such cancellation.

       The cancellation of accreditation shall not prevent the concerned CSO from applying for
accreditation again, subject to applicable laws and regulations.

       SEC. 11. Non-Accredited CSOs. – Non-accredited CSOs may still participate in the budget
process by submitting position papers for the consideration of the NGA or GOCC and by attending
and observing public consultations. They may be invited to expound on their critique or proposals
when said institutions deem it fit.

        Nothing in this Act shall be construed to deprive non-accredited CSOs of the rights,
privileges, and incentives accorded to them by the Constitution, existing laws, executive orders, rules
and regulations, and other issuances.

      SEC. 12. Public and Timely Disclosure of Budget Documents and Other Related Data. –
For CSOs to participate in an informed manner, the NGA or GOCC shall post on their websites or
other public posting areas, budget documents to which CSOs shall have access, as provided for under
Section 7 of this Act.

        The DBM, in coordination with the concerned NGAs and GOCCs, shall establish a
centralized and publicly accessible mechanism or portal through which relevant budget documents
may be accessed, subject to applicable laws and regulations.

       The documents and information to be disclosed shall include, but shall not be limited to:

       (a) The Budget Call and related budget preparation guidelines;

       (b) Agency budget proposals and supporting budget justifications;

       (c) Proposed programs, activities, and projects and their corresponding funding requirements;

       (d) The National Expenditure Program and supporting documents submitted to Congress;

       (e) Budget documents and amendments made during congressional deliberations;

       (f) Committee reports and other publicly releasable records relating to the deliberation and
authorization of the annual national budget; and

       (g) The enacted General Appropriations Act and other relevant budget execution documents.

       The disclosure of budget documents shall be undertaken in a timely, accessible, and
understandable manner to enable CSOs and the general public to meaningfully examine, analyze,
and provide recommendations on the allocation and utilization of public funds.

        SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DBM, in coordination with the Senate, the House of Representatives, the
Judiciary, Constitutional Offices, concerned NGAs and GOCCs, and accredited CSO coalitions, shall
promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations to ensure the effective implementation
of this Act.

       The respective Houses of Congress, the Judiciary, and Constitutional Offices shall adopt their
own implementing guidelines consistent with their respective constitutional mandates, institutional
independence, and internal rules.

       SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining part or provisions not otherwise affected shall remain in force.

        SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – Any law, decree, ordinance, administrative circulars not
consistent with any provision of this Act is hereby amended, repealed, or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 16. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.

Approved.
