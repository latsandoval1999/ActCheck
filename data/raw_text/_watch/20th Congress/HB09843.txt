                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The rapid pace of technological change, evolving labor market demands,
and the increasing prevalence of non-traditional forms of employment have
fundamentally transformed how Filipinos learn, work, and acquire skills. Today,
a growing number of learners are simultaneously employed, self-employed, or
engaged in livelihood activities while pursuing education and training.

      However, the existing education framework remains largely structured
around long-duration, full-time programs that often require learners to step away
from employment, resulting in lost income and limited access for working
individuals.

      This situation poses a significant challenge to inclusive human capital
development and workforce resilience. Many Filipinos—particularly employed
students, freelancers, contractual workers, and those in the informal sector—
require learning opportunities that are flexible, practical, affordable, and
responsive to real-world skills needs. There is therefore an urgent need for a legal
and policy framework that recognizes alternative learning pathways without
undermining academic quality and integrity.

       This proposed measure, entitled the “Abot-Kayang Pangarap Act,” seeks
to address this gap by formally recognizing microcredentials and short courses as
legitimate and integral components of the Philippine education and training
ecosystem. These credentials provide competency-based, outcomes-focused
learning opportunities that may be completed over shorter periods, delivered
through flexible modalities, and aligned with industry and workforce
requirements.

      The bill establishes a National Microcredential and Short Course Quality
Assurance and Credential Framework to ensure credibility, comparability, and
public trust in such credentials. It mandates harmonized standards among the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority (TESDA), and the Department of Education (DepEd),
while respecting institutional autonomy and existing mandates.

       Central to this measure is the promotion of stackability, portability, credit
equivalency, and Recognition of Prior Learning, allowing learners to accumulate
microcredentials and short courses toward formal academic qualifications
without unnecessary repetition of learning already demonstrated. The bill further
authorizes CHED to promulgate outcomes-based rules governing academic credit
conversion and articulation, guided by transparency, academic integrity, and
international best practices.

      Recognizing the realities faced by working learners, the Act provides
protections and reasonable accommodations for employed students and
encourages employer support for employee upskilling, consistent with labor laws
and management prerogatives. It also establishes a secure Digital Credential
Registry to safeguard credential integrity, enable verification, and promote
learner mobility.

       Ultimately, the Abot-Kayang Pangarap Act affirms the State’s
commitment to lifelong learning, inclusive access to education, and workforce
development by ensuring that opportunities for skills acquisition and professional
growth remain attainable, flexible, and responsive to national development
priorities—so that education remains within reach for all Filipinos, regardless of
employment status or economic circumstance.

SECTION 1. Title — This Act shall be known as the “Abot-Kayang Pangarap
Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote lifelong learning, inclusive human capital development, and workforce
resilience in response to technological change, evolving labor market demands,
and national development priorities.

The State recognizes that education and employment increasingly occur
simultaneously and that many learners are employed, self-employed, or
engaged in livelihood activities. Education policy must therefore enable
access to quality learning that is practical, flexible, and financially attainable,
without requiring withdrawal from employment, loss of income, or undue
economic burden.


Toward this end, the State shall recognize microcredentials and short courses
as legitimate components of the national education and training system, ensure
their quality, stackability, and portability, and align them with workforce
development strategies, employment policies, and inclusive economic growth,
so that opportunities for learning and skills development remain within reach
and accessible for all.


SEC. 3. Definition of Terms — For purposes of this Act, the following terms
are hereby defined:

   (a) Microcredentials     refer to competency-based, outcomes-focused
       certifications of learning attesting to specific, measurable, and
       verifiable competencies acquired over a short duration and assessed
       against defined academic, professional, or industry standards;


   (b) Short Courses refer to structured learning programs of limited duration
       which may be modular, stackable, or standalone, delivered through
       face-to-face, online, blended, hybrid, or work-based modalities;

   the Department of Education (DepEd) to offer microcredentials or short

SEC 4. Legal Recognition of Microcredentials and Short Courses —
Microcredentials and short courses are hereby recognized as lawful,
legitimate, and integral components of the Philippine education and training
ecosystem.

Such credentials shall complement, and not replace, formal undergraduate,
graduate, diploma, and certification programs and shall serve as flexible entry
points, exit points, or progression pathways within the education system.


No educational institution, employer, or government agency shall deny
consideration of duly accredited microcredentials solely on the basis of
delivery mode or duration, subject to compliance with quality assurance
standards under this Act.



SEC. 5. National Quality Assurance and Credential Framework — There is
hereby established a National Microcredential and Short Course Quality
Assurance and Credential Framework to ensure credibility, comparability,
integrity, and public trust in microcredentials and short courses.

The Framework shall govern accreditation standards, learning outcomes
formulation, assessment methodologies, instructional design, faculty or trainer
qualifications, learner support mechanisms, and articulation across education
and training sectors.


CHED, TESDA, and DepEd shall exercise regulatory authority within their
respective mandates and harmonize standards and procedures consistent with
                                                                                  5
the inter-agency coordination mechanism established under this Act.


SEC. 6. Stackability and Portability to Formal Education Programs —
Accredited microcredentials and short courses may be stacked and credited
toward formal education programs, subject to the standards and rules
promulgated by CHED under this Act.



Higher education institutions shall publish transparent policies on credit
conversion,   accumulation     limits,   validity   periods,   and   assessment
requirements applicable to undergraduate and graduate programs.


No learner shall be required to repeat learning already demonstrated through
accredited microcredentials or recognized prior learning, except where
justified by licensure requirements, public safety considerations, or substantial
academic grounds.


SEC. 7. Authority of CHED to Establish Credit Equivalency Rules — The
Commission on Higher Education (CHED) is hereby authorized and mandated
to promulgate rules, standards, and guidelines governing the credit
equivalency, articulation, and academic recognition of microcredentials and
short courses toward undergraduate and graduate degree programs.


Such authority shall be exercised pursuant to Republic Act No. 7722,
otherwise known as the Higher Education Act of 1994 and guided by
transparency, outcomes-based assessment, academic integrity, public interest,
and international best practices.


                                                                                    6
Institutional autonomy shall be respected, provided that no institutional policy
shall be inconsistent with minimum national standards issued by CHED.



SEC. 8. Credit Equivalency and Recognition of Prior Learning — CHED
shall establish an integrated framework for credit equivalency and
Recognition of Prior Learning, covering microcredentials earned through
academic, industry-based, work- based, or community-based learning.

Industry-recognized credentials may be considered for academic credit only
upon compliance with CHED standards and alignment with program learning
outcomes. Industry recognition alone shall not automatically confer academic
credit.


Assessment methods, credit valuation, validity periods, and documentation
requirements shall be standardized and verifiable through official records and
national systems.


The provisions of Republic Act 12313 or the Lifelong Learning Development
Act shall be deemed applicable in developing the framework for credit
equivalency and recognition of prior learning.



SEC 9. Industry Co-Design and Workforce Alignment — Microcredentials
and short courses shall be aligned with national, regional, and sectoral
workforce development strategies and labor market priorities.

Industry participation shall be institutionalized in identifying skills needs,
defining   competency     standards,   validating   learning   outcomes,    and
periodically reviewing credentials for labor-market relevance, without
                                                                                   7
diminishing academic authority.


Government workforce development, employment facilitation, reskilling, and
upskilling programs shall recognize accredited microcredentials as valid
training and employability credentials.


SEC. 10. Rights and Protections of Employed Students — Employed
students enrolled in accredited microcredentials or short courses shall be
afforded reasonable protection, flexibility, and support in recognition of the
dual demands of work and study.

No employed student shall be denied enrollment, continued participation,
academic progression, or completion solely by reason of employment status
or work schedule, provided academic requirements are met.


No employed student shall be dismissed, discriminated against, or otherwise
prejudiced in employment solely by reason of enrollment, absent willful
neglect of employment duties or violation of lawful company policies.



SEC. 11. Duties of Educational Institutions — Educational institutions
offering microcredentials and short courses shall adopt learner-centered and
work-sensitive policies, including flexible pacing, asynchronous or blended
delivery, reasonable assessment schedules, alternative assessment modes
where appropriate, and access to academic advising and learner support
services.


Such measures shall be implemented without compromising academic
standards, learning outcomes, or credential integrity and shall be applied in a
                                                                                  8
fair, transparent, and non-arbitrary manner.


SEC 12. Employer Accommodation and Labor Law Alignment —
Employers are encouraged to provide reasonable accommodation to employed
students consistent with the Labor Code of the Philippines and related labor
laws. Such accommodation may include flexible work arrangements, adjusted
schedules, or training leave, subject to operational requirements and
management prerogative exercised in good faith.


Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish existing labor rights,
employer prerogatives, or collective bargaining agreements.



SEC. 13. Complaints and Redress Mechanisms for Employed Students —
An employed student who believes that rights under this Act have been
violated may file a complaint with the appropriate regulatory agency.

Complaints involving educational institutions shall be filed with CHED or
TESDA, as applicable; complaints involving employers shall be filed with the
Department of Labor and Employment, without prejudice to remedies
available under existing laws.


No employed student shall be subjected to retaliation, intimidation, or adverse
action for filing a complaint or participating in proceedings under this Act.



SEC. 14. Incentives and Financial Assistance — The State may provide
scholarships, training vouchers, learning credits, grants, or targeted subsidies
to support participation in accredited microcredentials and short courses,

                                                                                   9
particularly for employed students and priority sectors.


Employers      supporting     employee      upskilling     through    accredited
microcredentials may be eligible for incentives, subject to existing tax, labor,
and fiscal laws.


SEC. 15. Digital Credential Registry — A centralized, secure, and
interoperable Digital Credential Registry is hereby established to record
all accredited microcredentials and short courses issued under this Act.


The Registry shall serve as the official national repository for credential
verification, learner mobility, portability, transparency, and protection against
credential fraud.


Only credentials duly recorded in the Registry shall be recognized for
academic credit, employment, promotion, professional development, or
workforce certification.


SEC. 16. Inter-Agency Coordination Mechanism — CHED, TESDA,
DepEd, and the Department of Labor and Employment shall coordinate to
ensure coherent implementation of this Act, harmonized standards, and
alignment with workforce and employment strategies.
The agencies may issue joint circulars, advisories, or guidelines as may be
necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act.


SEC. 17. Appropriations — The amounts necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations

of the Commission on Higher Education, the Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority, the Department of Education, and the Department of

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation

SEC 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, CHED, TESDA, DepEd, and the Department
of Labor and Employment shall jointly promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations.

SEC 19. Separability Clause — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC 20. Repealing Clause — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


SEC. 21. Effectively – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
