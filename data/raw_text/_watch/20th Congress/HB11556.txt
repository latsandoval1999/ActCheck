                                                 AN ACT

ORDAINING A NATIONAL POLICY OF FREE HOUSING THROUGH USUFRUCT,
AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR



                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE


          Article 25 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the right to housing is a recognized
fundamental human right ensuring that every person has access to a safe, secure, and affordable place to
live. Adequate shelter means much more than just a roof and four walls.

           In keeping with international law, Article XIII, Section 9 of the Philippine Constitution mandates
the State to undertake a continuing program of urban land reform and housing for the underprivileged and
homeless in line with the policy of the State to promote a just and dynamic social order as enshrined in
Section 9, Article II of our fundamental law.

           Despite the government's continuing efforts, however, millions of Filipinos remain without
affordable, adequate, and secure shelter. This predicament is particularly significant among low-income
families, informal settlers, and other vulnerable sectors who are unable to acquire or sustain homeownership
due to the high cost of land, construction, and financing for housing.

         This bill seeks to establish a national policy providing qualified beneficiaries with free access to
government-owned housing through usufruct. Under this arrangement, beneficiaries are granted the right
to use and enjoy a government housing property for a specific period without transferring ownership of the
property to the beneficiary. This approach allows the government to make productive use of its housing
assets while providing qualified families with decent shelter.
        This bill proposes to accord underprivileged and homeless Filipino citizens who do not own any
real property anywhere in the country. It shall also be extended to government employees under the auspices
of Local Government units (LGUs) and other government agencies provided that the beneficiary does not
own any real property within the territorial jurisdiction of the LGU where the employee is assigned or
employed. This provision may help government institutions attract and retain personnel, particularly those
assigned to areas where housing costs are beyond their means.

        To ensure that government housing resources remain available to future beneficiaries, the grant of
usufruct shall not constitute a transfer of ownership and shall be subject to reasonable conditions on
occupancy, maintenance, use, and termination. Beneficiaries are expected to use the property as their
principal residence and to comply with the terms of the usufruct agreement. These safeguards will promote
responsible stewardship of public housing assets and protect government property from unauthorized sale,
transfer, or commercial use.

      The proposed policy is also intended to complement, rather than replace, existing national and local
government housing programs. By introducing usufruct as an additional mechanism for providing secure
housing, the government can expand access to shelter while maximizing the use of available public land
and housing properties. It may likewise provide LGUs and government agencies with greater flexibility in
addressing the housing needs of vulnerable communities and their qualified personnel.

      The urgency of this measure is underscored by the country's continuing housing deficit. Government
estimates indicate that, without sustained intervention, the national housing backlog could reach
approximately 22 million units by 2040. Addressing this gap requires not only the construction of additional
housing units but also innovative approaches that make existing government resources more accessible to
families who need them most.

       Free housing through usufruct offers a practical means of advancing the constitutional right to decent
shelter while preserving public ownership of government housing assets. It places the State's existing land
and housing resources at the service of Filipino families who have no adequate home of their own, while
ensuring that these resources remain available for public benefit over the long term.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:


       SECTION 1. Short title. - This Act shall be known as the "Free Housing Usufruct Act".

       SEC 2. Statement of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to promote a just and dynamic
social order pursuant to Section 9, Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution. To this end,
Section 9, Article XIII of the 1987 Constitution mandates the State to undertake a continuing
program of urban land reform and housing for the underprivileged and homeless.

        SEC 3. Free Housing Through Usufruct Program. - The National Housing Authority
(NHA), Local Government Units (LGU) and other government agencies engaged in the National
Shelter Program shall, as far as practicable, distribute housing units to qualified beneficiaries for
free through contracts of usufruct.
The NHA, LGU or other government agencies implementing housing projects shall, as owner,
retain ownership of the title to the housing unit, while the beneficiary, as usufructuary, shall be
allowed to enjoy the use of the property.
       SEC 4. Qualified Beneficiaries. - Only underprivileged and homeless Filipinos without
any ownership of any real property anywhere in the country may qualify as beneficiaries of the
free housing through a usufruct program. Provided, That LGUs and other government agencies
may extend the benefit to qualified personnel as part of its employee housing program.
       SEC 5. Inclusions of the Usufruct. - Usufruct shall include the right to the enjoyment and
use of the property for housing. Charges for utilities such as electricity, water and communication,
shall be on the account of the beneficiary, unless otherwise provided in the usufruct contract.

      SEC 6. Period of Usufruct. - The beneficiary shall continue to enjoy the use of the property
until disqualified under this Act. In the event of death of the beneficiary, the immediate family,
which includes the spouse, children and parents living with the beneficiary, shall be prioritized to
assume over the usufruct. Provided, That the immediate family is qualified under this Act.
      SEC 7. Prohibitions on the Usufruct. - The property shall only be used by the beneficiary
or his immediate family for residential purposes. The beneficiary shall not lease, rent, sell or in
any other manner transfer possession of the property under usufruct, nor allow any person other
than the beneficiary or his immediate family to use the property in whatever manner. Any violation
of this provision shall result in the immediate forfeiture of the usufruct and shall constitute
disqualification of the beneficiary and his immediate family as beneficiary of any housing program
of the government.

      SEC 8. Financing and Management. - The NHA shall provide financial and technical
assistance to the LGUs in the implementation of the program. The LGU where the housing project
is located shall be responsible for the management and maintenance of the subject properties of
the program. For this purpose, management shall include the determination of the beneficiaries of
the program and maintenance shall include ensuring that adequate utility services are accessible to
the beneficiaries. The LGU may contract with private entities for the maintenance of the subject
properties of the program.

      SEC 9. Appropriation. - The funds necessary to carry out the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against available appropriations of the NHA, LGUs, and other government
agencies engaged in the National Shelter Program, subject to existing budgeting, accounting and
auditing laws, rules, and regulations. The appropriations necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be prepared in accordance with regular government budget
procedures and shall be included in the budget under the annual General Appropriations Act
(GAA).

       SEC 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the NHA, Department of the Interior and Local Government and the
Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development shall promulgate the rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.
     SEC 11. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act shall be declared invalid, the
remaining parts thereof shall remain in full force and effect.

      SEC 12. Repealing Clause. - All laws, orders, issuances, rules and regulations or parts
thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
       SEC 13. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete publication
in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
