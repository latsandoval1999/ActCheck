                             AN ACT
 RECOGNIZING MOTORCYCLES AS PUBLIC UTILITY VEHICLES, AMENDING
FOR THIS PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 4136, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
   “LAND TRANSPORTATION AND TRAFFIC CODE,” AS AMENDED, AND
     REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7160, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “LOCAL
GOVERNMENT CODE OF 1991,” AS AMENDED, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Republic Act No. 4136 does not currently recognize two-wheeled motorcycles
as public utility vehicles (PUVs). The Local Government Code authorizes cities and
municipalities to regulate tricycles under Sections 447(3)(vi) and 458(3)(vi), but it is
silent on motorcycles-for-hire. The result is a regulatory gap: motorcycles are the
country’s most accessible two-wheeled mode for short trips and first/last-mile
connectivity, yet their carriage of passengers remains governed only by scattered
issuances, local ordinances, and a time-bound pilot program.

       This bill closes that gap by expressly classifying motorcycles-for-hire as PUVs
in the Land Transportation and Traffic Code and by aligning LGU franchising powers
under the Local Government Code. It is a counterpart measure to Senator Imee R.
Marcos’ Senate Bill No. 751.

       Urban congestion underscores the need for safe, regulated two-wheel public
transport. The 2024 TomTom Traffic Index places Davao City, Manila, and Caloocan
among the world’s slowest city centers, with average travel times per 10 kilometers of
about 32:59, 32:10, and 30:44, respectively, and Davao ranked in the global top ten
for worst traffic in 2024. These figures reflect substantial annual hours lost in rush hour
for commuters in all three Philippine cities, based on TomTom’s floating-car-data
methodology.
        Policy and regulatory context also point to the need for a clear statute. Republic
Act No. 4136 (Land Transportation and Traffic Code) pre-dates platform-mediated
transport and contains no category for motorcycles-for-hire; conversely, Republic Act
No. 7160 (Local Government Code) explicitly empowers sanggunians to regulate and
franchise tricycles but does not address motorcycles-for-hire. The counterpart bill
amends Section 3 and Section 7 of RA 4136 to define and classify “motorcycle-for-
hire,” and aligns Sections 447(3)(vi) and 458(3)(vi) of RA 7160 so LGUs can franchise
motorcycles-for-hire operating strictly within their jurisdictions, subject to LTFRB
guidelines.

        The measure also harmonizes with established safety law. It preserves the
helmet mandate and standards under the Motorcycle Helmet Act (RA 10054) and
directs LTO to enforce roadworthiness through type-specific inspections, permitted
accessories, and speed-limiting/monitoring devices. It further declares motorcycles-
for-hire to be common carriers for purposes of Civil Code diligence and liability—an
approach consistent with jurisprudence that defines common carriers by the nature of
the transport business rather than by vehicle type.

        Finally, the bill provides institutional clarity between national and local
regulation. LTFRB remains the public service franchising authority for route and
service standards, while LGUs can set place-specific operating rules (e.g., terminals,
lanes, parking) within their territory, consistent with DPWH right-of-way and traffic
management standards. This is responsive to the transition from the Department of
Transportation’s motorcycle-taxi pilot to a permanent legal framework, following official
statements in 2025 acknowledging the 2024 end of the pilot and the urgency of
legislation that protects commuters and livelihoods alike.

        By codifying a national standard for motorcycles-for-hire — including
classifications, licensing, insurance, safety equipment, roadworthiness, and clear LGU
coordination — Congress will replace ad-hoc, uneven regulation with a uniform, rights-
and safety-based framework that matches how Filipinos actually move through their
cities. The measure is straightforward: legal recognition in RA 4136, aligned LGU
powers in RA 7160, explicit safety and insurance requirements, and Civil Code-based
accountability. In a country where city-center trips routinely take half an hour or more
per 10 kilometers, lawful, well-regulated motorcycles-for-hire are not a loophole; they
are part of the solution.

       This is a counterpart bill of the Senate Bill No. 751 filed by Sen. Imee Marcos.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
 RECOGNIZING MOTORCYCLES AS PUBLIC UTILITY VEHICLES, AMENDING
FOR THIS PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 4136, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
   “LAND TRANSPORTATION AND TRAFFIC CODE,” AS AMENDED, AND
     REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7160, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “LOCAL

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Motorcycles-for-Hire
Act.”

       Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It shall be the policy of the State to provide
basic transport services to the people, safeguard their safety, and promote their
general welfare. Toward this end, the use of motorcycles-for-hire as an alternative
mode of public transportation is hereby recognized and regulated to provide mobility
in unserved and underserved rural areas, as well as in traffic-congested urban centers.

      Sec. 3. Amendment to Section 3 of Republic Act No. 4136. – Section 3 of
Republic Act No. 4136, as amended, otherwise known as the “Land Transportation
and Traffic Code,” is hereby amended to read as follows:

        “Section 3. Words and phrases defined. – As used in this Act:
        xxx
      (N) MOTORCYCLE-FOR-HIRE – ANY TWO-WHEELED MOTOR VEHICLE
      USED AS A MEANS OF PUBLIC TRANSPORTATION AND CARGO
      REGISTERED WITH THE LAND TRANSPORTATION OFFICE OR THE LAND
      TRANSPORTATION FRANCHISING AND REGULATORY BOARD (LTFRB);
      PROVIDED, THAT FOR TRANSPORTING PASSENGERS, THE
      MOTORCYCLE SHALL HAVE A MINIMUM ENGINE DISPLACEMENT OF 125
      CUBIC CENTIMETERS AND A BACKBONE TYPE BUILT.
      MOTORCYCLES-FOR-HIRE MAY BE OPERATED INDEPENDENTLY BY
      THE OWNER OF THE MOTORCYCLE, OR MADE AVAILABLE THROUGH A
      NETWORK OR DIGITAL TRANSPORT VEHICLE SERVICE (TNVS).”

     Sec. 4. Amendment to Section 7 of Republic Act No. 4136. – Section 7 of
Republic Act No. 4136 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      “Section 7. Registration Classification. – Every motor vehicle shall be registered
      under one of the following described classifications:
      (a) xxx
      (b) xxx
      (c) xxx
      (d) Public utility automobiles;
      (e) Public utility trucks;
      (f) Taxis and auto-calesas;
      (g) Garage automobiles;
      (h) Garage trucks;
      (i) Hire trucks;
      (j) Trucks owned by contractors and customs brokers and customs agents;
      and,
      (k) MOTORCYCLES-FOR-HIRE.

      Application for registration under these classifications shall be accompanied by
      a certificate of public convenience or a special permit issued by the [Public
      Service Commission] LAND TRANSPORTATION FRANCHISING AND
      REGULATORY BOARD (LTFRB), and motor vehicles registered under these
      classifications shall be subject to the Public Service Law, rules and regulations,
      as well as the provisions of this Act.

      FURTHER PROVIDED THAT MOTORCYCLES-FOR-HIRE OPERATING
      STRICTLY WITHIN THE TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION OF A LOCAL
      GOVERNMENT UNIT, WHETHER THESE MOTORCYCLES BELONG TO A
      TRANSPORT NETWORK, OR ARE MEMBERS OF LOCAL COOPERATIVE
      OR CORPORATION, OR ARE INDIVIDUAL OWNERS/OPERATORS OF THE
      MOTORCYCLE, SHALL BE SUBJECT TO THE PROVISIONS OF SECTION
      447 (3)(VI) AND SECTION 458 (3)(VI) OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7160.”
      Sec. 5. Amendment to Section 447(3)(vi) of Republic Act No. 7160. –
Section 447(3)(vi) of Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the “Local
Government Code of 1991,” is hereby amended to read as follows:

      “(vi) Subject to the guidelines prescribed by the [Department of Transportation
      and Communications] LTFRB, regulate the operation of tricycles AND
      MOTORCYCLES-FOR-HIRE AS PUBLIC TRANSPORT OPERATING
      STRICTLY WITHIN THE TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION OF A LOCAL
      GOVERNMENT UNIT, WHETHER THESE MOTORCYCLES BELONG TO A
      TRANSPORT NETWORK, OR ARE MEMBERS OF LOCAL COOPERATIVE
      OR CORPORATION, OR ARE INDIVIDUAL OWNERS/OPERATORS OF THE
      MOTORCYCLE, and grant franchises for the operation thereof within the
      territorial jurisdiction of the municipality.”

      Sec. 6. Amendment to Section 458(3)(vi) of Republic Act No. 7160. –
Section 458(3)(vi) of Republic Act No. 7160 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      “(vi) Subject to the guidelines prescribed by the [Department of Transportation
      and Communications] LTFRB, regulate the operation of tricycles AND
      MOTORCYCLES-FOR-HIRE AS PUBLIC TRANSPORT OPERATING
      STRICTLY WITHIN THE TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION OF A LOCAL
      GOVERNMENT UNIT, WHETHER THESE MOTORCYCLES BELONG TO A
      TRANSPORT NETWORK, OR ARE MEMBERS OF LOCAL COOPERATIVE
      OR CORPORATION, OR ARE INDIVIDUAL OWNERS/OPERATORS OF THE
      MOTORCYCLE, and grant franchises for the operation thereof within the
      territorial jurisdiction of the city.”

        Sec. 7. Roadworthiness of Motorcycles-for-Hire. – The Land Transportation
Office (LTO) shall guarantee the roadworthiness of motorcycles-for-hire by requiring
emission testing and other safeguards before registration and every renewal. No
modification shall be made thereafter on motorcycles-for-hire, except for the possible
installation of luggage carriers, saddlebags, step boards or foot pegs, and appropriate
speed limiters and monitoring devices, based on LTO guidelines.

       Sec. 8. Liability and Diligence. – Motorcycles-for-hire are hereby declared as
common carriers for purposes of determining liability and the degree of diligence
required in transporting goods or passengers. The presumption of negligence in
breach of contract shall likewise apply, in accordance with existing laws.

       Sec. 9. Driver’s Safety. – In accordance with Section 23-A of Republic Act No.
4136, as amended, the LTO shall establish requirements for issuing driver’s licenses
to applicants for motorcycles-for-hire, including training, theoretical and practical
testing, and health examinations. Safety helmets shall be provided as required by law.
      Sec. 10. Insurance. – In addition to driver’s and motor vehicle insurance,
operators of motorcycles-for-hire shall be required to secure insurance coverage for
both passengers and cargo.

        Sec. 11. Local Government Unit Duties. – Prior to issuing franchises, LGUs
shall develop and issue guidelines for the operation of motorcycles-for-hire within their
jurisdiction, subject to approval by the Department of Public Works and Highways
(DPWH). Such guidelines may include designated motorcycle lanes, parking areas,
terminals, and other facilities for an efficient motorcycle-for-hire transport system.

      Sec. 12. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, decrees, presidential issuances,
executive orders, ordinances, rules, and regulations inconsistent with this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

         Sec. 13. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall not be affected and shall continue in
full force and effect.

       Sec. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation in the Philippines.
