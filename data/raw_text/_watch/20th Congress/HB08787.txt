                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to strengthen the regulatory powers of the National Food Authority (NFA)
to support the rice industry and ensure consumer protection through adequate supply and stable
prices of rice, amending for the purpose Republic Act No. 8178, otherwise known as the
“Agricultural Tariffication Act,” as amended.

         Presidential Decree No. 4 created the National Grains Authority, later renamed the National
Food Authority under PD No. 1770, with a clear mandate to ensure food security and stabilize the
price and supply of rice and other staple grains. Historically, the NFA exercised broad regulatory
powers, including licensing, warehouse inspection, seizure of hoarded stocks, and price
stabilization, enabling it to act decisively against market manipulation and to support rice farmers
through a guaranteed market for palay.

        However, with the enactment of Republic Act No. 11203 or the Rice Tariffication Law
(RTL), these regulatory functions were effectively removed. While the RTL liberalized rice imports
and replaced quantitative restrictions with tariffs, it significantly reduced the NFA’s role in
maintaining a limited buffer stock, primarily for disaster response. As a result, the NFA lost its
capacity to intervene in the market to stabilize rice prices and deter profiteering, hoarding, and
other abusive practices.

        The consequences have been substantial. The Philippines remains one of the largest rice
importers in the world, importing over 3 million metric tons of rice annually in recent years. Despite
increased imports, domestic rice prices remain volatile, and retail prices continue to be among the
highest in Southeast Asia. At the same time, farmgate prices of palay have declined in several
periods, placing significant pressure on local farmers and threatening the sustainability of domestic
rice production.

        For farmers, reduced government procurement and increased competition from imports
have exposed them to depressed prices and trader dominance, undermining rural livelihoods. For
consumers, weak regulatory oversight has contributed to price spikes, hoarding, and artificial
scarcity, particularly during supply disruptions and peak demand periods.

         Republic Act No. 12078 introduced corrective measures by granting the Department of
Agriculture certain regulatory powers over rice trade. However, these reforms remain insufficient
to fully address persistent price instability and supply vulnerabilities in the rice market.

       This bill therefore proposes to restore essential regulatory and market intervention powers
to the NFA. These include: (1) monitoring and regulating warehouse operations; (2) licensing
traders and processors across the rice supply chain; (3) seizing hoarded stocks and ordering their
public release in cases of scarcity or price manipulation; (4) establishing and enforcing floor prices
for palay to protect farmers’ incomes; and (5) managing buffer stocks through transparent and
market-responsive mechanisms. The measure also allows the NFA to utilize revenues from rice
sales to procure palay from local farmers at support prices.

        Importantly, this measure does not seek to reverse the liberalized import regime under the
RTL. Rather, it introduces calibrated and strategic state intervention in cases of market failure. By
restoring the NFA’s regulatory role, the bill aims to strike a balance between market efficiency and
public welfare, promote a more resilient rice sector, and strengthen national food security.

        The restoration of the NFA’s regulatory functions is crucial to curbing profiteering,
monopolistic pricing, and hoarding in the rice market. It enables the government to respond
effectively to food security challenges while maintaining an open and competitive trade
environment.

        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
