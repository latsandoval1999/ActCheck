                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to establish a Climate Finance Unit (CFU) under the Climate
Change Commission. This institutionalizes a comprehensive national climate
finance framework and enhances access to multilateral climate financing
mechanisms and public-private partnerships.

       The Philippines remains among the countries most vulnerable to the adverse
impacts of climate change. Increasingly frequent and severe typhoons, floods,
droughts, sea level rise, and extreme heat continue to threaten lives, livelihoods,
infrastructure, food security, and economic development. While the country has
adopted key climate policies, access to and coordination of climate finance remain
fragmented across government agencies and local government units, limiting the
timely implementation of climate adaptation and mitigation initiatives.

       The proposed measure likewise integrates climate finance into national and
local development planning, promotes innovative financing instruments, and
strengthens monitoring and reporting systems to ensure that public investments
generate measurable climate resilience and sustainable development outcomes.
Establishing a centralized climate finance mechanism shall improve resource
mobilization, strengthen fiscal accountability, and accelerate the implementation of
climate-resilient programs nationwide.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
MAINSTREAMING     CLIMATE     ACTION     ACROSS    NATIONAL
DEVELOPMENT, CREATING FOR THE PURPOSE A CLIMATE FINANCE UNIT
UNDER THE CLIMATE CHANGE COMMISSION, AND APPROPRIATING
FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Green Financing for
Climate Change Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
afford full protection and the advancement of the right of the people to a healthful
ecology in accord with the rhythm and harmony of nature. In this light, it recognizes
that climate change poses significant risks to national development, economic
stability, food and water security, and the well-being of vulnerable communities,
particularly local government units exposed to climate hazards, by advancing the
right of present and future generations to a climate-resilient and sustainable future.

        Towards this end, the State shall strengthen institutional capacity for the
mobilization, coordination, and effective utilization of climate finance from
domestic, international, public, and private sources and ensure that climate finance
is accessible, predictable, transparent, and accountable, and aligned with national
priorities as embodied in the National Climate Change Action Plan (NCCAP) and
other development frameworks.

       Cognizant of the vulnerability of the Filipino people, particularly women, the
elderly, and children, to potential dangerous consequences of climate change, the
State shall promote equitable access to climate finance, especially for local
government units, marginalized sectors, and climate-vulnerable communities and
enhance the country's ability to access and leverage international climate finance
mechanisms, including grants, concessional financing, and technical assistance.

       SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act seeks to achieve the following:

      SEC. 4. Establishment of the Climate Finance Unit. – There is hereby established
a Climate Finance Unit (CFU) under the CCC, which shall serve as the central
coordinating, advisory, and technical body for all climate finance-related matters.

       The CFU shall act a central gateway of all funds coming from domestic and
international sources such as bilateral institutions, multilateral institutions, special
climate funds, international NGOs, private foundations, and special climate funds
for purposes of identifying funding sources, establishing clear and strategic
spending priorities, disbursing funds, analyzing allocation and spending patterns,

Change Action Plan (NCCAP) and the National Framework Strategy on Climate
Change (NFSCC).

     SEC. 5. Powers and Functions of the CFU. – The CFU shall have the following
powers and functions:

          a.​ Formulate and periodically update a National Climate Finance
      Strategy, integrated into the NCCAP and Philippine Development Plan;​

          b.​ ﻿Serve as the national designated authority/focal point for international
      climate finance mechanisms, including but not limited to Green Climate Fund
      (GCF), Global Environment Facility (GEF), and Adaptation Fund;​

          c.​ Coordinate with the DOF on fiscal policy, debt instruments, and
      climate-related financial risk management;​

         d.​ ﻿Work with the DBM to institutionalize climate budget tagging and
      expenditure tracking across all agencies;​

          e.​ Collaborate with the DEVDep to ensure integration of climate finance
      in public investment programming;​

         f.​ ﻿Provide technical assistance and accreditation support to national
      agencies, government-owned and controlled corporations (GOCCs), and
      LGUs for direct access to climate funds;​

         g.​ Establish and maintain a National Climate Finance Registry, integrated
      with existing government financial management systems;​

          h.​ ﻿Develop and promote innovative climate financing mechanisms,
      including green, blue, and sustainability bonds; blended finance; risk-sharing
      instruments; and public-private partnerships;​

         i.​ Develop safeguards and standards consistent with international best
      practices on environmental and social governance (ESG);​

          j.​ ﻿Monitor, evaluate, and report on the effectiveness, equity, and impact
      of climate finance flows;​

         k.​ Embed gender equality and social inclusion in climate finance
      processes; and​

         l.​ Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out the
      objectives of this Act.




                                          3
      SEC. 6. Composition of the CFU. – The CFU shall be composed of a Director and
such number of technical, administrative, and support personnel as may be
necessary for the effective performance of its functions.

      The CFU shall include, but not be limited to, the following core divisions:

          a.​ Climate Finance Strategy and Policy Division;​

          b.​ Project Development and Facilitation Division;​

          c.​ Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification (MRV) Division;​

          d.​ Partnerships and Resource Mobilization Division; and​

          e.​ Administrative and Finance Division.

      The CCC may, as necessary, create additional divisions or units to respond to
emerging climate finance priorities.

        SEC. 7. Powers and Functions of the CFU Director. – The Director of the CFU
shall be the chief executive and administrative officer of the Unit and shall exercise
the following powers and functions:

          a.​ ﻿Provide overall leadership, strategic direction, and supervision over
      the operations of the CFU, ensuring alignment with national climate policies,
      including the NCCAP;​

         b.​ Oversee the formulation, implementation, and periodic updating of
      the National Climate Finance Strategy, subject to the approval of the CCC;​

          c.​ Lead efforts to mobilize climate finance from domestic and
      international sources, including multilateral climate funds, bilateral partners,
      and private sector investments;​

         d.​ Represent the Philippines in climate finance-related negotiations,
      partnerships, and engagements with international financial institutions and
      development partners;​

         e.​ ﻿Ensure effective coordination with key government agencies, including
      the DOF, DBM, DEPDev, and other relevant bodies;​

         f.​ ﻿Approve, endorse, or recommend climate finance projects and
      programs, consistent with established criteria, safeguards, and national
      priorities;​

        g.​ Oversee the provision of technical assistance to national agencies,
      LGUs, and other stakeholders in project development and access to climate

      SEC. 9. Establishment of Climate Finance Registry. – The CFU shall establish a
National Climate Finance Registry, which shall:

          a.​ ﻿Track all domestic and international climate finance flows;​

          b.​ ﻿Integrate with DBM’s budgeting systems and COA audit frameworks;​

          c.​ ﻿Ensure transparency through a publicly accessible digital platform;​

          d.​ ﻿Support climate budget tagging and performance monitoring; and​

          e.​ Avoid duplication and fragmentation of funding.

       SEC. 10. Strengthening Local Government Access to Climate Funds. – To facilitate
access to funding, the CFU shall:

         a.​ Provide dedicated support to LGUs in preparing climate finance
      proposals;​

          b.​ Establish regional climate finance hubs or help desks;​

        c.​ Require integration of climate finance strategies into Local Climate
      Change Action Plans (LCCAPs); and​

         d.​ ﻿Enable LGUs to access direct international climate finance, subject to
      national oversight.

       SEC. 11. Institutional Strengthening of the CCC. – The Climate Change
Commission is hereby authorized to create plantilla positions necessary for the CFU,
hire technical experts in climate finance, economics, and project development; and
establish partnerships with multilateral institutions, development partners, and the
private sector.

       SEC. 12. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement the provisions
of this Act shall be charged against the appropriations of the Climate Change
Commission under the General Appropriations Act.

       SEC. 13. Annual Report. – The CFU shall submit to both Houses of Congress,
not later than March 30 of every year following the effectivity of this Act, a report
giving a detailed account of the status of the implementation of this Act, a progress
report on the implementation of the National Climate Finance Strategy and
recommend legislation, where applicable and necessary.

       SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days after the
approval of this Act, the CFU shall, upon consultation with government agencies,
LGUs, private sector, NGOs and civil society, promulgate the implementing rules
and regulations of this Act: Provided, That failure to issue rules and regulations shall
not in any manner affect the executory nature of the provisions of this Act.

       SEC. 15. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 17. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
