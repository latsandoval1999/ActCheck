                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

Filipino consumers continue to pay among the highest electricity rates in Southeast Asia. Despite
efforts to improve the country's power sector, consumers remain liable for charges that do not
correspond to the electricity they actually consume.

Under Section 10 of Republic Act No. 7832, distribution utilities and electric cooperatives are
allowed to recover a prescribed level of distribution system losses from consumers through
electricity rates. Section 43(f) of Republic Act No. 9136, or the Electric Power Industry Reform Act
of 2001 (EPIRA), authorizes the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) to establish the
methodology for determining recoverable system loss charges. Pursuant to this authority, the ERC
through its Resolution No. 20, s. 2017, currently allows private distribution utilities to recover up to
5.5% system losses, while electric cooperatives may recover 8.25% to 12%, depending on their
classification.

The rationale behind allowing the recovery of system losses is to recognize that a certain amount of
electricity is inevitably lost in the process of delivering power from generation facilities to end-
users. As such, distribution utilities are currently allowed to pass a portion of these losses to
consumers through electricity rates. In effect, consumers pay not only for the electricity they actually
use but also for electricity that did not even reach them. This then raises the fundamental question
of whether consumers should bear expenses arising from operational risks and inefficiencies beyond
their control.

System losses consist of both technical losses, which occur during the transmission and distribution
of electricity, and non-technical losses resulting from electricity pilferage, unauthorized
connections, and other operational inefficiencies. Although technical losses may be inevitable to
some extent, this representation argues that requiring consumers to pay for system losses effectively
transfers to them the financial consequences of circumstances that are entirely beyond their control
and even beyond their responsibility. It weakens the incentive for distribution entities to modernize
their infrastructure and improve operational efficiency to reduce technical losses and intensify
efforts to prevent electricity theft and unauthorized connections. As a matter of fairness and sound
regulatory policy, the costs arising from operational risks and inefficiencies should be borne by the
entities responsible for managing the distribution system they themselves design and operate, rather
than by consumers who merely purchase and pay for the electricity that is actually delivered to them.

This bill seeks to prohibit the recovery of all system losses from consumers by amending Section
43(f) of Republic Act No. 9136 and Section 10 of Republic Act No. 7832. Instead, distribution
utilities and electric cooperatives shall bear these losses as part of their operational responsibility,
while the ERC shall strengthen efficiency standards and technical loss reduction targets to promote
accountability, operational excellence, and more equitable electricity pricing.
