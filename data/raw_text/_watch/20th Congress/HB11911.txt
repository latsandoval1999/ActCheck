                        AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A BARANGAY CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM
BASED ON INCOME, POPULATION, GEOGRAPHIC CONDITIONS, AND
DEVELOPMENT INDICATORS, ESTABLISHING A BARANGAY
DEVELOPMENT SUPPORT MECHANISM FOR LOW-CLASSIFIED
BARANGAYS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Barangays serve as the primary political, administrative, and planning units
of government and function as the frontline institutions for the delivery of basic
services and implementation of public programs. Despite their critical role in local
governance, the current legal framework does not provide for a comprehensive and
institutionalized barangay classification system reflective of the varying socio-
economic, demographic, geographic, and developmental conditions among
barangays nationwide.

      While Republic Act No. 11964, otherwise known as the Automatic Income
Classification of Local Government Units Act, institutionalized the automatic
income classification of provinces, cities, and municipalities, barangays remain
outside the scope of the law despite possessing substantial disparities in fiscal
capacity, population density, accessibility, disaster vulnerability, and administrative
capability.

                                                                                       1
        Many geographically isolated and disadvantaged barangays (GIDAs),
 conflict-affected communities, coastal and upland barangays, and low-income
 barangays continue to face structural disadvantages in accessing national
 government programs, infrastructure support, digital connectivity, and institutional
 development opportunities.

        This measure seeks to institutionalize a rational and responsive barangay
 classification system based not solely on income but likewise on population, land
 area, geographic conditions, accessibility, vulnerability, and developmental
 indicators. The measure further establishes mechanisms for targeted development
 assistance, capacity-building interventions, and priority access to national
 government programs for low-classified barangays to ensure equitable local
 development and genuine grassroots empowerment.

       Ultimately, the measure advances the constitutional principles of local
 autonomy, equitable development, and social justice through a data-driven and
 needs-based governance framework.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SEC 3. Definition of Terms.   For purposes of this Act;


     (a) Barangay Classification refers to the categorization of barangays based on
     income, population, geographic characteristics, accessibility, vulnerability,
     and developmental indicators;

      (b) Low-Classified Barangays refer to barangays determined under this Act to
      possess limited fiscal capacity, high development deficits, or significant
      geographic disadvantage;


      (c) Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs) shall refer to
      barangays identified by the Department of Health or other competent agencies
      as isolated or underserved due to distance, terrain, conflict, or lack of

      (d) Development Indicators refer to measurable socio-economic and

      programs, grants, subsidies, technical assistance, or developmental
      interventions available to local government units.


      SEC 4. Institutionalization of the Barangay Classification System.         A
comprehensive Barangay Classification System is hereby established and
institutionalized to serve as the official framework for determining the
developmental condition, fiscal capacity, governance capability, and geographic
circumstances of all barangays nationwide. The Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), in coordination with the Department of Finance (DOF),
Department of Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDev), Philippine
Statistics Authority (PSA), Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and

other concerned agencies, shall formulate the criteria, indicators, scoring systems,
and operational guidelines necessary for the effective implementation of the
Barangay Classification System established under this Act.


      In determining the classification of barangays, the State shall adopt a holistic
assessment framework taking into consideration, among others;


         (a) Average annual locally generated income;
         (b) Population size and density;
         (c) Poverty incidence;
         (d) Access to potable water;
         (e) Access to electricity and internet connectivity;
         (f) Road accessibility and transportation infrastructure;
         (g) Availability of health, education, and social services;
         (h) Disaster risk and climate vulnerability;
         (i) Economic activity and employment opportunities;
         (j) Geographic isolation and accessibility;
         (k) Presence of indigenous cultural communities;
         (l) Peace and order conditions;
         (m)       Other indicators as may be determined by the DILG.

      SEC 5. Barangay Classification Categories.         For purposes of this Act,
barangays shall be categorized according to their overall developmental condition,
fiscal capacity, geographic characteristics, and governance capability. The DILG
and DOF shall formulate the specific parameters and corresponding numerical
thresholds for each category through the implementing rules and regulations.

      The barangay classifications may include, but shall not be limited to, the
following:

      (a) Highly Urbanized Barangays, referring to barangays located within highly
      urbanized or metropolitan areas exhibiting high population density,
      substantial economic activity, advanced infrastructure systems, and relatively
      high fiscal capacity;

      (b) Urban Barangays, referring to barangays situated in urban or peri-urban
      areas with moderate to high levels of commercial activity, accessibility, and
      public service availability;

      (c) Rural Developed Barangays, referring to barangays located in rural areas
      with relatively stable local economies, adequate infrastructure, and functional
      delivery of basic services;




                                                                                   5
      (d) Rural Developing Barangays, referring to barangays with developing
      economies and limited but improving infrastructure and institutional capacity;

      (e) Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Barangays (GIDAs),
      referring to barangays experiencing significant physical isolation, inadequate
      transportation access, or difficulty in the delivery of government services due
      to terrain, distance, conflict, or environmental conditions;

      (f) Low-Income and Vulnerable Barangays, referring to barangays with high
      poverty incidence, limited fiscal resources, inadequate infrastructure, weak
      institutional capacity, or heightened exposure to climate and disaster risks;
      and

      (g) Such other classifications or subclasses may be necessary to accurately
      reflect the prevailing developmental and socio-economic conditions of
      barangays nationwide.

      The classification system shall ensure flexibility and responsiveness to
changing demographic, economic, environmental, and governance conditions and
may incorporate emerging indicators such as digital readiness, climate resiliency,
and sustainable development performance.

      SEC 6. Periodic Review.         The Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), in coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA),
Department of Finance (DOF), Department of Economy, Planning, and
Development (DEPDev), Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and other
concerned agencies, shall conduct a comprehensive review and reclassification of
all barangays every three (3) years to ensure that classifications accurately reflect

prevailing socio-economic, demographic, fiscal, geographic, and developmental
conditions.

      SEC 7. Establishment of the Barangay Development Support Mechanism.
  There is hereby established a Barangay Development Support Mechanism
(BDSM) to provide targeted interventions for Class D and Class E barangays.

      SEC 8. Forms of Assistance.        Barangays classified as Rural Developing
Barangays, Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Barangays (GIDAs), and
Low-Income and Vulnerable Barangays shall be eligible for targeted interventions
and support programs, subject to the availability of funds and applicable government
rules and regulations. Such assistance may include, but shall not be limited to:

      (a) Financial assistance and development grants for priority local projects;
      (b) Construction, rehabilitation, and upgrading of roads, bridges, water
      systems, drainage facilities, and other basic infrastructure;
      (c) Expansion of access to electricity, internet connectivity, and digital
      infrastructure;
      (d) Technical assistance in local planning, budgeting, revenue generation,
      project development, and governance reforms;
      (e) Capacity-building programs for barangay officials and personnel;
      (f) Livelihood development, agricultural support, and local economic
      development initiatives;
      (g) Health, education, nutrition, and social welfare programs;
      (h) Disaster risk reduction, climate change adaptation, and resilience-
      building interventions;




                                                                                     7
      (i) Peacebuilding and conflict-sensitive development programs in conflict-
      affected communities;
      (j) Environmental protection and natural resource management initiatives;
      and
      (k) Other forms of assistance may be necessary to address identified
      development deficiencies.

      In determining the type and extent of assistance to be provided, priority shall
be given to barangays exhibiting the highest levels of poverty, geographic isolation,
infrastructure deficits, disaster vulnerability, and service delivery gaps.

      SEC 9. Priority Development Program.           The DILG, in coordination with
concerned national government agencies, local government units, and other
stakeholders, shall formulate and implement a Priority Barangay Development
Program (PBDP) to address the specific development needs and challenges of
barangays classified Rural Developing Barangays, Geographically Isolated and
Disadvantaged Barangays (GIDAs), and Low-Income and Vulnerable Barangays.

      The PBDP shall serve as a strategic and evidence-based framework for
directing government interventions and investments toward disadvantaged
barangays and shall include measurable targets, timelines, funding requirements,
monitoring indicators, and implementation strategies. The Program may prioritize
projects relating to infrastructure development, poverty reduction, agricultural
productivity, education, healthcare, water and sanitation, digital connectivity,
climate resilience, disaster preparedness, peace and order, and local economic
development. The DILG shall ensure that beneficiary barangays participate in the
identification, planning, implementation, and monitoring of projects under the

Program to promote transparency, accountability, and community ownership of

       SEC 10. Performance-Based Incentives.        To encourage good governance,
efficient resource management, and sustained local development, the DILG shall
establish a Performance-Based Incentives and Advancement Mechanism for
barangays demonstrating measurable improvements in governance, fiscal
management, service delivery, community participation, and development
outcomes.

       Barangays that achieve significant improvements in their classification status
or attain outstanding performance based on criteria established by the DILG may be
eligible for incentive grants, additional development assistance, technical support,
capacity-building programs, digitalization assistance, infrastructure support,
recognition awards, and preferential access to national government development
programs.

       In determining eligibility for incentives, consideration shall be given to
indicators such as increases in locally generated revenues, reduction in poverty
incidence, improvements in health and education outcomes, enhancement of disaster
preparedness,    environmental     management      initiatives,   transparency   and
accountability measures, and successful implementation of development projects.
The DILG shall formulate a transparent evaluation and scoring system to ensure that
incentives are awarded fairly, objectively, and based on verifiable performance
results.

       SEC 11. Establishment of a Barangay Development Information System.
It shall establish and maintain a Barangay Development Information System (BDIS)



                                                                                   9
that shall serve as the official repository of information relating to barangay
classifications,   development    indicators,   fiscal   performance,     demographic
characteristics, infrastructure conditions, and other relevant socio-economic data.

      The BDIS shall function as a centralized, interoperable, and evidence-based
information management platform to support planning, budgeting, policy
formulation, monitoring, and evaluation at the national and local levels. The system
shall utilize digital technologies and geographic information systems (GIS) to
facilitate data collection, analysis, mapping, and reporting, and shall ensure that non-
confidential information is accessible to government agencies, local government
units, researchers, development partners, and the public in accordance with
applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

      SEC 12. Data Collection, Sharing and Reporting. All national government
agencies, local government units, government-owned and controlled corporations,
state universities and colleges, and other relevant institutions generating data
necessary for barangay classification and development assessment shall cooperate
with and provide timely, accurate, and complete information to the Barangay
Development Information System.

      The DILG shall prescribe uniform standards, reporting templates, data quality
protocols, and submission schedules to ensure consistency, comparability, and
reliability of information across all barangays. Participating agencies and local
government units shall designate focal persons responsible for coordinating data
submission, validation, and updating activities. The DILG may require periodic
reporting and conduct verification activities to ensure the integrity and accuracy of
submitted information.

      SEC 13. Barangay Classification and Development Council.               There is
hereby created a Barangay Classification and Development Council, hereinafter
        C   C   C   C        D C       C     C     C   C   C         C      -making,
advisory, and coordinating body responsible for overseeing the implementation of
this Act and ensuring the effective administration of the Barangay Classification
System and the Barangay Development Support Mechanism.

      The Council shall be chaired by the Secretary of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG) and shall be composed of representatives from the Department
of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), Department of Finance (DOF), Philippine Statistics Authority
(PSA), Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), National
Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), League of Provinces of the Philippines,
League of Cities of the Philippines, League of Municipalities of the Philippines, Liga
ng mga Barangay, accredited civil society organizations, and the academe. The
Council shall formulate policies, approve classification methodologies, monitor
implementation, recommend reforms, and ensure stakeholder participation in the
development and periodic review of the classification system.

      SEC 14. Powers and Functions of the Council. The Council shall have the
following powers and functions:

      (a) Formulate policies, guidelines, and standards necessary for the
      implementation of this Act;

      (b) Review, validate, and approve the criteria, indicators, and methodologies
      used in the classification of barangays;




                                                                                   11
      (c) Oversee the implementation of the Barangay Development Support
      Mechanism and the Priority Barangay Development Program;

      (d) Recommend policies and interventions to address disparities among
      barangays and promote balanced local development;

      (e) Monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of programs and projects
      implemented pursuant to this Act;

      (f) Facilitate inter-agency coordination and stakeholder collaboration in
      matters relating to barangay development;

      (g) Establish technical working groups, advisory bodies, and expert panels as
      may be necessary for the performance of its functions;

      (h) Recommend budgetary support and resource allocation priorities for
      disadvantaged barangays; and

      (i) Perform such other functions as may be necessary to achieve the objectives
      of this Act.

      The Council shall meet at least once every quarter and may convene special
meetings as necessary. The DILG shall serve as the Secretariat of the Council and
shall provide the administrative, technical, and operational support required for the
effective discharge of its functions.

       SEC 17. Annual Report to Congress.         The DILG shall submit to the
Congress, not later than March 31 of each year, a comprehensive Annual Barangay
Development Report covering the implementation of this Act during the preceding
calendar year.

       SEC 18. Monitoring, Evaluation and Impact Assessment.         The DILG, in
coordination with the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development
(DEPDev), Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), and other concerned agencies, shall establish a comprehensive
monitoring and evaluation framework to assess the effectiveness, efficiency,
sustainability, and impact of the Barangay Classification System and the Barangay
Development Support Mechanism.

       The framework shall include performance indicators, baseline measurements,
periodic assessments, and outcome-based evaluation tools to determine whether the
objectives of this Act are being achieved. The DILG shall conduct regular reviews
of   development    outcomes,    governance    improvements,     service   delivery
enhancements, and poverty reduction efforts in beneficiary barangays.

       An independent impact assessment shall likewise be conducted at least once
every five (5) years to evaluate the overall effectiveness of the Act and identify
necessary reforms, policy adjustments, and strategies for improving the delivery of
support to disadvantaged barangays.

      SEC 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within one hundred twenty

implementation of this Act.

      SEC 20. Appropriations.           The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
DILG. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation
of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act, with priority
given to Rural Developing Barangays, Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged
Barangays (GIDAs), and Low-Income and Vulnerable Barangays.

      SEC. 21. Separability Clause.       If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 22. Repealing Clause.      All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.
