                              AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A TARGETED FUEL SUBSIDY PROGRAM FOR PUBLIC
TRANSPORT WORKERS, FARMERS, MUNICIPAL FISHERFOLK, AND OTHER
VULNERABLE SECTORS DURING PERIODS OF ABNORMAL FUEL PRICE
INCREASES, PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE
High and unstable fuel prices have a direct impact on everyday life. When fuel costs
rise, public transport drivers, farmers and farmworkers, fisherfolk, and small
producers earn less because their expenses increase. At the same time, commuters
pay higher fares and the cost of transporting food goes up, which leads to more
expensive goods in the market. In the end, it is ordinary Filipinos who carry the
burden of these price increases.

The government has long recognized the need to help vulnerable sectors during fuel
price spikes. In the past, fuel subsidies and discounts were given to public transport
operators, farmers, small fisherfolk, and food haulers to reduce the impact of rising
costs. More recently, assistance such as fuel vouchers and cash support was provided
under the TRAIN Law to help ease the burden on affected sectors. These efforts show
that targeted support works and is necessary during times of sudden fuel increases.

This bill aims to make such support a regular and ready program. It establishes a Fuel
Subsidy Program that will automatically provide assistance during periods of
unusually high fuel prices. It identifies priority beneficiaries such as public transport
drivers and municipal fisherfolk, uses proper registries to ensure the right people
receive aid, and creates a system to monitor and adjust the program based on current
conditions.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Fuel Subsidy Program Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect the welfare and livelihood of vulnerable sectors whose income and essential
operations are directly and materially affected by fuel price volatility,
particularly public transport workers and operators, farmers and farmworkers,
municipal fisherfolk, and other low-income groups. Toward this end, the State
shall institutionalize a targeted, transparent, and accountable fuel subsidy
program, anchored on objective triggers and verified registries, to mitigate the
adverse effects of abnormal fuel price increases.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   (a) “Fuel” refers to gasoline and automotive diesel fuel, and such other petroleum
       products as may identified in the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) as
       directly relevant to the covered sectors.


                                                                               3 | Page
   (b) “Abnormal Fuel Price Increase” refers to any of the following, as certified by
       the Department of Energy (DOE) based on official price monitoring data:

       1. An increase in the national average retail price of gasoline or diesel by at
          least ten percent (5%) for three (3) consecutive weeks; or
       2. An increase in the national average retail price of gasoline or diesel by at
          least fifteen percent (10%) within any four (4)-week period; or
       3. Such other objective thresholds as may be provided in the IRR, provided
          that the standards are measurable and publicly verifiable.

   (c) “Qualified Beneficiary” refers to a natural person who meets the eligibility
       requirements under Section 6 of this Act and is included in the validated list
       prepared under Section 7.

   (d) “Program” refers to the Fuel Subsidy Program established under this Act.


SECTION 4. Establishment of Fuel Subsidy Program. — There is hereby
established a Fuel Subsidy Program, which shall be a regular and continuing
government program to provide targeted cash assistance to qualified beneficiaries
during periods of abnormal fuel price increases.

The subsidy shall be in the form of cash transfer or digital case assistance in an
amount not less than Five Thousand Pesos (Php5,000.00) per month per
qualified beneficiary during the period of activation under Section 5.

The amount and duration of the subsidy may be adjusted in accordance with Section
9 of this Act: Provided, That any adjustment shall remain consistent with the
standards and safeguards in this Act and the IRR.

SECTION 5. Activation and Duration of Fuel Subsidy. — The Program shall be
activated upon the issuance by the DOE of a Certification of Abnormal Fuel Price
Increase, in accordance with Section 3(b).

The initial subsidy period shall be one (1) month, renewable on a month-to-month
basis during the continued existence of abnormal fuel price increase: Provided, That
the DOE shall issue an updated certification at least every thirty (30) days while the
Program remains activated.




                                                                              4 | Page
SECTION 6. Coverage and Beneficiaries. — The Program shall prioritize low-
income individuals whose livelihood is directly affected by fuel price volatility.
Eligible beneficiaries include:

   a. Public Transport Drivers and Operators. Low-income drivers and
      operators of public utility vehicles (PUVs) including jeepneys, buses, UV
      express, taxis, tricycles, and transport network vehicle services (TNVS), duly
      registred or accredited with the Land Transportation Franchising and
      Regulatory Board (LTFRB) or the appropriate local government units (LGUs),
      as applicable, and included in official government databases.

   b. Municipal Fisherfolk. Municipal fisherfolk operating motorized fishing
      vessels within municipal waters, whether individually or through duly
      registered fisherfolk organizations, cooperatives, or associations: Provided,
      That they are listed in the registry system of the Department of Agriculture
      (DA) or the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR), and that the
      vessel is registered in the BFAR boat registry or the boat registry system of the
      concerned LGU, subject to validation under the IRR.

   c. Farmers and Farmworkers. Low-income farmers and farmworkers whose
      agricultural production and essential operations are fuel-dependent,
      including those who use fuel for farm machinery, irrigation pumps,
      postharvest handling, and hauling of agricultural inputs and
      produce: Provided, That they are listed in the RSBSA or other DA-validated
      registries to be specified in the IRR, subject to validation under Section 7.

   d. Other Low-Income Sectors. Other similarly situated groups demonstrably
      affected by fuel price increases, as may be identified by the DOE and the
      Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), in coordination with
      the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), based on
      objective criteria to be set in the IRR: Provided, That priority shall be given to
      individuals or households listed in the Community-Based Monitoring System
      (CBMS) or other government-verified poverty and vulnerability registries.


SECTION 7. Beneficiary Identification, Validation, and Masterlist. — The
implementing agencies shall establish and maintain a Fuel Subsidy Beneficiary
Masterlist, which shall be:




                                                                                5 | Page
   a. Registry-based, using existing databases and registries of the LTFRB/DOTr,
      DA (including RSBSA and other farmer registries) and BFAR, DSWD, LGUS, and
      other relevant agencies;

   b. Validated, through deduplication and identity checks, in coordination with
      the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) and other appropriate government
      systems as may be authorized by law and the IRR; and

   c. Updated at least semi-annually, and as necessary during Program activation
      periods.

      No person shall receive more than one (1) subsidy grant per month under this
      Act, subject to the IRR on household-level or individual-level rules, as
      applicable.

SECTION 8. Implementing Agencies; Modes of Disbursement. —

   a. Lead Implementing Agencies. The DOE, DOTr (through LTFRB, DA
      (including its relevant offices for farmer registries, and through BFAR for
      fisherfolk, and DSWD shall be the lead implementing agencies, within their
      respective mandates, for beneficiary identification, validation, and program
      delivery.

   b. Disbursement. Subsidies shall be disbursed through secure channels such as
      digital wallets, bank transfers, or other transparent payment systems:
      Provided, That cash payout mechanisms may be used only when digital or
      banking channels are not feasible, subject to strict controls in the IRR.

   c. Transparency. The implementing agencies shall publish periodic reports
      containing aggregated data on beneficiaries, amounts released, and
      geographic distribution, subject to data privacy and security requirements.


SECTION 9. Fuel Subsidy Advisory Council. — There is hereby created a Fuel
Subsidy Advisory Council to recommend to the implementing agencies the
appropriate subsidy amount, duration, and coverage adjustments, taking into account
prevailing fuel prices, inflation, and fiscal sustainability.

The Advisory Council shall be composed of:



                                                                           6 | Page
   a.   Secretary of Transportation — Chairperson;
   b.   Chairperson of the LTFRB — Co-Chairperson;
   c.   Secretary of Energy — Member;
   d.   Secretary of Agriculture — Member;
   e.   Secretary of Labor and Employment — Member;
   f.   Secretary of Budget and Management — Member;
   g.   Secretary of Finance — Member; and
   h.   Secretary of the Interior and Local Government — Member.

The LTFRB shall provide secretariat support. The Advisory Council shall meet at
least twice a year, and may convene special meetings during extraordinary fuel price
movements.

SECTION 10. Monitoring, Audit, and Penalties. —

   a. Monitoring and Reporting. Implementing agencies shall establish
      monitoring and evaluation mechanisms, including grievance redress systems,
      and shall submit consolidated implementation reports to Congress.

   b. Audit. All disbursements under this Act shall be subject to post-audit by the
      Commission on Audit (COA) and shall comply with applicable budgeting,
      accounting, and procurement rules.

   c. Penalties. Any person who knowingly submits falsified information, uses
      another person’s identity, or otherwise defrauds the Program shall be subject
      to administrative and criminal action under applicable laws, and shall be
      disqualified from future benefits under this Act, without prejudice to
      restitution and other remedies.


SECTION 11. Non-Diminution of Existing Benefits. — Nothing in this Act shall be
construed to reduce or withdraw existing benefits, assistance, or privileges granted
under existing laws, ordinances, rules, regulations, or programs. Any harmonization
of benefits shall be governed by the IRR to prevent duplication while protecting
legitimate entitlements.

SECTION 12. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
concerned agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued

implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act
(GAA).

SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOE, in coordination with the DOTr, DA, DSWD,
DEPDev, and other relevant agencies, and in consultation with stakeholders, shall
promulgate the IRR of this Act.

SECTION 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid, the
other provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, order, rules and regulations, or
parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended or modified accordingly.


SECTION 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspaper of general
circulation.
