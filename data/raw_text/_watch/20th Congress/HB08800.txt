                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to institutionalize a uniform and equitable retirement system
for judicial officials who have the same rank, salary, and privileges as justices and
judges. It addresses a long-standing policy gap that excludes similarly situated
judicial officials from the retirement system accorded to members of the judiciary,
despite the comparable nature of their functions and responsibilities.

       While Republic Act (RA) No. 910 originally provided a framework for judicial
retirement, subsequent amendments under RA No. 9946 narrowed the scope of
these benefits to encompass only members of the bench. As a result, high-ranking
judiciary officials, including Clerks of Court and court administrators conferred with
judicial rank, were excluded from these benefits. At the same time, RA No. 8291,
otherwise known as “The Government Service Insurance System (GSIS) Act of 1997”,
removed them from regular GSIS coverage due to their judicial classification, only
for them to find themselves excluded from the pension system. This leaves
dedicated public servants with limited benefits that do not reflect the length and
value of their public service.

       This proposed measure reinforces judicial independence by ensuring that
senior judiciary officials remain financially secure upon retirement. It also enhances
existing benefits by extending educational support to the children of qualified
retirees.

        This vital piece of legislation affirms the State’s recognition played by these
officials in the administration of justice and promotes a more equitable and
sustainable judiciary.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

       SEC. 4. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s savings of the Judiciary.
Thereafter, such sum as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 5. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not so declared shall remain in force and
effect.

       SEC. 6. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders and rules, and regulations
contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
amended accordingly.

      SEC. 7. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.
