                             AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE FUEL SUBSIDY PROGRAM TO PROMOTE

VOLATILITY, PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Oil plays a crucial role in the Philippine economy, with several sectors
dependent on oil. Among these sectors are the transportation sector, which primarily
consumes diesel and gasoline; the industry and manufacturing sector; and the
agriculture, forestry, and fisheries sector, which relies on fuel for machinery, tractors,
and fishing boats.

       To meet the country’s oil demands, the Philippines heavily relies on imports. In
2024, the country imported “$3.7B of crude petroleum, making it the 35th largest
importer of crude petroleum (out of 149) in the world.”1 Most of the country’s imported
crude petroleum were sourced from: “Saudi Arabia ($1.79B), United Arab Emirates
($1.22B), Iraq ($474M), Colombia ($65.5M), and Japan ($63.2M).”2 Thus, recent
geopolitical tensions in Europe and the Middle East have significantly affected the oil
prices3 in the Philippines.4

       As a result, the country’s sectors dependent on oil imports have also been
greatly affected. Drivers of public utility vehicles (PUVs) like jeepneys, buses, and

        As a response, several government agencies, such as the Department of
Interior and Local Government (DILG), the Department of Transportation (DOTr), and
the Department of Agriculture – Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (DA-
BFAR), and local government units (LGUs), have occasionally provided fuel subsidies
to affected workers, including tricycle drivers through the “Pantawid Pasada Program”5
and farmers and fisherfolk through the “Fuel Discount Program,”6 among others, to
alleviate the burden of increased oil prices.

        However, these responses have been largely reactive, occasional, and
dependent on the initiatives of the concerned government agencies and LGUs. As a
result, the temporary, or often one-time, fuel subsidy programs fail to provide the
affected workers the necessary assurance that they shall have the consistent support
to continue in their respective livelihoods amid the rising oil prices.

       This bill seeks to establish a regular and continuing Fuel Subsidy Program that
shall provide a monthly cash assistance of not less than Seven Thousand Pesos
(P7,000.00) to eligible beneficiaries registered in the databases of the concerned
agencies mentioned in this bill.

      Among the identified beneficiaries are public transport operators and drivers,
municipal fisherfolk, farmers and agricultural producers, and other low-income sectors
as may be identified by the Department of Energy (DOE) and Department of Social
Welfare and Development (DSWD).

        To ensure efficiency, accountability, and transparency in distribution, this bill
mandates that the subsidy be directly deposited into the beneficiaries’ bank accounts.
Through this bill, Filipino workers in sectors largely dependent on oil shall be provided
stability in their livelihoods regardless of geopolitical developments that inevitably
affect oil prices.

       In the 20th Congress, Senator Raffy Tulfo, Representatives Claudine Bautista-
Lim, Marlyn Primicias-Agabas, Ramon Gutierrez, Mikaela Suansinhis and Salvador
Pleyto have filed similar bills. This bill adopts several provisions from Senator Tulfo’s
bill.

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:
 1         SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Fuel Subsidy
 2   Program Act.”
 3
 4           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   protect and promote the welfare of sectors whose livelihoods are directly affected by
 6   volatile fuel price fluctuations. The State recognizes the essential role of transport
 7   workers, farmers, and fisherfolk in maintaining food security and economic
 8   productivity. To this end, the government shall institutionalize a sustainable and
 9   equitable fuel subsidy program to mitigate the adverse effects of fuel price volatility on
10   vulnerable sectors.
11
12          SEC. 3. Establishment of the Fuel Subsidy Program. – There is hereby
13   established a Fuel Subsidy Program, to be implemented as a regular and continuing
14   government initiative to provide targeted financial assistance to qualified beneficiaries
15   adversely affected by abnormal increases in fuel prices.
16
17           The Program shall provide cash assistance of not less than Seven Thousand
18   Pesos (P7,000.00) per month to each eligible beneficiary under this Act. The subsidy
19   shall be directly deposited into the bank accounts of the beneficiaries to ensure
20   efficiency, accountability, and transparency in distribution.
21
22         Provided, That the amount of the monthly subsidy shall be subject to annual
23   review and adjustment by the Department of Energy (DOE) and the Department of

                                                 1
 1   Budget and Management (DBM), in consultation with the Department of Economy,
 2   Planning, and Development (DEPDev), to account for inflation, prevailing fuel prices,
 3   and fiscal capacity.
 4
 5         The Fuel Subsidy Program shall be integrated into the annual General
 6   Appropriations Act (GAA) to ensure its sustainability and continuity.
 7
 8          SEC. 4. Beneficiaries. – The Fuel Subsidy Program shall primarily cover low-
 9   income individuals and sectors whose livelihoods are directly affected by irregular
10   fluctuations in fuel prices, as determined by the implementing agencies under this Act.
11
12         The following shall be considered eligible beneficiaries:
13
14             a) Public Transport Operators and Drivers – refer to low-income operators
15                and drivers of public utility vehicles (PUVs), such as jeepneys, buses,
16                taxis, tricycles, and transport network vehicle services (TNVS), duly
17                registered with the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory
18                Board (LTFRB) or with their respective local government units (LGUs):
19                Provided, That registration under the LTFRB's existing databases, or in
20                coordination with the Department of Transportation (DOTr), shall be a
21                requirement for eligibility under this Act;
22
23             b) Fisherfolk – refer to municipal fisherfolk engaged in fishing within
24                municipal waters, whether coastal or inland, who operate motorized
25                fishing vessels with maximum gross weight of three (3) gross, either
26                individually or through a fisherfolk organization, cooperative, or
27                association: Provided, That such beneficiaries are duly listed in the
28                registry system of the Department of Agriculture (DA), particularly in the
29                Municipal Fisherfolk Registration System: Provided, further, That their
30                fishing vessels are properly registered in the integrated boat registry
31                system in the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR) Boat
32                Registration, or the boat registry system of the concerned local
33                government unit (LGU): Provided, finally, That municipal fisherfolk and
34                fishing vessels that are not yet registered under aforementioned registry
35                systems may still be beneficiaries provided they register in any of the
36                aforementioned registry systems within the periods to be set by the
37                BFAR or LGU;
38
39             c) Farmers and Agricultural Producers – refer to low-income farmers,
40                farmworkers, and agricultural producers who utilize fuel-powered
41                machinery or equipment for land preparation, irrigation, post-harvest
42                processing, or the transport of agricultural products, as validated by the
43                Department of Agriculture (DA): Provided, That registration in the
44                Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA) shall be a

 2                 That farmers who are not yet registered in the RSBSA may still be served

 5             d) Other Low-Income Sectors – other similarly situated groups as may be
 6                identified by the Department of Energy (DOE) and the Department of
 7                Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), in coordination with the
 8                Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), who

14                assistance is extended to those most in need.
15
16          SEC. 5. Non-Diminution of Benefits. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
17   reduce, withdraw, or otherwise diminish any benefit, assistance, or privilege currently
18   enjoyed by transport workers, farmers, fisherfolk, and other eligible beneficiaries under
19   existing laws, ordinances, rules, regulations, or programs. These include, but are not
20   limited to, existing fuel subsidies, livelihood assistance, insurance coverage, training
21   opportunities, access to production resources, and other socio-economic support
22   programs being implemented by national government agencies or local government
23   units (LGUs): Provided, That no modification or discontinuance of such benefits shall
24   be made without due process and prior consultation with the affected sectors and
25   relevant stakeholders.
26
27           SEC. 6. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
28   of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department of
29   Energy (DOE), Department of Transportation (DOTr), Department of Agriculture (DA),
30   and Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD). Thereafter, such sums
31   as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in
32   the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA) under the respective budgets of the
33   DOE, DOTr, DA, and DSWD.
34
35           SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
36   the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Energy (DOE), in coordination with the
37   DOTr, DA, DSWD, DBM, and DEPDev, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
38   regulations to effectively implement the provisions of this Act, including the
39   identification of eligible beneficiaries, distribution mechanisms, monitoring, and
40   reporting systems.
41
42          SEC. 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
43   unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
44   and effect.
