                                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to integrate a comprehensive curriculum on digital literacy and
future skills into basic, higher, and technical-vocational education. It institutionalizes
digital literacy and future skills development across the Philippine education system
and for all Filipinos.

        Based on the Philippine Statistics Authority’s 2024 Functional Literacy,
Education, and Mass Media Survey (FLEMMS), approximately seventy-eight (78.2%)
of Filipinos aged fifteen (15) to sixty-four (64), or nearly fifty eight (57.91) million
individuals, use the internet. However, some sixteen (16.17) million did not. Among
internet users, only twenty-two percent (22.6%) demonstrated above-basic skills
across all five (5) internationally recognized areas of digital competence. Proficiency
was particularly low in areas essential to online safety and productivity. Only
fifty-four point three percent (54.3%) demonstrated above-basic skills in digital
safety, which covers the protection of personal data and privacy. An even lower
thirty five percent (35.0%) demonstrated proficiency in digital content creation,
which includes programming and the production of digital content.1 These
deficiencies are further compounded by inadequate digital infrastructure. As
recently as July 2025, nearly twelve thousand (12,000) public schools still lacked
internet connectivity.2

        The consequences extend beyond education and access to technology. The
information technology and business process management (IT-BPM) sector
employed one point eight (1.8) million full-time workers and generated revenues
equivalent to 6.9% of gross domestic product in 2024. Despite its economic
significance, the sector faces a substantial skill gap, with the Technical Education and
Skills Development Authority (TESDA) reporting more than one million (1,000,000)
vacant positions in the sector in May 2025, mostly for specialized roles such as web

       This measure establishes digital literacy as a foundational competency
beginning in basic education through higher and technical-vocational education. It
provides for the progressive development of skills in data science, cloud computing,
AI, cybersecurity, and other emerging fields. It likewise recognizes that digital skills
development must extend beyond formal schooling by providing opportunities for
continuing education and upskilling among overseas Filipino workers, senior
citizens, persons with disabilities, and out-of-school youth.

       Digital competence has become a precondition for full participation in
economic, civic, and social life. As artificial intelligence reshapes the very industries
that sustain millions of Filipino households, the State cannot afford to leave its
workforce unprepared.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING DIGITAL LITERACY AND FUTURE SKILLS
DEVELOPMENT IN THE PHILIPPINE EDUCATION SYSTEM AND FOR ALL
FILIPINOS

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Digital Literacy Act”.

        SEC 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
give priority to education, make quality education accessible to all, and recognize the
vital role of communication and information. Recognizing that full participation in
the global digital economy and information society now depends on the ability to
use digital technology safely, critically, and productively, the State affirms that every
Filipino must be equipped with the digital literacy and future skills needed to
participate fully, responsibly, and ethically in national and global life.

       Towards this end, the State shall integrate a comprehensive and progressive
curriculum on digital literacy and future skills across basic, higher, and
technical-vocational education, and shall periodically update it to keep pace with
emerging technologies.

       SEC 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

           a. ﻿Digital Literacy refers to the ability to access, manage, understand,
       integrate, communicate, evaluate, and create information safely and
       appropriately using digital technology. It encompasses competencies related
       to computer literacy, information and communication technology (ICT)
       literacy, information literacy, media literacy, and digital citizenship; and ​

           b. Future Skills refer to advanced digital and technical competencies
       critical for the Fourth Industrial Revolution, including, but not limited to,
       digital coding, digital marketing, data science, cloud computing, artificial
       intelligence, and cybersecurity.
       SEC 4. Inclusion of Digital Literacy and Future Skills in the Curriculum. – The
Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), in coordination with the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT) and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA), shall formulate, develop, and integrate a comprehensive and progressive
curriculum on Digital Literacy and Future Skills in the basic, higher, and
technical-vocational education curricula. This curriculum shall be periodically
reviewed and updated to include emerging Future Skills.

       SEC 5. Basic Digital Literacy in the National Service Training Program. – Basic
digital literacy shall be made an integral and mandatory component of the National
Service Training Program (NSTP), specifically in the Literacy Training Service (LTS)
and the Civil Welfare Training Service (CWTS) components. Prior to deployment,
NSTP students shall be equipped with the skills to effectively teach basic digital
literacy and future skills to communities.

        SEC 6. Access to Digital Learning Resources. – The DepEd, CHED, and TESDA
shall provide and facilitate access to necessary software, hardware, and internet
connectivity to all public school students and teachers across the country. This shall
include, but not be limited to, the provision of modern digital learning devices and
sufficient internet access to support the effective integration of the Digital Literacy
and Future Skills curriculum.

       SEC 7. Establishment of Digital Literacy and Future Skills Development Center. –
The TESDA, in collaboration with the DICT, Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), and other relevant stakeholders, shall establish a Digital
Literacy and Future Skills Development Center in every region of the Philippines,
aimed at providing free seminars, trainings, and certification programs on Digital
Literacy and Future Skills. These centers shall be established in easily accessible
public facilities, such as libraries, malls, universities and colleges, cultural centers, or
equivalent existing centers.

       To promote inclusivity and lifelong digital upskilling, the centers shall offer
both on-site and accessible online learning options catering to all Filipinos,
particularly underserved groups such as Overseas Filipino Workers, senior citizens,
persons with disabilities, and out-of-school youth.

       SEC 8. Digital Literacy on Wheels. – There is hereby established a Digital
Literacy on Wheels program under the DICT and TESDA, in coordination with local
government units, using mobile training facilities to complement the Digital Literacy
and Future Skills Development Centers and provide localized, hands-on training in
digital literacy to underserved communities in geographically isolated and
disadvantaged areas.

        SEC 9. Creation of Inter-Agency Task Force on Digital Literacy and Future Skills. –
There is hereby created an Inter-Agency Task Force on Digital Literacy and Future
Skills (IATF-DLFS) which shall ensure the effective and coordinated implementation
of this Act. The IATF-DLFS shall be chaired by the Secretary of the DICT, with the
Secretaries of the DepEd and DILG, and the Chairpersons of CHED and TESDA as
members. The Task Force shall be responsible for policy formulation, program
monitoring, and securing necessary funding.

       SEC 10. Digital Literacy Day. – To further promote Digital Literacy and Future
Skills Development, the second of December of every year is hereby declared as
“Digital Literacy Day” in the Philippines.

       SEC 11. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the concerned
implementing agencies. Thereafter, the amount necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the IATF-DLFS shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
