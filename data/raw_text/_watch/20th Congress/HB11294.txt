       AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF BARANGAY HEALTH
    WORKERS, PROFESSIONALIZING AND STRENGTHENING THE BARANGAY
 HEALTH WORKFORCE, PROVIDING FOR THEIR REGULAR EMPLOYMENT, JUST
      COMPENSATION, BENEFITS, RIGHTS, AND SECURITY OF TENURE, AND
                                APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

    Barangay health workers (BHWs) are the first and often the most accessible link
between Filipino families and the public health system. They assist in maternal and child
care, immunization, disease prevention and surveillance, health education, patient
navigation, community mobilization, and the maintenance of local health information.
Their work is indispensable to a primary care-led health system and to the effective
implementation of universal health care.
    Yet many BHWs continue to serve under uncertain volunteer arrangements, receive
widely varying honoraria, and remain vulnerable to displacement whenever local political
leadership changes. This situation undervalues essential community health work, much
of which is performed by women, and weakens continuity of care at the barangay level.
    This measure combines professionalization with a comprehensive framework for the
rights, welfare, development, and organization of BHWs. It provides for standardized
registration and competency certification, continuing education, a national information



                                                         1
system, progressive creation of regular plantilla positions, a minimum Salary Grade 1
entry level for employed BHWs, security of tenure, minimum benefits for volunteer BHWs
during the transition, and national assistance to local governments with limited fiscal
capacity.
    The bill also protects BHWs against discrimination and political interference,
recognizes their right to organize and participate in health policy discussions, and
establishes clear grievance and appeal mechanisms. By investing in the people who
sustain health care at the community level, the State strengthens both the dignity of BHWs
and the reliability of public health services for every family.
    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                             CHAPTER I
                                         GENERAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Magna Carta for Barangay Health
Workers."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to protect and promote the
right to health, adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to health development,
and make essential health and social services available and accessible to all.
    The State recognizes BHWs as indispensable community-based public health
workers and partners in achieving universal health care. It shall professionalize and
integrate them into the formal health workforce; ensure decent work, just compensation,
security of tenure, safe working conditions, social protection, and opportunities for career
advancement; recognize the economic value of community health work, particularly the


                                                         3
work performed by women; and protect BHWs from discrimination, political interference,
and arbitrary removal.

SEC. 3. Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all registered and certified BHWs, whether
serving as volunteers or employed by a barangay, municipality, city, province, or national
government agency, and to all government entities that recruit, deploy, supervise, or
mobilize BHWs.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. -
 (a) "Barangay health worker" or "BHW" means a person registered by the municipal or
     city health board who has undergone the training prescribed by the Department of
     Health (DOH) and voluntarily renders or is employed to render primary health care
     services in the community;
 (b) "Certified BHW" means a registered BHW who has satisfied the competency
    standards and certification requirements under this Act;
 (c) "Employed BHW" means a BHW appointed to a regular, permanent plantilla position
     in a local government unit (LGU) or national government agency;
 (d) "Volunteer BHW" means a registered or certified BHW who renders service without
     a regular plantilla appointment during the transition provided under this Act; and
 (e) "Primary health care" refers to accessible, continuing, comprehensive, and
    coordinated care provided as the initial and continuing point of contact in the health
    care delivery system.


                             CHAPTER II
          ROLE AND DEPLOYMENT OF BARANGAY HEALTH WORKERS

SEC. 5. Role in Primary Health Care. - BHWs shall form part of the community health
teams of barangay health stations, health centers, or other primary care facilities of the
municipal or city health office. They shall work under the day-to-day supervision of the
head of the concerned facility and shall perform functions consistent with their training
and certification, including:
 (a) assisting in the delivery of primary health care services, health promotion, disease
     prevention and surveillance, maternal and child care, immunization, nutrition, and
     other community health programs;
 (b) serving as health educators and community organizers;
 (c) assisting in patient navigation, referral, follow-up, and coordination within the health
     care provider network; and



                                             4
 (d) collecting and maintaining community health data and records, subject to data
     privacy and confidentiality laws.
     No BHW shall be required to perform a procedure or function beyond the BHW's
certified competence or without appropriate supervision, equipment, and protection.

SEC. 6. Coordination with the Barangay. - The municipal or city health office shall
coordinate with the punong barangay in implementing community health programs. The
barangay shall support the maintenance of health stations and provide the supplies,
equipment, transportation, communications, and logistical support reasonably necessary
for BHWs to perform their duties.

SEC. 7. Staffing Standard. - The DOH, in consultation with the Department of the Interior
and Local Government (DILG), LGUs, and recognized BHW organizations, shall
prescribe a needs-based staffing standard taking into account catchment population,
household density, geography, disease burden, the community health profile, and the
range of required services. As an initial benchmark, LGUs shall work toward a ratio of at
least one (1) BHW for every twenty (20) households, subject to validation and periodic
adjustment by the DOH at least once every four (4) years.
    BHWs should, as far as practicable, be residents of the communities to which they
are assigned.


                                        CHAPTER III
       REGISTRATION CERTIFICATION AND PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT
SEC. 8. Registration. - An applicant shall register with the municipal or city health board
of the locality where the applicant resides and intends to serve. The applicant must be at
least eighteen (18) years of age, physically and mentally fit to perform the duties of a
BHW with reasonable accommodation when necessary, and must have completed the
DOH-prescribed basic orientation and training within the year of application. The local
health board may prescribe additional reasonable and nondiscriminatory qualifications
consistent with national standards.
SEC. 9. Powers of Municipal and City Health Boards. - In addition to their functions
under Republic Act No. 7160, municipal and city health boards shall administer the
recruitment, selection, registration, certification, retention, and removal of BHWs and
resolve complaints and grievances involving them. A decision revoking registration or
ordering removal may be appealed to the provincial health board and thereafter to the
DILG. In highly urbanized and independent component cities, the appeal shall be directly
to the DILG.




                                            5
   The DILG and DOH shall issue uniform standards for notice, hearing, decision, and
appeal, consistent with administrative due process.

SEC. 10. National BHW Information System. - The DOH, in partnership with the
Department of Information and Communications Technology and LGUs, shall establish
and maintain a secure National BHW Information System as the authoritative registry of
BHWs. It shall contain information necessary for workforce planning, deployment,
training, compensation, and benefits, subject to Republic Act No. 10173 or the Data
Privacy Act of 2012.
    Municipal and city health boards shall keep records current and accurate. Provincial
health boards and the DOH shall provide technical assistance and monitor compliance.

SEC. 11. Certification of Competency. - A registered BHW may apply for certification
after completing at least two (2) years of continuous service in the locality, the prescribed
training and certification courses, and a satisfactory performance evaluation by the
supervising health professional. The DOH, in consultation with the Technical Education
and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), Commission on Higher Education (CHED),
and Civil Service Commission (CSC), shall establish competency standards and
assessment procedures.
    The municipal or city health board shall act on a complete application within thirty (30)
days and issue a certificate to a qualified applicant. The LGU and DOH shall ensure
reasonable access to assessment and certification without charging the applicant
prohibitive fees.

SEC. 12. Continuing Education and Training. - The DOH and TESDA, in collaboration
with the CSC, CHED, LGUs, educational institutions, and BHW organizations, shall
institutionalize a continuing competency-based curriculum covering clinical support
functions, health education, community organizing, records management, patient
navigation, disaster response, digital health, data privacy, occupational safety, and
emerging community health needs.
     All BHWs shall complete mandatory continuing community-based health education
at least once every three (3) years, or more frequently when required by public health
needs. Training shall be accessible through in-person, web-based, and mobile modalities
and shall be provided without loss of salary or honorarium.


                                         CHAPTER IV
       REGULAR EMPLOYMENT COMPENSATION AND SECURITY OF TENURE




                                             6
SEC. 13. Creation of Plantilla Positions. - The Department of Budget and Management
(DBM), in consultation with the CSC, DOH, DILG, LGUs, and duly recognized BHW
organizations, shall establish BHW position titles, qualification standards, and
corresponding salary grades in the Index of Occupational Services, Occupational Groups,
Classes and Salary Grades.
     Every municipality and city shall progressively create regular plantilla positions
sufficient to meet the staffing standard under Section 7. Certified BHWs with at least two
(2) years of continuous satisfactory service shall be given preference in appointment,
subject to merit and fitness and applicable civil service laws. In no case shall the entry-
level salary be lower than Salary Grade 1 under the prevailing Salary Standardization
Law.

SEC. 14. Transition to Regular Employment. - Within one (1) year from the effectivity
of this Act, every municipality and city shall prepare a five-year BHW regularization plan
identifying existing BHWs, required positions, annual targets, funding sources, and
priority underserved areas. At least twenty percent (20%) of the positions required under
the plan shall be funded and filled each year until full implementation, subject to
availability of qualified applicants.
    During the transition, volunteer BHWs shall retain their registration, assignments,
accrued service credit, honoraria, and benefits and shall not be displaced solely because
plantilla positions are being created.
SEC. 15. Security of Tenure and Continuity of Service. - An employed BHW shall
enjoy security of tenure and may be disciplined or removed only for cause and after due
process under civil service laws. A registered or certified volunteer BHW may be removed
from the registry or service only for a ground prescribed by uniform DOH-DILG rules and
after notice and hearing. No change in local political leadership shall constitute a ground
for nonrenewal, reassignment, removal, or replacement.SEC. 16. Credit for Prior Service.
- Upon appointment to a regular position, the years of continuous, documented, and
satisfactory service previously rendered as a registered BHW shall be credited for
purposes of step increment, leave entitlement, loyalty awards, and retirement, to the
extent allowed by law and implementing rules. The CSC, DBM, and Government Service
Insurance System (GSIS) shall issue the necessary guidelines.


                                      CHAPTER V
                             BENEFITS AND INCENTIVES


SEC. 17. Benefits of Employed BHWs. - In addition to basic salary, employed BHWs
shall receive the allowances, leave benefits, insurance, retirement, health emergency


                                            7
allowance, hazard pay, and other benefits available under civil service laws, Republic Act
No. 7305 or the Magna Carta of Public Health Workers when applicable, Republic Act
No. 11712, and other relevant laws. Membership and employer contributions in the GSIS,
Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), and Home Development Mutual
Fund shall be mandatory.
    A BHW assigned to a geographically isolated and disadvantaged area or exposed to
occupational hazards shall receive hazard allowance in accordance with applicable law,
but in no case lower than twenty percent (20%) of monthly basic salary when the
conditions for entitlement are present.

SEC. 18. Minimum Benefits of Volunteer BHWs During Transition. - Until appointed
to a regular position, every registered or certified volunteer BHW shall receive the
following minimum benefits, without prejudice to more favorable benefits granted by an
LGU:
 (a) a monthly honorarium of not less than Three thousand pesos (P3,000.00) for a
     registered BHW and not less than Five thousand pesos (P5,000.00) for a certified
     BHW, to be reviewed and adjusted by the DOH, DILG, and DBM every three (3)
     years;
 (b) a cash gift each December equivalent to not less than one (1) month of the applicable
     minimum honorarium;
 (c) transportation allowance or reimbursement of actual authorized transportation
     expenses, including official travel and patient transport, which shall not be deducted
     from the honorarium;
 (d) subsistence allowance for service outside the BHW's place of residence in an
     unserved or underserved area, at rates prescribed by the DBM and DOH;
 (e) a monthly rice subsidy of up to One thousand pesos (P1,000.00) or its equivalent in
     rice;
 (f) hazard allowance of not less than One thousand pesos (P1,000.00) per month when
      exposed to validated occupational hazards, armed conflict, disaster, outbreak, or
      comparable danger;
 (g) appropriate group insurance coverage, with premiums borne by the municipality or
     city, and PhilHealth coverage consistent with law;
 (h) health emergency allowance and other benefits under Republic Act No. 11712 when
     deployed during a public health emergency; and
 (i) a one-time service recognition incentive of not less than Ten thousand pesos
     (P10,000.00) upon voluntary separation after at least fifteen (15) years of satisfactory
     service.


                                             8
SEC. 19. Education and Career Advancement. - The DOH, CHED, TESDA, CSC,
Department of Education, LGUs, and educational institutions shall provide BHWs access
to scholarships, continuing education, study grants, field immersion, and ladderized or
equivalency programs. Documented years of service and completed training hours shall,
when consistent with law and academic standards, be credited under Republic Act Nos.
10968, 10647, and 12124.
    When a BHW cannot personally avail of an education grant, implementing agencies
may extend an appropriate scholarship or financial assistance program to one (1)
qualified child of the BHW, subject to guidelines and available appropriations.
SEC. 20. Civil Service Eligibility. - A certified BHW who has rendered at least five (5)
years of accumulated satisfactory service shall be granted a special BHW first-level
eligibility for health-related positions, subject to qualification standards and rules
promulgated by the CSC.
SEC. 21. Legal Assistance Livelihood and Mortuary Benefits. - The Public Attorney's
Office shall provide legal consultation and representation to a BHW in a civil, criminal, or
administrative case arising from the lawful performance of official duties, subject to its
mandate. Organized BHWs shall receive preferential access to appropriate government
livelihood programs. The Department of Social Welfare and Development and concerned
LGUs shall provide burial and funeral assistance to qualified BHWs and their families
under applicable programs.
SEC. 22. Non-Diminution of Benefits. - Nothing in this Act shall reduce or withdraw any
salary, honorarium, allowance, incentive, benefit, or privilege already enjoyed by a BHW
under Republic Act No. 7883, local ordinance, collective negotiation agreement, or
established practice.
                                      CHAPTER VI
                 RIGHTS RESPONSIBILITIES AND ORGANIZATION
SEC. 23. Rights of BHWs. - Every BHW shall have the right to dignity and respect; equal
opportunity and nondiscrimination; safe and humane working conditions; adequate
protective equipment; timely payment of compensation and benefits; access to training
and information; due process; freedom from harassment, coercion, retaliation, and
political interference; participation in decisions affecting community health and BHW
welfare; and self-organization consistent with law.




                                             9
SEC. 24. Duties and Ethical Standards. - BHWs shall perform their duties competently
and impartially, respect patient dignity and informed choice, protect confidential
information, avoid conflicts of interest, maintain required training and records, and comply
with lawful public health protocols and the code of conduct to be issued by the DOH.

SEC. 25. Prohibited Acts. - It shall be unlawful for any person to:
 (a) discriminate against, harass, coerce, threaten, or retaliate against a BHW on account
     of sex, gender, civil status, disability, age, religion, political belief or affiliation,
     ethnicity, or membership in a BHW organization;
 (b) withhold, divert, deduct, or delay without lawful cause any compensation, allowance,
     benefit, equipment, or supply intended for a BHW;
 (c) require a BHW to engage in partisan political activity as a condition for appointment,
     retention, assignment, compensation, or benefits; or
 (d) remove, replace, transfer, or disadvantage a BHW solely because of a change in
     political leadership or refusal to perform a partisan political act.
    Employed BHWs remain subject to constitutional and statutory restrictions on
partisan political activity applicable to civil service personnel. Volunteer BHWs may
exercise their political rights but shall not use government time, funds, equipment, or
authority for partisan purposes.
SEC. 26. Associations and Federations. - BHWs may form and join associations at the
municipal and city levels and federations at the provincial, regional, and national levels.
These organizations shall serve as venues for consultation, mutual support, policy
development, and the protection of BHW welfare. Their internal affairs shall be governed
by democratic constitutions and bylaws.
SEC. 27. Consultation and Representation. - Local health boards and concerned
national agencies shall consult duly recognized BHW associations and federations on
standards, staffing, training, compensation, working conditions, grievance mechanisms,
and policies directly affecting BHWs. Representation shall be inclusive of women,
geographically isolated communities, and other sectors within the BHW workforce.

                               CHAPTER VII
               FUNDING IMPLEMENTATION AND FINAL PROVISIONS



SEC. 28. BHW Regularization and Deployment Assistance Program. - The DOH and
DILG, in coordination with the DBM, shall establish a BHW Regularization and
Deployment Assistance Program to support LGUs with limited fiscal capacity in creating
plantilla positions, meeting staffing standards, and paying the minimum compensation
and benefits under this Act. Priority shall be given to lower-income LGUs and
geographically isolated, disadvantaged, unserved, or underserved areas.
    National government assistance for regularization shall be provided for a transition
period of ten (10) years, subject to annual performance, staffing, and financial reports.
The required LGU counterpart, if any, shall be calibrated according to income
classification and fiscal capacity and shall not prevent the timely payment of BHW
compensation.

SEC. 29. Funding Sources. - LGUs shall prioritize funds from their National Tax
Allotment and other lawful local revenues for BHW positions, compensation, and benefits.
The amounts necessary for the initial national implementation of this Act shall be charged
against the current appropriations of the DOH and other concerned agencies. Thereafter,
such amounts shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
    The DBM and DILG shall issue guidelines to facilitate compliance with personal
services limitations and other local budgeting rules without defeating the regularization
and minimum benefit requirements of this Act.
SEC. 30. Barangay Health Workers Day. - April 7 of every year is hereby declared
Barangay Health Workers Day. The DOH, DILG, and LGUs shall conduct appropriate
activities recognizing the service and contributions of BHWs.
SEC. 31. Annual Report. - The DOH and DILG shall submit to the President, the Senate,
and the House of Representatives, not later than March 31 of every year, a report on
BHW registration, certification, staffing, regularization, compensation, benefits, training,
grievances, program expenditures, implementation gaps, and recommended legislative
or administrative measures.
SEC. 32. Accountability. - A public officer or employee who commits a prohibited act
under Section 25 or willfully violates this Act shall be subject to administrative, civil, or
criminal liability under existing laws. Nothing herein shall preclude the filing of appropriate
actions under the Revised Penal Code, anti-graft laws, civil service laws, or other
applicable statutes.



                                              11
SEC. 33. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within one hundred eighty (180) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH and DILG, in consultation with the DBM, CSC,
TESDA, CHED, GSIS, PhilHealth, LGUs, recognized BHW organizations, public sector
unions, civil society, and academic and training institutions, shall promulgate the rules
and regulations necessary for its effective implementation.
SEC. 34. Congressional Oversight. - The appropriate committees of the Senate and
the House of Representatives shall exercise oversight over the implementation of this Act
and may require concerned agencies and LGUs to submit relevant reports and appear in
hearings.
SEC. 35. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue in full force
and effect.

SEC. 36. Repealing Clause. - Republic Act No. 7883, otherwise known as the Barangay
Health Workers' Benefits and Incentives Act of 1995, is hereby repealed. All laws,
decrees, executive orders, rules, regulations, and issuances inconsistent with this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 37. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
