21                                           EXPLANATORY NOTE
22
23          Energy is a key component of progress for a society. It is an essential tool for
24   nation-building and an effective weapon against poverty. In the Philippines, while access
25   to electricity has expanded across most of the country, it unfortunately remains
26   inaccessible for many, especially those who need it most.
27
28          The data is uncontestable. Despite an electrification level of 91.1% as of mid-2023,
29   approximately 2.45 million Filipino households remain without power. The majority of
30   them are in remote and off-grid areas,1 including in-city and off-city resettlement sites
31   occupied by Informal Settler Families (ISFs). The lack of reliable and affordable electricity
32   in resettlement sites also compromises the government’s program because ISFs find it
33   difficult, if not impossible, to remain in homes where there is no access to basic service
34   such as electricity.
35
36         Millions of Filipino households, especially resettlement sites, remain in the dark
37   because of two primary reasons. First, private utility companies require at least 90%
38   occupancy of subdivisions before connection to the grid is allowed.2 Second, alternatives

41
42          Solar Home System (SHS) – defined as a stand-alone or grid-tied photovoltaic
43   system designed to supply electricity for lighting, communications, and basic appliances
44   in a residential unit – can help solve this problem. It can, whenever applicable, exist
45   independently from grid connection, and effectively bypass the dependence of
46   households on private public utility companies as providers of access to electricity.
47
48          Unfortunately, while solar technology offers a solution, the next barrier is the cost
49   which is prohibitive. Currently, solar energy loans reach up to PhP 500,000.3 ISFs cannot
50   afford this. The remedy then is to spread out the obligation into manageable tranches and
51   include them as part of existing housing loans offered by the government, subject to
52   reasonable and favorable terms for the target beneficiaries.
53
54          By aligning new initiatives with – and enhancing – existing government programs,
55   the problem of households (especially ISFs) deprived of access to affordable electricity
56   could finally be solved. The importance of this initiative cannot be understated, given our
57   country’s vulnerability to external shocks, causing the costs of energy to shoot up, as the
58   recent geopolitical conflict in the Middle-East demonstrated.
59
60              In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

95                               AN ACT
96     CREATING THE SOLAR HOME SYSTEM FINANCING FOR SOCIAL HOUSING
97               PROGRAM, AND PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR
98
 99   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:
100
101         Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Solar Home System
102   Financing for Social Housing Program Act.”
103
104          Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes its constitutional duty to
105   make decent housing with basic services available at affordable cost to underprivileged
106   and homeless citizens in urban centers and resettlement areas. Electricity is indispensable
107   to health, education, safety, and economic participation.
108
109          Informal settler families (ISFs) displaced by government infrastructure projects,
110   urban renewal programs, or disaster risk reduction measures are disproportionately
111   excluded from grid electricity. Connection costs and occupancy-based service
112   requirements make grid power inaccessible to most resettlement communities, negating
113   the material benefits of resettlement.
114


                                                                                                      3
115           Consistent with the State’s policy of achieving energy self-reliance through
116   sustainable development, solar home systems are a proven, cost-effective solution for off-
117   grid and transitional housing communities. It is therefore the policy of the State to
118   integrate solar home system financing into socialized housing programs so that every
119   resettlement housing unit is delivered with a secure, clean, and affordable energy supply.
120
121          Section 3. Definition of Terms.
122
123           (a)   “Civil Society Organizations” or “CSOs” refer to non-government
124   organizations, people’s organizations, ISF homeowners’ associations, renewable energy
125   advocacy groups, housing cooperatives, trade unions, and foundations that are non-
126   profit and organized primarily for social or economic development purposes.
127
128          (b)    Informal Settler Families (ISFs) refer to households living in a lot, whether
129   private or public, without the consent of the property owner; or those without legal claim
130   over the property they are occupying; or those living in danger areas such as esteros,
131   railroad tracks, garbage dumps, riverbanks, shorelines, and waterways;
132
133         (c)   “Program” refers to the Solar Home System Financing for Social Housing
134   Program established under Section 4 of this Act.
135
136          (d)    “Socialized Housing Beneficiary” refers to an individual or household
137   that qualifies for and is allocated a socialized housing unit under the programs of the
138   National Housing Authority (NHA) or the SHFC (Social Housing Finance Corporation).
139
140          (e)   “Socialized Housing Unit” refers to a housing unit provided under the
141   programs of the NHA or the SHFC with a selling price at or below the ceiling prescribed
142   under Republic Act No. 7279 and its implementing rules, as may be adjusted from time
143   to time.
144
145          (f) “Solar Home System” or “SHS” refers to a stand-alone or grid-tied
146   photovoltaic system designed to supply electricity for lighting, communications, and
147   basic appliances in a residential unit. An SHS includes, at minimum, solar photovoltaic
148   panels, a charge controller or inverter, a battery storage system or grid-tie interface, and
149   associated wiring and mounting hardware.
150




                                                                                                4
151          (g)    “SHS Loan” refers to the financing component bundled into a socialized
152   housing loan to cover the cost of procuring and installing a solar home system on the
153   beneficiary’s housing unit.
154
155         Section 4. Solar Home System Financing for Social Housing Program. –
156
157          (a)   Establishment. The NHA and the SHFC shall establish and administer the
158   Solar Home System Financing for Social Housing Program. Under the Program, every
159   socialized housing unit in a new or ongoing resettlement project shall include a solar
160   home system as an integral component of the housing package delivered to the
161   beneficiary.
162
163           (b)  Financing Structure. The cost of the solar home system shall be
164   incorporated into the beneficiary’s housing loan as an SHS Loan component, subject to
165   the following:
166
167             (1) The SHS Loan shall be amortized over the same term as the housing loan,
168                 not to exceed thirty (30) years.
169             (2) The SHS Loan shall bear the same concessional interest rate applicable to
170                 the housing loan.
171             (3) No separate down payment for the SHS Loan shall be required of the
172                 beneficiary.
173             (4) The combined monthly amortization of the housing loan and SHS Loan
174                 shall not exceed the affordability threshold prescribed under the
175                 beneficiary’s housing program.
176
177          (c)    Procurement. The NHA and SHFC shall procure solar home systems
178   through competitive public bidding under Republic Act No. 9184, as amended. The
179   procurement shall prioritize systems that meet the minimum technical specifications
180   prescribed under Section 6 of this Act and carry a manufacturer’s warranty of not less
181   than ten (10) years on photovoltaic panels and five (5) years on all other components.
182
183          (d)   Installation and Commissioning. The NHA and SHFC shall ensure that
184   each solar home system is installed and commissioned prior to the turnover of the
185   housing unit to the beneficiary. Installation shall be performed by contractors accredited
186   by the Department of Energy or the Energy Regulatory Commission, as applicable.
187



                                                                                              5
188          (e)   Maintenance Reserve Fund. Each resettlement project covered under the
189   Program shall maintain a maintenance reserve fund, financed from a fixed monthly
190   contribution incorporated into the SHS Loan amortization. The fund shall be
191   administered by the implementing agency and used exclusively for routine upkeep and
192   component replacement of solar home systems within the project.
193
194         Section 5. Eligibility. – A beneficiary qualifies for the Program if the beneficiary:
195
196         (a)    Is a qualified socialized housing beneficiary of the NHA or the SHFC;
197
198           (b)    Is allocated a unit in an in-city or off-city resettlement site where grid
199   electricity is unavailable, unreliable, or where grid-connection costs exceed the
200   affordability threshold prescribed by the DHSUD; and
201
202          (c)    Does not hold an existing solar energy system loan from any government
203   financing institution covering the same housing unit.
204
205         Section 6. Technical Standards. – The Department of Energy shall, within sixty (60)
206   days from the effectivity of this Act, issue minimum technical specifications for solar
207   home systems eligible for financing under the Program.
208
209          Section 7. Community Orientation. – Before relocating beneficiaries, the NHA and
210   SHFC shall, in coordination with CSOs, orient each resettlement community on the
211   operation and maintenance of the solar home system, the terms of the SHS Loan, and the
212   procedures for reporting defects and disputes.
213
214          Section 8. Annual Report. – The NHA and SHFC shall jointly report to the
215   Committees on Housing and Urban Development of both chambers on or before March
216   30 of each year, covering the number of solar home systems installed, SHS Loan releases
217   and collections with delinquency rates, maintenance reserve fund balances per project,
218   and technical or operational issues with the corrective measures taken.
219
220          Section 9. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
221   this Act shall be charged against the existing appropriations of the NHA and SHFC.
222   Thereafter, the funds necessary for the full implementation of this Act shall be included
223   in the annual General Appropriations Act.
224
225           Section 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
226   effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of the Department of Human Settlements and Urban

                                                                                                    6
227   Development, as Chairperson, together with the authorized representatives of the NHA,
228   SHFC, National Home Mortgage Finance Corporation, Department of Energy, and
229   Department of the Interior and Local Government, shall jointly promulgate the
230   implementing rules and regulations necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act. At
231   least five (5) members of the drafting committee, selected by the DHSUD Secretary, shall
232   come from CSOs.
233
234           Section 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
235   unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force and
236   effect.
237
238          Section 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances, and rules and
239   regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
240   accordingly.
241
242         Section 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
243   publication in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation or in the Official Gazette.
