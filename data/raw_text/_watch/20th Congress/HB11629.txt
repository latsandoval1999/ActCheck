                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The 1987 Constitution provides that the State shall protect life and property, promote the
general welfare and the right to health, protect consumers from substandard or hazardous
products, and recognize the right of the people to information on matters of public
concern. Congress has already exercised this power over motor vehicles through multiple
statutory enactments which allow the LTO to inspect motor vehicles and to refuse
registration to those found unsafe, set forth conditions for registration in an emission test,
and prohibits deceptive sales acts and practices.

According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), 13,125 persons died in land
transport accidents in the Philippines in 2023. This was the highest number in more than
a decade and 7.2 percent more than the 12,240 deaths recorded in 2022.

Many of these deaths involved a defective vehicle which is unknown because the condition
of vehicles is barely recorded. According to the Metro Manila Accident Reporting and
Analysis System Annual Report 2020 of the Metropolitan Manila Development Authority
(MMDA), 63,741 of the 65,032 road crashes reported in Metro Manila in 2020, or 98
percent, were logged without any cause. Only six were attributed to brake malfunction and
nine to mechanical defect.

The registration process does not fill this gap. Republic Act No. 8749, or the Philippine
Clean Air Act of 1999, makes an emission test a condition for registration, but the law does
not expressly require a test of the brakes, tires, steering, or lights. According to the Land
Transportation Office (LTO), as reported by the Philippine Daily Inquirer on October 21,
2021, mandatory roadworthiness inspection has been suspended since August 2021. An
owner may instead renew registration through a private emission testing center, which tests
emissions only, together with a visual inspection by LTO personnel. According to the same



                                                                                            1
LTO statement, 80 percent of renewals take this route. A vehicle can thus be registered
year after year without its brakes ever being tested.

The same gap harms buyers of used vehicles. No official record shows whether a vehicle
was regularly serviced, was flooded or wrecked, or has had its odometer altered. Republic
Act No. 10642, or the Philippine Lemon Law, covers only brand-new vehicles.

The proposed measure therefore seeks to establish a National Vehicle History and Safety
Registry under the LTO that records the maintenance, repairs, inspections, odometer
readings, accidents, and safety recalls of every motor vehicle; require a roadworthiness
inspection, in addition to the emission test, before a motor vehicle is registered or its
registration is renewed; and penalize odometer tampering, false entries, and the
concealment of a vehicle's history.

In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                         AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL VEHICLE HISTORY AND SAFETY
REGISTRY, INSTITUTIONALIZING ROADWORTHINESS INSPECTION
AS A REQUIREMENT FOR THE REGISTRATION OF MOTOR VEHICLES,
REQUIRING THE REPORTING OF MAINTENANCE, REPAIR,
INSPECTION, AND ACCIDENT RECORDS OF MOTOR VEHICLES,
MANDATING DISCLOSURE IN THE SALE OF USED MOTOR VEHICLES,
PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF, AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

                                        CHAPTER I.
                                    GENERAL PROVISIONS

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "National Vehicle
History and Safety Registry Act of 2026."

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect the life,
safety, and property of all road users and to protect consumers against deceptive and unfair
sales acts and practices. Toward this end, the State shall:

        a. Maintain a single, reliable, and publicly accessible record of the condition and
           history of every motor vehicle operated on public roads;
        b. Promote the regular maintenance of motor vehicles as a shared responsibility
           of owners, the motor vehicle industry, and the government;
        c. Ensure that every buyer of a used motor vehicle is able to make an informed
           decision on the basis of complete and truthful information; and
        d. Uphold the right to privacy of motor vehicle owners in the collection and
           disclosure of vehicle information.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, and unless indicated
otherwise, the following terms shall be defined as:

       a. Critical safety defect refers to a defect or condition in a safety-critical system
          that makes the continued operation of the motor vehicle an imminent danger
          to its occupants or to other road users, as determined under standards set by
          the Land Transportation Office (LTO);
       b. Dealer refers to any person, natural or juridical, engaged in the business of
          selling new or used motor vehicles, including those who sell through online
          platforms, and any person who sells more than three (3) used motor vehicles
          within a period of twelve (12) months;
       c. Maintenance event refers to any periodic or preventive service performed on a
          motor vehicle, including the change of engine oil and filters, the replacement
          or replenishment of brake, transmission, and coolant fluids, and the inspection,
          adjustment, or replacement of parts subject to wear;
       d. Motor vehicle refers to any vehicle propelled by any power other than muscular
          power that is required to be registered with the LTO under Republic Act No.
          4136, as amended, including motorcycles, tricycles, and electric and hybrid
          vehicles;
       e. Registry refers to the National Vehicle History and Safety Registry established
          under this Act;
       f. Repair event refers to any work to restore or replace a damaged, defective, or
          failed part or system of a motor vehicle, including collision, body, and structural
          repair;
       g. Reporting entity refers to any person or office required under Section 8 of this
          Act to submit information to the Registry;
       h. Safety-critical system refers to the brakes, steering, suspension, tires and wheels,
          lights and signals, seat belts, airbags and other restraint systems, chassis and
          body structure, and, for electric and hybrid vehicles, the high-voltage battery
          and its protective systems, together with such other systems as the LTO may
          include;
       i. Safety recall refers to a campaign by a manufacturer, assembler, importer, or
          distributor to repair or replace a part or system of a motor vehicle model on
          account of a safety-related defect;
       j. Service and repair shop refers to any establishment or person engaged in the
          business of servicing, repairing, or maintaining motor vehicles for a fee,
          including dealer service centers, independent workshops, tire, battery, and
          brake shops, and body and paint shops;
       k. Used motor vehicle refers to a motor vehicle that has been previously registered
          in the name of, or delivered to, an owner other than a manufacturer, assembler,
          importer, distributor, or dealer holding it for sale as new;

       l. Vehicle History Report refers to the official report generated from the Registry
          under Section 18 of this Act; and
       m. Vehicle record refers to the electronic record of a particular motor vehicle in

       SEC. 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all motor vehicles registered or required
to be registered with the LTO, whether for private, for-hire, or government use. Motor
vehicles of the Armed Forces of the Philippines and the Philippine National Police that are
used for tactical or intelligence operations shall be exempt. The LTO may prescribe
modified rules for vintage vehicles registered under Republic Act No. 11698.


                              CHAPTER II.
            THE NATIONAL VEHICLE HISTORY AND SAFETY REGISTRY

        SEC. 5. Establishment of the Registry.. – There is hereby established a National
Vehicle History and Safety Registry, which shall be the central electronic repository of the
history and condition of every motor vehicle covered by this Act. The Registry shall be
developed, operated, and maintained by the LTO as a component of, or in full integration
with, its existing motor vehicle registration system. Each motor vehicle shall have one (1)
vehicle record, which shall be identified by its vehicle identification number or chassis
number, engine or motor number, plate number, and motor vehicle file number, and which
shall follow the vehicle throughout its life regardless of any change in ownership, plate, or
place of registration.

        Net increase in employment. – Qualified wages paid for a given month shall be
creditable only if the number of Filipino employees of the covered employer in that month,
as shown in its contribution reports to the SSS, exceeds its baseline headcount, and only
for a number of qualified provincial employees not exceeding that excess. Where the
qualified provincial employees outnumber the excess, the covered employer shall designate
those for whom the credit is claimed.

       SEC. 6. Contents of the Vehicle Record. – The vehicle record shall contain the
following:

       a. Identity and specifications – make, model, variant, year of manufacture, body
          type, color, fuel or power source, and, for imported units, the date and port of
          importation and whether the unit was imported as new or used;
       b. Registration and ownership history – dates of initial registration and of every
          renewal, the number and dates of transfers of ownership, the classification of
          use for each period, and any change of engine, chassis, color, or body
          configuration approved by the LTO;
       c. Encumbrances and alarms – any mortgage or other encumbrance annotated on


                                                                                           3
          the certificate of registration, and any alarm, hold order, or report of carnapping
          or loss;
       d. Odometer readings – the reading recorded at every maintenance event, repair
          event, inspection, and transfer of ownership;
       e. Maintenance history – the date, odometer reading, nature of the service
          performed, and identity of the service and repair shop for every maintenance
          event;
       f. Repair history – the date, odometer reading, system or part repaired or replaced,
          and identity of the service and repair shop for every repair event involving a
          safety-critical system, the engine, or the transmission;
       g. Inspection history – the date, result, and findings of every roadworthiness
          inspection and emission test;
       h. Incident history – every road crash reported to the police or to an insurer, every
          insurance claim for damage to the vehicle, any declaration of total loss, and any
          report of flood, fire, or structural damage;
       i. Safety recalls – every safety recall that applies to the vehicle and whether the
          remedy has been performed; and
       j. Such other information relevant to the safety and history of the vehicle as the
          LTO may require, after public consultation.

       SEC. 7. Integrity of Records. – No entry in a vehicle record shall be deleted. An
erroneous entry shall be corrected only by a dated annotation that identifies the correcting
party and preserves the original entry. Every entry, annotation, and access shall be recorded
in an audit trail. Each entry shall identify its source and shall be marked as verified when
submitted by a reporting entity, or as owner-declared when submitted under Section 10 of
this Act.

                                CHAPTER III.
                         REPORTING TO THE REGISTRY

      SEC. 8. Reporting Entities. – The following shall submit to the Registry the
information indicated:

       a. Manufacturers, assemblers, importers, and distributors – the identity and
          specifications of every motor vehicle sold or released in the Philippines, the
          manufacturer-recommended maintenance schedule for each model, and every
          safety recall and the vehicles it covers;
       b. Dealers – the delivery of every new motor vehicle, and every sale or acquisition
          of a used motor vehicle, together with the odometer reading at the time;
       c. Service and repair shops – every maintenance event and every repair event
          described in Section 6(e) and (f) of this Act, the performance of any safety recall
          remedy, and any critical safety defect found;


                                                                                           4
       d. Motor vehicle inspection centers and emission testing centers, whether
          government or private – the result and findings of every inspection or test;
       e. Insurance companies – every claim for damage to a motor vehicle, the nature
          and extent of the damage, and any declaration of total loss, under rules issued
          jointly with the Insurance Commission;
       f. The Philippine National Police and other agencies that investigate road crashes
          – every road crash involving a motor vehicle, and every report of carnapping or
          recovery;
       g. Banks, financing companies, and other mortgagees – the constitution and
          cancellation of any encumbrance on a motor vehicle; and
       h. The LTO – all registration, transfer, and alarm transactions, and any change in
          the identity or configuration of a motor vehicle that it approves.

       SEC. 9. Manner and Period of Reporting. – A reporting entity shall submit the
required information electronically within five (5) working days from the event to be
reported. No fee shall be charged for submitting information to the Registry. The service
and repair shop or inspection center shall record the odometer reading at the time of the
event and shall give the owner or the owner's representative a copy or electronic
confirmation of the entry.

        SEC. 10. Owner – Declared Entries. – A registered owner who personally performs
a maintenance event, or who has it performed by a person not registered under Section 11
of this Act, may declare the event to the Registry through the owner's account, with the
date, odometer reading, and nature of the work, and may attach receipts or photographs
as proof. The entry shall be marked as owner-declared.

        SEC. 11. Registration and Repair Shops. – Every service and repair shop shall
register with the LTO for purposes of reporting to the Registry. Registration shall be free
of charge, shall require only proof of identity and of a valid business permit or barangay
micro-business registration, and shall be completed within three (3) working days from
application. Registration under this section is separate from, and shall not be conditioned
on, accreditation by the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) under Presidential
Decree No. 1572. The LTO and the DTI shall share their lists of registered and accredited
shops.

        SEC. 12. Correction and Dispute Entries. – A registered owner may view the full
vehicle record of the owner's motor vehicle at any time, free of charge, and may dispute
any entry believed to be inaccurate. The LTO shall refer the dispute to the reporting entity
concerned, which shall confirm or correct the entry within ten (10) working days. The LTO
shall resolve the dispute within twenty (20) working days from filing. While a dispute is
pending, the entry shall be marked as disputed in any report generated from the Registry.

                                    CHAPTER IV.
                         SAFETY STATUS OF MOTOR VEHICLES

       SEC. 13. Safety Status Indicators. – The Registry shall automatically generate and
display the following indicators for every motor vehicle, each with the date of the entry on
which it is based:

       a. Maintenance status – whether periodic maintenance, including engine oil
          service, is up to date, overdue, or without record, measured against the
          manufacturer-recommended schedule or, in its absence, a default schedule set
          by the LTO by vehicle type;
       b. Safety-critical systems status – for the brakes, tires, steering, suspension, lights,
          and restraint systems, whether each passed, passed with findings, or failed the
          most recent inspection or service, or has no record;
       c. Inspection status – the date and result of the most recent roadworthiness
          inspection and emission test;
       d. Open safety recall – whether any safety recall remains unremedied;
       e. Major incident – whether the vehicle has a record of total loss, flood, fire, or
          structural damage;
       f. Odometer consistency – whether the recorded odometer readings are
          consistent, or show a decrease or other irregularity; and
       g. Unresolved critical safety defect – whether a critical safety defect reported
          under Section 16 of this Act remains unrectified.

        SEC. 14. Effect of Indicators. – The safety status indicators are a statement of what
has been reported to the Registry and when. The absence of a record shall not be construed
as a finding that a motor vehicle is unsafe. A maintenance status of overdue or without


                                                                                             6
record shall not, by itself, be a ground for the apprehension of a driver, the imposition of
a fine, the denial of registration, or the denial of an insurance claim. The LTO shall notify
the registered owner electronically when periodic maintenance or an inspection falls due,
and when a safety recall is issued for the owner's vehicle.

       SEC. 15. Roadworthiness Inspection as a Requirement for Registration. – In
addition to the emission test required under Republic Act No. 8749, or the Philippine Clean
Air Act of 1999, no motor vehicle shall be registered, and no registration shall be renewed,
unless the motor vehicle has passed a roadworthiness inspection of its safety-critical
systems. For this purpose:

       a. The LTO shall prescribe uniform inspection standards and procedures for each
          type of motor vehicle. These shall cover, at the minimum, the brakes, tires and
          wheels, steering, suspension, lights and signals, seat belts, windshield, wipers,
          mirrors, horn, and chassis and body structure, and shall include the recording
          of the odometer reading;
       b. The inspection shall be conducted by the LTO or by a motor vehicle inspection
          center authorized by it. An owner may have the motor vehicle inspected at any
          authorized center in the country, and no owner shall be assigned or restricted
          to a particular center. The roadworthiness inspection and the emission test shall,
          as far as practicable, be done in a single visit;
       c. A brand-new motor vehicle shall be exempt from the roadworthiness
          inspection during the validity of its initial registration;
       d. The DOTr shall fix the inspection fee, after public consultation, at a level that
          covers only the reasonable cost of the service. No fee shall be charged for one
          (1) reinspection made within thirty (30) days from a failed inspection;
       e. The motor vehicle must be physically presented for inspection. The result, the
          findings for each system inspected, and photographic or video proof of the
          inspection shall be transmitted to the Registry upon completion. The owner of
          a motor vehicle that fails shall be given the specific findings in writing;
       f. Where there is no authorized inspection center within a reasonable distance, as
          determined by the LTO, the inspection shall be conducted by the LTO district
          office or by a mobile inspection unit under the same standards; and
       g. This section shall be implemented by area as inspection capacity becomes
          available, and nationwide not later than twenty-four (24) months from the
          effectivity of this Act. Until then, the existing inspection and emission testing
          requirements of the LTO shall continue to apply.

       SEC. 16. Critical Safety Defects and Safety Recalls. –

       a. A service and repair shop or inspection center that finds a critical safety defect
          shall record the finding in the Registry and shall inform the owner or the


                                                                                           7
           owner's representative in writing before the motor vehicle is released. The
           owner shall have the defect rectified and the rectification reported by a
           registered service and repair shop or inspection center within thirty (30) days
           from the finding. An owner who contests the finding may submit the motor
           vehicle to the LTO or to any authorized motor vehicle inspection center, whose
           finding shall prevail. If the defect is neither rectified nor overturned within the
           period, the vehicle record shall carry an unresolved critical safety defect
           indicator, and the LTO may, after notice to the owner and pursuant to its
           authority under Republic Act No. 4136, as amended, require the motor vehicle
           to undergo a roadworthiness inspection ahead of its next renewal of
           registration.

       b. The manufacturer, assembler, importer, or distributor that issues a safety recall
          shall report it to the Registry within five (5) working days from its issuance,
          shall notify the registered owner of every affected motor vehicle using contact
          details relayed through the LTO, and shall perform the remedy free of charge
          regardless of the number of previous owners or of where the motor vehicle has
          been serviced.

                           CHAPTER V.
           TRANSPARENCY IN THE SALE OF MOTOR VEHICLES

        SEC. 17. Public Status Check and Plate Scanning. – Any person may, free of
charge and through a website or mobile application maintained by the LTO, enter the plate
number or vehicle identification number of a motor vehicle, or scan its plate, and view its
make, model, year, color, registration status, and safety status indicators under Section 13
of this Act. For this purpose, the LTO shall incorporate a plate scanning feature in its
existing official website and mobile application, by which a user may scan the plate number,
or the machine-readable code on the plate, using the camera of an ordinary mobile device.
The feature shall be integrated with the existing online systems of the LTO and shall not
require a separate application. Where the scanned plate does not match the make, model,
or color recorded in the Registry, the user shall be so informed and may report the
discrepancy to the LTO through the same facility.

        SEC. 18. Vehicle History Report. – The LTO shall issue, upon request and payment
of the prescribed fee, a Vehicle History Report containing the information in Section 6 of
this Act. The report shall bear a unique reference number and a machine-readable code by
which any person may verify its authenticity and date of issuance against the Registry. A
Vehicle History Report may be requested by the registered owner or, with the registered
owner's consent given through the Registry, by a prospective buyer, insurer, or financing
institution.



                                                                                            8
        SEC. 19. Mandatory Disclosure in the Sale of Used Motor Vehicles. – Every seller
of a used motor vehicle, whether a dealer or a private individual, shall furnish the buyer,
before the execution of the deed of sale, a Vehicle History Report issued not more than
fifteen (15) days before the sale. The reference number of the report shall be stated in the
deed of sale. In addition:

       a. A dealer shall display the reference number or verification code of the Vehicle
          History Report on every used motor vehicle offered for sale and in every
          advertisement or online listing of the vehicle;
       b. A seller who knows of any accident, flood, fire, or structural damage, odometer
          replacement or irregularity, or critical safety defect that does not appear in the
          Vehicle History Report shall disclose it to the buyer in writing; and
       c. Any stipulation waiving the right of the buyer to the report or to the disclosure
          required under this section, including a sale on an "as is, where is" basis, shall
          be void as to such waiver.

        SEC. 20. Transfer of Ownership. – The LTO shall not process the transfer of
registration of a used motor vehicle unless the deed of sale states the reference number of
a valid Vehicle History Report. The odometer reading at the time of sale shall be declared
by the seller and the buyer and recorded in the Registry upon transfer.

        SEC. 21. Remedies of the Buyer. – Where the seller fails to furnish a Vehicle
History Report, furnishes a falsified or altered report, or conceals a fact required to be
disclosed under Section 19(b) of this Act, the buyer may, within six (6) months from
delivery of the motor vehicle, either rescind the sale and recover the purchase price with
legal interest and damages, or retain the vehicle and demand a proportionate reduction of
the price with damages. These remedies are in addition to those available under the Civil
Code of the Philippines, Republic Act No. 7394 or the Consumer Act of the Philippines,
and other laws.

       SEC. 22. Data Privacy and Security. – The processing of personal information
under this Act shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012.
For this purpose:

       a. The public status check and the Vehicle History Report shall not disclose the
          name, address, contact details, or other personal information of any present or
          previous owner. Previous ownership shall be shown only as the number of
          owners, the period of each ownership, the classification of use, and the region
          of registration;
       b. Information in the Registry shall not be used for commercial solicitation,
          profiling of individuals, or any purpose other than those stated in this Act;
       c. Access by law enforcement agencies to personal information in the Registry
          shall be governed by existing laws and rules;

                                                                                          9
       d. The LTO shall conduct a privacy impact assessment in consultation with the
          National Privacy Commission before the Registry becomes operational and
          every three (3) years thereafter; and
       e. The LTO shall adopt security measures consistent with standards issued by the
          Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), and
          shall retain a vehicle record for five (5) years after the motor vehicle is
          permanently retired, scrapped, or exported.

                                   CHAPTER VI.
                                 ADMINISTRATION

         SEC. 23. Implementing Agency. – The LTO, under the supervision of the
Department of Transportation (DOTr), shall be the lead implementing agency of this Act.
It shall:

       a. Develop, operate, and maintain the Registry and the public website and mobile
          application;
       b. Register reporting entities and prescribe the data standards, forms, and
          procedures for reporting;
       c. Set the default maintenance schedules and the standards for determining critical
          safety defects, in consultation with the DTI and the motor vehicle industry;
       d. Monitor compliance, audit entries, and investigate violations of this Act; and
       e. Conduct a continuing public information campaign on the use of the Registry.

       SEC. 24. Vehicle Registry Advisory Council. – There is hereby created a Vehicle
Registry Advisory Council to advise the LTO on the implementation of this Act. It shall
be chaired by the Secretary of Transportation or a duly authorized representative, with the
head of the LTO as vice chairperson, and shall have as members one (1) representative
each from the DTI, the DICT, the National Privacy Commission, the Insurance
Commission, and the Philippine National Police, and one (1) representative each from the
following sectors, to be appointed by the Secretary of Transportation from nominees of
their organizations: motor vehicle manufacturers and importers, motor vehicle dealers
including used vehicle dealers, independent service and repair shops, motorcycle owners
and riders, public transport operators, and consumer organizations. Members shall serve
without additional compensation.

        SEC. 25. Interoperability and Ease of Reporting. – The Registry shall accept
information through a free web and mobile reporting facility usable on an ordinary
smartphone, and through direct electronic connection with the systems of reporting
entities under open technical standards published by the LTO. No reporting entity shall be
required to purchase any particular software, device, or service from a specified provider
as a condition for reporting.



                                                                                        10
       SEC. 26. Fees. – No fee shall be charged for registration as a reporting entity, for
reporting to the Registry, for the public status check, or for an owner's access to the owner's
own vehicle record. The DOTr, upon recommendation of the LTO and after public
consultation, shall fix a reasonable fee for the Vehicle History Report, which shall not
exceed the cost of issuing it.

       SEC. 27. Assistance to Micro and Small Enterprises. – The LTO, the DTI, and
the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority shall provide free orientation
and training on reporting to service and repair shops that are micro or small enterprises as
defined under Republic Act No. 6977, as amended, and shall make assisted reporting
available at LTO offices and other accessible venues in areas with limited internet
connectivity.

                                CHAPTER VII.
                       PROHIBITED ACTS AND PENALTIES

       SEC. 28. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts are prohibited:

        a. Resetting, altering, disconnecting, or replacing the odometer of a motor vehicle
           to misrepresent the distance it has travelled, or selling, installing, or offering any
           device or service for that purpose. The replacement of a defective odometer
           shall be reported to the Registry with the readings of both the old and the new
           unit;
        b. Submitting to the Registry any false or fictitious entry, including an entry for
           work or an inspection that was not performed or a false finding of a critical
           safety defect;
        c. Forging, altering, or counterfeiting a Vehicle History Report, or knowingly
           using one;
        d. Selling a used motor vehicle without furnishing the Vehicle History Report
           required under Section 19 of this Act, or concealing a fact required to be
           disclosed under Section 19(b);
        e. Failing or refusing, as a reporting entity, to submit required information within
           the period in Section 9 of this Act, or operating a service and repair shop
           without the registration required under Section 11;
        f. Charging a motor vehicle owner any fee for reporting to the Registry, or
           requiring a reporting entity to procure software, a device, or a service from a
           specified provider in violation of Section 25 of this Act; and
        g. Accessing, altering, or deleting any vehicle record without authority, or using
           information from the Registry for a purpose prohibited under Section 22 of this
           Act.

       SEC. 29. Penalties. – The following penalties shall be imposed:

           administrative fine of not less than Twenty thousand pesos (₱20,000.00) but
           not more than Fifty thousand pesos (₱50,000.00) and suspension of registration
           as a reporting entity for the third and subsequent offenses. The fines under this
           paragraph shall be reduced by one-half (1/2) for micro enterprises;
        e. For a violation of Section 28(f), an administrative fine of not less than Twenty
           thousand pesos (₱20,000.00) but not more than One hundred thousand pesos
           (₱100,000.00); and
        f. For a violation of Section 28(g), a fine of not less than One hundred thousand
           pesos (₱100,000.00) but not more than One million pesos (₱1,000,000.00), or
           imprisonment of not less than one (1) year but not more than three (3) years,
           or both, at the discretion of the court, without prejudice to prosecution under
           Republic Act No. 10173 and Republic Act No. 10175, or the Cybercrime
           Prevention Act of 2012.

        SEC. 30. Liability of Juridical Persons and Public Officers. – If the offender is a
corporation, partnership, or other juridical entity, the penalty shall be imposed on the
officers who knowingly authorized or permitted the violation, and the fine shall be imposed
on the entity. If the offender is a public officer or employee, the maximum penalty shall be
imposed, in addition to administrative liability and perpetual disqualification from public
office where warranted.

       SEC. 31. Administrative Sanctions and Due Process. – Administrative fines under
this Act shall be imposed by the LTO after notice and hearing. The LTO may, in addition,
recommend to the appropriate agency or local government unit the suspension or
revocation of the accreditation, authority, or business permit of a dealer, service and repair

shop, or inspection center that repeatedly violates this Act. The penalties under this Act
are without prejudice to civil liability and to prosecution under the Revised Penal Code and

                                CHAPTER VIII.
                    TRANSITORY AND FINAL PROVISIONS

      SEC. 32. Transitory Provisions. – The following shall govern the initial
implementation of this Act:

       a. The Registry shall be operational within eighteen (18) months from the
          effectivity of this Act. The LTO shall publish the date of operation in the
          Official Gazette and in a newspaper of general circulation;
       b. Upon the date of operation, the reporting obligations under this Act shall apply
          to manufacturers, assemblers, importers, distributors, dealers and their service
          centers, inspection and emission testing centers, insurance companies,
          mortgagees, and government agencies. They shall apply to all other service and
          repair shops twelve (12) months after the date of operation, and to those that
          are micro enterprises twenty-four (24) months after the date of operation;
       c. The LTO may implement this Act by type of motor vehicle, provided that all
          motor vehicles, including motorcycles and tricycles, are covered not later than
          thirty-six (36) months from the effectivity of this Act;
       d. Sections 19, 20, and 21 of this Act shall apply to sales made six (6) months after
          the date of operation;
       e. Existing registration, inspection, and alarm records of the LTO shall be
          migrated to the Registry. Reporting entities may submit records of events that
          occurred before the date of operation, which shall be marked as historical; and
       f. Every vehicle record and Vehicle History Report shall state the date from which
          reporting for that vehicle became mandatory, and that records before that date
          may be incomplete.

       SEC. 33. Annual Report and Congressional Oversight. – The LTO shall submit
to the President and to both Houses of Congress, not later than March 31 of every year, a
report on the implementation of this Act, including the number of vehicle records,
compliance by reporting entities, reports issued, violations acted upon, and data security
incidents. The Committees on Transportation of the House of Representatives and on
Public Services of the Senate shall exercise oversight over the implementation of this Act.

        SEC. 34. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the LTO. Thereafter,
such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 35. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOTr, through the LTO and in consultation with the DTI,
the DICT, the National Privacy Commission, the Insurance Commission, the Philippine
National Police, and stakeholders from the sectors named in Section 24 of this Act, shall
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its effective implementation.

       SEC. 36. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any provision of this Act, or any
part thereof, is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions hereof not
otherwise affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

        SEC. 37. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations or
parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 38. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
