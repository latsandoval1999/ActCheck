                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The digital age has fundamentally transformed the way Filipinos learn,
communicate, conduct business, access government services, and participate in
society. With the widespread availability of the internet, social media platforms, digital
applications, and emerging technologies, digital tools have become an integral part of
daily life. From education and employment to commerce and civic engagement, digital
technologies now serve as essential instruments for personal and national
development.

        However, the increasing reliance on digital platforms also highlights the
growing need for digital literacy among all Filipinos. While access to technology
continues to expand, many individuals still lack the knowledge and skills necessary to
navigate the digital environment safely, responsibly, and effectively. The ability to use
digital devices is no longer sufficient; citizens must also possess the capacity to
critically evaluate information, protect their privacy and security, communicate
respectfully online, understand their rights and responsibilities in digital spaces, and
utilize technology in a productive and ethical manner.

       As more Filipinos engage in online activities, the consequences of irresponsible
internet use become increasingly apparent. The careless sharing of unverified
information, exposure to online scams and fraudulent schemes, cyberbullying, identity
theft, privacy violations, and other harmful online practices underscore the need for
greater awareness and education. Every internet user bears a corresponding
responsibility to exercise good judgment, respect others, safeguard personal
information, and contribute positively to online communities.

      Digital literacy is therefore not merely a technical skill but a fundamental life
competency. It empowers individuals to become responsible digital citizens capable of
making informed decisions, engaging constructively in online discourse, utilizing
technology for learning and economic opportunities, and adapting to the demands of
an increasingly digital society. It also strengthens national competitiveness by
preparing the workforce for the digital economy and fostering innovation,
entrepreneurship, and lifelong learning.

       Recognizing the vital role of digital literacy in nation-building, this measure
seeks to establish a comprehensive national framework that promotes digital, media,
and information literacy among Filipinos. It aims to cultivate responsible digital
citizenship, encourage safe and ethical internet use, strengthen digital competencies
across all sectors, and ensure that every Filipino is equipped to participate
meaningfully and responsibly in the digital world.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                 AN ACT
     PROMOTING DIGITAL, MEDIA, AND INFORMATION LITERACY AMONG
     FILIPINOS, ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL DIGITAL LITERACY PROGRAM,
     STRENGTHENING RESPONSIBLE DIGITAL CITIZENSHIP, INTEGRATING
     DIGITAL LITERACY IN EDUCATION AND COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT
     INITIATIVES, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

 1   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
 2   Congress assembled:
 3
 4          SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the " Digital Literacy
 5   Act of 2026."
 6
 7           SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
 8   State to strengthen digital, media, and information literacy among Filipinos and to
 9   promote responsible, ethical, and informed engagement in digital spaces. Towards
10   this end, the State shall encourage critical thinking, accountability, transparency, and
11   responsible communication in the use of digital technologies. It shall foster a culture
12   of discernment and responsible information sharing, while safeguarding freedom of
13   expression and the free exchange of ideas consistent with the Constitution. The State
14   shall likewise promote ethical conduct in digital environments and develop a digitally
15   literate citizenry capable of effectively accessing, evaluating, creating, and sharing
16   information, and of participating responsibly and productively in an increasingly digital
17   society.
18
19          SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:
20
 1                (a) Digital Literacy refers to the ability to access, evaluate, analyze,
 2                create, and communicate information using digital technologies
 3                responsibly and effectively.
 4
 5                (b) Media Literacy refers to the ability to critically understand,
 6                evaluate, and assess information conveyed through various media
 7                platforms.
 8
 9                (c) Information Literacy refers to the ability to identify, locate, verify,
10                evaluate, and use information effectively.
11
12                (d) Misinformation refers to false, inaccurate, misleading,
13                manipulated, or unverified information that is shared or disseminated
14                regardless of intent.
15
16                (e) Disinformation refers to false or misleading information
17                deliberately created, altered, or disseminated to deceive the public.
18
19                (f) Digital Platform refers to any website, application, social media
20                service, online forum, messaging service, content-sharing platform, or
21                similar digital communication system.
22
23                (g) Digital Citizenship refers to responsible, ethical, and productive
24                participation in digital environments.
25
26                (h) Digital Safety refers to practices that protect individuals from
27                online risks, cyber threats, fraud, and harmful digital activities.
28
29                (i) Digital Inclusion refers to ensuring that all individuals have
30                opportunities to acquire digital skills regardless of age, gender, income
31                level, disability, geographic location, or social status.
32
33                (j) Responsible Sharing refers to the practice of verifying the
34                accuracy, authenticity, and reliability of information prior to reposting,
35                forwarding, distributing, or amplifying such information.
36
37                (k) Fact-Checking refers to the process of independently verifying the
38                accuracy of information through reliable and credible sources.
