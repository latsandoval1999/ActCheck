EXPLANATORY NOTE

The mango has long been recognized by tradition and custom as the national fruit
of the Philippines. In 1995, the Philippine Carabao Mango was recognized by the
Guinness Book of World Records as the sweetest mango in the world. Despite this
distinction, however, the Philippine mango industry has experienced a significant decline
in production over the past decade. From reaching more than 900,000 metric tons at its
peak during the middle of the 2010s, annual mango production has since fallen and, in
recent years, has generally remained within the range of 500,000 to 700,000 metric tons.'
This decline reflects persistent structural and production challenges that have constrained
the productivity, profitability, and competitiveness of the industry.

One of the most serious challenges confronting mango growers is the infestation
of cecid fly, locally known as “kurikong.” The pest attacks mangoes shortly after
flowering and causes brown, scab-like lesions on the fruit, severely affecting both yield
and marketability. In heavily affected areas, cecid fly infestation may result in losses of
up to 80 percent of mango fruits.? The persistent prevalence of this pest has also
contributed to the extensive use of chemical pesticides in mango production. The
Philippine Mango Industry Roadmap 2021-2025 identified cecid fly infestation as a
major industry concern and noted the distinct challenges faced by the Philippines
compared with other major mango-producing countries such as Thailand and Vietnam,
which have pursued more stringent regulation and management of agrochemical use.
Beyond production constraints, the Philippine mango industry also faces significant
market-related challenges that limit its ability to maximize the economic value of its
products and compete in both domestic and international markets. These include
difficulties in export market penetration, inconsistent product quality and standardization,
inadequate post-harvest handling and storage facilities, limited access to value-added
processing technologies, and insufficient market development and trade promotion
initiatives. These constraints affect the quality, shelf life, and marketability of Philippine
mangoes, limiting opportunities for farmers and industry stakeholders to access
higher-value markets and compete with neighboring mango-producing countries.

At present, mango-related policies, programs, and support services are dispersed
among various government agencies, including the Department of Agriculture (DA), the

Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI), the Philippine Council for Agriculture, Aquatic and
Natural Resources Research and Development (PCAARRD), and the Philippine Council
for Agriculture and Fisheries (PCAF), among others. While these agencies perform
essential functions within their respective mandates, the industry lacks a unified
industry-wide coordinating mechanism that can harmonize policies, align government
interventions, consolidate development priorities, and ensure the sustained
implementation of a comprehensive national strategy for the mango sector. The
establishment of a dedicated Philippine Mango Industry Development Board (PMIDB) is
therefore necessary to provide the institutional leadership, strategic direction, and
sustained coordination required to address the industry's interconnected challenges.
Rather than duplicate the functions of existing agencies, the PMIDB shall serve as the
central policy and coordinating body for the planning, development, and implementation
of programs for the mango industry. The PMIDB shall be responsible for formulating and
implementing a comprehensive Mango Industry Development Plan encompassing
short-term, medium-term, and long-term strategies covering production, pest and disease
management, post-harvest systems, processing, marketing, distribution, and export
development.

Complementing the PMIDB shall be the Mango Industry Planning and Science
Center (MIPSC), which shall serve as the technical, scientific, and research arm of the
national mango industry development framework. This proposed institutional framework
seeks to strengthen the capacity of Filipino mango farmers and industry stakeholders to
address longstanding production constraints, reduce excessive dependence on chemical
interventions through science-based pest management, improve fruit quality and
post-harvest systems, expand value-adding and processing opportunities, and strengthen
the country’s position in the global mango market. Equally important, this measure
recognizes that revitalizing the mango industry must be anchored on the welfare and
economic security of Filipino farmers, particularly smallholder growers and farming
communities whose livelihoods depend on the sector. Increased productivity and market
competitiveness must translate into better incomes, more stable livelihood opportunities,
and improved access to government support and essential services. Accordingly, the bill
promotes farmer capacity-building and training, access to crop insurance, scholarships
and social services, and rehabilitation assistance for growers and communities affected by
pests, diseases, calamities, and other adverse circumstances. By placing farmers at the
center of industry development, the measure seeks to ensure that the benefits of increased
production, improved market access, and industry modernization are shared across the
entire value chain.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought to revitalize
the Philippine mango industry, strengthen the livelihoods of Filipino mango farmers and
workers, and ensure that the country’s national fruit remains a source of pride,
employment, income, and sustainable economic opportunity for generations to come.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State
(a) Protect, invest in, and capitalize on the country’s strengths in mango

(b) Resolve production challenges as the root cause of poor industry performance

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall be defined as follows:

(a) Carabao Mango — refers to the Philippine mango variety recognized
internationally for its superior taste, sweetness, and export potential.

(b) Cluster-Based Production System — refers to cooperative or group farming
arrangements designed to improve efficiency, competitiveness, and access to
financing for mango farmers.

(c) Commercial-Scale Mango Farming — refers to large-scale mango farming
operations eligible for incentives and tax exemptions under this Act.

(d) Crop Insurance — refers to specialized insurance products designed to protect
mango farmers against losses due to pests, diseases, natural calamities, or
climate-related risks.

(e) Farmgate Price ~ refers to the price of mangoes at the point of sale from
farmers to buyers, exclusive of transport, packaging, and marketing costs.

(f) Farmgate Price Monitoring System ~ refers to the national system established
by the PMIDB to track, publish, and regulate mango farmgate prices to
prevent unfair trading practices.

(g) Mango Industry Development Plan — refers to the short-term, medium-term,
and long-term strategies formulated by the PMIDB covering production,
post-harvest, processing, marketing, distribution, and export.

(h) Mango Industry Planning and Science Center (MIPSC) — refers to the
technical and scientific arm of the PMIDB tasked with research, technology
development, laboratory services, and industry data management.

(i) Maximum Residue Level (MRL) — refers to the maximum concentration of
pesticide residues legally permitted in mangoes, in accordance with
international food safety standards.

(j) National Mango Brand - refers to the unified branding strategy developed by
the PMIDB to promote Philippine mangoes in domestic and international
markets.

(k) Federation of Mango Industry Stakeholders Associations (FMISA) — refers to
the organized body of mango industry stakeholders under the PMIDB, serving
as a collective voice in industry development and reform.

(1) Orchard Rehabilitation — refers to programs and interventions aimed at
restoring productivity of aging, damaged, or underutilized mango orchards.

(m)Philippine Mango Industry Development Board (PMIDB) -— refers to the
central governing body created under this Act, mandated to plan, coordinate,
and implement programs for the development of the mango industry.

===== [page 5] =====
(n) Post-Harvest Facilities — refers to infrastructure such as cold storage, drying,
packaging, and processing centers necessary to preserve mango quality and
extend shelf life.

(0) Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) Standards — refers to international standards
ensuring food safety, plant health, and compliance for mango exports.

(p) Smallholder Mango Farmers — refers to farmers cultivating limited mango
orchard areas, prioritized for support programs under this Act.

(q) Stakeholder Training Management Program — refers to structured training
initiatives for mango farmers and processors, regularly updated to reflect
modern technologies and best practices.

SEC. 4. Objectives. - To carry out the foregoing policy, the government shall
pursue the attainment of the following objectives:

(a) Revitalize Mango Industry — To restore the global competitiveness of the
Philippine mango industry by addressing production challenges and
strengthening its position in both domestic and international markets.

(b) Improve Farmer Livelihoods — To secure sustainable livelihoods for mango
farmers, particularly smallholders, through increased productivity, fair pricing,
and access to financing and support services.

(c) Promote Research and Modernization — To institutionalize scientific research,
field trials, and technology adoption aimed at pest management, disease
control, climate resilience, and compliance with international standards such
as MRL and SPS.

(d) Strengthen Industry Institutions — To establish and empower the Philippine
Mango Industry Development Board (PMIDB), the Mango Industry Planning
and Science Center (MIPSC), and the Federation of Mango Industry
Stakeholders Associations (FMISA) as the primary institutions for industry
governance, planning, and stakeholder representation.

(e) Enhance Market Competitiveness — To develop a national mango brand,
enforce quality standards, and promote Philippine mangoes globally through
coordinated marketing, branding, and export strategies.

(f) Ensure Farmer Protection — To provide crop insurance, orchard rehabilitation
programs, and emergency assistance for farmers affected by pest infestations,
diseases, natural calamities, or climate-induced production losses.

(g) Support Cooperative Development — To encourage cooperative formation and
cluster-based production systems, supported by incentives, technical
assistance, and financing mechanisms.

(h) Provide Infrastructure and Resources — To invest in post-harvest facilities,
cold storage, laboratory services, and other infrastructure necessary to reduce
costs, improve quality, and expand market access.

(i) Institutionalize Private Sector Participation — To ensure regular consultations
with industry stakeholders, thereby aligning government programs with the
needs and realities of mango producers and processors.

(j) Promote Sustainability and Long-Term Growth — To implement short-term,
medium-term, and long-term development plans that guarantee industry
sustainability, cost reduction, and continuous market expansion.

SEC. 6. Mandate and Functions of the Philippine Mango Industry
Development Board (PMIDB). - The Philippine Mango Industry Development Board
(PMIDB) shall have the following powers and functions:

(a) Formulate and implement an integrated mango industry development plan that
shall include export strategy, branding approach and global promotion;

(b) Establish and enforce national standards for mango quality, grading,
traceability, packaging, and branding to protect the integrity, reputation, and
market competitiveness of Philippine mango in domestic and international
markets;

(c) Institute a Farmgate Price Monitoring System that shall maintain a transparent
national mango farmgate price monitoring and market information system
accessible to all stakeholders. The Board shall likewise formulate guidelines to
deter unfair trading practices, price manipulation, and exploitative buying
arrangements prejudicial to mango producers;

(d) Encourage research, training, and technology adoption to improve production
and competitiveness;

(e) Boost both domestic and export competitiveness of Philippine mangoes;

(f) Implement specialized crop insurance products and emergency rehabilitation
assistance programs for mango farmers affected by pest infestations, disease
outbreaks, natural calamities, or climate-induced production losses;

(g) Promote Cooperative Formation and Cluster Development with incentive
mechanisms, technical assistance programs, and financing support for mango
farmer cooperatives, associations, and cluster-based production system; and

(h) Oversee coordination among government agencies and private stakeholders.

SEC. 15. Construction of Mango Farm Roads. - Consistent with the Mango
Roadmap and the Farm-to-Market Road Master Plan, the DA-BPI, in coordination
with the concerned local government units (LGUs), the DPWH, and the resident
mango producers, shall identify priority locations of roads linking the mango farms to
the market, which shall be known as Daan Manggahan. In the construction of the
Daan Manggahan, the concerned LGU and DA-BPI shall consider the investment cost
for mango production in the area, the number of mango producers and their families
who shall benefit from the venture, and the amount of mango produced or to be
potentially produced in the mango production areas. Thereafter, the LGU and DA-BPI
shall coordinate with the DA-Bureau of Agricultural and Fisheries Engineering in
collaboration with DPWH and NCIP should the project be in the ancestral domain for
the latter to undertake the construction, improvement, and maintenance of the Daan
Manggahan.

Farm-to-market roads in mango-producing areas, certified by the DPWH as
essential for farms under five (5) hectares, are exempt from land conversion
restrictions under agrarian reform laws to improve market access for small farmers.

SEC. 16. Network for Research and Promotion of Mango Farming
Methodologies and Techniques. - The DA-BPI, in close coordination with the SUCs,
DA-Bureau of Agricultural Research, and DA-Philippine Center for Postharvest

production of mango even under erratic weather patterns. It shall also encourage the
mechanization of mango production from pre- to post-harvest and develop
technologies that promote alternative methods and cost-effective techniques in mango
production.

The DA-BPI shall immediately transfer the results of their completed
study/technology/product to mango producers/manufacturers.

Additionally, the government will establish a health and medical program
specifically for farmers and their families, ensuring they have access to necessary
healthcare services and support.

The benefits provided by this Act shall not be considered in any manner as a
replacement for already existing benefits, or additional benefits to be added by the
private sector or the government.

SEC. 21. Good Agricultural Practices (GAP). - The DA shall promote the
adoption of GAP among mango farmers to ensure food safety, quality and
environmental sustainability. The GAP shall include:

(a) Proper use of fertilizers and pesticides;

(b) Effective pest and disease management;

(c) Compliance with Philippine national standards for mango grading and
quality; and

(d) Implementation of traceability systems to ensure food safety.

SEC. 22. Variety-Specific Guidelines. — The DA, together with the DTI, shall
develop specific guidelines for the production, handling, and marketing of different
mango varieties, taking into account their unique characteristics and market
requirements.

SEC. 23. Mango Farming and Mango Producing Cooperatives and
Associations. - The CDA shall assist in the formation of cooperatives of local mango

distribution of Philippine mangoes. It shall, likewise, assist in the organizational
strengthening, capacity building, and financial literacy of cooperatives and their
members so that they can leverage their own resources, ensure sustainable operations,
and increase the number of successful cooperative-managed mango farms in the

LGUs, through the Local Cooperatives Development Office, shall take the

cooperatives to enhance their viability as an economic enterprise and social
organization, including, training and education, business management, finance, and

SEC. 24. Trade and Export Assistance. - The DTI and the DA shall assist and
support local mango producers in the trade and exportation of their product through a
dedicated Mango Export Promotion Fund, the amount of which to be determined by
the Council and shall be sourced from the mango industry development trade defense
strategy such as against non-tariff barriers, and market intelligence system under DTI
and DA. It shall enhance the capabilities and global competitiveness of potential and
existing producers and exporters through export financing; business matching;
provision of trade and market information; promotion of locally produced mango to
local and international markets; organization of trade fairs and business missions;
conduct of seminars, workshops, conferences and symposiums on export-related
subjects, including export documentation and procedures; product design and
development; and market and product consultancy. The government shall give
preference to locally produced mangoes in government purchases.

SEC. 25. Role of Local Government Units (LGUs). — LGUs with mango
product, as far as practicable, shall establish their respective Mango Industry
Development Task Force (Mango Task Force), provide extension services, disease
quarantine enforcement, and co-financing of infrastructure with DA. LGUs shall
regularly conduct a survey of existing mango farms and mango processing enterprises
in their respective localities.

LGUs, in cooperation with the DENR, DA-BPI, and DTI and the appropriate
DOST Regional Office, shall identify areas for local mango production in their
respective localities.

SEC. 26. Exportation and Importation of Mango. - No person shall export or
import mango and mango planting materials without first securing a permit from the
following agencies through a single-window permit system for mango imports
managed by DA-BPI with DOH-FDA technical clearance:

(a) DA-BPI and DOH-FDA for food grade mango; and
(b) DA-BPI for non-food grade mango.

The DOH-FDA and DA-BPI are hereby mandated to harmonize and streamline
their processes.

SEC. 27. Preferential use of Domestically Produced Mango. - The
government shall encourage the use of domestically produced mango in its programs.

SEC. 28. Categorization of Domestically Produced Mango. - The mango
produced by mango farmers who are registered and in good standing with the BPI
shall be automatically categorized as food grade. The burden of categorizing mango
shall not be on the mango farmers and shall be the duty of the processors. For farms
that both produce and process their own mango, their obligation subsists.

Food grade mango may be sold by farmers to mango processors in compliance
with this Act; Provided, That FDA compliance shall be done by the processors.

SEC. 29. Labeling of Mango Made in the Philippines - All mangoes produced
or manufactured in the Philippines for export shall be labeled as "Product of the
Philippines" in a prominent and conspicuous manner on the product, its packaging,
and accompanying documentation, and shall comply with the requirements of
Republic Act No. 7394, otherwise known as the "Consumer Acct of the Philippines”.

For domestically produced mangoes intended for the local market, mango
manufacturers, producers or farmers are encouraged to provide a label "Product of the
Philippines".

The DTI and other relevant agencies shall assist domestic mango
manufacturers, producers or farmers on this labeling requirement.

SEC. 30. Incentives. - The following incentives shall be provided to investors
in mango farms development and mango processing facilities:

(a) Disease-resistant planting material subsidy;

(b) Farm Mechanization support from PhilMech;

(c) The Board of Investments shall classify mango farms as preferred areas of
investment under its Investment Priorities Plan subject to pertinent rules and
regulations;

(d) Mango farm owners, mango processors and other related businesses shall be
exempt from the payment of import duties for imported machines and
equipment to be actually and directly used in their businesses, subject to
pertinent rules and regulations;

(e) The mango producers and processors shall be given priority to access credit
assistance and guarantee schemes granted by GFIs, special lending programs
of the DA such as, the Agricultural Competitiveness Enhancement Fund
(ACEF), financing programs from Special Projects such as the Philippine
Rural Development Programme (PRDP), and Agriculture Fisheries and Rural
Development Financing System pursuant to RA No. 11901, also known as the
“Agriculture, Fisheries, and Rural Development Financing and Enhancement
Act of 2022”; and

(f) Mango farm development and their equipment shall be covered by the PCIC.

SEC. 31. Congressional Oversight. - The COCAFM shall regularly exercise
its oversight powers to ensure the full implementation of this Act.

SEC. 32. Agencies Regular Programs. - The additional mandates, functions
and activities of the various departments and agencies provided for and identified in
this Act shall be considered regular programs and shall be funded accordingly in their
annual budgets.

SEC. 33. Appropriations. -

(a) An annual appropriation of Eight Hundred Million Pesos (P800,000,000.00)
shall be allocated from the General Fund to fund the operations of the
Philippine Mango Industry Development Board (PMIDB) with an automatic
increase of ten percent (10%) annually after five (5) years from the time this
Act has been enacted. Other sources of funds, including but not limited to
borrowings from local and international financial institutions, shall also be
considered to further support the PMIDB

(b) At least forty percent (40%) of the annual appropriations under this Act shall
be earmarked for programs directly benefiting smallholder mango farmers,
including but not limited to, extension services, training, orchard
rehabilitation, planting materials, post-harvest facilities, and certification
assistance

(c) The Philippine Mango Industry Development Board (PMIDB) shall come up
with an annual budget plan to deliver the following:

(i) Programs of the Federation of Mango Industry Stakeholders (FMIS)
(ii) Incentives for mango growers and processors
(iii) Emergency funds for pest infestations and unforeseen setbacks.

SEC. 34. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The DA, in cooperation
and consultation with relevant government agencies and stakeholders, shall
promulgate the rules and regulations within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of
this Act.

SEC. 35. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the other provisions of this Act not affected by such declaration
shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 36. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations
or other issuance or parts inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 37. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
