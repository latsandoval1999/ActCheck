                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to establish the National Coordinating Council for
Education (NCCE), a body under the Office of the President, which shall
harmonize policies, plans, and investments across the Philippine education
sector. It shall strengthen coordination among the Department of Education
(DepEd), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Technical Education
and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and other key agencies to ensure
that education reforms are aligned with national development priorities and
labor market demands.

       The Philippine education system continues to face fragmented
governance, resulting in weak coordination and policy consistencies. The Second
Congressional Commission on Education pointed out that the historical
“trifocalization” of the Philippine education system, which split DepEd, CHED,
and TESDA has led to fragmented planning and a lack of accountability. This
resulting absence of a central coordinating mechanism has contributed to
persistent learning gaps, skills mismatch, and inefficiencies in education
planning and resource allocation.1

       This measure establishes the NCCE as the government’s principal
coordinating body for education and workforce development. It integrates
aligning curricula with labor market needs, strengthening data-driven
policymaking national education policies and aligns curricula with labor market
needs.

      Education is the cornerstone of national development. Ensuring effective
coordination across all levels of the education sector is indispensable to
producing globally competitive, highly skilled, and future-ready Filipinos. This

                         AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL COORDINATING COUNCIL FOR
EDUCATION, DEFINING ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS, AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National
Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE) Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the rights of all citizens to accessible quality education at all
levels and to relevant and efficient technical education and skills development.
For this purpose, the State shall establish a coordinating body which shall
integrate, coordinate, and interface the programs and policies of the formal and
non–formal educational systems, that is responsive to national development
goals and the evolving labor market.

       SEC. 3. National Coordinating Council for Education. – There is hereby
created a National Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE), hereinafter
referred to as the Council, under the Office of the President. It shall serve as the
primary inter–agency mechanism for directing, harmonizing, and monitoring
education and workforce development reforms across the Executive Branch.

      SEC. 4. Composition. – The President shall serve as the Chairperson of the
Council, consistent with the established practice in inter–agency bodies of
national significance. The President may designate a Cabinet Secretary as Vice
Chairperson to oversee operational coordination, with the following members:

          a. The Secretary of the Department of Education (DepEd);

       SEC. 5. Duties and Functions. – The Council shall exercise the following
duties and functions:

        a.​ Ensure the alignment of policies, plans, and budgets across DepEd,
      CHED, and TESDA to eliminate duplication and policy incoherence;​

         b.​ Oversee and monitor the implementation of priority reforms,
      including the recommendations of the Second Congressional Commission
      on Education (EDCOM II);​

         c.​ Formulate and periodically update a multi–year National Education
      and Workforce Development Plan to ensure that national education goals
      are aligned with the country’s economic priorities and are coherent with
      resource allocation; ​

         d.​ Formulate a comprehensive human resource plan for the education
      sector, covering both teaching and non–teaching personnel;​

         e.​ Mandate alignment with the Philippine Qualifications Framework
      to ensure “ladderized” pathways and credit transfers between vocational
      and higher education;​

         f.​ Mandate the creation of a Unified National Education Database to
      track learner progress and employment outcomes across all levels;​

         g.​ Strengthen the national labor market information to provide
      real–time data to educational institutions and to ensure that curriculum
      development and scholarship allocations are responsive to the industry
      needs;​

          h.​ Review and recommend the alignment of international grants,
      research funding, and scholarship programs to ensure they support the
      strategic priorities with the National Education Plan;​

         i.​ Propose priority legislative measures and executive policies through
      the Legislative–Executive Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) to
      sustain long–term educational reforms;​

         j.​ Conduct a skill gap analysis and facilitate public–private
      partnerships for internships, apprenticeships, and the integration of
      work–based learning into the curriculum; and​

         k.​ Conduct annual consultations on the ground–level effectiveness of
      reforms, ensuring that policies are evaluated based on student welfare,
      and mental health support.

      SEC. 6. Meetings. – Regular meetings of the Council shall be held at least
once a month. Special meetings may be held as often as may deemed necessary:
Provided, That in the absence or incapacity of a regular member to attend any
regular or special meeting of the Council, the duly designated representative of
the member shall attend and be vested with the same power and authority as a
regular member.

       A quorum of the Council shall consist of four (4) members holding office
at the time of the meeting: Provided, however, That the chairperson of the Council
or the designated vice–chairperson is among those present in the meeting.

      SEC. 7. Compensation. – The member of the Council shall be entitled to per
diems and honoraria as may be approved by the Council. They shall serve
without service credit, benefit, or pay, except the approved per diems and
honoraria.


                                        3
      SEC. 8. The Council Secretariat. – The Secretariat shall serve as the central
technical, analytical, and program management arm of the Council; and shall be
responsible for policy analysis, program coordination, monitoring and
evaluation, data gathering and integration, and the provision of strategic and
administrative support necessary for the effective discharge of the council’s
mandate. It shall be headed by an Executive Director with the rank of
Undersecretary and shall be appointed by the President to ensure effective
cross–agency coordination.

      SEC. 9. Appropriations. – The fund necessary to carry out the provisions of
this Act is hereby charged against the current appropriations of the Office of the
President. Thereafter, such amount as may be necessary to implement shall be
included in the budget proposal of the Office of the President in the annual
General Appropriations Act, subject to existing budgeting,accounting and
auditing rules and regulations.

      SEC. 10. Annual Report. – The Council shall submit an annual report to the
President and both Houses of Congress on the progress of the National
Education and Workforce Development Plan, including specific learning gains
and employment outcomes.

       SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within thirty (30) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Council shall convene to formulate the necessary
rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

       SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – Should any provision of this Act be
subsequently declared unconstitutional, the other provisions not so declared
shall remain in full force and effect.


      SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, ordinances, administrative
orders, rules and regulations, or any part thereof, which are inconsistent with this
Act are repealed or amended accordingly.


      SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
