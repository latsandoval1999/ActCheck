                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State recognizes the vital role of children in nation-building and
affirms its duty to protect and promote the rights and welfare of every child,
especially those who require specialized care, intervention, and support.
Children with special needs (CSN) often face barriers in accessing quality
healthcare, therapy, education, and social services, while their families bear
significant financial, emotional, and social burdens.

      Despite existing laws protecting persons with disabilities and promoting
inclusive education, many Filipino children with special needs continue to
experience inadequate access to early intervention programs, therapeutic
services, assistive devices, and inclusive learning opportunities, particularly in
underserved communities. Families likewise encounter high costs of treatment
and limited institutional support.

      This measure seeks to establish a comprehensive and inclusive framework
that will ensure accessible therapy and education, strengthen support systems
for families and caregivers, expand financial assistance and health coverage,
promote public awareness and acceptance, and reinforce inter-agency
coordination for the welfare of children with special needs.

      The bill likewise strengthens the participation of Local Government Units
(LGUs) in implementing community-based intervention programs and
establishes a national registry and data collection system, subject to the
provisions of the Data Privacy Act of 2012, to support evidence-based
policymaking and effective service delivery.

       Furthermore, this proposed measure is aligned with the principles and
policies embodied in existing laws such as the Magna Carta for Disabled Persons,
the Inclusive Education Act, and the Universal Health Care Act, ensuring a
harmonized and rights-based approach to inclusive development.
      Through this proposed legislation, the State endeavors to uphold the
dignity, inclusion, and full development of children with special needs and
empower their families toward a more compassionate, equitable, and inclusive
society.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            “AN ACT
      PROMOTING THE INCLUSIVE DEVELOPMENT AND WELL-BEING
      OF CHILDREN WITH SPECIAL NEEDS, PROVIDING FOR
      COMPREHENSIVE SUPPORT SYSTEMS, AND FOR OTHER
      PURPOSES”

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Inclusive
Development and Well-Being of Children with Special Needs Act.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of
the State to protect and promote the rights of children with special needs,
ensuring their full and inclusive development. The State recognizes that children
with special needs possess inherent dignity and the right to a quality of life that
enables them to achieve their fullest potential.

      To this end, the State shall:

      a.) Prioritize and integrate the needs of children with special needs into all
          relevant legislative efforts, policies, programs, and services;

      b.) Ensure access to quality, comprehensive, and appropriate intervention,
          therapeutic, and educational services;

      c.) Alleviate the financial and emotional burdens on families of children
          with special needs through various mechanisms;

      d.) Foster a society that embraces inclusivity, free from stigma and
          discrimination against children with special needs and their families;
          and
       e.) Strengthen inter-agency collaboration and establish a comprehensive
           support system for children with special needs and their families.

      This Act shall be implemented in a manner consistent with the policies
and principles embodied in the Magna Carta for Disabled Persons,1 the Inclusive
Education Act,2 and the Universal Health Care Act.3

       SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

       a.) Children with Special Needs (CSN) refer to persons below eighteen (18)
           years of age who have developmental delays, disabilities, or conditions
           that require specialized interventions, including but not limited to
           Autism Spectrum Disorder, Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder,
           Down Syndrome, Cerebral Palsy, intellectual disability, specific
           learning disabilities, sensory impairments (visual or hearing), and
           speech and language disorders.

       b.) Early Intervention Services refer to a range of services provided to
           infants and toddlers with developmental delays or disabilities and their
           families to promote development and minimize the effects of disability.

       c.) Therapeutic Services refer to professional interventions such as
           occupational therapy, physical therapy, speech and language therapy,
           behavioral therapy, and other related services.

       d.) Special Education (SPED) refers to educational programs designed to
           meet the unique needs of CSN, including SPED centers, inclusive
           classrooms, and home-based programs.

       e.) Underserved Areas refer to geographical regions where access to
           specialized services is limited or non-existent.

       f.) Assistive Tools and Medical Care refer to devices, equipment,
           technologies, medications, and healthcare services that assist CSN in
           their daily living, communication, mobility, and overall health
           management.

       SECTION 4. Accessible Therapy and Education. - The State shall
ensure that all CSN have equitable access to therapy and education. To this end,
the following shall be implemented:

     a.) Expansion of SPED Schools and Centers. – The Department of
         Education (DepEd), in coordination with the Department of Social
         Welfare and Development (DSWD) and the Department of Health
         (DOH), shall establish and expand SPED schools and centers in
         underserved areas, ensuring adequate facilities and qualified SPED
         teachers and therapists.

     b.) Subsidies for Therapy Services. – The DOH and DSWD, in coordination
         with the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), shall
         develop and implement a subsidy program for therapeutic services
         covering a significant portion of rehabilitation costs, prioritizing
         indigent and low-income families.

     c.) Inclusion in Regular Schools. – The DepEd shall strengthen its
         inclusion program, providing training for teachers and learning
         resources to integrate CSN into mainstream classrooms wherever
         appropriate.

     SECTION 5. Financial Relief for Families. - To mitigate financial
burdens on families of CSN, the following measures shall be undertaken:

     a.) Vouchers and Grants. – The DSWD shall establish vouchers and grants
         to support costs of therapy, assistive tools, and specialized education,
         prioritizing indigent families.

     b.) Tax Incentives. – The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) shall
         recommend tax incentives for parents or guardians of CSN, including
         deductions for therapy, medical care, and specialized educational fees.

     c.) PhilHealth Coverage Expansion. – PhilHealth shall expand coverage to
         include therapeutic services, diagnostic procedures, and assistive
         devices for CSN, with higher benefit limits and streamlined claims
         procedures.

      SECTION 6. Public Awareness and Acceptance Campaigns. - The State
shall promote public awareness and acceptance of CSN to combat stigma
through:

     a.) National Awareness Campaigns. – Led by DepEd, DOH, DSWD, and the
         Philippine Information Agency (PIA), focusing on early identification,
         empathy, acceptance, and inclusion.

     b.) Educational Curriculum Integration. – The DepEd shall integrate
         modules on inclusivity and disability rights in curricula.
       SECTION 7. Support Systems for Families and Caregivers. - The State
shall establish support systems for families and caregivers of CSN, including:

      a.) Community Centers for CSN. - The DSWD and Local Government Units
          (LGUs) shall establish or support community centers offering
          subsidized therapy, parent training, and emotional support groups.

      b.) Parental Training and Empowerment Programs. – The DSWD and DOH,
          in cooperation with the Technical Education and Skills Development
          Authority (TESDA), shall develop training for parents to equip them
          with skills to support child development.

      c.) National Registry and Data Collection System. - The Department of
          Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), in coordination with the
          Department of Health (DOH), Department of Education (DepEd),
          National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA), Philippine Statistics
          Authority (PSA), and Local Government Units (LGUs), shall establish
          and maintain a centralized database and registry of Children with
          Special Needs (CSN) for purposes of policy planning, service delivery,
          monitoring, and program development, subject to the provisions of the
          Data Privacy Act of 2012.

      d.) Local Government Participation. –Local Government Units (LGUs) shall
          formulate and implement local programs and services for CSN and their
          families, including the establishment of community-based early
          intervention programs, therapy centers, inclusive recreational
          activities, and referral systems, subject to the availability of local funds
          and in coordination with relevant national government agencies.

      SECTION 8. Stronger Policies for Inclusion. - The State shall ensure full
inclusion of CSN in all aspects of society by:

      a.) Inclusion in Schools. – The DepEd shall mandate inclusive education
          guidelines and anti-discrimination practices.

      b.) Accessibility in Public Spaces. -     All government and private
          establishments must comply with the accessibility laws.


      SECTION 9. Inter-Agency Coordinating Council. - There is hereby
created an Inter-Agency Coordinating Council for Children with Special Needs
(IACCSN), composed of representatives from the DSWD, DOH, DepEd, National
Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA), PhilHealth, and two (2) private sector
representatives. The Council shall:
      a.) Formulate and oversee a comprehensive national plan for CSN welfare;

      b.) Coordinate     programs    of   government      and    non-government
          organizations;

      c.) Evaluate program effectiveness and recommend improvements; and

      d.) Recommend legislative and policy reforms.

       SECTION 10. Appropriations. - The amount necessary to carry out the
provisions of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act. The implementing agencies may likewise utilize available funds from existing
programs and appropriations related to health, education, social welfare, and
disability support, subject to existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws,
rules, and regulations.

      SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, in coordination with the
DOH, DepEd, NCDA, PhilHealth and in consultation with other relevant
agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

     SECTION 12. Separability Clause. - If any part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain valid.

      SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, rules and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

      SECTION 14. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.

      Approved.
