                             AN ACT
 EXCLUDING SYSTEM LOSS FROM VALUE-ADDED TAXES (VAT) IN THE SALE
      OF ELECTRICITY TO RESIDENTIAL HOUSEHOLD CONSUMERS

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Every month, Filipino families open their electricity bills and pay for power they
might not even use. Buried within the sum billed for “kilowatt-hours consumed” is a
charge for electricity that was lost in the wires — dissipated as heat, siphoned off
through pilferage, or otherwise unaccounted for before it ever reached a household
meter. This is the System Loss Charge, and under current practice, it is taxed as
though it were a service rendered to the consumer.

        The Value-Added Tax (VAT) is, by design, a tax on consumption. Sections 105
to 108 of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC) of 1997, as amended, impose
VAT on the sale, barter, or exchange of goods and services actually rendered or
delivered. On the other hand, system loss, by definition, is the difference between the
electricity entering a power network and the amount eventually measured and billed
to customers1. In other words, it is electricity generated, purchased, or transmitted but
never delivered to the end-user. Therefore, to impose VAT on this component is to tax
a transaction that, from the standpoint of the residential household consumer, never
took place.

        Thus, this proposed measure.

     This bill seeks to exempt the system loss component charged to residential
household consumers from the value-added tax by amending Article 109 of the NIRC,

as amended. In addition, to ensure that the benefit inures to the households for whose
protection this measure is introduced, this bill directs the Energy Regulatory

       By removing the tax burden on electricity which never reaches the Filipino
household, this measure restores basic fairness to utility billing. This very injustice is
what this bill seeks to rectify.
