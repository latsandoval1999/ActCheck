                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) have long been recognized as
indispensable partners in the delivery of primary health care (PHC). They play a
crucial role in identifying at-risk individuals, providing culturally appropriate
interventions, and bridging the gap between communities and formal health
institutions. In many areas where medical professionals are scarce, BHWs stand
at the frontline of health promotion, disease prevention, and referral, making them
vital contributors to the overall health system.

       The Philippines was among the pioneering countries that embraced the
Alma-Ata Declaration on Primary Health Care in 1979, which paved the way for
the institutionalization of the BHW program in 1981. This commitment was
further reinforced through Republic Act No. 7883, otherwise known as the
“Barangay Health Workers’ Benefits and Incentives Act of 1995,” and more
recently through the Universal Health Care Act of 2019. Despite these policy
milestones, however, the full potential of BHWs has not yet been maximized.
          Current challenges include the absence of standardized, continuing
   education and certification programs, limited opportunities for professional
   growth, and a lack of accreditation and employment protection mechanisms.
   While BHWs are at the frontline of service delivery, they often remain
   undertrained, underrecognized, and under-resourced, thereby undermining their
   ability to contribute effectively to health system resilience.

         This bill, therefore, seeks to establish a formal institution—through
   regional academies—that will provide systematic and standardized continuing
   education, training, and certification of BHWs. By institutionalizing these
   mechanisms, the measure aims to:

      1. Upgrade the skills and competencies of BHWs through structured training
         and capacity-building programs.
      2. Ensure their accreditation and accountability as community health
         partners;
      3. Strengthen their employment protection and entitlement to appropriate
         benefits; and
      4. Secure sustainable funding support for their professional development and
         service delivery.

         By empowering BHWs through education, certification, and institutional
   support, this measure advances the objectives of the State to make essential health
   care and social services accessible to all, consistent with the mandates of the
   Constitution, the Alma-Ata Declaration, and the Universal Health Care Act.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
   sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Barangay Health
Workers Academy Act.”


SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right to health of people and adopt an integrated
and comprehensive approach to health development, and pursuant thereto, it shall
endeavor to make essential goods, healthcare and other social services available
to all the people. In furtherance of this, the State shall uphold its duty to enhance
the competence and capacity of Barangay Health Workers (BHWs), who serve as
the primary health care frontliners at the community level, through
institutionalized education, training, and continuing professional development.


SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act, the following
definitions shall apply:
          a. Academy refers to the Barangay Health Workers Academy
             established under this Act, which shall serve as the lead institution
             for training, certification, continuing education, and capacity
             development of BHWs.
          b. Capacity-Building refers to the process of developing and
             enhancing the knowledge, skills, attitudes, and competencies of
             Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) through education, training,
             mentoring, and other learning interventions to enable them to
             effectively perform their roles in primary health care delivery.
          c. Certification refers to the formal recognition issued by a duly
             authorized government agency or institution, attesting that a BHW
             has successfully met the prescribed standards, qualifications, and
             competencies required for the performance of barangay health
             services.
          d. Continuing Education refers to structured and systematic learning
             activities beyond the initial training of BHWs, designed to update,
             maintain, and improve their professional knowledge and skills, in
             response to emerging health needs, policies, and technologies.


SECTION 4. Coverage. – The provisions of this Act shall apply to all Barangay
Health Workers (BHWs), whether accredited or non-accredited, as defined under
Republic Act No. 7883, otherwise known as the ‘Barangay Health Workers’
Benefits and Incentives Act of 1995. For the purposes of this Act, the term
barangay health worker shall refer to a person who has undergone a training
program under any accredited government or non-government organization
primarily to render health care services.


SECTION 5. Establishment of the Barangay Health Workers Academy. – This
Act hereby establishes, under the administration and supervision of the
Department of Health (DOH) and implementing agencies such as TESDA,
CHED, SUCs and LGU, a system of Barangay Health Workers Academies, to be
initially organized on a regional basis, and progressively expanded to provincial,
city, and municipal levels subject to availability of funds. It serves as the lead
institution for the certification, continuing education, and leadership training of
Barangay Health Workers.


      The institution shall coincide with the accreditation process of BHWs as a
health workforce in accordance with Republic Act No. 7883 or otherwise known
as the “Barangay Health Workers’ Benefits and Incentives Act of 1995”.


      The Academy shall also be a component unit of the DOH and be headed
by the Executive Director: Provided, That it shall be appointed by the Secretary
of the Department of Health (DOH). Subsequently, organizational structure,
number of positions and staff qualification, function and compensation shall be
determined by the Department of Health (DOH), Civil Service Commission
(CSC) and the Department of Budget and Management (DBM).


SECTION 6. Powers and Function of the Academy. – For continuing skill
development of in-service Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) in the community,
the Academy shall be mandated to perform the following functions;
          a. Develop standardized certification pathways for Barangay Health
             Workers (BHWs);
          b. Administer continuing education and re-skilling programs among
            BHW, responsive to emerging health needs and trends in the
            community;
         c. Determine mandatory schedule for capacity-building of BHWs per
            municipality or city: Provided, that such schedule shall ensure that
            all barangays are covered and that all BHWs are afforded equitable
            access to said programs.
         d. Conduct leadership and management training to prepare BHWs for
            supervisory and community health leadership roles;
         e. Serve as the national repository of training modules, competency
            standards, and capacity development resources for BHWs;
         f. Collaborate with Barangay Council, Municipal/City Health Office
            and Local Health Board on the accreditation, benefits and incentives
            of Barangay Health Workers (BHWs);
         g. Establish linkages and partnerships with government agencies,
            academic institutions, civil society organizations, and international
            partners for training and resource sharing;
         h. Manage the budget allotted from the DOH’s appropriated fund,
            which shall be necessary to carry out its objective, function,
            operation, improvement, programs and activities;
         i. Promulgate rules and regulations and exercise such other powers and
            functions as may be necessary to effectively carry out the purpose
            and objectives of this Act.


SECTION 7. Governing Board. – The Department of Health shall constitute a
Board of Trustees and determine its composition and function. The Board shall
formulate policies, approve the curriculum and certification standards, oversee
program implementation, and exercise such powers as may be necessary and
incidental to the attainment of the purposes of this Act; Provided, That its
membership shall include representatives from the concerned national
implementing agencies, local accreditation bodies such as the Barangay Council,
Municipal/City Health Office, and Local Health Board, as well as duly elected
representatives of the BHW sector, with at least one representative from each
province, highly urbanized city, and independent component city.


SECTION 8. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Department of Health. Thereafter, the necessary sums for its continued
operation and maintenance shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA) under the budget of the DOH.


SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulation (IRR). – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with the DBM,
CSC, TESDA, LGUs, and other concerned agencies and stakeholders, shall
promulgate the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) necessary to carry out
the provisions of this Act.


SECTION 10. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision of
this Act shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, other parts of provisions thereof
not affected shall thereby continue to be in full force and effect.


SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof contrary
to or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


SECTION 12. Effectivity. - This Act shall take fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
