                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The transition from education to employment remains one of the most critical
yet underserved stages in the development of Filipino youth. Many graduates and
early-career professionals face significant barriers in accessing guidance,
professional networks, and industry exposure necessary for successful career entry.

      While various government agencies, educational institutions, and private
organizations implement mentorship initiatives, these programs remain fragmented,
unevenly distributed, and largely inaccessible to youth outside urban centers or elite
academic institutions. There is currently no unified national framework that ensures
equitable, structured, and measurable access to early-career mentorship
opportunities.

      This bill seeks to institutionalize the Early Career Mentorship Accessibility
Act by establishing a national framework that integrates mentorship programs across
government agencies, educational institutions, and private sector partners. It shall

 promote the use of digital platforms to ensure accessibility, scalability, and
 inclusivity, particularly for geographically isolated, disadvantaged, and

        By formalizing mentorship pathways, standardizing program implementation,
 and strengthening public-private collaboration, this measure aims to bridge the gap
 between education and employment, enhance youth employability, and support the
 development of a competent, competitive, and future-ready Filipino workforce.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Early Career
Mentorship Accessibility Act”

      SEC 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to promote inclusive, equitable, and accessible opportunities for youth development
and employment readiness. The State recognizes mentorship as a critical component




                                                                                 1
of human capital development and as a bridge between education and meaningful
employment.

       Towards this end, the State shall institutionalize a national framework for
early-career mentorship that is structured, accessible, technology-enabled, and
outcome-oriented. It shall ensure that all Filipino youth, regardless of geographic
location, socioeconomic status, or institutional affiliation, are provided with equal
access to mentorship opportunities that enhance career readiness, skills
development, and professional growth. The State shall likewise promote multi-
sectoral collaboration among government agencies, educational institutions, and
private sector partners to ensure the sustainability and effectiveness of mentorship
programs nationwide.

      SEC 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:
      (a) Early-Career Individuals refers to students, graduates, and young
      professionals within five (5) years from completion of tertiary education or
      entry into the workforce;
      (b) Mentorship refers to a structured developmental relationship in which
      experienced professionals provide guidance, support, and knowledge to early-
      career individuals;
      (c) Mentor refers to a qualified professional, practitioner, or expert who
      provides guidance under this program;
      (d) Mentee refers to an early-career individual who participates in the
      mentorship program;
      (e) Mentorship Platform refers to a digital or hybrid system used to facilitate
      mentorship matching, engagement, monitoring, and evaluation;

     SEC 4. Establishment of the National Early Career Mentorship Program
— There is hereby established a National Early Career Mentorship Program
(NECMP), which shall serve as the primary framework for implementing structured
mentorship initiatives for Filipino youth.

The program shall be led by the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), in
coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher


                                                                                   2
Education (CHED), Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA), Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT),
Civil Service Commission (CSC), and relevant private sector partners.

      The NECMP shall establish standardized mentorship guidelines, accreditation
systems for mentors, and a national mentorship database to ensure quality,
consistency, and accessibility of services.

      SEC 5. Objectives — The National Early Career Mentorship Program shall
have the following objectives:

          a) To bridge the gap between education and employment through
             structured mentorship support;
          b) To enhance career readiness, employability, and professional
             development of Filipino youth;
          c) To institutionalize accessible, inclusive, and scalable mentorship
             opportunities across sectors;
          d) To promote industry-academia-government collaboration in workforce
             development;
          e) To provide equitable access to mentorship through digital and
             community-based platforms;
          f) To develop a sustainable national system for mentor recruitment,
             training, and evaluation;
          g) To support regional and sector-specific career guidance aligned with
             national development priorities.


      SEC 6. National Mentorship Framework and Platform — A centralized
National Mentorship Framework and Digital Platform shall be developed and
maintained by the DICT, in coordination with DOLE and partner agencies.

      The platform shall feature a comprehensive mentor-mentee matching system
designed to pair individuals based on their specific skills, interests, and professional
career goals. To facilitate these connections, the system shall support both virtual


                                                                                      3
and hybrid mentorship sessions, complemented by integrated tools for tracking and
monitoring engagement levels. Furthermore, the platform shall provide users with
access to extensive resource libraries and career development tools, while
incorporating robust feedback and evaluation mechanisms to ensure the continuous
improvement and effectiveness of the mentorship experience.

       The platform shall be designed to be accessible, user-friendly, and inclusive,
particularly for users in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

       SEC 7. Integration with Educational Institutions — All public and private
educational institutions shall be encouraged to integrate mentorship programs into
their career guidance, internship, or student development frameworks. CHED,
TESDA, and DepEd shall ensure that mentorship opportunities are embedded in
existing curricula or co-curricular programs, including internship programs,
practicum requirements, and career orientation activities. Institutions shall designate
focal persons to coordinate mentorship activities and ensure student participation in
the program.

      SEC 8. Private Sector and Government Participation — The State shall
encourage active participation of private corporations, professional associations,
non-government organizations, and government agencies in providing mentorship
opportunities.

       Participating entities may register as accredited mentorship providers and
shall be recognized for their contribution to workforce development. Government
agencies shall likewise designate personnel to serve as mentors, subject to capacity
and relevance to their expertise.

       SEC 9. Mentor Accreditation and Standards — The NECMP shall establish
a national accreditation system for mentors to ensure quality and professionalism.
Accredited mentors shall undergo orientation or training on mentorship principles,
ethical standards, communication, and youth engagement. Only accredited mentors
shall be allowed to formally participate in the national mentorship platform.



                                                                                     4
      SEC 10. Inclusion and Accessibility Measures — The program shall ensure
equitable access to mentorship opportunities by prioritizing:

   a) Youth in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs);
   b) Low-income and marginalized communities;
   c) Persons with disabilities;
   d) Out-of-school youth and alternative learning system graduates.

     Offline, hybrid, and community-based mentorship modalities shall be
implemented to complement digital platforms.


       SEC 11. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting — A comprehensive
monitoring and evaluation system shall be established to ensure program
effectiveness.

      Key indicators shall include:
        a) Number of registered mentors and mentees;
        b) Frequency and duration of mentorship engagements;
        c) Employment or career progression outcomes of mentees;
        d) Geographic and sectoral coverage;
        e) Program satisfaction and feedback ratings.

      Annual reports shall be submitted by DOLE to Congress and made publicly
accessible.

       SEC 12. Funding – The initial funding for the implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the appropriations of the DOLE and other participating
agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the General
Appropriations Act.

      Public-private partnerships and international development assistance may
also be mobilized to support program implementation.
