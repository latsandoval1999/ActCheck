                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

Traveling should never be a privilege that only a few can afford. Yet, for many
ordinary Filipino families and everyday travelers, the travel tax imposed under
current legislation makes even domestic and regional travel unnecessarily
expensive. This financial burden limits Filipinos’ ability to visit loved ones, pursue
education and employment opportunities, and engage in cultural exchange, despite
constitutional guarantees of the right to travel.

Tourism is a proven driver of inclusive economic growth, and employment. In 2024,
the tourism sector contributed approximately 8.9 % of the Philippines’ gross
domestic product (GDP), equivalent to a direct gross value added of ₱2.35 trillion,
and supported an estimated 6.75 million jobs, or about 13.8 percent of total
employment in the country. Domestic tourism expenditure also grew significantly,
totaling over ₱3.16 trillion, reflecting strong internal demand and the sector’s
potential to sustain broad‑based economic activity across regions.1

While the travel tax generates revenue, its abolition must be viewed in light of the
broader economic gains from increased tourism activity, consumption, and job
creation.

Every extra cost added to travel makes fewer people able to afford it, especially
ordinary Filipino families who carefully budget their expenses. When travel
becomes even slightly cheaper, more people are encouraged to take trips, visit
provinces, and explore new places. Removing the travel tax can lead to more
passengers, more visitors to local destinations, and more income for hotels,
restaurants, transport services, and small businesses that depend on tourism.

Recognizing these challenges, the proposed “Abolition of the Travel Tax Act” seeks
to repeal the travel tax, making travel more accessible, equitable, and inclusive. This
legislation will help dismantle systemic barriers to mobility and advance broader
socio-economic inclusion. By lowering the cost of travel, families can allocate
resources toward education, health, and livelihood, while ordinary citizens can
participate fully in social, cultural, and economic life.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                  AN ACT
                         ABOLISHING THE TRAVEL TAX

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Abolition of the Travel
Tax Act”.

       SEC. 2. Repeal. – Provisions of laws relating to the authority to collect or
receive funding from the collection of Travel Tax, including but not limited to,
Republic Act (RA) No. 1478, entitled “An Act to Create a Board of Travel and Tourist
Industry, Defining its Powers, Duties and Responsibilities, and for Other Purposes”, as
amended; RA No. 7722, otherwise known as the “Higher Education Act of 1994”; and
RA No. 9593, otherwise known as “The Tourism Act of 2009”, are hereby repealed.

        SEC. 3. Effect of Repeal. – The collection of travel taxes will be prohibited
after the effectivity of this Act. For flights that are scheduled on or after the
effectivity of this Act, the travel taxes paid shall be refunded immediately.

       SEC. 4. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the programs funded by
the travel tax collections and for the implementation of this Act shall be charged to
the respective appropriations under the current General Appropriations Act (GAA)
of the Department of Tourism for the Tourism Infrastructure and Enterprise Zone
Authority (TIEZA), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) for the Higher
Education Development Fund, and the National Commission for Culture and the
Arts (NCCA) for the National Endowment Fund for Culture and the Arts.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual GAA.

       SEC. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from
the approval of this Act, the TIEZA shall formulate the implementing rules and
regulations in connection with the immediate refund provided in Section 3 hereof.

       SEC. 6. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
subsequently declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which are
not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 7. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
