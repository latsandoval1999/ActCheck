                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

Food waste is a major socio-economic and environmental issue that impacts national food
security, economic efficiency, and environmental sustainability. According to estimates from
the World Wide Fund for Nature (WWF) Philippines and the Department of Agriculture,
thousands of tons of edible food are wasted daily in the country, even as millions of Filipino
families experience involuntary hunger and nutritional deficits.

While supply chain inefficiencies and retail practices contribute significantly to food loss,
consumer behavior—particularly wasteful consumption patterns—plays a critical role in
generating preventable food waste. Establishing lifelong habits of mindful consumption, respect
for food resources, and awareness of nutritional value must begin at an early age.

Schools serve as the primary institutional setting for shaping the values, social consciousness,
and daily behaviors of young citizens. By integrating comprehensive, age-appropriate food
waste reduction, zero-waste principles, and responsible consumption education into the basic
education curriculum, the State can foster a culture of sustainability among future generations.

This bill seeks to institutionalize food waste reduction and prevention education across all public
and private basic education institutions in the Philippines. It mandates the Department of
Education (DepEd), in coordination with relevant agencies, to incorporate practical food
management skills, zero-waste concepts, and agricultural literacy into existing curricular
frameworks. Furthermore, it encourages schools to establish zero-waste canteen management
guidelines, composting initiatives, and school garden programs.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING FOOD WASTE REDUCTION AND PREVENTION
EDUCATION IN ALL PUBLIC AND PRIVATE BASIC EDUCATION
INSTITUTIONS, PROMOTING RESPONSIBLE FOOD CONSUMPTION AMONG
LEARNERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. This Act shall be known as the "Menu Labeling and Nutritional Transparency
Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and promote the right
to health of the people, instil health consciousness, and safeguard consumers from health risks
associated with poor nutrition. To this end, the State shall ensure that consumers have access
to accurate, transparent, and visible nutritional information when purchasing food prepared by
food service establishments.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall mean:

   1. Covered Food Establishment — Any retail establishment, restaurant, fast-food chain,
      bakery, cafeteria, or drive-thru facility operating under a common name or business
      model with five (5) or more branches or locations nationwide that sells food prepared
      for immediate consumption.
   2. Standard Menu Item — Any food or beverage item offered for sale that appears on a
      menu, menu board, or electronic display for more than sixty (60) days in a calendar
      year.
   3. Nutritional Information — Data specifying the caloric value and nutrient content of a
      standard menu item, including total calories, saturated fat, sodium, total carbohydrates,
      and total sugars per standard serving size.
   4. Menu or Menu Board — Any written, printed, or electronic display used by a food
      establishment to list food and beverage items available for purchase by consumers,
      including drive-thru displays, online ordering platforms, and mobile applications.

SEC. 4. Mandatory Display of Nutritional Information. —
   1. All covered food establishments shall display adjacent to the name or price of every
      standard menu item on all menus and menu boards:
          o The total number of calories per standard serving size; and
          o A clear reference statement indicating the recommended daily caloric intake for
             an average adult (e.g., "A 2,000 calorie daily intake is used for general
             nutrition advice, but calorie needs vary").
   2. Covered food establishments shall maintain written nutritional documentation
      containing a complete breakdown of total calories, saturated fat, sodium, total
      carbohydrates, and total sugars for all standard menu items. This information shall be
      made immediately available to customers upon request.

SEC. 5. Exemptions. — The following items shall be exempt from the requirements of this
Act:

   1. Temporary or daily specials offered for fewer than sixty (60) consecutive days per
      calendar year;
   2. Custom or special orders made to individual consumer specifications;
   3. Condiments and self-serve items placed on tables or counters for general use; and
   4. Test market items offered for less than ninety (90) days.

SEC. 6. Implementation and Regulatory Authority. — The Department of Health (DOH), in
coordination with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and the Food and Drug
Administration (FDA), shall serve as the primary implementing agency. The DOH shall:

   1. Prescribe the standard size, font, and visibility guidelines for displaying calorie and
      nutritional information;
   2. Issue guidelines for acceptable methods of nutritional analysis, including laboratory
      testing or calculation using standardized nutrient databases; and
   3. Conduct periodic compliance checks and inspections.

SEC. 7. Penalties. — Any covered food establishment found violating any provision of this
Act or its Implementing Rules and Regulations shall be subject to the following penalties:

   1. First Offense: A administrative fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00) and a
      written warning.
   2. Second Offense: An administrative fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos
      (₱100,000.00) and suspension of the establishment's health clearance for a period not
      exceeding thirty (30) days.
   3. Third and Subsequent Offenses: An administrative fine of Two Hundred Fifty
      Thousand Pesos (₱250,000.00) and revocation of the business permit of the offending
      branch or location.

SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with the DTI, FDA, National Nutrition
Council (NNC), and industry stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations
necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 9. Grace Period. — All covered food establishments shall be given a transition period
of one (1) year from the publication of the Implementing Rules and Regulations to fully
comply with the provisions of this Act.
SEC. 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining parts or provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, rules, and regulations inconsistent
with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

SEC. 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
