                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Prison is meant not only to punish, but also to help people change. Once a person has
served their sentence or has been legally released, they should be given a real chance
to start over, find work, and live honestly. However, many former prisoners struggle
to get jobs because of stigma, lack of connections, and limited opportunities. Without
support, these challenges can push them back into crime and make it harder for them
to reintegrate into society.

The government already recognizes that helping former prisoners rebuild their lives
is important for both public safety and the common good. One existing tool is the
Public Employment Service Office (PESO), which helps people find jobs, provides
training, and offers career guidance. By strengthening and using this system, the
government can directly address the barriers that former prisoners face when
looking for employment.

This bill proposes a Former Prisoners’ Employment Program that will connect
qualified individuals to job opportunities through PESO. It will offer job matching,
skills training, and support to help them become employable. At the same time, the
bill encourages businesses to give former prisoners a fair chance by providing
incentives to employers who hire and retain them, while still ensuring that labor
standards are followed.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Former Prisoners’
Employment Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote the
reformation, social reintegration, and productive employment of persons released
from detention or imprisonment, and to strengthen coordination among government
agencies, local government units, and the private sector to reduce recidivism and
support public safety.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   (a) “Former prisoner” refer to any person who was convicted by final
       judgment and has been released from confinement by reason of service of
       sentence, probation, parole, pardon, or other lawful mode of release, including
       those released from preventive imprisonment who are no longer detained by
       lawful order.

   (b) “Employer” refers to any private business establishment, partnership,
       corporation, cooperative, or sole proprietorship duly registered in the

      Philippines, including government-owned and -controlled corporations
      (GOCCs) when applicable, that hires a Former Prisoner pursuant to this Act.

   (c) “Program” refers to the Former Prisoners’ Employment Program
       (FPEP) instituted under this Act.

   (d) “PESO” refers to the Public Employment Service Office established and
       maintained by local government units and linked with the Department of
       Labor and Employment (DOLE).

SECTION 4. Former Prisoners’ Employment Program (FPEP). — There is hereby
instituted a Former Prisoners’ Employment Program (FPEP), which shall include:

   (a) Employment facilitation and job matching through PESOs and other DOLE-
       recognized employment service channels;
   (b) Pre-employment readiness and life skills training, including workplace
       values, financial literacy, and interview skills;
   (c) Skills assessment, certification, or referral to training in coordination
       with concerned agencies; and
   (d) Employer engagement, including orientation on fair chance hiring and
       workplace support mechanisms.

SECTION 5. Lead Agencies; Inter-Agency Coordination. —

   (a) The DOLE shall be the lead implementing agency for employment facilitation,
       job matching, employer engagement, and reporting, consistent with its
       administration of employment facilitation programs.

   (b) The Department of Justice (DOJ), through its relevant offices and in
       coordination with correctional authorities, shall provide pre-release referral,
       maintain coordination for documentation support, and assist in developing
       reintegration protocols.

   (c) The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall support
       coordination with LGUs for PESO participation and local reintegration
       initiatives.

   (d) LGUs shall ensure that PESOs under their jurisdiction provide accessible
       referral and facilitation services for Former Prisoners, consistent with PESO
       operational standards.

       This approach aligns the Program with the existing statutory PESO network
       administered by DOLE and maintained by LGUs.

SECTION 6. Employer Incentives (Tax Treatment). —

   (a) Incentive. Employers hiring a Former Prisoner under the Program and
       retaining such employee for at least six (6) months shall be entitled to
       an additional deduction equivalent to fifteen percent (15%) of the total
       salaries and wages actually paid to such Former Prisoner during the taxable
       year, subject to the limitations and substantiation requirements under
       the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC), as amended, and the
       implementing rules to be jointly issued by the Department of Finance (DOF)
       and the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), in coordination with DOLE and DOJ.

   (b) Anti-abuse and documentation. The claim of incentive shall require: (i) proof
       of employment and payment of wages; (ii) proof of the employee’s status as a
       Former Prisoner; and (iii) compliance with labor standards and non-
       discrimination requirements under existing laws.

SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOLE and DOJ, in coordination with
the DILG, DOF/BIR (for the tax incentive), and other relevant stakeholders, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

SECTION 8. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary to implement this Act shall
be charged against the current appropriations of the agencies concerned. Thereafter,
such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA).

An initial amount of One Hundred Million Pesos (Php100,000,000.00) is hereby
authorized to be appropriated for the first year of implementation.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, other provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
