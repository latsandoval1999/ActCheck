                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


      The rapid proliferation of vaporized nicotine and non-nicotine products,
commonly known as electronic cigarettes or vapes, poses a significant public health
concern, particularly among the youth. Marketed as safer alternatives to
conventional tobacco products, these devices in reality expose users to more
harmful substances, including toxic irritants, heavy metals, and carcinogenic
compounds. Such exposures are associated to cardiovascular disease, neurological
impairments, nicotine dependence, and respiratory injury, including E-cigarette or
Vaping Use-associated Lung Injury (EVALI), an emerging condition not observed in
conventional tobacco use, which recorded its first Filipino fatality in 2024.


      Considering the associated major health risks, youth uptake remains
particularly alarming. Flavored products, “toy-like” devices, and the expansion of
online sales have lowered the barrier to youth access, facilitating early nicotine
initiation and increase the risk of long-term addiction.


      Thus, the Department of Health (DOH) is strongly pushing for a ban on
vapes and e-cigarettes, citing severe health risks like EVALI, nicotine
addiction, and increased usage among youth. The DOH argues these products
pose safety hazards and urges lawmakers to restrict access, treating them as
dangerous, not as safer alternatives to smoking. Incidents of devices
exploding, such as one involving a 17-year-old boy, have increased urgency
for a ban. The DOH advises parents and guardians to be vigilant against youth
accessing these products, noting that 20% of users are in the 15-20 age
group, says a Facebook post by CTFK Global.
      Despite the enactment of Republic Act No. 11900, otherwise known as the
“Vaporized Nicotine and Non-Nicotine Products Regulation Act,” enforcement
challenges persist. Regulatory safeguards have been undermined by market
availability to minors and persistence of illicit trade. As a result, the current
framework has failed to curb youth access, prevent unregulated distribution, or
mitigate growing public health burden associated with vaping.


      This measure is anchored on Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution,
which mandates the State to protect and promote the right to health of the people
and instill health consciousness among them. In furtherance of this mandate, the
State is empowered to exercise its police power to enact measures necessary to
safeguard public health, subject to the requirements of reasonableness and public
safety. Likewise, this proposed bill is consistent with the country’s obligations
under the World Health Organization Framework Convention on Tobacco Control
(WHO-FCTC), which calls State Parties to adopt effective measures to reduce
tobacco use, including nicotine dependence.


      Given the demonstrated harms associated with vapor products, supported
by the recognition that no regulatory framework will be sufficient to prevent youth
access, curb illicit trade, and mitigate health risks and address the demonstrated
harms of exposure to vapor products, the adoption of a comprehensive prohibition
constitutes a proportionate and necessary response to protect public health.


      Toward this end, this measure seeks to prohibit the manufacture,
importation, sale, distribution, and use of vaporized nicotine and non-nicotine
products and repeal RA No. 11900 to replace this regulatory regime with a
prohibition model.


      In light of the growing scientific evidence on the harms of vapor products
and the demonstrated limitations of the existing regulatory framework, the shift
toward prohibition is justified, compelling, and necessary.


      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
PROHIBITING THE MANUFACTURE, IMPORTATION, SALE, DISTRIBUTION,
AND USE OF VAPORIZED NICOTINE AND NON-NICOTINE PRODUCTS,
PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES, THEREBY REPEALING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11900

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in the Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Vapor Products
Public Health Protection Act.”


      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State, pursuant to Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution, to protect and
promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among
them. In the valid exercise of the State’s police power, subject to the test of
reasonableness and public necessity, the State recognizes that vapor products pose
serious and deleterious risks to public health, including respiratory injury,
cardiovascular diseases, neurological harm, and nicotine dependence, particularly
among children and the youth.


      The State further recognizes that the increasing prevalence of vapor product
use, coupled with the widespread availability of flavored and technology-driven
vapor products, including online distribution channels, facilitates youth initiation,
thereby constituting a public health concern of national significance.


      Finally, the State ultimately recognizes that no regulatory framework,
including the existing regulatory mechanisms provided under Republic Act No.
11900, will be sufficient to prevent youth access, curb illicit trade, and mitigate
health risks and address the demonstrated harms of exposure to vapor products.
      Toward this end, the State shall adopt a comprehensive prohibition on vapor
products in order to safeguard public health, safety, and welfare.


      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:


      (a)    Components refer to any part, accessory, or element of a vapor device
      or consumable, including but not limited to batteries, heating elements,
      coils, chargers, mouthpieces, tanks, cartridges, and similar parts, whether
      sold separately or assembled;


      (b)    Consumables refer to any substance, whether liquid, gel, or solid,
      used in conjunction with Vapor Devices, including cartridges, pods, refills,
      sticks, capsules, and similar materials;


      (c)    EVALI refers to Electronic Cigarette or Vaping Use-Associated Lung
      Injury;


      (d)    Vapor Devices refer to any apparatus, component, or accessory
      designed to deliver vapor products, including electronic cigarettes, vape
      pens, pod systems, tanks, batteries, and similar equipment; and


      (e)    Vapor Products refer to either Electronic Nicotine Delivery System
      (ENDS) or Electronic Non-Nicotine Delivery System (ENNDS) that produce an
      aerosol by heating a substance, intended for inhalation.


      SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. – Except as provided under Section 9 of this Act, it
shall be unlawful for:


      (a)    An individual or entity to manufacture, import, sell, distribute, or
      otherwise place in commerce vapor devices, including its consumables and
      components;


      (b)    An individual to possess vapor products for the purpose of sale or
      distribution;
      (c)    An individual to use or consume such vapor products in any place,
      whether public or private, after the lapse of the transitory period provided
      under section 9 of this Act.;


      (d)    An individual or entities to advertise, promote, endorse, sponsor or
      market vapor products through any medium, including digital and online
      platforms.


      SEC. 5. Roles of Agencies. – The Department of Health (DOH) shall serve
as the lead agency responsible for policy implementation, public health monitoring
and overall coordination of this Act.


      Enforcement of this Act shall be undertaken by the following agencies within
their respective mandates:


      (a)    Food and Drug Administration (FDA). – Shall serve as the primary
      regulatory and technical authority to identify prohibited products, issue
      cease and desist orders, and conduct seizure and disposal of vapor products
      in domestic commerce;


      (b)    Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and the
      Philippine National Police (PNP). – Shall lead the enforcement of the ban on
      public use and closure of retail establishments in coordination with Local
      Government Units (LGUs);


      (c)    LGUs. – Shall enforce the provisions of this Act within their respective
      jurisdictions and may enact complementary ordinances consistent with this
      Act, including enforcement of existing regulations and ordinances related to
      vapor product regulation during the transitory period provided under
      Section 9 of this Act;


      (d)    Bureau of Customs (BOC). – Shall strictly implement the ban on entry
      of all vapor products, including vapor devices, consumables, and
      components at all ports of entry, and shall treat such items as prohibited
      importations under the Customs Modernization and Tariff Act; and
      (e)    Other Deputized Agencies. – The DOH may deputize other government
      agencies or law enforcement units to assist in the implementation and
      enforcement of this Act.


      No government agency shall, including the Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI), issue or renew licenses, permits, or authorization for any activity
prohibited under this Act.


      SEC. 6. Penalties. – Violations of this Act shall be subject to the following
penalties:


      (a)    Violation of Sections 4(a) and (b). – On the first offense, in addition to
      mandatory confiscation and forfeiture of vapor products, a fine of not less
      than Five Thousand Pesos (P5,000.00) or an imprisonment of not more than
      thirty (30) days, or both. For succeeding offenses, both penalties shall apply.


      For offenders caught in possession of more than one thousand (1,000) liters
of consumables, in addition to mandatory confiscation and forfeiture of vapor
products, a fine of not less than One Million Pesos (P1,000,000.00) but not more
than Ten Million Pesos (P10,000,000.00), and imprisonment of not less than six (6)
years but not more than ten (10) years.


      (b)    Violation of Section 4(c). – On the first offense, a fine of not less than
      Five Hundred Pesos (P500.00) but not more than One Thousand Pesos
      (P1,000.00) shall be imposed.
      On the second offense, a fine of not less than One Thousand Pesos
(P1,000.00) but not more than Five Thousand Pesos (P5,000.00) shall be imposed.
      On the third and succeeding offenses, a fine of not less than Five Thousand
Pesos (P5,000.00) but not more than Ten Thousand Pesos (P10,000.00).


      (c)    Violation of Section 4(d). – On the first offense, a fine of not more than
      One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00) or imprisonment of not more
      than one (1) year, or both. On the second offense, a fine of Two Hundred
      Thousand Pesos (P200,000) or imprisonment of not more than two (2) years,
      or both. On the third offense, a fine of not more than Four Hundred
      Thousand Pesos (P400,000.00) or imprisonment of not more than three (3)
      years, or both.
      (d)    If the offender is a corporation, partnership, association, or any other
      juridical entity, the penalty of imprisonment shall be imposed upon the
      president, managing partner, general manager, or any other officer
      responsible for the violation. In addition to the fines and imprisonment, the
      business permit and license to operate of the juridical entity shall be
      perpetually revoked.


      (e)    If the offender is an alien, such offender shall be deported, subject to
      applicable immigration laws and due process, after serving the sentence,
      and shall forever be barred from re-entering the Philippines.


      SEC. 7. Confiscation and Disposal. – All confiscated vapor products shall,
including those seized at ports of entry or through online transactions, be turned
over to the DOH for proper documentation and disposal in accordance with
environmental and public health standards.


      SEC. 8. Public Education and Awareness.            – The DOH, in coordination
with the Department of Education, Commission on Higher Education, Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority, and LGUs, shall conduct sustained
public education campaigns on the health risks of vapor products and nicotine
addiction.


      SEC. 9. Transitory Provision. – In order to facilitate an orderly transition
and ensure compliance with the requirements of this Act, the following transitory
rules shall apply:


      (a)    Grace Period for Existing Licenses. – All existing licenses, permits, and
      authorizations related to vapor products shall remain valid for a period of
      ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, solely for the purpose of
      winding down operations and inventory depletion. Upon the lapse of the
      grace period, such licenses, permits, or authorizations shall be deemed
      revoked. No new applications for registration or licensing shall be accepted
      within the grace period;


      (b)    Inventory Declaration. – All manufacturers, importers, distributors,
      and retailers shall submit a notarized declaration of existing inventory to the
      FDA within thirty (30) days from the effectivity of this Act.
      (c)    Limited Sell-Off of Existing Inventory. - During the grace period, the
      sale and distribution of existing inventories shall be allowed strictly for the
      purpose of depletion, subject to the following conditions:


             (i)     No sale to minors shall be permitted;


             (ii)    No new manufacturing, importation, or product introduction
      shall be allowed;


             (iii)   No expansion of business operations shall be allowed; and


             (iv)    No advertising, promotion, or marketing activities shall be
      conducted;


      All pending shipments arriving after the effectivity of this Act shall be denied
entry to the Philippines.


      (d)    Temporary Allowance for Personal Use. – The use of vapor products
      shall be temporarily allowed within the grace period; Provided, That use of
      vapor products in all public places, shall remain regulated in accordance
      with existing laws and ordinances;


      (e)    Post-Transition Enforcement. – Upon the expiration of the grace period,
      a full prohibition regime shall be enforced in accordance with Section 4 of
      this Act.


      (f)    Disposition of Remaining Inventory. – Upon the expiration of the grace
      period, all remaining vapor products shall be deemed prohibited and subject
      confiscation and disposal provided under Section 7 of this Act.


      SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH shall, in coordination with the FDA, and in
consultation with other agencies and stakeholders, promulgate the necessary rules
and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.
       SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts of this Act which are
not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.


       SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – Republic Act No. 11900, otherwise known as
the “Vaporized Nicotine and Non-Nicotine Products Regulation Act,” is hereby
repealed.


       All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, letters of instruction,
administrative rules and regulations or parts thereof, which are contrary to or
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.


       SEC. 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
