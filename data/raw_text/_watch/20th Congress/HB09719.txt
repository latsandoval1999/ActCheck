                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Literacy Coordinating Council (LCC) was established under
Republic Act No. 7165 in 1991 to lead the country’s efforts in eradicating
illiteracy, and was later strengthened by Republic Act No. 10122 in 2010 to
expand its coordination and partnership functions.

       Despite these measures, universal literacy remains unmet. Based on the
2024 Functional Literacy, Education and Mass Media Survey (FLEMMS),
93.1% of Filipinos aged 10 to 64 are basically literate, leaving about 5.87
million unable to read, write, or perform basic computations. More notably,
only 70.8% are functionally literate, meaning around 24.8 million Filipinos
lack higher-order comprehension and critical thinking skills.

      These gaps highlight persistent challenges in literacy and education
outcomes, underscoring the need for stronger coordination and more
responsive institutional mechanisms.

       This bill seeks to repeal Republic Act No. 7165 and transform the LCC
into the National Literacy Council, with strengthened powers, a streamlined
structure, and better alignment with Republic Act No. 11510 (Alternative
Learning System Act) and Republic Act No. 11315 (Community-Based
Monitoring System Act). It transfers the Council’s secretariat to the Bureau of
Alternative Education and designates Local School Boards as local literacy
bodies to strengthen community-level implementation.

      The measure also mandates a three-year national literacy roadmap,
with clear targets and data-driven strategies, to be mirrored at the local level.

       By improving coordination, strengthening local support, and
institutionalizing long-term planning, this measure aims to accelerate the
achievement of universal literacy.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                              AN ACT
     RENAMING THE LITERACY COORDINATING COUNCIL TO THE
     NATIONAL LITERACY COUNCIL, STRENGTHENING ITS POWERS AND
     FUNCTIONS, STREAMLINING ITS MEMBERSHIP STRUCTURE,
     PROVIDING ITS LOCAL SUPPORT MECHANISMS, REPEALING FOR
     THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7165, AS AMENDED, AND
     APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
           Congress assembled:


 1             SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National
 2      Literacy Council Act”.

 3             SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Pursuant to Article II, Section 17 of the
 4      1987 Constitution, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to give priority to
 5      education to foster patriotism and nationalism, accelerate social progress, and
 6      promote total human liberation and development. Article XIV, Section 2(4)
 7      likewise mandates the State to encourage non-formal, informal, and indigenous
 8      learning systems, as well as self-learning, independent, and out-of-school study
 9      programs particularly those that respond to community needs.
10             To this end, the State shall give priority to the adoption of measures for the
11      universalization of literacy.

 1          SEC. 3. Renaming of the Literacy Coordinating Council. – The name of
 2   the Literacy Coordinating Council (LCC), established pursuant to Republic Act
 3   (R.A.) No. 7165, as amended, is hereby changed to National Literacy Council,
 4   hereinafter referred to in this Act as the “Council”.
 5          The Council shall serve as the lead inter-agency coordinating and advisory
 6   body to the various National Government Agencies (NGAs), the Local
 7   Government Units (LGUs), and the private sector on matters pertaining to the
 8   formulation of policies and the adoption and implementation of measures for the
 9   universalization of literacy.
10          The Council shall be attached to the Department of Education (DepEd).
11          SEC. 4. Composition of the Council. – The Council shall be governed by
12   the following:
13          (a) The Secretaries of Education and the Interior and Local Government,
14               as    Co-Chairpersons,     who     may      designate   their   respective
15               undersecretaries as permanent representatives;
16          (b) The National Statistician and Civil Registrar General of the Philippine
17               Statistics Authority (PSA) or a duly designated representative;
18          (c) The Director of the Bureau of Alternative Education (BAE) of the
19               DepEd;
20          (d) The President of the Union of Local Authorities of the Philippines; and
21          (e) One (1) representative from the non-government organizations
22               (NGOs).
23           The NGO representative must be a citizen of the Philippines with
24      demonstrated personal or professional commitment and advocacy in the
25      propagation of literacy.
26           Through the adoption of a resolution, the Council shall approve and
27      confirm the election of the NGO representative who shall serve for a term of
28      three (3) years, subject to reappointment on the basis of merit and performance.

 1          SEC. 5. Council Meetings. – The Council shall conduct its regular
 2   meetings quarterly and may hold special meetings to consider urgent matters upon
 3   the call of the Chairperson or a majority of its members.
 4          The Council shall meet at any agreed location within the Philippines, and
 5   the presence of a majority of all its incumbent members shall constitute a quorum.
 6          The members of the Council shall serve without compensation, but shall be
 7   entitled to an appropriate honorarium for attendance at meetings as well as
 8   reasonable allowances and reimbursement of expenses related to their participation
 9   in the official activities of the Council, in accordance with pertinent laws, rules and
10   regulations.
11          The duly designated representatives to the Council shall be afforded equal
12   opportunity to vote and participate in any deliberation during meetings of the
13   Council. They shall likewise be afforded, whenever necessary, reimbursement of
14   any actual expenses incurred during the performance of Council-related functions
15   and duties, subject to the provisions of existing laws, rules, and regulations.
16          SEC. 6. Powers and Functions of the Council. – The Council shall
17   exercise the following powers and functions:
18          (a) Act as the lead advisory and coordinating body of the government and
19                  initiate, promote and recommend the adoption of policies for all
20                  literacy endeavors in the country, as well as the formulation of
21                  legislative agenda related to literacy;
22          (b) Formulate a three-year roadmap that shall incorporate strategies on
23                  how to achieve zero illiteracy, including relevant measurable targets
24                  and indicators for the introduction of interventions that are based on
25                  empirical results, and which shall be subject to annual review and
26                  updating to determine its implementation status and ensure that the
27                  objectives of this Act are achieved;

13          SEC. 8. Establishment of Local Literacy Councils through the Local
14   School Boards. – The Council shall promote the establishment of Local Literacy
15   Councils (LLCs) at the provincial, city, and municipal levels, which shall serve as
16   the coordinating and advisory bodies at the local level on matters pertaining to the
17   adoption and implementation of measures for the universalization of literacy.
18          For this purpose, the Local School Boards (LSBs) established pursuant to
19   R.A. No. 7160 or the “Local Government Code of 1991”, as amended, shall act as
20   the de facto LLCs in the provinces, cities and municipalities, with the following
21   functions in addition to Section 9 thereof:
22          (a) Formulate a local roadmap, anchored on the Council’s three-year
23                roadmap under Section 6 of this Act, to guide its strategies on how to
24                achieve zero illiteracy in the community, based on empirical data and
25                determined by relevant measurable targets and indicators, and subject
26                to an annual implementation review and assessment;
27          (b) Implement literacy programs and endeavors at the local level,
28                including the identification of priority literacy programs, provision of
