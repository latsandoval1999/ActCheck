                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Constitution enjoins the State to protect and promote the right to health
of the people and to instill health consciousness among them.1 It directs the State to
adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to health development, one that
endeavors to make essential goods, health and other social services available to all
our people at affordable cost, and that gives priority to the needs of the
underprivileged and the sick.2 It declares, further, that science and technology are
essential to national development, and requires that the State give priority to
research, invention, and their utilization.3 Congress gave these mandates concrete
form in the Universal Health Care Act4 and in the National Integrated Cancer Control
Act,5 the latter enacted to carry cancer services beyond the metropolitan centers and
within reach of the marginalized sector.6 This measure is offered in the same spirit,

      Our physicians detect and stage cancer through scans that depend on small
amounts of radioactive material administered to the patient. However, the nation
produces very little of this material. Four machines in the entire country are capable
of making it, and every one of them is privately held.8 The Philippine Nuclear
Research Institute (PNRI) prepares the substance used in our most common scans
and supplies thirty-five nuclear medicine centers nationwide,9 yet the raw material
it works from is imported in its entirety, secured through negotiation with foreign
suppliers.10 As a result, the cost of medical imaging services are expensive. What
renders this dependence perilous is the nature of the material itself: it loses half its
strength in roughly six hours.11 It cannot be stored, stockpiled, or held in reserve
against emergency. A shipment delayed at the port is a shipment lost. A supplier's
shutdown abroad reaches our wards within days. The price of this fragility is already
being paid, and it is paid in lives.

       Lung cancer alone claimed 20,953 Filipinos in 2022, and more than seven in
ten of those who contract it are found only when the disease has already advanced.12
Furthermore, across Southeast Asia, one year after diagnosis, twenty-nine percent of
cancer patients had died and forty-eight percent had been driven into financial ruin
² and those discovered late fared worst on both counts.13

       The country cannot entrust the detection of our deadliest diseases to a supply
line we neither control nor can replenish on short notice, especially when the lives
of Filipinos are at stake. Furthermore, the cost of these scans remain beyond the
reach of most Filipinos, priced as they are for a material imported in full and paid
for largely out of pocket. To address this grave problem, the country must be able

 to produce these materials locally. It shortens the distance between producer and
 patient, which is the only remedy for a substance that perishes on the way. It lowers
 the price: preparing the material locally was projected to reduce by a fifth the cost
 of importing it.14 And it retains within the Republic the value of an industry we now
 purchase wholly from abroad, while cultivating the skilled workforce and research
 capacity upon which further medical and industrial applications must depend.

        To this end, the measure establishes the Radioisotope Industry Support
 Program under the Department of Science and Technology and the Department of
 Health, directed by a Program Board, with PhilATOM present for regulation. The
 Board shall adopt and regularly revise a national plan to lessen our dependence upon
 imports, extending support across technology, facilities, infrastructure, research,
 workforce, supply, and access for the regions. To ensure sustainability, this measure
 accordingly empowers the Board to undertake the purchase of what our producers
 make, while government hospitals shall buy from local producers where supply and
 price permit. The measure also requires PhilHealth to cover these procedures and
 that locally produced supply be cleared and carried swiftly, above all to the regions.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. ± This Act shall be known as the "Industry Support for
Radioisotopes Production for Medical Imaging Act."

      Section 2. Declaration of Policy. ± The State recognizes its duty under Article
II, Section 15 of the Constitution to protect and promote the right to health of the
people, and its mandate under Article XIII, Section 11 thereof to adopt an integrated
and comprehensive approach to health development, giving priority to the needs of
the underprivileged, sick, and elderly, and to endeavor to make essential goods,
health, and other social services available to all the people at affordable cost.



                                                                                    1
      The State likewise recognizes, pursuant to Article XIV, Section 10 of the
Constitution, that science and technology are essential for national development and
progress, and shall give priority to research and development, invention, innovation,
and their utilization.

      Pursuant to the foregoing, and in furtherance of Republic Act No. 11223,
otherwise known as the "Universal Health Care Act," and Republic Act No. 11215,
otherwise known as the "National Integrated Cancer Control Act," and consistent
with Republic Act No. 12305, which vests regulatory authority over nuclear and
radiation safety in the Philippine Atomic Energy Regulatory Authority
(PhilATOM), it is hereby declared the policy of the State to develop a local industry
producing the materials used in medical imaging, and to make such imaging
available and affordable in every region. Toward this end, the State shall support
local production of covered radioactive materials used to detect or monitor disease
and the medicines prepared from them.

      Section 3. Definition of Terms. ± As used in this Act:

   a. Covered materials shall refer to radioactive materials produced for use in
      examining the body to detect or monitor disease, and the medicines prepared
      from them.
   b. Licensed producer shall refer to a producer of covered materials holding a
      valid license from PhilATOM and the corresponding authorization from the
      Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and all other permits required by law.
   c. Eligible producer shall refer to a licensed producer determined by the Board
      to meet the criteria for support under this Act.




                                                                                   2
      Section 4. The Program and Its Objectives. ± There is hereby established the
Radioisotope Industry Support Program, which aims to:

   a. Increase the volume of radioisotopes and radiopharmaceuticals for medical
      imaging produced in the country and ensure steady supply;
   b. Bring down the cost of imaging to the patient;
   c. Build a Filipino workforce able to run the industry; and
   d. Reduce, over time, the country's dependence on imported supply.

      Section 5. Program Board. ± A Program Board is hereby created to
implement the Radioisotope Industry Support Program, chaired by the Secretary of
Science and Technology (DOST), co-chaired by the Secretary of Health (DOH),
with the PNRI Director, the FDA Director General, the head of the Philippine Health
Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), and a representative of the nuclear medicine
profession as members.

      The PhilATOM Director General shall sit as a non-voting observer and shall
not take part in decisions on producers it regulates.

      Section 6. Support for Producers. ± The Board shall adopt and periodically
update a national plan in line with the Radioisotope Industry Support Program to
reduce dependence on imported covered materials, either through government
funding or by sharing costs with private producers. Support shall include:

   a. Technology ² acquiring, adapting, and maintaining the means of producing
      covered materials;
   b. Facilities ² building, upgrading, and operating production and delivery
      facilities, whether government or private;




                                                                                 3
   c. Infrastructure ² the storage, handling, and transport capacity needed to
       deliver each material within its useful life;
   d. Research ² studies and trials to establish and improve local production, and
       the institutions undertaking them;
   e. Workforce ² training and retaining the specialists the industry requires;
   f. Supply ² securing the raw materials, equipment, and supplies production
       depends on; and
   g. Regional access ² extending production and delivery to regions without
       existing capacity.

       The Board may likewise provide for the continued operation of eligible
producers, including procurement: Provided, That government hospitals and
facilities shall procure from eligible producers whenever supply is available and the
price is reasonable.

       Support under this Section shall be extended only to eligible producers. The
Board shall determine eligibility on the basis of technical capacity, financial
capacity, and contribution to the objectives of this Act, and shall publish the criteria
it applies.

       Support shall be withdrawn and recovered upon the loss or suspension of any
license or authorization required by law, upon failure to comply with any applicable
law, rule, or permit condition, or upon failure to meet the conditions of the support
granted. Nothing in this Section shall be construed as a license, or as a substitute for
any license or authorization required from PhilATOM, the FDA, or any other
authority.




                                                                                      4
       Section 7. Patient Coverage. ± The PhilHealth shall provide benefit coverage
for medical imaging procedures using covered materials, for conditions where the
medical evidence supports their use.

       Section 8. Clearing Delays. ± The distribution and supply of covered
materials produced locally, particularly to the regions, shall be given fast clearance,
release, and carriage.

       Section 9. Reporting. ± The Board shall submit an annual report to the
President and Congress on the state of domestic production, the supply of covered
materials, patient access, and the use of funds under this Act.

       Section 10. Peaceful Use and Waste. ± All activity under this Act shall be
peaceful, and no facility supported under this Act shall use any material usable for
weapons, consistent with the commitments of the Republic on nuclear non-
proliferation and safeguards and with Republic Act No. 12305.

       Every producer supported under this Act shall also set aside funds for waste
disposal and facility shutdown, in the amount PhilATOM requires, before operating.

       Section 11. Appropriations. ± The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year's appropriations
of the DOST and the DOH. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its
continued implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.

       Section 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. ± Within one hundred
eighty (180) days from the approval of this Act, the DOST and the DOH, in
consultation with PhilATOM, the FDA, PhilHealth, and other concerned agencies,

shall promulgate the Implementing Rules and Regulations to carry out the provisions

      Section 13. Separability Clause. ± If any provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

      Section 14. Repealing Clause. ± All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, presidential proclamations, rules and regulations or parts thereof contrary to
or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      Section 15. Effectivity. ± This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
