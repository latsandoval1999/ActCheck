                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

This bill seeks to create a National Disaster Risk Finance and Insurance (NDRFI)
Framework and appropriate funds therefor. The proposed measure builds on the
country’s disaster management strategy which is aimed at protecting economic gains
from natural disaster shocks and reducing the impact of disasters on the poorest and
most vulnerable.

The location of the Philippines along the Pacific Ring of Fire and typhoon belt makes
it extremely vulnerable to natural disasters. As one of the most disaster-prone
countries in the world, it is constantly threatened by earthquakes, volcanic eruptions,
typhoons, floods, and rising sea levels. Every year, the nation is hit by about twenty
typhoons, which cause extensive flooding and landslides. Climate change-related
sea level rise intensifies already-existing risks, affecting coastal communities and
agriculture.     Substantial social and economic costs are incurred in rescue,
rehabilitation, repair and rebuilding of the damage and loss caused by these natural
disasters. It has become an annual vicious cycle of loss and spending with no end in
sight.

Disaster Risk Finance and Insurance refers to pre-arranged financial tools that
protect against climate and disaster risks by shifting costs from reactive recovery to
proactive financial resilience. It is part of a comprehensive risk management
approach that uses a variety of solutions to improve a country’s ability to recover
financially after a disaster. This approach helps avoid economic shocks and improve
risk management.

The DRFI framework involves applying a set of core principles and tailoring a
strategy to a country’s specific risk profile and needs. An optimal DRFI framework is
integrated into a broader disaster risk management plan and uses a combination of
financial instruments to ensure timely access to funds after a disaster. Key elements
and best practices include risk assessment, risk laying, prompt disbursement,
institutional coordination, and capacity-building.

Several international organizations provide critical guidance and support in the
development of DRFI frameworks. The World Bank’s Disaster Risk Financing and
Insurance Program serves as a leading partner for developing countries, helping
them design and implement comprehensive financial protection strategies. The
Group of Twenty and the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development
have established a Methodological Framework for Disaster Risk Assessment and Risk
Financing, offering global standards and insights into best practices and common
challenges. Likewise, the United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction promotes
a structured five-step approach to building national financing frameworks for disaster
risk reduction.

Ultimately, the best framework is one that is tailored to a country’s specific context
and integrates seamlessly into its overall fiscal planning and disaster management
strategy. This bill seeks to create the best framework for the Philippines.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 8          SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Disaster

 9   Risk Finance and Insurance Framework Act.”

10          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to

11   strengthen national resilience and fiscal stability against the impact of disasters by

12   establishing a comprehensive and inclusive system of disaster risk financing and

13   insurance framework, one that protects national budgets and the lives and

14   livelihoods of the people from the impact of disasters. Toward this end, the State

15   shall develop and establish a national disaster risk finance and insurance framework

16   to reduce financial burdens from disasters, protect development gains, and build

17   resilience by shifting from reactive to proactive financial preparedness for

18   governments, businesses, and vulnerable households, ensuring rapid funding for

19   recovery, and strengthening fiscal stability.

20          SEC. 3. National Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Framework.

21   – The Department of Finance shall, in coordination with the National Disaster Risk
 1   Reduction and Management Council, Climate Change Commission, and the Philippine

 2   Statistics Authority, Office of Civil Defense, develop and establish a National Disaster

 3   Risk Financing and Insurance Framework, hereinafter referred to as the Framework,

 4   which shall be an integral part of the disaster risk management to decrease

 5   vulnerability and build financial resilience during a disaster. The Framework shall

 6   serve as a guide for the development, coordination and implementation of disaster

 7   risk financing and insurance policies and instruments, and must be:

 8          (a)      Proactive   instead      of   reactive,   focusing   on    having     financial

 9   arrangements in place before a disaster occurs, rather than relying solely on reactive

10   or post-disaster funding;

11          (b)      Risk-layered or using different financial tools for different levels of

12   risk, such as government funds for minor events and risk transfer products like

13   insurance or catastrophe bonds for major catastrophes;

14          (c)      Vulnerability-focused, protecting the poorest and near-poor from

15   falling into poverty due to disasters;

16          (d)      Public-private   partnerships       driven,   leveraging    private     sector

17   innovation with public sector reach; and

18          (e)      Guided by the principles of fiscal sustainability, risk-based decision

19   making, equity and social protection, transparency and accountability and alignment

20   with national disaster risk management and climate policies.

21          SEC. 4. Components. – The Framework includes:

22          (a)      Disaster risk assessment and fiscal risk analysis;

23          (b)      Layered financial strategies for disasters of different frequency and

24   severity;


                                                                                                  4
 1           (c)     Budgetary instruments and contingency financing;

 2           (d)     Insurance and risk transfer mechanisms;

 3           (e)     Protection of households, businesses, and vulnerable groups;

 4           (f)     Coverage of public assets and infrastructure; and

 5           (g)     Post-disaster financial management and transparency mechanisms.


 6           SEC. 5. Financial Tools. – The Framework must prioritize the establishment

 7   of financial arrangements before the occurrence of disasters, rather than relying

 8   solely on post‑disaster funding. It must employ appropriate financial tools matched

 9   to different levels of risk, categorized as follows:

10           (a)     Contingency fund or budgetary allocation for frequent low-impact

11   events;

12           (b)     Parametric insurance for medium-intensity events like typhoons,

13   floods, earthquakes and similar force majeure events;

14           (c)     Catastrophe bonds and international risk pools for rare and high-

15   impact disasters; and

16           (d)     Emergency credit lines from multilateral partners to ensure fiscal

17   stability.

18           SEC. 6. Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Fund. – There is hereby

19   established a National Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Fund, hereunder

20   referred to as the Fund, to provide immediate liquidity for disaster risk financing and

21   insurance mechanisms under this Act. The Fund shall be administered by the

22   Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Board created under Section 7 of this Act. All

23   disbursements from the Fund under this Act shall be subject to audit by the

24   Commission on Audit in accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations.
