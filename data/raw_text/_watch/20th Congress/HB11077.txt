                         AN ACT

ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL VOLUNTEER PROGRAM FOR TRAINED

THEREFOR

                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE
       The Philippines continues to confront a worsening mental health crisis
notwithstanding advances in public discourse and the enactment of Republic Act
No. 11036, otherwise known as the “Mental Health Act,” which established the
country’s landmark policy framework on mental health. Despite its passage,
significant gaps in implementation and service delivery persist nearly a decade
thereafter.
       In 2023, the mental health experts characterized the situation as ‘a rising
epidemic’, a condition that remains evident to date, as reflected in the increasing
volume of crisis calls reported by the National Center for Mental Health (NCMH)1.
This growing demand is further exacerbated by a shortage of mental health
professionals which constraint attainment of access to timely, adequate and quality
care. The urgency of addressing the country’s deteriorating mental health landscape

        Similarly, the 1987 Philippine Constitution, under Article II, Section 13,
recognizes the vital role of the youth in nation-building. Notwithstanding the
growing call for mental health-responsive communities and collective action, the
potential of the youth remains underutilized due to fragmented and uncoordinated
platforms for engagement. In 2021, UNICEF reported that young people
increasingly seek mental health information and demonstrate a heightened
willingness to participate in mental health discussions, as well as peer support
programs and mechanisms3. Globally, there has likewise been a notable rise in youth
engagement in mental health advocacy, peer counseling, and volunteer-driven
initiatives4.
       Thus an urgent need to complement professional services with trained peer
support systems that can provide immediate psychosocial assistance and facilitate
early intervention at the primary care service level. Empowering the youth as mental
health advocates not only expands access to care but also reduces stigma and
promotes a culture of help-seeking behavior to expand.

        This bill seeks to establish a Youth Mental Health Corps, a national volunteer
program composed of trained youth advocates equipped with skills in psychosocial
first aid, suicide prevention, peer counseling, and crisis listening. By mobilizing the
youth as frontliners of mental health support in schools, communities, and disaster
settings, the State strengthens its commitment to safeguarding the mental well-being
of every Filipino and uphold mental health as a fundamental human right

        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                         AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A YOUTH MENTAL HEALTH CORPS,
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL VOLUNTEER PROGRAM FOR TRAINED
YOUTH MENTAL HEALTH ADVOCATES, AND PROVIDING FUNDS
THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Youth Mental
Health Corps Act.”


      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to promote and protect the mental health and well-being of the youth as a vital
component of national development. The State recognizes young people as key



                                                                                   1
partners in nation-building and affirms their right to accessible, responsive, and
culturally appropriate mental health services. Accordingly, the State shall establish
community-based and youth-responsive mental health systems that complement
existing services, promote mental health awareness and preventive care, and address
service gaps, particularly in underserved communities. It shall empower the youth
through capacity-building, volunteerism, and peer support initiatives, while
strengthening early identification, intervention, and referral mechanisms.


      The State shall adopt a whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach,
ensuring coordination among relevant stakeholders, and uphold gender-responsive,
child-sensitive, and rights-based principles. All programs shall be guided by
inclusivity, confidentiality, ethical practice, and respect for human dignity, with
adequate safeguards for both beneficiaries and youth volunteers.


      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
      (a). Youth shall refer to those persons whose ages range from fifteen (15) to
      thirty (30) years old as defined in Republic Act No. 8044;


      (b). Psychological First Aid (PFA) refers to an evidence-informed approach
      built on the concept of human resilience and aims to reduce stress symptoms
      and assist in a healthy recovery following a traumatic event, natural disaster,
      public health emergency, or even a personal crisis;


      (c). Peer Counseling refers to a supportive process where trained individuals
      provide emotional support to peers; and

      (d). Youth Mental Health Corps (YMHC) refers to a national volunteer corps
      of trained youth mental health advocates.


      (f) Transplant Center refers to a hospital duly licensed and accredited by the
      Department of Health (DOH) to perform transplantation procedures;


      (g) Organ Procurement refers to the process of identifying, retrieving, and
      preserving organs for transplantation.


      SECTION 4. Establishment of the Youth Mental Health Corps. — Thereby
established a Youth Mental Health Corps (YMHC), a national volunteer program
composed of trained youth mental health advocates, under the supervision of the
Department of Health (DOH). The DOH shall serve as the lead implementing agency
and shall exercise overall policy direction, coordination, and program management.
(g) Generate data analytics for policy and planning.


      The DOH, in coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd),
Commission on Higher Education (CHED), National Youth Commission (NYC),
Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and all Local
Government     Units   (LGUs),     shall   ensure   the   effective   implementation,
institutionalization, and sustainability of the Corps at the national and local levels.
Such agencies shall integrate the Youth Mental Health Corps into their respective
programs, systems, and structures, and shall provide the necessary administrative,
technical, and logistical support for its operations. Local Government Units shall
serve as the primary implementing bodies at the local level and shall facilitate




                                                                                     3
recruitment, organization, and deployment of Corps members within their respective
jurisdictions.


      SECTION 5. Objectives – The Youth Mental Health Corps shall pursue the
following objectives:


      (a) To provide accessible, timely, and community-based psychosocial support
      to individuals, particularly the youth, experiencing mental health distress;
      (b) To promote mental health awareness, literacy, and preventive care, and to
      reduce stigma associated with mental health conditions;
      (c) To strengthen early detection and intervention mechanisms through peer
      support, crisis listening, and psychosocial first aid;
      (d) To establish a functional linkage and referral pathway between
      communities and licensed mental health professionals, health facilities, and
      appropriate service providers;
      (e) To mobilize trained youth volunteers as frontliners in mental health
      promotion, crisis response, and community engagement;
      (f) To support mental health and psychosocial response efforts during
      disasters, emergencies, and other crisis situations, including deployment in
      evacuation centers and affected communities; and
      (g) To contribute to the development of resilient, inclusive, and mentally
      healthy communities through sustained youth participation and engagement.




                                                                                     4
      SECTION 6. Composition and Deployment. The Youth Mental Health
Corps shall be composed of duly trained, certified, and registered youth volunteers
who have successfully completed the required training under this Act.


      Corps members shall be strategically deployed in key settings, including but
not limited to public and private elementary and secondary schools, colleges and
universities, barangays, workplaces, community-based organizations, youth centers,
and evacuation centers during disasters and emergencies, to ensure broad
accessibility of psychosocial support services. Each barangay shall establish and
maintain at least one (1) Youth Mental Health Team, composed of an adequate
number of certified Corps members, taking into consideration population size,
geographic conditions, and identified mental health needs of the community. Such
teams shall operate in close coordination with barangay officials, local health units,
school authorities, and accredited mental health professionals.


      Deployment of Corps members shall be aligned with identified community
needs and priorities, and shall be subject to the supervision of designated licensed
mental health professionals in accordance with this Act. The assignment and
mobilization of volunteers during emergencies shall be coordinated through the
Local Government Units (LGUs) and relevant disaster risk reduction and
management structures. The Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
partner agencies, shall issue guidelines to ensure the proper organization, equitable
distribution, and effective utilization of Corps members nationwide.


      SECTION 7. Training and Certification. – All members of the Youth Mental
Health Corps shall undergo standardized and competency-based training to ensure


                                                                                    5
the quality, safety, and effectiveness of services rendered. The Department of Health
(DOH), in coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on
Higher Education (CHED), National Youth Commission (NYC), and other relevant
institutions, shall develop and implement a unified training framework and
curriculum.


      The training program shall consist of not less than forty (40) hours of
foundational instruction, which shall include, but not be limited to, psychosocial first
aid, suicide prevention and risk identification, peer counseling techniques, crisis
listening and communication skills, mental health literacy, cultural and gender
sensitivity, child protection, ethical standards, confidentiality, and proper referral
systems. Participants shall likewise be required to complete supervised practicum,
simulation exercises, or field-based exposure to ensure practical application of skills.
No individual shall be deployed as a Corps member without having satisfactorily
completed all training requirements.


      To ensure continued competency and responsiveness to emerging mental
health concerns, all Corps members shall undergo annual refresher training of not
less than eight (8) hours and shall participate in continuing education programs as
may be prescribed by the DOH. Certification shall be issued by the DOH, in
coordination with partner agencies, to individuals who have successfully completed
the required training and demonstrated competency. Such certification shall be
subject to renewal based on compliance with continuing education and performance
standards.




                                                                                      6
      SECTION 8. National and Local Registry. — A National Youth Mental
Health Corps Registry is hereby established under the Department of Health, serving
as the centralized database of all certified Corps members for purposes of
monitoring, coordination, and mobilization. Local Government Units shall likewise
maintain corresponding Local Registries at the barangay, city, and municipal levels,
containing updated information on members within their jurisdiction, including their
identity, contact details, deployment assignments, training and certification status,
and service availability. The National and Local Registries shall be regularly updated
and integrated to ensure accuracy, accessibility, and efficient deployment,
particularly in emergencies and crisis situations. All registry data shall be processed
in accordance with applicable data privacy and confidentiality laws, with access
restricted to authorized personnel for official purposes only.


      SECTION 9. Roles and Functions. – Members of the Youth Mental Health
Corps shall perform the following roles and functions within the scope of their
training and under appropriate supervision:


      (a) Provide immediate psychosocial support through active listening,
      emotional reassurance, and basic psychological assistance to individuals in
      distress;
      (b) Conduct mental health awareness, education, and advocacy activities
      within schools, communities, and organizations to promote mental health
      literacy and reduce stigma;
      (c) Identify early signs of mental health concerns and facilitate appropriate
      and timely referral to licensed mental health professionals, health facilities, or
      relevant authorities;


                                                                                      7
      (d) Assist in the establishment and facilitation of peer support groups, youth
      safe spaces, and community-based mental health initiatives;
      (e) Support mental health and psychosocial response efforts during disasters,
      emergencies, and crisis situations, including deployment in evacuation centers
      and affected areas;
      (f) Maintain proper documentation of activities and referrals in accordance
      with established protocols and ethical standards.


      Corps members shall strictly observe the limits of their role and shall not
engage in clinical diagnosis, prescription of medication, or provision of professional
therapeutic interventions.


      SECTION 10. Benefits and Incentives. — To promote sustained
participation and recognize the valuable contribution of volunteers, members of the
Youth Mental Health Corps shall be entitled to appropriate benefits and incentives,
subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations.


      Such benefits shall include free access to training, certification, and
continuing education programs; reasonable allowances during official deployment
or authorized activities; and insurance coverage for risks and hazards encountered in
the performance of their duties. Volunteers shall also be granted access to mental
health support services, including counseling and psychological debriefing, to
safeguard their well-being. Recognition and incentive programs shall likewise be
established at the local and national levels to acknowledge exemplary service.




                                                                                    8
      Subject to applicable guidelines, Corps members may be given priority
consideration in government scholarship programs, internships, and public service
employment opportunities, in recognition of their service and contribution to
national development.


      SECTION 11. Institutionalization in Schools and Communities. – The
Youth Mental Health Corps shall be institutionalized in schools, higher education
institutions, and communities as a key component of youth development and mental
health promotion programs.


      The Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) shall integrate the Corps into existing student development
frameworks, organizations, and co-curricular activities, and shall support the
establishment of school-based peer support systems and safe spaces. Local
Government Units, through their barangays, shall allocate and maintain appropriate
venues for youth mental health safe spaces and community-based psychosocial
support activities. The Sangguniang Kabataan (SK) shall play an active role in the
recruitment, mobilization, and engagement of youth volunteers and shall assist in
the implementation of programs under this Act. Coordination among schools, local
government units, and community organizations shall be strengthened to ensure a
cohesive and accessible mental health support network.


      SECTION 12. Professional Supervision. – All members of the Youth Mental
Health Corps shall operate under the supervision and guidance of licensed mental
health professionals, including psychologists, psychiatrists, or registered guidance




                                                                                   9
counselors, as may be designated by the Department of Health or the Local
Government Units.


      Supervising professionals shall provide technical guidance, case consultation,
and oversight to ensure that all activities and interventions conducted by Corps
members adhere to established standards, ethical principles, and referral protocols.
They shall also assess the readiness of volunteers for deployment and provide
feedback for continuous improvement. No Corps member shall independently
handle high-risk cases without proper supervision and referral to qualified
professionals.


      SECTION 13. Psychological Debriefing and Volunteer Welfare. – The
State shall prioritize the mental health and well-being of all Youth Mental Health
Corps members by ensuring access to comprehensive support mechanisms. Regular
psychological debriefing sessions shall be conducted for volunteers, particularly
after exposure to crisis situations, disaster response operations, or emotionally
distressing cases. Such debriefing shall be facilitated by qualified mental health
professionals and shall aim to prevent burnout, secondary trauma, and emotional
fatigue.


      Volunteers shall likewise have access to confidential counseling and mental
health services, as well as peer support systems within the Corps. Mechanisms shall
be established to monitor the well-being of volunteers and to provide timely
intervention when necessary. No volunteer shall be assigned duties beyond their
level of training, competence, or emotional capacity, and participation in high-risk
or high-intensity situations shall be voluntary and subject to appropriate safeguards.


                                                                                   10
      SECTION 14. Gender-Sensitive and Child Protection Measure – All
programs under this Act shall be gender-responsive and compliant with existing
laws, including the Magna Carta of Women (Republic Act No. 9710), Special
Protection of Children Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act
(Republic Act No. 7610), and Safe Spaces Act (Republic Act No. 11313).
Volunteers shall undergo gender sensitivity and child safeguarding training, strictly
observe confidentiality, and immediately refer cases involving abuse, exploitation,
or self-harm to appropriate authorities. Safeguarding mechanisms, including a code
of conduct and reporting systems, shall be established.


      SECTION 15. Monitoring, Evaluation and Reporting. A comprehensive
Monitoring and Evaluation system shall be established by the Department of Health
to assess the implementation and effectiveness of the program. Key indicators shall
include coverage, service delivery, capacity development, and program outcomes.
Barangays shall submit monthly reports to LGUs, LGUs shall submit quarterly
reports to the Department of Health, and the Department of Health shall submit an
annual report to Congress and the Office of the President.


      SECTION 16. Annual National Convening of the Youth Mental Health
Corps. - An Annual National Convening of the Youth Mental Health Corps is hereby
institutionalized to ensure program alignment, knowledge exchange, and continuous
improvement of services. The Department of Health, in coordination with the
National Youth Commission, Department of Education, Commission on Higher
Education, Department of the Interior and Local Government, and Local
Government Units, shall organize and facilitate the convening.


                                                                                  11
        The Annual Convening shall serve as a platform for the presentation of
program accomplishments, sharing of best practices, capacity-building sessions,
policy updates, and consultation with stakeholders, including mental health
professionals, youth leaders, and partner institutions. Regional and local pre-
convenings may also be conducted to ensure broader participation. Participation of
selected Corps representatives from all regions shall be supported by the
government, subject to existing accounting and auditing rules and regulations.


        SECTION 17. Continuing Education and Capacity Development. - The
State shall ensure the continuous learning and development of all Youth Mental
Health Corps members through a structured and sustained capacity-building
program. The Department of Health, in coordination with partner agencies, shall
develop and implement continuing education modules, which may include advanced
training in psychosocial first aid, suicide intervention, crisis response, cultural
sensitivity, and emerging mental health concerns.


        All Corps members shall be required to complete periodic continuing
education and refresher courses as a condition for maintaining active status in the
registry. Access to updated learning materials, online and in-person training
opportunities, and mentorship programs shall be provided to ensure that volunteers
remain competent, responsive, and aligned with current standards of mental health
care.


        SECTION 18. Integrated Referral System of Care. - An integrated and
coordinated referral system of care is hereby established to ensure that individuals


                                                                                 12
identified by Youth Mental Health Corps members receive timely and appropriate
professional intervention. The Department of Health, in coordination with Local
Government Units and relevant agencies, shall develop standardized referral
protocols, including clear pathways from community-based support to licensed
mental health professionals, health facilities, and appropriate government services.


      The referral system shall ensure:
      (1) proper documentation and tracking of referred cases;
      (2) confidentiality and data privacy in accordance with existing laws;
      (3) feedback mechanisms between volunteers and supervising professionals;
      and
      (4) prioritization of urgent and high-risk cases, including those involving
      suicide risk, abuse, or severe psychological distress.


      All Corps members shall be trained in the proper implementation of referral
protocols and shall strictly adhere to the principle that they are not authorized to
provide clinical diagnosis or treatment.


      SECTION 19. Funding. - The initial funding shall be charged against the
current appropriations of the DOH. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall
be included in the General Appropriations Act. LGUs are encouraged to allocate
funds for local implementation.


      SECTION 20. Implementing Rules and Regulation (IRR). - Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with partner
agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations.

      SECTION 21. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.


      SECTION 22. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, and regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


      SECTION 23. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
