                             AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL POLICY FRAMEWORK FOR AUTISM AND
NEURODIVERSITY, PROVIDING FOR EARLY IDENTIFICATION AND
INTERVENTION, INCLUSIVE AND SPECIALIZED EDUCATION, EMPLOYMENT
AND VOCATIONAL SUPPORT, COMMUNITY-BASED SERVICES, COORDINATED
GOVERNANCE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Constitution mandates the State to protect the rights and promote the welfare of
persons with disabilities and to ensure their full participation in society. Republic Act No. 7277,
as amended, and Republic Act No. 10754 provide the legal foundation for disability rights and
benefits. However, for autistic and neurodivergent Filipinos—particularly children—
implementation remains fragmented and uneven.

        The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), in its 2020 Census of Population and
Housing, reports that 8.7% of Filipinos aged five (5) years and over experience at least one
domain of functional difficulty. UNICEF Philippines (2023–2024) estimates that
approximately 1.6 million Filipino children live with disabilities. Conservative prevalence
benchmarks further indicate that at least 1% of the population—over one million Filipinos—
may be on the autism spectrum. Yet registry data from the National Council on Disability
Affairs (NCDA) as of February 2026 reflect only about 2.5 million registered persons with
disabilities nationwide, without comprehensive national disaggregation for autism. The
disparity between prevalence estimates and formal registration reflects substantial under-
detection and uneven service access.

        Early identification is not systematically integrated into primary healthcare nationwide.
Diagnostic services remain concentrated in urban centers. Educational accommodations vary
across schools and local government units. Structured transition pathways from education to
employment remain limited. Many families shoulder high out-of-pocket therapy costs and
long-term caregiving responsibilities with minimal inter-agency coordination.

       The economic implications are measurable. Using conservative projections based on
PSA 2024 wage data and labor force statistics, productivity losses associated with employment
gaps among autistic adults and reduced workforce participation of caregivers may amount to
approximately ₱50 to ₱60 billion annually, or roughly 0.2% of national GDP. This estimate
excludes additional out-of-pocket household expenditures and long-term social protection
costs. Delayed diagnosis and fragmented services increase lifetime dependency and reduce
economic participation.

       The current fragmented system imposes a recurring and preventable economic burden.
When services are uncoordinated, costs rise. When intervention is delayed, opportunity
narrows. Families absorb expenses that structured systems could reduce, and the nation bears
avoidable productivity losses.

       This proposed measure establishes a National Policy Framework on Autism and
Neurodiversity to align the Department of Health, Department of Education, Commission on
Higher Education, Department of Labor and Employment, TESDA, DSWD, LGUs, and the
Philippine Statistics Authority under a coordinated life-course strategy. It strengthens and
operationalizes the rights guaranteed under Republic Act No. 7277 and Republic Act No. 10754
through measurable standards, inter-agency accountability, and data-driven governance—
without creating duplicative bureaucracy.

       The measure is informed by structured international governance models, including the
coordinated framework adopted by the Government of Dubai for Persons of Determination,
adapting best-practice principles of inter-agency integration and centralized oversight to
Philippine conditions. The use of the term “People of Determination” affirms dignity and
capability while remaining fully consistent with existing disability law.

        More than one million Filipinos may be on the autism spectrum. Over 1.6 million
children live with disabilities. Billions of pesos in productivity are lost each year due to delayed
detection and fragmented support.

        This representation affirms that coordinated governance reduces public costs, while
early intervention strengthens lifetime outcomes and national productivity.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Autism and Neurodiversity
Empowerment Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to recognize,
respect, protect, and promote the rights, dignity, and full participation of autistic persons and
other neurodivergent individuals in society. The State shall adopt a life-course, inclusive, and
evidence-based approach that ensures access to early identification, appropriate education,
meaningful employment, community-based support, and equal opportunities, consistent with
Republic Act No. 7277, as amended, Republic Act No. 10754, and other relevant laws.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
       a) Autism Spectrum Condition refers to a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by
          differences in social communication, behavior, and sensory processing, manifested across
          a spectrum of strengths and support needs;
       b) Neurodiversity refers to the natural variation in neurological development, including but
          not limited to autism spectrum conditions and other neurodevelopmental differences;
       c) Neurodivergent Individuals refer to persons whose neurological development differs from
          prevailing norms and who may require specific support services;
       d) People of Determination refers to autistic persons and other neurodivergent individuals
          who are recognized as persons with disabilities under existing laws. The use of the term
          “People of Determination” in this Act is descriptive and policy-oriented and shall not create
          a new legal classification nor diminish, expand, or otherwise alter any rights, privileges, or
          entitlements provided under Republic Act No. 7277, Republic Act No. 10754, and related
          statutes; and
       e) Early Intervention refers to services provided during early childhood designed to address
          developmental delays and promote optimal cognitive, behavioral, and social development.
SEC. 4. National Policy Framework for Autism and Neurodiversity. – There is hereby
established a National Policy Framework on Autism and Neurodiversity to ensure coordinated,
efficient, and accountable service delivery for People of Determination across all stages of life.

The Framework shall harmonize programs, standards, and services among national government
agencies and local government units (LGUs) and shall promote measurable performance
targets and centralized monitoring.

SEC. 5. Early Identification, Diagnosis, and Intervention. –In order to promote timely
developmental support for People of Determination. the Department of Health (DOH) shall
integrate developmental and autism screening into maternal, newborn, and child health
services, including primary care and barangay-level health programs.

Public health facilities shall be capacitated to conduct screening, referral, and early intervention
services and priority access to intervention services shall be provided to indigent and
financially disadvantaged families.

SEC. 6. Inclusive and Specialized Education. – To ensure equitable access to education for
People of Determination:
   a) The Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on Higher Education
       (CHED) shall ensure inclusive and specialized education responsive to
       neurodevelopmental needs;
   b) Individualized Education Plans (IEPs) shall be developed and implemented for learners
       who are People of Determination in public schools;
   c) Teacher education institutions shall integrate autism and neurodiversity competencies
       into training programs; and
   d) Structured transition planning programs shall be established to support People of
       Determination moving from education to employment or higher learning.

SEC. 7. Employment and Vocational Inclusion. – The Department of Labor and Employment
(DOLE) and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) shall
develop autism-inclusive vocational training and employment pathways.

Employers shall provide reasonable accommodation consistent with Republic Act No. 7277
and other applicable labor laws; and supported employment, job coaching, and workplace
transition mechanisms shall be promoted.

SEC. 8. Community-Based Support and Family Services. – Local government units, in
coordination with the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), shall integrate
autism and neurodiversity considerations into local development plans and social protection
programs for People of Determination.

Community-based services, caregiver education, and referral mechanisms shall be
strengthened and public awareness initiatives shall promote understanding and inclusion of
People of Determination.

SEC. 9. National Autism and Neurodiversity Council. – There is hereby created a National
Autism and Neurodiversity Council to oversee implementation of this Act and advance the
welfare of People of Determination.
The Council shall:
   a) Formulate a National Strategic Action Plan;
   b) Coordinate inter-agency implementation;
   c) Establish monitoring indicators; and
   d) Submit an annual report to Congress.

The Council shall be supported by existing personnel and resources and shall not create
additional plantilla positions unless authorized in the General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 10. Data, Research, and Evidence-Based Policy. – In order to ensure evidence-based
policymaking, effective service delivery, and informed resource allocation under this Act:

   a) The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), in coordination with concerned agencies,
      shall conduct periodic data collection on autism and neurodiversity prevalence and
      service access affecting People of Determination;
   b) Agencies shall maintain disaggregated data consistent with the Data Privacy Act; and
   c) A monitoring and evaluation framework shall guide policy improvements.

SEC. 11. Appropriations. – The funds necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the concerned agencies. Thereafter, such
sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health, in coordination with concerned agencies and
stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
implementation.

SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete publication
in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
