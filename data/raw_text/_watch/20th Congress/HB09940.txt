AN ACT ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL INTEGRATED PROGRAM TO PREVENT
      AND TREAT PSORIASIS AS A PUBLIC HEALTH PROBLEM AND
                APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Psoriasis is widely misunderstood as a mere skin condition. This perception is
consequentially wrong. In truth, psoriasis is a chronic autoimmune disorder caused by
a dysregulation of T-cell activity, which accelerates skin cell turnover and generates
the visible plaques on the body.1 These skin lesions also silently damage the
cardiovascular system, joints, and mental health of the patient.2 It is associated with
serious comorbidities including psoriatic arthritis, depression, and cardiometabolic
disease.3 The skin, in this sense, is not the disease. It is merely where the disease is
noticed.

       It is estimated that between one and two million Filipinos are presently living
with the disease; a possible undercount given the many undiagnosed cases outside of
urban centers.4 The disease is not limited to the elderly. Studies on Filipino patients
show a mean age of onset of 25.9 years old, with patients typically seeking

       Left untreated, psoriasis has severe consequences. Studies found that psoriasis
is associated with a 50% higher relative risk of cardiovascular disease.7 Severe cases
are linked to increased rates of myocardial infarction and stroke even after adjusting
for traditional risk factors.8 Psoriatic arthritis, which develops in approximately one-
third of untreated patients, causes irreversible joint destruction.9 Depression is also a
well-documented comorbidity and has itself been shown to further increase
cardiovascular mortality in patients.10 A Philippine study at Rizal Medical Center found
that 57.4% of patients already had moderate to severe psoriasis, and 63.9% reported
an extremely large effect on their quality of life.11

        Treatment is both lifelong and prohibitively expensive for most Filipinos.
Modern biologic therapies like interleukin inhibitors, which are the international
standard of care for moderate to severe psoriasis, cost upwards of PHP 40,000 per
injection, with multiple injections required per year.12 Yet, a cross-sectional study at
the Southern Philippines Medical Center found that 53% of psoriasis patients had
monthly household incomes below PHP 8,000.13 The same study found a work
impairment rate of 65.4% among patients and reported that 55% had changed
employment status as a direct result of the disease.14

        Because psoriasis is misunderstood as a contagious skin infection, Filipinos
living with it face widespread discrimination in schools, workplaces, and communities.
A 2020 PsorPhil survey of 350 members confirmed significant negative impacts on the
psychological well-being and productivity of Filipino patients.15 This stigma is not
merely a social injustice. It deters patients from seeking care, worsens depression,
and triggers disease flares.

       Despite all this, the Philippines has no dedicated national program for psoriasis:
no structured care pathway, no tailored PhilHealth benefit package, no national patient
registry, and no primary-care treatment guidelines. In 2014, the World Health
Organization formally recognized psoriasis as a serious noncommunicable disease and
called on governments to ensure access to care, essential medicines, and universal
health coverage for patients.16 This bill is the Philippines’ answer to that call.

     For these reasons, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
recommended.

     Congress assembled:

 1         Section 1. Title. - This Act shall be known as the "National Integrated Psoriasis
 2   Prevention and Treatment Program Act.”
 3
 4          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and
 5   promote the right to health of the people, including the right of persons suffering from
 6   psoriasis to survival and full and healthy development as individuals through access
 7   to timely health information and adequate professional medical care. In pursuit of
 8   such policy, and in recognition that psoriasis is a serious noncommunicable disease
 9   (NCD) with no clear cause or cure, the State shall institutionalize a system that is
10   people-centered, comprehensive, integrative and sustainable and shall facilitate
11   collaboration among government and non-government agencies and organizations at
12   the national and local levels, private sector, professional health organizations,
13   academic institutions, communities and families towards the provision of early and
14   sustainable care of persons afflicted with psoriasis. The State recognizes the crucial
15   role of research in defining health programs and activities to address the needs of
16   patients with psoriasis. The State also recognizes that an effective public education
17   program is vital in helping ensure the early diagnosis and treatment of psoriasis and
18   in preventing those afflicted with them from being the subject of ridicule and
19   stigmatization.
20          Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:
 1              a) Carer - refers to anyone who provides care for psoriasis patients and
 2                 family members;
 3              b) National Integrated Psoriasis Prevention and Treatment Program -
 4                 refers to the program of the national government for the prevention
 5                 and treatment of psoriasis in the country; and
 6              c) Psoriasis - refers to an immune-mediated, genetic disease manifesting
 7                 in the skin or joints or both.
 8
 9           Sec. 4. National Integrated Psoriasis Prevention and Treatment Program. –
10   There is hereby established a National Integrated Psoriasis Prevention and Treatment
11   Program (NIPPTP) under the Department of Health (DOH), which shall serve as the
12   framework for all psoriasis-related activities of the government. The NPCCP shall have
13   the following objectives:
14               a) Promote and provide mechanisms for early identification, screening,
15                  and diagnosis of persons suffering from psoriasis;
16               b) Establish an extensive and sustainable health care system integrated
17                  within the public health care delivery system for early and sustainable
18                  care for patients suffering from psoriasis through the provision of
19                  comprehensive care from multidisciplinary teams of specialists,
20                  including dermatologists, rheumatologists, psychologists, psychiatrists,
21                  pediatricians, cardiologists and others;
22               c) Improve the access of psoriasis patients to professional medical care,
23                  and comprehensive, individually adapted treatment;
24               d) Provide referral services to patients for appropriate medical expertise
25                  and to identify medical facilities suited to the needs of patients;
26               e) Maintain an updated list of health care institutions and practitioners
27                  concerned with the treatment of psoriasis;
28               f) Facilitate the manufacture and importation of affordable drugs,
29                  especially those included in the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines,
30                  including systemic therapies;
31               g) Provide regulatory and fiscal incentives to support research and
32                  development studies on psoriasis particularly on the epidemiology,
33                  etiology, treatment and ways to improve health care for psoriasis
34                  patients and collaborate with other institutions for the conduct of
35                  research activities pertaining to the disease;
36               h) Provide an avenue for the sharing of best practices on issues relating
37                  to early detection, prevention, cure, treatment, and rehabilitation of
38                  persons diagnosed with psoriasis;
39               i) Promote an advocacy discouraging bias against and stereotyping of
40                  persons afflicted with psoriasis;
41               j) Establish sustainable support networks and orientation and counselling
42                  services for families of persons diagnosed with psoriasis;
 1              k) Network with other organizations, institutions, and professionals
 2                 working on public programs for persons diagnosed with psoriasis;
 3              l) Integrate public educational and informational campaigns in the current
 4                 programs to identify persons afflicted with psoriasis and help the public
 5                 understand the special needs of such persons;
 6              m) Facilitate the regular collaborative activities among stakeholders
 7                 regarding the realization of the objectives of this Act;
 8              n) Publish a newsletter on current developments and research on
 9                 psoriasis, as well as other reading materials on the early detection,
10                 prevention, and treatment of psoriasis, and best practices for the care
11                 of diagnosed persons;
12              o) Conduct of continuing education and training programs on the proper
13                 care of persons with psoriasis; and
14              p) Establish a databank, information, and monitoring system on local
15                 government unit (LGU)-specific statistics pertaining to psoriasis.

25          Sec. 6. Development of Medical Guidelines for Psoriasis Treatment. - The DOH,
26   in consultation with relevant agencies of government, psoriasis focused professional
27   societies, and patients' organizations, shall spearhead the development of medical
28   guidelines regarding the diagnosis of psoriasis and its treatment and shall implement
29   standards relating to medical care for psoriasis patients such as establishment of
30   objectives of care and treatment plan, adequate assessment of progress of therapy,
31   using uniform tools to assess the severity of the disease and patient quality of life.

33          Sec. 7. Psoriasis Patients as Persons with Disabilities. - Persons living with
34   psoriasis, shall be considered as persons with disability (PWDs) in accordance with
35   Republic Act No. 7277, as amended, otherwise known as the "Magna Carta for
36   Disabled Persons".

41   Republic Act No. 7277, as amended, are granted to them. Further, the Department of
42   Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall adopt programs which promote work and
 1   employment opportunities for persons with psoriasis.

 4   Department of Education (DepEd), Technical Education and Skills Development

 7   workplace and community.

 9          Sec. 10. Establishment of Psoriasis Assistance Fund. - There is hereby
10   established a Psoriasis Assistance Fund to support patients in their medical treatment.
11   The DOH shall manage the Fund in accordance with existing budgeting, accounting
12   and auditing rules and regulations and shall make a quarterly report to the Office of
13   the President and Congress on the disbursement of the Fund. The DOH may solicit
14   and receive donations which shall form part of the Fund and such donations shall be
15   exempt from income and or donor's tax and all other taxes, fees and charges imposed
16   by the government. Likewise, fund raising activities may be conducted and the
17   proceeds of which shall accrue to the Fund and shall be exempt from any and all taxes.
18   Receipts from donations, whether in cash or in kind, shall be accounted for in the
19   books of the donee government agency in accordance with accounting and auditing
20   rules and regulations. The receipts from cash donations and proceeds from sale of
21   donated commodities shall be deposited with the National Treasury and recorded as
22   a special account in the General Fund and shall be available to the implementing
23   agency concerned through a special budget pursuant to Section 35, Chapter 5, Book
24   VI of Executive Order No. 292. The cash value of the donations shall be deemed
25   automatically appropriated for the purpose specified by the donor. Donations with a
26   term not exceeding one (1) year shall be treated as trust receipts.
27
28          The donee-agency concerned shall submit the quarterly reports of all donations
29   received, whether in cash or in kind, and expenditures or disbursements thereon with
30   electronic signature to the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), through
31   the Unified Reporting System, and to the Speaker of the House of the Representatives,
32   the President of the Senate of the Philippines, the House Committee on
33   Appropriations, the Senate Committee on Finance and the Commission on Audit, by

39          Sec. 11. PhilHealth Benefits for Psoriasis. - The Philippine Health Insurance
40   Corporation (PhilHealth) shall expand its benefit packages to include primary care
41   screening, detection, diagnosis, treatment assistance, supportive care, management
42   and follow-up care for all types and severity of psoriasis. It shall also develop
 1   innovative benefits such as support for community-based models of care to improve
 2   psoriasis treatment journey and reduce costs of care. The development or expansion
 3   of any PhilHealth benefit shall go through a proper, transparent and standardized
 4   prioritization setting process, such as the Health Technology Assessment and actuarial
 5   feasibility study, to avoid inequitable allocation of funds for health care services. The
 6   Fund and PhilHealth benefits shall be made available in public and private DOH
 7   licensed facilities. The DOH and PhilHealth shall prescribe, in consultation with
 8   stakeholders, the coverage rates and applicable rules on options to charge co-payment
 9   for services rendered. Processes to avail of such funding shall be streamlined to ensure
10   timely provision of psoriasis care.

12          Sec. 12. Social Protection Mechanisms. - The DOH, in collaboration with the

14   Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO), DOLE, DSWD, PhilHealth, and LGUS
15   shall develop appropriate and easily accessible social protection mechanisms psoriasis
16   patients, their families and carers. It shall aim to encourage for the underprivileged
17   and marginalized people living with psoriasis to undergo the necessary treatment and
18   care.

20         The Insurance Commission shall mandate the Health Maintenance
21   Organizations (HMOs) to cover counseling and testing, psoriasis screening, diagnostics
22   and care as well as certain therapeutics of all member employees.
23

29   packages of PhilHealth while the employees in the formal sector shall be offered cost-
30   sharing PhilHealth benefit packages. Children with Psoriasis shall be given free access
31   to education or scholarship program by the government through the Department of
32   Education (DepEd) and Commission of Higher Education (CHED) until such time that
33   he/she finishes college education.

35           Sec. 13. Psoriasis and Related Supportive Care Medicines. - The DOH, and other
36   concerned government agencies, shall implement reforms supporting early access to
37   essential medicines, innovative medicines and health technologies, to ensure highest
38   possible quality of life among people with psoriasis. The reforms include facilitating
39   quick access to drugs for compassionate use and developing a more responsive system
40   for effectively addressing emergency cases.
41           The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) shall create a dedicated and
42   streamlined process, not exceeding one (1) year, for the licensing of innovator and
 1   generic psoriasis medication, subject to appropriate quality checks and compliance
 2   with minimum standards, such as, but not limited to, being approved and used for
 3   psoriasis treatment in other countries.

 5         The DOH shall ensure sufficient supply of medicines for psoriasis-related care
 6   and management that are available at affordable prices. Further, the DOH shall
 7   formulate a monitoring system to check that psoriasis medications are safe and
 8   administered in correct dosages.

 7          Sec. 23. National Psoriasis Awareness Month. - The month of October of every
