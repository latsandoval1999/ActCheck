                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The increasing frequency of flooding, urban heat, and environmental
 degradation in Philippine cities underscores the urgent need to rethink how
 public infrastructure-particularly road network�is planned and constructed.
 Roads occupy a significant portion of urban land, yet they are traditionally
 designed using "gray infrastructure• approaches that prioritize concrete,
 asphalt, and drainage pipes, often neglecting ecological and climate
 considerations.

       While existing policies and standards of the Department of Public Works
 and Highways (DPWH) already require drainage systems, slope protection, and
environmental safeguards, these measures remain largely engineering-based
and do not fully maximize the benefits of green infrastructure or nature-based
solutions. Current practices tend to treat vegetation, stormwater absorption, and
ecosystem services as optional or secondary features rather than essential
components of infrastructure design.

Appropriations Act (GAA) and climate-related mandates, have begun
encouraging the integration of tree planting, pedestrian-friendly shaded
