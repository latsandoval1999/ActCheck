                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

           The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to protect and promote
   the right to health of the people (Article II, Section 15) and to undertake appropriate
   health human resource development responsive to the country’s needs and problems
   (Article XIII, Section 12).

          Midwives serve as frontline healthcare providers, particularly in primary care
   and community-based settings, where they play a critical role in ensuring safe
   motherhood and improving maternal and neonatal outcomes. They provide essential
   services including prenatal, intrapartum, and postnatal care, family planning, health
   education, and basic emergency obstetric interventions. In many rural and
   underserved areas, midwives are often the first—and sometimes the only—point of
   contact for maternal and child health services.

         Despite their vital contributions, government midwives remain among the
   lowest-compensated health professionals in the public sector. The current salary grade
   does not adequately reflect the responsibilities they carry, the conditions under which
   they work, nor their indispensable role in reducing maternal and infant mortality and
   advancing public health outcomes.

          Moreover, the continued disparity in compensation contributes to difficulties in
   recruitment and retention, particularly in geographically isolated and disadvantaged
   areas. Strengthening the compensation structure for midwives is therefore essential to
   sustaining an effective primary health care system and ensuring equitable access to
   maternal and child health services.

         This measure seeks to upgrade the minimum entry-level salary grade of
   government midwives from Salary Grade 9 to Salary Grade 11. The proposed
   adjustment aims to promote equitable compensation, enhance workforce stability, and
   recognize the critical role midwives play in the health care system.

         Ultimately, this measure affirms the State’s commitment to uphold decent work
   standards in the public health sector and to strengthen the delivery of accessible,
   responsive, and quality health care services for all Filipinos.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
   sought.

                             “AN ACT
            UPGRADING THE MINIMUM SALARY GRADE OF
            GOVERNMENT MIDWIVES FROM SALARY GRADE 9 TO
            SALARY GRADE 11”

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Government
Midwives Salary Upgrading Act of 2026. "

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to ensure adequate and just compensation for government midwives in order
to attract and retain competent professionals in the public health sector, consistent
with the State’s mandate to provide accessible and quality healthcare services to all
Filipinos.

       SECTION 3. Upgrading the Government Midwives' Minimum Salary
Grade Level - The present minimum salary grade of government midwives (Midwife
I) shall be upgraded from Salary Grade 9 to Salary Grade 11.

       The salary grades of midwives occupying higher positions shall be
correspondingly adjusted in accordance with existing compensation and position
classification systems.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all midwives employed in public
health facilities and other health-related institutions owned and operated by the
national government, local government units, and other government
instrumentalities.

     SECTION 5. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s available
appropriations of the Department of Health (DOH) and other concerned agencies.

       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
       SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the Civil Service
Commission (CSC), shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its
effective implementation.

     SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

      SECTION 9. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
