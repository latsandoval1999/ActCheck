                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE


Demolition and eviction remain persistent and systemic threats to underprivileged and
homeless communities. These actions displace vulnerable families, disrupt livelihoods, sever
access to basic services, and dismantle vital social support systems. Rather than resolving
urban development challenges, indiscriminate demolition and eviction often deepen housing
insecurity and exacerbate social tensions by eroding the survival mechanisms of already
marginalized populations. At the same time, rapid urbanization and economic expansion
continue to draw underprivileged groups into urban centers in search of livelihood
opportunities, contributing to the growth of informal settlements and urban encroachment.

The Republic Act No. 7279, otherwise known as the Urban Development and Housing Act
(UDHA) of 1992, mandates the State to provide accessible, affordable, and adequate housing
to underprivileged and homeless citizens, recognizing housing as an essential component of
social justice and human dignity. Section 28 of the Act strictly regulates eviction and
demolition, allowing such actions only as a measure of last resort, after all viable alternatives,
particularly meaningful consultation and the provision of appropriate relocation, have been
fully exhausted.

The Presidential Commission for the Urban Poor (PCUP) is mandated to safeguard the rights
and welfare of underprivileged and homeless citizens by ensuring that eviction and
demolition activities are conducted in a just, humane, and lawful manner. In accordance with
Section 28 of the UDHA, the PCUP facilitates mandatory consultations and monitors
compliance with statutory safeguards, including the provision of adequate notice, the proper
identification and presence of authorized officials, the humane conduct of demolition
activities, and the availability of suitable relocation sites. While the PCUP does not approve
or disapprove demolition activities, it documents, reports, and elevates instances of
non-compliance and procedural deficiencies.

Despite these statutory safeguards, eviction and demolition activities frequently proceed
without a unified mechanism to ensure full compliance with Section 28, resulting in
fragmented implementation and weakened protection for affected communities. This situation
effectively undermines the safeguard mechanisms established under the UDHA. It is within
this context that vesting the sole clearing house function and authority in the PCUP becomes
imperative to ensure uniform adherence to the law and to prevent unjust and inhumane
displacement of underprivileged and homeless citizens.
Moreover, effective protection under the UDHA necessarily extends beyond the regulation of
eviction and demolition to encompass the entire resettlement process. To prevent relocation
from merely transferring displacement from one site to another or compelling affected
families to re-encroach in urbanized areas due to the absence of livelihood opportunities and
basic services it is essential to strengthen the role of the PCUP in monitoring
pre-resettlement, actual resettlement, and post-resettlement activities. Enhanced monitoring
ensures that relocation is adequately planned, lawfully implemented, and sustainable, with
due regard to access to basic services, livelihood opportunities, and the preservation of
community networks.

Equally critical is the legal status of urban poor organizations. At present, many urban poor
organizations often organized as neighborhood or community associations lack juridical
personality. Existing policy frameworks typically require registration with the Department of
Human Settlements and Urban Development (DSHUD) pursuant to the Magna Carta for
Homeowners’ Associations, based on the presumption that their primary purpose is land
acquisition or housing development. This framework has produced unintended consequences,
as registration is frequently limited to housing-related contexts and often contingent upon
agreements with property owners. As a result, many legitimate urban poor organizations
remain unregistered and without legal recognition, despite the fact that their concerns extend
beyond housing to include livelihood, access to basic services, health, education, disaster risk
reduction, and broader community development.

The absence of juridical personality has constrained these organizations from effectively
representing their members, entering into lawful agreements, receiving grants or assistance,
and partnering with government agencies and development institutions. This legal and
institutional gap undermines the constitutional guarantee of participatory governance and
weakens the State’s capacity to implement inclusive, rights-based, and sustainable urban
development interventions.

In view thereof, this measure seeks to: (1) vest the PCUP with the sole clearing house
function to ensure uniform compliance with Section 28 of the UDHA; (2) strengthen the
PCUP’s authority to monitor pre-resettlement, actual resettlement, and post-resettlement
activities whenever relocation of underprivileged and homeless citizens occurs; and (3) vest
in the PCUP the authority to register urban poor organizations, enabling them to obtain legal
recognition appropriate to their sectoral role and social function, without being unnecessarily
constrained by registration frameworks designed primarily for housing or land development.

“AN ACT STRENGTHENING AND INSTITUTIONALIZING THE PRESIDENTIAL
COMMISSION FOR THE URBAN POOR (PCUP), EXPANDING ITS MANDATE
AND ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE, CREATING ADDITIONAL PLANTILLA
POSITIONS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR”

SECTION 1. Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Presidential Commission for the
Urban Poor (PCUP) Strengthening Act.”


SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy.
It is hereby declared the policy of the State to uphold the rights, dignity, and welfare of
underprivileged and homeless citizens and to promote social justice through humane, lawful,
and sustainable urban development, consistent with Republic Act No. 7279, otherwise known
as the Urban Development and Housing Act of 1992. Eviction and demolition shall be
undertaken only as a measure of last resort and strictly in accordance with the safeguards
prescribed under Section 28 thereof, and the State shall ensure the uniform and effective
implementation of such safeguards to prevent unjust and inhumane displacement. To this end,
institutional mechanisms shall be strengthened, including the designation of the Presidential
Commission for the Urban Poor as the primary clearing house for eviction and demolition
activities, to ensure compliance with mandatory consultation, adequate notice, humane
conduct, and the provision of suitable relocation. The State further recognizes that effective
protection under the Act extends beyond eviction and demolition to include the entire
resettlement process; thus, relocation shall be adequately planned, lawfully implemented, and
sustainably supported, with due regard to access to basic services, livelihood opportunities,
and the preservation of community networks, and monitoring of pre-resettlement, actual
resettlement, and post-resettlement activities shall be strengthened to prevent repeated
displacement and urban re-encroachment. The State likewise affirms the role of urban poor
organizations in participatory governance and inclusive development and shall ensure that
such organizations are afforded appropriate legal recognition consistent with their sectoral
role and social function, without being unduly constrained by registration frameworks
designed primarily for housing or land development, in furtherance of inclusive, rights-based,
and sustainable urban development.
SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following are defined
as follows:

   a. Administrative Bodies Exercising Quasi-Judicial Functions – refers to government
      agencies, commissions, boards, or tribunals that are primarily administrative in nature
   but are empowered by law to hear and decide disputes, adjudicate claims, determine
   rights, impose sanctions, or issue orders in specific matters within their jurisdiction, in
   a manner similar to courts, subject to applicable procedural and substantive rules of
   law.

b.​ Alliance of Urban Poor Associations- refers to a duly organized and
    community-based network or coalition composed of at least two (2) accredited Urban
    Poor Associations within the same city, municipality, or province, formed to
    coordinate programs, strengthen collective advocacy, and advance the common
    interests, housing security, human development, and socio-economic welfare of their
    member associations and communities.

c.​ PCUP-Facilitated Dialogue refers to a non-adversarial, consultative, and
    problem-solving process convened and facilitated by the Presidential Commission for
    the Urban Poor, through which parties to a dispute or issue involving underprivileged
    and homeless citizens are assisted in communicating, clarifying concerns, and
    exploring mutually acceptable solutions, without the Commission acting as a neutral
    adjudicator or arbitrator, and without prejudice to the voluntary resort to other
    remedies available under existing laws.

d.​ Adequate Housing and Relocation — refers to relocation or resettlement that
    complies with the standards of adequacy under Republic Act No. 7279 of 1992 and
    international human rights principles recognized by the Philippines, including security
    of tenure, availability of basic services and facilities, affordability, habitability,
    accessibility, location with access to livelihood opportunities, and cultural adequacy.
    Relocation shall not result in homelessness, repeated displacement, or deprivation of
    access to essential services.

e.​ Clearing House — refers to the centralized compliance verification and inter-agency
    coordination mechanism exercised by the PCUP to document and confirm adherence
    to the safeguards prescribed under Section 28 of Republic Act No. 7279 of 1992 prior
    to the implementation of demolition or eviction activities affecting underprivileged
    and homeless citizens.

f.​ Community Development — refers to sustained, participatory, and integrated
    interventions aimed at improving the socio-economic conditions of urban poor
    communities, including access to adequate housing, basic services, livelihood
    opportunities, disaster risk reduction and management consistent with Republic Act
    No. 10121 of 2010, and strengthening of community institutions, in coordination with
    concerned national government agencies and local government units pursuant to
    Republic Act No. 7160 of 1991;

g.​ Compliance Certificate — refers to the written confirmation issued by the PCUP
    stating that the mandatory requirements and statutory safeguards under Section 28 of
    Republic Act No. 7279 of 1992 have been complied with.

h.​ Constructive Eviction – refers to any act or series of acts committed by any person,
    including but not limited to the construction or placement of fences, barriers, debris,
    or any other materials around or within an area occupied by informal settlers while
   such settlers remain in actual possession thereof, which results in their isolation or the
   restriction of their free and lawful ingress and egress.

   It shall likewise include acts committed after the occurrence of a fire or similar
   calamity, whereby affected informal settlers are prevented from returning to,
   accessing, clearing, rebuilding, repairing, or otherwise re-entering the area they
   previously occupied, through enclosure, barricading, or any other means that
   effectively excludes them while the issue of legal possession has not been finally
   resolved by a competent court.

   Constructive eviction shall further include acts that create hazardous or unsafe living
   conditions, including the obstruction of access for ambulances, fire trucks, or other
   emergency responders, or the limitation of informal settlers’ ability to enter or exit the
   area for purposes of employment, education, medical care, or other essential daily
   activities;

i.​ Demolition — refers to the dismantling by the Local Government Unit (LGU), or any
    legally authorized agency of government, of all structures within the premises subject
    to clearing;

j.​ Eviction — refers to the removal of persons and their belongings from a subject
    structure or area, or both, in accordance with law;

i.​ Family – refers to persons related by blood, marriage, adoption, or recognized
    common-law union who constitute a single domestic unit by reason of legal or natural
    ties. A family shall be treated as a distinct and independent unit for purposes of this
    Act, notwithstanding that it resides in the same dwelling or shares common
    arrangements for cooking, food consumption, or other basic living expenses with
    another family.

j.​ Federation of Urban Poor Associations- refers to a duly organized and registered
    federation composed of at least five (5) accredited Urban Poor Associations or duly
    recognized Alliances of Urban Poor Associations representing at least two (2) cities or
    municipalities, established with a formal governance structure, constitution and
    by-laws, and elected officers, for the purpose of providing consolidated
    representation, policy advocacy, program coordination, and negotiation at the
    provincial, regional, or national level.

k.​ Key Shelter Agencies — refer to the Department of Human Settlements and Urban
    Development (DHSUD), the National Housing Authority (NHA), the Social Housing
    Finance Corporation (SHFC), and other government instrumentalities primarily
    tasked with housing and resettlement programs.


l.​ Land Appropriate for Adequate Housing - refer to a land that is suitable for
    residential use and has reasonable access to essential services and facilities, including
    at least secondary-level schools, public markets, health centers, and available
    livelihood opportunities.

m.​ Limited Juridical Personality — refers to the authority granted by the PCUP to
    registered urban poor organizations for purposes of sectoral representation and
   community development participation, without             substituting   for   registration

n.​ National Eviction, Demolition, and Resettlement Registry — refers to the
    centralized database maintained by the PCUP containing data on eviction and
    demolition activities, compliance status under Section 28 of Republic Act No. 7279,
    relocation outcomes, and post-resettlement conditions, subject to applicable data
    privacy laws.

o.​ Resettlement — refers to the comprehensive process of transferring underprivileged
    and homeless citizens from their place of origin to a relocation site, including housing
    provision, service integration, and post-relocation support consistent with Republic
    Act No. 7279.

p.​ Relocation Readiness — refers to the verified availability of housing units, access to
    basic services, site safety, and minimum standards necessary to ensure lawful and
    humane transfer of affected families.

q.​ Social Preparation — refers to the comprehensive and participatory process of
    organizing, consulting, informing, and capacitating affected underprivileged and
    homeless citizens prior to demolition, eviction, relocation, or resettlement, consistent
    with Section 28 of Republic Act No. 7279, Executive Order No. 69 (s. 2012), and
    participatory governance principles under the 1987 Constitution. It includes
    community organizing, people’s planning, capacity-building, livelihood readiness,
    and conflict prevention mechanisms to ensure informed decision-making and
    sustainable resettlement outcomes;


r.​ Underprivileged and homeless citizens” - refers to the beneficiaries of this act and
    to individuals or families family income falls below the National Economic And
    Development Authority [NEDA] -set poverty threshold and/or those who cannot
    sustainably meet their minimum basic needs for food, health, education, housing, and
    other amenities and who do not own residential land or land appropriate for adequate
    housing facilities purposes. This shall include those who live in makeshift dwelling

s.​ Urban Poor - Refer to individuals or families residing in urban or urbanizable areas

    Economic And Development Authority [NEDA] -set poverty threshold and/or
    those who cannot sustainably meet their minimum basic needs for food, health,

   This shall include those living in makeshift dwelling units, informal settlements, or

SECTION 4. Composition, Functions, Powers of the Officials, and Organizational
Structure of the Commission. — The PCUP shall operate in accordance with Republic Act
No. 12231 of 2025, otherwise known as the Government Optimization Act, and other
applicable laws, rules, and issuances governing the organization, rationalization, and
optimization of the Executive Branch.

(a)​ Composition

       The Commission shall be composed of five (5) Members appointed by the President
       of the Philippines, one of whom shall be designated as Chairperson.

       In appointing the Members of the Commission, due consideration shall be given to
       demonstrated competence, integrity, experience in urban poor affairs, community
       development, housing, social protection, public administration, or law, and
       commitment to social justice principles under Article XIII of the 1987 Constitution.

(b)​Functions of the Commission

       The Commission shall continue to exercise its mandate under Executive Order No. 82
       s. 1986 and current functions conferred under Republic Act 7279, Executive Order 69
       s. 2012, Executive Order 152 s. 2002 as amended and other laws and issuances. In
       addition thereto, the Presidential Commission for the Urban Poor (PCUP) shall have
       the following powers and functions:

   1.​ Register urban poor associations, federations, and alliances, and confer upon them
       limited juridical personality in accordance with this Act, without prejudice to the
       registration requirements of other competent agencies exercising exclusive or primary
       jurisdiction.

   2.​ Serve as the clearinghouse for all demolition and eviction cases involving
       underprivileged and homeless families; supervise, convene, and facilitate
       Pre-Demolition Conferences (PDCs); monitor and validate compliance of local
       government units, project proponents, and other government instrumentalities with
       Republic Act No. 7279 and related laws, rules, and regulations; and issue compliance
       certificates as a prerequisite to the granting of authorized police assistance prior to
       any demolition or eviction.

   3.​ Formulate, recommend, and implement policies, standards, and guidelines on urban
       poor development, welfare, protection, and participation in governance; serve as the
       government’s principal policy research and advisory body on urban poor concerns;
       generate data and evidence-based recommendations; and participate as a regular
       member or resource institution in policy discussions and deliberations of the
       Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD), Key Shelter
       Agencies (KSAs), Local Housing Boards (LHBs), and other similar bodies.

   4.​ Strengthen coordination with local government units and national government
       agencies; provide technical assistance, advisory support, and mediation services to
       prevent unlawful, premature, or violent evictions and demolitions; request assistance,
       data, and necessary information from any government agency or instrumentality; and
       ensure the effective implementation of flagship programs and community-based
       initiatives aligned with inclusive, resilient, and sustainable urban development.

   5.​ Exercise such other powers as may be necessary or incidental to the effective
       discharge of its mandate under this Act.

(c)​ Powers of Officials

   i.​ The Chairperson

   The Chairperson shall have the rank of Chairperson IV and shall receive compensation
   and benefits equivalent to an Undersecretary, subject to Republic Act No. 6758, as
   amended, and other applicable compensation laws. The Chairperson shall serve as
   Presiding Officer of the Commission En Banc and, concurrently, as Chief Executive
   Officer and Head of Agency.
   As Presiding Officer, the Chairperson shall convene and preside over meetings and
   deliberations of the Commission En Banc, ensure the orderly conduct of its proceedings,
   approve the agenda in coordination with the Members, sign and certify resolutions,
   decisions, and other official acts of the Commission, and perform such other functions as
   may be necessary to effectively exercise the Commission’s collegial authority.
   As Chief Executive Officer and Head of Agency, the Chairperson shall exercise general
   supervision and control over the operations, personnel, regional offices, and programs of
   the Commission in accordance with applicable laws; ensure the faithful implementation
   of resolutions, decisions, and policies adopted by the Commission En Banc: Provided,
   that such implementation shall not diminish or restrict the Chairperson’s powers and
   functions as Chief Executive Officer under existing laws; represent the Commission in
   national and international meetings, hearings, and proceedings, including those before
   Congress, constitutional bodies, boards, committees, and task forces; appoint officials and
   personnel of the Commission, except those whose appointment is expressly vested in the
   President or other authorities under existing law; approve the annual work and financial
   plan, proposed budget, and major programs prior to submission to the appropriate
   authorities; and perform such other executive and administrative functions as may be
   provided by law or necessary to carry out the Commission’s mandate.

    ii. Commission Member III (Commissioner). — The four (4) Commissioners shall
        receive compensation equivalent to that of an Assistant Secretary, subject to existing
        compensation laws. Each Commissioner shall be assigned geographic oversight over
        the National Capital Region (NCR), Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao, respectively, and
        shall assist in policy formulation and strategic planning; monitoring of clearinghouse
        compliance with Section 28 of Republic Act No. 7279; oversight of social preparation
        and community development programs; inter-agency and local government unit
       coordination pursuant to Republic Act No. 7160 and Republic Act No. 11201; and
       supervision of regional implementation within their respective assigned clusters.

       Geographic assignments shall not diminish the collegial authority of the Commission.
       All major decisions, regulatory issuances, and strategic actions shall require approval
       by the Commission En Banc: Provided, that matters falling within the exclusive
       authority of the Chairperson, in the exercise of his or her powers as Chief Executive
       Officer and Head of Agency, shall not be subject to En Banc approval. Central Office
       Structure

       The PCUP Central Office shall serve as the principal policy, legal, regulatory,
       administrative, and oversight arm of the Commission and shall be composed of the
       following key plantilla positions:

           1.​ Executive Assistant VI (co-terminus) – Provides executive, technical, and
               strategic support to the Chairperson and Commission En Banc, including
               coordination of inter-agency engagements, policy tracking, and preparation of
               official communications and reports;
           2.​ Director III – Serves as Chief Legal Counsel and head of the Legal Services,
               providing legal advisory services, overseeing compliance verification under
               Section 28 of Republic Act No. 7279, reviewing applications for compliance
               certification, and ensuring adherence to due process and applicable laws;
           3.​ Director III for Policy, Research and Development – Leads the formulation of
               evidence-based policy recommendations, legislative proposals, and research
               initiatives, and maintains data systems to support strategic planning and urban
               poor policy development;
           4.​ Director III for Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation – Oversees results-based
               planning, compliance monitoring, performance measurement, impact
               evaluation, and development of information management systems to ensure
               effective implementation of the Commission’s mandate; and
           5.​ Director III for Administrative and Finance Services – Manages cash,
               budgeting, accounting, procurement, human resource administration, records
               management, and internal administrative operations in accordance with civil
               service, DBM, and COA regulations;

(d)​Regional Offices.

   The Presidential Commission for the Urban Poor (PCUP) shall maintain Regional Offices
   for the National Capital Region (NCR), Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao to ensure
   effective, decentralized, and responsive implementation of its mandate, consistent with
   applicable laws. The Regional Offices shall serve as the operational arm of the
   Commission and shall execute and implement its programs, policies, and field functions
   within their respective territorial jurisdictions.

   Each Regional Office shall be headed by a Director III, in accordance with civil service
   laws and regulations. Regional Directors shall operate under the administrative
   supervision and control of the Chairperson and shall coordinate with the Commissioner
   assigned to the corresponding geographic cluster, without diminishing the authority of the
   Chairperson as Chief Executive Officer.
   As the operational arm of the Commission, the Regional Offices shall carry out field
   operations, including the conduct of Pre-Demolition Conferences (PDCs) and compliance
   verification pursuant to Section 28 of Republic Act No. 7279; implementation of social
   preparation, people’s planning, and community development programs; coordination with
   local government units, national government agencies, and other stakeholders;
   participation in Local Housing Boards and similar bodies; provision of technical
   assistance and mediation services; monitoring of pre-relocation, relocation, and
   post-relocation conditions; processing of accreditation or registration applications of
   urban poor organizations subject to Central Office review; and submission of periodic
   operational and compliance reports to ensure alignment with national policies, standards,
   and directives.

(e)​ Organizational Authority.

   To effectively discharge its expanded mandate, the Presidential Commission for the
   Urban Poor (PCUP) is hereby authorized, subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations,
   to rationalize and strengthen its organizational structure; create and fill such technical,
   legal, research, monitoring, administrative, and community development positions as may
   be necessary to carry out its functions; establish, merge, reorganize, or maintain regional
   and field offices as operationally required; and adopt and institutionalize modern
   information management, data banking, and compliance tracking systems to promote
   transparency, efficiency, and accountability, consistent with the policies and standards set
   forth under Republic Act No. 11032.

SECTION 5. PCUP Clearing House Authority for Demolition and Eviction.

    In the exercise of its clearing house function, the PCUP shall:

   1.​ Review and verify documentary compliance with the requirements of Section 28 of
       Republic Act No. 7279 and its implementing rules and regulations;

   2.​ Confirm the conduct of meaningful consultation and the issuance of the required
       notices;

   3.​ Validate the availability and readiness of relocation sites, or where applicable, the
       provision of financial assistance; and

   4.​ Coordinate with the LGU, the Philippine National Police (PNP), the court sheriff, and
       other relevant agencies to harmonize implementation consistent with law.

    All government agencies, including local government units (LGUs), government-owned
    or -controlled corporations (GOCCs), and other proponents of demolition or eviction
    activities, shall, prior to implementation, secure from the Presidential Commission for
    the Urban Poor (PCUP) the prescribed checklist, applicable guidelines, and a
    Compliance Certificate, to be issued by the PCUP Chairperson, who may, in appropriate
    cases, delegate such function to the Regional Directors.

Administrative demolitions or evictions, including those undertaken in the exercise of
quasi-judicial, regulatory, or police powers, as well as voluntary or summary removals, shall
strictly comply with Section 28 of Republic Act No. 7279 and other applicable laws prior to
implementation.
In cases of court-ordered demolition or eviction, the sheriff or other officer duly authorized
by the court to implement the judgment shall, prior to execution, formally request the conduct
of a Pre-Demolition Conference (PDC) from the PCUP and secure a Compliance Certificate
from the PCUP Chairperson, or from the Regional Director if such authority has been
delegated, certifying compliance with the applicable legal requirements.

Prior to the issuance of Compliance Certificates or the endorsement of any request for
authorized police assistance, the PCUP shall, as far as practicable, convene and preside over a
Pre-Demolition Conference (PDC) within fifteen (15) days from the filing of the application,
ensuring meaningful consultation and participation of the affected families and other relevant
stakeholders. The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the concerned
local government units (LGUs), including barangays, are enjoined to assist the PCUP in
facilitating the PDC, including ensuring that an appropriate venue is made available within
the prescribed period, the proper service of notices to affected families, and the provision of
any other assistance deemed appropriate to effectively carry out the purpose of the PDC.

Any compromise reached during the PDC, whether resulting in a full or partial waiver of
rights, that is voluntarily agreed upon and based on the informed consent of the parties, shall
be duly considered by the PCUP in the issuance of the Compliance Certificate.

Police assistance in the conduct of demolition or eviction activities shall be extended only
upon certification by the PCUP that full compliance with the requirements of Section 28 of
Republic Act No. 7279, its implementing rules and regulations, and all PCUP-issued
guidelines, including the prescribed checklist and compliance certificate, has been
established.

Failure by any proponent of a demolition or eviction activity to submit the required checklist
to the Presidential Commission for the Urban Poor (PCUP), or the submission of a false or
fraudulent checklist, shall make the responsible government official or local chief executive
liable to disciplinary action under Section 61 of Republic Act No. 7160 and other applicable
laws, rules, and regulations, without prejudice to separate civil or criminal proceedings.
Similarly, any PCUP official who causes or allows the endorsement of a demolition or
eviction activity without a completed or duly certified checklist, or any Philippine National
Police (PNP) officer who participates in or assists in such activity without prior PCUP
authorization, shall likewise be subject to administrative disciplinary action, in addition to
potential civil or criminal liability as may be warranted.

SECTION 6. Mandatory Housing Allocation for Court-Ordered Demolition and
Eviction.

In order to ensure compliance with the State’s obligations under Republic Act No. 7279 to
provide adequate housing and relocation for underprivileged and homeless citizens affected
by eviction and demolition, all key shelter agencies are hereby mandated to allocate at least
ten percent (10%) of their annual housing production, whether through in-city, near-city, or
off-city housing programs, for families affected by court-ordered demolition and eviction.
Similarly, all local government units (LGUs) shall annually appropriate funds for the
acquisition of land and the development of resettlement sites for their informal
settler-families, in accordance with their powers and responsibilities under existing laws.

The beneficiaries under this section shall be limited to underprivileged and homeless citizens
who are directly affected by court-issued writs of demolition or eviction and who have been
identified, profiled, and endorsed by the PCUP after due validation and social preparation.
The PCUP shall coordinate with the Department of Human Settlements and Urban
Development (DHSUD), the National Housing Authority (NHA), and other key shelter
agencies, as well as with LGUs, to ensure the timely inclusion of qualified beneficiaries in
housing programs, monitor compliance with this section, and submit periodic reports to the
appropriate oversight bodies.

Nothing in this section shall be construed to diminish or impair the obligation of local
government units (LGUs) to provide relocation, resettlement, or financial assistance to
affected families under Republic Act No. 7279 and other applicable laws.

SECTION 7. National Eviction, Demolition, and Resettlement Registry. - The PCUP
shall establish, maintain, and regularly update a centralized national registry of eviction,
demolition, and resettlement cases involving underprivileged and homeless families.

The registry shall include, but not be limited to:

   1.​ Data on pending, ongoing, and completed demolition and eviction activities;
   2.​ Documentation of compliance with Section 28 of Republic Act No. 7279 and its
       implementing rules and regulations;
   3.​ Information on relocation site readiness and availability;
   4.​ Resettlement outcomes, including housing occupancy status;
   5.​ Post-relocation conditions, including access to basic services, livelihood integration,
       and community sustainability indicators.

All concerned government agencies, local government units, key shelter agencies, and
proponents of demolition or relocation projects shall provide relevant information necessary
for maintaining the registry, subject to applicable data privacy laws and confidentiality
safeguards.

The PCUP shall submit an annual report to Congress and the Office of the President
summarizing compliance trends, systemic gaps, and policy recommendations arising from the
registry data.

SECTION 8. Assessment and Monitoring of All Government Housing Projects. — The
PCUP shall assess and evaluate the readiness, adequacy, and sustainability of all government
housing and resettlement projects intended for the urban poor. For this purpose, the PCUP
shall:
    (a) Assess and ensure the existence, completeness, and implementation of relocation and
    resettlement action plans prior to project execution;
    (b) Assist the DHSUD and other concerned agencies in coordination between sending
    and receiving LGUs in the planning, coordination, and implementation of relocation and
    resettlement activities, including inter-local government arrangements, social
    preparation, and beneficiary profiling;
    (c) Monitor and ensure compliance with standards of adequate housing, disaster risk
    reduction and preparedness, site readiness, and long-term community development;
    (d) Facilitate and assist coordination with DHSUD, NHA, the Department of Social
    Welfare and Development (DSWD), the Department of the Interior and Local
    Government (DILG), and other concerned agencies to ensure the timely provision of
    basic and essential services, including potable water, power supply, livelihood
    opportunities, education, and health services; and
    (e) Ensure and facilitate meaningful consultation with affected families and their active
    participation in the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of relocation
    and resettlement programs.

To resolve issues affecting relocated and resettled communities, the PCUP may convene and
facilitate inter-agency or tri-sectoral meetings, dialogues, and other coordination mechanisms.

SECTION 9. Enhanced Social Preparation and Community Development. — Consistent
with Executive Order No. 69 (s. 2012), the PCUP shall institutionalize and lead a holistic,
sustainable, and whole-of-nation approach to social preparation for underprivileged and
homeless citizens affected by demolition, eviction, relocation, and resettlement.
For projects implemented by national government agencies, the PCUP shall assume primary
responsibility for social preparation, in coordination with concerned agencies, LGUs, the
private sector, and civil society organizations. Social preparation shall include, but not be
limited to:
(a) Meaningful consultations with affected families and stakeholders;
(b) Information, education, and communication campaigns on rights, obligations, and project
    impacts;
(c) People’s planning and community organizing;
(d Capability-building, entrepreneurial and livelihood readiness and sustainability; and
(e) Multi-sectoral dialogues and conflict prevention mechanisms.

SECTION 10. PCUP-Facilitated Dialogue.
The Presidential Commission for the Urban Poor (PCUP) shall exercise preventive and
facilitative functions to prevent, manage, and assist in the resolution of disputes involving
underprivileged and homeless citizens, particularly those arising from eviction, demolition,
relocation, resettlement, land acquisition, and the planning, implementation, or development
of housing and urban projects, whether on public or privately owned land.
For this purpose, the PCUP may convene and facilitate PCUP-Facilitated Dialogues among
affected communities, local government units, national government agencies, project
proponents, private entities, and other stakeholders, with the objective of promoting fair,
timely, and mutually acceptable solutions consistent with Republic Act No. 7279 and other
applicable laws.
A compromise agreement voluntarily reached in the course of a PCUP-Facilitated Dialogue,
signed by all parties having rights, interests, or obligations related to the project or land
in question and attested by the PCUP, shall have the same enforceability as a compromise
agreement under the Lupon ng Tagapamayapa mechanism and may be recorded and enforced
in accordance with existing laws. The exercise of PCUP-Facilitated Dialogue under this
section shall be non-adversarial and without prejudice to the rights of parties to avail
themselves of judicial or administrative remedies under existing laws.
SECTION 11. Registration of Urban Poor Organizations. — The PCUP is hereby
authorized to register urban poor organizations, federations, and similar groupings, and to
confer upon them limited juridical personality for purposes of sectoral representation and
community development.
Registered organizations may, consistent with this Act:
    (a) represent their members in government consultations and proceedings;
    (b) participate in policy and program processes affecting the urban poor;
    (c) receive and manage lawful grants and assistance;
    (d) enter into contracts and agreements; and
    (e) sue and be sued.
Registration with the PCUP shall not be construed as a substitute for, nor shall it prejudice,
registration or compliance with other government agencies when the matter involved falls
within the exclusive or primary jurisdiction of such agencies. In particular, urban poor
organizations shall be required to register with the DHSUD in cases involving housing, land
development, land acquisition, or related matters; with the Department of Labor and
Employment (DOLE) when organized as labor organizations or workers’ associations; with
the Cooperative Development Authority (CDA) when organized as cooperatives; or with the
Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) when organized as non-stock or non-profit
corporations, associations, or foundations; or with other government agencies within their
respective exclusive jurisdiction, as may be applicable.
The PCUP shall promulgate the rules and regulations governing the registration, suspension,
or cancellation of registration of urban poor organizations, consistent with due process,
transparency, and the policy of promoting meaningful participation of the urban poor in
governance.
SECTION 12. Policy Recommendation. — The PCUP, through the Commission En Banc
composed of the Chairperson and four (4) Commissioners, shall exercise collegial authority
in the formulation, approval, and adoption of policies, resolutions, and regulatory issuances
necessary to carry out its mandate under this Act, Republic Act No. 7279, Executive Order
No. 69 (s. 2012), and other applicable laws.

In furtherance of the State policy under Article XIII of the 1987 Constitution and Republic
Act No. 7279, the Commission shall:

    (a) Develop and adopt policy recommendations for submission to the Office of the
    President, Congress, DHSUD, and other relevant national government agencies and local
    government units;
    (b) Issue guidelines, standards, compliance frameworks, and operational procedures
    governing the exercise of its clearing house authority, social preparation functions,
    monitoring mechanisms, and registration of urban poor organizations;
    (c) Recommend legislative and administrative measures to strengthen the protection of
    underprivileged and homeless citizens, including amendments to existing housing, urban
    development, and social protection laws;
    (d) Establish institutional performance standards, key performance indicators (KPIs),
    and accountability mechanisms consistent with the Republic Act No. 11032 (Ease of
    Doing Business Act), and applicable civil service and audit regulations;
    (e) Submit annual and special reports to the Office of the President and Congress on the
    state of eviction, demolition, relocation, and resettlement activities nationwide, including
    compliance assessments and policy gaps.

All major policy issuances and regulatory guidelines shall be adopted by majority vote of the
Commission En Banc and shall be published or disseminated in accordance with existing
laws to ensure transparency, accountability, and public participation.
SECTION 13. Participation in Housing and Urban Development Bodies. — The PCUP
shall be a regular member of key shelter agencies and inter-agency bodies involved in
housing, resettlement, and urban development, including those under the DHSUD.
SECTION 14. Automatic Membership in Local Housing Boards. — The PCUP, through
its duly authorized representative, shall be an ex officio member of all Local Housing Boards,
Local Urban Development and Housing Boards, or similar bodies created by LGUs pursuant
to Republic Act No. 7279 and related laws. In such capacity, the PCUP shall participate in the
formulation, review, and approval of local housing, resettlement, relocation, and
demolition-related policies, programs, and projects involving underprivileged and homeless
citizens, and shall ensure compliance with social preparation, consultation, and other
safeguards mandated by law.
SECTION 15. Inter-Agency Coordination. — All government agencies whose programs
affect the urban poor shall coordinate with the PCUP in the planning, implementation,
monitoring, and evaluation of such programs and shall participate in consultations and
tri-sectoral dialogues as may be necessary.
SECTION 16. Appropriations. — For the initial implementation and transition of this Act,
the amount of Sixty Million Pesos (P60,000,000.00) is hereby appropriated, which shall be
sourced from the Contingent Fund or other available savings as determined by the
Department of Budget and Management (DBM). This initial funding shall cover the
establishment and operationalization of PCUP offices, creation of additional plantilla
positions, and other start-up requirements. Funding for subsequent years shall be provided in
the annual General Appropriations Act, based on the final determination of the DBM in
accordance with the approved organizational structure, manpower complement, and other
incidental expenses, as may be approved by Congress.
SECTION 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the PCUP, in coordination with concerned agencies, shall promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations of this Act.
SECTION 18. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid, the
remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.
SECTION 19. Repealing Clause. — All laws, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
SECTION 20. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
