                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to further strengthen and modernize Republic Act No. 8525,
otherwise known as the “Adopt-a-School Act of 1998”, by addressing long-standing
implementation gaps and enhancing incentives to mobilize greater private sector
participation in public education.

       Findings from the Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM
II) reveal that from 2018 to 2023, only four hundred and seventy six (476) tax
endorsement claims were recorded under the Adopt-a-School Program (ASP),
equivalent to an estimated cumulative Php 1.8 billion in tax-supported assistance1.
More notably, the number of endorsements declined over the years: from a hundred
and forty five (145) in 2018 to sixty-six (66) in 2023, with even lower figures in the
intervening years.

       Consultations conducted by EDCOM II point to the lack of a streamlined
process and the inconvenience faced by private sector partners as key factors behind
the low and decreasing uptake. Bureaucratic delays in tax claim processing, unclear
guidelines on allowable forms of assistance, limited staffing at the ASP National
Secretariat, and the absence of a simplified, business-friendly system have
discouraged participation. Many private entities continue to support public schools
voluntarily, even without availing of tax incentives, indicating that the incentive
framework itself is not being maximized due to procedural inefficiencies.

       In contrast, Brigada Eskwela generated approximately Php 4.8 billion in
financial and in-kind support in 2023 alone. This is 11.5 times more than the
resources supported through ASP tax claims that year, showing the strong
willingness of the private sector to contribute when participation is straightforward
and accessible.

Adopt-a-School One-Stop Shop and online portal to streamline applications and
processing; mandating clearer identification of priority needs; strengthening
inter-agency coordination; and enhancing incentives, including an additional Fifty
percent (50%) deduction on qualified expenses and a Twenty percent (20%)
additional deduction for hiring public Senior High School graduates.

       By simplifying processes and making participation more accessible, this bill
aims to reverse the declining trend in tax endorsements and unlock sustained,
strategic private sector investment in public education.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

      SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, CHED, TESDA, DTI and DOLE in
consultation with the DOF, including the BIR, and the BOC, and the Early
Childhood Care and Development (ECCD) Council Secretariat, shall formulate the
implementing rules and regulations to implement this Act.

        SEC. 11. Transitory Clause. — To prevent any delay in the delivery of goods,
services, and real property, the provisions of Republic Act No. 8525 shall apply to
pending and ongoing transactions and/or donations by adopting individuals or
entities.

       SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
