                                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Despite its heavy reliance on imported petroleum products, the Philippines lacks a
comprehensive legal framework establishing a government-owned and controlled strategic
petroleum reserve. In contrast, many countries maintain strategic petroleum reserves as a core
component of their energy security policies. Based on reports, Japan holds one of the largest
petroleum reserves in Asia, with supplies estimated to last over 200 days.1 South Korea and China
have likewise substantial reserves, reflecting strong commitment to energy security planning
among major Asian economies. A research published in Energy Policy notes that a strategic
petroleum reserve is a practical tool for stabilizing supply and demand, moderating price
fluctuations, handling emergencies, and safeguarding the national economy.2

        In the recent disruptions affecting the Strait of Hormuz, nations with established strategic
petroleum reserves were able to release oil stocks to stabilize domestic supply and cushion
economic impact. In comparison, the Philippines had no comparable reserve to draw from and
remained largely exposed to market volatility, relying instead on existing commercial inventories
and external supply conditions. This vulnerability contributed to cumulative oil price hikes
exceeding 100 percent, which could push an additional 1.34 million Filipinos to poverty, according
to the policy brief of the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS).3 This highlights the
urgent need for the Philippines to establish its own strategic petroleum reserve as a safeguard
against future supply shocks and economic disruption.

        The proposed measure seeks to establish a Philippine Strategic Petroleum Reserve to
function as a vital buffer against severe oil supply disruptions. It provides for the creation of a
government-managed reserve composed of crude oil and refined petroleum products, the
development of storage facilities, management and maintenance of the reserves, clear guidelines
for the release of reserves, and implementation timelines based on technical, financial,
infrastructure, and logistical considerations.

           In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING A PHILIPPINE STRATEGIC PETROLEUM RESERVE SYSTEM TO
      ENSURE ENERGY SECURITY, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Strategic Petroleum
Reserve Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to ensure
energy security, economic stability, and national resilience by establishing and maintaining a
Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR).

        The State shall adopt proactive measures to safeguard the country against petroleum
supply disruptions, mitigate the impact of global oil price volatility, and ensure the availability of
fuel during emergencies to avoid severe consequences on critical infrastructures, public welfare,
and national development.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. –
  (a) Crude Oil – refers to oil in its natural state before the same has been refined or otherwise
      treated, but excluding water, bottoms, sediments and foreign substances.
  (b) Petroleum Products – refer to gasoline, diesel, kerosene, jet fuel, liquefied petroleum gas
      (LPG), and other refined petroleum products determined by the Department of Energy
      (DOE).
  (c) Stock Rotation – refers to the systematic replenishment, replacement, or exchange of
      stored petroleum products to maintain quality and operational readiness.
  (d) Strategic Petroleum Reserve – refers to government-controlled stockpiles of crude oil and
      petroleum products maintained for use during national emergencies or severe supply
      disruptions.
  (e) Supply Disruption – refers to any sudden reduction in the availability of crude oil or
      petroleum products caused by geopolitical conflict, natural disaster, global market shock,
      or other extraordinary circumstances affecting national energy supply.

SECTION 4. Establishment of the Philippine Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR). – There is
hereby established a Philippine Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) to be owned and managed
by the Department of Energy.
       The SPR shall maintain petroleum stocks equivalent to not less than nine (90) days of
average national consumption, which may be increased by Congress upon recommendation of
the Department of Energy.

SECTION 5. Composition of Reserve Stocks. – The SPR shall be composed of:
  (a) crude oil;
  (b) refined petroleum products, including diesel, gasoline, jet fuel, and LPG; and
  (c) other energy products as may be deemed critical by the DOE.

      The DOE shall determine the optimal mix of crude and refined products based on
consumption patterns and supply risk assessments.

SECTION 6. Storage Facilities. – The SPR may be stored through a combination of the following:
  (a) government-owned storage terminals;
  (b) leased or contracted commercial storage facilities;
  (c) underground storage structures where technically and economically feasible;
  (d) floating storage systems; and
  (e) other secure and cost-effective storage infrastructure.

SECTION 7. Management and Maintenance of the SPR. – The DOE shall ensure the proper
management, safety, and operational readiness of the SPR. It shall:
  (a) ensure that the reserves are geographically distributed across key locations in the country
      to enhance resilience against natural disasters, infrastructure failure, or security threats;
  (b) ensure that all storage facilities and operations are in compliance with safety,
      environmental, and regulatory standards; and
  (c) implement inventory rotation mechanisms to maintain the quality, usability, and economic
      efficiency of stored petroleum. For this purpose, petroleum stocks may be periodically
      sold, exchanged, or replaced in order to prevent degradation of stored fuels and to reduce
      financial losses associated with prolonged storage.

SECTION 8. Conditions for Release of Reserve Stocks. – The President, upon the
recommendation of the DOE, may authorize release of SPR stocks under any of the following
conditions:
   (a) severe supply disruption;
   (b) declaration of a state of national emergency;
   (c) extraordinary price spikes or market volatility threatening economic security;
   (d) natural disasters affecting fuel logistics or distribution systems.

        All releases shall be conducted in a transparent manner and shall be reported to Congress
within thirty (30) days from such release.

SECTION 9. Public-Private Participation. – The DOE may enter into contracts or agreements
with private entities, for the financing, design, construction, operation, or maintenance of storage
facilities and related infrastructure for the SPR. Such arrangements may be undertaken through
public-private partnership mechanisms consistent with existing laws

SECTION 10. Phased Implementation. – The SPR shall be implemented in phases which shall
include planning and initial stock build-up and infrastructure expansion and optimization. The DOE
shall determine and adjust timelines based on technical, financial, infrastructure, and logistical
considerations, subject to oversight by Congress.
SECTION 11. Reporting. – The DOE shall submit an annual report to Congress detailing the
status of the SPR, including stock levels, reserve coverage, storage capacity, financial
expenditures, acquisition strategies, and any releases conducted during the year.

SECTION 12. Lead Agency. – The Department of Energy shall be the lead agency for the
implementation of this Act in coordination with the Philippine National Oil Company, the National
Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council, the Department of Finance, Department of
Economy, Planning, and Development, and other relevant government agencies.

SECTION 13. Appropriations. – The funds necessary to carry out the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the respective budget of concerned agencies for the current
year. Thereafter, such amount as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DOE, in coordination with relevant
agencies, shall issue the necessary Implementing Rules and Regulations within ninety (90) days
after the effectivity of this Act.

SECTION 15. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances, and rules and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 17. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
