AN ACT
PROHIBITING THE PLACEMENT OF EMPLOYEES ON FLOATING STATUS,
PROVIDING FOR MANDATORY REDEPLOYMENT AND CONTINUITY OF
EMPLOYMENT OR TEMPORARY OFF-DETAIL, PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR
VIOLATIONS THEREOF

EXPLANATORY NOTE

Article XIII, Section 3 of the 1987 Constitution directs the State to afford protection to
labor, secure for workers “just and humane conditions of work,” and guarantee their
right to security of tenure. Article II, Section 18 further mandates the State to “afford
full protection to labor, local and overseas, organized and unorganized, and promote
full employment and equality of employment opportunities.” Article II, Section 9
obliges the State to ensure that workers receive a living wage and enjoy the fruits of
their labor.

Consistent with these guarantees, Department of Labor and Employment (DOI-E)
Department Order No. 147-15, or the Rules on Administering Employment
Termination and Security of Tenure, reiterates that security of tenure is the
constitutional right of workers not to be removed from work except for just or
authorized causes.

Security of tenure, however, is under siege in the business process outsourcing (BPO)
industry, where the mechanism of “floating status” has degenerated from a
temporary, good-faith arrangement into a routine instrument of disguised

===== [page 2] =====
termination and labor-only cost-shifting. Reports gathered by the BPO Industry
Employees Network (BIEN) from workers across the industry from November 2025
to July 2026 — 665 unique reports from 639 workers spanning 174 companies and
331 accounts — show that this is not an isolated grievance but an organized pattern
of attacks on employment continuity.

Floating status was tagged in 214 of 665 unique reports, second only to illegal
dismissal (287), and floating status, illegal dismissal, and layoff appear together in
recurring combinations, showing that employers treat these as interchangeable tools
to drive workers out. BPO workers describe the classic revolving door: they are
placed on floating status “pending client decision”; no transfer or recall comes;
promised lateral moves are recast as new job applications; and workers are
pressured to resign or accept DOLE settlements rebranded as “financial help” with
waivers of their lawful claims. The same device appears at other companies, where
agents describe monthly selection onto unpaid floating status with two to three
days’ notice, allegedly “client mandated,” followed by fresh hiring on the very same
account — a textbook revolving-door scheme that manufactures joblessness and
re-entry at the workers’ expense.

The human toll of unpaid floating is immediate and severe: workers placed on
floating status receive no wages, lose SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG contributions,
exhaust their savings within weeks, and are induced into “voluntary” resignation —
which the employer then cites as evidence of voluntary separation. For the millions
of BPO workers who live from paycheck to paycheck, being left waiting for
redeployment quickly means unpaid bills, mounting debts, and difficulty providing
for their families.

Workers can spend weeks or months waiting for redeployment with their
employment hanging in the balance. Worse, some are required to undergo
interviews or assessments for redeployment, creating a situation where a worker
who has already performed the job can effectively lose employment for the sole
reason that the worker was not “selected” for another assignment. Termination
disguised as redeployment is no less termination, and it is no less unlaw ful.

This bill therefore seeks to guarantee workers’ security of tenure and protect them
against unjust and unlawful termination. To give effect to these propositions, the
salient features of the proposed measure are the following:

1. It explicitly defines “floating status” and bars employers from placing
employees on such status, regardless of the terminology used to describe the
arrangement.

===== [page 3] =====
2. It protects workers’ security of tenure by requiring redeployment, retraining,
or assessment pathways that do not lead to arbitrary termination, and by
compelling employers to exhaust all means of redeployment before
retrenchment and separation.

3. It mandates that workers placed on floating status for legitimate causes be
afforded their full salaries, allowances, and statutory benefits while
redeployment pathways are ongoing.

4. It empowers DOLE to issue appropriate guidelines for the foregoing
protections and to exercise administrative supervision to protect the BPO
labor force.

For BPO workers, security of tenure must mean more than having a contract. It must
mean meaningful protection when a client account ends, a project is downsized, or a
company decides to redeploy workers. The floating-status device, as currently
abused, transfers the costs of business risk to those least able to bear them and
hollows out the very security of tenure the Constitution commands the State to
protect. This bill affirms that employment, once entered, cannot be suspended into
oblivion, terminated by attrition, nor ended through an interview the worker should
never have had to take.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT
PROHIBITING THE PLACEMENT OF EMPLOYEES ON FLOATING STATUS,
PROVIDING FOR MANDATORY REDEPLOYMENT AND CONTINUITY OF
EMPLOYMENT OR TEMPORARY OFF-DETAIL, PROVIDING PENALTIES FOR
VIOLATIONS THEREOF

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Prohibition of Floating
Status and Protection of Employment Continuity Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

a. Guarantee security of tenure, full employment, decent work, and protection
against unjust or unlawful termination, including against diminution of
wages, benefits, rank, seniority, and other favorable terms and conditions of
employment;

b. Prohibit schemes used to circumvent security of tenure from loss, reduction,
expiration, or termination of a client, account or etc such as floating status and
similar arrangements; and

c. Ensure that legitimate business suspensions remain subject to the safeguards
and requirements of the Labor Code.

SECTION 3. Floating Status. — refers to any arrangement where an employee who
remains formally employed is deprived of actual work and wages because the
employer has no current assignment, client, account, project, post, position, or other
work available for the employee. It includes, but is not limited to, arrangements

===== [page 5] =====
described as “floating,” “off-detail,” “standby,” “bench,” “forced leave without pay,”
“involuntary leave without pay,” or any substantially similar arrangement.

SECTION 4. Prohibited Acts. — It shall be unlawful for any employer to:

a. Place an employee on floating status, off-detail, standby, forced or involuntary
leave without pay, or any similar arrangement due to lack of available
assignment, client, account, post, project, or work while the business
continues to operate;

b. Place an employee on floating status when substantially similar work is
available within the employer's operations and the employee is qualified or
can reasonably be qualified to perform such work through ordinary training.

c. Place an employee on floating status while simultaneously recruiting or
hiring another person for the same or substantially similar work, unless the
employer proves a legitimate and documented reason why the affected
employee cannot reasonably be assigned to such work;

d. Use the failure of an employee to pass an internal interview or evaluation
administered for redeployment or reassignment as grounds for termination of
employment or valid justification to extend the maximum allowable period of
floating status;

e. Place an employee on a floating status or similar arrangements of more than
thirty (30) days since the actual and substantial cessation of a business or
undertaking without redeployment or retraining; and

f. Place an employee on continuous, rotating, or successive floating statuses
interspersed with token, brief work assignments solely to reset, break, or
circumvent the statutory maximum period provided under this Act.

SECTION 5. Mandatory Notice and Burden of Proof. -— The burden of proving that the
floating status is borne by bonafide business causes, such as the legitimate loss of a
client contract without fault on the part of the employer, and that no available posts
or alternative deployments exist, shall rest entirely upon the employer whereas,
written notice shall be given at least thirty (30) days prior to placing any employee
on floating status to the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE).

SECTION 6. Floating Status for Bonafide Business Causes. — A placement of an
employee can be considered as a bonafide business cause when:

a. It involves an actual and substantial cessation of the operation of the business
or undertaking, or a clearly identifiable portion thereof;

b. It is based on legitimate and documented business circumstances;

c. It is not undertaken for the purpose of defeating the rights of employees
under this code;

===== [page 6] =====
d. It is implemented in good faith and in accordance with objective and
non-discriminatory criteria; and

e. It is reported to the department of labor and employment in accordance with
applicable rules and regulations;

SECTION 7. Financial Support and Subsistence Allowance During Floating Status. — In
the case of floating status borne by bonafide business causes:

a. Employees placed on floating status shall be entitled to receive a Subsistence
Allowance equivalent to fifty percent (50%) of their basic daily wage and
statutory benefits from the employer starting on the 11th day of the floating
status up to the actual day of re-assignment or separation, but not to exceed
the maximum period allowed by law; and

b. The period of floating status shall not interrupt the continuity of service of the
employee for purposes of reckoning seniority rights, retirement, and other
service-linked benefits.

SECTION 8. Constructive Dismissal. — If an employee remains on floating status
beyond the maximum periods prescribed under Section 4 of this Act without being
offered a definite, comparable, and reasonable re-assignment or post, the
employment shall be automatically deemed terminated by way of constructive
dismissal.

The affected employee shall immediately be entitled to:

a. Reinstatement without loss of seniority rights, or separation pay equivalent to
at least one (1) month pay, or one (1) month pay for every year of service,
whichever is higher, if reinstatement is no longer feasible; and

b. Full backwages, inclusive of allowances and monetary equivalents of other
benefits, computed from the exact time the employee's compensation and
regular assignments were withheld up to the time of actual satisfaction or
reinstatement.

SECTION 9. Penalties and Sanctions. - Any employer, or the responsible officers,
directors, or partners of a corporation, partnership, or agency who violates any
provision of this Act shall be punished by:

a. A fine ranging from One Hundred Thousand Pesos (?100,000.00) to Five
Hundred Thousand Pesos (?500,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than six
(6) months to one (1) year, or both, at the discretion of the court;

b. Mandatory revocation or suspension of the operating license, business permit,
or accreditation of the erring security agency, contractor, or manpower entity

===== [page 7] =====
with the DOLE, Philippine National Police (PNP-SOSIA, where applicable),
and other regulatory bodies; and

c. Payment of double the monetary claims due to the employee, as penalty for
bad-faith labor practices.

SECTION 10. DOLE Monitoring and Rule-Making Power. — The Department of Labor
and Employment, in coordination with relevant tripartite stakeholders, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations (IRR) to effectively implement the
provisions of this Act within ninety (90) days from its effectivity. DOLE shall
maintain an active registry of all workers currently placed on floating status
nationwide to monitor employer compliance.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder thereof not affected thereby shall continue
to be in full force and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, ordinances, rules and regulations, other
issuances or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this law are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in at least two newspapers of general circulation.
