                                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       More and more public and private services are moving from the counter to the
screen. For many, this means greater convenience. For some senior citizens, however, it
can also mean difficulty accessing government services, financial transactions,
transportation, health services, and even benefits already granted by law.

      This challenge will only become more significant as the country ages. The 2024
Census of Population recorded 11.42 million senior citizens, or 10.2% of the household
population, up from 8.5% in 2020.1

        This demographic shift is unfolding alongside the rapid digitalization of essential
services, even as many older Filipinos continue to face barriers in navigating digital
technologies. The Philippine Institute for Development Studies has reported that digital
literacy is particularly low among persons aged 65 years and above.2

       Recognizing that digitalization must remain inclusive, Republic Act No. 12254, or
the “E-Governance Act,” requires the continued availability of government services to
persons without internet access and provides for alternative modes of delivery. The
proposed Digital Inclusion for Seniors Act builds on this principle by addressing the
particular needs of senior citizens as more public and essential services move online.

       The proposed measure ensures reasonable assisted digital support in government
and essential services; promotes senior-friendly digital design; protects access to statutory
senior citizen benefits in online transactions; and allows the use of digital identification
without making a smartphone, application, or electronic credential the exclusive means
of proving eligibility.

           The bill recognizes that difficulty with technology does not mean incapacity. A

         ACCESS TO ASSISTED DIGITAL SUPPORT AND ESSENTIAL SERVICES,

 1          Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Digital Inclusion for
 2   Seniors Act.”
 3
 4           Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to ensure that digital
 5   transformation promotes, rather than diminishes, the autonomy, participation, safety, and
 6   access to essential services of senior citizens.
 7
 8           Towards this end, the State shall promote digital literacy and confidence among
 9   senior citizens; ensure meaningful assistance for those who require help in accessing
10   digital services; prevent exclusion from government and essential services solely because
11   of inability or difficulty in using digital technologies; ensure that privileges and benefits
12   granted by law remain reasonably accessible regardless of the mode of transaction; and
13   promote digital services that are accessible, understandable, secure, and responsive to the
14   needs of senior citizens.
15
16          Nothing in this Act shall be construed to presume incapacity on the basis of age or
17   to diminish the right of a senior citizen to make decisions, enter into transactions, manage
18   property, or exercise any right independently.
19
20          Section 3. Objectives. – This Act shall:
21          (a) Enable senior citizens to participate safely and confidently in an increasingly
22              digital economy and society;
23          (b) Ensure reasonable assisted digital support in government and essential

 9         Section 6. Assisted Access to Government Services. – Consistent with Republic Act
10   No. 12254, covered government entities shall ensure that senior citizens who are unable
11   or have substantial difficulty accessing a government digital service are provided
12   reasonable assisted digital support or another appropriate mode of service delivery.
13
14          Assisted digital support may be integrated into existing frontline service desks,
15   help desks, public assistance centers, contact centers, local government facilities, or other
16   existing service delivery mechanisms.
17
18           Government digital platforms intended for general public use shall, where
19   technically appropriate and consistent with applicable accessibility standards, provide
20   senior-friendly features, which may include adjustable text size, adequate visual contrast,
21   text-to-speech or voice assistance, clear navigation, and readily identifiable access to
22   human assistance.
23
24           Integrated government digital platforms, including the eGovPH application or its
25   successor, shall, to the extent practicable, provide convenient access to information on
26   senior citizens’ rights and privileges and to relevant government health, social protection,
27   livelihood, identification, and other services.
28
29        No senior citizen shall be denied access to a government service, benefit,
30   document, payment, application, or other transaction solely because the senior citizen:
31        (a) Does not own or possess a smartphone or similar device;
32        (b) Has not installed a particular mobile application;
33        (c) Does not have a personal electronic mail address or internet subscription; or
34        (d) Is unable, after reasonable effort, to independently navigate the prescribed
35            digital process.
36
37           Nothing in this section shall require an agency to dispense with a security,
38   identification, evidentiary, authentication, or other requirement imposed by law or
39   reasonably necessary to protect the integrity of the transaction.
40
41           Where a process is required by law or valid regulation to be electronic, the agency
42   shall, to the extent practicable, provide assistance that enables the senior citizen to comply

20          Section 8. Chosen Support Person. – A senior citizen may request the assistance of
21   a chosen support person in accessing, navigating, understanding, or completing a digital
22   transaction.
23
24          The presence or assistance of a chosen support person shall not:
25          (a) constitute evidence of incapacity;
26          (b) authorize such person to make decisions, give consent, enter into contracts,
27              dispose of property, access an account, or otherwise act in place of the senior
28              citizen;

32              representative authority.

35   person to leave at any stage of the transaction.

38   attorney-in-fact, representative, or other person lawfully authorized to act on behalf of the

 5          Section 12. Safeguards in Assisted Digital Transactions. – Assisted digital support
 6   shall preserve the autonomy, privacy, and security of the senior citizen.
 7
 8          A person providing assisted digital support or acting as a chosen support person
 9   may assist in reading instructions, navigating menus, locating information, entering non-
10   confidential information at the direction of the senior citizen, or explaining the steps
11   involved in a transaction.
12
13         Unless otherwise authorized by law or pursuant to valid legal representation, such
14   person shall not:
15         (a) Request, require, record, retain, use, or disclose the senior citizen’s password,
16             personal identification number, one-time password, private cryptographic
17             key, biometric credential, or comparable confidential authentication
18             information;
19         (b) Make a material decision or provide consent in lieu of the senior citizen;
20         (c) Alter the transaction selected by the senior citizen without the latter's
21             knowledge and consent;
22         (d) Use information obtained through the assistance for any purpose unrelated to
23             the transaction; or
24         (e) Induce, pressure, deceive, or mislead the senior citizen into purchasing,
25             transferring, authorizing, or availing of a product, service, or transaction not
26             knowingly chosen by the senior citizen.
27
28          Except where a duly authorized representative is acting pursuant to law or valid
29   authority, the senior citizen shall personally make or confirm any material decision and,
30   whenever practicable, personally enter or provide any confidential authentication
31   credential.
32
33          Covered government entities and essential service providers shall, where
34   practicable, design assisted processes so that confidential authentication may be
35   completed privately by the senior citizen even when another person is providing
36   navigation or other assistance.
37
38          Assisted digital support, by itself, shall not create an agency, guardianship,
39   fiduciary relationship, power of attorney, authority over an account, or authority to
40   transact on behalf of the senior citizen.
