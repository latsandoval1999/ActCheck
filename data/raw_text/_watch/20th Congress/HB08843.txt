13                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
14
15   Farmers and fisherfolks sustain the nation’s food security, yet they continue to belong
16   to the poorest and most vulnerable sectors of Philippine society. Based on the
17   Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) data for 2021, poverty incidence reached 30.6%
18   among fisherfolks and 30.0% among farmers. More recent PSA data for 2023 still
19   show fisherfolks and farmers among the sectors with the highest poverty incidence,
20   at 27.4% and 27.0%, respectively, underscoring that poverty remains a persistent and
21   serious barrier to their families’ well-being and opportunities.
22
23   One of the gravest consequences of this reality is the limited access of their children
24   and dependents to quality education. For many farming and fishing families, daily
25   survival takes precedence over school-related expenses, forcing their children to
26   study under severe financial strain or, in some cases, to abandon their education
27   altogether. This cycle of poverty deprives families not only of immediate
28   opportunities, but also of the long-term benefits that education provides in improving
29   income, mobility, and quality of life.
30
31   This bill seeks to break that cycle by granting educational assistance to the children
32   and dependents of farmers and fisherfolk listed in the Registry System for Basic
33   Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA), from basic education until completion of tertiary
34   education in public schools. The assistance covers essential educational needs such
35   as materials, board and lodging, transportation, clothing, and other similar expenses.
36   To ensure sustained implementation, the bill also creates an Educational Benefits for
37   Farmers and Fisherfolks Fund in the amount of ₱50,000,000.00, to be administered
38   by the Unified Student Financial Assistance System for Tertiary Education
39   (UNIFAST) Governing Board, in consultation with the Department of Agriculture
40   (DA) and representatives of the sectors concerned.
41
42   This proposed measure is both a social justice reform and an investment in national
43   development. By helping the children of farmers and fisherfolk stay in school and
44   finish their education, the State affirms its duty to make education truly accessible
45   while empowering families who have long borne the burden of poverty despite the
46   essential role they play in the country’s economy. Strengthening educational access
47   for these sectors is a concrete step toward reducing intergenerational poverty,
48   promoting inclusive growth, and honoring the dignity of those who feed the nation.

13   AN ACT PROVIDING FOR EDUCATIONAL ASSISTANCE TO CHILDREN
14    AND DEPENDENTS OF FARMERS AND FISHERFOLKS, PROVIDING
15                       FUNDS THEREFOR
16
17   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
18   Congress assembled:
19
20   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Educational Assistance
21   for Children and Dependents of Farmers and Fisherfolks Act.”
22
23   SECTION 2. Educational Assistance for Children and Dependents of
24   Farmers and Fisherfolks. – Children and dependents of farmers and fisherfolks
25   listed in the Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA) shall be entitled
26   educational assistance from basic education until their completion of tertiary
27   education in public school. The educational assistance includes free educational
28   materials, board and lodging, transportation expenses, clothing and other similar
29   expenses.
30
31   SECTION 3. Appropriations of Special Funds for Educational Benefits. –
32   In order to carry out the provisions of this Act, there is hereby established a Special
33   Fund, to be known as the "Educational Benefits for Farmers and Fisherfolks Fund",
34   in the amount of Fifty Million Pesos (₱50,000,000.00). Such Fund shall be
35   perpetually maintained and used exclusively for the benefit of children and
36   dependents of farmers and fisherfolks: Provided, That not more than 5% of this Fund
37   shall be used for the administration of the Special Fund.
38
39   SECTION 4. Administration of the Fund. – The Unified Student Financial
40   Assistance System for Tertiary Education (UNIFAST) Governing Board, in
41   consultation with the Department of Agriculture (DA) and three (3) representatives
42   from the farmers and fisherfolks (selected from a verified procedure of selection as
43   agreed upon by the farmers and fisherfolks), shall have the primary responsibility of
44   managing and supervising the Fund. It should also formulate policies, implement
45   rules and regulations, guidelines and other related functions to oversee, manage and
46   supervise operations of the Fund.
47
48   The UNIFAST Secretariat shall serve as the Secretariat providing administrative
49   support to the Governing Board and shall perform functions as authorized by the
50   UNIFAST Governing Board.

 2   SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
 3   from the effectivity of this Act, the UNIFAST Governing Board, in consultation with

 5   provisions of this Act.
 6
 7   SECTION 6. Separability Clause. – If any part, section or provision of this Act
 8   is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any other
 9   provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in full force and effect.
10
11   SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, acts, decrees, executive orders, rules,
12   and regulations, or other issuances or parts thereof which are contrary to and
13   inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified
14   accordingly.
15
16   SECTION 8. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
17   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
