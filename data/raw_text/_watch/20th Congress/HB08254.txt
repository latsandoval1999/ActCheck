                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

The right to life is the most fundamental of all human rights as manifested in Section 5, Article
II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution which explicitly mandates that "The maintenance of peace
and order, the protection of life, liberty, and property, and promotion of the general welfare are
essential for the enjoyment by all the people of the blessings of democracy." Furthermore, the
State’s Police Power authorizes the legislature to enact laws that restrain individual liberty for the
common good and public safety.

The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that road traffic injuries are the leading cause of
death for children and young adults globally. In the Philippines, the Department of Health
(DOH) and the Metro Manila Development Authority (MMDA) have consistently identified
alcohol as a top contributing factor to "human error" in road crashes, which amounts to
approximately 25% to 30% of fatal road accidents in the Philippines involve drivers under the
influence of alcohol.

Road safety remains a critical public health issue. Under the current Republic Act No. 10586, or
the Anti-Drunk and Drugged Driving Act of 2013, law enforcement is largely restricted to a reactive
stance where officers generally only intervene when a driver displays manifestations of
intoxication. The probable cause requirement often comes untimely, which only becomes
apparent frequently only after a near-miss or a fatal collision.

The proposed measure introduces Random Breath Analyzer Testing (RBAT). By
institutionalizing random checkpoints, the wait-and-see approach is overturned. Furthermore,
the proposed Bill’s amendment also increases the penalties for those who fail the test or refuse to
cooperate, ensuring that the cost of driving under the influence (DUI) far outweighs the
perceived convenience, thereby drastically reducing vehicular fatalities and fostering a culture of
responsible driving.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this Bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING RANDOM BREATH ANALYZER TESTING FOR
MOTORISTS AND INCREASING PENALTIES THEREFOR, AMENDING FOR
THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10586 ALSO KNOWN AS ‘AN ACT
PENALIZING PERSONS DRIVING UNDER THE INFLUENCE OF ALCOHOL,
DANGEROUS DRUGS, AND SIMILAR SUBSTANCES’

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Proactive Anti-Drunk
Driving Act of 2026."

        SEC 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall protect the life and property of its
citizens by adopting proactive, non-discriminatory measures to eliminate drunk driving.

         SEC 3. Section 6 of Republic Act No. 10586, or “An Act Penalizing Persons Driving Under The
Influence Of Alcohol, Dangerous Drugs, And Similar Substances” is hereby amended to read as follows:

                  "Section 6. Conduct of Field Sobriety, Chemical and Confirmatory Tests. –
         A law enforcement officer who has probable cause to believe that a person is driving
         under the influence of alcohol, dangerous drugs and/or other similar substances by
         apparent indications and manifestations, including overspeeding, weaving, lane
         straddling, sudden stops, swerving, poor coordination or the evident smell of alcohol in a
         person’s breath or signs of use of dangerous drugs and other similar substances, shall
         conduct field sobriety tests.

                 If the driver fails in the sobriety tests, it shall be the duty of the law enforcement
         officer to implement the mandatory determination of the driver’s blood alcohol
         concentration level through the use of a breath analyzer or similar measuring instrument.

                  If the law enforcement officer has probable cause to believe that a person is
         driving under the influence of dangerous drugs and/or other similar substances, it shall
         be the duty of the law enforcement officer to bring the driver to the nearest police
         station to be subjected to a drug screening test and, if necessary, a drug confirmatory test
         as mandated under Republic Act No. 9165.

     SEC. 6. RANDOM CONDUCT OF BREATH ANALYZER TESTS. – LAW

NATIONAL POLICE (PNP) ARE HEREBY AUTHORIZED TO CONDUCT
RANDOM BREATHALYZER TESTS ON ANY DRIVER OF A MOTOR
VEHICLE AT DESIGNATED, WELL-LIT CHECKPOINTS. THE
REQUIREMENT      OF   PROBABLE     CAUSE   OR    PHYSICAL

        Law enforcement officers and deputized local traffic enforcement officers shall
be responsible in implementing this section.

SEC. 4. Section 8 of Republic Act No. 10586 is hereby amended to read as follows:

        "SEC. 8. Refusal to Subject Oneself to Mandatory RANDOM Tests. –
A driver of a motor vehicle who refuses to undergo the mandatory field sobriety
and drug tests under Sections 6, 7 and 15 of this Act shall be penalized by the
confiscation and automatic revocation of his or her driver’s license, in addition to
other penalties provided herein and/or other pertinent laws. REFUSES TO
UNDERGO THE RANDOM BREATHALYZER TEST SHALL BE
DEEMED TO HAVE A POSITIVE RESULT FOR ALCOHOL. THE
LAW ENFORCEMENT OFFICERS SHALL PROCEED TO:

        (a)​CONFISCATE AND AUTOMATICALLY SUSPEND THE

        (b)​PERMANENTLY REVOKE THE LICENSE FOR THE

        (c)​IMMEDIATELY IMPOUND THE VEHICLE FOR A

        SEC 6. Regularity in the Performance of Law Enforcement Officers (LEOs). – All
Random Breath Analyzer Test (RBAT) operations must be recorded via body-worn cameras by
the Law Enforcement Officers. The Land Transportation Office shall maintain a centralized
digital database of all breathalyzer results and checkpoint logs to ensure transparency and
prevent extortion practices.

       SEC. 7. Mandatory Procurement and Calibration. – The Department of
Transportation (DOTr) shall allocate an annual budget for the procurement of high-precision
Alcohol Breath Analyzers. The units must undergo calibration every six (6) months by the
Department of Science and Technology (DOST).

       SEC. 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional,
the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force and effect.

        SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations or parts
thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
