                                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Republic Act No. 10627 or the Anti-Bullying Act of 2013 was the glimmer of
hope for countless learners suﬀering in silence. Yet, the sad and painful reality is that
the law has not served as a suﬃcient deterrent against bullies, nor has it oﬀered a
strong shield for the victims.

       We can no longer stay complacent while our children are subjected to all sorts
of acts or words that instill fear, humiliation, and life-threatening harm in spaces
meant to be safe for learning and growth. The undeniable need to fortify our laws and
impose penalties has never been clearer.

       According to the updated 2022 report of the Program for International Student
Assessment (PISA), 1 out of 3 Filipino students reported being bullied in schools. Just
recently, there was an 8-year-old student in Davao City who died from a traumatic
brain injury after being mercilessly struck by a classmate, 1 and two 16-year-old
students in Zamboanga City who were stabbed during a violent altercation fueled by
bullying.2

       The Department of Education (DepEd) has aWempted to stem the tide through
internal administrative frameworks. Under DepEd Order No. 55, s. 2013, the
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) for the Anti-Bullying Act required schools
to form Child Protection CommiWees and adopt local intervention programs.
Acknowledging the escalating severity of school violence, DepEd recently issued
DepEd Order No. 006, s. 2026, or the Guidelines on Ensuring a Safe and Motivating

       However, administrative department orders alone are not enough to dismantle
this systemic issue. While DepEd policies outline vital procedures for intervention and
school-level discipline, the statutory law itself must evolve to mandate harsher
penalties and broader institutional accountability.

       To address the gap, this bill seeks to strengthen the protection aﬀorded to the
learners by clearly deﬁning what constitutes “bullying” and outline corresponding
penalties for such acts. This aims to aﬀord protection to all the student population,
particularly those who are disproportionately targeted, including students suﬀering
from mental and/or physical disabilities, members of the LGBTQ Community, and all
other vulnerable individuals who suﬀer silently in the hands of bullies.

We should aim to create a society where bullying does not exist. Only when this
culture of intimidation stops can we expect our students to thrive and excel, and our
communities to remain peaceful. Thus, the swift passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
   STRENGTHENING THE PROTECTION AGAINST BULLYING IN SCHOOLS,
         DEFINING AND PENALIZING THE CRIME OF BULLYING

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Stop Bullying Act”.

        Section 2. Bullying, defined. - For purposes of this Act, “bullying” shall refer to any
severe or repeated use by an individual or a group of persons, of a written, verbal or electronic
expression, or a physical act or gesture, or any combination thereof, directed at another
individual or a group of persons, that has the effect of actually causing or placing the victim in
reasonable fear of physical or emotional harm or damage to the latter’s dignity or reputation;
creating a hostile environment for the latter at a school, or at any public space, or any private
establishment open for public use.

       Section 3. Specific acts of Bullying. – A bully may either be a student, teacher, or any
person who commits the act of bullying as defined by this Act.

         Any person may be a victim of bullying, regardless of age, gender, socio-economic
status, religion, or health condition.

       Acts of bullying shall include, but not limited to, the following:

   a. Any unwanted physical contact between the bully and the victim like punching,
      pushing, shoving, kicking, slapping, tickling, headlocks, inflicting excessive pranks,
      teasing, fighting, or the use of available objects as weapons;
   b. Any slanderous statement or accusation that causes the victim undue emotional distress
      like directing foul language or profanity at the target, name-calling, tormenting and
      commenting negatively on victim's looks, clothes and body;
   c. Mocking or imitating the physical disability or manner of speech of the victim;
   d. Making multiple unwarranted rude comments regarding a person’s skin tone, gender,
      age, or physical appearance or disability;
   e. Spreading gossip in the school, or through social media;
   f. Cyber-bullying, or bullying done through the use of technology through use of other
      form of technology such as, but not limited to, texting, email, instant messaging,
      internet chat, social media, online game, other platforms or formats. It may also be
      committed during an online class, live streaming, or by commenting on a public social
      media post or sharing the same with a caption that is harmful to the victim’s dignity or
      reputation;
   g. Gender-based bullying act that humiliates or excludes a person on the basis of perceived
      or actual sexual orientation and gender identity.;
   h. Any other act that causes damage to a victim's psychological and/or emotional well-
      being; and
   i. Any act of retaliation against a person who reports bullying, or who provides
      information during an investigation of bullying, or who is a witness to or has reliable
      information about bullying is likewise prohibited.

   Section 4. Penalties. –

   a. The penalty of imprisonment ranging from six (6) months and one (1) day to six (6)
       years, a fine of Fifty thousand pesos (P50,000), or both shall be imposed upon any
       person found guilty for violating this Act.

   b. Liability of Minors. An individual, acting alone or as a part of a group, which violates
       the offense at age 15 years old or lower or above 15 years old but below 18 who acted
       without discernment is exempted from criminal liability. Any minor which violates this
       Act shall be penalized in accordance with Republic Act No. 9344, as amended, and
       other pertinent laws. The exemption from criminal liability herein established does not
       include exemption from civil liability, which shall be enforced in accordance with
       existing laws.

   c. Civil Liability. A penalty of One hundred thousand pesos (P100,000) but not exceeding
       Three hundred thousand pesos (P300,000) shall be imposed upon any person found
       guilty of bullying.

       Section 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) calendar days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Education, Commission on Higher
Education, Department of Justice, Department of Labor and Employment, Department of
Social Welfare and Development, and Department of Interior and Local Government, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.
        Section 6. Separability Clause. - If, for any reason, any provision of this Act is
declared to be unconstitutional or invalid, the other sections or provisions hereof which are not
affected thereby shall continue to be in full force or effect.

        Section 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations or parts
thereof which are inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended or modified accordingly.

       Section 8. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in a newspapers of general circulation or in the Official Gazette.
