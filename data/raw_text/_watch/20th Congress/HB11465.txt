                             AN ACT

                   CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED


                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE
        Tax systems should be designed to promote a fair, progressive, and efficient system that
requires those with greater capacity to contribute a proportionately greater share toward the financing
of government programs and public services. The present tax structure, however, does not fully
capture consumption expenditures associated with luxury and other non-essential goods. Expanding
the coverage and increasing the rate of excise tax on such goods will help address this gap while
discouraging conspicuous consumption and generating additional revenues for national development.

        The proposed measure aims to achieve greater tax progressivity and promote a more equitable
distribution of wealth since luxury and high-value goods are generally purchased by consumers with
greater disposable income and capacity to pay. By raising the cost of high-value discretionary
purchases, the measure may discourage conspicuous consumption and redirect part of household
resources toward savings, investment, or socially productive expenditure.

        This bill amends Section 149 of the NIRC of 1997, as amended, to put a limit to the current
ceiling whilst introducing a new bracket above the current ceiling: automobiles priced over Four
Million Pesos (P4,000,000.00) up to Eight Million Pesos (P8,000,000.00) will be taxed at fifty percent
(50%), while automobiles priced beyond Eight Million Pesos (P8,000,000.00) will be taxed at
seventy-five percent (75%). It likewise amends Section 150 of the Tax Code to raise the excise tax
rate on non-essential goods from twenty percent (20%) to twenty-five percent (25%), to expressly
bring yachts, jet skis, speedboats, sailboats, and motorboats, aircraft, planes, jets, and helicopters
within the coverage of non-essential goods when acquired for pleasure, private use, or sport, and
finally to remove perfumes and toilet waters from the coverage of the tax altogether.
        Crucially, this measure touches no one who cannot afford it. The tax treatment of ordinary
passenger vehicles, motorcycles, and commercial vehicles such as trucks and cargo vans remains
entirely untouched. Its entire burden falls on a narrow slice of transactions at the very top of the
market. By removing perfumes and toilet waters from the coverage of Section 150, it also lifts an
existing tax on everyday personal-care purchases that has never meaningfully reached the wealthy
segment the tax was intended to target.

        In exchange, it is expected to generate additional, sorely needed revenue to help fund
government priority programs, without asking anything more from the ordinary Filipino taxpayer. The
increase in automobile tax alone translates to an estimated annual revenue of ₱3.91 billion.

        Strengthening the taxation of luxury consumption constitutes a practical means of enhancing
the progressivity of the Philippine tax system. It enables the Government to tap revenue sources that
are readily identifiable and administratively accessible, while ensuring that goods and assets used for
essential, livelihood, public transportation, or productive purposes are not unduly burdened.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is urgently sought.
