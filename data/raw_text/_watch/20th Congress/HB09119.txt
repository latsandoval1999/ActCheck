                        AN ACT
MANDATING THE DEPLOYMENT OF LICENSED ALLIED HEALTH
PROFESSIONALS   TO   GEOGRAPHICALLY  ISOLATED   AND
DISADVANTAGED AREAS (GIDAs) AND UNDERSERVED REGIONS
AND PROVIDING INCENTIVES THEREFOR

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE


       Access to quality healthcare remains uneven across the Philippines, with
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs) and underserved regions
continuing to face significant shortages in health human resources. While
government programs have made strides in deploying physicians and nurses, there
remains a critical gap in the availability of allied health professionals—such as
medical technologists, pharmacists, physical therapists, and other essential
practitioners—who play a vital role in diagnostics, treatment support, rehabilitation,
and overall patient care.

      This bill seeks to address this gap through the establishment of a National
Allied Health Deployment Program (NAHDP) under the Department of Health
(DOH). The program aims to ensure the systematic, equitable, and sustained
deployment of licensed allied health professionals to communities that need them
most. By aligning deployment with local health system needs and strengthening

                                                                                      1
 coordination with local government units and health facilities, the measure intends
 to improve service delivery and health outcomes in underserved areas.

        Furthermore, the bill strengthens partnerships with academic institutions,
 training hospitals, and professional organizations to build a sustainable pipeline of
 allied health workers. It integrates community-oriented training, supports research
 and innovation, and establishes a scholarship and return-service program to
 encourage students—particularly those from underserved areas—to pursue allied
 health professions and serve their communities.

       By investing in allied health professionals and ensuring their presence in all
 regions of the country, especially in GIDAs, the State takes a decisive step toward
 equitable, accessible, and quality healthcare for all Filipinos.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “National Allied Health
Corps Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to ensure equitable
access to health services across all regions of the country, especially in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs). To this end, the



                                                                                 1
government shall deploy a sufficient number of licensed allied health professionals
to underserved communities and provide them with appropriate incentives and
support.


SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
defined as follows:


   a. Allied Health Professionals refer to licensed practitioners other than
      physicians and nurses, including but not limited to physical therapists,
      occupational therapists, medical technologists, radiologic technologists,
      respiratory therapists, pharmacists, speech-language pathologists, dietitians,
      and midwives.


   b. GIDAs refer to Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas as classified
      by the Department of Health (DOH), which lack access to basic health
      services due to location, security, or socioeconomic factors.


   c. Underserved Regions refer to Areas with low health human resource density,
      as determined by the DOH and the Department of Interior and Local
      Government (DILG).


SEC. 4. Deployment of Allied Health Professionals. - The Department of Health
(DOH) shall develop and implement a National Allied Health Deployment Program
(NAHDP) that shall:




                                                                                   2
   a. Deploy licensed allied health professionals to GIDAs and underserved regions
        on a rotating or long-term basis;
   b. Assign professionals based on local health needs, with a focus on diagnostics,
        rehabilitation, pharmacy, and support services;
   c. Coordinate with Local Government Units (LGUs), Rural Health Units
        (RHUs), and DOH regional offices for effective placement and supervision.


SEC. 5. Selection Criteria for Deployment of Allied Health Professionals. – The
DOH shall establish a merit-based and needs-responsive system for the selection and
recruitment of allied health professionals for deployment to Geographically Isolated
and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs) and underserved regions. The selection shall be
based on, but not limited to, the following criteria:


   a.      Professional Qualifications and Competence – Possession of a valid
           license, relevant certifications, and demonstrated clinical or technical
           competence;
   b.      Relevant Experience – Prior work experience in primary care, public
           health, community-based practice, or service in underserved or resource-
           limited settings;
   c.      Willingness and Commitment to Serve in GIDAs – Including expressed
           intent to complete a required deployment period provided under the
           NAHDP;
   d.      Geographic and Cultural Affinity – Preference may be given to applicants
           originating from, residing in, or with familiarity with the language, culture,
           or conditions of the target area;




                                                                                        3
   e.      Adaptability and Resilience – Capacity to work in low-resource
           environments and respond to public health needs;
   f.      Equity Considerations – Ensuring fair representation across regions and
           disciplines, and prioritizing applicants from disadvantaged backgrounds,
           where appropriate.


The DOH shall develop a transparent scoring and ranking system based on the
foregoing criteria. The specific weights, indicators, documentary requirements, and
evaluation procedures shall be prescribed in the Implementing Rules and
Regulations (IRR).


The selection process shall ensure transparency, fairness, and accountability,
including the publication of criteria, establishment of a selection committee, and
provision for appeal or reconsideration mechanisms, as may be further defined in
the IRR.


The criteria and corresponding weights may be periodically reviewed and adjusted
by the DOH through the IRR to respond to evolving health system needs, without
diminishing the standards set in this Act.


SEC. 6. Incentives and Support Mechanisms. - To attract, retain, and support
allied health professionals deployed under this Act, the following incentives and
support mechanisms shall be granted:


   a. Compensation and Allowances. – Deployed allied health professionals shall
        receive a competitive compensation package, including:


                                                                                  4
   i.     A Hardship Allowance of not less than thirty percent (30%) of the basic
          salary, without prejudice to the grant of higher rates as may be
          determined in the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), taking
          into account the degree of isolation, risk, and cost of living;
   ii.    Such other applicable allowances as may be authorized under existing
          laws, rules, or regulations.
b. Housing and Living Support. – Provision of safe and adequate housing, or a
   commensurate housing allowance, in the place of assignment, including
   access to basic utilities, as may be further defined in the IRR.
c. Transportation and Deployment Support. – Coverage of transportation,
   relocation, and periodic travel expenses, including but not limited to initial
   deployment, mid-assignment travel where applicable, and return to point of
   origin upon completion of service.
d. Professional Development and Career Incentives. –
   i.     Grant of Continuing Professional Development (CPD) credits for
          services rendered, in coordination with the Professional Regulation
          Commission and relevant Professional Regulatory Boards;
   ii.    Access to scholarships, training, and specialization programs, subject
          to existing rules;
   iii.   Priority consideration in hiring, plantilla positions, or career
          advancement in government health facilities and programs, consistent
          with civil service laws and regulations.
e. Insurance and Occupational Protection. – Provision of comprehensive health
   and life insurance coverage, including disability benefits, for the duration of
   deployment, in addition to benefits under the Philippine Health Insurance
   Corporation and other existing programs.


                                                                                 5
   f. Workplace Support and Safety. – Assurance of adequate working conditions,
      including necessary equipment, supplies, and personal protective equipment,
      as well as mechanisms for occupational safety, security, and psychosocial
      support, especially in high-risk or resource-constrained areas.
   g. Non-Monetary      Incentives.   –   Recognition      through   service   awards,
      certifications, and eligibility for government service incentives, as may be
      determined.


The DOH shall ensure a baseline standard of incentives applicable nationwide. The
specific amounts, additional benefits, eligibility conditions, and mechanisms for
delivery shall be prescribed in the IRR and may be enhanced or adjusted periodically
in coordination with relevant agencies and local government units.


SEC. 7. Monitoring, Reporting, and Utilization of Deployed Allied Health
Professionals. - The DOH shall establish an evaluation, monitoring and reporting
system to ensure the effective utilization, performance, and welfare of allied health
professionals deployed under this Act. The reporting system shall be designed to:


   a. Assess service delivery and health outcomes in the assigned areas;
   b. Ensure that deployed personnel are assigned functions aligned with their
      professional qualifications and scope of practice;
   c. Identify gaps in resources, supervision, or working conditions; and
   d. Inform policy, planning, and program improvements.




                                                                                     6
The conduct such periodic evaluation shall include consultations with deployed
personnel, host facilities, and local government units, to improve deployment
strategies and working conditions.


The DOH shall ensure that reporting requirements are streamlined, not duplicative,
and facilitated through digital or simplified systems, to avoid undue administrative
burden on deployed personnel.


SEC. 8. Partnership with Academic and Professional Institutions. – The DOH
shall, in coordination with relevant government agencies and stakeholders, establish
and strengthen partnerships with academic institutions, allied health schools,
training hospitals, and professional organizations to support the implementation of
this Act. Such partnerships shall include, but not be limited to, the following:


   a. Deployment and Training Pipeline. – Mobilization of interns, clerks,
      residents, fellows, and post-graduate trainees, as appropriate, for supervised
      field exposure and service delivery in GIDAs, consistent with their level of
      training and scope of practice;
   b. Capacity Building and Supervision. – Provision of structured training,
      mentorship, and clinical supervision to deployed and early-career allied health
      professionals, including the designation of accredited training and supervising
      institutions;
   c. Curriculum Development and Alignment. – Integration of community-based
      education, primary care, and rural health competencies into allied health




                                                                                    7
      curricula, in coordination with the Commission on Higher Education and
      other relevant bodies;
   d. Research and Innovation. – Development of research, extension, and service-
      learning programs addressing local health system needs, health workforce
      distribution, and innovative service delivery models in underserved areas; and
   e. Continuing Professional Development. – Support for continuing education
      and upskilling programs, in coordination with the Professional Regulation
      Commission and accredited professional organizations.


The DOH may accredit participating institutions and prescribe minimum standards
for training quality, supervision, and participant welfare, as may be further defined
in the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).


The roles, responsibilities, and cost-sharing arrangements among participating
institutions, including national government agencies and local government units,
shall be clearly defined in formal agreements, consistent with applicable laws, rules,
and regulations.


SEC. 9. Scholarship and Return-Service Program for Allied Health
Professionals. - The DOH, in coordination with the Commission on Higher
Education, state universities and colleges, and other educational institutions, shall
establish and administer a scholarship and financial assistance program to support
the education and training of allied health professionals needed in Geographically
Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs) and underserved regions. The
scholarship program shall prioritize:




                                                                                    8
   a. Qualified students originating from or residing in GIDAs and underserved
      areas;
   b. Courses and specializations identified as critical to local health system needs;
      and
   c. Applicants who demonstrate commitment to public service, particularly in
      rural or resource-limited settings.


Scholars shall be required to render a return-service obligation equivalent to a period
to be determined in the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), in a GIDA or
underserved area, under terms and conditions set by the DOH.


SEC. 10. Mandatory Credential Verification. - All applicants to the program shall
undergo strict verification of licensure, registration status, and professional standing
prior to deployment. The DOH shall coordinate with the Professional Regulation
Commission and relevant Professional Regulatory Boards to ensure the authenticity
and validity of submitted credentials.


The DOH shall establish a secure and interoperable verification system, which may
include digital databases, license validation platforms, and direct coordination with
regulatory bodies, to prevent the submission or acceptance of falsified or expired
licenses.


SEC. 11. Accountability of Public Officials and Employees. – Any public official
or employee who knowingly participates in, facilitates, or negligently allows the
deployment of unqualified or unlicensed individuals under this program shall be




                                                                                      9
subject to administrative, civil, and/or criminal liability in accordance with existing
laws, rules, and regulations.


SEC. 12. Liability for Fraud and Misrepresentation. – Any individual who
falsifies, misrepresents, or uses fraudulent credentials to qualify for deployment or
to receive benefits under this Act shall:


   a. Be immediately disqualified or removed from the program;
   b. Be required to return any compensation, allowances, or benefits improperly
      received, without prejudice to applicable penalties; and
   c. Be subject to prosecution under applicable laws, including but not limited to
      those penalizing falsification, estafa, and illegal practice of a profession.


SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The DOH shall, in coordination
with the relevant agencies, promulgate the rules and regulations to implement the
provisions of this Act within sixty (60) days after its effectivity.


SEC. 14. Separability Clause. - If any part or provision of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.


SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders, issuances, decrees, rules
and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are deemed
amended, modified or repealed accordingly.
