                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Foreign interference in domestic and governmental processes has emerged as
a significant and evolving threat to democratic states, including the Philippines.
Global trends reveal how foreign actors utilized coordinated disinformation
campaigns during elections, covert funding of political groups, and cyber operations
targeting critical infrastructure to influence public opinion and policy outcomes.

        The European Commission, through its Action Plan Against Disinformation,
documented the exploitation of online platforms to manipulate democratic discourse
by polarizing communities and inciting antagonism in the society. 1 The North
Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) warns that information threats can cause real-
world harm, directly endangering national security.2 Similarly, the Koffi Annan
Foundation observed that new digital platforms and internet culture contributed to
the scale, nature, and reach of foreign interference operations. 3 In the Philippine
context, the absence of a comprehensive and unified legal framework specifically
addressing foreign interference leaves critical gaps, as existing laws on espionage,
cybercrime, and election offenses remain fragmented and insufficient to address the
full scope of these modern threats.

        This measure is anchored on the constitutional principle of national
sovereignty, territorial integrity, and national interest, while guaranteeing the
protection of civil liberties under the Bill of Rights. It complements existing statutes
such as the Anti-Money Laundering Act of 2001 and the Anti-Wiretapping Law, the
Foreign Agents Act of 1979, which promotes transparency in foreign-linked political
activities.

        The proposed Foreign Interference Prevention Act establishes a
comprehensive framework by clearly defining and penalizing foreign interference,
including disinformation, covert influence operations, and support to foreign
intelligence services; requiring the registration of foreign agents engaged in political
or advocacy activities; addressing digital threats through access blocking; enabling
financial investigation; strengthening inter-agency coordination; while explicitly
safeguarding civil liberties, ensuring that speech, journalistic, and academic
freedoms are respected and protected.

       The enactment of this measure is expected to strengthen national sovereignty
and security by deterring foreign interference, protect the integrity of our election
and democratic processes, enhance transparency and accountability in foreign-
linked activities, improve the State’s capacity to respond to hybrid and cyber threats,
and align the Philippines with international standards in countering foreign
interference.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
PREVENTING AND PENALIZING FOREIGN INTERFERENCE IN THE
PHILIPPINES, STRENGTHENING MECHANISMS TO DETECT AND COUNTER
FOREIGN INTERFERENCE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in the Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Foreign
Interference Prevention Act.”

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to safeguard national sovereignty, democratic institutions, and governmental
processes from unlawful or malign foreign influence. Consistent with the
Constitution, in the State’s relation with other states, the paramount consideration
shall be national sovereignty, territorial integrity, national interest, and the right to
self-determination.

       Toward this end, the State shall adopt measures to detect, prevent, and
respond to foreign interference, while ensuring the protection of civil liberties,
including freedom of expression, privacy, and due process of law.

       SEC. 3. Safeguard of Civil Liberties. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed
to prohibit or penalize the lawful expression of opinion, academic research,
journalism, or legitimate advocacy, including those involving foreign entities, absent
proof of conduct that is covert, deceptive, coercive, corrupt, or fraudulent in nature,
as defined under this Act.

      The exercise of rights guaranteed under the Constitution, including freedom
of expression, association, and privacy, shall at all times be respected in the
implementation of this Act.

       SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

       (a)    Access Blocking Order refers to a lawful order issued by a Regional Trial
       Court directing an internet service provider to restrict access to specific
       electronic content proven to be part of foreign interference activity.

       (b)    Coordinated Disinformation Campaign refers to the organized and
       deliberate dissemination of false or materially misleading information
       presented as fact by multiple actors acting in concert or under common
       direction;
      (c)   Critical National Infrastructure refers to systems or assets essential to
      national security, including telecommunications, energy, financial systems,
      transportation networks, and defense installations;

      (d)    Electronic Communications Activity refers to the use of digital platforms,
      internet services, or electronic networks to publish, transmit, alter, or
      distribute information;

      (e)    Foreign Agent refers to any person who knowingly acts within the
      Philippines under the direction, control, funding, or supervision of a foreign
      principal to influence political or governmental processes;

      (f)    Foreign Intelligence Service refers to any agency or organization of a
      foreign state responsible for intelligence collection, covert operations, or
      influence operations;


      (g)    Foreign Interference refers to any activity that is covert, deceptive,
      coercive, corrupt, or fraudulent in nature, and is undertaken by or on behalf
      of a foreign principal, with the intention to materially influence or disrupt:
      (1) Elections or political processes; (2) Government decision-making process,
      through unlawful, undisclosed, or deceptive means; (3) Public opinion through
      coordinated disinformation campaign with the intent to deceive; (4) Critical
      national infrastructure; or (5) National security or diplomatic relations;

      (h)    Foreign Principal refers to any foreign government, foreign political
      party, foreign national, or foreign entity substantially owned or controlled by
      foreign nationals; and

      (i)     Foreign Proxy refers to any person or entity knowingly acting on behalf
      of a foreign principal to advance its strategic interests;

      SEC. 5. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts are hereby declared unlawful
and shall be punishable under this Act:

      (a)   Engaging in acts constituting foreign interference as defined under
      Section 3(g) of this Act;

      (b)     Acting as an undisclosed foreign agent in political or governmental
      activities;


      (c)    Providing material support, funding, logistical assistance, or
      information to a foreign intelligence service intended to influence Philippine
      political processes;


      (d)    Conducting coordinated disinformation campaigns on behalf of a
      foreign principal designed to influence elections, public policy, or public trust
      in democratic institutions;
      (e) Facilitating foreign interference through manipulation of digital
      communications system or platforms.

       SEC. 6. Foreign Interference through Electronic Communications
Activity. – Any person who knowingly publishes, transmits, or manipulates
electronic information on behalf of a foreign principal with the intent to influence
elections, undermine national security, or disrupt democratic processes, through
covert, deceptive, or coordinated disinformation activities shall be liable under this
Act.

       Upon application by the Department of Justice (DOJ) and upon judicial
determination of probable cause, the court may issue an Access Blocking Order
directing relevant internet service providers to disable access to the electronic
content involved.


       SEC. 7. Planning and Conspiracy to Commit Foreign Interference. – Any
person who participates in the planning, preparing, or facilitating the commission of
foreign interference with another person shall be liable under this Act.

       SEC. 8. Aiding or Abetting. – Any person who knowingly aids, abets, or
protects persons committing foreign interference shall be liable under this Act.

       SEC. 9. Registration of Foreign Agents. – Any person acting as a foreign
agent within the Philippines for the purpose of political advocacy, lobbying, public
relations, or strategic communications shall register with the DOJ within thirty (30)
days of commencing such activity. Foreign agents who fail to register shall be liable
under this Act.

       SEC. 10. Penalties. – Offenses committed under this Act shall have the
following penalties:

      (a)   Offenses committed under Sections 5, 6, and 7 of this Act shall be
      punishable by life imprisonment or a fine of not less than Five Million Pesos
      (P5,000,000.00) but not more than Ten Million Pesos (P10,000,000.00), or
      both.


      (b)    Offenses committed under Section 8 of this Act shall be punishable by
      imprisonment of not less than twelve (12) years but not more than twenty
      (20) years or a fine of not less than Two Million Pesos (P2,000,000.00) but not
      more than Five Million Pesos (P5,000,000.00), or both.


      (c)     Offenses committed under Section 9 of this Act shall be punishable by
      imprisonment of not less than six (6) years but not more than twelve (12) years
      or a fine of not less than One Million Pesos (P1,000,000.00) but not more than
      Two Million Pesos (P2,000,000.00), or both.

       SEC. 11. Offenses Committed by Public Officials. – If the offender is a
public officer or employee, the maximum penalty shall be imposed and the offender
shall suffer perpetual disqualification from public office.
       SEC. 12. Deportation of Foreign National Offenders. – If the offender is a
foreign national, the offender shall be deported after serving sentence and
permanently barred from re-entry into the Philippines.

       SEC. 13. Financial Investigation. – Foreign interference shall be considered
an unlawful activity under Section 3(i) of Republic Act No. 9160, otherwise known as
the “Anti-Money Laundering Act of 2001,” as amended. The Anti-Money Laundering
Council (AMLC) shall be authorized to conduct financial investigations and to
petition for the issuance of freeze orders and the institution of civil forfeiture
proceedings before the appropriate court.

       SEC. 14. Surveillance and Interception of Communications. – Consistent
with the provisions of Republic Act No. 4200, otherwise known as the “Anti-
Wiretapping Law,” a duly authorized law enforcement officer may, upon written
authority by the head of the agency, file an ex parte application before the proper
court for the issuance of a written order to intercept, track, and record any wire, oral,
or digital communication.

        No such order shall be issued except upon a finding of probable cause that a
violation of this Act has been committed, is being committed, or is about to be
committed, that the communication sought to be intercepted are relevant and
material to the investigation, and that it has been established that no other less
intrusive means are reasonably available to obtain the information sought.

      The court order shall specify with particularity the persons, facilities,
accounts, or communication systems subject of the interception, as well as the
nature and duration of the surveillance.

       The authorization shall be effective for the period specified in the order, which
shall not exceed sixty (60) days from the date of issuance, unless extended or
renewed by the court for a period not exceeding thirty (30) days upon proof of
continued necessity.

       All intercepted materials shall, including communications, recordings, and
notes, within forty-eight (48) hours be deposited in a sealed package before the
issuing court, accompanied by an affidavit of the executing officer stating the
contents of the package and certifying that no duplicates or copies of the whole or
any part of the materials have been made, or if made, that such duplicates or copies
are included in the package deposited with the court. The package deposited shall
not be opened, or the recordings replayed or used in evidence, or their contents
revealed, except upon order of the court, which shall not be granted except upon
motion, with due notice and opportunity to be heard to the person or persons whose
conversation or communications have been recorded.

       SEC. 15. Extraterritorial Application. – This Act shall apply to Filipino
citizens who commit acts of foreign interference outside the territory of the
Philippines when such acts affect Philippine national security, territorial integrity, or
governmental processes.

       Foreign nationals who commit acts of foreign interference outside the territory
of the Philippines shall be held liable and subject to the penalties provided herein
once they enter the territory of the Philippines.
      SEC. 16. Coordination and Advisory Body. – The National Security Council
(NSC) shall serve as the primary coordinating and advisory body for the
implementation of this Act.

      The National Security Adviser shall ensure coordination among relevant
agencies, including but not limited to the DOJ, National Intelligence Coordinating
Agency (NICA), Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT),
Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA), AMLC, and law enforcement agencies.

      For purposes of this Act, the NSC shall:

      (a)   Formulate and periodically update a national strategy to prevent and
      counter foreign interference;

      (b)   Establish mechanisms for intelligence-sharing            and   operational
      coordination among concerned agencies; and


      (c)    Oversee the development of capabilities to detect and respond to
      foreign interference, including cyber-enabled and information operations.

       SEC. 17. Submission of Annual Report to Congress. – The NSC through
the National Security Adviser shall submit an annual report to Congress on the
implementation of this Act, including emerging threats, enforcement actions, and
policy recommendations.

       SEC. 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOJ shall, in coordination with the DFA, DICT,
the National Bureau of Investigation, and NICA, and in consultation with other
agencies and relevant stakeholders, promulgate the necessary rules and regulations
for the effective implementation of this Act.

      SEC. 19. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 20. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
letters of instruction, administrative rules and regulations or parts thereof, which
are contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act, are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

      SEC. 21. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
