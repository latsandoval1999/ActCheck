                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Constitution declares that the State shall promote a just and dynamic
social order that improves the quality of life, raises the standard of living, and frees the
people from poverty through sound economic and social policies. It likewise mandates
that the rule of taxation shall be uniform and equitable, and that Congress shall evolve
a progressive system of taxation. These constitutional principles recognize that the tax
system must not only generate revenues for the government but must also be fair,
responsive, and sensitive to the economic realities faced by Filipino taxpayers.

        In 2017, Congress enacted Republic Act No. 10963, otherwise known as the Tax
Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) Law, which increased the income tax
exemption threshold from ₱10,000 to ₱250,000 annually. The reform was intended to
provide meaningful tax relief to Filipino workers by allowing them to keep more of
their earnings. At the time, the increase reflected prevailing wage levels and the cost of
living, and significantly improved the disposable income of millions of taxpayers.

       Nearly a decade has passed since the enactment of the TRAIN Law. During this
period, the country has experienced unprecedented economic challenges, including the
COVID-19 pandemic and sustained increases in the prices of basic necessities.
Although nominal wages have gradually increased, the purchasing power of Filipino
families has been steadily eroded by rising living costs. Consequently, many workers
whose annual incomes now exceed ₱250,000 are not necessarily better off than those
who benefited from the original exemption under the TRAIN Law.

       The existing income tax exemption ceiling no longer reflects present-day
economic conditions. Taxpayers with modest incomes are increasingly brought into the
taxable bracket despite continuing financial pressures brought about by inflation and
the higher cost of everyday living. Updating the exemption threshold is therefore
necessary to preserve the intent of the TRAIN Law.

      This measure proposes to increase the income tax exemption ceiling from
₱250,000 to ₱350,000 annually and to correspondingly adjust the graduated income tax
brackets. It likewise increases the exemption threshold for self-employed individuals
and professionals who avail of the eight percent (8%) income tax option. These
adjustments provide much-needed tax relief while ensuring that the tax system remains
progressive, equitable, and responsive to current economic realities. At a time when
many Filipinos continue to grapple with the rising cost of living, providing reasonable
tax relief is both a matter of fairness and sound economic policy.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT INCREASING THE INCOME TAX EXEMPTION CEILING FOR
INDIVIDUAL TAXPAYERS TO THREE HUNDRED FIFTY THOUSAND PESOS
(PHP 350,000) ANNUALLY, THEREBY AMENDING SECTION 24(A)(2)(a) AND (b)
OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      Section 1. Declaration of State Policy - Pursuant to Article II, Section 9 of the 1987
Constitution, which mandates the State to promote a just and dynamic social order that
improves the quality of life for all, and Article VI, Section 28(1), which requires that the rule
of taxation shall be uniform and equitable and that Congress shall evolve a progressive system
of taxation, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to provide meaningful tax relief to
Filipino taxpayers by ensuring that income tax exemptions remain fair, equitable, and
responsive to prevailing economic conditions.
