EXPLANATORY NOTE

Millions of Filipino families depend on money sent home by overseas Filipino workers for
their daily needs, education, medical expenses, housing, and other essential expenses. Every
peso deducted from these remittances through excessive charges, unfavorable exchange rates,
or undisclosed fees reduces the amount that ultimately reaches an OF W's family.

OFWs already make significant sacrifices to earn their income abroad. Sending their hard-
earned money home should therefore be safe, transparent, affordable, and convenient.

This bill seeks to strengthen the protection of OF W remittances by promoting reasonable
remittance costs and requiring greater transparency in fees, charges, and foreign exchange rates.
Before completing a transaction, an OFW should be clearly informed of how much the
transaction will cost and, as far as practicable, the actual amount that the intended beneficiary
will receive.

The measure likewise recognizes that remittances are increasingly being sent through digital
platforms and electronic financial services. Consumer protection must therefore apply
regardless of whether money is sent through a traditional bank or remittance center or through
an authorized digital or electronic remittance service.

The bill also strengthens mechanisms for addressing unauthorized transactions, fraud,
erroneous transfers, unreasonable delays, and other problems encountered by OF Ws and their

===== [page 2] =====
beneficiaries. Financial institutions and remittance service providers must maintain accessible
channels through which complaints can be promptly addressed.

At the same time, OF Ws and their families should be provided with clear information that
allows them to compare remittance costs and make informed financial decisions.

The money sent home by an OFW represents hours of work and years of sacrifice away from
family. Government must ensure that this money reaches its intended beneficiaries through a
financial system that is fair, transparent, secure, and accountable.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

TWENTIETH CONGRESS
Second Regular Session

HOUSE BILL No. 11626

Introduced by Representative EDGAR R. ERICE

AN ACT
PROTECTING THE REMITTANCES OF OVERSEAS FILIPINO WORKERS,
PROMOTING FAIR, TRANSPARENT, SECURE, AND AFFORDABLE
REMITTANCE SERVICES, STRENGTHENING CONSUMER PROTECTION, AND
FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SEC. 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Overseas Filipino Workers Remittance
Protection Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. \t is hereby declared the policy of the State to recognize the
significant contribution of overseas Filipino workers (OFWs) to their families and to the
national economy and to protect their hard-earned remittances.

Towards this end, the State shall promote a remittance system that is fair, transparent,
competitive, accessible, affordable, secure, and responsive to the needs of OF Ws and their
beneficiaries.

The State shall likewise protect OF Ws and their beneficiaries against excessive or undisclosed
charges, deceptive or unfair practices, fraud, unauthorized transactions, and other practices that
unnecessarily diminish or place at risk their remittances.

SEC. 3. Coverage. This Act shall apply to banks, non-bank financial institutions, remittance
and transfer companies, electronic money issuers, and other entities authorized by the Bangko
Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) to provide remittance or money transfer services involving
remittances sent by OF Ws to beneficiaries in the Philippines.

The provisions of this Act shall apply to remittances transmitted through physical, electronic,
digital, mobile, or other authorized channels.

SEC. 4. Fair and Reasonable Remittance Charges. The BSP shal! adopt appropriate measures
to promote fair, reasonable, transparent, and competitive fees and charges for OF W remittance
services.

In formulating such measures, the BSP shall consider the actual cost of providing remittance
services, prevailing domestic and international practices, technological developments,
financial inclusion, market competition, and the need to ensure the continued availability and
accessibility of safe remittance channels.

No remittance service provider shall impose a fee or charge that has not been clearly disclosed
to the sender prior to the completion of the transaction.

SEC. 5. Full Disclosure of Remittance Cost. Before an OFW confirms a remittance
transaction, the remittance service provider shall clearly and prominently disclose, in a form
readily understandable to the sender:

a) The amount to be remitted;

b) All fees, service charges, commissions, and other amounts to be paid by the sender:

c) The applicable foreign exchange rate, whenever currency conversion is involved;

d) Any amount or charge that may be deducted from the remittance before receipt by the
beneficiary, when known to the provider;

e) The estimated or guaranteed amount, as applicable, that the beneficiary will receive;

f) The expected date or period within which the remittance will become available to the
beneficiary; and

g) Such other information as may be required by the BSP.

The sender shall be provided with an appropriate electronic or physical record of the transaction
containing the foregoing information.

SEC. 6. Transparency in Foreign Exchange Conversion. Whenever a remittance transaction
involves foreign exchange conversion, the applicable exchange rate shall be clearly disclosed
to the sender prior to confirmation of the transaction.

Remittance service providers shall not represent a transaction as having “zero fee,” “no fee,”
“free transfer,” or similar terms where the cost of the transaction is recovered through an
undisclosed or inadequately disclosed exchange-rate margin or other charge.

The BSP shall prescribe appropriate rules for the transparent disclosure of exchange rates,
conversion charges, and other relevant costs.

SEC. 7. Digital and Electronic Remittance. OFW remittances transmitted through authorized
digital, mobile, electronic, or online channels shall be entitled to the same standards of
transparency, security, consumer protection, and accountability provided under this Act.

Digital remittance providers shall adopt appropriate security measures, authentication
procedures, fraud-detection systems, and consumer assistance mechanisms consistent with
standards prescribed by the BSP.

SEC. 8. Protection Against Fraud and Unauthorized Transactions. Remittance service
providers shall establish appropriate safeguards to detect and prevent fraud. unauthorized
transactions, account takeover, identity theft, and other unlawful activities affecting OFW
remittances.

Upon receiving notice of a suspected unauthorized or fraudulent transaction, the provider shall
promptly investigate the matter and, when legally and technically possible, take appropriate
measures to prevent or limit further loss.

The BSP shall prescribe standards governing the investigation and resolution of unauthorized
or fraudulent remittance transactions, including appropriate procedures for reimbursement or
other remedies where the OFW or beneficiary is entitled thereto under applicable laws and
regulations.

SEC. 9. Erroneous Transfers, Delays, and Failed Transactions. Remittance service providers
shall maintain appropriate procedures for addressing erroneous transfers, failed transactions,
unreasonable delays, duplicate transactions, and similar remittance problems.

Where a remittance transaction fails through no fault of the sender or beneficiary, the amount
remitted and any fees or charges required to be refunded shall be returned within the period
prescribed by the BSP.

Nothing in this Section shall prevent reasonable verification and fraud-prevention procedures
necessary to protect the sender, beneficiary, and financial system.

Section 10. Complaint and Redress Mechanism. Every remittance service provider covered
by this Act shall maintain an accessible, responsive, and reasonably prompt mechanism for
receiving and resolving complaints from OF Ws and their beneficiaries.

Complaint channels shall, as far as practicable, be accessible electronically and from outside
the Philippines.

OFWs and beneficiaries who are unable to obtain satisfactory resolution from the remittance
service provider may avail themselves of the appropriate consumer assistance or dispute
resolution mechanisms of the BSP and other competent government agencies.

SEC. 11. Comparison and Consumer Information. The BSP. in coordination with the
Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) and other concerned agencies, shall promote access
to clear and understandable information that will enable OF Ws to compare remittance services
based on fees, exchange rates, speed, accessibility, and other relevant considerations.

Where practicable, such information shall be made available through official government
websites, digital platforms, migrant workers offices, and other appropriate channels accessible
to OF Ws.

SEC. 12. Financial Education. The BSP and DMW, in coordination with the Overseas
Workers Welfare Administration (OWWA), concerned government agencies, financial
institutions, and other stakeholders, shall strengthen financial education programs for OF Ws
and their families.

Such programs shall include information on remittance costs, exchange rates, digital financial
services, fraud and scam prevention, savings, investments, financial planning, and the
responsible use of financial products and services.

Programs under this Section shall, as far as practicable, be integrated with existing government
financial education programs for OF Ws to avoid unnecessary duplication.

SEC. 13. Coordination with Overseas Posts. The DMW, through Migrant Workers Offices
and in coordination with the Department of Foreign Affairs and Philippine foreign service
posts, shall assist in disseminating information to OF Ws regarding their rights under this Act,
safe remittance practices, available complaint mechanisms, and advisories concerning
remittance-related fraud and scams.

SEC. 14. Implementing Agencies. The BSP shall be the lead regulatory agency for the
implementation of provisions relating to remittance service providers, fees and charges,
disclosure, foreign exchange conversion, digital remittances, consumer protection, complaints,
and remedies.

The DMW, OWWA, and other concerned agencies shall assist in information dissemination,
financial education, OF W assistance, and such other matters within their respective mandates.

SEC. 15. Prohibited Acts. Subject to rules and regulations to be issued by the BSP, the
following acts are prohibited:

a) Imposing undisclosed fees or charges on an OF W remittance;

b) Misrepresenting or concealing the applicable foreign exchange rate or material cost of
currency conversion;

c) Falsely representing a remittance service as free or without charge when a material cost
is recovered through an undisclosed exchange-rate margin or other mechanism;

d) Knowingly providing false or misleading information regarding the amount to be
received by the beneficiary or the expected completion of the transaction;

e) Unjustifiably refusing to act upon a valid complaint concerning a remittance
transaction; and

f) Such other unfair, deceptive, or abusive practices as may be defined by the BSP
pursuant to this Act and existing laws.

SEC. 16. Administrative Sanctions. Any remittance service provider that violates this Act or
its implementing rules and regulations shall be subject to the applicable administrative
sanctions and penalties that the BSP is authorized to impose under existing banking, payment
system, financial consumer protection, and other applicable laws.

The imposition of administrative sanctions shall be without prejudice to any civil or criminal
liability arising under other laws.

SEC. 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the BSP, in coordination with the DMW, OWWA, Department of Finance, and
other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act.

SEC. 18. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current appropriations of the concerned agencies.

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included
in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 19. Separability Clause. If any provision or part of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the remaining provisions or parts not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.
