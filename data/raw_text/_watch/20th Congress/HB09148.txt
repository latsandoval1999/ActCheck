         ESTABLISHING THE KALINGA PROGRAM TO SHIELD FILIPINO
        HOUSEHOLDS, WORKERS, FARMERS, FISHERFOLK, TRANSPORT
    OPERATORS, AND MICRO, SMALL, AND MEDIUM ENTERPRISES (MSMEs)
     FROM FUEL-DRIVEN INFLATION, ENERGY SUPPLY DISRUPTIONS, AND
        ESSENTIAL GOODS PRICE SHOCKS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Fuel price volatility remains one of the most immediate drivers of increases in
the cost of living of Filipino households, affecting transport costs, food prices, power
generation, logistics, and the survival of micro, small, and medium enterprises
(MSMEs). The need for an immediate and coordinated government response is
underscored by the sharp acceleration of inflation in April 2026, when the country’s
headline inflation rose to 7.2%, from 4.1% in March 2026 and 1.4% in April 2025.
The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) identified the leading contributors to overall
inflation are food and non-alcoholic beverages, contributing 31.9% or 2.3 percentage
points, transportation costs contributing 27.0% or 1.9 percentage points, and housing,
water, electricity, gas and other fuels, contributing 23.2% or 1.7 percentage points.
Food inflation likewise accelerated to 6.1% in April 2026 from 2.7% in the previous
month, driven mainly by the increase in rice inflation to 13.7%, from 3.5% in March
2026. These figures demonstrate that inflation is no longer confined to fuel prices
alone, but has already spread to food, transportation, utilities, and other basic
necessities, thereby justifying the establishment of a coordinated, targeted, and time-
bound national response framework.1

       Consequently, the Department of Energy reported that as of 28 April 2026,
domestic oil price movements resulted in year-to-date net increases of ₱44.23 per liter
for gasoline, ₱48.96 per liter for diesel, and ₱57.99 per liter for kerosene, amid
elevated global crude prices, geopolitical tensions, and foreign exchange pressures.2

oil prices average US$80 per barrel in 2026, even before considering second-round
effects.3 This bill therefore provides a necessary, targeted, and fiscally responsible
mechanism for government response. Instead of relying on fragmented or purely
reactive subsidies, this measure, anchored on House Bill No. 8834 earlier filed, seeks
to establish the KALINGA Program or the Komprehensibong Alalay sa Livelihood,
Inflation, Negosyo and Goods Assistance Program, as a national emergency response
framework to mitigate fuel-driven inflation, secure energy and essential goods supply,
and provide targeted, time-bound assistance to vulnerable sectors.

        To further strengthen the responsiveness, integrity, and accountability of the
measure, the bill introduces additional safeguards and implementation mechanisms,
including a beneficiary identification to ensure accurate targeting and avoid duplication
of aid, anti-hoarding, anti-profiteering, and price manipulation safeguards to prevent
abuse during periods of supply and price instability, data privacy and cybersecurity
protection to secure beneficiary information and payment disbursement, fuel and
essential goods priority allocation to protect critical sectors and public services during
supply disruptions, penalties for fraudulent claims to deter misuse of public funds and
false availment of benefits and a mandatory public dashboard to promote
transparency, public monitoring, and accountability in the release and utilization of
government assistance.

        In view of the continuing threat of fuel-driven inflation and its direct impact on
Filipino families, workers, farmers, fisherfolk, transport operators, MSMEs, and
consumers, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

 1         SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “KALINGA Act of 2026”
 2   or the “Komprehensibong Alalay sa Livelihood, Inflation, Negosyo and Goods Assistance
 3   Act.”
 4
 5        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 6   adopt and implement a comprehensive, responsive, and fiscally responsible intervention
 7   framework to protect Filipino consumers, workers, farmers, fisherfolk, the transport
 8   sector, and micro, small, and medium enterprises from the adverse effects of fuel price
 9   shocks, energy supply disruptions, and inflationary pressures.
10         Towards this end, the State shall ensure the continuous supply and availability of
11   oil, fuel, electricity, and essential goods, stabilize prices and prevent excessive inflation;
12   support logistics and supply chains nationwide, promote energy conservation and
13   demand reduction, and authorize flexible tax, regulatory, and fiscal interventions when
14   necessary.
15         The State shall likewise maintain fiscal discipline by ensuring that all interventions
16   are targeted, time-bound, transparent, and designed to avoid unnecessary increases in
17   the budget deficit and government borrowing, while providing the government sufficient
18   flexibility to respond swiftly and effectively to fuel price volatility and energy-related
19   emergencies.

21        SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act, the following terms are
22   defined, as follows:

 1          (a) “Disadvantaged and underemployed workers” refers to a broad category of
 2   individuals who need government assistance by reason of underemployment or
 3   displacement, including self-employed persons who have lost their livelihood due to an

 5         (b) “Farmers” are persons primarily engaged in agricultural production.

 9   the poverty threshold as defined by the Department of Economy, Planning, and
10   Development (DEPDev).
11         (e) “Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs)” are those defined under
12   Republic Act No. 6977, as amended.
13        (f) “Minimum wage earners” are those defined under the National Internal
14   Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.
15         (g) “Overseas Filipino workers” are those defined under Republic Act No. 10801.
16          (h) “Public transport drivers” are individuals duly licensed to operate a motor
17   vehicle and who drive, as common carriers, for the conveyance of passengers along
18   public highways, roads, or streets. This shall include, but is not limited to, drivers of
19   jeepneys, buses, taxis, tricycles, transport network vehicle services (TNVS), UV Express,
20   and other vehicles operating under a franchise or permit issued by the Land
21   Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB).

23        SEC. 4. Establishment of the KALINGA Program. - There is hereby established
24   a KALINGA Program, which shall serve as the centralized national emergency response
25   framework for addressing fuel-driven inflation, energy supply disruptions, and logistics
26   cost shocks.
27        All measures, assistance programs, regulatory interventions, supply management
28   measures, and emergency responses under this Act shall be implemented through and
29   as part of the KALINGA Program.
30       The KALINGA Program shall include the following components:
31       1. Fuel Price Stabilization Component
32       2. Energy Supply, Security, and Inventory Management Component
33       3. Targeted Assistance Component
34       4. Essential Goods and Logistics Stabilization Component
35       5. MSME Energy Relief and Business Continuity Component
36       6. Energy Conservation and Demand Reduction Component
37       7. Flexible Fiscal and Regulatory Measures Component
38
39        SEC. 5. Eligible Beneficiaries.- For purposes of targeted assistance under the
40   KALINGA Program, the vulnerable sectors under this Act covers the farmers, fisherfolk,
41   homeless families, indigenous peoples, persons and families belonging to the informal
42   settler sector, those residing in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas,

 8         SEC. 6. Fuel Price Stabilization Component. - The Fuel Price Stabilization
 9   Component shall include measures to stabilize fuel prices and mitigate fuel-driven
10   inflation, including:
11      (a) Targeted fuel subsidy programs for transport, agriculture, fisheries, and logistics
12          sectors;
13      (b) Flexible fuel tax adjustment, including temporary suspension, reduction, or
14          staggered implementation of excise taxes on petroleum products;
15      (c) Fuel price smoothing mechanisms;
16      (d) Temporary transport fare support programs; and
17      (e) Other price stabilization interventions as may be necessary.
18           All subsidies shall be targeted, time-bound, and subject to periodic review to
19      ensure fiscal sustainability.
20
21        SEC. 7. Energy Supply, Security, and Inventory Management Component.
22   - The Energy Supply Security Component shall ensure the availability of oil, fuel,
23   electricity, and other energy products. The government is authorized to:
24      (a) Enter into government-to-government fuel supply agreements;
25      (b) Diversify sources of fuel imports and energy supply;
26      (c) Monitor national fuel inventory levels;
27      (d) Require and enforce minimum fuel inventory levels from oil companies and
28          distributors;
29      (e) Implement priority allocation of fuel for essential sectors;
30      (f) Ensure fuel supply for power generation and essential services;
31      (g) Implement emergency fuel supply and distribution measures;
32      (h) Coordinate and direct oil companies, energy suppliers, logistics providers and
33          distributors to ensure continuous supply of fuel and energy products nationwide;
34      (i) Conduct emergency procurement and enter into supply contracts; and
35      (j) Conduct national energy and logistics contingency planning.
36
37      SEC. 8. Targeted Assistance Component. - The Targeted Assistance
38   Component shall provide direct and targeted assistance to vulnerable sectors, including:
39      (a) Direct cash assistance to minimum wage earners and low-income households;
40      (b) Fuel subsidies for public transport drivers, farmers, fisherfolk, and logistics
41          providers;
42      (c) Transport assistance for low-income workers;
43      (d) Public transport fare subsidies; and

                                                 3
 1      (e) Other targeted assistance as may be necessary.
 2           All assistance shall be time-bound, targeted, and delivered through digital and
 3      national targeting systems where practicable.

 5        SEC. 9. Essential Goods and Logistics Stabilization Component. - To ensure
 6   the continuous availability, efficient movement, and reasonable pricing of essential
 7   goods during periods of fuel price volatility, inflationary pressure, or supply disruption,
 8   the government shall implement measures to stabilize logistics, protect supply chains,
 9   and prevent unreasonable increases in the prices of basic necessities and prime
10   commodities, including the following:
11      (a) Support for transport and distribution of essential goods;
12      (b) Buffer stocking and food supply stabilization measures;
13      (c) Shipping, inter-island transport, and logistics support;
14      (d) Cold chain and storage support programs;
15      (e) Priority fuel allocation for logistics transporting essential goods; and
16      (f) Temporary price stabilization measures consistent with existing laws.
17
18       SEC. 10. MSME Energy Relief and Business Continuity Component. - To
19   protect micro, small, and medium enterprises from business closures, job losses, and
20   rising energy costs during periods of fuel price volatility or supply disruption, the
21   government shall provide support through the following measures:
22      (a) Targeted energy and fuel subsidies where necessary;
23      (b) Low-interest loans and credit guarantee programs;
24      (c) Tax payment deferment and regulatory fee relief;
25      (d) Incentives for energy efficiency and fuel-saving technologies;
26      (e) Logistics and distribution support for MSMEs; and
27      (f) Implementation of a moratorium of up to six (6) months on the payment of
28          interest and/or suspension of loan collections for qualified MSMEs under
29          government financial institutions and government directed lending programs,
30          without the imposition of penalties, surcharges, or additional interest.
31      (g) Government loan guarantees and credit support programs in lieu of direct
32          subsidies where feasible.
33
34        SEC. 11. Energy Conservation and Demand Reduction Component. - To
35   reduce fuel consumption, stabilize energy supply, and promote responsible energy use
36   during periods of fuel price volatility or supply disruption, the government may
37   implement the following measures:
38      (a) Work-from-home arrangements in government and encouraged in the private
39          sector;
40      (b) Compressed workweek and staggered working hours;
41      (c) Carpooling and public transport promotion programs;
42      (d) Reduced fuel consumption by government agencies;
43      (e) Energy conservation programs in government and commercial establishments;

 1      (f) Speed regulation and fuel-saving measures; and

 4          SEC. 12. Flexible Fiscal and Regulatory Measures Component. - To
 5      provide flexibility in responding to fuel price shocks, the government is authorized
 6      to implement the following measures:
 7      (a) Temporary suspension or reduction of fuel excise taxes and import duties;
 8      (b) Tax payment deferment for affected sectors and MSMEs;
 9      (c) Reduction of regulatory fees and charges;
10      (d) Streamlined permits and regulatory processes;
11      (e) Anti-hoarding and anti-profiteering measures;
12      (f) Price monitoring and public price bulletins; and
13      (g) Other temporary regulatory interventions necessary to stabilize prices and
14          supply.
15           Provided, that any suspension or reduction on taxes shall:
16         i. Be subject to prior notification to Congress; and
17         ii.Be automatically reviewed by Congress within sixty (60) days from
18             implementation.
19
20       SEC. 13. Automatic Trigger Mechanisms. - Activation or expansion of KALINGA
21   Program components shall be guided by automatic triggers, including:
22      (a) The Global oil price, including the average Dubai crude oil price based on the
23          Mean of Platts Singapore (MOPS), exceeds market thresholds;
24      (b) Sustained increases in domestic fuel prices of at least thirty percent (30%) within
25          a period of thirty (30) days;
26      (c) Extraordinary inflation;
27      (d) National fuel inventory levels falling below thirty (30) days of supply, as
28          determined by the Department of Energy (DOE);
29      (e) Imminent supply disruptions or energy shortages, as determined by the DOE;
30      (f) Declaration of a state of national energy emergency by the President; or
31      (g) Other extraordinary external shocks resulting in a significant increase in domestic
32          fuel prices or supply.
33

12        SEC. 15. KALINGA Inter-Agency Task Force. - There is hereby created a
13   KALINGA Inter-Agency Task Force which shall oversee, coordinate, and ensure the
14   effective implementation of all components of the KALINGA Program.
15        The Task Force shall be chaired by the Executive Secretary and co-chaired by the
16   Secretary of Energy and the Secretary of Finance. It shall be composed of
17   representatives from relevant government agencies, including the Department of
18   Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), Department of Budget and
19   Management (DBM), Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), Department of Trade and
20   Industry (DTI), Department of Agriculture (DA), Department of Transportation (DOTr),
21   Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Philippine Statistics Authority
22   (PSA), and such other agencies as may be necessary for the implementation of this Act.
23       The Task Force shall have the following powers and functions:
24       (a) Formulate, coordinate, and implement the KALINGA Program;
25       (b) Prepare the Comprehensive KALINGA Program Implementation Plan;
26       (c) Monitor fuel prices, inflation, energy supply, logistics costs, food prices, and
27   other relevant economic indicators;
28      (d) Recommend the activation, expansion, modification, or suspension of program
29   components based on the trigger mechanisms and standards provided under this Act;
30        (e) Ensure coordination among national government agencies, local government
31   units, government financial institutions, and other concerned stakeholders; and
32         (f) Submit monthly and quarterly reports to Congress on the implementation, fund
33   utilization, program outcomes, and recommendations concerning the KALINGA
34   Program.

12        SEC. 17. Role of Local Government Units. - Local government units (LGU) shall
13   assist in the implementation of the KALINGA Program under the direction and
14   coordination of the KALINGA Inter-Agency Task Force.
15       For this purpose, LGUs shall:
16   (a) Implement and localize KALINGA Program interventions consistent with national
17       policies and guidelines;
18   (b) Identify and validate beneficiaries within their respective jurisdictions;
19   (c) Assist in the distribution of assistance and delivery of services;
20   d) Monitor local prices, supply conditions, and fuel related impacts, and submit periodic
21       reports to the Task Force; and
22   (e) Coordinate with national agencies and ensure alignment of local measures with the
23       KALINGA program.
24
25        SEC. 18. Anti-Hoarding, Anti-Profiteering, Price Manipulation, and
26   Economic Sabotage Measures. - During the implementation of the KALINGA
27   Program, the DTI, DOE, DA, DOTr, PCC, and other concerned agencies shall intensify
28   monitoring and enforcement against hoarding, profiteering, cartel-like behavior, artificial
29   shortages, unjustified price increases, and price manipulation involving fuel, energy
30   products, transport services, logistics, food, agricultural and fishery products, and
31   essential goods.
32         When the prohibited act involves agricultural or fishery products and constitutes
33   economic sabotage under Republic Act No. 12022, or the Anti-Agricultural Economic
34   Sabotage Act, the offender shall be prosecuted and penalized under said law, including
35   life imprisonment and a fine of five (5) times the value of the agricultural and fishery
36   products subject of the crime, without prejudice to penalties under other applicable
37   laws.
38        SEC. 19. Data Privacy and Cybersecurity. - All agencies implementing this Act
39   shall comply with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and applicable cybersecurity standards.
40   Personal information collected for the KALINGA Program shall be used solely for
41   beneficiary identification, verification, delivery of assistance, monitoring, audit, and
42   evaluation. Unauthorized disclosure, misuse, sale, or sharing of beneficiary data shall
43   be prohibited and penalized under existing laws.

33         SEC. 24. Fraudulent Claims and Misuse of Benefits. - Any person who
34   knowingly submits false information, uses falsified documents, claims benefits under

37   applicable civil, administrative, or criminal laws. Public officials or employees who
38   facilitate fraudulent claims, manipulate beneficiary lists, or divert program funds shall
