                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill seeks to reclassify certain forest lands in the Province of Benguet into
alienable and disposable lands of the public domain in order to align legal land
classification with actual land use and to enable Indigenous Cultural
Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) to secure ownership documents over
lands they have long possessed, occupied, and cultivated.

        A significant portion of the land area of Benguet remains classified as forest
land under existing national land classification policies. However, many of these
areas have long been developed and utilized for agricultural production, residential
settlements, commercial establishments, public infrastructure, and other community
purposes. This long-standing mismatch between the legal classification of land and
its actual use has created persistent challenges for local communities, particularly for
ICCs/IPs who have lived on and stewarded these lands since time immemorial.

        Because these lands remain officially classified as forest lands, many
residents, including ICCs/IPs, cannot secure formal land ownership documents such
as titles, tax declarations, or other tenure instruments. As a result, they face
limitations in exercising their property rights, accessing government services and
development programs, and using their lands as collateral for financial
opportunities. This situation also creates uncertainty in land tenure and restricts the
ability of local government units to effectively regulate development and implement
land use plans.

        Updated and accurate land classification is therefore essential to ensure sound
land governance and sustainable development. Reclassifying areas that have long
been developed or occupied will help harmonize legal land status with present
realities on the ground. Such action will support local government units in the
formulation and updating of their Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs) and
other development plans, thereby promoting more effective land use management
across the Province of Benguet.

      Furthermore, this measure complements the policy of the State to recognize
and promote the rights of Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples
under Republic Act No. 8371, otherwise known as the Indigenous Peoples’ Rights
Act of 1997. Facilitating appropriate land classification and tenure mechanisms will
help ensure that ICCs/IPs are able to secure the legal recognition and protection of
the lands they have traditionally occupied and cared for.

      In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is respectfully sought.

                         AN ACT
RECLASSIFYING CERTAIN FOREST LANDS IN THE PROVINCE OF
BENGUET INTO ALIENABLE AND DISPOSABLE LANDS TO REFLECT
ACTUAL LAND USE AND TO ENABLE INDIGENOUS CULTURAL
COMMUNITIES/  INDIGENOUS   PEOPLES   (ICCs/IPs) TO SECURE
OWNERSHIP DOCUMENTS OVER THEIR LANDS

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to    recognize  and     promote       the      rights   of    Indigenous      Cultural
Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) within the framework of national
unity and development, consistent with the provisions of the Constitution and
Republic Act (RA) No. 8371, otherwise known as the “Indigenous Peoples’ Rights
Act of 1997.”

        The State likewise recognizes the need to ensure that land classification
reflects actual land use in order to promote equitable access to land ownership,
sustainable development, and effective land use planning.

       SEC. 2. Reclassification of Certain Forest Lands. - Subject to existing laws, rules,
and regulations, certain portions of forest lands in the Province of Benguet which
have long been occupied, cultivated, or developed for agricultural, residential,
commercial, institutional, and other community purposes are hereby identified for
reclassification into alienable and disposable lands of the public domain, provided
that such areas are no longer needed for forest purposes.

        SEC. 3. Identification and Delineation of Areas. - The Department of Environment
and Natural Resources (DENR), in coordination with the National Commission on
Indigenous Peoples (NCIP), the Provincial Government of Benguet, and the
concerned Local Government Units (LGUs), shall conduct the necessary land
classification, survey, and validation to determine the specific areas to be reclassified
under this Act. In undertaking such process, priority shall be given to areas:

       a. Already used for agricultural production;
       b. Occupied for residential and community purposes;
       c. Developed for public infrastructure and institutional facilities; and
       d. Traditionally occupied or possessed by ICCs/IPs since time immemorial.

​     SEC. 4. Recognition of Indigenous Peoples’ Rights. – The reclassification of lands
under this Act shall respect and uphold the rights of ICCs/IPs to their ancestral
domains and ancestral lands, consistent with the provisions of RA No. 8371.

      Nothing in this Act shall prejudice the rights of ICCs/IPs to apply for and
secure Certificates of Ancestral Domain Title (CADT) or Certificates of Ancestral
Land Title (CALT) under existing laws.

        SEC. 5. Issuance of Land Ownership Documents. - Upon completion of the land
classification process and the declaration of the areas as alienable and disposable
lands, qualified occupants, including ICCs/IPs and other legitimate occupants, may
apply for appropriate land ownership documents, titles, tax declarations, or other
tenure instruments, subject to existing laws and regulations.

       SEC. 6. Support for Local Land Use Planning. - The updated land classification
resulting from this Act shall serve as a basis for the formulation or updating of the
Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs) of the local government units in the
Province of Benguet, consistent with national land use policies and sustainable
development goals.

       SEC. 7. Implementing Agencies. - The Department of Environment and Natural
Resources (DENR), in coordination with the National Commission on Indigenous
Peoples (NCIP), the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and
the Provincial Government of Benguet, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations to effectively implement the provisions of this Act within one hundred
eighty (180) days from its effectivity.

      SEC. 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders, rules and regulations, or
parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

      SEC. 10. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

       Approved,
