                             EXPLANATORY NOTE


The world is experiencing exponential growth in Waste Electrical and Electronic
Equipment (WEEE), commonly referred to as "e-waste." Driven by rapid
technological innovation, global e-waste generation is increasing three times
faster than the human population growth.
In the Philippines, the widespread adoption of digital technologies, remote work
paradigms, and online commerce has accelerated domestic electronic
consumption. However, our waste management systems have not evolved
alongside this digital transformation. Millions of electronic units reach their end-
of-life annually, with the vast majority entering a fragmented, unmonitored waste
stream.
While the Philippines possesses landmark environmental legislation, there is a
clear structural gap regarding e-waste management. Republic Act No. 9003
(Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000) focuses primarily on municipal
solid waste (organic, recyclable, residual, and special wastes). It lacks specific
directives, standards, or technical mandates for complex electronic assemblies
containing toxic elements.
Republic Act No. 6969 (Toxic Substances and Hazardous and Nuclear Wastes
Control Act of 1990) regulates toxic chemicals and hazardous materials in bulk,
but its enforcement mechanisms are largely designed for industrial
manufacturing and chemical imports—not for post-consumer retail electronics
widely dispersed among millions of households.
Republic Act No. 11898 (Extended Producer Responsibility Act of 2022)
established a precedent by mandating producer responsibility for plastic
packaging. However, its scope excludes electronics, leaving one of the fastest-
growing and most toxic waste streams without a clear extended liability
mechanism.
Consequently, e-waste in the Philippines exists in a regulatory grey area—neither
effectively managed by local municipal systems nor adequately captured by
industrial hazardous waste frameworks.
This bill institutionalizes a national, closed-loop E-Waste Management System
built upon four strategic pillars:
      1. Mandatory Segregation and Landfill Prohibition: Explicitly bans the
         dumping, mixing, or incineration of e-waste within general municipal
         solid waste networks and standard sanitary landfills.

      2. Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) Framework: Follows
         international best practices (such as the EU WEEE Directive) by
         requiring manufacturers, brand owners, and importers ("Obliged
         Enterprises") to take financial and operational responsibility for the
         post-consumer phase of their products. This includes establishing
         convenient consumer take-back points, funding reverse logistics, and
         meeting progressive annual recovery and recycling targets.

      3. Inclusive Formalization of Informal Recyclers: Rather than criminalizing
         informal waste pickers, the bill mandates a structured transition plan.
         Led by DOLE, TESDA, and LGUs, informal workers will be provided
         technical training, safety gear, health monitoring, and formal
         accreditation—integrating them into certified Treatment, Storage, and
         Disposal (TSD) collection chains as recognized green workers.
Local government units are required to designate specialized E-Waste Collection
Hubs within local Materials Recovery Facilities (MRFs). To incentivize private
sector investment in high-tech urban mining and recycling facilities, accredited
TSD operators will be eligible for fiscal and non-fiscal incentives under existing
investment priority systems.
The "National Electronic Waste Management Act" bridges a critical legislative gap.
It protects public health, safeguards fragile ecosystems, secures valuable raw
materials, and ensures that the burden of waste management is shared equitably
among manufacturers, distributors, consumers, and state regulators.
In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
    INSTITUTIONALIZING A COMPREHENSIVE ELECTRONIC WASTE
     MANAGEMENT SYSTEM, MANDATING EXTENDED PRODUCER
 RESPONSIBILITY, PROVIDING INFRASTRUCTURE AND INCENTIVES FOR
         E-WASTE RECYCLING, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "National
Electronic Waste Management Act."

     SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy - Consistent with Section 16, Article II of the
1987 Philippine Constitution to protect and advance the right of the people to a
balanced and healthful ecology, it is the policy of the State to:

      (a) Institutionalize a national framework for the segregation, collection,
          transport, storage, reuse, recycling, and environmentally sound
          disposal of Electronic Waste (E-waste);

      (b) Adopt the principle of Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) by
          holding manufacturers, importers, and distributors accountable for the
          lifecycle of their electronic products;

      (c) Protect informal waste handlers and surrounding communities from
          hazardous chemical exposures associated with unsafe e-waste
          dismantling; and

      (d) Promote resource recovery, urban mining, and the transition toward a
          circular economy.
      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

      (a) Electrical and Electronic Equipment (EEE) – Refers to equipment
          dependent on electric currents or electromagnetic fields to work
          properly, including household appliances, IT and telecommunications
          equipment, consumer electronics, and medical devices.

      (b) Electronic Waste or E-Waste – Refers to discarded, obsolete, broken, or
          end-of-life EEE, including all components, subassemblies, and
          consumables that are part of the product at the time of discarding.

      (c) Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) – Refers to an environmental
          policy approach in which a producer's responsibility for a product is
          extended to the post-consumer stage of its lifecycle.

      (d) Obliged Enterprises – Refers to manufacturers, brand owners, or
          importers who introduce EEE into the domestic market.

      (e) Treatment, Storage, and Disposal (TSD) Facility – Refers to a facility
          accredited by the Department of Environment and Natural Resources
          (DENR) equipped to safely handle, process, recycle, or dispose of
          hazardous wastes, including e-waste.


      SEC. 4. Institutional Framework. –

        The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) shall be
the primary regulatory and enforcement agency. Through the Environmental
Management Bureau (EMB), the DENR shall establish standards, accredit TSD
facilities, monitor EPR compliance, and maintain a National E-Waste Registry.
     The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) shall enforce mandatory
product labeling, component standards for reusability, and monitor compliance
among EEE retailers and distributors.
      Local Government Units (LGUs) shall integrate e-waste collection into their
local solid waste management plans, establish designated E-Waste Materials
Recovery Facilities (E-MRFs) or collection points, and conduct public information
campaigns.
      SEC. 5. Prohibition on Mixed Disposal and Landfilling. –

      (a) The disposal of e-waste alongside general municipal solid waste is
          strictly prohibited.

      (b) No operator of a sanitary landfill, open dump, or waste-to-energy
          facility shall accept unsegregated e-waste.

      (c) Consumers and commercial establishments shall surrender e-waste
          exclusively to accredited LGU collection points, retailer buy-back
          stations, or licensed TSD facilities.


      SEC. 6. Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) Framework for Electronics.

      a.    Mandatory Take-Back Programs: Within twelve (12) months from the
            effectivity of this Act, all Obliged Enterprises shall establish or
            participate in an EPR program for the recovery, recycling, or safe
            disposal of their products.
      b.    Minimum Recovery Targets: Obliged Enterprises shall meet
            progressive annual recovery targets set by the DENR, starting at a
            minimum of twenty percent (20%) of their average annual sales
            volume within three (3) years of enactment, scaling up to sixty
            percent (60%) within eight (8) years.
      c.    Producer Responsibility Organizations (PROs): Enterprises may form
            or join registered PROs to collectively fulfill their compliance
            obligations under this Act.


      SEC. 7. Formalization of the Informal Recycling Sector. - The Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE), in coordination with the DENR, Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and LGUs, shall formulate
a National Transition Plan to:
      (a) Provide technical training, safety equipment, and accreditation to
informal waste collectors and junkshop operators;
      (b) Integrate informal workers into formal collection, aggregation, and
sorting supply chains connected to certified TSD facilities.


      SEC. 8. Incentives. –
      (a) Fiscal Incentives: Registered enterprises and accredited TSD facilities
engaging in specialized e-waste processing and high-grade material extraction
shall be eligible for fiscal incentives under the Corporate Recovery and Tax
Incentives for Enterprises (CREATE) Act or applicable investment priority plans.
      (b) Non-Fiscal Incentives: Simplified permitting and prioritization in
government green procurement programs.


      SEC. 9. Prohibited Acts and Penalties.

      (a) Indiscriminate Disposal or Open Burning of E-Waste:

            First Offense: Fine of Php 10,000 to Php 50,000.

            Subsequent Offenses: Fine of Php 50,000 to Php 200,000, or
            community service, or both.

      (b) Non-Compliance with EPR Obligations by Obliged Enterprises:

            Fine ranging from Php 200,000 to Php 5,000,000 depending on the
            scale of enterprise, or suspension of business permit until
            compliance.

      (c) Operating an Unaccredited E-Waste TSD Facility or Illegal
          Dismantling:

            Fine of Php 500,000 to Php 2,000,000 and imprisonment of two (2)
            to six (6) years.


       SEC. 10. E-Waste Management and Innovation Fund. – A special account
under the DENR is hereby created, to be funded through administrative fines,
fees, and voluntary contributions. The fund shall be dedicated strictly to research
and development on sustainable electronics, LGU infrastructure subsidies, and
capacity-building programs for informal sector transition.


      SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) - Within six (6) months
from the approval of this Act, the DENR, in consultation with DTI, DILG, DOLE,
DOST, and relevant industry stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for its implementation.


       SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
      SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
presidential issuances, and other administrative rules and regulations or parts
thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

      SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
