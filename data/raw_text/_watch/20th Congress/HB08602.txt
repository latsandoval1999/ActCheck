15                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

16   Republic Act No. 9367, otherwise known as the Biofuels Act of 2006, was enacted to promote
17   the development and utilization of indigenous renewable energy sources, reduce dependence on
18   imported fuels, and provide sustainable livelihood opportunities for Filipino farmers and workers
19   in the biofuels sector.

20   However, current global and domestic developments have significantly altered the fuel price
21   landscape. In recent months, volatility in the international oil market, supply constraints, and
22   geopolitical tensions have resulted in sustained increases in fuel prices, placing considerable
23   pressure on fuel-importing countries such as the Philippines.

24   Based on the Oil Price Watch as of March 14, 2026 published by the Philippine Daily Inquirer,
25   fuel prices are expected to surge further, with projected increases of approximately ₱14.00 to
26   ₱17.00 per liter for gasoline and ₱19.30 to ₱22.30 per liter for diesel in the succeeding pricing
27   adjustment. These projected increases come on top of successive weeks of price hikes,
28   highlighting the severity and persistence of the ongoing fuel price crisis.

29   Such unprecedented increases have far-reaching economic consequences. As fuel prices rise,
30   transportation costs escalate, leading to higher prices of food and other essential goods. The
31   Philippines, being heavily dependent on imported fuel, remains highly vulnerable to these
32   external shocks, which ultimately erode the purchasing power of Filipino households and
33   disproportionately affect low-income sectors.

34   Under the existing biofuels blending mandate, there are periods when blended gasoline and
35   diesel fuels are priced higher than pure fossil fuels due to the relatively higher production costs
36   of bioethanol and biodiesel. In the context of already elevated fuel prices, this additional cost
37   differential further compounds the financial burden on consumers, particularly public utility
38   drivers, logistics operators, small enterprises, and ordinary Filipino families.
39   At a time when fuel prices directly affect the cost of living, there is an urgent need for a
40   responsive and flexible policy mechanism that prioritizes consumer welfare without abandoning
41   long-term energy and agricultural development goals.

42   This proposed measure introduces a calibrated, objective, and time-bound authority for the
43   President to allow the temporary importation of biofuel components when a clear price trigger is
44   met—specifically, when blended fuel prices exceed pure fuels by at least five percent (5%). By
45   enabling access to potentially lower-cost biofuel inputs, the measure is expected to mitigate
46   pump price increases and provide immediate and tangible relief to consumers.

47   At the same time, the bill ensures that domestic stakeholders remain protected. All revenues
48   derived from tariffs, duties, and taxes imposed on such importation shall be earmarked
49   exclusively for social amelioration programs benefiting farmers and workers in the local biofuels
50   industry. This ensures that any temporary policy adjustment does not come at the expense of
51   those who rely on the biofuels sector for their livelihood.

52   Ultimately, this measure enhances the State’s ability to respond decisively to fuel price pressures,
53   ensuring that policies remain equitable, adaptive, and grounded in present economic realities. It
54   affirms that while the promotion of renewable energy remains a national priority, the immediate
55   welfare of the Filipino people must remain paramount.

56   In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

92       AN ACT AMENDING SECTION 5 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9367, OTHERWISE
93      KNOWN AS THE “BIOFUELS ACT OF 2006,” BY ADDING A NEW PARAGRAPH
94                       5.4, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

95    Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
96    Congress assembled:

97    SECTION 1. Amendment of Section 5.

98    Section 5 of Republic Act No. 9367, otherwise known as the “Biofuels Act of 2006,” is hereby
99    amended by adding a new paragraph 5.4, which shall read as follows:

100     “Section 5. Mandatory Use of Biofuels – Pursuant to the above policy, it is hereby
101     mandated that all liquid fuels for motors and engine sold in the Philippines shall contain
102     locally-sourced biofuels components as follows:

103          xxx

104          Within two years from the effectivity of this Act, the NBB created under this Act is
105          empowered to determine the feasibility and thereafter recommend to DOE to
106          mandate a minimum of two percent (2%) blend of biodiesel by volume which may be
107          increased taking into account considerations including but not limited to domestic
108          supply and availability of locally-sourced biodiesel component.

109          5.4. Notwithstanding the provisions of this Act on the prioritization of locally
110          produced biofuel components, the President of the Philippines may authorize, for
111          a period not exceeding one (1) year, the importation of biofuel components,
112          including but not limited to bioethanol and biodiesel, regardless of the supply
113          level of domestically produced biofuel components, under the following condition
114          as determined by NBB:
115               (a) That the price of blended gasoline and/or diesel engine fuels exceeds by
116          at least five percent (5%) the prevailing price of pure gasoline and/or diesel
117          engine fuels; and

118             (b) That such importation is deemed necessary to protect consumers from
119          undue increases in fuel prices and to ensure energy affordability and stability.

120          Provided, That all proceeds derived from tariffs, duties, and taxes imposed on the
121          importation of biofuel components during the authorized period shall be
122          earmarked exclusively for the implementation of social amelioration programs
123          benefiting farmers and workers in the domestic biofuels industry.

124          Provided, further, That the appropriate government agencies shall ensure
125          transparency and accountability in the collection, allocation, and utilization of
126          such funds.”

127

128   SECTION 2. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within sixty (60) days from the
129   effectivity of this Act, the Department of Energy, in coordination with the Department of
130   Finance, Department of Agriculture, and other relevant agencies, shall promulgate the necessary
131   rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

132   SECTION 3. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
133   or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

134   SECTION 4. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
135   or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

136   SECTION 5. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
137   the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
