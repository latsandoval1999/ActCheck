                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to strengthen the protection of agricultural workers and their
families from the health and environmental hazards arising from pesticide use in
agricultural sites. The duty of the State to protect the right of the people to a
balanced and healthful ecology, extends to the millions of Filipinos whose
livelihoods depend on agriculture. As one of the country’s largest employment
sectors, agriculture sustains a significant portion of the population, making the
safety and welfare of farm workers a matter of national concern.

       Despite existing government programs that support farmers through
equipment, training, and research, protection in actual farm working conditions
remain inadequate, particularly in areas where pesticides are heavily used.
International and local studies have long documented the prevalence of
unintentional pesticide poisoning worldwide, resulting in serious illness and
thousands of deaths annually.

      Many farmers and farm workers also lack sufficient knowledge on the proper
use, handling, and disposal of pesticides. These conditions endanger not only
workers but also their families through secondary exposure in homes and
communities.

       This measure therefore establishes concrete obligations for farm owners and
operators to adopt safety standards that reduce pesticide-related risks, ensure early
detection of exposure, and promote awareness and accountability. By prioritizing
the health and dignity of agricultural workers and their families, the bill places their
welfare at the forefront of public service and recognizes their indispensable role in
sustaining the nation’s food security.

      In   view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A UNIFIED NATIONAL EMERGENCY HOTLINE
“911” AS THE NATIONWIDE EMERGENCY TELEPHONE NUMBER FOR
PUBLIC SAFETY AND SECURITY, PROVIDING ITS FRAMEWORK AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “National Emergency
Hotline Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
maintain peace and order, ensure the safety and security of its citizens, and protect
its people through a unified and efficient emergency response system. To this end,
the State shall institutionalize a national emergency hotline, “911”, to enhance public
order and safety throughout the country.

       SEC. 3. Institutionalization of “911” as the National Emergency Hotline. –

          a. The telephone number “911” is hereby designated as the only national
       emergency hotline which shall be available nationwide for landline and
       wireless telephone systems;

           b. There shall be a “911” Emergency and Public Safety Response Center,
       hereinafter referred to as Call Center, in every region, province, city and
       municipality nationwide. The local government units (LGU) are enjoined to
       establish and run local 911 call centers within their area of jurisdiction which
       shall be under the supervision of the National Development Office;

          c. All calls received through the “911” National Emergency Program shall
       be recorded, monitored, and reviewed by the 911 National Development
       Office and shall be monitored through a Global Positioning System (GPS) to
       aid in the accurate and timely dispatch of emergency services;

    h.​ All private communication carriers and telecommunications

         i.​ In areas where an emergency telephone number does not yet exist, the

          j.​ All existing agreements in connection with the effective operation of

      SEC. 4. Creation of the “911” Hotline Commission. – There is hereby created a
“911” Hotline Commission, hereinafter referred to as the Commission, which shall
serve as the policy-making body and overseer of the Emergency 911 National
Program and shall be composed of the following members:

         a.​ Secretary of the Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG)
      as Chairperson;

         b.​ Secretary of the Department of National Defense (DND), as
      Co-Chairperson;

         c.​ Secretary of the Department of Information and Communications
      Technology (DICT) as Vice-Chairperson;

          d.​ Representatives of the following government agencies and offices with
      a rank not lower than Director IV or its equivalent, as members:

                i.​   NDRRMC;

               ii.​   DSWD;

              iii.​   DOH;

              iv.​    NTC;

               v.​    MMDA; and

              vi.​    PNP.

         e.​ Two (2) other members with a term of two (2) years, to be selected by
      the Chairperson from the private sector, upon recommendation of the
      Commission.

      SEC. 5. Functions of the Commission. – The following are the functions of the
911 Hotline Commission:



                                        3
         a.​ Provide policy direction and formulate necessary guidelines in the
      implementation and supervision of the Emergency 911 National Program;

         b.​ Prepare and recommend policies and solutions that are responsive to
      crime prevention, public order and public safety;

         c.​ Submit an annual report to Congress and the President on the
      implementation of the Emergency 911 National Program; and

          d.​ Perform such other duties as the President may direct.

        SEC. 6. The “911” National Development Office. – The existing DILG Hotline
“117” Development Group, activated by the DILG under the PATROL “117”
Commission is hereby institutionalized as the Hotline “911” National Development
Office.

       Under the direction of the Commission, the “911” National Development
Office shall function as the primary implementing office of the Emergency 911
National Program.

       It shall serve as the Secretariat of the “911” Hotline Commission which shall
consist of existing uniformed personnel of the interior sector and the non-uniformed
Plantilla personnel of the PATROL “117” Commission.

       It shall be headed by a Director with an equivalent position of Director IV, to
be appointed by the President, upon the recommendation by the Secretary of DILG,
subject to civil service rules and regulations. It shall have parallel offices in the
regional level to be headed by a regional officer and in the field level to monitor
provincial, city and municipal operations.

      SEC. 7. Functions of the “911” National Development Office. – The Emergency
“911” National Development Office shall have the following functions:

          a.​ Act as the Secretariat of the Commission;

         b.​ Serve as the primary implementing arm of the Commission in carrying
      out the objectives of this Act;

         c.​ Institutionalize localized networks of service responders upon the
      direction of the Commission;

         d.​ Conduct reviews and readiness tests, and monitor and evaluate the
      response capabilities of various aspects of the Emergency 911 National
      Program;

         e.​ Conduct reviews and monitor calls received by Emergency and Public
      Safety Response Centers nationwide;



                                          4
          f.​ Develop and implement training               programs       for   emergency
       communications personnel and responders;

           g.​ Process accreditation of NGOs, hospital and medical institutions and
       facilities, and other civic sector institutions, facilities, and volunteers in line
       with the objectives of this Act; and

          h.​ Undertake such other duties as the Emergency 911 Commission may
       direct.

       SEC. 8. Free Service to the Calling Public. – The Commission shall ensure that all
calls made to the “911” National Emergency Hotline shall be free of charge and other
fees.

       SEC. 9. Involvement of Other Government Agencies. – All government agencies
are stakeholders in the promotion of peace and order and public safety. They shall
provide the necessary support in the implementation of the program with the
Secretary of Interior and Local Government as the lead coordinator.

       SEC. 10. Participation of the Public Sector. – The private sector and NGOs are
enjoined to actively participate in whatever capacity in advocating crime prevention
and public safety by supporting Hotline “911”.

        SEC. 11. Penalties for Illegitimate “911” Calls. – A call shall be deemed
illegitimate when a person accesses the “911” National Emergency Hotline for the
following purposes:

          a.​ Making a prank call;

          b.​ Cause a false alarm, disorder, or unrest to the public;

          c.​ Making deceitful complaints or giving false information; or

          d.​ Harassing emergency responders by using obscene, vulgar or profane
       language which interferes or disrupts the emergency telephone service;

       For this purpose, a false report shall mean the fabrication of an incident or
alleged crime which the person making the report knows to be false at the time of
making said report.

       Any person found to guilty under this provision shall be punished as follows:

          1.​ First offense, a fine of Ten thousand pesos (P10,000.00);

          2.​ Second offense, a fine of Twenty thousand pesos (P20,000.00), or
       imprisonment of Arresto Menor;

         3.​ Third offense, a fine of Fifty thousand pesos (P50,000.00), or
      imprisonment of Arresto Mayor; and

         4.​ For succeeding offenses, a fine of One hundred thousand pesos
      (P100,000.00), or imprisonment of Prision Correccional.

      The foregoing shall be without prejudice to any other liability or penalty that
may be imposed arising from existing laws.

      SEC. 12. Appropriations. – The amount needed for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be taken from the current year’s appropriations of the DILG.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – DILG, as the lead agency, shall
promulgate the implementing rules and regulations within ninety (90) days after the
effectivity of this Act.

       SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, decrees, executive orders,
issuances, rules and regulations, or part thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
likewise repealed or amended accordingly.

       SEC. 15. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any section or provisions of
this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other sections or provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
