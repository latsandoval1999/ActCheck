                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

The National Capital Region (NCR) confronts severe traffic congestion that impedes economic
productivity, compromises public safety, and hinders sustainable urban development as the
nation’s principal economic center. According to the TomTom Traffic Index (2025), Metro
Manila ranks as Asia’s most traffic congested city and the Philippines being the third most
congested country in the entire world. Motorists losing 143 hours annually during peak periods
which is equivalent to nearly six full days and congestion levels rising by 0.8% from 2024. In
2025 alone, MMDA Chairperson Don Artes said that an average of 3.6 to 3.8 million vehicles
pass through Metro Manila roads daily.

Recent statistics highlight the escalating driver safety and economic toll. The Department of
Health (DOH) reported that road accidents surged to 1,384 injuries over the 2026 New Year
period, marking a 101% increase from the previous year. According to the Japan International
Cooperation Agency (JICA) study, daily traffic-related losses in NCR amount to P3.5 billion,
projected to reach P6 billion by 2030. Ordinary 10-kilometer trips now average 31 minutes and
45 seconds during rush hours due to fragmented public transport systems and overburdened
roadways.

This proposed legislation establishes a comprehensive, integrated, and modern mass transport
system across the NCR. According to MRT Line 3 specifications, a single modern train carries
1,182 to 1,576 passengers, equivalent to 40–50 cars, vastly reducing road traffic per trip. This
bill if passed will further support prior initiatives such as Executive Order No. 67, it mandates
coordination between the Department of Transportation (DOTr) and the Metropolitan Manila
Development Authority (MMDA) to deliver seamless operations, reduce reliance on private
vehicles, and advance United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 11 for sustainable cities.

Immediate enactment of this measure is essential to alleviate road congestion, mitigate accident
rates, improve commuter accessibility, and further minimize economic loss due to traffic.

                            AN ACT
  PROVIDING FOR A COMPREHENSIVE, INTEGRATED, AND MODERN MASS
   TRANSPORT SYSTEM THROUGHOUT THE NATIONAL CAPITAL REGION
            (NCR) AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “NCR Integrated Modern
Mass Transport System Act.”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote
efficient, safe, reliable, affordable, and environmentally sustainable public transportation in the
National Capital Region by developing a comprehensive and integrated modern mass transit
system.

        SEC. 3. Establishment of the NCR Integrated Mass Transport System. – There shall
be established a Light Rail Transit (LRT), Metro Rail Transit (MRT), Subway, Monorail, or
such other appropriate modern mass transport system covering the entirety of NCR, including
but not limited to major cities, municipalities, business districts, government centers, airports,
seaports, and transport terminals.

       SEC. 4. Implementing Agency. – The Department of Transportation (DOTr), in
coordination with relevant government agencies and local government units, shall plan,
construct, operate, maintain, and expand the NCR Integrated Mass Transport System.

       SEC. 5. System Integration. – The system shall:
       a) Interconnect existing rail lines, bus systems, and other modes of public
       transportation;

       b) Provide seamless transfers through common stations and unified ticketing systems;

       c) Integrate with provincial transport systems to serve commuters from adjacent
       regions;

       d) Include intermodal terminals and park-and-ride facilities.

       SEC. 6. Transit-Oriented Development. – The government shall promote transit-
oriented development (TOD) around stations to maximize accessibility, reduce travel demand,
and encourage sustainable urban growth.

        SEC. 7. Environmental and Social Safeguards. —All projects under this Act shall
comply with environmental laws, climate resilience standards, and social safeguards, including
fair resettlement and livelihood assistance for affected persons.

       SEC. 8. Public-Private Partnerships. —The DOTr may enter into public-private
partnership agreements and other financing arrangements to accelerate project implementation.

       SEC. 9. Appropriations. —The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the DOTr. Thereafter, such
sums as may be needed shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).

        SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. —Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOTr shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its
effective implementation.

       SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. - All laws and executive orders, rules and regulations, or
any part thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed
and/or modified accordingly.

       SEC 12. Separability Clause - In case any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, such declaration shall not affect the other remaining provisions, which shall
remain valid.

        SEC. 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its complete
publication in two (2) newspapers of general circulation in the Philippines.
