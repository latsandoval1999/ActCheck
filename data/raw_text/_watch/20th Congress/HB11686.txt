                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

      For many Filipinos who walk out of our jails and prisons, the real sentence
begins on the day of their release. They have paid their debt to society, yet the
stigma of imprisonment follows them to every job interview. Since most
employers require clearances from the National Bureau of Investigation and the
Philippine National Police, a single “hit” on a clearance is often enough to close
the door on a person who only wants an honest living.

       The number of Filipinos in this situation is large and growing. According
to recent data. more than 60,000 persons deprived of liberty were released from
prisons under the current administration as between January and September
2025,1 not counting the many more released every year from city, municipal, and
provincial jails, as part of the current administration’s push to decongest jail
cells. The BuCor itself has recognized the problem, launching in 2025 an
aftercare program with halfway houses and job placement support for those
about to be released. These efforts deserve the backing of law.

       Without work, a released person cannot support his or her family, and is
pushed back toward the same circumstances that led to the offense. With decent
work, he or she earns an income, rebuilds his or her identity, forms healthy
relationships, and regains a sense of purpose. Employment is one of the surest
ways to prevent a person from returning to crime.

      This bill gives released persons deprived of liberty a fair chance. It grants
employers who hire them an additional tax deduction equivalent to twenty
percent of the wages and health benefits paid to them, following the same
approach our laws already use to encourage the hiring of persons with

     Bagong laya, bagong buhay. A person who has been set free deserves the
chance to truly start again, and our communities are safer when they do.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
  PROMOTING THE EMPLOYMENT AND REINTEGRATION OF RELEASED
    PERSONS DEPRIVED OF LIBERTY, PROVIDING INCENTIVES TO
   EMPLOYERS THEREFOR, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Bagong Laya,
Bagong Buhay Act of 2026.”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – Pursuant to Article II, Section 11 and
Article XIII, Section 3 of the Constitution, the State values the dignity of every
human person and shall promote full employment and equality of employment
opportunities for all. Toward this end, the State shall support the reformation of
persons deprived of liberty and their reintegration into society upon release,
remove unjust barriers to their employment, and encourage the private sector to
employ them.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

      (a) Certificate of release refers to the certificate of discharge or release
          issued by the Bureau of Corrections (BuCor), the Bureau of Jail
          Management and Penology (BJMP), a provincial jail, or the Parole and
          Probation Administration (PPA), or a copy of the court order of release,
          acquittal, or dismissal;
      (b) Committee refers to the Inter-Agency Committee on the Reintegration
          of Released Persons Deprived of Liberty created under Section 4 of this
          Act;
      (c) Employer refers to any natural or juridical person in the private sector
          who employs a released person deprived of liberty; and
      (d) Released person deprived of liberty or released PDL refers to any person
          who has been detained or confined in a jail, prison, or penal institution
          and has been released therefrom:
            a. after service of sentence;
            b. on parole, probation, pardon, or other form of executive
               clemency; or
            c. by reason of acquittal or the dismissal of the case against him or
               her.

      SECTION 4. Inter-Agency Committee on the Reintegration of Released
Persons Deprived of Liberty. – There is hereby created an Inter-Agency Committee
on the Reintegration of Released Persons Deprived of Liberty, which shall be
chaired by the Secretary of Justice and co-chaired by the Secretary of Labor and
Employment. Its members shall be the heads, or their duly authorized
representatives, of the following:

      (a) Department of the Interior and Local Government;
      (b) Department of Social Welfare and Development;
      (c) Bureau of Corrections;
      (d) Bureau of Jail Management and Penology;
      (e) Parole and Probation Administration;
      (f) Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA);
      (g) Civil Service Commission (CSC); and
      (h) Two (2) representatives from civil society organizations working on the
          welfare and reintegration of persons deprived of liberty, to be selected
          in accordance with the implementing rules and regulations.

      The Committee shall coordinate the programs under this Act, monitor their
implementation, and submit an annual report to the appropriate committees of
the Senate and the House of Representatives.

      SECTION 5. Additional Deduction for Employers. – Employers who hire
released PDLs shall be entitled to an additional deduction from their gross
income equivalent to twenty percent (20%) of the total amount paid as salaries,
wages, and health benefits to such employees, subject to the provisions of
Section 34 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended: Provided,
That the following conditions are met:

      (a) The released PDL has been employed for a continuous period of at least
          six (6) months;
      (b) The released PDL is paid wages and benefits not lower than those
          required under existing labor laws;
      (c) The hiring of the released PDL does not result in the displacement of
          existing employees; and
      (d)    The employer submits to the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) a
             copy of the certificate of release of the employee, with the employee’s
             written consent, and proof of employment.

       The additional deduction shall be availed of only for the taxable years
during which the released PDL remains employed, and shall not exceed a period
of three (3) years for each employee.

      SECTION 6. Fair Chance in Hiring. – An employer shall not:

      (a) Inquire into the criminal record of an applicant in the initial application
          form or before a conditional offer of employment is made; or
      (b) Refuse to hire, or terminate the employment of, any person solely on
          the basis of his or her criminal record, unless the offense is directly and
          substantially related to the duties of the position.

      This Section shall not apply to positions for which existing laws or
regulations require the disclosure or verification of criminal records. Information
on the criminal record of an applicant or employee shall be treated as sensitive
personal information under Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the
“Data Privacy Act of 2012.”

      SECTION 7. Clearances. – The National Bureau of Investigation and the
Philippine National Police shall indicate in the clearance of a released PDL, upon
presentation of his or her certificate of release, the status of the case or sentence,
such as “sentence served,” “acquitted,” or “case dismissed,” as the case may be.

      SECTION 8. Reintegration Support Program. – The BuCor and the BJMP,
in coordination with the TESDA, the Department of Labor and Employment
(DOLE), and the Department of Social Welfare and Development, shall implement
a Reintegration Support Program for persons deprived of liberty, which shall
include:

      (a) Pre-release skills training, and assessment and certification by the
          TESDA;
      (b) Job-matching and referral services through the Public Employment
          Service Offices established under Republic Act No. 8759, otherwise
          known as the “Public Employment Service Office Act of 1999,” as
          amended; and
      (c) Halfway houses and aftercare services for released PDLs who need
          transitional support.

      SECTION 9. Employment in Government. – A released PDL shall not be
disqualified from employment in the government, except as provided by law or
by the penalty imposed in a final judgment of conviction. The CSC shall issue
the necessary guidelines to implement this Section.

       SECTION 10. Penalties. – Any employer who violates Section 6 of this Act
shall be punished with a fine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos (₱50,000.00)
but not more than Two hundred thousand pesos (₱200,000.00). If the employer
is a juridical person, the penalty shall be imposed upon its responsible officers.

     Any employer who claims the additional deduction under Section 5
without meeting the conditions therein shall be liable for the disallowed
deduction and the corresponding penalties under the National Internal Revenue
Code of 1997, as amended.

       SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Justice and the DOLE, in
coordination with the Department of Finance, the BIR, the Department of the
Interior and Local Government, the BuCor, the BJMP, the PPA, the National
Bureau of Investigation, the Philippine National Police, the CSC, and the TESDA,
shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the implementation of
this Act.

       SECTION 12. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the
implementation of this Act shall be included in the General Appropriations Act
of the year following its enactment into law and thereafter. An initial amount of
One hundred Million Pesos (PhP 100,000.000.00) shall be allocated for the first
year of its implementation.

       SECTION 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts not affected
shall remain in full force and effect.

       SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules, and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
