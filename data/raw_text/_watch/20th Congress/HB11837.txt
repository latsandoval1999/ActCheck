AN ACT
ENHANCING TRANSPARENCY AND ACCOUNTABILITY IN PUBLIC
INFRASTRUCTURE THROUGH THE PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE
MONITORING SYSTEM (PIMS) AND MANDATORY TIMELAPSE
DOCUMENTATION FOR ALL PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE PROJECTS

EXPLANATORY NOTE

Public infrastructure consumes billions of pesos in public funds and leaves
a permanent footprint on the lives of Filipinos. Every road, bridge, school
building, flood control structure, water facility, port, airport, and other public
work represents a public investment that must deliver what was promised, when
it was promised, and at the quality for which the government paid.

Yet accountability in infrastructure cannot depend solely on what appears
in contracts, accomplishment reports, inspection certificates, or financial records.
There must also be reliable evidence of what actually happened at the project
site.

This is the accountability gap that this measure seeks to address.

The government should not have to rely exclusively on periodic reports to
know whether a project is progressing as scheduled. Citizens should not have to
wait unti! a project is completed—or until an audit is conducted—to discover that
a project was delayed, substantially deviated from its approved plan, or failed to
deliver the expected physical output.

Modern technology makes it possible to close this gap.

Hence, this bill establishes the Public Infrastructure Monitoring System
(PIMS), a government digital platform that will provide continuous, verifiable,
and accessible documentation of covered public infrastructure projects. It
requires contractors to maintain and submit timelapse documentation and
project implementation data, while requiring implementing agencies to establish
project baselines, disclose planned and actual accomplishments, document
material changes, and make project information accessible through public
dashboards.

More importantly, the measure connects information with accountability.
PIMS will enable government and the public to compare what was planned with

===== [page 2] =====
what was actually accomplished; identify delays and material variances;
preserve a chronological record of project implementation; generate compliance
alerts; facilitate risk-based verification; and provide reliable electronic records
for auditing, investigation, and other lawful government proceedings.

This is not simply a transparency measure. It is a_ preventive
accountability mechanism.

The objective is to identify problems while they can still be corrected—not
only after public funds have already been spent and the opportunity for
corrective action has passed. By making project implementation more visible
and verifiable, the measure seeks to strengthen existing procurement,
engineering, financial management, and auditing safeguards and make it more
difficult for irregularities to remain hidden behind paper reports.

At the same time, the bill recognizes that technology must not become
another source of bureaucracy or unnecessary expense. It therefore requires
interoperability with existing government systems, technology-neutral
standards, cybersecurity and data protection safeguards, risk-based monitoring,
and mechanisms for emergencies and connectivity failures. It likewise preserves
the constitutional and statutory mandates of the Commission on Audit and other
oversight institutions.

Public money deserves public accountability. A government that can
digitally track financial transactions, procurements, and other government
activities should likewise be capable of showing the public the physical progress
of the infrastructure it finances.

The PIMS seeks to make that principle operational: what government
funds, government should be able to monitor; what government builds, the
public should be able to see; and what government reports, authorized oversight
bodies should be able to verify.

Through this measure, transparency is transformed from a passive
disclosure requirement into an active system of monitoring, verification, early
warning, and accountability.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is

earnestly sought.

AN ACT
ENHANCING TRANSPARENCY AND ACCOUNTABILITY IN PUBLIC
INFRASTRUCTURE THROUGH THE PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE
MONITORING SYSTEM (PIMS) AND MANDATORY TIMELAPSE
DOCUMENTATION FOR ALL PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE PROJECTS

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Public
Infrastructure Monitoring System (PIMS) Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to ensure that
public infrastructure projects are implemented efficiently, transparently, and in
accordance with approved plans, specifications, budgets, contracts, and
implementation schedules, and that public funds devoted thereto are used
prudently, lawfully, and for their intended purposes.

The State recognizes that timely, continuous, and verifiable documentation of
project implementation is an important component of effective public
infrastructure monitoring. The availability of reliable visual, geotagged, time-
stamped, and other project implementation data enables implementing agencies
and authorized oversight bodies to more effectively assess actual project
progress, identify delays, deviations, and other implementation concerns, and
facilitate timely corrective action and accountability.

For this purpose, the State shall promote the use of appropriate digital
technologies to strengthen transparency, accountability, efficiency, public
participation, and evidence-based oversight in the implementation of
government-funded public infrastructure projects. The State shall establish
mechanisms for the systematic collection, verification, preservation, integration,
and appropriate public disclosure of project implementation data, including
timelapse documentation, while ensuring data integrity, cybersecurity, privacy,
accessibility, and interoperability with existing government monitoring,
procurement, financial management, and auditing systems.

The implementation of this Act shall complement and not diminish the
existing duties and powers of implementing agencies, project engineers,
procurement officers, auditors, and other authorized government bodies in

===== [page 4] =====
relation to project inspection, certification, procurement, financial management,
contract administration, audit, and accountability.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

(a) Contractor refers to any person, partnership, corporation, joint venture,
cooperative, association, or other juridical entity duly licensed under Republic
Act No. 4566, or the Contractors' License Law, as amended, that undertakes,
contracts, or is engaged by a government agency to implement, construct,
repair, improve, rehabilitate, maintain, or complete a public infrastructure
project;

(b) Implementing Agency refers to any national government agency,
government-owned or controlled corporation (GOCC), state university or college
(SUC), local government unit (LGU), or other government entity responsible for
implementing a public infrastructure project funded wholly or partially from
public funds;

(c) Public Infrastructure Project refers to any construction, rehabilitation,
improvement, repair, or maintenance project involving roads, bridges, buildings,
flood control systems, water supply systems, seaports, airports, and other
physical infrastructure funded wholly or partially from public funds;

(d) Public Funds refers to all moneys collected or received by the
government, including national funds, local government funds, Official
Development Assistance (ODA) funds, and any other funds managed by or on
behalf of any government entity;

(e) Public Infrastructure Monitoring System (PIMS) refers to the government
digital platform used for monitoring and reporting the progress of public
infrastructure projects;

(f) Timelapse Video refers to a video recording produced by capturing images
at a set interval over a period of time and then played back at a higher speed,
resulting in a compressed visual record of construction activities occurring at a
project site;

(g) Project Site refers to the physical location where a public infrastructure
project is being implemented; and

(h) Geotagged Data refers to electronic data associated with a specific
geographic location through coordinates, location identifiers, or similar
technologies capable of identifying the physical location of a project site.

SEC. 4. Scope and Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all public infrastructure
projects funded wholly or partially from public funds and covered by contracts
entered into after the effectivity of this Act.

SEC. 5. Establishment of the Public Infrastructure Monitoring System (PIMS).
- The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), in coordination with
the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) shail
hereby establish and administer a Public Infrastructure Monitoring System, or

===== [page 5] =====
PIMS, which shall serve as a real-time digital platform for the monitoring and
reporting of government public infrastructure projects.

All public infrastructure projects covered by this Act shail be integrated into
the PIMS. The contractor for each project shal! be required to upload timelapse
videos under Section 6 of this Act, and to submit timely and accurate
implementation data, progress reports, and such other information as may be
required for the effective operation of the PIMS.

SEC. 6. PIMS Standards. - The DPWH, in coordination with the DICT, shail
ensure that the PIMS is capable of:

(a) Receiving, storing, and organizing timelapse video uploads from
contractors nationwide;

(b) Providing public access to all timelapse video uploads, subject to
reasonable limitations on bandwidth and data protection;

(c) Generating automated alerts to concerned government agencies when
contractors fail to comply with required upload schedules;

(d) Integrating timelapse video data with other project monitoring metrics
such as financial disbursements, inspection reports, and project milestones;

(e) Maintaining an audit trail of all uploads, modifications, and access to
uploaded materials; and

(f) Generating dashboard summaries and project status reports for public
viewing.

The DPWH and the DICT shall ensure that the PIMS is integrated and
interoperable with existing and future government digital platforms to promote
transparency, accountability, and effective monitoring of public infrastructure
projects and the utilization of public funds.

SEC. 7. PIMS Technical and Operational Standards. — The DPWH and the
DICT shall jointly establish minimum technical and operational standards for
cameras, recording equipment, data transmission, storage, metadata,
timestamps, geotagging, image quality, system availability, cybersecurity, and
data integrity.

The standards shall be technology-neutral and shail, to the extent
practicable, permit the use of commercially available and interoperable
equipment and systems.

The standards shall consider the geographic, environmental, technical, and
connectivity conditions of project sites throughout the country.

SEC. 8. Mandatory Timelapse Video Upload Requirement. - All contractors
covered under Section 4 of this Act shall be required to:

===== [page 6] =====
(a) Install and maintain at least one (1) timelapse camera at each project
site, positioned to capture a comprehensive view of the ongoing construction
activities. For large-scale projects involving multiple construction fronts, the
implementing agency may require the installation of additional cameras;

(b) Upload timelapse videos of construction progress to the PIMS at least
once every two (2) weeks, or at such intervals as may be prescribed in the
project’s Contract and applicable implementing rules and regulations, from the
commencement of construction until the issuance of the Certificate of
Completion and Final Acceptance by the government;

(c) Ensure that timelapse videos uploaded to the PIMS clearly capture
construction activities, are time-stamped, geotagged, and are of sufficient clarity
and resolution to allow effective monitoring of project progress and verification
of project conditions;

(d) Maintain physical storage or cloud backup of all timelapse footage for a
period of at least ten (10) years from the date of final acceptance of the project,
and make such footage available to authorized government agencies and
oversight bodies upon request; and

{e) Submit a written certification, signed by the contractor's authorized
representative and the project engineer, accompanying each timelapse video
upload, attesting that the uploaded footage is authentic, unaltered, and
accurately represents the actual state of the project at the time of recording.

SEC. 9. Project Registration and Baseline Data. - Prior to the commencement
of construction activities for a covered public infrastructure project, the
implementing agency shall ensure that the project is registered in the PIMS and
that its baseline implementation information is entered into the system.

The baseline information shall include, as applicable:

(a) Approved plans and specifications;

(b) Approved contract amount;

(c) Approved program of works;

(d) Contract duration and major project milestones;

(e) Notice to Proceed;

(f) Target completion date;

(g) Physical accomplishment schedule;

(h) Financial accomplishment schedule;

(i) Location and geographic coordinates;

(j) Identified construction fronts or major components;

===== [page 7] =====
(k) Approved variations, extensions, suspensions, or other authorized
modifications, when applicable; and

(1) Such other information as may be prescribed under the implementing
rules and regulations.

The baseline information shall serve as the reference against which actual
physical and financial progress shall be monitored.

SEC. 10. Project Dashboard. - Each implementing agency shall maintain and
regularly update a PIMS Project Dashboard for every covered public
infrastructure project under its jurisdiction. The dashboard shail contain, at a
minimum, the following information:

(a) Project title and description;

(b) Implementing agency;

(c) Name of contractor and major subcontractors, where applicable;

(d) Contract amount;

(e) Funding source;

(f) Contract duration and target completion date;

(g) Notice to Proceed date;

(h) Percentage of physical accomplishment;

(i) Timelapse videos and related progress documentation;

(j) Implementation status; and

(k)} Such other information as may be necessary to promote transparency
and accountability.

The PIMS Project Dashboard shall be publicly accessible through an online
portal.

SEC. 11. Planned Versus Actual Project Performance. - The PIMS Project
Dashboard shall, in addition to the information required under Section 7, provide

a comparison, where applicable, between the approved project schedule and
actual implementation.

The dashboard shall, at a minimum, enable the public and authorized
government agencies to determine:

(a) The planned physical accomplishment as of a specified reporting period;

(b) The actual physical accomplishment;

===== [page 8] =====
(c) The variance between planned and actual accomplishment;
(d) The planned financial accomplishment;
(e) The actual financial accomplishment; and

(f} The number of days of delay, if any, based on the approved project
schedule.

Where a project experiences substantial delay, the implementing agency shall
indicate in the PIMS the principal documented cause of the delay and the
corrective measures undertaken, subject to applicable laws on confidential! or
privileged information.

SEC. 11. Project Status Classification. - The PIMS shall provide standardized
project status classifications to facilitate public understanding and government
monitoring.

The implementing rules and regulations shall establish objective criteria for
classifications including, but not limited to:

(a) On schedule;

(b) Ahead of schedule;

(c) Delayed;

(d) Suspended;

(e) Under remedial action;

(f) Substantially completed; and

(g) Completed and finally accepted.

No project shall be classified as completed or finally accepted in the PIMS
unless the implementing agency has entered the appropriate certification or
acceptance information required under existing laws, rules, and regulations.

SEC. 13. Public Feedback and Reporting Mechanism. - The PIMS shall provide
an accessible mechanism through which members of the public may submit
observations, reports, photographs, videos, or other relevant information

concerning the implementation of covered public infrastructure projects.

The system shall, where practicable, allow users to identify the project
concerned and indicate the nature and location of the reported observation.

Reports received through the PIMS shail be referred to the appropriate
implementing agency for evaluation and appropriate action.

===== [page 9] =====
The submission of a public report shall not, by itself, constitute a finding of
violation, irregularity, fraud, or liability.

The implementing agency shall establish procedures for acknowledging,
ans and appropriately acting upon material reports received through the

SEC. 14. Project Monitoring History. - The PIMS shall maintain a chronological
project monitoring history showing material changes in project implementation
status, including, where applicable:

(a) Changes in physical accomplishment;

(b) Changes in financial accomplishment;

(c) Extensions of contract time;

(d) Suspensions and resumptions;

(e) Approved variation orders;

(f) Major changes in project scope;

(g) Changes in contractors or major subcontractors, where authorized;

(h) Issuance of notices of delay or similar notices;

(i) Corrective measures; and

(j) Completion and final acceptance.

The monitoring history shall be maintained in a manner that prevents the
unauthorized deletion or alteration of previously recorded entries.

SEC. 15. Data Integrity and System Audit Trail. - The PIMS shall employ
appropriate technical safeguards to ensure the authenticity, integrity,

traceability, and availability of information uploaded or generated through the
system.

The PIMS shall maintain an immutable or otherwise tamper-evident audit trail
showing, as appropriate:

(a) The identity or authorized account of the person or entity that uploaded
or modified information;

(b) The date and time of upload or modification;
(c) The nature of the modification;
(d) Relevant metadata;

(e) Access to restricted information; and

===== [page 10] =====
(f) System-generated alerts and compliance notifications.

No person shall alter, delete, suppress, or otherwise interfere with an audit
trail except through authorized system administration procedures.

SEC. 16. Cybersecurity and Business Continuity. — The DPWH and the DICT
shall establish and maintain appropriate cybersecurity, backup, disaster
recovery, access control, authentication, and business continuity measures for
the PIMS.

The PIMS shall maintain redundant backups of critical data in accordance with
applicable government information security standards.

In the event of a system outage, connectivity failure, natural disaster, or
other circumstance beyond the reasonable control of the contractor or
implementing agency, alternative methods of data submission and subsequent
synchronization with the PIMS shall be established in the implementing rules and
regulations.

Such alternative submission shail not exempt the responsible party from the
requirement to preserve the original data and submit it to the PIMS within the
prescribed period after restoration of the system or connectivity.

SEC. 17. Data Privacy and Protection of Sensitive Information. - The
implementation of this Act shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise
known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012, and other applicable laws and
regulations on privacy and data protection.

The PIMS shall adopt appropriate safeguards to prevent the unnecessary
disclosure of personal information, sensitive personal information, security-
sensitive information, and other information protected by law.

Public access shall be limited or appropriately redacted where disclosure
would violate applicable law, compromise legitimate security interests, expose
protected personal information, or otherwise be prohibited by law.

The implementing rules and regulations shall establish procedures for the
identification, protection, redaction, retention, and lawful disclosure of protected
information.

SEC. 18. Independent Verification and Risk-Based Monitoring. - The
implementing agency shall establish mechanisms for the independent verification
of reported project accomplishment using available inspection, engineering,
financial, geospatial, photographic, timelapse, or other reliable evidence.

The implementing agency may adopt risk-based monitoring procedures that
prioritize projects based on factors including:

(a) Project size and cost;

(b) Project complexity;

===== [page 11] =====
(c) Geographic or environmental conditions;

(d) Significant variance between physical and financial accomplishment;
(e) Repeated delays or extensions;

(f) Material discrepancies among submitted project data;

(g) Public reports or complaints received through the PIMS; and

(h) Other objectively established risk indicators.

Nothing in this Section shall diminish the audit authority of the COA or the
inspection and monitoring responsibilities of the implementing agency.

SEC. 19. Responsibilities of the Implementing Agency. - The implementing
agency shall:

(a) Include the timelapse video upload requirement as a standard provision in
all contracts for covered public infrastructure projects;

(b) Ensure the timely and accurate updating of the PIMS Project Dashboard
for projects under its jurisdiction;

(c) Monitor contractor compliance with the timelapse upload requirement and
incorporate such compliance into project monitoring reports;

(d) Include compliance with the timelapse upload requirement as a condition
for the processing and release of progress billings; and

(e) Ensure that no progress billing shall be processed unless the contractor is
compliant with all applicable timelapse upload requirements.

SEC. 20. National Inter-Agency PIMS Coordinating Committee. - There is
hereby created a National Inter-Agency PIMS Coordinating Committee which
shall provide policy coordination and oversight for the implementation of the
Public Infrastructure Monitoring System.

The Committee shall be composed of the following:

(a) The Secretary of the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH),
as Chairperson;

(b) The Secretary of the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT), as Vice Chairperson;

(c) The Secretary of the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG);

(d) The Secretary of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM);

===== [page 12] =====
(e) The Chairperson of the Commission on Audit (COA), or a duly authorized
representative;

(f) The Executive Director of the Government Procurement Policy Board
(GPPB), or a duly authorized representative; and

(g) Such other government agencies as may be designated by the Committee
when necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

The Committee shall harmonize policies and standards for the PIMS, promote
interoperability with existing government systems, resolve inter-agency
implementation issues, and ensure that the PIMS supports existing public
financial management, procurement, auditing, and infrastructure monitoring
systems.

The Committee shall not diminish the statutory mandates, technical
authority, audit jurisdiction, or procurement authority of any participating
government agency.

SEC. 21. Accountability of Responsible Officials. - The head of the
implementing agency and the officials or personnel specifically designated to
administer project monitoring shall ensure compliance with the responsibilities
imposed upon the implementing agency under this Act.

Willful and repeated failure by a responsible public official or employee to
perform a duty expressly imposed under this Act, without lawful justification,
shall constitute an administrative offense and shall be dealt with in accordance
with applicable civil service, administrative, and other relevant laws.

Nothing in this Section shall impose liability for good-faith errors, technical
failures, circumstances beyond the reasonable control of the responsible official
or employee, or other causes recognized under applicable law.

SEC. 22. Contractor Compliance and Corrective Action. — In addition to the
requirements under Section 9, the implementing agency shall establish
procedures for notifying a contractor of deficiencies in timelapse documentation
or implementation data and providing an appropriate period for correction when
the violation is capable of being cured.

Repeated or willful noncompliance shall be documented in the contractor's
project record and shall be considered in accordance with applicable
procurement, contract administration, suspension, and blacklisting rules.

SEC. 23. Procurement and Contract Integration. - The requirements of this
Act shall be incorporated into the appropriate procurement and contract
documents for covered public infrastructure projects.

Bidding documents and contracts shall clearly specify:

(a) The technical requirements for timelapse documentation;

(b) Upload frequency and reporting requirements;

===== [page 13] =====
(c) Data retention requirements;

(d) Responsibility for the installation, maintenance, operation, and security of
monitoring equipment;

(e) Procedures for equipment failure and connectivity interruptions;
(f) Requirements concerning authenticity and data integrity; and

(g) Contractual consequences of noncompliance, consistent with this Act and
applicable procurement laws and regulations.

The implementation of this Section shall be consistent with Republic Act No.
9184, otherwise known as the Government Procurement Reform Act, as
amended or replaced by subsequent legislation, and its implementing rules and
regulations.

SEC. 24. Emergency and Force Majeure. — Where compliance with a
particular requirement under this Act is rendered temporarily impossible by a
natural disaster, armed conflict, widespread telecommunications failure,
government-declared emergency, or other circumstance beyond the reasonable
control of the responsible party, the implementing agency may authorize an
appropriate temporary alternative method of documentation and submission.

The circumstances justifying the alternative method shall be documented in
the PIMS as soon as practicable.

The availability of an emergency or force majeure provision shall not excuse
the preservation and subsequent submission of required project records once the
circumstances preventing compliance have ceased.

SEC. 25. Limited Exemption for Protected Projects. - The requirements for
public disclosure under this Act may be modified, restricted, or exempted, in
whole or in part, for specific project information where disclosure is prohibited or
restricted under the Constitution or existing jaws on national security, defense,
intelligence, law enforcement operations, critical infrastructure security, personal
privacy, or other legally protected information.

Any exemption shall:

(a) Be limited to the information or documentation strictly necessary to
protect the legally recognized interest;

(b) Be supported by a written determination of the responsible authority;
(c) Identify the legal basis for the restriction;

(d) Be recorded in a manner accessible to authorized oversight and auditing
bodies; and

===== [page 14] =====
(e) Not be used merely to avoid transparency, conceal project delays, or
prevent legitimate monitoring.

SEC. 26. No Automatic Increase in Project Cost. - Compliance with this Act
shall not, by itself, justify an increase in the approved contract price, except
where additional costs are duly authorized under applicable procurement,
contract, budgeting, and accounting laws and regulations.

Any authorized additional cost directly attributable to compliance with this Act
shall be properly documented and subject to applicable procurement,
accounting, auditing, and reporting requirements.

SEC. 27. Evidentiary Value. - Timelapse videos, raw footage, certifications,
upload logs, metadata, audit trails, and other electronic records generated,
maintained, or stored within the PIMS shall be admissible as electronic evidence
in accordance with the Rules on Electronic Evidence and other applicable laws.

SEC. 28. Prohibited Acts. - The following acts are prohibited under this Act:

(a) Failure or refusal to submit or upload the implementation data, progress
reports, timelapse videos, or other information required under this Act;

(b) Uploading of altered, edited, fabricated, or misrepresented timelapse
videos;

(c) Deliberate obstruction, tampering, or destruction of timelapse recording
equipment;

(d) Submission of false certification;

(e) Unauthorized access to, deletion of, or tampering with timelapse videos
stored in the PIMS.

SEC. 29. Penalties. - Any person who willfully commits any of the prohibited
acts under Section 10 involving fraud, falsification, deliberate tampering, or
other intentional misconduct shall, upon conviction, be punished with a fine of up
to Three Million Pesos (P3,000,000.00), without prejudice to suspension or
blacklisting under existing procurement laws and any applicable civil or criminal
liability under existing laws.

If the offender is a juridical entity, the responsible officers who authorized or
knowingly participated in the commission of the violation shall be held liable.
Public officials found liable for willfully violating this Act shail, in addition to the
applicable penalties under this Act, be subject to the appropriate administrative,
civil, and criminal sanctions under existing laws.

SEC. 30. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Rules and regulations
necessary for the effective enforcement of the provisions of this Act shall be
formulated by the DPWH, in coordination with the DICT and the Department of
Interior and Local Government (DILG), and shall be implemented within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act.

===== [page 15] =====
SEC. 31. Appropriations. - The amount necessary to implement the
provisions of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations
of the DPWH. Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act (GAA).

SEC. 32. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not so declared shall remain in force and
effect.

SEC. 33. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders and rules and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or amended accordingly.

SEC. 34. Effectivity. - Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the implementing
rules and regulations, this Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
