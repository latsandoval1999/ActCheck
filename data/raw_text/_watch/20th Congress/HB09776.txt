                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The vital role of public school teachers in nation-building cannot be
overstated. As the backbone of the country’s public education system, they are
tasked not only with the intellectual development of the youth but also with various
civic duties that extend well beyond the classroom. However, the demanding nature
of their profession often takes a severe toll on their physical and mental well-being.

       Under Republic Act No. 4670, otherwise known as the Magna Carta for Public
School Teachers, educators are explicitly entitled to free annual medical
examinations. Despite this statutory mandate, full implementation remains a
challenge due to persistent budgetary constraints. Consequently, many public school
teachers are forced to shoulder the costs of routine checkups, diagnostic laboratory
procedures, and maintenance medications out of their own personal funds.

       This financial burden is counterproductive to the state's goal of maintaining a
robust and highly motivated teaching force. When teachers suffer from unaddressed
health issues, the quality of instruction and the overall stability of the classroom
environment are inevitably compromised.

       To bridge this gap, this legislative measure proposes the institutionalization of
a dedicated Medical Examination and Treatment Allowance for all public school
teachers. By providing a direct and reliable financial subsidy specifically for
healthcare, this bill ensures that our educators can proactively manage their health,
access timely medical interventions, and alleviate the financial strain associated with
medical upkeep.

      Providing institutional healthcare support to our teachers is a matter of
administrative necessity and social justice. It is an essential investment in the very
custodians of our educational system. In view of the foregoing, the immediate
passage of this bill is earnestly sought

                        AN ACТ
PROVIDING FOR A MEDICAL EXAMINATION AND TREATMENT
ALLOWANCE FOR PUBLIC SCHOOL TEACHERS AND APPROPRIATING
FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Title. This Act shall be known and cited as "The Medical
Examination and Treatment Allowance for Public School Teachers Act of 2026."


      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared to be the policy of the
State to promote and improve the economic status of teachers and assist them in
providing quality education to their students. To this end, the State shall also
promote the basic right of teachers to physical and mental health, which duty
includes protecting them against the consequences of employment injuries, such as
the effects of the physical and nervous strain on the teacher's health, among others.


      SEC. 3. Medical Examination and Treatment Allowance. A Medical
Examination and Treatment Allowance for the annual physical and mental
examination, treatment, and hospitalization is hereby granted in the amount of Ten
thousand pesos (P10,000.00) per teacher per school year: Provided, That this
allowance shall be exempt from income tax.




                                          2
       SEC. 4. Coverage. The grant of the allowance authorized in this Act shall be
granted to teachers as defined by Republic Act 4670 or The Magna Carta for Public
School Teachers Act.


       SEC. 5. Funding. The amount necessary for the grant of Medical
Examination and Treatment Allowance authorized under this Act shall be charged
against the appropriations of the DepEd under the General Appropriations Act (GAA).


       SEC. 6. Periodic Review for Increases. The Secretary of Education is
hereby mandated to conduct a periodic review of the Teaching Supplies Allowance,
taking into account the current prices of classroom supplies, and, if warranted,
recommend the necessary increase in the amount of the allowance.


       SEC. 7. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, resolutions, orders or
ordinances, rules and regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act, are
hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.


       SEC. 8. Effectivity Clause. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
