                                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE​

               “When justice is done, it brings joy to the righteous but terror to evildoers.”

                                                                                                         Proverbs 21:15

The 1987 Constitution declares that “the maintenance of peace and order, the protection of
life, liberty and property, and the promotion of the general welfare are essential for the
enjoyment by all the people of the blessings of democracy.”1 Under Republic Act No. 6975,
the Bureau of Jail Management and Penology (BJMP) is tasked to ensure the “humane
safekeeping” of the more than 113,000 persons deprived of liberty (PDLs) in our district,
city, and municipal jails.2 Yet a 2024 Commission on Audit report found 69.42 percent of the
BJMP’s 484 jail facilities congested, some at rates as high as 2,141 percent of intended
capacity, leaving roughly one (1) jail officer for every five (5) PDLs nationwide, and
stretching security to its limits.3

Against this backdrop, contraband continues to slip through our jail gates. In Cebu City,
authorities intercepted 27 cellphones, 25 chargers, and 10 headsets smuggled into a single
dormitory between 2019 and 2025, with BJMP officials themselves admitting that PDLs keep
directing drug transactions from inside their cells using smuggled phones, sometimes with
the complicity of jail personnel.4 A similar episode in November 2025 saw a Negros
Occidental jail recover a smuggled cellphone and suspected shabu, prompting charges

At present,      no comprehensive national law specifically penalizes the smuggling of
contraband into jails. Existing laws on drugs, firearms, and bladed weapons punish unlawful
possession but do not fully address the security risks within jails or provide uniform search
procedures and appropriate penalties for bringing contraband into jail facilities. Absent such
a law, local governments have improvised: a Cebu City councilor moved to pass a local
anti-contraband ordinance, explaining that “[i]deally, Congress should address this
nationally, but since no law has been passed, we are acting on it at the city level.” A problem
this widespread calls for a uniform national policy.6

This measure fills that gap by expressly penalizing the possession or smuggling of
contraband, as well as assisting or abetting a PDL in procuring it. It distinguishes between
illegal contraband, such as drugs, firearms, and bladed weapons, and nuisance contraband,
such as cellphones, recording devices, alcohol, and other items prohibited by jail rules.
Officials and employees involved in these acts face heavier penalties, including perpetual
disqualification from public office.

The bill also covers visitor briefing and registration, jail search procedures, and safeguards
for visitors, including prior written informed consent for more intrusive searches and the
option of no-contact visitation for those who withhold consent.

Through these penalties, procedures, and safeguards, the measure seeks to keep
contraband out of jail facilities, hold violators accountable, and protect the rights of PDLs
and visitors.​

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Title. This Act shall be known as the “Jail Contraband Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. Peace and order, the protection of life, liberty, and
property, and the promotion of general welfare are essential to the enjoyment by all people
of the blessings of democracy.

The State recognizes its responsibility to strengthen the delivery of basic services through
efficient and competent jail services.Pursuant to R.A. 6975, otherwise known as the
“Department of the Interior and Local Government Act of 1990,” the BJMP is mandated to
enhance public safety through the humane safekeeping of Persons Deprived of Liberty (PDL)
in all district, city, and municipal jails.

Towards this end, the State shall enact laws or provide a mechanism that will bolster and
intensify the campaign against the entry of contraband to maintain the stability and security
of jail facilities towards the maintenance of peace and order and enhancement of public
safety.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms.
  (a) "Body Cavity Search" refers to a search conducted to locate contraband hidden in
      body orifices, such as the rectum, vagina, or mouth. It can be a "visual" search (no
      physical contact) or a manual, "invasive" inspection.
  (b) "Conjugal Visitation" refers to scheduled private, monthly or periodic visits for a
      legally married Person Deprived of Liberty and their spouse. The visitation occurs in
       specialized, private and monitored conjugal rooms or facilities provided by the jail to
       ensure comfort and safety.
  (c)​ "Frequent Visitor" refers to a visitor who visits on a regular or scheduled basis or has
       visited the jail more than three times and who has previously registered and
       undergone briefing or familiarized oneself with the jail rules and regulations.
  (d)​ "Human Body Scanner" refers to a device that detects objects on or inside a person's
       body for security screening purposes, without physically removing clothes or making
       physical contact.
  (e)​ "No Contact Visitation" refers to face-to-face jail visitation where visitors and PDL are
       separated by physical barriers (such as glass or acrylic) and use intercoms to
       communicate.
  (f)​ "Person Deprived of Liberty" (PDL) refers to a detainee, inmate, or prisoner, or other
       person under confinement or custody in any other manner.
  (g)​ "Pat-Down Search" refers to a search wherein the duty searcher runs his/her hands
       along the outer garments of visitors to detect any concealed contraband.
  (h)​ "Strip Search" refers to a search that requires a person to remove his or her clothing
       to expose underclothing, breasts, buttocks or genitalia.
  (i)​ "Visitor" refers to any person who entered or intends to enter the jail facility, not
       being a BJMP personnel, for a PDL's visitation, civic or religious activities or any other
       legitimate purposes. This includes but is not limited to the PDL's immediate family,
       medical doctor, priest or religious minister, or counsel. The PDL's "immediate family"
       shall include his or her spouse, parent or child, brother or sister, grandparent or
       grandchild, uncle or aunt, nephew or niece, and guardian or ward.

SECTION 4. What Is Considered Contraband Under This Act. Contraband refers to
any article, item, or thing prohibited by and/or forbidden by jail rules that would pose a
security hazard or endanger the lives of the PDL, other visitors, personnel or any person. For
purposes of this Act, it is divided into two categories:
  (a)​ Illegal Contraband is that considered illegal in itself and not because of extraneous
        circumstances, such as:
        1.​ Prohibited Drugs and Paraphernalia — shall have the same meaning as that
              provided under Republic Act No. 9165, otherwise known as the "Comprehensive
              Dangerous Drugs Act of 2002," and all other illegal substances producing similar
              effects prohibited therein;
        2.​ Firearms, Ammunition, Explosives and Incendiary Devices — shall have the
              same meaning as that provided under Republic Act No. 10591, otherwise known
              as the "Comprehensive Firearms and Ammunition Regulation Act," and Section
              3 of Presidential Decree No. 1866, as amended by Republic Act Nos. 8294 and
              9516; and
        3.​ Deadly and Bladed Weapons — shall include those enumerated under B.P. Blg.
              6.
  (b)​ Nuisance Contraband refers to those articles/items that may not be classified as
        illegal under Philippine laws but are forbidden by jail rules, such as:
        1.​ Alcoholic or intoxicating beverages/liquors;
       2.​  Cigarettes, tobacco products, electronic nicotine delivery systems (ENDS),
            electronic non-nicotine delivery systems (ENNDS), heated tobacco product
            systems (as defined in Republic Act No. 11900, otherwise known as “Vaporized
            Nicotine and Non-Nicotine Products Regulation Act), and other similar devices
            and their derivatives;
       3.​ Cellular/mobile phones;
       4.​ Any camera (video or still) or recording device in any form, including those
            deliberately hidden (e.g., in shades, eyeglasses, headgear, ballpoint pens);
       5.​ Money in any currency that the jail authority considers excessive;
       6.​ Commodities of exchange such as jewelry, appliances and gadgets;
       7.​ Bladed weapons, pointed/sharpened objects;
       8.​ Pornographic materials;
       9.​ Firearms or incendiary devices;
       10.​ Gambling paraphernalia and other similar products considered vices;
       11.​ Any object designed or intended to be used as a weapon, to facilitate a
            detained person's escape from prison, jail or detention facility, or to harm any
            person within the said facility; and
       12.​ Any other object that threatens the order, discipline, operations or security of
            jail facilities.

SECTION 5. Briefing and Registration Requirement. Except for those considered
frequent visitors, all persons desiring to visit a PDL or gain entry into any jail facility shall,
before or during the actual visit, undergo a briefing on jail rules and regulations, including
the list of contraband and the procedures for entering the jail premises, to be conducted by
the BJMP personnel designated as a searcher, and shall accomplish a registration form which
shall contain the following information:
   (a)​ Full name and complete address of the visitor, who shall present any of the following
         documents:
         1.​ Valid passport;
         2.​ National Identification Card;
         3.​ Driver's license;
         4.​ Voter's Identification Card;
         5.​ Senior Citizen Card;
         6.​ School Identification Card;
         7.​ Company-issued identification card with photograph; and
         8.​ Other government-issued identification card with photograph.
   (b)​ Visitor's relationship to the PDL; and
   (c)​ Unless related to the PDL or the latter's counsel of record, the purpose of the said
         visit.

SECTION 6. Searching Procedures. The following searching procedures may be
allowed:
   (a)​ Pat-Down Frisking: This will be the routine mode of inspection for all visitors. Only
        personnel of the same gender as the visitor are allowed to perform pat-down
        frisking.
  (b)​ Strip Searching: This may be performed on visitors who:
       1.​ Show reasonable suspicion during an initial pat-down search;
       2.​ Are reported or suspected of facilitating the entry of contraband; or
       3.​ Are availing themselves of conjugal visitations.
  (c)​ Visual Body Cavity Inspection: When a visitor who was previously subjected to a strip
       search is suspected of concealing contraband within a body orifice, he or she may be
       subjected to a visual body cavity inspection.

In all instances of strip searching and visual body cavity inspection, prior written informed
consent is required. If the visitor declines to sign the informed consent, he or she may still
be allowed entry, but only through a "no-contact visitation."

In the alternative, if the jail or prison facility has installed a human body scanner, such
device must be utilized prior to entry of the visitor, thus dispensing with the aforementioned
searching procedures.

SECTION 7. Prohibited Acts. Any person who commits any of the following prohibited
acts shall be punished in accordance with the succeeding section:
  (a)​ Possesses or sneaks into, or attempts to sneak into, the jail facility any contraband
       defined and enumerated under Section 4 of this Act;
  (b)​ Assists or abets in the procurement by a PDL of any contraband defined therein; and
  (c)​ Being a PDL, makes, possesses, obtains or attempts to make or obtain contraband.

SECTION 8. Penalties. Any person found to be in violation of the prohibited acts under
Section 7 shall suffer the penalty of one (1) month and one (1) day to six (6) months
imprisonment and a fine of Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000);

In addition to the foregoing penalties, acts constituting offenses under the following laws
shall be penalized in accordance with their applicable provisions:

  a.​   For possession of prohibited drugs and paraphernalia as enumerated under Section
        6, paragraph A, item 1, he/she shall be penalized in accordance with the provision of
        Republic Act No. 9165, otherwise known as the Comprehensive Dangerous Drugs Act
        of 2002, including the disposal of the seized contraband.
  b.​   For illegal possession of firearms, ammunition, explosives, or incendiary devices,
        he/she shall be penalized in accordance with the provisions of RA 10591 and PD
        1866, as amended by RA 8294 and RA 9516, including the disposal of the seized
        contraband; and
  c.​   For a violation involving a bladed and deadly weapon, he/she shall be penalized in
        accordance with B.P. Blg. 6.

If the offender is a public official or employee, he shall, in addition to the penalties herein
provided, suffer the additional penalty of absolute perpetual disqualification from holding
public office and the forfeiture of all his/her retirement benefits and accrued leave credits.
SECTION 9. Forfeitures. All contraband confiscated shall be forfeited in favor of the
government. Unless the confiscation and disposition of contraband are governed by
applicable laws referred to in Section 4, paragraph A of this Act, all other contraband
confiscated shall, within fifteen (15) days from the termination of the trial, be turned over to
the appropriate LGU for proper disposal or shall be deposited to the general fund of the city
in case of cash.

SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within sixty (60) days from the
approval of this Act, the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and the
Bureau of Jail Management and Penology shall issue the necessary rules and regulations to
implement its provisions.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. If for any reason, any provision or section of this Act
shall be held to be unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions or sections hereof, which
are not affected thereby, shall continue to be in full force and effect.


SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after the completion
of its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
