AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE TEACHING OF THE WEST
PHILIPPINE SEA, THE 2016 SOUTH CHINA SEA ARBITRAL AWARD, AND
THE PHILIPPINES' MARITIME RIGHTS AND ENTITLEMENTS AS PART
OF THE PHILIPPINE HISTORY CURRICULUM IN BASIC EDUCATION

                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
In 2026, the Philippines commemorates the tenth anniversary of the landmark 2016 South
China Sea Arbitral Award, a historic legal victory that reaffirmed the country's maritime
rights under the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS). The
Award clarified the legal status of maritime features in the South China Sea and declared that
China's expansive “nine-dash line” claim has no legal basis under international law. It is a
defining milestone in the pursuit of a rules-based international order, providing a final and
legally binding resolution to disputes over maritime entitlements.
As emphasized by the National Maritime Council (NMC), the 2016 Arbitral Award remains
the Philippines' strongest legal foundation for defending its rights in the West Philippine Sea.
The Council described the Award as "not merely a legal victory" but "a beacon that guides
our national policy and collective action in protecting our maritime rights and ensuring that
the West Philippine Sea remains a source of peace, stability, and prosperity for present and
future generations of Filipinos." The Council likewise stressed that the Award is final and
legally binding between the Philippines and China and continues to guide the country's
maritime policy and diplomacy while promoting the peaceful settlement of disputes.1
Beyond its legal significance, the West Philippine Sea is an integral part of the nation's
history, identity, security, and economic future. It sustains the livelihoods of Filipino
fisherfolk, serves as a vital maritime corridor for trade and commerce, and contains valuable
marine and natural resources essential to national development. As such, every Filipino
should possess a clear understanding of the historical, legal, and geopolitical issues
surrounding the West Philippine Sea. Recent government efforts have shown that increasing
public awareness, especially among the youth, helps foster a better understanding of the

Recognizing that an informed citizenry is essential to safeguarding the country's maritime
rights, the present administration launched a transparency initiative to systematically
document and publicize developments and unlawful activities in the West Philippine Sea. By
exposing coercive actions at sea and grounding the Philippines' position in international law,
the initiative has strengthened public awareness, reinforced domestic support, and garnered
greater international solidarity behind the country's rules-based position.3
While the transparency initiative keeps the public informed of current developments, public
awareness must be sustained through formal education. Institutionalizing the study of the
West Philippine Sea and the 2016 South China Sea Arbitral Award in the Philippine History
curriculum ensures that Filipino students acquire, as early as primary and secondary
education, a factual and evidence-based understanding of the country's maritime history, legal
rights, and national interests. In this way, the measure transforms public awareness into
enduring civic education and equips future generations to appreciate and uphold the
Philippines' sovereignty, sovereign rights, and maritime entitlements through peaceful and
lawful means.
Despite its significance, discussions on the West Philippine Sea and the 2016 Arbitral Award
remain limited in the basic education curriculum. There is therefore a compelling need to
institutionalize the teaching of these subjects to ensure that future generations are equipped
with knowledge grounded in Philippine history, the Constitution, and international law.
This measure seeks to integrate the study of the West Philippine Sea, the 2016 South China
Sea Arbitral Award, and the Philippines' maritime rights and entitlements into the Philippine
History curriculum in primary and secondary education. This bill seeks to ensure that every
Filipino student understands the substance of this victory. It aims to cultivate historical
awareness, civic consciousness, patriotism, and respect for the rule of law while encouraging
students to appreciate the importance of safeguarding the country's sovereignty, sovereign
rights, and maritime interests through peaceful and lawful means.
By educating the youth on these matters, the State fulfills its constitutional duty to develop
informed, patriotic, and responsible citizens who understand the nation's rights and
responsibilities in the international community.
In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE TEACHING OF THE WEST PHILIPPINE SEA, THE 2016

EDUCATION, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “West Philippine Sea and Maritime
Rights Education Act.”
SECTION 2. Declaration of State Policy. It is the policy of the State to uphold its national
sovereignty, territorial integrity, and national interest in the pursuit of an independent foreign
policy, as mandated by the Constitution.
Consistent with the State’s duty to inculcate patriotism and nationalism in the youth, the State
recognizes that formal education is a vital tool in developing citizens who are informed and
protective of the nation's patrimony and maritime wealth.
The State adheres to the generally accepted principles of international law and the policy of
peace and cooperation with all nations.
Toward this end, the State shall institutionalize an evidence-based and constitutionally
grounded curriculum regarding the West Philippine Sea and the 2016 South China Sea Arbitral
Award to ensure that the defense of our maritime rights remains rooted in the rule of law and
national unity
SECTION 3. Inclusion in the Philippine History Curriculum. — The Department of Education
(DepEd) shall integrate the study of the West Philippine Sea, the 2016 South China Sea Arbitral
Award, and the Philippines' maritime rights and entitlements into the Philippine History
curriculum in all primary and secondary educational institutions, both public and private,
consistent with the national curriculum and existing educational standards.
SECTION 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
defined as follows:
   (a) 2016 Arbitral Award. refers to the Award on Jurisdiction and Admissibility and the
       Final Award issued by the Arbitral Tribunal constituted under Annex VII to the 1982
       UNCLOS in the matter of PCA Case Nº. 2013-19;
   (b)​Maritime Features. refers to the classification of geographical features as islands, rocks,
       or low-tide elevations, which determines their capacity to generate a Territorial Sea,
       EEZ, or Continental Shelf under Articles 13 and 121 of UNCLOS;​

   (c)​ West Philippine Sea. refers to the maritime zones on the western side of the Philippine
        archipelago, including the Luzon Sea and the territorial seas of Bajo de Masinloc and the
        features of the Kalayaan Island Group, as defined in Republic Act No. 12064, otherwise
        known as the “Philippine Maritime Zones Act.”


SECTION 5. Objectives. — In implementing this Act, the DepEd shall be guided by the following
objectives:
       (a) To foster among Filipino learners a deeper understanding of the historical, legal,
       economic, and geopolitical significance of the West Philippine Sea to the Philippines;
       (b) To educate students on the 2016 South China Sea Arbitral Award and its significance
       in affirming the Philippines’ maritime rights and entitlements under international law,
       particularly the UNCLOS;
       (c) To strengthen appreciation for the Philippines' sovereign rights and jurisdiction over
       its maritime wealth, consistent with the Constitution and applicable international law;
       (d) To cultivate informed, responsible, and patriotic citizens who are equipped with a
       critical understanding of issues affecting the country's territorial integrity, national
       security, and maritime interests;
       (e) To promote respect for the rule of law, the peaceful settlement of disputes through
       international law and third-party adjudication, and adherence to international legal
       principles in addressing maritime issues; and
       (f) To encourage historical awareness, civic consciousness, and national unity grounded
       in the factual and final nature of the 2016 Award.
SEC. 3. Implementing Rules and Development of Instructional Materials. — Within sixty
(60) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department shall, in consultation with the National
Historical Commission of the Philippines (NHCP), the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA), the
National Maritime Council (NMC), the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG), maritime law experts,
historians, educators, and other relevant stakeholders, promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.
The Department shall likewise develop, review, and periodically update textbooks, teaching
guides, digital resources, and other instructional materials to ensure that the subject matter is
accurate, evidence-based, and consistent with the Constitution, Philippine laws, and the
country's international legal commitments.
SECTION 6. Core Curriculum Content. — The curriculum shall be grounded on factual and
legal bases, including the 1987 Constitution, Republic Act No. 12065 or the Philippine Maritime
Zones Act and the Philippine Archipelagic Sea Lanes Act, applicable Philippine laws and
legislation, decisions and jurisprudence of the Supreme Court of the Philippines, the 2016 South
China Sea Arbitral Award, the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), other
applicable rules and principles of international law, and authoritative and internationally
recognized scholarly sources, journals, and publications.
Further, the integration of these topics into the Philippine History curriculum shall include, as
appropriate, the following substantive findings and determinations of the 2016 Arbitral Award:
   (a)​ The legal status of the “nine-dash line” and the primacy of UNCLOS, including the
        determination that claims of “historic rights” beyond the maritime entitlements
        recognized under UNCLOS have no legal effect;​

   (b)​The characterization of Scarborough Shoal (Bajo de Masinloc/Panatag Shoal) as a rock
       under Article 121(3) of UNCLOS, which is entitled to a 12-nautical-mile territorial sea
       but does not generate an exclusive economic zone or continental shelf;​

   (c)​ The status of Mischief Reef (Panganiban Reef), Second Thomas Shoal (Ayungin Shoal),
        and Subi Reef as low-tide elevations that do not, in their natural condition, generate
        maritime zones of their own;​

   (d)​The determination that Mischief Reef and Second Thomas Shoal are located within the
       exclusive economic zone and continental shelf of the Philippines;​

   (e)​ The protection and preservation of the marine environment, including the obligations of
        States under Articles 192 and 194 of UNCLOS to protect and preserve the marine
        environment and to prevent, reduce, and control marine pollution, including from
        activities such as destructive fishing practices and harmful land reclamation;​

   (f)​ The recognition of traditional fishing rights and the importance of protecting the safety,
        livelihood, and welfare of Filipino fisherfolk; and​

   (g)​ Other relevant findings, determinations, principles, and legal conclusions of the 2016
        Arbitral Award, as well as subsequent developments in international law and Philippine
        jurisprudence concerning the Philippines' maritime rights and entitlements.
SECTION 7.. Implementing Rules and Development of Instructional Materials. — Within
sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, in consultation with the Department of
Foreign Affairs (DFA), the National Maritime Council (NMC), the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG),
maritime law experts, educators, and other relevant government agencies and non-government
organizations, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.
The DepEd shall develop, adopt, and periodically update appropriate textbooks, learning
modules, teaching guides, digital resources, maps, trainings, and other instructional materials to
ensure that the teaching of the West Philippine Sea, the 2016 South China Sea Arbitral Award,
and the Philippines' maritime rights and entitlements is accurate, objective, evidence-based, and
consistent with Philippine law and applicable principles of international law.

Such instructional materials shall accurately reflect the official Philippine names and
internationally recognized names of relevant geographic and maritime features, as applicable
and consistent with authoritative sources, including, among others, Reed Bank (Recto Bank),
Fiery Cross Reef (Kagitingan Reef), and Johnson Reef (Mabini Reef). The DepEd shall likewise
ensure that maps and other visual materials accurately depict the location, geographic character,
and applicable maritime entitlements of relevant features in accordance with the 2016 Arbitral
Award and other authoritative sources.


SECTION 8. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the DepEd.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).
SECTION 9. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.
SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
and other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
SECTION 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
