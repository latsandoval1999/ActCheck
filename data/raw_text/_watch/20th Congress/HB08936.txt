                       AN ACT
MANDATING HAZARD PAY FOR TRAFFIC ENFORCERS AND AIDES,
STREET SWEEPERS, AND GARBAGE COLLECTORS, PROVIDING
IMPLEMENTATION AND FUNDING MECHANISMS, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Traffic Enforcers and
Aides, Street Sweepers, and Garbage Collectors Hazard Pay Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the state to afford full
protection to labor and guarantee humane conditions of work and a living wage; to
affirm labor as a primary socioeconomic force and promote workers’ welfare; and to
advance social justice, human dignity, and the general welfare, it is the policy of the
State to protect labor, promote safe and healthy working conditions, and ensure just
compensation for work that entails exposure to hazards, pursuant to Article XIII,
Section 3; Article II, Section 18; and supporting policies in Article II, Sections 9–11 of
the 1987 Constitution.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   a. Traffic aide refers to a person employed or engaged by a national government
      agency (NGA), local government unit (LGU), government-owned or -controlled
      corporation (GOCC), or government financial institution (GFI) whose primary
      duty is on-street traffic direction, management, enforcement assistance, or
      incident response on public roads.

   b. Traffic enforcer refers to a person employed or engaged by a national
      government agency (NGA), local government unit (LGU), government-owned
      or -controlled corporation (GOCC), or government financial institution (GFI)
      whose primary duty is on-street traffic law enforcement, direction, incident
      response, or management on public roads

   c. Street sweeper refers to any person engaged by a national government agency
      (NGA), local government unit (LGU), government-owned or -controlled

   corporation (GOCC), or government financial institution (GFI), whether as a
   permanent, temporary, casual, contractual, or job order worker, whose primary
   duty is the manual or mechanical cleaning, sweeping, collection, or removal of
   litter, debris, or waste along public roads, sidewalks, esteros, waterways, or
   public open spaces.

d. Garbage collector refers to a person employed or engaged by an NGA, LGU,
   GOCC, or GFI whose primary duty is the curbside collection, handling, loading,
   transport, or transfer of municipal solid waste, including collection vehicle
   crew.

e. Government contractor personnel refers to personnel of a private contractor
   performing any of the services described in (a) to (d) under a government
   procurement contract.

   SEC. 4. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to:

a. All traffic enforcers and aides, street sweepers, and garbage collectors
   employed or engaged by NGAs, LGUs, GOCCs, and GFIs, regardless of
   employment status (regular, casual, contractual, temporary, job order); and
b. Government contractor personnel while rendering any of the services
   described in Section 3 under a government contract.
   SEC. 5. Nature and Grant of Hazard Pay. —

a. Inherently hazardous assignments. — The occupations of traffic enforcer and
   aide, street sweeper, and garbage collector are hereby declared inherently
   hazardous for purposes of hazard pay entitlement under this Act.
b. Automatic entitlement. — Covered workers shall be automatically entitled to
   hazard pay for all days actually worked performing their covered duties,
   without need of case-by-case area certifications or discretionary approvals.
c. Contractor personnel. — Procuring entities shall ensure, through mandatory
   contract stipulations, that government contractor personnel receive not less
   than the hazard pay rates prescribed in Section 6 for all days actually worked
   performing covered duties. Such payments shall be a cost component of the
   contract price.
   SEC. 6. Rates and Computation. —

a. Daily rate. — Hazard pay shall be equivalent to not less than twenty percent
   (20%) of the basic daily wage/salary for each day actually worked performing
   covered duties.
b. Monthly minimum. — For full-time covered workers, monthly hazard pay shall
   not be less than Two Thousand Pesos (₱2,000.00), prorated for less than full-
   time service.
c. More favorable terms. — LGUs and agencies may grant higher rates subject to
   budgeting laws and regulations.

   d. Indexation. — Beginning the second fiscal year from effectivity, the Department
      of Budget and Management (DBM) shall review and may adjust the monthly
      minimum in subsection (b) to account for inflation and risk factors, subject to
      applicable budget laws.
       SEC. 7. Work-Related Accident Insurance. — NGAs, LGUs, GOCCs, and GFIs
shall procure and maintain, at their expense, a group accident insurance policy
covering all covered workers for accidents arising out of and in the course of
performing covered duties under this Act, including while on authorized duty travel
and official training directly related to such duties. For services rendered through
government procurement, procuring entities shall require contractors to provide
equivalent coverage to their personnel.

       Claims shall be processed in accordance with the policy upon proof of a covered
accident, regardless of employer fault, without prejudice to remedies against negligent
third parties. Payment shall be made directly to the worker or beneficiaries, or directly
to medical providers when covered.

        SEC. 8. Non-Diminution and Relationship to Other Benefits. — Hazard pay
under this Act shall be in addition to, and shall not diminish or replace, any existing
compensation, allowances, or benefits. Where other laws, ordinances, contracts, or
collective bargaining agreements provide more favorable hazard pay or risk
allowances, the more favorable benefit shall apply.

       SEC. 9. Funding and Appropriations. —

   a. NGAs, GOCCs, and GFIs shall charge hazard pay to their Personnel Services
      (PS) appropriations; savings may be realigned consistent with budget law.

   b. LGUs shall charge hazard pay to the PS component of their annual budgets,
      subject to the Local Government Code personnel services limitations; Provided,
      That there is hereby created a Hazard Pay Support Fund to be included in the
      General Appropriations Act (GAA) for the first three (3) fiscal years from
      effectivity to provide matching grants to fiscally constrained LGUs, as
      determined by DBM in consultation with the Department of the Interior and
      Local Government (DILG).

   c. For contracts covering services under Section 3, procuring entities shall ensure
      that the Approved Budget for the Contract (ABC) and the contract price include
      the cost of hazard pay, and that such cost is remitted by contractors to their
      personnel.

       SEC. 10. Implementation, Documentation, and Audit. —

   a. The DBM, in coordination with the DILG and the Department of Labor and
      Employment (DOLE), shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations
      (IRR) within sixty (60) days from effectivity, including standard payroll
      documentation, timesheets, and compliance templates.
   b. Procuring entities shall insert hazard pay compliance clauses in all bidding
      documents and contracts for covered services, require submission with each
      progress billing of payrolls, payslips, government contribution remittance

       proofs, and worker acknowledgments, and impose withholding or liquidated
       damages for non-compliance.

   c. The Commission on Audit (COA) may issue audit guidelines consistent with
      this Act and its IRR, and shall audit compliance by covered entities and
      contractors.

        SEC. 11. Protection Against Retaliation. — No covered worker shall be
dismissed, disciplined, or otherwise discriminated against for asserting rights under
this Act or assisting in its enforcement. Violations shall be administrative offenses for
public officials and grounds for contract sanctions for contractors, without prejudice
to civil and criminal liabilities under other laws.

       SEC. 12. Supersession Clause. — Notwithstanding Section 68 of the
Administrative Code of 1987 and any provision of the General Appropriations Acts,
rules, or issuances that condition the grant of hazard pay on area certifications,
discretionary approvals, or agency-prescribed rates, the entitlement, rates, and
conditions under this Act shall exclusively govern hazard pay for traffic enforcers and
aides, street sweepers, and garbage collectors. All inconsistent laws, rules, and
issuances are hereby repealed or modified insofar as they apply to the occupations
covered by this Act.

      SEC. 13. Transitory Provision. — Within ninety (90) days from effectivity,
covered employers shall adjust payroll systems and, for contractor arrangements,
amend existing contracts to incorporate hazard pay consistent with this Act.

        SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.
       SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or amended accordingly.

       SEC. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
