                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

    The Optical Media Board (OMB) was established by Republic Act No. 9239 in 2003 to
regulate the manufacture, mastering, and replication of optical media (CDs, DVDs, and
VCDs) during a period when physical piracy was at its peak. For over two decades, the OMB
served as the primary shield against the unauthorized reproduction of copyrighted content in
physical formats.
    However, the rapid advancement of technology has fundamentally changed how Filipinos
consume media. We have moved from the era of physical discs to a digital-first economy
dominated by high-speed internet, cloud storage, and streaming platforms. In 2026, the
relevance of optical media has dwindled to near-obsolescence. The regulatory framework of
the OMB, which focuses heavily on the "manufacturing" of physical storage devices, no
longer addresses the modern challenges of digital piracy and online intellectual property (IP)
infringement.
   This bill seeks to abolish the Optical Media Board and transfer its remaining powers and
functions to the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL). This transition is
guided by three primary objectives:
   1. Right-Sizing the Government: To eliminate redundant bureaucratic functions and
      optimize the use of public funds by merging agencies with overlapping mandates.
   2. Centralized IP Enforcement: To consolidate the country’s intellectual property
      enforcement under a single, specialized agency (IPOPHL) that is better equipped to
      handle both physical and digital IP rights violations.
   3. Modernizing Regulation: To shift the focus from regulating obsolete hardware to
      protecting content in the digital landscape.
    By integrating the OMB's enforcement functions into the IPOPHL, we ensure a more
streamlined, efficient, and cost-effective approach to protecting the creative industry in the
Philippines.
   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

AN ACT ABOLISHING THE OPTICAL MEDIA BOARD (OMB), REPEALING FOR
THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9239, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
"OPTICAL MEDIA ACT OF 2003," AND TRANSFERRING ITS POWERS AND
FUNCTIONS TO THE INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY OFFICE OF THE
PHILIPPINES (IPOPHL), AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

Section 1. Title. - This Act shall be known as the "OMB Abolition and IP Enforcement
Streamlining Act."

SEC.    2. Establishment. - It is the policy of the State to promote the efficient delivery of
government services and to optimize the use of resources by eliminating agencies that have become
redundant. The State also recognizes the need to protect intellectual property rights in a manner that is
responsive to technological advancements.

SEC. 3. Abolition of the Optical Media Board. – The Optical Media Board (OMB), created
under Republic Act No. 9239, is hereby abolished.

SEC. 4. Transfer of Functions, Powers, and Assets. – The powers and functions of the OMB
under Republic Act No. 9239 are hereby transferred to the Intellectual Property Office of the
Philippines (IPOPHL). All applicable funds, records, equipment, property, and other assets of
the OMB are likewise transferred to the IPOPHL.

SEC. 5. Absorption and Separation of Personnel. –

    1. Absorption: Affected permanent employees of the OMB may be absorbed by the
       IPOPHL or other agencies under the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), subject
       to existing civil service laws and the evaluation of their professional qualifications.

    2. Separation Benefits: Any employee who is not absorbed or who chooses to retire
       voluntarily shall be entitled to separation benefits and incentives under Republic Act
       No. 6656 (An Act to Protect the Security of Tenure of Civil Service Officers and
       Employees in the Implementation of Government Reorganization) and other existing
       laws.
SEC. 6. Transition Period. – A transition period of not more than six (6) months from the
effectivity of this Act shall be allowed for the full transfer of functions and assets. During this
period, the Director General of the IPOPHL, in coordination with the Department of Budget
and Management (DBM), shall oversee the transition.

SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the IPOPHL, the DTI, and the DBM shall jointly promulgate the rules
and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 8. Separability Clause. - If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall remain
in force and effect.

SEC. 9.. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders, decrees, issuances, rules and
regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 10. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspaper of general circulation.
