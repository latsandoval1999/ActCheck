                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

     The recent school shootings in Tacloban City, Zamboanga City, and Banga,
South Cotabato, have resulted in the loss of young lives and injuries to learners.
These incidents have brought renewed urgency to the duty to protect children
while they are in school. They also underscore the need for schools nationwide
to be prepared to identify threats, respond promptly to emergencies, and support
affected learners and personnel.


     School safety requires more than a response to an incident after it occurs.
Schools must have clear preventive measures, accessible reporting channels,
trained personnel, emergency protocols, and reliable referral systems. Learners
and school personnel affected by violence must also receive appropriate care and
assistance in returning safely to school.


     This bill seeks to institutionalize a National Comprehensive School Safety
Framework for all public and private basic education institutions. It requires each
school to adopt a School Safety Plan suited to its circumstances and establishes
standards for learner protection, violence prevention, physical and digital safety,
mental health support, incident response, and recovery. It further strengthens
coordination among the Department of Education, other government agencies,
local government units, families, and communities.


       The State has a duty to ensure that schools remain places where learners
can pursue their education without fear and where school personnel can discharge
their responsibilities in safety. In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage
of this bill is earnestly sought.

       In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

                                     TILE I
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Comprehensive
School Safety Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right of all learners and school personnel to an enabling,
safe, healthy, and supportive learning and working environment where all
learners and school personnel are protected and secured from any form of abuse,
violence, or harm.

       The State likewise recognizes that health promotion and healthy learning
environments are integral to school safety, and shall therefore promote
conditions, policies, practices, and environments that prevent harm, support
physical, mental, and social well-being, and enable learners and school personnel
to thrive.

Towards this end, the State shall:

   a) Adopt a comprehensive school safety framework that fosters a whole-of-
       society and all-hazards approach in the development, implementation,
       monitoring, and evaluation of school safety policies, programs and plans;

   b) Institutionalize an integrated school safety system for basic education
       institutions that ensures prevention from, response to, and recovery from
       threats and incidents relating to school safety.

   c) Strengthen coordination among the DepEd, national government agencies,
       LGUs, and other community stakeholders in the implementation of school
       safety measures;

   d) Ensure the provision of adequate human resources, infrastructure,
       equipment, technology, financial support, and other resources necessary
       for the effective implementation of this Act; and

   e) Promote     learner-centered,    fully   inclusive,   evidence-based,   and
       developmentally appropriate approaches in the design and implementation
       of school safety programs and interventions.



                                                                                2
SEC. 3. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all public and private
basic education institutions, hereinafter referred to as “schools,” including
elementary and secondary schools and other basic education institutions as may
be covered by the Department of Education (DepEd).

      The provisions of this Act shall apply to all learners, school personnel, and
other members of the school community and shall encompass measures for the
prevention of, response to, and recovery from hazards and threats to their safety
and well-being within school premises and in other venues related to school
activities, whether conducted on or off campus, including those conducted
through or involving digital platforms and technologies.

      Acts such as violence, child abuse, neglect, exploitation, bullying,
discrimination, harassment, gender-based violence, corporal punishment, and
retaliation committed by students or school personnel against students or school
personnel are prohibited.

      For purposes of this Act, school safety encompasses the protection and
promotion of the physical, mental, emotional, psychosocial, and digital safety and
well-being of learners, school personnel, and other members of the school
community, as well as the prevention and appropriate management of violence,
abuse, bullying, discrimination, harassment, exploitation, emergencies, disasters,
and other threats to their safety and well-being.

             CHAPTER I. SCHOOL SAFETY FRAMEWORK

SEC. 4. Establishment of the National Comprehensive School Safety
Framework. – The Department of Education (DepEd), in coordination with the
Department of Health (DOH), the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD), the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG), the Philippine National Police (PNP), Local Government Units (LGUs),
National Youth Commission (NYC), and other relevant government stakeholders
shall establish, maintain, and periodically update a National Comprehensive
School Safety Framework, hereinafter referred to as the “Framework,” to guide
the implementation of this Act in all schools.

      The Framework shall, at the minimum, prescribe national standards for the
prevention of, response to, and recovery from threats and incidents relating to
school safety, including:

   a) The development, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of School
      Safety Plans and School Safety Handbooks;

   b) School safety personnel, staffing guidelines, competency standards, and
      capacity-building;

   c) School safety protocols, infrastructure, facilities, equipment, and security
      measures;

   d) Learner protection, violence prevention, and anti-bullying mechanisms;

   e) Digital safety, responsible technology use, and cybersecurity;


                                                                                4
   f) Learner well-being, mental health promotion, counseling services, and
      psychosocial support;

   g) Emergency preparedness, disaster risk reduction and management, crisis
      response, recovery, and business continuity;

   h) Periodic engagement with parents and other community stakeholders;

   i) Meaningful participation and empowerment of learners and the youth in
      matters affecting school safety and well-being;

   j) Reporting school safety concerns such as those involving school bullying
      and other acts and forms of violence;

   k) Detection of health and safety concerns, as well as assessment, referral,
      case management, and inter-agency coordination to address health and
      safety concerns and mitigate risk;

   l) Monitoring, evaluation, reporting, and continuous improvement; and

   m) Such other standards as may be necessary to effectively implement this
      Act.

      The Framework shall likewise harmonize and integrate existing laws,
policies, and mechanisms on school safety, protection, health and well-being into
a unified system.

      The DepEd, in consultation with other stakeholders, shall develop and
periodically update policies, templates, manuals, protocols, reporting forms,
training modules, assessment tools, and other implementation resources
necessary to assist schools in complying with this Act.



                                                                               5
      The Framework shall be reviewed at least once every two years to ensure
that it remains responsive to new and emerging challenges in school safety.

SEC. 5. Establishment of School Safety Plans. – Every school shall develop and
annually update a School Safety Plan that localizes and operationalizes the
Framework to the school’s unique needs, risks, and resources, emphasizing the
safety, protection, and well-being of learners, as well as school personnel.

      The School Safety Plan shall be harmonized with other school plans related
to safety, school improvement, Disaster Risk Reduction and Management
(DRRM), child protection, and healthy learning institutions, among others.

      The Plan shall include:

   a) School safety standards, including standards for prevention, response, and
      recovery;

   b) Protocols and manuals for emergencies, including hotlines for reporting,
      designated hospitals, police stations, safety zones, evacuation areas,
      referrals for mental health needs, and a fixed-timeline referral pathway for
      serious threats and abuse;

   c) Clear roles and responsibilities of members of the school community;

   d) Protective safety measures, including security infrastructure and
      equipment;

   e) Policies on safe, responsible, developmentally appropriate, and healthy use
      of digital technologies, including gadgets, social media, gaming, artificial
      intelligence, and other emerging technologies, as well as measures
      addressing digital safety, cybersecurity, and digital wellness, on and off
      campus;

                                                                                6
   f) Clear mechanisms for recovery, such as intervention and reintegration
      plans for learners and personnel affected, coordination referrals to
      government agencies, and post-incident reviews;

   g) Guidelines and protocols for off-campus activities;

   h) Community engagement and partnership strategies to strengthen school
      safety, including coordination with parents and guardians, LGUs,
      community organizations, health and mental health service providers,
      security and emergency response groups, and other relevant stakeholders;
      and

   i) Monitoring and evaluation mechanisms.

      The School Safety Plan shall be developed in consultation with the school
community, including school personnel, learners, parents or guardians, and other
relevant stakeholders.

      The School Head shall be responsible for overseeing the implementation
of the School Safety Plan within the school. The School Head shall submit the
Plan to the Schools Division Office (SDO) for review and approval at the
beginning of each school year. The SDO shall ensure that the Plan is aligned with
the Framework and complies with the minimum standards set forth in this Act.
Upon approval, the Plan shall be presented to the Local School Board to facilitate
coordination with the local government and inform the development and updating
of relevant local plans and programs.

SEC. 6. School Safety Handbook. – Every school shall prepare, maintain, and
regularly update a School Safety Handbook, as part of the school’s Learner
Handbook, to communicate essential school safety information to learners,

      The School Safety Handbook shall present, in a clear, concise, accessible,

during emergencies and other school safety situations. It shall be made available
in Filipino and prevailing regional languages.

      At a minimum, the School Safety Handbook shall contain:

   a) The school's commitment to safety and learner well-being;

   b) The School Code of Conduct and key school safety policies for learners
      and school personnel;

   c) Procedures for reporting bullying, violence, abuse, discrimination,
      harassment, emergencies, and other school safety concerns;

   d) Emergency procedures for fire, earthquakes, severe weather, lockdowns,
      medical emergencies, evacuations, and other applicable situations;

   e) Emergency contact information for school officials, emergency
      responders, designated hospitals, and relevant government agencies;

   f) School maps showing evacuation routes, emergency exits, assembly and
      evacuation areas, clinics, School Care Centers, guidance offices, nearest
      hospitals, and other important locations;

   g) Visitor management procedures and campus access guidelines;



                                                                               8
   h) Learner support services, including counseling, psychosocial support,
      referral services, and School Care Centers;

   i) Digital safety guidelines and responsible technology use;

   j) Such other practical school safety information as may be prescribed by the
      DepEd; and

   k) Digital   wellness    and    responsible      technology    use,   including
      developmentally appropriate use of digital devices, social media, online

      digital habits; prevention of digital harms; and pathways for help-seeking
      and referral when technology use adversely affects learner health,
      development, education, relationships, or daily functioning.

      The School Safety Handbook shall be made readily available in printed and
electronic formats, as appropriate, and shall be disseminated to learners, parents
or guardians, school personnel, and other members of the school community at
the beginning of every school year and whenever significant revisions are made.

      Schools shall also conduct an annual orientation on the School Safety
Handbook to ensure that members of the school community understand their
rights, responsibilities, and the procedures for preventing and responding to
safety-related concerns.

      The School Safety Handbook shall be periodically reviewed in
consultation with learners, parents or guardians, school personnel, and other
relevant stakeholders.

                CHAPTER II. SCHOOL SAFETY POLICIES

SEC. 7. Policy on Safe School Culture. – Every school shall foster a safe,
inclusive, respectful, and supportive school culture that cultivates socioemotional
learning, empathy, peer connectedness, and the preservation of human dignity, in
accordance with applicable DepEd and DOH standards on the social and physical
environment of schools. Schools shall promote positive relationships, mutual
respect, responsibility, resilience, and meaningful participation among all
members of the school community through preventive, developmentally
appropriate, and evidence-based policies, programs, and practices that strengthen
positive behavior, respectful conflict resolution, and a shared commitment to
maintaining a safe learning environment.

SEC. 8. Policy Against Violence and Harm. – Every school shall adopt and
enforce a policy against all forms of violence and harm, including physical
violence, child abuse, exploitation, bullying, discrimination, harassment, sexual
and gender-based violence, corporal punishment, retaliation, and other acts that
threaten the safety, dignity, or well-being of learners and school personnel,
whether occurring in person or through digital or other means. Violence or
harmful conduct perpetrated by school personnel against learners shall be subject
to applicable child protection laws and appropriate administrative, disciplinary,
and legal measures. For incidents involving learners, schools shall ensure that
violence and harmful conduct are promptly addressed and shall implement
interventions that are fair, proportionate, developmentally appropriate, trauma-
informed, and restorative, where appropriate, with due regard to the safety and
rights of all persons involved.

SEC. 9. Anti-Bullying and Learner Protection Policy. – Pursuant to Republic
Act No. 10627, or the Anti-Bullying Act of 2013, every school shall adopt and
implement a comprehensive Anti-Bullying and Learner Protection Policy that
                                                                                10
promotes the safety, dignity, rights, and well-being of all learners and ensures the
prevention, early identification, prompt reporting, timely intervention, and
appropriate    resolution   of     incidents   involving   bullying,   cyberbullying,
harassment, including technology-facilitated harassment and abuse, violence,
child abuse, neglect, exploitation, discrimination, impersonation, non-consensual
sharing of personal or intimate content, retaliation, and other acts that threaten
the safety or welfare of learners. The Policy shall provide for prompt mental
health and psychosocial assessment, support, and appropriate intervention for
learners affected by bullying, including both the learner who has been subject to
bullying and the learner who has engaged in bullying behavior, in coordination
with their respective families and licensed professionals, as appropriate.

SEC. 10. School Code of Conduct. – The School Safety Handbook shall include
a School Code of Conduct that promotes a culture of respect, responsibility,
integrity, inclusivity, and accountability among members of the school
community. The Code shall clearly define unacceptable acts, corresponding
disciplinary and restorative interventions, and the rights and responsibilities of all
members of the school community. In implementing the Code, schools shall
ensure that disciplinary measures are fair, proportionate, developmentally
appropriate, non-discriminatory, and restorative in nature, with due regard for the
best interests of the child, due process, and the rights of school personnel.

SEC. 11. Learner Participation and Voice. – Every school shall recognize and
promote the right of learners to participate meaningfully in matters affecting their
safety, well-being, and school life, consistent with their evolving capacities and
the best interests of the child.

       Schools shall establish age-appropriate, inclusive, and learner-responsive
mechanisms through which learners may express their views, report safety
concerns, contribute to school safety initiatives, and participate in the
                                                                                   11
development, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of school safety
policies and programs. Schools shall provide opportunities for the student council
and other recognized learner organizations to participate in school safety
initiatives and such processes.

Learner participation shall be safe, respectful, and free from discrimination,
retaliation, or intimidation.

SEC. 12. Consistency with Existing Laws and Policies. – The policies,
programs, standards, and procedures adopted and implemented pursuant to this
Act shall be consistent with existing laws, rules and regulations, and applicable
policies of the DepEd and other relevant agencies on child protection, anti-
bullying, positive discipline, mental health, gender-based violence, safe spaces,
learner welfare, and other matters relating to the safety, dignity, rights, and well-
being of learners and school personnel.


                                    TITLE III
                     SCHOOL SAFETY GOVERNANCE

SEC. 13. School Safety Governance. – Every school shall establish a
comprehensive school safety governance system to ensure the effective
implementation of this Act and to promote a safe, secure, inclusive, supportive,
and resilient learning environment. This may utilize or expand on existing school
governance mechanisms, with clear designation of school safety functions and
responsibilities.

      Every school shall organize or designate an appropriate school safety
governance mechanism composed of school personnel responsible for learner
protection, counseling and psychosocial support, school safety and security,



                                                                                  12
health services, disaster risk reduction and management, and other functions as
may be prescribed by the DepEd.

SEC. 14. Key School Safety Personnel. – Every school shall ensure the
availability of qualified school personnel necessary to effectively implement this
Act.

       The DepEd shall prescribe staffing standards, competency requirements,
qualifications, deployment guidelines, training, and continuing professional
development requirements for key school safety personnel. Schools shall
progressively strengthen the staffing of key personnel in public schools,
consistent with national staffing standards and subject to the availability of funds.
Key school safety personnel may be designated from among qualified existing
school personnel.

   a) School Head. – The School Head shall serve as the overall lead for the
       implementation of this Act at the school level, and shall:

          1. Oversee the implementation of the School Safety Plan;

          2. Ensure compliance with national school safety standards;

          3. Provide leadership in fostering a safe, inclusive, and supportive
             school environment;

          4. Allocate school resources to support school safety initiatives;

          5. Ensure coordination among school personnel, parents, LGUs,
             concerned agencies, and relevant stakeholders;

          6. Oversee incident management and post-incident recovery; and



                                                                                  13
      7. Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the
         DepEd.

   Education Mental Health and Well-Being Promotion Act, the School
   Counselor shall serve as the lead professional responsible for guidance,
   counseling, learner mental health promotion, and psychosocial support,
   and shall:

      1. Provide counseling services;

      2. Conduct risk and psychosocial assessments;

      3. Lead mental health promotion initiatives;

      4. Coordinate referrals to appropriate service providers;

      5. Support case management involving learners requiring specialized
         intervention;

      6. Provide technical guidance to school personnel on learner well-
         being; and

      7. Perform such other responsibilities as may be prescribed by the
         Department.

c) School Counselor Associates. – School Counselor Associates shall assist
   the School Counselor in implementing learner support programs and shall
   facilitate access to appropriate guidance and psychosocial services.

         In cases where a School Counselor is not available, School
   Counselor Associates may serve as the lead professional for the delivery
   of school-based mental health services, while ensuring appropriate

                                                                          14
   referrals to mental health professionals, pursuant to Republic Act No.
   12080.

d) Learner Formation Officer. – Consistent with Republic Act No. 10627, the
   Learner Formation Officer shall serve as the primary focal person for
   learner formation and the first responder to reports of bullying and other
   learner-related incidents. The Learner Formation Officer shall:

      1. Receive, document, and facilitate the appropriate initial response to

         involving learners;

      2. Ensure that reported incidents are promptly referred to the School
         Head, guidance or counseling personnel, School Care Center, Child
         Protection Committee, or other appropriate authorities, as
         warranted;

      3. Coordinate the implementation of preventive and formative
         programs that promote safe school culture;

      4. Support the implementation of learner-led and peer-based

      5. Promote learner awareness of their rights and responsibilities, the
         School Code of Conduct, reporting mechanisms, and available
         protection and support services;

      6. Monitor patterns and recurring concerns involving bullying, peer
         violence, and learner behavior, and recommend appropriate
         preventive interventions;


                                                                           15
     7. Regularly monitor actions taken on referrals of incidents of bullying,

     8. Coordinate with classroom advisers, guidance or counseling

     1. Coordinate implementation of the School Safety Plan;

     2. Monitor compliance with school safety standards;

     3. Coordinate emergency preparedness activities;

     4. Oversee school safety inspections;

     5. Manage a non-threatening reporting system within the school;

     6. Coordinate reporting and documentation;

     7. Facilitate coordination with government agencies and emergency

     8. Support school safety training and drills; and

f) School Health Personnel. – School nurses, school physicians, school
  dentists, and other qualified school health personnel shall provide health



                                                                           16
      services and support school health and wellness programs consistent with
      existing laws and DepEd policies. They shall:

          1. Provide appropriate school health services;

          2. Participate in emergency preparedness and response;

          3. Support mental health and psychosocial interventions;

          4. Assist in health promotion and disease prevention activities;

      Where qualified school health personnel are not available on-site, schools

   g) Security Personnel. – Security guards and other authorized security

      Adviser shall:

         1. Monitor learner well-being and classroom climate;

         3. Identify learners requiring additional support based on indicators

   i) Student Leaders. – Student leaders, including officers of the student

         2. Promote positive behavior, peer support, and responsible bystander

SEC. 15. Responsibilities of Government Agencies. – For the effective
implementation of this Act, the concerned government agencies shall be
responsible for the following:

   a) DepEd. – The DepEd shall serve as the lead agency responsible for the
      implementation of this Act and shall exercise overall policy direction,
      coordination, supervision, monitoring, and evaluation of the National
      Comprehensive School Safety Framework.

                                                                             20
         In addition to its responsibilities under other applicable laws, the
   DepEd shall:

      1. Formulate, harmonize, and periodically update national policies,
         standards, guidelines, and operational manuals for school safety;

      2. Prescribe minimum standards for school safety infrastructure,
         equipment, staffing, programs, and services;

      3. Provide schools with technical assistance, capacity-building, and
         implementation support;

      4. Develop a model School Safety Plan, School Safety Handbooks,
         reporting systems, and other implementation tools;

      5. Establish national systems for school safety monitoring, reporting,
         and evaluation;

      6. Provide financial and technical support, to strengthen school safety
         implementation;

      7. Coordinate with relevant government agencies and stakeholders;

      8. Develop and periodically update, in coordination with relevant
         government agencies and stakeholders, national standards and
         operational guidelines on digital safety and responsible technology
         use in schools; and

      9. Perform such other functions as may be necessary to carry out this
         Act.

b) DOH. – The DOH shall serve as the technical authority for health, health
   promotion, health services, mental health promotion, disease prevention,

                                                                             21
   and healthy school environments, consistent with its mandate. It shall
   support the DepEd in the implementation and strengthening of school
   mental health and psychosocial services, and in supporting emergency
   medical preparedness. The DOH shall provide guidance in establishing
   referral mechanisms to DOH-retained hospitals, facilities, and health
   professionals.

c) DSWD. – The DSWD shall provide technical assistance and support to
   schools in handling cases involving children in conflict with the law and
   victims of abuse. It shall also assist in the provision of psychosocial support
   and rehabilitation services for affected learners, families, and personnel,
   and shall coordinate with schools for the proper case management and
   referral of incidents.

d) DILG. – The DILG shall facilitate coordination between schools and LGUs
   and encourage local support for school safety initiatives.

e) PNP. – The PNP shall assist schools in maintaining campus security and
   in responding to incidents of violence and other threats. It shall coordinate
   with schools and LGUs for the deployment of security personnel, the
   conduct of security audits, and the enforcement of laws on the prohibition
   of deadly weapons in school premises.

f) LGUs. – LGUs shall support implementation of school safety programs,
   encourage schools to participate in local emergency planning, strengthen
   community partnerships, and assist schools in disaster preparedness and
   emergency response, consistent with their respective mandates and
   available resources. They shall also coordinate with the schools in the
   implementation of community diversion programs relative to children in
   conflict with the law.

SEC. 16. Community Engagement. – Schools shall promote meaningful
participation among learners, parents or guardians, families, LGUs, leagues of
LGUs,    civil   society   organizations,   community      organizations,      youth
organizations, faith-based organizations, and other stakeholders in the planning,
implementation, monitoring, and continuous improvement of school safety
programs.

      Community participation shall complement and strengthen school-based
efforts to create safe, secure, inclusive, supportive, and resilient learning
environments.

      Schools shall establish appropriate mechanisms to ensure that stakeholders
are regularly informed, consulted, and engaged in school safety initiatives.

   a) Parents and Guardians. – Parents and legal guardians shall be recognized
      as essential partners of schools in promoting the safety, well-being, and
      holistic development of learners. Schools shall conduct a mandatory
      orientation session for parents and guardians on the School Safety Plan,
      safety protocols, and other pertinent information regarding school safety.
      Parents and guardians shall be encouraged to support the implementation
      of school safety policies, reinforce positive behavior and learner well-being
      at home, promptly communicate safety concerns to the school, participate
                                                                                  23
   in school safety orientations, consultations, and emergency preparedness
   activities, and cooperate with school authorities in implementing
   interventions affecting their children.

         Schools shall establish mechanisms to ensure meaningful parent and
   guardian   participation     in   the      implementation   of   the   National
   Comprehensive       School        Safety      Framework     through     regular
   communication, consultation, and collaborative interventions.

b) Parents-Teachers Association (PTA). – The Parents-Teachers Association
   shall serve as the primary organized partner of the school in promoting
   school safety and learner well-being.

         The PTA shall participate in the development, implementation, and
   periodic review of the School Safety Plan, assist in promoting school safety
   awareness among parents and families, support school programs on school
   safety, assist the school in mobilizing community participation and
   volunteer support for school safety initiatives, and provide feedback on
   school safety concerns affecting learners and parents, subject to guidelines
   prescribed by the DepEd.

c) Child Protection Committee (CPC). – Every school shall establish a Child
   Protection Committee (CPC), composed of the School Head, School Safety
   Officer, Learner Formation Officer, and designated representatives of
   learners, teachers, parents, and the community. The CPC shall support the
   school in the prevention of and response to child abuse, violence,
   exploitation, discrimination, bullying, and other child protection concerns.
   The CPC shall, as may be applicable, assist in the implementation of child
   protection policies and preventive programs; facilitate the appropriate
   handling, referral, and follow-up of child protection concerns; coordinate

                                                                                24
      with relevant school personnel, parents or guardians, the Barangay Council
      for the Protection of Children (BCPC), the City or Municipal Social
      Welfare and Development Office (C/MSWDO), and other appropriate
      authorities; and recommend measures to strengthen the school’s child
      protection mechanisms.

              The establishment or functioning of a CPC shall not relieve the
      School Head or any other responsible school personnel of their duties and
      accountability under this Act and other applicable child protection laws,
      rules, and policies.

              The DepEd shall encourage and facilitate community partnerships
      that strengthen the implementation of this Act.




                                   TITLE IV
                                PREVENTION

    CHAPTER I. School Safety Infrastructure and Protective Measures

SEC. 17. Safety Infrastructure and Equipment. – Schools shall be equipped
with appropriate safety infrastructure and security equipment, including, as
applicable:

   a) Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV). - CCTV cameras shall be installed in
      common areas, entrances and exits, perimeter areas, and other locations
      identified through the school's risk assessment. CCTV shall not be installed
      in areas where there is a reasonable expectation of privacy. The collection,
      storage, access, use, and disposal of CCTV footage shall comply with
      applicable data privacy laws and regulations and shall be used solely for
      investigating documented bullying or security incidents.
                                                                               25
   b) Screening Equipment. – Schools shall have access to appropriate screening
      equipment, including metal detectors, where warranted by the school's risk
      profile. Screening shall be conducted by authorized and appropriately
      trained personnel in a reasonable, proportionate, non-discriminatory, and
      child-sensitive manner.

   c) Visitor Management System. – Schools shall establish appropriate systems
      to regulate and monitor visitor entry into and movement within school
      premises, including visitor identification, registration, verification, and
      access control.

   d) Security Personnel and Protective Presence. – Schools shall ensure
      appropriate security and protective presence based on their size,
      enrollment, location, and risk profile. This may include licensed security
      guards and coordinated support from the Philippine National Police (PNP),
      barangay tanods, safety officers, community volunteers, and other
      authorized personnel.

   e) Perimeter and Access Security. – Schools shall maintain appropriate
      physical measures to secure school boundaries and regulate access,
      including, as appropriate, gates, fencing, lighting, locks, barriers, and other
      protective infrastructure.

      SEC. 18. Emergency, Health, and Life-Safety Equipment. – Schools shall
be equipped with appropriate emergency, health, and life-safety equipment,
including, as applicable:

   a) first aid kits and essential emergency medical supplies;

   b) fire extinguishers and other required fire safety equipment;



                                                                                  26
   c) emergency lighting, flashlights, batteries, emergency power supplies, and
      other disaster preparedness supplies;

   d) rescue and evacuation equipment appropriate to identified hazards; and

   e) emergency communication and alert systems, including public address
      systems, alarms, emergency notification systems, two-way radios, and
      other equipment.

      SEC. 19. Preventive Safety Measures. – Every school shall implement
preventive safety measures appropriate to its circumstances to reduce risks,
strengthen preparedness, and promote a safe and secure learning environment. At
a minimum, schools shall implement:

   a) Risk Mapping and Assessment. – Regularly identify physical, social,
      behavioral, environmental, and digital risks, including locations and
      circumstances that may increase the likelihood of violence, bullying,
      abuse, harassment, accidents, emergencies, digital harm, harmful or unsafe
      technology use, or other threats to learner safety, health, development, and
      well-being. Findings shall inform the School Safety Plan and preventive
      interventions.

   b) Campus Security Monitoring. – Maintain appropriate monitoring
      measures, including security patrols, monitoring of entrances and exits,
      observation of common and high-risk areas, and review of security camera
      footage where available, consistent with the school's risk profile and
      applicable privacy and child protection laws.

   c) Visitor Screening. – Establish procedures for visitor identification,
      registration, verification, issuance of passes where appropriate, and



                                                                               27
   monitoring of visitor access, while ensuring access for emergency
   responders and other authorized persons.

d) Mandatory Screening and Inspection. – Conduct mandatory screening of
   persons and belongings, including bag inspections or use of metal
   detectors, where authorized and warranted by the school's risk assessment
   and applicable DepEd standards. Screening shall be conducted by
   authorized personnel in a respectful, non-discriminatory, child-sensitive,
   and developmentally appropriate manner.

e) Confiscation and Disposition of Prohibited Items. – Establish procedures
   for the identification, confiscation, documentation, safekeeping, turnover,
   and lawful disposition of prohibited items, including appropriate due
   process, notification of parents or guardians when necessary, and
   coordination with authorities when required by law.

f) Emergency Drills. – Regularly conduct developmentally appropriate,
   trauma-informed, and inclusive drills for fires, earthquakes, severe
   weather, medical emergencies, active attacks, other security incidents, and
   other relevant hazards. Such drills shall be designed to prepare learners and
   school personnel to respond appropriately to emergencies while
   minimizing unnecessary fear or distress and shall be conducted with
   appropriate advance notice and in consultation with relevant professionals.

g) Safety Orientation and Awareness. – Provide developmentally appropriate
   and role-appropriate safety orientation for learners, school personnel,
   parents and guardians, and other members of the school community. These
   orientations shall cover the National Comprehensive School Safety
   Framework and the School Safety Plan, protocols for prevention from,



                                                                             28
      response to, and recovery from school safety threats and incidents, digital
      safety, and responsible technology use.

   h) Safety Audits and Preventive Maintenance. – Conduct an annual School
      Safety Audit and regular inspections of infrastructure, facilities,
      equipment, emergency systems, and other safety measures, and implement
      corrective and preventive measures based on identified hazards and
      deficiencies. Findings shall inform the School Safety Plan and resource
      priorities.

      SEC. 20. General Standards for School Safety Measures. – All school
safety policies, infrastructure, equipment, and measures under this Chapter shall
be appropriate to the school's size, enrollment, location, risk profile, specific
needs, and subject to the availability of funds.

      The DepEd, in coordination with relevant national government agencies
and other stakeholders, shall establish national standards and operational
guidelines for the provision, implementation, use, maintenance, and monitoring
of infrastructure and equipment under this Chapter, including, where applicable,
their installation, inspection, replacement, and data management and disposal.

SEC. 21. Nature- and Climate-Related School Safety Protocols. – The DepEd,
in coordination with the DILG, DOST, DOH, DENR, DPWH and other relevant
agencies, shall establish and periodically update national policies for the
assessment, management, risk-reduction and recovery from nature- and climate-
related hazards affecting schools. These standards shall cover extreme heat
conditions, tropical cyclones, flooding, earthquakes, air quality, and other related
hazards that may endanger the safety of learners and school personnel.

      The policy shall include a systemic plan for the construction of disaster-
resilient infrastructure, and assessment and prioritization for retrofitting or
                                                                                 29
replacement of unsafe building structures, taking into consideration the natural-
and climate-related hazards of the area.




            CHAPTER II. Safety During Off-Campus Activities

SEC. 22. Safety During Off-Campus Activities. – Schools conducting authorized
off-campus activities shall adopt and implement measures to ensure the safety,
security, health, and well-being of learners and school personnel participating in
such activities. Schools shall take reasonable and appropriate measures to prevent
foreseeable harm to learners and school personnel throughout the activity.

      Prior to any authorized off-campus activity, the school shall conduct an
appropriate risk assessment and prepare an activity-specific safety plan, which
shall include learner supervision arrangements and designated personnel-in-
charge, transportation safety measures, emergency communication and response
procedures, emergency contact information for participating learners, and such
other safety measures as may be prescribed by DepEd.

      Schools shall also ensure full disclosure to parents or guardians of
information regarding the activity, including the objectives of the itinerary,
transport   arrangements,    accommodations,     identified   risks,   emergency
procedures, and contact information of responsible personnel. Schools shall
likewise ensure that reasonable safety equipment is made available during the
activity, and that appropriate informed consent is secured in accordance with
applicable laws and DepEd policies.

      The DepEd shall prescribe minimum operational standards governing
educational tours, work immersion, athletic competitions, cultural activities,
community engagement activities, and other authorized off-campus activities.

                                                                               30
       CHAPTER III. Digital Safety and Responsible Technology Use

SEC. 23. Digital Safety and Responsible Technology Use. – Schools shall adopt
and implement comprehensive policies and programs to ensure that the use of
digital technologies in teaching, learning, communication, and school operations
is safe, responsible, ethical, age-appropriate, and consistent with the best interests
and developmental needs of learners.

      Such policies and programs shall promote digital wellness and responsible
technology use, including developmentally appropriate use of digital devices,
social media, gaming, artificial intelligence, and other emerging technologies.

      Schools shall establish measures to protect learners and school personnel
from cyberbullying, online abuse, cybersecurity risks, and other forms of digital
harm. Such measures shall include, as appropriate, the prevention and early
identification of digital-related harm and harmful or unsafe technology use,
appropriate intervention and referral mechanisms, media and information literacy
programs, orientation programs for parents and guardians, procedures for
reporting and responding to digital safety incidents, and other digital safety
measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

      Schools shall prohibit the use of mobile devices and other electronic
gadgets by learners within school premises during class hours, except for
learning-related activities, health and well-being-related activities, and purposes
related to safety, emergency response, or risk management, including in response
to a perceived threat or danger, and during field trips and other school-related
activities conducted outside school premises.

      Implementation of this Section shall be consistent with applicable laws on
child protection, data privacy, cybersecurity, and online safety.


                                                                                   31
                 CHAPTER IV. LEARNER WELL-BEING

SEC. 24. Learner Well-Being and Psychosocial Support. – Schools shall
establish and implement a comprehensive, coordinated, and age-appropriate
system for promoting learner health and well-being, providing mental health
services, and ensuring psychosocial support, consistent with the principles of
prevention, early identification, appropriate referral, inclusion, and protection of
learners.

   a) Health and Well-Being Promotion. – Schools shall promote the physical,
      mental, emotional, and social health and well-being of learners through
      healthy learning environments, health-promoting policies and practices,
      health education and skills development, and measures that address the
      conditions and determinants that influence learner health and well-being.
      Preventive health promotion and the development of healthy learning
      environments shall be implemented as integral components of school
      safety and shall be guided by applicable policies of the DepEd and the
      DOH.

   b) Mental Health Services. – Schools shall facilitate access to appropriate
      mental health services, including prevention, early identification,
      assessment, treatment, referral, and other professional interventions, in
      accordance with Republic Act No. 12080 and other applicable laws, rules,
      standards, and guidelines. Professional mental health services shall be
      provided by appropriately qualified and authorized mental health
      professionals within their respective scopes of practice.

   c) Psychosocial Support. – Schools shall provide or facilitate appropriate
      psychosocial support to learners experiencing stress, distress, crisis,
      trauma, loss, violence, abuse, displacement, disasters, or other

                                                                                 32
      circumstances affecting their well-being. Such support may include
      psychological first aid, supportive and responsive school-based
      interventions, peer and social support, referral and linkage to appropriate
      services, and other non-clinical measures that strengthen learners’ coping,
      resilience, connectedness, and sense of safety and belonging. Psychosocial
      support shall complement, and shall not substitute for, professional mental
      health services when such services are required.




                                      TITLE V
                                    RESPONSE

SEC. 25. Incident Reporting, Assessment and Immediate Response. – Every
school shall establish and maintain responsive mechanisms and protocols for the
reporting, assessment, protection interventions, and immediate response to school
safety concerns involving learners, school personnel, and other members of the
school community. Such mechanisms shall form part of the School Safety Plan
and shall be implemented in accordance with this Act and applicable laws.

      Schools shall ensure that the implementation of this Section is guided by
the principles of child protection and the best interests of the child, confidentiality
and   respect    for   privacy,    timely    intervention,   trauma-informed       and
developmentally appropriate practices, due process, coordinated inter-agency
response, and survivor-centered, rights-based, and restorative approaches, where
appropriate.

      Schools shall ensure the availability of multiple reporting channels, which
may include in-person reporting, anonymous reporting systems, digital reporting
platforms, designated reporting officers, emergency hotlines, and such other
reporting mechanisms as may be prescribed by the DepEd.
                                                                                    33
      Upon receipt of a report, the school shall:

   a) Immediately record the report;

   b) Conduct an initial risk assessment;

   c) Determine whether immediate protective measures are necessary;

   d) Notify the School Head and appropriate school personnel;

   e) Notify parents or guardians, where appropriate;

   f) Activate emergency response procedures when necessary;

   g) Coordinate with the PNP, LGU, and other relevant local authorities in
      cases involving violence, imminent threats to safety or security, and other
      emergencies;

   h) Document actions taken; and

   i) Determine whether referral or case management should be initiated.

      Where the initial risk assessment identifies indicators of suicide risk, self-
harm, active psychosis, or imminent danger to self or others, the school shall
immediately activate emergency protocols and refer the learner for same-day
psychiatric evaluation, in coordination with parents or guardians and the nearest
DOH-accredited mental health facility or service provider.

      Every school shall ensure that all members of the school community are
informed of the reporting mechanisms, available support services, and emergency
procedures established under this Act. Schools shall likewise ensure that
reporting mechanisms are accessible to learners with disabilities and other
learners requiring additional support.


                                                                                 34
      Every school shall maintain updated emergency contact information and
establish procedures for the immediate notification of parents or guardians,
emergency responders, LGUs, and other appropriate authorities during
emergencies and critical incidents.

      SEC. 26. Referral, Case Management, and Coordination. – Every school
shall establish a formal case management and referral system for learners
requiring continuing intervention and support.

The system shall provide for:

   a) Designation of case managers, where appropriate;

   b) Multidisciplinary case conferences involving relevant school personnel;

   c) Counseling and psychosocial interventions;

   d) The establishment of a tiered referral pathway to hospitals, mental health
      and well-being offices, health professionals, psychologists, psychiatrists,
      social workers, child protection authorities, law enforcement agencies, or
      other service providers, consistent with Republic Act No. 12080 and other
      applicable laws and policies;

   e) Development of individualized intervention plans, where appropriate;

   f) Regular monitoring and follow-up;

   g) Coordination with parents or guardians;

   h) Documentation and secure case records; and

   i) Safe reintegration into the school community.



                                                                                35
      The DepEd shall establish standardized case management forms, referral
protocols, documentation procedures, and inter-agency coordination mechanisms
to promote consistency across schools.

      Schools shall coordinate with the DSWD, the DOJ, the PNP, and LGUs for
the proper handling of cases, including children in conflict with the law, and for
the provision of medical, legal, and psychosocial support.




                                   TITLE VI
                                 RECOVERY

SEC. 27. School Recovery. – Schools shall establish and implement recovery and
reintegration measures for learners, school personnel, and other affected members
of the school community.

      Recovery measures shall aim to restore safety, promote healing, minimize
disruption to learning, strengthen resilience, and facilitate the safe return of
affected persons to school.

      Schools shall, at a minimum:

   a) Provide timely counseling, psychosocial support, psychological first aid,
      and other appropriate interventions;

   b) Develop individualized intervention and reintegration plans for learners or
      school personnel requiring continuing support, where appropriate;

   c) Implement academic accommodations and learning continuity measures
      for affected learners;



                                                                               36
   d) Engage parents or guardians in planning and supporting the recovery of
       affected learners;

   e) Coordinate referrals to appropriate government agencies, health
       professionals, and community-based service providers;

   f) Monitor the progress of affected learners and school personnel following
       reintegration;

   g) Implement measures to prevent retaliation, stigmatization, discrimination,
       or re-traumatization; and

   h) Conduct post-incident reviews to identify lessons learned and improve
       school safety systems.

       Recovery     and     reintegration   measures   shall   be   developmentally
appropriate, trauma-informed, culturally responsive, and consistent with the best
interests of the child.

SEC. 28. National Support for School Recovery and Continuous Improvement.
– The DepEd shall establish a National School Recovery and Support Program to
strengthen the capacity of schools to respond to and recover from incidents
affecting school safety and learner well-being. This shall be integrated into the
School Safety Plan.

       The Program shall include:

   a) Technical assistance to affected schools;

   b) Deployment of specialized psychosocial support teams, where appropriate;

   c) Development of standardized recovery and reintegration protocols;

   d) Capacity-building for school personnel on post-incident recovery;
                                                                                 37
   e) Technical assistance for learning continuity and academic recovery;

   f) Collection and analysis of post-incident data and lessons learned;

   g) Dissemination of good practices and model recovery interventions; and

   h) Such other support measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

      The DepEd shall coordinate with the DOH, the DSWD, LGUs, and other
appropriate agencies to strengthen community-based recovery and reintegration
services.

      Subject to the availability of appropriations, the DepEd shall prioritize
technical assistance and specialized support for schools significantly affected by
major emergencies, disasters, violence, or other critical incidents.

      The DepEd may organize or designate regional or division-level
multidisciplinary support teams to provide technical assistance, psychosocial
support, crisis intervention, and other specialized services to schools affected by
major incidents, emergencies, or disasters. These shall include or have rapid
access to a psychiatrist, alongside psychologists and social workers, for post-
incident deployment as needed.




                                                                                38
                                    TITLE VII
                   PROTECTION OF SCHOOL PERSONNEL

SEC. 29. Protection and Support for School Personnel. – All schools shall
ensure that school personnel receive appropriate protection, support, and
resources necessary to safely and effectively perform their responsibilities under
this Act.

      Such measures shall include:

   a) Access to counseling, psychosocial support, employee wellness, and
      mental health services, including mental health leave certification,
      psychological or psychiatric assessments, or fitness-to-return assessments
      following a critical incident;

   b) Training and continuing professional development on school safety,
      learner protection, socioemotional learning, emergency preparedness,
      crisis management, and trauma-informed response;

   c) Appropriate legal assistance and institutional support for school personnel
      acting in good faith in the performance of official duties;

   d) Protection against retaliation, intimidation, harassment, or threats arising
      from the implementation of school safety measures;

   e) Provision of appropriate personal protective equipment, emergency
      equipment, and occupational safety measures where necessary;

   f) Access to post-incident debriefing and recovery support following critical
      incidents;

   g) Recognition of school safety responsibilities in workload planning and
      administrative support; and
                                                                               39
   h) Such other measures as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish existing rights, benefits,
and protections available to school personnel under existing laws.




                                  TITLE VIII
       MONITORING, EVALUATION, AND ACCOUNTABILITY

SEC. 30. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Accountability. – The DepEd shall
establish a comprehensive national monitoring and evaluation system to assess
compliance with this Act, measure the effectiveness of school safety programs,
and support evidence-based planning, policymaking, budgeting, and continuous
improvement. These shall be harmonized with existing indicators and assessment,
self-appraisal and reporting mechanisms to avoid duplicative reporting
requirements.

      The monitoring and evaluation system shall include:

   a) Monitoring of compliance with the National Comprehensive School Safety
      Framework and the minimum standards established under this Act;

   b) Periodic review of School Safety Plans and School Safety Handbooks;

   c) Assessment of school safety infrastructure, equipment, personnel, and
      services;

   d) Monitoring of school safety incidents, trends, response times, recovery
      outcomes, and implementation of corrective actions;




                                                                                40
   e) Collection and analysis of data on learner well-being, school climate,
      violence prevention, bullying, digital safety, emergency preparedness, and
      other school safety indicators;

   f) Monitoring of capacity-building activities and competency development of
      school personnel;

   g) Monitoring of appropriate indicators of digital wellness and emerging
      digital risks, including learner-reported digital well-being, harmful or
      unsafe technology use, gaming or social media use, technology-associated
      sleep disruption, and other digital-related behavioral health concerns,
      subject to applicable child protection and data privacy laws;

   h) Periodic compliance assessments of public and private schools; and

   i) Such other monitoring mechanisms as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

      SEC. 31. National School Safety Information System. – The DepEd shall
establish and maintain a National School Safety Information System,
interoperable with existing DepEd information systems, to support planning,
monitoring, research, policy development, and resource allocation under this Act.

      The System shall collect, consolidate, analyze, and report information on:

   a) Incidents involving violence, bullying, abuse, discrimination, harassment,
      emergencies, disasters, and other school safety concerns;

   b) Learner well-being, school climate, and behavioral trends;

   c) Implementation of school safety programs, infrastructure, equipment, and
      personnel;

   d) Emergency preparedness and disaster resilience;

                                                                              41
   e) Digital safety and technology-related incidents and behavioral health
      trends, including emerging risks associated with digital technologies,
      subject to applicable child protection, confidentiality, and data privacy
      laws;

   f) Implementation of recovery and reintegration measures;

   g) Capacity-building and professional development activities;

   h) School safety performance indicators; and

   i) Such other information as may be prescribed by the DepEd.

      Information collected under this Section shall be used to support evidence-
based policymaking, strategic planning, budget preparation, program evaluation,
and continuous improvement of school safety programs.

      The collection, processing, storage, sharing, and publication of information
under this Section shall comply with applicable laws on child protection,
confidentiality, and data privacy.

      SEC. 32. Annual School Safety Report. – Every school shall submit an
Annual School Safety Report to the DepEd, through the Schools Division Office,
summarizing the implementation of its School Safety Plan, including the status
of school safety implementation, significant incidents and trends, actions taken,
implementation challenges, resource needs, and such other information as may
be prescribed by the DepEd.

      The Schools Division Office shall review the reports submitted by schools
and provide appropriate technical assistance, recommendations, and other
support necessary to address identified gaps and strengthen the implementation
of School Safety Plans.

                                                                               42
      The DepEd shall prescribe the form, content, submission schedule, and
reporting procedures for the Annual School Safety Report.

      A copy of the Annual School Safety Report shall also be furnished to the
Local School Board, barangay LGU, and the Barangay Council for the Protection
of Children (BCPC) for coordination purposes and inclusion of community-based
safety measures in the local plans.

      SEC. 33. National School Safety Report to Congress. – The DepEd shall
submit a National School Safety Report to Congress every two years
summarizing the nationwide implementation of this Act and the National
Comprehensive School Safety Framework, including national school safety
trends, significant findings, implementation status, emerging challenges, and
recommendations for policy, programmatic, and budgetary improvements.

      The National School Safety Report shall be made publicly available,
subject to applicable laws on child protection, confidentiality, and data privacy.




                                      TITLE IX
                             FINAL PROVISIONS

SEC. 34. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DepEd.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the full implementation of this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. LGUs shall provide
support for the implementation of this Act through their respective local budgets
and other available and authorized sources of funds, including the Special
Education Fund (SEF) and local disaster risk reduction and management funds,
as may be applicable, subject to existing laws, rules and regulations.

                                                                                43
SEC. 35. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DepEd, in coordination
with the DOH, DSWD, DILG, Leagues of LGUs, and other relevant stakeholders,
shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act within sixty
(60) days from its effectivity.

SEC. 36. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part hereof is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provision not otherwise affected
shall remain valid and subsisting.

SEC. 37. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations or
other issuances or parts inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 38. Effectivity. – Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the implementing
rules and regulations, this Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
