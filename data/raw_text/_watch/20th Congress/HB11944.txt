                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article II, Section 15 of the 1987 Constitution expressly provides that “The
State shall protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
consciousness among them.”1 Article XIII, Section 11 further provides that “The
State shall adopt an integrated and comprehensive approach to health development
which shall endeavor to make essential goods, health and other social services
available to all the people at affordable cost. There shall be priority for the needs of
the under-privileged, sick, elderly, disabled, women, and children. The State shall
endeavor to provide free medical care to paupers.”2 These constitutional duties are
embodied and given concrete legislative effect through Republic Act No. 11223, or
the “Universal Health Care Act,” which advances an integrated and people-centered
health system,3 and Republic Act No. 12254, or the “E-Governance Act,” which
establishes a comprehensive, integrated, interoperable, progressive, secure, and

       Government hospitals must be equipped to respond to increasing and
changing patient needs through sufficient emergency and treatment capacity,
functional medical equipment, adequate diagnostic services, safe and well-
maintained facilities, and reliable digital infrastructure. The Philippine Health
Facility Development Plan 2020–2040 recognizes the need for coordinated, needs-
based, and long-term investments in health facilities and services.5 This bill therefore
seeks to modernize qualified government hospitals so they can accommodate and
treat more patients, reduce delays in accessing care, improve sanitation and
ventilation, and provide facilities that support safe treatment and faster recovery.

       The bill establishes a Government Hospital Modernization and Digital
Transformation Program that prioritizes overcrowded, under-equipped, strategically
located, and underserved government hospitals. It authorizes the expansion and
upgrading of emergency departments, treatment stations, critical care units, wards,
laboratories, diagnostic facilities, patient rooms, utilities, ventilation systems,
sanitation facilities, medical equipment, and other infrastructure necessary to
increase hospital capacity. It also promotes digital health infrastructure that allows
patients to obtain information on hospital operating hours and available services,
request appointments, receive laboratory and diagnostic results electronically, and
avoid unnecessary travel and waiting. Authorized health facilities may likewise
access timely information on available beds, blood and blood components, medical
oxygen, ambulances, diagnostic services, specialty services, and other essential
resources to facilitate faster and more appropriate referrals. All modernization
projects must be necessary, cost-effective, sustainable, and directly responsive to
patient needs.

      The modernization of government hospitals is necessary to ensure that public
healthcare keeps pace with population needs, medical developments, and advances
in digital technology. The World Health Organization recognizes that building
emergency and critical care capacity is fundamental to health-system readiness and

PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR
      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Government
Hospital Modernization and Digital Access Act.”

      Section 2. Declaration of Policy and Objectives. — It is hereby declared the
policy of the State to ensure timely and equitable access to safe, adequately
equipped, sanitary, resilient, digitally connected, and patient-centered government
hospitals.

      Towards this end, the State shall undertake the phased, needs-based, and cost-
effective modernization of qualified government hospitals to:
(a) Expand emergency, treatment, diagnostic, inpatient, critical care, and other
essential hospital capacity;

(b) Enable government hospitals to safely accommodate, diagnose, and treat
more patients;

(c) Reduce delays in accessing hospital services, obtaining diagnostic
examinations and results, and locating facilities capable of providing the
required care;

(d) Upgrade hospital buildings, patient rooms, medical equipment,
ventilation, sanitation, hygiene, infection-prevention systems, utilities, and
facilities necessary for treatment and recovery;

(e) Establish or improve digital health and telecommunications infrastructure
where these can improve access, service delivery, coordination, processing
time, and continuity of care;

(f) Improve the coordination and availability of hospital beds, blood and blood
components, medical oxygen, diagnostic services, emergency services, and
other essential health resources;

(g) Strengthen emergency, disaster, and continuity-of-service capacity; and

(h) Ensure that government hospitals remain responsive to present and
emerging healthcare needs through appropriate infrastructure, equipment,
technology, and service capacity.
Modernization shall respond to verified patient and public health needs and shall not
be construed as prescribing the clinical or administrative operations of individual
hospitals beyond what is provided under existing laws and regulations.

      Section 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

      (a)   Digital      health    infrastructure     refers    to    the   connectivity,
      telecommunications, hardware, software, electronic records, information
      systems, cybersecurity measures, and technical capacity used to improve
      access to and delivery of hospital services;

      (b) Government hospital refers to any hospital, medical center, specialty
      hospital, infirmary, or hospital-level health facility owned or operated by the
      National Government, a local government unit, a government-owned or
      controlled corporation, a state university or college, or any other government
      instrumentality;

      (c) Modernization refers to the systematic repair, rehabilitation, completion,
      expansion,      upgrading,    equipping,      digitalization,   retrofitting,   and
      improvement of a government hospital to increase its capacity, efficiency,
      safety, accessibility, technological capability, and responsiveness to patient
      needs; and

      (d) Qualified government hospital refers to a government hospital that
      satisfies the eligibility and prioritization requirements prescribed under this
      Act and its implementing rules and regulations.
      Section 4. Government Hospital Modernization and Digital Transformation
Program. — There is hereby established a Government Hospital Modernization and
Digital Transformation Program under the Department of Health.

      The Program shall serve as a coordinated mechanism for the phased upgrading
of qualified government hospitals through improvements in infrastructure,
equipment, utilities, patient capacity, digital health systems, telecommunications,
emergency readiness, and other essential hospital capabilities.

      The Program shall be implemented through existing Department of Health
programs, offices, health facility development plans, healthcare provider networks,
and digital health systems.

      It shall supplement and support the implementation of Republic Act No.
11223, otherwise known as the “Universal Health Care Act,” Republic Act No.
12254, otherwise known as the “E-Governance Act,” and other applicable laws.

      Existing functional hospital, referral, telecommunications, and information
systems shall be integrated, strengthened, or expanded whenever practicable instead
of being unnecessarily replaced or duplicated.

      No new department, attached agency, government-owned or controlled
corporation, or permanent national office shall be created solely for the
implementation of this Act.

      Section 5. Qualification, Prioritization, and Modernization Plan. — The
Department of Health shall prepare and periodically update the Government
Hospital Modernization and Digital Transformation Plan.
       A government hospital shall qualify for assistance subject to a verified
assessment by a DOH official designated by the Secretary of Health, in accordance
with standards, requirements, and procedures prescribed pursuant to this act and its
implementing regulations.

Priority shall be given to hospitals that:

      (a) Serve geographically isolated, disadvantaged, low-income, island,
      conflict-affected, or disaster-prone communities;

      (b) Experience persistent overcrowding, excessive waiting periods,
      inadequate emergency capacity, or substantial patient backlogs;

      (c) Serve as regional, provincial, trauma, specialty, or inter-local referral
      hospitals;

      (d) Lack sufficient emergency rooms, triage areas, treatment stations, hospital
      beds, critical care facilities, diagnostic equipment, operating rooms,
      laboratories, or other essential services;

      (e) Have inadequate structural safety, ventilation, sanitation, water, electricity,
      medical oxygen, waste-management, or infection-prevention systems;

      (f) Lack reliable digital health, telecommunications, or referral capability;

      (g) Serve a substantial number of indigent or underserved patients; or

      (h) Are located far from another hospital capable of providing equivalent
      services.
        Qualification shall not be denied solely because of the limited fiscal capacity
of the hospital or its operating local government unit.

        Except in cases of disaster, public health emergency, structural danger, or
other urgent necessity, projects funded under this Act shall be consistent with the
Plan.

        Section 6. Scope of Hospital Modernization. — Assistance under the
Program may be used for:

        (a) Repair, rehabilitation, completion, retrofitting, expansion, and upgrading
        of existing hospital buildings and facilities;

        (b) Expansion and upgrading of emergency departments, triage areas,
        resuscitation bays, treatment stations, observation areas, critical care units,
        operating rooms, wards, and other facilities necessary to accommodate and
        treat more patients;

        (c) Expansion and improvement of diagnostic, imaging, laboratory, blood
        service, pharmacy, rehabilitation, isolation, and specialty facilities;

        (d) Procurement, installation, replacement, and maintenance of necessary
        medical, emergency, diagnostic, laboratory, surgical, life-support, and
        hospital equipment;

        (e) Additional hospital beds, treatment spaces, medical oxygen systems,
        backup power, water supply, and other utilities necessary to support increased
        hospital capacity;
      (f) Improvement of ventilation, indoor air quality, sanitation, sterilization,
      sewage, drainage, laundry, infection prevention, and healthcare waste-
      management systems;

      (g) Improvement of patient rooms, wards, waiting areas, recovery areas,
      access routes, and facilities for persons with disabilities, senior citizens,
      children, pregnant women, and other vulnerable patients;

      (h) Digital health infrastructure, telecommunications, electronic medical
      records, hospital information systems, telehealth, digital diagnostic systems,
      resource inventories, and patient-access systems;

      (i) Emergency transport, disaster-response, and continuity-of-service
      facilities;

      (j) Helipads or access to authorized helicopter landing zones when justified
      by documented need, technical feasibility, and cost-effectiveness; and

      (k) Other projects directly necessary to modernize the hospital, increase its
      service capacity, or improve patient access, safety, treatment, recovery, and
      continuity of care.

      The Department of Health shall determine the appropriate capacity,
equipment, technology, and technical specifications according to the classification,
function, patient volume, referral role, and verified needs of each hospital.

Repair, rehabilitation, completion, upgrading, and expansion of existing serviceable
facilities shall be preferred over entirely new construction whenever these can
adequately meet the identified need.
      Section 7. Integrated Digital Health and Patient Access Infrastructure. —
The Department of Health, in coordination with the Department of Information and
Communications Technology and other concerned agencies, shall establish,
designate, or integrate interoperable digital health and telecommunications
infrastructure for government hospitals.

Such infrastructure shall form part of, or be interoperable with, the Philippine Digital
Health System and existing healthcare provider and referral networks.

It shall support, as appropriate to the needs and capability of each hospital:

      (a) Public Hospital Information. — Access through telephone, electronic,
      web-based, mobile, or other appropriate channels to regularly updated
      information on:

             (1) Hospital location and official contact information;

             (2) General operating hours and schedules of outpatient clinics,
             laboratories, diagnostic units, pharmacies, specialty clinics, and other
             services;

             (3) Whether a hospital department, clinic, diagnostic unit, procedure,
             or service is available, temporarily unavailable, or operating on a
             limited schedule;

             (4) Services, specialties, equipment, and treatment capabilities
             available at the hospital;

             (5)   Appointment,      consultation,    admission,     diagnostic,    and
             documentary requirements; and
      (6) Temporary service interruptions, equipment downtime, or changes
      in schedules.

(b) Patient Access and Coordination. — Systems for appointments, pre-
registration, queuing, notifications, patient navigation, referrals, and
coordination of laboratory, diagnostic, consultation, and follow-up schedules,
where these can materially reduce waiting time, congestion, unnecessary
travel, or repeat visits; provided that any processing of personal information
or sensitive personal information shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173,
its implementing rules, and applicable privacy and security standards, and
shall be limited to what is necessary and proportionate to the stated purpose

(c) Digital Release of Results. — Secure electronic access to laboratory,
imaging, pathology, and other diagnostic results by patients or their duly
authorized representatives, subject to proper authentication, validation by the
appropriate health professional, data privacy, cybersecurity, and applicable
clinical protocols; and

(d) Referral and Resource Coordination. — Secure information for
authorized health facilities and personnel on:

      (1) Available hospital and critical care beds;

      (2) Blood and blood components;

      (3) Medical oxygen and other critical supplies;

      (4) Emergency, diagnostic, laboratory, surgical, dialysis, rehabilitation,
      and specialty services;
             (5) Ambulance and patient-transfer capability; and

             (6) Other essential hospital resources identified by the Department of
             Health.

      Information on organ and tissue donation and transplantation shall be
confined to secure and authorized systems and shall remain subject to existing laws
on donation, matching, allocation, confidentiality, and transplantation.

      Digitalization under this Section shall be undertaken when it can reasonably
modernize hospital services or improve patient access, accuracy, coordination,
processing time, resource utilization, or continuity of care.

      No manual process, serviceable system, or existing platform shall be replaced
solely for the purpose of digitalization when integration, improvement, or partial
automation would be more cost-effective.

      The    technical,   operational,    interoperability,     accessibility,   privacy,
cybersecurity, and information-update standards shall be prescribed by the
implementing agencies, consistent with Republic Act No. 10173, its implementing
rules, and applicable data privacy and security requirements.

      All processing of personal data under this Section shall be limited to what is
necessary and proportionate to the stated purpose, shall observe data minimization,
access controls, confidentiality, auditability, retention limits, and other safeguards
under Republic Act No. 10173 and its implementing rules.

      For any data sharing between government agencies for public functions or
public services under this Section, the concerned agencies shall comply with a data
sharing agreement and with the requirements of transparency, legitimate purpose,
and proportionality under Rule IV, Section 18 of the IRR of Republic Act No. 10173,
and with the access restrictions on sensitive personal information under Rule VII,
Section 31 of the IRR of Republic Act No. 10173.

      Section 8. Hospital Safety, Capacity, and Patient Recovery. —
Modernization projects shall comply with standards prescribed by the Department
of Health on structural safety, accessibility, ventilation, indoor air quality, water,
sanitation, hygiene, infection prevention, and healthcare waste management.

      Patient rooms, wards, waiting areas, treatment areas, and recovery facilities
shall, as appropriate, provide sufficient space, ventilation, lighting, sanitation,
privacy, cleanliness, safe water, and protection from excessive crowding, noise, heat,
and avoidable exposure to infection.

      Hospital modernization shall promote patient dignity, mobility, rest,
rehabilitation, recovery, and the efficient delivery of healthcare services.

      The Department of Health shall establish progressive modernization standards
appropriate to the classification, function, patient volume, location, resources, and
service capability of each hospital.

Such standards shall promote continuing improvements in infrastructure, equipment,
patient capacity, safety, resilience, digital readiness, and quality of care.

      Section 9. Emergency and Aeromedical Access. — Strategic referral
hospitals may be provided with appropriate emergency transport and patient-transfer
infrastructure based on documented need, geographic conditions, referral function,
technical feasibility, and cost-effectiveness.
      The Department of Health may approve the construction or upgrading of a
hospital helipad or access to a nearby authorized helicopter landing zone where
aeromedical transport is necessary and operationally sustainable.

      A helipad shall not be required for every government hospital.

      Nothing in this Act shall authorize the purchase or operation of an aircraft
without separate legal authority and appropriation.

      Section 10. Cost-Effectiveness and Prudent Use of Funds. — No project
shall be approved unless the Department of Health determines that it:

      (a) Addresses a verified hospital, service-capacity, equipment, facility, or
      digital infrastructure gap;

      (b) Is proportionate to present and reasonably projected patient demand;

      (c) Contributes directly to the modernization, capacity, accessibility, safety,
      efficiency, or technological capability of the hospital;

      (d) Does not unnecessarily duplicate existing facilities, equipment, services,
      or systems;

      (e) Is more practical and cost-effective than reasonably available alternatives;

      (f) Has adequate provisions for staffing, operation, maintenance, utilities,
      supplies, connectivity, cybersecurity, and technical support; and

      (g) Provides reasonable patient and public health benefits in relation to its total
      life-cycle cost.
        Funds under this Act shall not be used for ornamental, ceremonial, luxury,
promotional, or prestige projects or for expenditures that do not directly contribute
to hospital modernization, patient care, access, safety, capacity, recovery, digital
connectivity, or continuity of service.

        The unnecessary replacement of serviceable facilities, equipment, or digital
systems shall be prohibited.

        Section 11. Penalties. — Any public officer or employee responsible for the
operation, upkeep, or maintenance of any facility or equipment acquired,
constructed, or provided under this law who, by bad faith, gross negligence, or
inexcusable neglect, fails to ensure its proper maintenance, repair, or safekeeping,
thereby causing material damage, operational impairment, or undue injury to the
government or to the intended beneficiaries, shall, after due notice and hearing, be
held administratively, civilly, and/or criminally liable under applicable existing
laws.

        Section 12. Implementation and Responsibilities. — The Department of
Health shall be the lead implementing agency of this Act.

        The Department of Information and Communications Technology shall
provide assistance on telecommunications, connectivity, digital architecture,
interoperability, cybersecurity, shared infrastructure, and applicable e-government
standards.

        Other concerned agencies and local government units shall provide assistance
within their respective mandates.
      Assistance for a locally owned or operated government hospital shall be
governed by a memorandum of agreement defining responsibilities for
implementation, staffing, operation, maintenance, utilities, connectivity, reporting,
and accountability.

      A reasonable local counterpart may be required based on the fiscal capacity
of the local government unit and the nature of the project. No counterpart shall be
required when it would materially prevent or delay a necessary modernization
project serving a low-income, geographically isolated, disadvantaged, or highly
vulnerable population.

      Recipient hospitals shall properly use and maintain the facilities, equipment,
and digital infrastructure provided under this Act and submit such information as
may be required for monitoring and evaluation.

      Section 13. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
Department of Health and other concerned agencies.

      Funding may also be sourced from lawful local government funds, Special
Health Funds, government digitalization funds, grants, donations, official
development assistance, public-private partnership arrangements, and other sources
authorized by law.

      No grant, donation, financing agreement, or partnership shall confer any right
to own, sell, commercially exploit, or obtain unauthorized access to patient or
government health information.
      Section 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred
eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health, in
coordination with the Department of Information and Communications Technology
and other concerned agencies and stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for its implementation.

      The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe the qualification,
prioritization, technical, capacity, equipment, digital-health, telecommunications,
interoperability, privacy, cybersecurity, procurement, reporting, and modernization
standards necessary to implement this Act.

      Section 15. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

      Section 16. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, presidential proclamations, rules and regulations or parts thereof contrary to
or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      Section 17. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
