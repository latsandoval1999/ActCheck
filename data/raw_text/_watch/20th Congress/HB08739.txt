                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

         Agriculture remains the backbone of our economy, providing food on the table of every
Filipino household and livelihood for millions of farmers and fisherfolk. Yet, the sector is also the
most vulnerable to forces beyond human control. Each year, typhoons, floods, droughts, pest
infestations, and plant and fish diseases wipe out the labor and hopes of small farmers and fisherfolk,
leaving them in debt and uncertainty.

        To address these recurring losses, Presidential Decree No. 1467 created the Philippine Crop
Insurance Corporation (PCIC) in 1978. Over the years, the PCIC has extended protection to farmers
and fisherfolk, but the increasing frequency of natural calamities and the expanding demands of
food security require a stronger, more responsive, and more inclusive crop insurance system.

         This proposed measure strengthens the Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation by revising
its charter and expanding its powers and coverage. It ensures that not only palay and corn but also
high-value commercial crops, livestock, fisheries, aquaculture, agroforestry, and even non-crop
agricultural assets such as machinery and infrastructure are covered by insurance. It also introduces
weather index-based insurance, reinsurance, and provisions for catastrophic loss funds.

        The bill likewise provides for a realistic capitalization framework and sustainable premium-
sharing arrangements. It recognizes the role of government in subsidizing the premium payments
of subsistence farmers and fisherfolk while ensuring that lending institutions and private entities
share in the responsibility of risk protection. By doing so, the measure prevents farmers from
sinking deeper into debt and strengthens their credit standing.

         Moreover, this Act introduces safeguards against fraudulent claims, clearer settlement
procedures, and “no-claim benefits” for responsible farmers and fisherfolk who sustain good
practices over time. It also requires inter-agency cooperation, recognizing that crop insurance is not
the work of one corporation alone but the collective responsibility of government, lending
institutions, and private stakeholders.

        This legislation reaffirms the government’s duty to protect the agricultural sector, not only
to safeguard the livelihood of farmers and fisherfolk but to secure the nation’s food supply. A
strengthened PCIC will give our food producers the confidence to recover from loss, reinvest in
production, and contribute to the country’s food security and economic stability.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

1            SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Revised Charter of the
2    Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation”.

 3           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to provide
 4   comprehensive support to agriculture through adequate financial, production, marketing, and other
 5   necessary support services. In pursuit of this policy, the State shall ensure food security by
 6   intensifying food production, promoting access to agricultural credit, and broadening the coverage
 7   of mandatory crop insurance, particularly in light of the recurring disasters and calamities, whether
 8   natural or otherwise, such as floods, typhoons, heavy rains, and droughts, which continue to cause
 9   widespread destruction of crops, especially palay and other grains and food-security-critical
10   commodities.

11           The State shall likewise alleviate the financial burden borne by farmers and fisherfolk due
12   to uncompensated losses resulting from such disasters and calamities by keeping them away from
13   excessive indebtedness, enhancing their credit standing, and providing additional government
14   support to enable them to sustain and increase agricultural production. Further, the State shall assist
15   banks and financial institutions by strengthening their capacity to extend credit and financial
16   services to the agricultural sector. Finally, the State shall strengthen the organizational capability
17   and expand the coverage of the Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation (PCIC) in order to provide
18   effective insurance protection to farmers and fisherfolk against losses arising from natural
19   calamities, fortuitous events, plant and fish diseases, and pest infestations.

1            SEC. 3. Expanded Purposes and Coverage of the PCIC. – The Philippine Crop Insurance
2    Corporation (PCIC), a government-owned corporation established pursuant to Presidential Decree
3    No. 1467, with its principal place of business in Metro Manila, shall insure qualified farmers and
4    fisherfolk against losses arising from natural calamities, fortuitous events, plant and fish diseases,
5    and pest infestations.

 6            The PCIC shall provide insurance coverage for palay and corn crops, high-value
 7   commercial crops, livestock, aquaculture and fishery products, agroforestry crops and forest
 8   plantations; non-crop agricultural assets such as machineries, equipment, transport facilities, and
 9   other related infrastructure; and life and accident term insurance coverage for farmers and
10   fisherfolk. Such crop insurance shall cover, in every case, the cost of production inputs, the value
11   of the labor of the farmer or fisherfolk, and members of their households including the value of the
12   labor of hired workers, and a portion of the projected value of the crops. Such insurance protection,
13   however, shall exclude losses arising from avoidable risks emanating from or due to the negligence,
14   malfeasance, or fraud committed, by any member of the immediate farm household or employee,
15   or the failure of the insured to follow proven farm practices.

16            The PCIC shall also insure properties and facilities which are owned or used by government
17   agencies involved in agri-fishery-forestry projects or activities and government-financed agri-
18   fishery-forestry projects. It shall also provide reinsurance coverage to agri-fishery-forestry
19   properties and facilities underwritten by private and government insurance companies, and weather
20   index-based insurance (WIBI) and reinsurance for palay and corn crops, high-value commercial
21   crops, livestock, aquaculture and fishery products, agroforestry crops, and forest plantation.

17          SEC. 5. Who shall be Insured. – It shall be compulsory for farmers to take out insurance
18   for palay and other crops essential for food security, as determined by the Department of
19   Agriculture (DA).

20            For other crops, insurance coverage shall be compulsory upon all farmers obtaining
21   production loans under the supervised credit program of the government and optional on the part
22   of self-financed farmers: Provided, That they agree to place themselves under the supervision of
23   agricultural production technicians.

24           As used in this Act, the term supervised credit program refers to the production credit
25   program wherein the farmer who obtains production loans agrees in writing to apply proven farm
26   practices necessary to conserve the land, improve its fertility and increase its production, and abide
27   by the approved farm plan and budget jointly prepared by such farmer and the duly accredited
28   supervised credit supervision of agricultural production technician refers to the supervision,
29   pursuant to Presidential Decree No. 409, dated March 11, 1974, undertaken by agricultural
30   production technicians who are duly accredited by the appropriate government agencies concerned,
31   as required under the supervised credit program of the government.

42   or fisherfolk shall be reasonably affordable: Provided, furthermore, That the Government shall

18               b) Yearly projections of the premium subsidy shall be submitted by the PCIC to the

20                  government’s budgetary appropriation;

22                  crop insurance and shall be released to and administered by the PCIC;

26                  months until the amount of government subscription is fully paid.

11            SEC. 9. Reserve Fund for Catastrophic Losses. – A State Reserve Fund, herein referred
12   to as the Fund, for catastrophic losses in the amount of Five Hundred Million Pesos (Php
13   500,000,000.00) shall be created exclusively to answer for the proportion of all losses in excess of
14   risk or pure premium under the PCIC’s Crop Insurance Program for small farmers and fisherfolk.
15   The Fund may be increased, subject to the approval of the President of the Philippines. The Fund
16   shall be administered by the government financial institution to be designated by the PCIC’s Board.

13          SEC. 14. Settlement of Claims. – The following are the procedures for the filing,
14          reconsideration, appeal, and automatic approval of claims with the PCIC.

15              a) Claims for indemnity against the PCIC shall be settled by the PCIC´s regional
16                 manager concerned or the appropriate officer to whom the function is delegated.
17                 However, if in the opinion of the regional manager the claim is novel, difficult, or
18                 controversial, the matter may be elevated by the regional manager to the President
19                 of the PCIC for decision;
20              b) The claimant aggrieved by the decision of the regional manager may, within thirty
21                 (30) days from receipt of the decision, request for reconsideration thereof, and if
22                 not satisfied therewith, elevate the matter to the President of the PCIC;
23              c) Any party aggrieved by a decision, order, or ruling of the President of the PCIC
24                 may appeal said decision, order, or ruling to the Board;
25              d) Any claim not acted upon within sixty (60) days from submission of complete
26                 claims documents to the PCIC shall be considered approved.

27           SEC. 15. No Claim Benefits. – Any insured farmer or fisherfolk who has not filed any
28   claims during the immediately preceding three (3) crop seasons, or any insured fishpond or fish
29   cage operator who has not filed any claim during the immediately preceding three (3) harvest
30   seasons shall be entitled to a no-claim benefit of at least ten percent (10%) of premium share paid
31   for said crop seasons, to be deposited in a trust fund to be managed by the PCIC. Such trust fund
32   may be used to finance premium rebate or premium credit applicable to the immediately following
33   crop seasons as determined by the Board.
