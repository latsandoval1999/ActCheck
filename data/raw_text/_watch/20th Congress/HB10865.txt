                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Philippines, at its core, an agricultural economy, yet the persistent
paradox of its farm sector is not scarcity, but supply mismatch. The bountiful
harvests of our farmers often go to rot or are sold at a loss, while nearby markets
face shortages and higher prices for the very same commodities. This chronic
disruption is a traceable crisis that recurs due to failure of information and
coordination between production and demand.

       As a country with agricultural yields, it is highly necessary that we bank
on the strengths of our existing industries. It is also shown during times of
emergency and crisis that there exists a gap in the information system involving
our farming sectors. This pattern has repeated with striking regularity in recent
years. In Benguet, in one instance, the country’s so-called “Salad Bowl” that
supplies nearly 80% of the upland vegetables sold in Metro Manila, cabbage
farmers have seen farmgate prices collapse to as low as P4 to P10 per kilo, forcing
many to either sell at a loss or abandon their harvest altogether, even as prices
in the end-market remain multiple times higher.1

      Likewise, in Nueva Ecija and Rizal provinces, tomato farmers faced a
similar collapse in early 2025, with farmgate prices falling to P4 to P5 per kilo,
prompting Department of Science and Technology – Philippine Council for
Agriculture, Aquatic, and Natural Resources Research and Development (DOST-
PCAARRD) and Sen. Kiko Pangilinan to launch multiple initiatives, such as
“Operation Sagip-Kamatis-an” to rescue the unsold produce and calling on local
government units to procure the excess for feeding programs.2 As one farmer-

       The government has not been without initiatives to address this gap. The
Department of Agriculture (DA) currently maintains several digital platforms,
among them the Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA), the
e-Kadiwa e-commerce platform, and the Bantay-Presyo price-monitoring
application. These are valuable, but largely function as centralized, top-down
tools; none of them is mandated to operate as real-time, two-way local matching
system at the city, municipal, and barangay level where oversupply and
undersupply typically originate, and where the earliest, most actionable
information exists. The gap that this bill seeks to address is precisely that last-
mile layer, the absence of a legally mandated, LGU-anchored information system
that captures hyperlocal production data as it is harvested, and connects, in real
time, to buyers and to the national platforms/registries that already exist.

      It was recently shown that a lot of farming communities are forced to throw
away their crops due to the supposed oversupply. However, upon careful
investigation, it shows that there exists demand in several areas in the country
which are not taken account of because of this information gap. Such gaps have
become more urgent to close now that the National Government has significantly
scaled up its investment in agricultural infrastructure.

       For example, the 2026 National Budget substantially increased funding
for farm-to-market roads, precisely to move produce out of the farms and into
markets faster. Roads without a matching information system to tell farmers
where the demand is, and buyers where the supply is, will only get produce to
market faster without ensuring that it goes to where it is needed. 4 Physical
infrastructure and information infrastructure must be developed together.

      It is for these reasons that this bill seeks to reestablish, in an updated and
strengthened form, the Agriculture Information System, built to interoperate
with existing national platforms rather than duplicate them, while accessible to
farmers who lack reliable internet access through SMS and community kiosks
and not just a web application, and backed by a funding mechanism that does
not leave under-resources municipalities, communities, and farmer groups to
shoulder an unfunded mandate alone.

        Hence, it is for these reasons that this bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
    ESTABLISHING THE AGRICULTURE INFORMATION SYSTEM IN ALL
                   CITIES AND MUNICIPALITIES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Agriculture
Information System Act.”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to uplift the
socio-economic conditions of farmers and farming communities nationwide in
order to promote their welfare and empower their sector in the overall
development and progress of the country. Pursuant to this, an integrated,
computerized, web-based agricultural commodities supply inventory and
registry system linking farmers and organized communities all over the country
needs to be established to provide an easy-to-access local and global real time
information for agricultural commodities, with an end view of creating a wider
range of market linkages among farmers and agricultural buyers. Furthermore,
through a synchronized agriculture information system, the National
Government should have access to real-time agricultural information which can
rationalize the objectives and policy directives in the agricultural sector.

      SECTION 3. Definitions. – As used in this Act:

      (a) Agriculture information system refers to the integrated,
          computerized database and multi-channel platform established under
          this Act for the collection, consolidation, and dissemination of
          agricultural production, demand, and market information;
      (b) Interoperability refers to the capacity of the Information System to
          exchange and make use of data with other government agricultural
    databases, including but not limited to the Registry System for Basic
    Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA), the e-Kadiwa platform, the Bantay-
    Presyo price-monitoring application, and the Commodity Trade Volume
    Watch System of the Department of Agriculture, and the relevant
    databases of the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) and the Philippine
    Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration
    (PAGASA);
(c) Farmer refers to a natural person, whether individually or as a member
    of a cooperative, association, or organized farming group, who is
    directly engaged in the cultivation of agricultural land, the raising of
    livestock or poultry, or aquaculture;
(d) Buyer refers to any natural or juridical person, including traders,
    consolidators, institutional buyers, retailers, exporters, and
    government agencies, seeking to purchase agricultural commodities
    registered in the Information System;
(e) Oversupply or undersupply alert refers to an automated notification
    generated by the Information System when production data for a given
    commodity, in a given locality and timeframe, substantially exceeds or
    falls short of corresponding demand data; and
(f) Last-Mile Farmer refers to a farmer located in a geographically
    isolated, disadvantaged, or low-connectivity area, for whom access to
    the Information System must be facilitated through non-internet-
    dependent channels.

SECTION 4. Objectives. – This Act aims to attain the following objectives:

a) Provide an up-to-date database of agricultural products in the locality;
b) Facilitate linkages from farm-to-market among local government units;
c) Provide data specific commodities readily available in the particular
   locality for stakeholders;
d) Detect and provide early warning of impending oversupply or
   undersupply of specific commodities to allow timely intervention by
   local and national government;
e) Secure for farmers additional and more stable sources of income by
   reducing reliance on unplanned distress sales;
f) Encourage the farmers to engage in synchronized agricultural mass
   production to meet the voluminous demands of the market;
g) Provide multi-channel access to the Information System, including a
   web application, a mobile application, SMS or text-based services, and
   community-based kiosks or help desks, to ensure last-mile farmers are
   not excluded for lack of internet access or digital literacy;
h) Streamline training and seminars in the management of the
   operations;
i) Monitor the distribution and use of farm inputs provided to farmers;
j) Facilitate information sharing on commodity flows, specifically, supply
   and demand;
      k) Protect the personal and livelihood data of farmers and buyers
         collected through the Information System; and
      l) Ensure that government financial assistance is given only to duly
         formed cooperatives and farmers groups.

       SECTION 5. Establishment of the Agriculture Information System. – All
cities and municipalities are hereby mandated to establish an Agriculture
Information System, hereinafter referred to as Information System, which shall
serve as a dynamic online computer database for information on the demand for
specific agricultural commodities is gathered simultaneously with the
production data from farmers in every barangay. The information system shall
merge and synchronize agricultural data from different sources into a cohesive
database designed to facilitate linkages from place of origin or the farm to local,
and ultimately, global markets, and shall be interoperable with the national
databases identified in Section 3(b) of this Act.

      SECTION 6. Features and Contents of the Information System. – The
Information System shall have the following features and contents:

      a) Multi-channel accessibility, consisting at minimum of (i) a web
         application, (ii) a mobile application optimized for low-bandwidth use,
         (iii) an SMS or text-based interface for last-mile farmers without
         internet access, and (iv) a physical help desk or kiosk in every
         municipal or city agriculture office;
      b) Municipal and city-wide identification of farmers producing specific
         farm products in small or large quantities;
      c) Crop harvests, volume, and type forecasting;
      d) Inventory of specific classification of crop varieties planted and
         livestock raised in a particular local government unit;
      e) Farmers’ demographics, including age group, gender, and seminars
         and trainings attended;
      f) Automated generation and dissemination of Oversupply or
         Undersupply Alerts, as defined in Section 3(e), to the concerned local
         government unit, the provincial and regional offices of the Department
         of Agriculture, and registered institutional buyers;
      g) Selected reports presented in both tabular and graphical form
         accessible through a public dashboard maintained at the city or
         municipal level;
      h) Registry of farmers' contact details and farm location to facilitate
         market linkages, collected and maintained in accordance with Section
         11 of this Act;
      i) A search function allowing farmers to search for potential buyers and
         buyers to search for farms, farmers, and available agricultural
         products; and
      j) A customizable dashboard, accessible only to registered farmer clients,
         containing the farmer's profile, farm details, land area, and crops
         planted or livestock raised.

       SECTION 7. Registration. – All cities and municipalities shall encourage
and facilitate the free registration of all farmers and buyers transacting within
their respective jurisdictions in the Information System. Registration shall
require the informed consent of the farmer or buyer as to the collection, use, and
sharing of their personal data consistent with Section 11 of this Act, and shall,
to the extent practicable, be cross-referenced with existing records under the
Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA) to avoid duplicate data
collection.

      SECTION 8. Functions and Responsibilities of Local Government Units. –
The Local Government unit shall exercise the following functions and
responsibilities:

         a) Gather, encode, and consolidate the profile and demographics of
            farmers and buyers as well as other pertinent agricultural data;
         b) Monitor and control farm inputs including machineries and
            equipment;
         c) Establish and maintain a physical help desk or kiosk, and provide
            SMS-based access, for last-mile farmers who lack internet access or
            digital literacy;
         d) Conduct regular digital-literacy training for farmers on the use of
            the Information System, in coordination with the Department of
            Agriculture      and  the    Department     of   Information    and
            Communications Technology (DICT);
         e) Prepare and submit real time reports to the provincial office of the
            Department of Agriculture;
         f) Act on Oversupply or Undersupply Alerts by, among others,
            coordinating with institutional buyers, feeding programs, and
            neighboring local government units for the absorption of excess
            produce; and
         g) Establish farmer and buyer linkages and act as the gatekeeper at
            the city or municipal level for the nationwide marketing of
            agricultural products.

      SECTION 9. Functions and Responsibilities of the Provincial Government. –
The provincial government should have the following functions and
responsibilities:

         a) The Provincial Planning and Development Office shall act as the web
            administrator at the provincial level and shall be responsible for
            integrating farm locations into the geographic information system
            maps for agriculture;
         b) The Local Economic and Investment Promotion Office shall act as
            the lead administrator of the Agriculture Information System at the
            provincial level and shall serve as market mitigator, coordinating the
            response to Oversupply or Undersupply Alerts across municipalities
            and cities within the province; and
         c) The provincial office of the Department of Agriculture shall monitor
            the submission of reports from local government units, ensuring the
            completeness, validity, and accuracy of data submitted by the
            Municipal Agriculture Office.

      SECTION 10. Functions and Responsibilities of the Regional Office of the
Department of Agriculture. – The Regional Office of the Department of Agriculture
shall have the following functions and responsibilities.

         a) Collaborate with market linkages from other regions, integrating
            geographic information system data on project interventions, soil
            maps, suitability maps, and commodity maps within the region;
         b) Recommend to the central office of the Department of Agriculture
            appropriate interventions in the form of marketing, importation, and
            training assistance needed at the provincial level;
         c) Validate data from the provincial offices of the Department of
            Agriculture, acting as the System Administrator at the regional level
            and providing real-time reports to the Central Office of the
            Department of Agriculture; and
         d) Coordinate with the regional office of the Philippine Atmospheric,
            Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA) to
            integrate weather and climate advisories into the Information
            System's production forecasting features.

       SECTION 11. Data Privacy and Security. – The collection, processing,
storage, and sharing of personal data under this Act shall be governed by the
following:

         a) All personal data of farmers and buyers collected through the
            Information System shall be processed in accordance with Republic
            Act No. 10173, or the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and its
            implementing rules and regulations;
         b) Each city or municipality shall designate a Data Protection Officer
            responsible for ensuring compliance with data privacy requirements
            in the operation of the Information System;
         c) Farmers and buyers shall be informed, in a language or dialect they
            understand, of the purpose, extent, and recipients of any collection
            or sharing of their personal data prior to registration;
         d) Personal data shall not be shared with any third party, including
            private buyers or platforms, without the explicit consent of the
            farmer or buyer concerned, except as may be required for
            interoperability with the national databases identified in Section 3(b)
            of this Act; and
         e) Any breach of data security shall be reported to the National Privacy
            Commission and to the affected farmers or buyers in accordance
            with existing law.

      SECTION 12. Role of the Department of Information and Communications
Technology. – The Department of Information and Communications Technology
(DICT) shall provide technical standards, cybersecurity oversight, and cloud
infrastructure support for the Information System, and shall assist the
Department of Agriculture in ensuring that the Information System is
interoperable with the national government's broader e-Government
infrastructure and complies with applicable data security standards.
      SECTION 13. Functions and Responsibilities of the Central Office of the
Department of Agriculture. – The Central Office of the Department of Agriculture
shall have the following functions and responsibilities:
      (a) Allocate funding for the establishment, operation, and maintenance of
          the Information System;
      (b) Approve prioritized interventions recommended by the regional offices
          and allocate corresponding funding;
      (c) Collaborate with other agencies, including the Philippine Statistics
          Authority, for the validation of data and the creation of market linkages
          in the domestic and international markets;
      (d) Ensure the interoperability of the Information System with the Registry
          System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA), the e-Kadiwa
          platform, the Bantay-Presyo application, and the Commodity Trade
          Volume Watch System, in coordination with the DICT; and
      (e) Provide safety nets in the importation and exportation of agricultural
          products in order to support farmers.

      SECTION 14. Capacity Building and Digital Literacy Program – The
Department of Agriculture, in coordination with local government units, the
DICT, and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA),
shall develop and implement a continuing digital-literacy program to train
farmers, cooperative officers, and local government personnel in the use of the
Information System, with particular attention to elderly farmers and Last-Mile
Farmers as defined under this Act. Training materials shall be made available in
relevant regional languages or dialects.
       SECTION 15. Public-Private Partnerships. – The Department of Agriculture
may enter into data-sharing or interoperability agreements with accredited
private e-commerce platforms, logistics providers, agricultural lending
institutions, and insurance providers to expand market and financial linkages
available to registered farmers through the Information System, subject to the
data privacy safeguards under Section 11 of this Act and to guidelines issued by
the Department in its implementing rules and regulations.
      SECTION 16. Duties of the Secretary of Agriculture. – The Secretary of
Agriculture shall monitor and ensure compliance by all cities and municipalities
with this Act and shall, as soon as practicable but not later than one (1) year
from the effectivity of this Act, establish a centralized agricultural information
system that shall integrate and link all city- and municipal-level Information
Systems established under this Act with existing national agricultural
databases.
      SECTION 17. Reports. – The Secretary of Agriculture shall prepare and
submit to the President and both Houses of Congress an annual report on the
program's performance, which shall contain the list of accomplishments, the
number of Oversupply or Undersupply Alerts issued and the corresponding
interventions undertaken, and recommendations. A summary of the report shall
likewise be made available to the public through a dashboard maintained on the
Department of Agriculture's official website.
       SECTION 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred
eighty (180) days after the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Agriculture in
coordination with the DICT and the National Privacy Commission, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation
of this Act.
      SECTION 19. Appropriations. – The initial amount necessary for the
development, establishment, and nationwide rollout of the Information System,
including its interoperability infrastructure, shall be charged against the current
year's appropriation of the Department of Agriculture. Thereafter, the amount
necessary for the operation and maintenance of the Information System at the
city or municipal level shall be included in the annual appropriations of the
concerned local government unit, which may be sourced from its Internal
Revenue Allotment or other local funds allocated for agricultural development,
supplemented as needed by the Department of Agriculture. Such sums as may
be necessary for the continued operation, maintenance, and enhancement of the
Information System shall thereafter be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.
      SECTION 20. Repealing Clause. – All provisions of laws, presidential
decrees, letters of instruction, and other presidential issuances that are
incompatible or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby deemed
amended or repealed.
      SECTION 21. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act, or part
hereof, is held invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or provision
not otherwise affected shall remain valid and subsisting.
      SECTION 22. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
