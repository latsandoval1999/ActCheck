                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Section 6, Article X of the 1987 Constitution provides that local
government units (LGUs) shall have a just share in national taxes, which shall be
automatically released to them. This fiscal arrangement is intended to strengthen
local autonomy and ensure that provinces, cities, municipalities, and barangays
possess the necessary financial resources to discharge their devolved
responsibilities. Under Section 285 of Republic Act No. 7160, the allocation of
the Internal Revenue Allotment (IRA), now referred to as the National Tax
Allotment (NTA), is expressly reserved for local government units.


      The Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA) is a
government agency tasked with providing metro-wide services in Metropolitan
Manila, established to coordinate, monitor, and regulate services that transcend
local boundaries. These services include: transport and traffic management; solid
waste disposal and management; flood control and sewerage management; urban
renewal, zoning, and land use planning; health sanitation; and public safety.


     The MMDA’s receipt of a national tax share is a historical and legislative
anomaly rooted in Section 10(b) of Republic Act No. 7924, which mandated that
the Authority continue receiving the allotment previously granted to its
predecessor, the Metro Manila Authority (MMA), under Executive Order No.
392. However, the MMDA is not a local government unit. In the landmark case
of MMDA v. Bel-Air Village Association, Inc., the Supreme Court categorically
ruled that the MMDA is neither a political subdivision nor a public corporation
endowed with legislative power, but merely a development authority created to
coordinate and administer metropolitan-wide services.


      With the full implementation of the Mandanas-Garcia Ruling and the
transition to the National Tax Allotment (NTA), the pool of funds intended for
LGUs has expanded to include all national taxes. Allowing a non-LGU entity like
the MMDA to continue drawing from this specific pot further dilutes the "just
share" of legitimate LGUs, particularly those in lower-income classes outside of
the National Capital Region who are struggling to fund their newly devolved
functions.


      Despite this, the MMDA continues to receive allocations derived from
national taxes, which are constitutionally intended for local government units.
Such practice runs contrary to the constitutional framework governing fiscal
decentralization and undermines the principle that national tax shares should
accrue solely to LGUs.


      Thus, this bill seeks to amend Republic Act No. 7924 to withdraw the
Internal Revenue Allotment of the MMDA and align the funding structure of the
Authority with the constitutional framework on local government fiscal
autonomy.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      Section 1. – Section 10 of Republic Act No. 7924 is hereby amended to
read as follows:
                   “Sec. 10 Sources of Funds and the Operating Budget of
             MMDA -


                   (a) To carry out the purposes of this Act, the amount of One
             billion pesos (Php 1,000,000,000) is hereby authorized to be
             appropriated for the initial operation of the MMDA. Thereafter, the
             annual expenditures including capital outlays of the MMDA shall be
             provided in the general Appropriations Act.
                    [(b) The MMDA shall continue to receive the Internal
             Revenue Allotment (IRA) currently allocated to the present MMA.]


                    [(c)] (b) The MMDA is likewise empowered to levy fines and
             impose fees and charges for various services rendered.


                    [(d)] (c) Five percent (5%) of the total annual gross revenue
             of the preceding year, net of the internal revenue allotment, of each
             local government unit mentioned in Section 2 hereof, shall accrue
             and become payable monthly to the MMDA by each city or
             municipality. In case of failure to remit the said fixed contribution,
             the DBM shall cause the disbursement of the same to MMDA
             chargeable against the IRA allotment of the city or municipality
             concerned, the provisions of Section 286 of RA 7160 to the contrary
             notwithstanding.”


      Section 2. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act
are hereby modified, amended, or repealed accordingly.


      Section 3. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15)
days following its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper
of general circulation.


      Approved,
