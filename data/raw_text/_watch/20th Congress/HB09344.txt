                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

Solo parents carry the immense responsibility of raising and supporting their children while
often facing economic, emotional, and social challenges on their own. In many cases, they
serve as the sole providers of food, education, healthcare, and overall welfare for their families.
Despite their resilience and sacrifice, many solo parents continue to struggle with rising living
costs, limited employment opportunities, and insufficient access to social support services.

The State recognizes the vital role of solo parents in nation-building and affirms its duty to
promote their welfare and protect their families. Section 1, Article XV of the 1987 Constitution
declares that the family is the foundation of the nation and mandates the State to strengthen
its solidarity and actively promote its total development. Likewise, Article XIII on Social Justice
directs the State to prioritize measures that protect vulnerable sectors and reduce social and
economic inequalities.

Republic Act No. 11861, otherwise known as the “Expanded Solo Parents Welfare Act,”
strengthened the benefits and privileges accorded to solo parents. However, many solo-parent
families, particularly those belonging to low-income households, continue to face difficulties in
meeting their daily needs due to inflation, increasing educational expenses, healthcare costs,
and other financial burdens.

Therefore, this bill seeks to provide more responsive and accessible assistance to solo parents
through a comprehensive support program covering food, medical, educational, livelihood,
and other essential forms of financial assistance. By institutionalizing targeted interventions
for qualified solo parents, the State can help ease their financial burdens and enable them to
provide a more secure and stable future for their children.

Ultimately, this bill aims to uphold the dignity and welfare of solo parents by ensuring that they
are not left behind in the country’s social protection framework. Through this measure, the
State reaffirms its commitment to inclusive growth, family welfare, and the holistic
development of Filipino children.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE FOR SOLO PARENTS
      PROGRAM (FASPP), PROVIDING FOOD, MEDICAL, EDUCATIONAL,
  LIVELIHOOD, AND CASH RELIEF SUPPORT, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                            THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in the
Congress assembled:


SEC. 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Financial Assistance for Solo Parents
Program (FASPP) Act”.
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall protect and promote the welfare of
solo parents and their dependents by providing adequate and timely financial, medical,
educational, and livelihood assistance. The State shall adopt measures to uplift the
standard of living of solo parent families, ensure access to basic services, and enable
their full participation in economic and social development.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms shall mean:
   a. FASPP – refers to the Financial Assistance for Solo Parents Program
      implemented by the DSWD, providing various forms of assistance to qualified
      solo parents;
   b. Solo Parent – refers to an individual who qualifies as a solo parent under
      Republic Act No. 11861 and its implementing rules and regulations;
   c. Beneficiary – refers to a solo parent whose monthly income does not exceed
      Fourteen Thousand Pesos (PhP 14,000.00), and is qualified to receive
      assistance under this Act, subject to assessment and submission of
      documentary requirements;
   d. Dependent – refers to a child or a dependent qualified under the Expanded
      Solo Parents Welfare Act;
   e. Certificate of Eligibility (CE) – refers to a document issued by the DSWD
      certifying that the solo parent is eligible to avail of assistance under the FASPP;
   f. General Intake Sheet (GIS) – refers to the form used by DSWD social workers
      to record relevant information on the solo parent and dependents;
   g. Social Case Study Report (SCSR) – refers to the document prepared by a
      professional social worker describing the socio-economic condition,
      assessment, and recommended interventions for the solo parent;
   h. Service Providers – refers to entities providing goods and services covered by
      the FASPP, including but not limited to hospitals, clinics, educational
      institutions, pharmacies, and other relevant providers; and
   i. Guarantee Letter (GL) – refers to a document issued by the DSWD
      guaranteeing payment to service providers on behalf of the solo parent.
SEC. 4. Coverage and Modalities in Providing Assistance. – Financial and other
assistance shall be provided through the DSWD and in partnership with local
government units (LGUs), subject to eligibility and availability of funds, as follows:
   a. Implementation through the DSWD – Qualified solo parents who need financial
      assistance for goods and services may avail of:

      1. Food Assistance – this is an outright cash assistance provided to meet the
         need for food and other nutritional requirements for sustenance of the solo
         parent’s children;
      2. Medical Assistance – this is an outright cash or Guarantee Letter provided
         to cover hospitalization expenses and professional fees, cost of medicines
         and other medical treatment or procedures, such as common laboratory
         tests, and diagnostic imaging for any illness or condition, including
         postpartum complications. This assistance does not extend to other
         healthcare expenses that are unrelated to medical treatment or aftercare;
      3. Educational Assistance for Dependents – refers to an outright cash
         assistance or Guarantee Letter provided to cover school-related expenses
         of the solo parent’s dependent(s), such as tuition and miscellaneous fees,
         purchase of school supplies, uniforms, books, payment of transportation
         expenses, and other incidental costs necessary for the dependent’s
         continued attendance and completion of primary, secondary, or tertiary
         education, including technical-vocational education or skills training
         programs, subject to assessment by the DSWD social worker;
      4. Livelihood Assistance – refers to an outright cash assistance or support
         package granted to a qualified solo parent for the purpose of initiating,
         sustaining, or expanding a small-scale livelihood project or income-
         generating activity. This assistance may cover the procurement of
         necessary tools, raw materials, training costs, or other inputs directly related
         to the livelihood undertaking, as determined by the assessment and
         recommendation of the DSWD social worker; and
      5. Cash Relief Assistance – this is an outright cash assistance provided to
         qualified beneficiaries for reasons other than for food, medical, or funeral
         expenses, which, upon the assessment of the DSWD social worker, may
         appear to be directly related to the effects of high inflation (i.e., difficulty in
         paying for house rental, electricity, and water expenses, etc.).
      Cash assistance may be granted outright in amounts from Five Thousand
      Pesos (PhP 5,000.00) up to Fifteen Thousand Pesos (PhP 15,000.00), subject
      to the DSWD’s assessment. For amounts exceeding Fifteen Thousand Pesos
      (PhP 15,000.00), the same shall be provided through a Guarantee Letter
      approved by the Secretary of DSWD or his duly authorized representative.
      The distribution of assistance is without prejudice to the adoption of digital
      payment solutions once the necessary infrastructure is in place.
   b. Implementation through Local Government Units (LGUs) – The financial
      assistance that will be provided to qualified beneficiaries through the LGUs shall
      be called rice assistance. For this purpose, the DSWD shall enter into a
      Memorandum of Agreement with the local government units to provide the
      details of the transfer of funds and the requirements for its liquidation, in
      accordance with the existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules and
      regulations.
      The amount of rice assistance that can be provided to qualified beneficiaries
      shall be equivalent to half of the cost of twenty-five (25) kilograms of rice based
      on the suggested retail price from the Department of Agriculture (DA). The
      amount of each transfer to the LGU shall not exceed Thirty Million Pesos (PhP
      30,000,000.00).
      The financial assistance under this Act may complement any support provided
      by the LGU, government agencies, and non-government organizations. A
      beneficiary who has received rice assistance through the LGU may still qualify
      to receive other financial assistance from the DSWD, subject to the
      requirements under this Act, and professional assessment of the DSWD social
      worker.
SEC. 5. Documentary Requirements. – For purposes of assessment and evaluation,
the solo parent shall submit the following:
   a. Solo Parent Identification Card (SPIC);
   b. Proof of residency such as barangay certificate, utility bill, or Voter’s ID; and
   c. Affidavit of two (2) disinterested persons attesting that the children/dependents
      are under the parental care and support of the solo parent.
SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within six (6) months from the
effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of the DSWD, in consultation with relevant
agencies and stakeholders, shall formulate and promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations to effectively implement the provisions of this Act.
SEC. 7. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the DSWD.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
SEC. 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.
SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, proclamations, rules,
regulations, and other issuances or parts inconsistent with the provisions of this Act
are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.
SEC. 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) national newspapers of general circulation.
