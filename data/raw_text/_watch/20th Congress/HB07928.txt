                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Kidney failure patients depend on dialysis to stay alive, but many Filipinos cannot
obtain this treatment because government hospitals lack adequate dialysis facilities
or because the cost is beyond their means. For poor and inancially vulnerable
families, the long-term expense of dialysis often leads to debt, treatment interruption,
or avoidable loss of life.

This measure mandates the establishment or designation of dialysis units in all
national, regional, and provincial government hospitals to improve availability of
services nationwide. It also strengthens inancial protection by ensuring that dialysis
treatments are covered primarily by PhilHealth and supplemented through Malasakit
Centers, so that indigent and inancially incapacitated patients are spared from heavy
out-of-pocket payments.

By making dialysis services more accessible and affordable in public hospitals, this
bill advances the right to health and social protection. It re lects the State’s
responsibility to provide life-saving care, reduce health inequities, and ensure that no
Filipino is forced to choose between survival and inancial ruin.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                              AN ACT
REQUIRING THE ESTABLISHMENT OR DESIGNATION OF DIALYSIS CLINICS IN ALL
NATIONAL, REGIONAL, AND PROVINCIAL GOVERNMENT HOSPITALS, ENSURING
FINANCIAL RISK PROTECTION FOR DIALYSIS SERVICES FOR INDIGENT AND
FINANCIALLY INCAPACITATED PATIENTS THROUGH PHILHEALTH AND
MALASAKIT CENTERS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Government Hospital
Dialysis Access Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health and to make essential health services accessible and
affordable, particularly for indigent and inancially incapacitated patients. Consistent
with the State policy of universal and compulsory national health insurance coverage,
the State shall ensure that patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) have timely
access to safe dialysis services in government hospitals and are protected from
catastrophic health spending.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   (a) Dialysis clinic/unit refers to a hospital-based facility providing hemodialysis
       and such other renal replacement modalities as may be allowed by DOH
       regulation, with appropriate equipment, water treatment system, infection
       control standards, and trained personnel.
                                                                            2|Page
   (b) Government hospitals covered refer to national, regional, and provincial
       hospitals, including DOH-retained hospitals and provincial hospitals operated
       by local government units (LGUs).

   (c) Indigent and inancially incapacitated patient refers to a patient identi ied
       and validated by the DSWD and/or through the Malasakit Center screening
       process, using standards to be provided in the IRR, for purposes of medical and
        inancial assistance.

SECTION 4. Establishment or Designation of Dialysis Clinics/Units. —

   (a) Requirement. All covered government hospitals shall establish or
       designate a dialysis clinic/unit, subject to DOH licensing standards, within the
       phased timeline provided in the IRR.

   (b) Minimum capacity and standards. The DOH shall prescribe minimum
       service capacity based on catchment population, ESRD burden, and geographic
       access, and shall set standards for staf ing complement, including nephrology-
       trained physicians, dialysis nurses/technicians, and a nutrition
       professional/dietitian for patient counseling.

   (c) Lead agency. The DOH, in coordination with LGUs and other relevant
       agencies, shall lead implementation, including planning, procurement,
       staf ing, and quality assurance.

SECTION 5. Financing and Provision of Dialysis Services for Indigent and
Financially Incapacitated Patients. —

   (a) PhilHealth as primary payer. The DOH and the Philippine Health Insurance
       Corporation (PhilHealth) shall ensure that dialysis services rendered in
       covered government hospitals are reimbursable under the National Health
       Insurance Program, consistent with universal and compulsory coverage.

   (b) Zero or minimal out-of-pocket for indigent patients. For indigent and
        inancially incapacitated patients, the DOH, PhilHealth, DSWD, and other
       partner agencies shall adopt mechanisms to ensure no balance billing or
       minimal out-of-pocket expenses, to the extent allowed by PhilHealth bene it
       design and applicable rules, with remaining eligible costs chargeable to

      available assistance programs coursed through Malasakit Centers where
      applicable.

   (c) Integration with Malasakit Centers. In DOH hospitals and the Philippine
       General Hospital where Malasakit Centers are required, all requests for
       dialysis-related medical and inancial assistance shall be processed through
       the Malasakit Center to streamline aid from DOH, DSWD, PCSO, and PhilHealth.

SECTION 6. Donations; Tax Incentives; Importations. —

   (a) Authority to accept donations. Covered hospitals, through the DOH or the
       concerned LGU for LGU-run hospitals, may accept donations, gifts, grants, and
       bequests to augment resources for dialysis clinic establishment, upgrading,
       and operations.

   (b) Donor’s tax treatment; conditions. Donations made for the bene it of
       dialysis clinics/units in covered hospitals shall be exempt from donor’s tax and
       considered allowable deductions from gross income, subject to applicable
       requirements under the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC), as amended,
       and subject to the condition that donated equipment shall be used actually,
       directly, and exclusively for dialysis services and shall not be disposed of,
       transferred, or sold except in accordance with rules to be set in the IRR.

   (c) Customs duty and tax privileges for importation. Importation of dialysis
       equipment, machinery, and supplies donated for and used actually, directly,
       and exclusively by the dialysis clinic/unit may be granted customs duty and tax
       privileges to the extent allowed by law and subject to DOF/Bureau of Customs
       rules, and consistent with hospital-equipment incentive policies.

SECTION 7. Standards, Referral Network, and Quality Assurance. —

   (a) The DOH shall issue clinical and operational standards, including infection
       prevention and control, water quality, reuse policies (if any), adverse event
       reporting, and patient education.

   (b) The DOH shall establish a referral and scheduling system to decongest
       facilities and improve continuity of care, including linkages with nearby
       government and accredited private dialysis providers where appropriate.

SECTION 8. Reporting, Transparency, and Audit. —

   (a) Annual report. The DOH, in coordination with PhilHealth and the DSWD, shall
       submit an annual report to the Of ice of the President and to the appropriate
       committees of Congress on implementation, utilization, patient coverage, and
       outcomes, using the reporting model under the Malasakit Center law.

   (b) Stewardship and iscal safeguards. Funds and reimbursements utilized
       under this Act shall be managed with iduciary responsibility and shall be
       subject to applicable COA rules, mindful that public funds and program funds
       must be used only as authorized by law.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, PhilHealth, DSWD, and other relevant
agencies shall issue the implementing rules and regulations, consistent with the joint-
IRR approach used in the Malasakit Center law.

SECTION 10. Appropriations. —

   (a) Initial appropriation. The amount of One Billion Pesos (PHP
       1,000,000,000.00) is hereby authorized to be appropriated for initial
       implementation, subject to budgeting, release, and accountability rules.

   (b) Continuing appropriations. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for
       continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
       Appropriations Act.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder not affected thereby shall continue to be in
full force and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
