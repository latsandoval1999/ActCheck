                       AN ACT
CREATING THE NATIONAL COORDINATING COUNCIL FOR
EDUCATION, DEFINING ITS MANDATE, POWERS, AND
FUNCTIONS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Constitution commands the State to protect and promote the right of all
citizens to quality education at all levels and to establish, maintain, and support a
complete, adequate, and integrated system of education. Fulfilling this mandate
requires more than the effective performance of individual education agencies. It
demands a coherent governance framework that enables the entire education
sector to pursue common priorities, align resources, and sustain reforms across
administrations.

      The present trifocalized system assigns distinct mandates to the Department
of Education (DepEd), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and the
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA). While this
structure allows each agency to focus on its respective sector, the challenges
confronting Philippine education increasingly cut across institutional boundaries.
Learner progression, teacher and education workforce requirements, skills
development, labor market responsiveness, data interoperability, scholarship
policy, and the transition of learners from basic education to higher education,
technical-vocational education and training, and employment all require
sustained coordination among several agencies of government.
      The Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II) has
underscored the need for seamless and integrated delivery of education and for
stronger coordination across the education system. In response to this governance
gap, Administrative Order No. 36, series of 2025, established the Education and
Workforce Development Group as a central coordinating mechanism for
education and workforce development. The gains from such coordination,
however, should not depend solely on an administrative issuance or on the
priorities of a particular administration. A permanent statutory mechanism is
necessary to provide institutional continuity, clear accountability, and sustained
policy alignment.

      This bill therefore seeks to create the National Coordinating Council for
Education (NCCE) under the Office of the President as the primary mechanism
for directing, coordinating, and monitoring education and workforce
development reforms across the Executive Branch. Chaired by the President, the
Council will bring together the principal agencies responsible for education, skills
development, science and technology, labor and employment, budgeting, and
national development planning.

       The proposed Council is tasked to align agency policies, plans, programs,
and budgets with the National Education and Workforce Development Plan;
coordinate and monitor priority reforms; develop a comprehensive human
resource development plan for the education sector; improve labor market
information to better guide learners and institutions; establish an interoperable
learner data system; rationalize overlapping inter-agency mechanisms; and
institute a results-based monitoring and evaluation framework. It will likewise
provide a regular venue for ensuring that major education reforms are
implemented coherently and that responsible agencies remain accountable for
agreed targets and outcomes.

       By institutionalizing a permanent apex coordinating body, this measure
seeks to address fragmentation without diminishing the statutory mandates of the
country's education agencies. Rather, it provides the mechanism through which
their respective mandates may operate as parts of a single, coherent education and
workforce development system. This will help ensure continuity of reforms, more
efficient use of public resources, and policies that respond to the needs of Filipino
learners and the evolving demands of the economy.


      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “National
Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE) Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels and
to take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all. The State
recognizes the need to address the learning crisis and ensure the seamlessness of
the education system. To this end, the State shall establish a permanent
mechanism to ensure strong, effective, and continuous coordination among the
agencies involved in education and workforce development, ensuring that
reforms and priorities remain consistent regardless of changes in national
administration.
SEC. 3. Creation of the National Coordinating Council for Education. — There
is hereby created under the Office of the President a high-level inter-agency body
to be known as the National Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE),
hereinafter referred to as the Council, which shall serve as the primary mechanism
for directing, coordinating, and monitoring education and workforce
development reforms across the Executive Branch.

      The Council shall operate under the supervision of the Office of the
President and shall ensure alignment of agency policies, plans, programs, and
budgets with the National Education and Workforce Development Plan, and other
subsequent midterm and long-term plans on education and workforce
development.

SEC. 4. Composition of the Council. — The President of the Philippines shall
serve as Chairperson of the Council. The President may designate a Cabinet
Secretary as Vice Chairperson to oversee day-to-day coordination, with the
following as members:

   (a) Secretary, Department of Education (DepEd);
   (b) Chairperson, Commission on Higher Education (CHED);
   (c) Director General, Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
       (TESDA);
   (d) Secretary, Department of Science and Technology (DOST);
   (e) Secretary, Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE);
   (f) Secretary, Department of Budget and Management (DBM); and
   (g) Secretary, Department of Economy, Planning, and Development
       (DEPDev).

SEC. 5. Powers and Functions. — The Council shall have the following powers
and functions:

   (a) Serve as venue for regular collaboration and coordination to ensure
       harmonized policies and programs of the education agencies, and other
       relevant government bodies;
   (b) Implement, refine, and update the National Education and Workforce
       Development Plan, to inform and ensure coherence of targets, priorities,
       and resource allocations of member agencies;

(c) Ensure alignment and convergence with existing inter-agency
    mechanisms, including those established under Administrative Order No.
    36, s. 2025, and harmonize policies, plans, and programs across relevant
    bodies to avoid duplication, strengthen coordination, and maximize the
    efficient use of government resources;
(d) Oversee, coordinate, and monitor the implementation of priority
    education and workforce development reforms, including those identified
    by the Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II),
    and such other reform initiatives as may be adopted by the Council, in
    order to ensure coherence, continuity, and sustained impact across
    administrations;
(e) Develop and implement a comprehensive human resource in education
    development plan, including the supply and demand of teachers and non-
    teaching professionals and personnel required to support the needs of the
    education sector;
(f) Review and endorse major education sector budget proposals prior to
    submission to DBM;
(g) Propose and jointly implement, through relevant agencies, scholarship
    programs, research, grants, trainings, and other initiatives, to support the
    above plans and to strengthen the capacity of the system as a whole;
(h) Upgrade the country’s labor market information (LMI) system to better
    inform student and school choice, including offerings in Senior High
    School, TVET, higher education, and other lifelong learning
    opportunities, and to serve as evidence-based in the strategic
    identification of government priorities for scholarship programs and other
    grants;
(i) Establish, implement, and maintain an interoperable data system that
    tracks learner progress beginning in early childhood, ensuring integration
    of data across relevant agencies, in partnership with, but not limited to,
    the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), Department of Health (DOH),
    National Nutrition Council (NNC), Department of Social Welfare and
    Development (DSWD), and Department of Information and
    Communications Technology (DICT);
(j) Review and propose mechanisms to rationalize interagency bodies
    participated in by the education agencies, in coordination with the
    Department of Budget and Management, consistent with Republic Act
    (RA) No. 12231 or the Government Optimization Act;

SEC. 6. Technical Working Groups. — To support the work of the Council, the
body may create Technical Working Groups in identified priority areas. Said
Groups shall be composed of senior officials from member agencies, with at least
the level of Assistant Secretary or Director IV of the relevant offices handling
these concerns.

     Further, the Council may invite or enjoin relevant heads of departments and
agencies to join the Council and/or Groups, as necessary. This includes other
executive agencies, heads of industry and educational associations and other
organizations, as well as representatives of relevant sectoral groups, to ensure
overall efficiency and effectiveness.

SEC. 7. Convergence and Accountability. — All Heads of the member-agencies
of NCCE, including their respective attached agencies, are hereby directed to
render their full and timely assistance and cooperation to the Council, and provide
such information and data as may be required to carry out its mandates. The
Council may call upon the assistance of such other agencies of the Government,
as may be necessary in the performance of its functions.

     All member agencies shall be accountable for the timely and effective
implementation of programs, projects, and activities aligned with the National
Education and Workforce Development Plan.

     Furthermore, the Council shall establish a results-based monitoring and
evaluation framework, with clear targets, performance indicators, and reporting
mechanisms. Member agencies, as well as those invited or enjoined to support
the work of the Council, shall regularly submit such data, reports, and information
as may be required to assess progress and ensure accountability.
                                                                                 4
SEC. 8. The Council Secretariat. — The Secretariat shall serve as the central
technical, analytical, and program management arm of the Council, and shall be
responsible for policy analysis, program coordination, monitoring and evaluation,
data integration, and the provision of strategic and administrative support
necessary for the effective discharge of the Council’s mandate. It shall be headed
by an Executive Director with the rank of Undersecretary, who shall be appointed
by the President.

      The Secretariat may be staffed by a core complement of permanent
personnel and augmented, as necessary, by technical experts, seconded personnel
from member and other government agencies, and contracted specialists, subject
to existing civil service, budgeting, procurement, and auditing laws, rules, and
regulations.

SEC. 9. Rationalization and Convergence of Inter-Agency Bodies. — To
promote policy coherence and administrative efficiency, the Council shall serve
as the primary inter-agency platform for matters relating to education and
workforce development.

      Accordingly, existing inter-agency councils, committees, and similar
bodies with substantially similar mandates or memberships shall, to the extent
practicable, conduct their coordination, deliberations, and decision-making
through the Council or its Technical Working Groups.

      Meetings convened by the Council or its duly authorized Technical
Working Groups shall be deemed compliance with the meeting and coordination
requirements of such bodies, subject to applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

SEC. 10. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Office of the
President. Thereafter, the amount necessary for its continuous implementation
shall be included in the budget proposal of the Office of the President in the
annual General Appropriations Act, subject to existing budgeting, accounting,
and auditing rules and regulations.

SEC. 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts not affected shall remain in full
force and effect.
                                                                                  5
SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
administrative orders, rules and regulations, issuances, or parts thereof
inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
amended accordingly.

SEC. 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
