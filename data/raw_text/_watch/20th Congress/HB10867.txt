                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The State, under Article II, Section 15 of the Constitution, is mandated
to protect and promote the right to health of the people. In line with this, the
Department of Health issued Administrative Order No. 2014-0030 requiring
prepackaged food products to list their ingredients, including allergens, to
safeguard consumers. However, this protection remains incomplete, as food
served in restaurants, cafeterias, and other establishments is not subject to
the same mandatory disclosure, leaving millions of Filipinos vulnerable to
accidental allergic reactions.

       Food allergies pose a serious and recurring public health concern. Even
the smallest serving of an allergen can trigger severe reactions ranging from
swollen tissues, vomiting, abdominal cramps, and painful blisters to difficulty
breathing, coma, and even death. Studies in the Philippines confirm that food
allergies affect a growing number of children and adults, with many cases
going unreported due to limited awareness and lack of disclosure
mechanisms. These risks are magnified in provinces and rural communities
where access to emergency medical care is limited, making prevention
through transparency all the more critical.

      This bill seeks to institutionalize the duty of food establishments to
automatically disclose allergy-causing ingredients in their menu items. By
complementing the existing requirement for prepackaged foods, this measure
ensures that consumers are protected not only in grocery aisles but also in
everyday dining settings. It recognizes that diligence must be shared: while
customers have the responsibility to inquire, establishments must carry the
equal responsibility to inform.

      This measure embodies humane regulation as it seeks to protect
vulnerable groups like children, the elderly, and those in marginalized
communities, by reducing preventable health risks. It also strengthens trust
between consumers and establishments, fostering a safer and more inclusive
dining culture across the country.
      The institutionalization of allergen disclosure is a simple yet powerful
step toward health equity. It ensures that every Filipino, regardless of where
they live or what they can afford to eat, enjoys a safer and more dignified
dining experience.

     It is for these reasons that the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                         AN ACT
 MANDATING THE DISCLOSURE OF FOOD ALLERGENS IN PRODUCTS
 OFFERED BY FOOD ESTABLISHMENTS AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Food Allergen
Disclosure Act of 2026.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect
and promote the right to health of the people and to instill health
consciousness among them. To this end, the State affirms its duty to ensure
the highest standards of food safety in all establishments engaged in the sale,
preparation, and service of food, from manufacturing to distribution and
consumption. Recognizing that food allergies and intolerances pose serious
and preventable health risks, the State shall mandate transparency and
accountability in the disclosure of food allergens, thereby safeguarding
consumers from accidental exposure.

       In pursuit of inclusive and humane regulation, the State shall ensure
that allergen disclosure requirements are applied equitably across urban
centers and provincial communities, empowering consumers regardless of
location or economic status. This measure complements existing Department
of Health issuances on food labeling and extends protection to dining
establishments, carinderias, cafeterias, canteens, and other food service
providers, thereby fostering a culture of health awareness, consumer trust,
and community safety.

       SECTION 3. Definitions. – The following words and phrases when used
in this Act shall have the meanings given to them in this Section unless the
context clearly indicates otherwise:

         a) Food allergen shall mean any ingredient used in food
            preparation that is capable of producing an allergic reaction
            including but not limited to the eight major allergens recognized
            globally: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, and
            soy. The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or the National
            Nutrition Council may update this list as new allergens are
            identified.

         b) Hypoallergenic shall mean unlikely to cause an allergic reaction
            and prepared in a manner that minimizes cross-contamination.

         c) Food establishment shall mean any establishment engaged,
            whether primarily or incidentally, in the business of selling food
            to the general public, such as but not limited to restaurants,
            canteens, food stalls, etc.

       SECTION 4. List of Common Food Allergens. - The Food and Drugs
Administration (FDA), through the Center for Food Regulation and Research,
shall create and maintain an inventory of common food allergens and publish
the same on its official website. The list shall be updated annually in
consultation with medical experts, consumer groups, non-government
organizations, and food industry stakeholders. A copy of the said inventory
shall be disseminated to establishments applying for a License to Operate
(LTO) and to local government units for provincial and barangay-level
awareness.

       SECTION 5. Disclosure of Food Allergens. – Food establishments shall
identify food allergens in their offerings, in accordance with the list published
by the FDA, and shall make such allergens known to customers by including
them in the description of every item on their menu. Disclosure shall be visible
at the point of sale, in printed menus, and in online menus or delivery
platforms. Establishments shall use standardized allergen icons or labels to
ensure accessibility for consumers with limited literacy. Should it be
impracticable to print a list of every allergen for every menu item, the
establishment shall endeavor to make it known to its customers that such list
of allergens shall be disclosed to them upon request.

       SECTION 6. Alternatives. – Food establishments shall, as far as
practicable, provide hypoallergenic substitutes for allergy-causing ingredients
in their offerings. Where substitution is not possible, the food establishment
shall give its customer the option to have the allergy-causing ingredient
removed. In any case, the food establishment shall ensure that they have
hypoallergenic offerings at all times. Establishments shall also train staff on
allergen handling and cross-contamination prevention, including the use of
separate utensils and cooking surfaces.

      SECTION 7. Penalties. – Food establishments that fail, despite due
notice and warning, to comply with the provisions of the present Act shall be
fined an amount not less than fifty thousand pesos (PHP 50,000.00) but not
more than one hundred thousand pesos (PHP 100,000.00). Repeated
violations of this Act shall be a ground for the cancellation or revocation of
the establishment’s business permit and/or license to operate.

      In cases where a consumer suffers harm due to undisclosed allergens,
the establishment shall shoulder the medical expenses incurred as a result of
such negligence.

       SECTION 8. Exemptions. – Exempted from the provisions of this Act are
carinderias, backyard food stalls, ambulant food vendors, food kiosks, and
other similar businesses. However, they are encouraged to voluntarily disclose
allergens in their meals and food products.

      SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60)
days from the approval of this Act, the Secretary of Health, through the Food
and Drug Administration, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations required for the effective implementation of the provisions of this
Act. The IRR shall be drafted in coordination with LGUs, barangay health
workers, relevant non-government organizations, and consumer groups, and
shall include provisions for public education campaigns on allergen
awareness, especially in schools and provincial communities.

      SECTION 10. Appropriations – The amount necessary to carry out the
provisions of this Act shall be charged against those authorized in the current
and subsequent General Appropriations Act.

      SECTION 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act
is held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts
unaffected shall remain in full force and effect.

       SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders,
presidential decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders,
rules, and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 13. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
