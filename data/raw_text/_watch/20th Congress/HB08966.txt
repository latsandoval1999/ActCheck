                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE


In December 1967, the United Nations Declaration on Population was signed by 18 heads of states,
where it was stated that: “The population problem must be recognized as a principal element in
long-range planning if governments are to achieve their economic goals and fulfill the aspirations
of their people.”

The Commission on Population or POPCOM, was originally created in 1969 by virtue of
Executive Order (EO) 171 which established a 22-member Commission on Population.
Subseguently, Republic Act (RA) 6365, or the Population Act of the Philippines, was enacted into
law on August 16, 1971, which established the “National Population Policy”. Under Presidential
Decree No. 79, signed on December 8, 1972, the "Revised Population Act of the Philippines,"
reorganized the Commission on Population (POPCOM) as the central planning and coordinating
body for national population policies, including serving as the monitoring agency for the
Philippine Population Management Program (PPMP). It amended R.A. 6365 to strengthen
population control, integrating it with social and economic development.

In 1970 the Ad Hoc Commission on Population, created the year prior, recommended the launch
of the National Population Program through EO 233. Toward the next decade, POPCOM was
listed as an attached agency to the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) by
virtue of EO 123 in 1986. In 1990, EO 48, placed POPCOM under the Office of the President in
order to “facilitate coordination of policies and programs relative to population.” The following
year, EO 476, made POPCOM an attached agency of the NEDA.

However, in managing the national population program, POPCOM was limited to family planning,
health and welfare for decades – more focused on simple population control and family planning,
rather than actually informing development strategies. It is high time that the national population
and development policy is strengthened so as to manage population growth as a strategy for
economic progress and improved living standard, and enhance demographic resilience.

AN ACT STRENGTHENING THE NATIONAL POPULATION AND DEVELOPMENT
    POLICY TO ENHANCE THE COUNTRY’S DEMOGRAPHIC RESILIENCE,
 REPEALING FOR THIS PURPOSE PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 79, SERIES OF
             1972, AS AMENDED, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES


    Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Population and
Development Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policies. It is the policy of the State to:

   a. Foster demographic resilience or the country’s capacity to adapt, thrive and sustain
      socioeconomic growth and stability amidst the evolving population dynamics or changes
      through comprehensive, coherent, and responsive policies that mitigate challenges,
      leverage opportunities, and optimize available dividends;

   b. Effectively develop its human resources through increased investments in education,
      health, and skills through a person’s life course to ensure that they can meaningfully and
      productively participate in the economy and society in whatever demographic context the
      country has;

   c. Enable and support couples and individuals to fully exercise their rights to form and raise
      their families, including their right to have the number, timing, and spacing of children they
      desire within the demands of responsible parenthood and informed choices ensuring that
      all social and development policies are fully responsive to their diverse and changing
      needs; and

   d. Anticipate and respond to global megatrends such as climate change, demographic shifts,
      social inequality, among others, through digitalization and other innovations to foster
      resilience and sustainability for an inclusive future.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act, the following shall refer to:

   a. Demographic Dividend – Refers to the economic growth potential that can result from
      shifts in a population’s age structure, mainly when the share of the working-age population
      (15 to 64) is larger than the non-working-age share of the population (14 and younger, and
      65 and older).
   b. Demographic Resilience – Pertains to the capacity of the population to cope with and
      adapt significant demographic changes such as aging population, migrations patterns and
      fluctuation in birth rates ensuring economic stability and addressing challenges posed by
      demographic shifts.
   c. Life-Course Approach to Population Development – This pertains to a is a way of
      designing population and development strategies that considers the impact of experiences,
      conditions, and capacities at each stage of life in the later human development stage and
      the cumulative effect of investments and interventions in early life stage (before birth,
      during childhood, adolescence, adulthood, old age) over time. It focuses on the continuity
      and connections between generations, recognizing that today’s investments shape
      tomorrow’s demographic and socioeconomic outcomes.
   d. People-centered Development – Refers to an approach to development that focuses on
      improving the capacity of people to participate aand contribute to relevant development
      initiatives and in their community and equitably benefit from the fruits of socioeconomic
      progress.
   e. Population and Development – The explicit consideration of population dynamics and
      factors in development initiatives towards more people-centered interventions.

SECTION 4. Key Components of Demographic Resilience. The State shall build and sustain
its demographic resilience through the following key integrated components:

   a. Data-driven governance. The government shall strengthen its capacity to collect, analyze,
      and use population data to understand and anticipate changes as basis for policy and
      strategic decisions. This includes the capacity of the government institutions to consider
      the forecasted or projected impact of population dynamics on relevant development
      strategies and programs.

   b. Human capital development through life course. The State shall harmonize initiatives
      to support the continuous process of building, enhancing, and sustaining individuals’
      health, skills, knowledge, and capacities from early childhood through old age, recognizing
      that human development unfolds across interconnected phases of life—each influencing
      future outcomes and overall well-being.

   c. Family-centered development. The State shall strengthens its capacity to absorb, adapt
      to, and recover from demographic changes through effective family-centered approaches


                                                                                               4
       in development initiatives, which places people and family at the core of policies,
       programs, and interventions, recognizing and strengthening their capacity as the primary
       social institution responsible for nurturing human capital, transmitting values, and
       providing emotional, social, and economic support across the life course.

   d. Social and economic adaptation. The government shall endeavor to institute reforms in
      social protection systems, adapt to changing labor markets, and pursue responsive
      economic infrastructure and reforms to meet the needs of a changing population structure,
      dynamics, and outcomes.

   e. Leveraging demographic opportunities. The government shall actively seek out and
      promote opportunities that arise from demographic shifts, such as potential demographic
      dividend from a large youth population or economic engagement of older adults.

   f. Life-course and intergenerational policies and approaches in healthy and active
      ageing. The government shall formulate coherent policies and approaches to ensure that
      individuals accumulate health, skills, and social support over time, while fostering
      solidarity and mutual support across age groups. This entails the institution of policies that
      strengthen the health systems, social protection, environments, and community support to
      enable older persons to remain healthy, independent, productive, and socially engaged for
      as long as possible.

   g. Migration and Labor Mobility. The government shall develop and pursue immediate to
      long-term solutions to efficiently and effectively manage social and economic issues and
      opportunities resulting from population movement such as rapid urbanization in the
      receiving areas and depopulation in sending localities. It also includes policies and
      strategies to promote more equitable distribution of socioeconomic development across
      regions and provinces.

   h. Social Inclusion and Intergenerational Cohesion. The government shall ensure that all
      population groups are integrated in the mainstream of development with no one left behind
      particularly those in the marginalized sectors. The State shall likewise foster solidarity,
      and mutual support among people of different age groups or generations within a family,
      community, or society




SECTION 5. Population and Development (POPDEV) Plan of Action. A long-term
Philippine Population and Development Plan of Action, herein referred to as “PPD-POA,”
shall be formulated through multisectoral collaboration upon assumption of the new
Administration. The PPD-POA shall serve as the inter-agency plan for the implementation of the
key components of demographic resilience provided in Section 4 of this Act. It shall be approved



                                                                                                  5
by the President and shall be aligned with the Philippine Development Plan and other relevant
sectoral development plans.

SECTION 6. Strengthening the Commission on Population and Development (CPD). The
Commission on Population and Development, herein referred to as “CPD,” shall serve as the lead
agency in the implementation of this Act. It shall be strengthened to serve as the primary policy,
planning, coordinating, monitoring, and evaluating arm of the Executive Branch of the government
in pursuing the country’s policies and the strategies in ensuring and enhancing the country’s
demographic resilience to support its socioeconomic development thrust. The CPD shall maintain
its attachment with the Department of Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDev) for
purposes of policy and program coordination as provided in Republic Act No. 12145 (Economy,
Planning and Development Act).

SECTION 7. Powers and Functions of the CPD. The CPD shall have the following powers and
functions:

   a. Policy, Plan and Program Development
       i. Lead the formulation, coordination, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the
          PPD-POA and strategies in pursuing the thrusts of this Act;
      ii. Provide information and knowledge-based technical input to government agencies and
          relevant institutions in addressing and integrating population dynamics into their
          development policies, plans, programs, and strategies at all levels.
     iii. Collaborate with regional and local government units or institutions in developing and
          implementing strategic population and development interventions within their
          demographic contexts.
     iv. Develop innovative programs and approaches specifically on health, education, and
          other relevant advocacies (e.g. human capital development) addressing population and
          development issues for adoption and institutionalization of relevant institutions at the
          national and sub-national levels.
      v. Recommend relevant policies to Congress in ensuring the demographic resilience of
          the country.

   b. Institutional Capacity Development
       i. Capacitate or train relevant institutions at all levels in analyzing and addressing the
          impact of population dynamics in their development agenda and strategies.
      ii. Capacitate national, regional, local, and community-based population workers on
          population and development-related strategies, skills, plans, and other interventions or
          services.
    iii. Establish online and other accessible digital platforms for population and development
          knowledge products and training for various stakeholders.
     iv. Provide relevant training and capacity building to pre-marriage orientation facilitators,
          local and community-based population and development and workers.

   c. Population Data, Information and Knowledge Generation and Management
      i. Generate and disseminate knowledge, including research, on emerging population
         trends and issues and its current and potential impact on critical development concerns

             in collaboration with government statistical and research agencies and/or
             academic/research institutions.
       ii.   Develop and establish databases or population surveillance systems such as the
             registry of migrants and marginalized population, among others, in collaboration with
             relevant agencies to strengthen evidence-based planning and policy development,
             subject to Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173).
      iii.   Formulate projections and estimates on the potential impact of population issues on
             socioeconomic development at all levels to inform planning, policy development, and
             projection of service requirements at all levels of governance.

   d. Education, Promotion, and Communication
       i. Develop, implement, and assess promotion and communication strategies and
          materials using effective channels and media at the national and regional levels to
          increase the level of awareness of stakeholders on the interrelationships of population
          and development.
      ii. Collaborate with educational institutions and agencies in integrating population and
          development in curricular or educational programs.

   e. National and International Cooperation
       i. Collaborate with relevant national, regional, and local government agencies and other
           institutions in pursuing the objectives of the national population and development
           strategies.
       ii. Actively participate in international dialogue or conferences and collaborate with
           international organizations in forging international consensus and cooperation on
           global population and development agenda.
      iii. Pursue and strengthen south-south technical cooperation or collaboration or
           knowledge-sharing with other countries on population and development.
     iv. Enter, on behalf of the Republic of the Philippines, into relevant contracts, agreements
           or arrangements with national and international government or private agencies as will
           be necessary, contributory and desirable in the implementation this policy, subject to
           relevant protocols in international engagement.

   f. Resource Generation and Mobilization
       i. Generate, accept, and disburse grants, donations, and other resources in support of the
          implementation of population and development strategies.
      ii. Foster private sector participation in promoting the objectives of the population and
          development policy.
     iii. Collect and retain revenue from the training fees for the maintenance and sustainability
          of capacity building activities for stakeholders, subject to government accounting rules
          and regulations.

SECTION 8. CPD Board of Commissioners. The CPD Board of Commissioners (BOC), referred
to as the Board, shall serve as the executive collegial body responsible for directing and providing
overall policy direction on population matters as they relate to demographic resilience to promote
inclusive and sustainable economic growth and development in the country. The Board shall be
composed of the Secretaries/Heads of the following agencies:


                                                                                                  7
  a.   Department of Economy, Planning and Development (DEPDev), as the Chairperson;
  b.   Department of Health (DOH);
  c.   Department of Education (DepEd);
  d.   Department of Health (DOH);
  e.   Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD);
  f.   Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);
  g.   Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE);
  h.   Department of Agriculture (DA);
  i.   Department of Trade and Industry (DTI);
  j.   Department of Agrarian Reform (DAR);
  k.   Department of Human Settlement and Urban Development (DHSUD);
  l.   Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR);
  m.   Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH);
  n.   Department of Migrant Workers (DMW);
  o.   Department of Budget and Management (DBM);
  p.   University of the Philippines Population Institute (UPPI); and,
  q.   Two (2) representatives from civil society organizations, academe or private institutions
       with expertise on population and development to be appointed by and co-terminus with the
       President.

The Secretaries of government agencies may be represented by their authorized representatives,
who shall not be lower than a Director level.

SECTION 10. Powers and Duties of the Board. The Board shall have the following powers and
duties:

   a. Direct and approve the formulation, adoption and implementation of policies, plans,
      programs, strategies and projects that address emerging population issues with national
      importance as they relate to socioeconomic development in the country.
   b. Enjoin relevant government agencies and its instrumentalities to integrate, adopt, consider,
      and support the population and development strategies to collectively attain the goals and
      objectives of the PPD-POA.
   c. Ensure the adoption and integration of agreed population and development strategies
      within the relevant mandates, programs, and strategies of the Board’s member agencies.
   d. Constitute committees consisting of members of the Board or such other experts and
      consultants from both the private and public sectors as are deemed necessary in the
      preparation of technical inputs needed in the discharge of its functions.
   e. Approve the level of annual CPD budget for consideration in the National Expenditure
      Program for the succeeding fiscal year.
   f. Issue rules and regulations necessary for the effective discharge of its powers and
      functions.

SECTION 11. Functions of the Chairperson of the Board. The Chairman of the CPD Board
shall have the following powers and duties:


                                                                                                8
   a. Call and preside over the meetings of the Board or delegate the same to his/her authorized
      representative or to the most senior official of the members of the Board present in the
      meeting;
   b. Resolve, with the concurrence of the Board, emerging major policy-related issues;
   c. Appoint, with the concurrence of the Board, the Executive Director and such number of
      Deputy Executive Directors and Regional Directors as the exigencies of the service may
      require and recommend to the Board their suspension and removal of said officials for
      cause;
   d. Delegate to the Executive Director, subject to the approval of the Board, any of the powers
      vested in him/her as may be necessary in the performance of functions; and
   e. Perform such other duties as may be assigned to him by the Board from time to time.

Section 12. Meetings of the CPD Board. The Board shall meet every four months for a meeting.
Special meetings can be called by the Chairperson as needed. The majority of the members of the
Board shall constitute a quorum and no action shall be considered as a decision of the Board unless
it carries the affirmative vote of the majority members present and voting. The deliberation and
voting of the Board for urgent or emerging issues or resolutions that may arise in between regular
meetings of the Board may be carried out via ad referendum to be reported during the regular
meeting. The authorized representative may sign resolutions and other pertinent documents on
behalf of their principal, provided that a certification or authorization for representation specified
such power or duty.

The members of the Board who are government officials shall serve without additional
compensation except for the reimbursement of actual travel expenses. The appointed
representatives from the civil society organization, academe, or private sector shall be entitled to
honorarium to be agreed by the Board based on existing government rules and regulations.

Section 13. The Executive Director – The management, direction, and overall operation of the
Commission on Population and Development in the implementation of its mandate shall be vested
in an Executive Director, to be appointed by the Chairperson of the Board, subject to the
concurrence of the members of the Board. The Executive Director shall serve for a fixed term of
six (6) years and maybe reappointed. The Executive Director shall receive the emoluments
attached to the rank of an undersecretary as provided for in Executive Order No. 71 series of 2018.

The Executive Director shall have solid education, training, and expertise related to the
development, implementation, studies, and coordination of population and development-related
strategies and programs and substantial managerial experience as minimum qualifications.

The Executive Director shall have the following powers, duties and responsibilities:

   a. Directs and manages the affairs and operations of CPD, in accordance with its duties and
      functions provided in this law, policies enunciated by the Board, and existing government
      rules and regulations;
   b. Serves as the secretariat to the CPD Board of Commissioners;


                                                                                                    9
   c. Sits in all meetings of the Board and participate in its deliberations without the right to
      vote;
   d. Reviews, recommends, establishes, and maintains the approved organizational structure of
      the CPD;
   e. Appoints officials and employees below the Regional Director and to remove, suspend and
      otherwise discipline, for just cause, any subordinate personnel;
   f. Disburses, administers, and obligates appropriations of the CPD and report the financial
      performance of the agency to the Board;
   g. Submits annual and/or supplemental budget estimates to the Chairperson of the Board for
      endorsement to the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) for approval and
      inclusion in the National Expenditure Program (NEP) and General Appropriations Act
      (GAA);
   h. Facilitates the preparation of the PPD-POA and its corresponding accomplishment report
      for approval of the Board;
   i. Recommends to the Board major policies and strategies on population and development
      that entails adoption by the member agencies within their mandates and key programs and
      for submission to Congress for their consideration and enactment;
   j. Enter into and sign institutional agreements required and necessary for the implementation
      of the PPD-POA and policies enshrined in this Act including amendments, renewal, and/or
      extension thereof;
   k. Represents the agency in high level meetings at the national and international levels; and
   l. Performs other duties as may be assigned to him/her by the Board and the Chairperson.

Section 14. Organizational Structure and Staffing Pattern of the CPD. The CPD shall be
composed of the Central Office and Regional Offices. The CPD shall be headed by the Executive
Director to be assisted by two (2) Deputy Executive Directors:
   a. Deputy for Technical Operations; and,
   b. Deputy for Administrative and Financial Services.

Under the Office of the Executive Director, there shall be the following support units:
   a.   Legal Services;
   b.   International Cooperation and Special Projects;
   c.   BOC Secretariat and Institutional Partnership; and,
   d.   Internal Audit.

The Deputy Executive Director for Technical Operations shall manage and supervise the
operations of the following Divisions, to be headed by a Division Chief:
   a.   Policy, Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation;
   b.   Research and Data Management;
   c.   Capacity Development and Localization;
   d.   Communications and Knowledge Management; and,
   e.   Information and Communications Technology and Digitalization Division.

The Deputy Executive Director for Administrative and Financial Services shall manage and
supervise the operations of the following Divisions, to be headed by a Division Chief:


                                                                                              10
   a. Human Resource Management;
   b. Administrative and Logistics Services; and,
   c. Financial Management and Services.

The CPD shall have a structure in accordance with its major functions. The CPD Management
shall review its existing organizational and staffing pattern every five (5) years or as often as
necessary to recommend an efficient organizational and staffing pattern to the Board. The Board
shall endorse the said recommendation to the Department of Budget and Management (DBM),
upon review and approval, to ensure more effective delivery of CPD’s mandates, objectives, tasks,
and functions.

Subject to the DBM’s approval, the CPD may create new divisions or units with plantilla positions
as it may deem necessary, and shall appoint officers and employees in accordance with the civil
service law, rules, and regulations.

Section 15. CPD Regional Offices. The CPD shall establish, operate, and maintain an office in
each region of the country, except in Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao
(BARMM), which may be created under the Organic Law for the BARMM (Republic Act No.
11054). Each regional office shall be headed by a Regional Director, who shall be assisted by an
Assistant Regional Director. The CPD Regional Offices shall discharge the functions of the CPD,
as applicable, at the regional level.

As part of the staffing pattern of the CPD Regional Offices, there shall be a Provincial Population
and Development Officer for every province, to provide technical assistance to local government
units in the planning and implementation of population and development strategies based on their
contexts.

Section 16. Creation and Mobilization of Inter-agency Regional Coordinative and Advisory
Bodies on Population and Development. To promote interagency collaboration in advancing the
population and development agenda at the regional levels, a Regional Population and Development
Sub-Committee under the Regional Development Committee (RDC) at the regional level shall be
organized and mobilized. The RPD-SC shall discuss, recommend, organize and assess interagency
actions in addressing the population and development policies and agenda in Section 2 and 4 of
this Act. The RPD-SC shall consist of the agency member of the CPD Board and other relevant
institutions as deemed by the sub-committee.

Section 17. Population and Development Personnel as Public Health Workers. All personnel
of the CPD shall be classified as public health workers in accordance with the pertinent provisions
under Republic Act (R.A.) No. 7305, also known as the Magna Carta of Public Health Workers
and its Revised Implementing Rules and Regulations (RIRR);

Section 18. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting. The CPD shall initiate the monitoring and
assessment of this Act based on approved results framework. It shall submit to the Congress,
Office of the President and CPD Board, a comprehensive State of the Philippine Population and
Development Report (SPPDR) every beginning, middle and end of the new Congress and
Administration. The Report shall serve as an input in the formulation of the socioeconomic



                                                                                                11
development agenda. Moreover, the CPD shall submit an annual agency accomplishment report
to the Office of the President and CPD Board.

Section 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within one hundred twenty (120) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the CPD Board shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to
carry out the provisions of this Act in consultation with other concerned government agencies,
private agencies, and civic society.

Section 20. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the effective and continued implementation
of the provisions of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. A
program convergence budget shall be instituted for the national population and development
program to harmonize and mobilize existing financial resources.

Section 21. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act or any part thereof be declared
invalid, the other provisions, insofar as they are separable from the invalid ones, shall remain in
full force and effect.

Section 22. Repealing Clause. Presidential Decree No. 79 series of 1972 and all laws, decrees,
orders, issuances, rules and regulations or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

Section 23. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete publication
in newspapers of general circulation.
