                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

In the Philippines, disasters are not rare events, they are a constant reality. Typhoons, floods,
earthquakes, and volcanic eruptions strike with such frequency that they have become part of the
daily lives of our people. Each storm that destroys homes, each tremor that shakes our land, is a
reminder that our nation sits at the center of one of the most dangerous regions in the world.

The 2025 World Risk Index confirms a difficult reality: the Philippines remains the most
disaster‑prone country in the world, holding this position for four consecutive years. This is not just
a number on a report—it reflects the daily struggles of millions of Filipinos who live under constant
threat from typhoons, earthquakes, floods, and other natural hazards that place lives, livelihoods,
and communities at risk.

This ranking is a reminder of the urgent need to strengthen disaster preparedness, improve
infrastructure, and invest in climate resilience. At the same time, it highlights the courage and
resilience of the Filipino people, who continue to rebuild and rise after each calamity. Their strength
must be matched by decisive action from government, civil society, and international partners to
protect communities, uphold dignity, and secure a safer future for generations to come.

The Climate Change Commission estimates that disasters cost our economy around 200 billion pesos
annually. Since 1990, more than 565 natural disasters have struck the Philippines, claiming nearly
70,000 lives and causing damages worth $23 billion. These numbers show the heavy burden
disasters place on our people and our economy.

Republic Act No. 10121, or the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010,
marked a significant advancement in national policy. It redefined disaster management from a
reactive framework to a proactive system centered on preparedness and risk reduction. Nevertheless,
despite these institutional reforms, the economic consequences of disasters remain substantial and
continue to challenge financial resilience.

This proposed measure seeks to strengthen our resilience by creating a Disaster Risk Financing and
Insurance Council. The Council will develop a framework to ensure quick, reliable, and well-
coordinated financing for disaster-related repair, reconstruction, and rehabilitation of government-
owned infrastructure. It will use both insurance and non-insurance mechanisms to guarantee that
funds are available when urgently needed.
The bill also establishes a National Public Asset Insurance Consortium, led by the Government
Service Insurance System (GSIS) and composed of insurance and reinsurance companies offering
public asset coverage. This Consortium will craft a national disaster risk insurance strategy, ensure
faster claims processing, and support rapid recovery of damaged public assets.

By protecting critical infrastructure and ensuring timely financial support, this measure will help
communities recover faster, reduce economic losses, and limit the long-term impact of disasters on
our people. It is a step toward building a stronger, safer, and more resilient Philippines.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:
 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Public Infrastructure Financing and
 2   Insurance Act".
 3   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State shall ensure a diverse and multi- layered financing
 4   strategy to safeguard its public infrastructure assets from debilitating damage arising from natural
 5   or man-made disasters and ensure the continuous and effective delivery of goods and services. To
 6   this end, the State shall coordinate all existing disaster response financing in relation to the repair,
 7   reconstruction, rehabilitation, and mitigation of public infrastructure assets, and shall develop and
 8   implement a similar disaster risk financing and insurance (DRFI) strategy with the private sector.
 9   The DRFI strategy shall follow up-to-date internationally-accepted disaster risk financing principles
10   and standards, shall be information and data-driven, and shall be anchored on a robust public asset
11   management system. The DRFI strategy shall also integrate and utilize up-to-date technology, such
12   as but not limited to artificial intelligence, to advance and optimize current practices in its
13   implementation.
14   SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For the purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be used as
15   follows:
16
17      (a) "Asset Management" - defined under ISO 55000:2024 as a set of interrelated and
18          interacting elements of an organization, whose function is to establish the asset management
19          policy and asset management objectives, and the processes needed to achieve those
20          objectives. Elements of the Asset Management System include the policies, plans, business
21          processes and information systems, which are integrated to give assurance that the asset
22          management activities will be delivered in line with organizational objectives
23      (b) "Disaster" - a serious disruption of the functioning of a community or a society involving
24          widespread human, material, economic or environmental losses and impacts, which exceeds
25          the ability of the affected community or society to cope using its own resources. Disasters
26          are often described as a result of the combination of exposure to a hazard; conditions of
27          vulnerability that are present; and insufficient capacity or measures to reduce or cope with
28          the potential negative consequences. Disaster impacts may include loss of life, injury, disease
29          and other negative effects on human, physical, mental and social well-being, together with
30          damage to property, destruction of assets, loss of services, social and economic disruption
31          and environmental degradation.
32      (c) “Disaster Risk” - the potential disaster losses in lives, health status, livelihood, assets and
33          services, which could occur to a particular community or a society over some specified future
34          time period.
35   SEC. 4. Coverage and Scope. - This Act shall apply to all public infrastructure assets owned by the
36   Government, including those owned under Republic Act No. 6957 or the Build-Operate-Transfer
37   Law, as amended. Assets covered under this Section shall include:
38          a) Buildings, including but not limited to, schools, hospitals, and government offices;
39          b) Roads;
40          c) Bridges;
41          d) Airports;
42          e) Ports;
43          f) Railways;
44          g) Power generation and transmission structures;
45          h) Water resource management and supply structures; and
46          i) Other similar structures necessary for effective public service delivery.

47   Insurance and non-insurance financing shall cover repair, reconstruction, and rehabilitation
48   or other similar activities to be undertaken as a result of disasters, natural or man-made in
49   origin, to ensure asset recovery and continuity of public service delivery.

50   SEC. 5. Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Council. - A council which shall be
51   known as the Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Council (DRFIC) is hereby created,
52   which shall be headed by the Secretary of the Department of Finance (DOF) as the
53   Chairperson, and with the Secretary of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM)
54   and the Secretary of the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev)
55   as Vice Chairpersons. The DRFIC shall be further composed of the following members:
56          a) Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST);
57          b) Secretary of the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT);
58          c) Secretary of the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);
59          d) Secretary of the Department of National Defense (DND);
60          e) Secretary of the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH);
61   The Vice Chairpersons and Member-Cabinet Secretaries may designate permanent representatives
62   to the DRFIC with a rank of not lower than Assistant Secretary.
63   SEC. 6. Powers and Functions of the DRFIC. - The DRFIC shall be empowered with policy-
64   making, coordination, and monitoring and evaluation functions, as follows:
65      (a) Develop a strategic and comprehensive Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance for Public
66          Assets Framework, hereinafter referred to as the DRFI Framework, which shall ensure a
67          streamlined, seamless, responsive, and         timely financing       for     disaster-related
68          repair, reconstruction, mitigation, and rehabilitation of public assets through both non-
69          insurance and insurance financing mechanisms. Financing shall be secured from a
70          combination of sources, such as the public sector, the private sector, or from relevant
71          international financing mechanisms of which the Government of the Republic of the
72          Philippines is a party thereto, to ensure a multi-layered protection for public assets;
73      (b) Promote policies which shall attract, develop, and expand domestic and overseas investment
74          and capital for disaster risk-related financing;
75      (c) Conduct relevant valuation, analysis, risk modeling, and forecasting of public asset-related
76          data to identify, develop, and enhance DRFI policies;
77      (d) Ensure the data integrity of the NARS;
78      (e) Manage and ensure the insurance of Government public assets;
79      (f) Review and approve the proposed insurance policies to be entered by the Government with
80          the Consortium;
81      (g) Develop and establish insurance claim guidelines in coordination with the Consortium;
82      (h) Ensure competitive practice within the Consortium;
83      (i) Exercise general oversight and regularly monitor and evaluate compliance and
84          performance of the Consortium;
85      (j) Develop and maintain an integrated, centralized, and interoperable digital claims
86          management module which shall track, facilitate, and record:
87                1. Public asset insurance transactions such as claims and claims payments between
88                the Government and the Consortium;
89                2. Financing requests and payments charged under the National Disaster
90                Infrastructure Response Fund (NDIRF); and
91                3. Financing requests to the international financing instruments, such as but not
92                limited to the Loss and Damage Fund.
93   Provided, That the digital module shall have a separate sub-module for insurance-related
94   transactions falling under subparagraph (1) of this subsection, and shall be accessible and integrated
 95   into the insurance claims mechanism of the Consortium.
 96      (k) Manage, under a separate and special account the National Disaster Response for
 97          Infrastructure Fund (NDRIF);
 98      (l) Provide guidelines and criteria for funding under the NDRIF: Provided, That after evaluation
 99          and approval of the funding request, the DRFIC shall authorize the release of funds from the
100          same; m) Provide guidelines and criteria for funding under the Loss and Damage Fund with
101          the Loss and Damage Fund Board, as provided by Republic Act No. 12019 or the Loss and
102          Damage Fund Board Act, and all other international financing mechanism available with the
103          respective managing bodies;
104      (m) Provide guidelines for the monitoring, evaluation, and auditing of the utilization of public
105          asset financing from all applicable sources as provided under subsection (a) of this Section;
106      (n) Submit annual monitoring and evaluation reports on the utilization of public asset financing
107          from all applicable sources to the Commission on Audit (COA);
108      (o) Create additional plantilla positions and/or subordinate offices as may be necessary to
109          perform the functions of the DRFIC, subject to the approval of the DBM;
110      (p) Ensure guidelines, criteria, and relevant processes in relation to DRFIPA are standardized
111          and non-redundant as far as practicable to cut bureaucratic red tape and ensure swift service
112          delivery; and
113      (q) Perform such other functions and implement additional policies as may be necessary for the
114          effective implementation of this Act.
115   SEC. 7. National Public Asset Insurance Consortium. - A National Public Asset Insurance
116   Consortium is established, hereby referred to as the Consortium, composed of all insurance and
117   reinsurance companies offering public asset insurance and shall be led by the Government Service
118   Insurance System (GSIS). 30 31 32 33 34 The Consortium shall develop the country's domestic local
119   disaster risk insurance strategy, and shall aid government-owned in the protection of public assets
120   through the provision of collective public asset insurance, and shall ensure rapid claims processing
121   and asset recovery.
122   SEC. 8. Functions of the NPAIC. - The Consortium shall:
123          a) Issue insurance policies specifically for public assets annually;
124          b) Ensure collective risk sharing among its members;
125          c) Evaluate and approve insurance claims made by the Government in a timely manner;
126          d) Oversee claims processing and subsequently release approved insurance claims to the
127          Government; and
128          e) Submit data regarding insurance claims and payments to the Consortium.
129   SEC. 9. National Disaster Response for Infrastructure Fund. - The existing financing functions
130   for infrastructure for repair, reconstruction, rehabilitation, or disaster mitigation under the National
131   Disaster Risk Reduction Management Fund is hereby transferred to the National Disaster Response
132   for Infrastructure Fund (NDRIF), hereby created under this Act. The NDRIF shall be managed by
133   the DBM, and its use shall be guided by the principles of the DRFI Framework.
134   Financing requests under the NDRIF shall be subject to the evaluation and approval of the DRFIC:
135   Provided, That the DRFIC shall, to the greatest extent necessary, ensure that the NDRIF shall not
136   be the sole funding source for repair, reconstruction, rehabilitation, or disaster mitigation.
137   SEC. 10. Tax Incentive for Members of the Consortium. - To spur investment into Disaster Risk
138   Financing and Insurance, a reduction of five percent (5%) on the prevailing Corporate Income Tax
139   on member companies of the Consortium shall be implemented for the first five (5) years after its
140   effectivity. Thereafter, Congress shall review the tax incentive policy for remedial legislation.

160   SEC. 13. Annual Report. - The DRFIC shall submit to the to the Office of the President, the House

163   SEC. 14. Sunset Review. - Within five (5) years after the effectivity of this Act, or as the need arises,
164   the Congressional Oversight Committee shall conduct a sunset review. For purposes of this Act, the

168   SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The DRFIC, in consultation with the Consortium
