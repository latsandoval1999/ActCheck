                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines is a fuel-import dependent economy, making domestic fuel
prices highly vulnerable to fluctuations in global oil markets. Geopolitical tensions,
supply disruptions, and armed conflicts in key oil-producing regions, particularly in
the Middle East, have repeatedly triggered sharp increases in global crude oil prices.
These shocks inevitably translate into higher pump prices in the country, affecting not
only motorists but the entire economy.

        Recent geopolitical tensions have once again underscored this vulnerability.
Reports of renewed tensions in the Middle East have raised concerns about possible
disruptions in global oil supply, which could lead to another surge in fuel prices
affecting Filipino motorists. Such developments highlight the Philippines’ continued
reliance on imported petroleum and its exposure to volatility in the international oil
market.1

        Republic Act No. 10963, otherwise known as the Tax Reform for Acceleration
and Inclusion (TRAIN) Law, increased the excise tax on petroleum products
beginning in 2018 through a series of scheduled rate adjustments implemented from
2018 to 2020. The law likewise introduced a safeguard mechanism allowing the
suspension of the next scheduled excise tax increase when the average Dubai crude
oil price reaches eighty United States dollars (USD 80) per barrel for three consecutive
months.

       However, the practical effect of this safeguard has already been exhausted. The
scheduled excise tax increases under the TRAIN Law were fully implemented by 2020,
leaving no further adjustments to suspend under the existing mechanism. Moreover,
the law expressly provides that such suspension shall not result in any reduction of
the excise tax already being imposed at the time of the suspension. As such, the
safeguard mechanism merely prevents further increases in fuel excise tax rates but
does not allow a reduction of the existing tax burden, thereby limiting its effectiveness
as a policy tool to mitigate future fuel price surges.2

       Proposals have recently emerged to grant the President emergency authority
to suspend or reduce fuel excise taxes during periods of extraordinary increases in
global oil prices. While such measures may provide temporary relief, they rely heavily
on discretionary action and may raise concerns regarding fiscal stability and
predictability in tax policy. This approach has gained renewed attention amid rising
geopolitical tensions affecting global oil markets. Reports indicate that several
senators have expressed support for granting President Ferdinand “Bongbong”
Marcos Jr. the authority to suspend or reduce fuel excise taxes as global oil prices react
to the escalating conflict in the Middle East.3 While this proposal reflects the urgency
of addressing fuel price volatility, it likewise underscores the need for a more
structured, rules-based mechanism that can provide timely relief without relying
solely on extraordinary executive authority.

       The importance of prudent economic preparation has long been recognized
even in historical accounts such as the biblical narrative of Joseph in the Book of
Genesis,4 where foresight and careful planning enabled a nation to withstand years of
severe famine. In much the same way, sound public policy requires the establishment
of mechanisms that allow governments to anticipate and respond to periods of
economic volatility rather than rely solely on ad hoc measures during times of crisis.

        To ensure that such relief measures remain fiscally sustainable, the bill
establishes a Fuel Excise Tax Stabilization Fund to serve as a financial buffer that can
offset revenue losses resulting from temporary excise tax reductions during periods
of high global oil prices. Similar stabilization mechanisms are implemented in several
countries, including Thailand, Indonesia, and Malaysia, to mitigate the impact of oil
price volatility on domestic markets.5 The measure is further reinforced by a Fiscal
Safeguard Provision, directing the Department of Finance, in coordination with the
Department of Budget and Management, to recommend appropriate fiscal measures
should the temporary reductions significantly affect government revenues, thereby
ensuring that consumer relief measures are implemented without compromising
fiscal stability.

       This bill seeks to amend Section 148 of the National Internal Revenue Code of
1997, as amended by the TRAIN Law, to introduce an automatic and graduated excise
tax adjustment mechanism tied to global oil price thresholds. Under the proposed
measure, temporary reductions in fuel excise taxes shall be implemented once the
average Dubai crude oil price reaches specified levels, thereby providing timely relief
to consumers during periods of extraordinary oil price increases.

        In view of continuing exposure to global oil price volatility, the bill adopts a
structured, rules-based, and time-bound mechanism that authorizes temporary and
graduated reductions in excise taxes on certain petroleum products once the Dubai
crude oil price reaches specified thresholds. The reductions are automatically
implemented based on objective price triggers and are likewise subject to automatic
restoration once global oil prices normalize. This approach ensures that consumer
relief is predictable, transparent, and not dependent on discretionary intervention.

        Complementing the excise tax adjustment mechanism, the bill likewise
provides for a temporary suspension of the twelve percent (12%) value-added tax
(VAT) on the importation of the same covered petroleum products when the same
price triggers are reached. Considering that the Philippines relies heavily on imported
fuel, VAT imposed at the point of importation significantly increases the landed cost
and immediate cash outlay required for fuel imports during periods of elevated global
oil prices. Temporarily suspending VAT under clearly defined price triggers is
therefore intended to moderate the pass-through of extraordinary global price shocks
to domestic fuel prices and inflation, while ensuring that the relief mechanism remains
targeted, automatic, and time-bound.

       Furthermore, this measure proposes to extend the monitoring and adjustment
framework for fuel excise taxes until 2030, allowing the government to respond to
global energy market volatility in a more structured, predictable, and rules-based
manner.By institutionalizing a price-triggered tax adjustment mechanism, this
measure seeks to strike a balance between consumer protection and fiscal
responsibility. It provides timely relief during periods of significant fuel price
increases while preserving the government’s capacity to generate revenues necessary
to support infrastructure development, social services, and economic recovery
programs.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Insertion of a New Section 148-B under the National Internal Revenue
Code. — A new section designated as Section 148-B of Republic Act No. 8424,
otherwise known as the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, is
hereby inserted immediately after Section 148-A, to read as follows:

                      SEC. 148-B. Temporary Adjustment of Excise Taxes on Certain
               Petroleum Products Based on Dubai Crude Price Triggers. — To mitigate the
               impact of extraordinary increases in global crude oil prices on domestic
               fuel prices and inflation, the excise tax rates imposed on covered
               petroleum products under Section 148 of this Code shall be temporarily
               reduced in accordance with this Section:

               (a) Covered Products. — The temporary adjustments under this Section
               shall apply to the following petroleum products subject to excise tax
               under Section 148, as amended, and as described therein:

                      (1) gasoline (including unleaded premium gasoline and regular
                          gasoline);
                      (2) diesel fuel oil;
                      (3) kerosene: Provided, That kerosene, when used as aviation
                          fuel, shall be subject to the same tax applicable to aviation
                          turbo jet fuel, such tax to be assessed on the user thereof; and
                      (4) aviation turbo jet fuel (including Jet A-1 or equivalent aviation
                          turbine fuel/jet fuel); and


                                           Page 4 of 9
       (5) liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), including LPG used for
           motive power at the equivalent rate as diesel fuel oil, as
           applicable under Section 148.

      For avoidance of doubt, this Section shall cover both domestically
produced and imported covered products, consistent with excise tax
administration under this Code.

(b) Price Reference; Computation. — For purposes of this Section,
“Dubai crude oil price” refers to the monthly Dubai crude oil price based
on the Mean of Platts Singapore (MOPS) monthly average of daily
assessments for each relevant month, or such successor benchmark as
may be recognized in the implementing rules. The Department of
Energy (DOE) shall adopt a uniform computation method and publish
the computation table and data sources.

(c) Trigger for Temporary Reduction; Graduated Rates. — Once the DOE
certifies and publishes under paragraph (e) that the Dubai crude oil
price has reached or exceeded any of the following thresholds, the excise
tax rates applicable to the covered products under Section 148 shall be
temporarily reduced as follows:

   (1) if at least United States Dollars Eighty (USD 80) per barrel:
       reduction of twenty-five percent (25%) of the applicable excise tax
       rate under Section 148;
   (2) if at least United States Dollars Ninety (USD 90) per barrel:
       reduction of fifty percent (50%) of the applicable excise tax rate
       under Section 148; and
   (3) if at least United States Dollars One Hundred (USD 100) per
       barrel: reduction of seventy-five percent (75%) of the applicable
       excise tax rate under Section 148.

       Where multiple thresholds are met, the highest applicable
reduction shall apply.

(d) Restoration of Original Rates. — Any temporary reduction under
paragraph (c) shall remain in effect until the DOE certifies and publishes
that the Dubai crude oil price has fallen below USD 80 per barrel for one
(1) month, at which time the original excise tax rates under Section 148
shall be automatically restored effective on the first day of the
immediately succeeding month.

(e) Certification; Publication; Determination of Applicability. — The
DOE shall compute and certify the monthly Dubai crude oil price and
determine whether the conditions for (i) the imposition of a temporary
reduction under paragraph (c), or (ii) the restoration of original rates

             fifth (5th) day of each month, the certification and computation table on
             its official website and in at least one (1) newspaper of general
             circulation.

             (f) Implementing Revenue Issuances; Effectivity for BIR and BOC
             Collection. — Within five (5) days from DOE publication under

                 (2) covered products imported and released from customs custody,

                  on or after said effectivity date, consistent with existing excise tax
             administration rules.

             (g) Transparency; Monitoring of Domestic Price Movements. — During
             periods when a temporary reduction under this Section is in effect, the
             DOE shall monitor and publish the movement of domestic retail prices
             of the covered products, including the estimated excise tax component
             per liter and changes thereto, in the manner provided in the
             implementing rules.

             (h) Relation to Prior Provisos under Section 148; Separability from
             Suspension Mechanism. — This temporary reduction mechanism is
             separate from, and in addition to, any suspension mechanism provided
             under Section 148 and Section 148-A. The proviso under Section 148
             stating that any suspension of the increase in excise tax shall not result
             in any reduction of the excise tax being imposed at the time of the
             suspension shall not apply to temporary reductions implemented under
             this Section.

             (i) Sunset. — No temporary reduction under this Section shall apply
             after 31 December 2030, unless extended by Congress.”

       SEC 2. Temporary Suspension of the Twelve Percent (12%) Value-Added Tax on
Importation of Covered Fuel Products Upon Price Triggers. — Notwithstanding any
provision of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, to the contrary,
once the DOE certifies and publishes under Section 148-B(e) that the Dubai crude oil


                                       Page 6 of 9
price has reached or exceeded United States Dollars Eighty (USD 80) per barrel, the
twelve percent (12%) value-added tax (VAT) on importation shall be temporarily
suspended with respect to the importation of the covered products under Section 148-
B(a), subject to the following:

       (a) Scope. — The suspension shall apply only to VAT imposed on the
       importation of the covered products under Section 148-B(a), and shall not be
       construed as:
              (1) an exemption from VAT on the sale, barter, exchange, or lease of
                  goods or properties or the sale of services in the Philippines; or
              (2) an exemption from any excise tax, customs duty, or other charge
                  imposed by law.

       (b) Effectivity; Administration. — The suspension shall take effect on the first
       day of the month immediately following DOE publication under Section 148-
       B(e). Within five (5) days from such publication, the DOF, through the BIR and
       the BOC, shall issue the necessary revenue issuance(s) to implement the
       suspension for covered importations released from customs custody on or after
       said effectivity date.

       (c) Restoration. — The VAT on importation suspended under this Section shall
       be automatically restored effective on the first day of the month immediately
       following DOE publication under Section 148-B(e) that the restoration
       condition under Section 148-B(d) has been met.

       (d) Sunset. — No suspension under this Section shall apply after 31 December
       2030, unless extended by Congress.

        SEC 3. Annual Report to Congress. — The DOE shall submit to the Senate
Committee on Energy, the Senate Committee on Ways and Means, the House
Committee on Energy, and the House Committee on Ways and Means, an annual
report on the implementation of this Act, including its impact on: (a) retail fuel prices;
(b) inflation; and (c) national government revenues from excise taxes on petroleum
products and taxes collected on fuel importation.

       SEC 4. Fuel Excise Tax Stabilization Fund. — There is hereby created a Fuel Excise
Tax Stabilization Fund (the “Fund”), which shall be maintained as a special account
in the General Fund, to be administered by the Department of Finance, subject to
applicable government accounting and auditing rules.

       (a) Sources. — The Fund shall consist of:

              (1) Stabilization Contributions. — During periods when the DOE
                  certifies and publishes that the Dubai crude oil price is below United
                  States Dollars Seventy (USD 70) per barrel for one (1) month, there
                  shall be credited to the Fund a portion of collections from excise


                                         Page 7 of 9
                  taxes on petroleum products under Section 148: Provided, That such
                  crediting shall not exceed an amount equivalent to one percent (1%)
                  of total monthly collections from excise taxes on petroleum products
                  under Section 148, and shall not impair the cash requirements of
                  existing appropriations;

              (2) Such appropriations as may be provided by Congress;

              (3) Interest and other income derived from the placement of amounts
                  in the Fund in authorized government financial instruments, subject
                  to applicable rules; and

              (4) Donations or grants accepted in accordance with existing laws,
                  rules, and regulations.

       (b) Uses. — Amounts in the Fund may be used exclusively to offset revenue
       losses directly attributable to temporary excise tax reductions under Section
       148-B and the temporary VAT suspension on importation under Section 2 of
       this Act, and to support duly authorized programs mitigating the impact of fuel
       price volatility, subject to appropriation and release in accordance with law.

       (c) Reporting. — The DOF shall submit to Congress, within ninety (90) days
       after the end of each fiscal year, an annual report on the status of the Fund,
       including amounts credited, disbursements, and outstanding balance.

       SEC 5. Fiscal Safeguard Provision. — In the event that temporary excise tax
reductions under Section 148-B and the temporary VAT suspension on importation
under Section 2 of this Act result in significant revenue loss materially affecting the
fiscal position of the National Government, the DOF, in coordination with the
Department of Budget and Management (DBM), shall recommend to the President
and report to Congress appropriate measures, including budgetary adjustments,
expenditure prioritization, or other revenue measures, to maintain fiscal sustainability
while ensuring the effective implementation of this Act.

        SEC 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOF, the BIR, the BOC, and the DOE shall jointly
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of
this Act.

      SEC 7. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

       SEC 8. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.
