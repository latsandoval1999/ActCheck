The important fact is that an agricultural drone is not merely a machine that flies above
a field. Properly deployed, it is a field-level public service: an instrument for locating crop
stress, documenting disaster exposure, producing maps that can guide protective works, and
applying farm inputs with a degree of precision that manual or indiscriminate application may
not readily achieve. Yet the value of the technology is defeated if its benefits remain confined
to entities that can purchase equipment, employ specialized personnel, navigate separate
regulatory requirements, and absorb the cost of an operational mishap. The policy question,
therefore, is not whether agriculture should use drones. It is whether a smallholder farmer can

obtain a safe, accountable, and affordable drone service when it is needed.

The Food and Agriculture Organization reported in 2016 that it was helping farmers
in the Philippines prepare for and assess damage from extreme weather events through
drones. According to the Department of Agriculture Director of Field Operations quoted by
the FAO, drones can cover up to 600 hectares a day for agricultural risk analysis.? The FAO
further reports that drone-generated information may identify agricultural systems exposed
to natural disasters and inform measures including ground contouring, retaining walls, and
protective vegetation. These findings demonstrate an important public capability: a well-
planned flight can help translate a broad weather threat into a practical picture of where
preventive action is most urgently required. They also expose the unresolved question. A
capability that can survey wide areas rapidly must be organized so that the farmer with a small

parcel is not last in line for information, assistance, and recovery.

The Government has already begun building the technical foundation upon which this
measure proceeds. The Department of Agriculture and the FAO initiative has used drones
equipped with photogrammetric and navigation equipment for rapid and reliable assessments,
while DA and FAO technical specialists have received a three-week intensive course
consisting of lectures, simulation exercises, actual flying, and mission planning. Through the
Drones4Rice Project, a sub-component of the Department of Agriculture National Rice
Program, the Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority is developing protocols, standards, and
monitoring guidelines for drone use in agriculture. The project brings together the DA
National Rice Program, DA Regional Field Offices 2, 4A, and 5, the Fertilizer and Pesticide
Authority, and the Bureau of Agricultural Research.> This bill does not displace these efforts.
It supplies the missing national service framework that can bring trained operators, shared
access, safety requirements, compensation, and accountable local delivery into one coherent

system.

The strongest objection to wider agricultural drone deployment deserves direct
recognition. Precision equipment can create new risks when operators are inadequately
trained, when chemical application is poorly supervised, when flights disregard airspace or
nearby communities, when farm data are used without meaningful safeguards, or when an
accident leaves no clear avenue for redress. The Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority itself
recognizes that, despite drone availability and existing regulations, further work is needed to
reduce regulatory constraints, standardize operations, develop digital precision-agriculture
tools, and scale adoption.‘ Potential users may also need training and may hesitate to integrate
new tools into established farm practices. This measure answers those concerns not by
treating innovation as self-executing, but by requiring competence, operational standards,
environmental safeguards, privacy-protective data governance, liability rules, and insurance

before drone services are presented as a dependable public-facing agricultural option.

The measure establishes the National Agricultural Drone Service and Safety Program,
to be led by the Department of Agriculture and delivered through accessible shared-service
arrangements, including farmer cooperatives and qualified local service providers. Its design
recognizes that ownership is not the only path to technological benefit. A farmer should be
able to request certified crop assessment, disaster-risk mapping, or precision application
services without first having to purchase, maintain, insure, and learn to operate an aircraft.
The Program will connect service access with operator certification, mission planning,
traceable records, safe flight practices, calibrated application standards, and a clear
responsibility chain. It will also protect farm-generated data by limiting its collection and use
to legitimate purposes, securing it against unauthorized access, and recognizing that
information about a farm has economic and personal consequences for the people who work

it.

One principle captures the architecture of this bill: every smallholder farmer must be
able to obtain the benefits of agricultural drone technology as a safe, certified, affordable, and
accountable service, rather than as an expensive private gamble. The bill is complementary
to the pending measure on unmanned systems regulation, which recognizes agricultural drone
use and proposes rules for such operations. It also complements the pending initiative for
local agriculture information systems, insofar as useful agricultural information must reach
farmers through systems they can understand and use. This proposed measure is distinct in
its focus: it creates the service-and-safety bridge between technical capability and the farmer
standing in the field. Experience abroad also shows the value of this approach. In Italy, an
agricultural-drone operational concept and safety analysis identified operational hazards,
assessed ground and air risks, and set mitigation and contingency measures before a generic
operational authorization was issued. Such planning is precisely what responsible scale

requires.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL AGRICULTURAL DRONE SERVICE
AND SAFETY PROGRAM TO PROVIDE SMALLHOLDER FARMERS ACCESS
TO CERTIFIED DRONE-BASED CROP ASSESSMENT, DISASTER-RISK
MAPPING, AND PRECISION FARM-INPUT APPLICATION SERVICES,
PRESCRIBING STANDARDS FOR OPERATORS, FLIGHT OPERATIONS, DATA
GOVERNANCE, ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION, LIABILITY, AND
INSURANCE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress

assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Handa-Bukid Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. —- Pursuant to Article If, Section 21 and Article
XIII, Section 5 of the Constitution, it is the policy of the State to make safe, affordable, and
accountable agricultural drone services available to smallholder farmers, particularly those in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, so that they may assess crop conditions,
prepare for disasters, and apply farm inputs with appropriate technical supervision without

being required to purchase or own a drone.

The State recognizes agricultural drones as tools that may support timely crop

assessment, disaster-risk mapping, and precision application of farm inputs when operated by

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. —- As used in this Act:

(a) "Agricultural drone" refers to an unmanned aircraft system used for crop
assessment, disaster-risk mapping, field surveying, or precision application of seeds,

fertilizers, pesticides, biological agents, water, or other farm inputs;

(b) “Agricultural drone service" refers to a service performed through an agricultural

drone for a farmer, farmers’ organization, cooperative, or agricultural enterprise;

(c) "Certified operator" refers to a natural person determined by the Department of
Agriculture to have completed the applicable competency, safety, environmental
protection, and mission-planning requirements under this Act, without prejudice to
permits, licenses, or authorizations required by the Civil Aviation Authority of the

Philippines under Republic Act No. 9497, or by other government agency;

(d) "Department" refers to the Department of Agriculture;

(e) "Farm data" refers to geospatial, visual, sensor-generated, agronomic, operational,
and other data collected from or generated in relation to an identified farm, farm

parcel, crop, or farmer through an agricultural drone service;

(f) "Geographically isolated and disadvantaged area" refers to an area so identified

under applicable government criteria;

(g) "Precision farm-input application” refers to the controlled aerial application of a

farm input based on a documented field plan, prescription, or operational protocol;

SECTION 4. National Agricultural Drone Service and Safety Program. — The
Department shall establish and administer the National Agricultural Drone Service and Safety

Program, hereinafter referred to as the Program.

The Program shall provide shared-service access to certified agricultural drone
services for crop assessment, disaster-risk mapping, and precision farm-input application. It
shall prioritize the persons and areas identified in Section 2 of this Act and shall initially
deploy services in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas and disaster-prone

agricultural communities, subject to the availability of appropriations.

The Program shall support service delivery through farmer cooperatives, associations,
local service providers, and other accredited providers. It shall not require a smallholder
farmer to acquire an agricultural drone as a condition for receiving assistance or participating

in the Program.

No local government unit shail be required to procure drones, employ personnel,
establish an office, or incur any expense under this Act unless funds for that purpose have
been appropriated, transferred, or otherwise made available to it, or unless it voluntarily

undertakes such activity using lawfully available local funds.

SECTION 5. Service Access, Accreditation, and Shared-Service Support. — The
Department shall establish a transparent process for accrediting service providers and

matching them with participating farmers and agricultural communities.

In accrediting providers and allocating shared-service support, the Department shall

enterprises, and providers principally serving geographically isolated and disadvantaged
areas. The preference shall be based on the objective of correcting unequal access to
equipment, trained personnel, and commercial agricultural services, and shall not excuse

compliance with applicable safety and environmental standards.

The Department may provide service vouchers, mission subsidies, equipment-sharing
arrangements, training support, group insurance support, and other forms of assistance

Accreditation under this Act shall not substitute for any registration, permit, license,

competent government authority, including the testing and registration of agricultural

Fisheries Mechanization (AFMech) Law,” and the registration and use of fertilizers and

pesticides under Presidential Decree No. 1144.

SECTION 6. Operator Competency and Operational Standards. — The Department,
through its appropriate offices and in consultation with the Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority
created under Presidential Decree No. 1144, farmer organizations, agricultural workers,
agricultural education and research institutions, service providers, and the Civil Aviation
Authority of the Philippines under Republic Act No. 9497, shall adopt competency and

operational standards for the Program.

The standards shall include mission planning; pre-flight and post-flight inspection;
site assessment; weather and hazard assessment; safe handling and loading of farm inputs;
equipment calibration; recordkeeping; emergency procedures; communication with affected
persons; and measures to prevent drift, spills, unauthorized discharge, collision, and damage

to persons, crops, livestock, property, and the environment.

Precision farm-input application shall be conducted only by a certified operator acting

under a documented field plan and in accordance with applicable product labels,

SECTION 7. Farm Data Governance and Informed Consent. — A service provider
shall obtain the informed consent of the farmer or lawful farm operator before collecting farm
data, except when collection is required for an emergency response authorized by law.
Consent shall identify the data to be collected, the service purpose, the persons or entities
with whom data may be shared, the period of retention, and the means by which the farmer
may request access to, correction of, or deletion of data, subject to lawful retention

requirements.

Farm data shall be used only for the purpose consented to or otherwise authorized by
law. A service provider shall implement reasonable administrative, technical, and physical
safeguards against unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure, loss, or misuse of farm data.
Farm data that constitute personal information shall be processed in accordance with Republic

Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012.”

The Department may use aggregated or de-identified farm data for agricultural
planning, disaster preparedness, research, and program evaluation, provided that the data
cannot reasonably identify an individual farmer or specific farm without lawful authority and

appropriate safeguards.

be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

The Department may accept grants, donations, technical assistance, and equipment
from lawful sources, subject to applicable laws, rules, and auditing requirements. Such
assistance shall not diminish the Department's duty to preserve public contro! over Program

standards, beneficiary selection, farm-data safeguards, and conflict-of-interest protections.

SECTION 10. Relationship to Other Laws. — This Act shall be construed in harmony
with applicable laws, rules, and issuances, including Republic Act Nos. 9497, 10173, 10601,

SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. —- Within one hundred eighty
(180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department shall promulgate the implementing
rules and regulations necessary for its effective implementation, in consultation with the Civil
Aviation Authority of the Philippines, the Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority, farmer
organizations, agricultural workers, service providers, local government units, and other

concerned stakeholders.

The rules shall establish accreditation procedures, competency requirements, mission
records, minimum insurance standards, incident reporting procedures, data-governance
safeguards, beneficiary selection procedures, grievance mechanisms, and standards for

evaluating service quality.

The absence or delay of the implementing rules and regulations shall not suspend

rights and duties that are sufficiently defined by this Act.

SECTION 12. Monitoring, Public Reporting, and Grievance Redress. — The
Department shall establish a simple and accessible mechanism through which farmers,
workers, neighboring landholders, and service providers may submit complaints, incident

reports, and requests for review concerning services under the Program.

The Department shall submit an annual report to Congress on the implementation of
this Act. The report shall identify the areas served, the categories of beneficiaries served,
training and accreditation activities, incidents reported, claims resolved, expenditures,

service-delivery constraints, and recommendations for improvement.

In evaluating service providers, the Department shall assess both demonstrated
achievement and measurable improvement over the provider's own prior performance, and
shall compare providers serving substantially similar terrain, crop conditions, service types,
and resource circumstances. A provider shall not receive an adverse performance finding for
delays, shortages, or failures caused by factors not attributable to the provider, as documented

in the record.

SECTION 13. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared

unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force

and effect.

SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby

repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 15. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its

publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
