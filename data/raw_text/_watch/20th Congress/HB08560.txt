                              AN ACT
AUTHORIZING THE PRESIDENT, UNDER CLEAR AND LIMITED CONDITIONS, TO
TEMPORARILY REDUCE OR SUSPEND THE IMPOSITION OF EXCISE TAXES ON
CERTAIN PETROLEUM PRODUCTS DURING EXTRAORDINARY GLOBAL OIL
PRICES SHOCKS, PROVIDING SAFEGUARDS AND CONGRESSIONAL OVERSIGHT,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Fuel prices have a direct and immediate effect on the daily lives of Filipinos. When
global oil prices rise, the cost of gasoline, diesel, and other petroleum products also
increases in the Philippines. These increases raise transportation and logistics costs,
which then push up the prices of food and basic goods. In recent months, pump prices
in many parts of the country have remained high, placing a heavy burden on
commuters, workers, and small businesses. For many Filipino families, higher fuel
prices mean less money for food, education, and other essential needs.

Under the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion Law (Republic Act No.
10963), petroleum products are subject to excise taxes that form part of the pump
price paid by consumers. While the law was designed to generate revenue for
important government programs, it also recognized that extraordinary increases in
global oil prices may require temporary relief. During periods of unusually high oil
prices, maintaining the full excise tax on fuel can further increase pump prices and
worsen inflationary pressures on households and businesses.

This bill proposes the creation of a Fuel Price Stabilization and Excise Tax
Flexibility mechanism that will allow the government to temporarily reduce or

suspend fuel excise taxes during periods of extraordinary global oil price shocks. The
measure sets clear conditions and requires coordination among economic and energy
agencies to ensure that the relief is carefully targeted, temporary, and fiscally
responsible. It also requires oil companies to fully pass on any tax reduction to
consumers so that the benefit will immediately appear in lower pump prices.

By providing a practical and time-bound tool to respond to sudden fuel price spikes,
this measure aims to protect Filipino consumers, reduce inflationary pressure, and
help stabilize the economy during global oil crises.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Fuel Price Stabilization
and Excise Tax Flexibility Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect consumers
and the national economy from severe and extraordinary fuel price shocks that
materially threaten price stability, transport costs, and the purchasing power of
households, while maintaining iscal responsibility.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed as permanently modifying the excise tax
structure established under the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) “Code” refers to the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.



                                                                           3|Page
   (b) “Extraordinary Global Oil Price Shock” refers to a sustained, abnormal increase
       in international crude oil prices accompanied by domestic macroeconomic
       stress, as determined under Section 5 of this Act.

   (c) “MOPS Dubai” refers to the Mean of Platts Singapore (MOPS) Dubai crude oil
       price benchmark, or such other internationally recognized benchmark as the
       Department of Finance (DOF) may adopt by regulation for purposes of this Act,
       provided it is publicly veri iable and consistently applied.

SECTION 4. Grant of Limited Authority; Nature of Delegation. — Subject to the
standards, limitations, and procedures in this Act, the President may temporarily
reduce or suspend the imposition of excise tax on petroleum products under the Code,
as amended by Republic Act No. 10963 (TRAIN).

The authority under this Act shall be exercised only:

   (a) Upon recommendation of the Secretary of Finance;
   (b) After consultation with the Secretary of Energy and the Secretary of the
       Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev); and
   (c) Upon certi ication of the existence of an Extraordinary Global Oil Price Shock
       under Section 5.

SECTION 5. Trigger Conditions; Certi ication; Recommendation. —

   (a) Trigger Conditions. — The authority under this Act may be exercised only
       upon a determination that an Extraordinary Global Oil Price Shock exists,
       evidenced by the concurrence of at least two (2) of the following conditions,
       occurring within the same three (3)-month reference period:

       (1) International Price Trigger. — The average MOPS Dubai crude oil price
           reaches or exceeds Ninety United States Dollars (US$90) per barrel for
           three (3) consecutive months;

       (2) In lation Trigger. — The national in lation rate breaches the government’s
           in lation target range or shows a signi icant upward deviation, and such
           breach or deviation is determined by the DOF and DEPDev, in consultation
           with the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), to be materially attributable to
           domestic fuel and transport costs; and/or



                                                                           4|Page
       (3) Exchange Rate Trigger. — The Philippine peso depreciates by at least ten
           percent (10%) against the United States dollar, measured by the average
           reference exchange rate for the same three (3)-month period, compared to
           the immediately preceding three (3)-month period.

   (b) Certi ication and Recommendation. — Within ten (10) days from the end of
       the three (3)-month reference period, the Secretary of Energy, in coordination
       with the Secretary of Finance and the Secretary of DEPDev, shall submit to the
       President a written certi ication stating:

       (1) Which trigger conditions are present and the date supporting the same;
           and
       (2) The recommended scope, rate, and duration of the excise tax reduction or
           suspension.

SECTION 6. Coverage. — The authority under this Act shall apply to the petroleum
products subject to excise tax under Section 148 of the Code including but not limited
to:
    (a) Diesel fuel oil;
    (b) Gasoline;
    (c) Kerosene;
    (d) Lique ied petroleum gas (LPG);
    (e) Aviation turbo jet fuel; and
    (f) Such other petroleum products as may be enumerated under the Code and the
        implementing rules.

SECTION 7. Limitation on the Extent and Duration of Reduction or Suspension.
—

   (a) Extent. — Any reduction or suspension shall be limited to the minimum level
       reasonably necessary to mitigate the extraordinary shock, and shall not reduce
       the excise tax below zero (0).

   (b) Duration. — Any exercise of authority under this Act shall be effective for a
       period not exceeding three (3) months, renewable once for another three (3)
       months: Provided, that any further extension shall require an Act of Congress
       of a joint resolution issued in accordance with Congress’ rules.

   (c) Product and Geographic Targeting. — When warranted by the data, the
       President may limit the reduction or suspension to particular petroleum

                                                                          5|Page
       products most directly affecting public transport, food logistics, and power
       generation, or to speci ic geographic areas experiencing supply disruption:
       Provided, That the basis for such targeting shall be stated in the proclamation
       or executive issuance implementing this Act.

SECTION 8. Implementing Issuance; Publication; Effectivity. — The President
shall implement any reduction or suspension under this Act through an executive
issuance stating the legal and factual basis, scope, rate, and duration. The executive
issuance shall be published and shall take effect on the date stated therein, but not
earlier than publication.

SECTION 9. Mandatory Pass-Through; Price Monitoring. —

   (a) Pass-Through. — Any excise tax reduction or suspension under this Act shall
       be re lected fully in the selling price of petroleum products at the next pricing
       cycle after effectivity, as determined by the Department of Energy (DOE).

   (b) Monitoring and Transparency. — The DOE, in coordination with the
       Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), shall:

       (1) Monitor compliance with pass-through;
       (2) Require submission of price and inventory data in such forms as may be
           provided in the implementing rules, and
       (3) Publish at least weekly price monitoring reports during the effectivity of
           the tax reduction or suspension.

   (c) Enforcement. — Non-compliance with the pass-through requirement shall
       subject the violating entity to administrative penalties under applicable laws,
       rules, and regulations, without prejudice to prosecution under other
       applicable laws.

SECTION 10. Fiscal Safeguards; Expenditure and Financing Plan. — Within ifteen
(15) days from effectivity of any reduction or suspension, the DOF and the
Department of Budget and Management (DBM) shall submit to Congress a iscal
impact statement and, when necessary, an expenditure and/or inancing plan to
maintain iscal sustainability.

SECTION 11. Report to Congress; Congressional Oversight. — Within thirty (30)
days from the issuance of an executive issuance under Section 8, the President shall
submit to Congress a report containing:

                                                                            6|Page
   (a) The trigger conditions and supporting data;
   (b) The duration, products covered, and rates reduced or suspended;
   (c) Estimated revenue impact and mitigation measures; and
   (d) Observed and projected effects in pump prices, in lation, and transport costs.

SECTION 12. Sunset Clause. — The authority granted under this Act shall expire ive
(5) years after its effectivity unless extended by Congress.

SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (6) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOF, in coordination with the DOE, the Bureau of Internal
Revenue (BIR), the DBM, the DTI, and DEPDev, shall promulgate the implementing
rules and regulations.

SECTION 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
