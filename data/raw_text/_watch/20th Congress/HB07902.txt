    AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL COASTAL GREENBELT PROGRAM,
         PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippines, being an archipelagic nation with one of the longest coastlines in the
world, is among the most vulnerable countries to the impacts of climate change.1 It
affects the day-to-day lives and poses a significant risk to the livelihood of every
Filipino, wherever they are. Our coastal zones, which host a significant portion of the
population and economic activity, are increasingly exposed to intensifying natural
hazards, including more frequent and severe typhoons, rising sea levels, and
catastrophic storm surges.

Despite their critical importance, our coastal ecosystems are in a state of rapid decline
due to the widespread conversion of mangrove forests into fishponds and the
proliferation of illegal structures that have stripped our coastlines of their natural
defenses.2 Furthermore, without natural vegetation, shorelines continuously erode
and degrade, which costs Filipinos their livelihoods, property, and integral
infrastructure that serve the public. Evidently, the lack of a coherent national strategy
for coastal management and protection enabled the above-mentioned problems to
persist.

Coastal Greenbelt Program aspect of Integrated Coastal Management (ICM) as a

11   SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act, the following terms shall mean:
12
13      (a) Abandoned, Undeveloped or Underutilized (AUU) Fishponds refer to
14          fishponds that have been leased to qualified persons and fisherfolk
15          organizations/cooperatives that are not fully producing within five (5) years
16          from the date of approval of the lease contract that shall be automatically
17          reverted to the public domain for reforestation, in accordance with RA No. 8550,
18          as amended by RA No. 10654, otherwise known as the Philippine Fisheries
19          Code;
20
21      (b) Climate Change refers to a change of climate which is attributed directly or
22          indirectly to human activity that alters the composition of global atmosphere,
23          and which is in addition to natural climate variability observed over comparable
24          time periods;
25
26      (c) Coastal Greenbelts refer to at least 100-meter-wide strip of natural or
27          planted coastal vegetation, primarily of mangrove and beach forest species,
28          extending from the seaward edge of mangroves (middle intertidal zone)
29          towards land, or extending from the seaward edge of beach forest (high tide
30          line) towards land, in cases where mangroves are absent. Its function is to
31          absorb wave energy from the sea towards land, prevent coastal erosion, and
32          mitigate the adverse impacts of natural coastal hazards on human lives and
33          property;
34
35      (d) Coastal Zone is a band of dry land and adjacent ocean space (water and
36          submerged land in which terrestrial processes and uses directly affect oceanic
37          processes and uses, and vice versa; its geographic extent may include areas
38          within a landmark limit of one kilometer from the shoreline at high tide to
39          include mangrove swamps, brackish water ponds, nipa swamps, estuarine
40          rivers, sandy beaches and other areas within a seaward limit of 200 meters
41          isobath to include coral reefs, algal flats, seagrass beds and other soft-bottom
42          areas;
 1
 2   (e) Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP) refers to the document formulated
 3       by the cities and municipalities, in consultation with its stakeholders, that
 4       defines or provides guidelines on the allocation, utilization, development, and
 5       management of all lands within a given territory or jurisdiction according to the
 6       inherent qualities of the land itself and supportive economic, demographic,
 7       socio-cultural and environmental objectives as defined in RA No. 11201 or the
 8       Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development Act;
 9
10   (f) Comprehensive Development Plan (CDP) refers to a six-year multi-
11       sectoral plan formulated at the city or municipal level embodying the vision,
12       sectoral goals, objectives, development strategies, and policies within the terms
13       of the officials of the LGU, and the medium-term. It contains an ecological
14       profile, sectoral development plan, and implementation instruments;
15
16   (g) Designated area refers to site-specific stretches of vegetation in the coastal
17       zone, defined in relation to risk factors and vulnerability to coastal hazards, and
18       reserved for protection and maintenance under the National Coastal Greenbelt
19       Management Action Plan. The area may undergo rehabilitation, reforestation,
20       or afforestation with locally appropriate mangrove and beach forest species.
21       Designated areas with abandoned fishponds require the reversion of these
22       fishponds to mangroves through natural regeneration or replanting with locally
23       appropriate species. Designated areas with illegal structures, such as
24       breakwaters, permanent residential/commercial structures, and the like,
25       require removal of such structures;
26
27   (h) Fisherfolk Settlement Areas refer to certain areas, particularly close to or
28       near fishing grounds, that have been granted to or reserved for the settlement
29       of municipal fisherfolk;
30
31   (i) Integrated Coastal Management refers to a holistic and integrative
32       management that incorporates the following components: (1) connectivity of
33       various ecosystems – environment and resources; (2) various sectors and
34       stakeholders; (3) institutional arrangements that are relevant; and (4)
35       assessment of drivers of coastal ecosystem degradation, management planning
36       and implementation, including research and education, to address coastal
37       degradation and biodiversity losses, and to improve the well-being of
38       communities in coastal and interconnected areas;
39
40   (j) Local Climate Change Action Plan (LCCAP) refers to the action plan
41       formulated by LGUs to address climate change concerns. It focuses on both
42       climate change and adaptation and mitigation and describes how LGUs plan to
 1         respond to impacts of climate change and mainstream them into mandated
 2         local plans, i.e., land use plan, sectoral development plan, investment program,
 3         pursuant to Section 14 of R.A. No. 9729, or the Climate Change Act of 2009,
 4         as amended;
 5
 6      (k) Local Coastal Greenbelt Management Action Plan (LCGMAP) refers to
 7          the local action plan adopted by local government units which shall be based
 8          on the NCGMAP. It shall contain specific goals and targets for the
 9          implementation of the program set in the NCGMAP.
10
11      (l) Mangroves refer to a community of intertidal plants in the tropics and
12          subtropics including all species of trees, shrubs, vines and herbs growing along
13          tidal mudflats and shallow water coastal areas extending inland along rivers,
14          streams and their tributaries where the water is generally brackish;
15
16     (m) Mangrove Forest refers to the forested wetland growing along tidal mudflats
17         and along shallow water coastal areas extending inland along rivers, streams,
18         and their tributaries where the water is generally brackish and composed of low
19         elevation species, such as Avicennia marina, A. alba, and Sonneratia alba. The
20         mid zone is dominated by Rhizophora species, Sonneratia caseolaris,
21         Xylocarpus granatum and Nypa fruticans while the landward zone or areas with
22         higher elevation are inhabited by Avicennia rumphiana, A. officinalis, and
23         Bruguiera gymnorrhiza;

25   SEC. 4. National Coastal Greenbelt Management Action Plan. - The DENR,
26   through the Biodiversity Management Bureau (BMB), shall, within six (6) months,
27   identify and convene the National Coordinating Council, consisting of all national
28   agencies responsible for foreshore management, mangrove and beach forest
29   protection and utilization, coastal land and sea-use planning, coastal tourism
30   development, social welfare of coastal communities, and other relevant mandates, to
31   prepare an integrated National Coastal Greenbelt Management Action Plan (NCGMAP),
32   which shall serve as the guide for the LCGMAP. The DENR shall be responsible for
33   consolidating the NCGMAP not later than twelve (12) months from the effectivity of
34   this Act.
35
36   The NCGMAP shall, at the minimum, contain the following:
37
38      a. Spatial representation, and a periodic quick/rapid inventory of the status of
39         coastlines and foreshores, including the status of mangroves, beach forests,
40         settlements, structures and fishponds within one hundred (100) meters therein,
41         ensuring that temporal and spatial national capital accounting data for coastal
42         greenbelts and the corollary ecosystem services supplied is readily available;
 1
 2   b. Assessment of priority areas to be designated as Coastal Greenbelt Zones, for
 3      each coastal province, city and municipality, based on their vulnerability to
 4      storm surges, waves, tsunamis and the like. This shall be completed within
 5      twelve (12) months from the adoption of the NCGMAP;
 6
 7   c. Designation of priority areas as Coastal Greenbelt Zones that are already
 8      included as either a protected area under the Expanded National Integrated
 9      Protected Areas System (RA No. 11038), or as a fish refuge or sanctuary under
10      the Amended Fisheries Code (RA No. 8550 as amended by RA No. 10654), or
11      as a local marine protected area as may be declared by municipalities and cities
12      through local ordinances. The designation shall be completed within six (6)
13      months from the completion of the assessment. Designation shall also be done
14      through a proposal to the appropriate agency, municipality or city as may be
15      deemed necessary. In the event an area is designated as a priority area as
16      Coastal Greenbelt Zone, no structure shall be allowed in said area. Designation
17      as a priority area, however, shall not affect structures found in fisherfolk
18      settlement areas reserved for the settlement of municipal fisherfolk and shall
19      not impair existing foreshore leases and other similar agreement: Provided, that
20      existing grantees shall be tasked to implement the plans and conditions laid
21      down under the NCGMAP and LCGMAP, as the case may be;
22
23   d. Operational Plan I for the rehabilitation, reforestation or afforestation of
24      designated priority coastal greenbelts with ecologically appropriate mangrove
25      and beach forest species, not less than one hundred (100) meters in width.
26      Existing forests or plantations greater than the minimum width shall not be
27      reduced. The program for rehabilitation, reforestation or afforestation shall be
28      community-based, long-term, and implemented through the municipality or city
29      government. The Operational Plan shall indicate that the program for
30      rehabilitation, reforestation and afforestation must cover a minimum target
31      area of twenty percent (20%) of the designated priority areas in the first five
32      (5) years for maximum protection of the most vulnerable communities in the
33      city or municipality. The remaining priority areas must be completed within ten
34      (10) years from the designation of the priority areas;
35
36   e. Operational Plan II for the reversion of all abandoned, undeveloped or
37      underutilized (AUU) fishponds to mangroves through natural regeneration or
38      replanting with locally appropriate species. All fishponds that are declared by
39      the DENR and DA-BFAR through a joint assessment as AUU fishponds shall be
40      turned over to the DENR within one (1) year after the assessment. The DENR
41      will then revert the AUU fishponds to their original mangrove state through
42      natural or artificial reforestation in accordance with Section 49 of RA No. 8550,
 1          as amended by RA No. 10654, otherwise known as the Amended Fisheries
 2          Code. All AUU fishponds turned over to the DENR shall be declared as part of
 3          a Coastal Greenbelt Zone. The minimum target for the first year of
 4          implementation of the NCGMAP will include all AUU fishpond areas turned over
 5          to the DENR for reversion to their original mangrove state. The NCGMAP shall
 6          indicate that the identification and recovery of possession from delinquent
 7          fishpond lease agreement holders shall be completed within one (1) year from
 8          the adoption thereof;
 9
10      f. Operational Plan III for the removal of illegal structures (such as breakwaters,
11         permanent residential/commercial structures, and the like), in the identified
12         priority Coastal Greenbelt Zones, subject to existing laws on fisherfolk
13         settlement areas. The NCGMAP shall indicate that the declaration of and notice
14         to persons responsible for removal of illegal structures shall be completed
15         within twelve (12) months from the issuance of Notice to Vacate. The removal
16         of illegal structures in and start of rehabilitation, reforestation or afforestation
17         of these areas shall form part of the minimum target for the first year of
18         implementation of the NCGMAP; and
19
20      g. Monitoring and evaluation plan, with quantitative and qualitative targets
21         consistent with (c), (d), (e) and (f) above, appropriate indicators and
22         reasonable means of verification. The plan shall become an integrated part of
23         the NCGMAP, NCGMAP-aligned LCGMAP and the Annual Report of the DENR to
24         Congress.

34   SEC. 5. Designated Areas for Coastal Greenbelt Zones. - Areas designated for
35   Coastal Greenbelt Zones shall cover natural, rural, and urban areas with mangrove or
36   mangrove areas, AUU fishponds turned over to the DENR, site specific stretches of
37   vegetation in areas requiring the reversion to mangroves through natural regeneration
38   or replanting with locally appropriate species and beach forests, subject to prior vested
39   rights. All applicable biophysical, socioeconomic, and geological assessments shall be
40   conducted prior to the designation of coastal greenbelts. Upon determination by the
41   DENR of a particular area as a designated area for coastal greenbelt under Sec. 4(c)
42   of this Act, CLUPs and zoning ordinances shall be updated accordingly. AUU fishponds
 1   turned over to the DENR shall be prioritized in the designation of coastal greenbelts
 2   and its corresponding reversion to category of public lands as mangrove reforestation
 3   sites shall be completed within the year of identification as such following the
 4   guidelines endorsed by the National Technical Working Group pursuant to Section 10
 5   of this Act.

 7   SEC. 6. Integrated Coastal Management. - The Integrated Coastal Management
 8   (ICM) shall be the national framework that seeks to promote the conservation,
 9   preservation and protection of as well as address the human impacts on coastal
10   greenbelts comprising of mangrove and beach forest ecosystems. The National ICM
11   Framework shall be developed and implemented by the DENR to provide direction,
12   support, and guidance to relevant LGUs, support agencies, and other stakeholders.

45          and actions as mandated and aligned with the NCGMAP, including the

 8          technological, and environmental conditions;

10          on their NCGMAP-responsive LCGMAP;
11      (e) Mobilize and allocate the necessary personnel, resources, and logistics to

21      (i) Ensure consistency and/or enter into inter-LGU collaboration and cooperation

27      (k) Lead in the actual implementation of programs aimed at rehabilitation,
28          reforestation and afforestation of Coastal Greenbelt Zones, including engaging
29          in capacity building efforts for managing mangrove nurseries, proper planting
30          procedures, and conducting maintenance activities, among others;
31      (l) Develop and maintain a proper database containing information on all local
32          Coastal Greenbelt Zones within their territorial jurisdiction; and
33     (m) Implement corrective actions against illegal encroachments and violations

35          Section 51, Water Code of the Philippines.
