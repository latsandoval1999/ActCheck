                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Science is one of the quiet architects of national progress. It powers industries,
drives innovation, and provides the intellectual tools needed to solve complex problems.
Nations that invest in scientific education cultivate not only knowledge but also the
human capital necessary for sustained economic growth and technological advancement.

       Recognizing this imperative, the 1987 Constitution directs the State to give priority
to science and technology as essential instruments for national development. This
mandate is reinforced by the Enhanced Basic Education Act of 2013, which integrates
Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM) education into the country’s
basic education curriculum.

       Despite these efforts, access to specialized science education remains uneven
across the country. In many provinces, academically gifted students must rely on general
secondary schools that lack the advanced laboratories, research opportunities, and
specialized faculty necessary for rigorous STEM training. This disparity limits the
development of promising young minds and contributes to regional inequalities in
educational opportunity.

       These national disparities are reflected in local communities such as the
Municipality of Guiguinto in the Bulacan. While the municipality supports science-
oriented education and hosts programs that nurture early scientific aptitude among our
learners, it does not have a legislated science high school capable of sustaining this
academic trajectory at the secondary level.

      A feasibility study conducted by education stakeholders in the Schools Division of
Bulacan underscores the need for establishing such an educational institution. The study
found strong support among students, parents, educators, and local officials and
concluded that “the establishment of Guiguinto Science High School is expected to
promote better educational outcomes, skill development, and local economic
advancement.”1

       The proposed Guiguinto Science High School seeks to address this gap by
providing a specialized secondary institution dedicated to science, technology,
engineering, and mathematics. With advanced laboratories, research-oriented curricula,
and highly qualified faculty, the school will cultivate scientific inquiry, critical thinking,
and innovation among students.

        Beyond academic development, the school is expected to strengthen local human
capital, promote innovation, and expand access to quality STEM education for talented
students across socioeconomic backgrounds.

        In a global economy increasingly shaped by science and technology, strengthening
institutions that nurture scientific talent is essential. Establishing the Guiguinto National
Science High School is a meaningful step toward that goal.

          In view of the foregoing, the immediate enactment of this bill is earnestly sought.

     assembled:

 1          Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Guiguinto National
 2   Science High School Act.”
 3
 4          Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to promote quality
 5   science and technology education and develop a pool of scientifically literate and
 6   technologically competent citizens. Toward this end, the State shall strengthen specialized
 7   science secondary schools that nurture students with aptitude in Science, Technology,
 8   Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM).
 9
10           Section 3. Establishment of the Guiguinto Science High School. – There is hereby
11   established, under the supervision of the Department of Education (DepEd), a national
12   science high school in the Municipality of Guiguinto, Province of Bulacan, to be known as
13   the Guiguinto National Science High School (GNSHS).

15          Section 4. Funding. – The Secretary of Education shall immediately include in the
16   Department’s program the operationalization of the Guiguinto National Science High
17   School, the funding of which shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
