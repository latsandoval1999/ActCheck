                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

         It is the declared policy of the State to promote a just and dynamic social order that
ensures prosperity, social justice, and respect for human dignity. Under the Constitution, the
State must guarantee equitable distribution of opportunities, income, and wealth, provide for
full employment, and protect the underprivileged in all phases of national development (cf.
Art. II, Secs. 10-11; Art. XII, Sec. 1).

          In 2023, national poverty incidence stood at 15.5%, approximately 17.5 million
Filipinos, which, though lower than in 2021, remains well above acceptable levels. 1 The
Government, through NEDA, aims to reduce poverty incidence to 10-11% by 2027, and 8-9%
by 2028, as specified in the Philippine Development Plan 2023-2028. 2 Concurrently, MSME
credit exposure has grown by 10.8% by mid-2025, indicating expanding banking support for
small enterprises, yet many still lack access to sufficient capital and market opportunities. 3 The
Government has set aside ₱1.5 billion for microfinancing support under the Pondo sa
Pagbabago at Pag-Asenso (P3) program in FY 2026, a three-fold increase from FY 2025
levels. 4

        This bill seeks to institutionalize a national framework that strengthens social
enterprises and MSMEs as key partners in poverty alleviation, ensuring they are provided with
incentives, easy access to capital, transparent market linkages, and credible social security
measures. By doing so, the State reaffirms its constitutional obligations and positions itself to
accelerate inclusive growth, raise living standards, and ensure that economic gains benefit all
Filipinos, particularly the marginalized and rural poor.

1            SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Poverty Reduction through
2    Social Entrepreneurship Act.”

3            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote a
4    just and dynamic social order that will ensure the prosperity and independence of the nation and
5    free the people from poverty through policies that provide adequate social services, promote full
6    employment, a rising standard of living, and an improved quality of life for all.

7             In Article II, Sections 10 and 11, it is further declared that the State shall provide social
8    justice in all phases of national development and that the State values the dignity of every human
9    person and guarantees full respect for human rights.

10          In Article XII, Section 1, it is further stated that the goals of the national economy are a
11   more equitable distribution of opportunities, income, and wealth; a sustained increase in the amount
12   of goods and services produced by the nation for the benefit of the people; and an expanding
13   productivity as the key to raising the quality of life for all, especially the underprivileged.

14           The State shall promote industrialization and full employment based on sound agricultural
15   development and agrarian reform, through industries that make full and efficient use of human and
16   natural resources, and which are competitive in both domestic and foreign markets. However, the
17   State shall protect Filipino enterprises against unfair foreign competition and trade practices.

18          The State also recognizes the important role of enterprises in the economy as the major
19   agent of development, creating off-farm employment opportunities and providing transitional
20   means for improving people's livelihoods.

21          Toward this end, the State shall pursue a poverty reduction program that promotes an
22   environment conducive to the development and growth of a vibrant social enterprise sector engaged
23   in poverty reduction, and economic and social development. It shall empower the marginalized

26           SEC.4.Social Enterprise (SE); General Concept and Coverage. – A Social Enterprise
27   (SE), for purposes of this Act, refers to a Social Enterprise with the Marginalized as Primary
28   Stakeholders (SEMPS) as defined in Sec. 3 (a) of this Act.

29          An SE employs any of the following development strategies in the pursuit of its social
30   mission: 1) empowerment strategy; 2) social inclusion strategy; 3) intermediation strategy; and 4)
31   resource mobilization strategy.

32            In the case of social enterprises organized as stock corporations, partnerships, or sole
33   proprietorships, these social enterprises should invest at least 60% of their net revenues to sustain
34   the fulfillment of their social mission and provide transformational services for the wellbeing of
35   the marginalized sector they serve. They must not be a branch, subsidiary or division of a private
36   business enterprise, regardless of the size of such private business enterprise, nor may its policies
37   be determined by a private business enterprise. Provided, that this shall not preclude an SE from
38   accepting subcontracts from large private business enterprises or firms or from joining in
39   cooperative or joint-venture activities with other SEs or foundations.

40           SEC.5. Qualifications for Benefits and Incentives. - To qualify for the benefits and
41   incentives provided for in this Act, the SE shall:

42           a. Provide or facilitate the provision of a combination of transactional and/or

42   sectors as primary stakeholders, a comprehensive and fully-integrated Poverty Reduction Through

44   principles:

46             a major sustainable and comprehensive strategy;

49             improve the effectiveness of each program;

 6          e. Enabling SEs to overcome constraints and to take advantage of opportunities for

34   credit window for qualified SEs.

38   collateral based on their balance sheet as of the end of the previous quarter and make the same
39   available for SEs through a special credit window.

40           The BSP, in consultation with the Council, shall formulate the rules for the effective
41   implementation of this provision; Provided, That the purchase of government notes, securities and
42   other negotiable instruments shall not be deemed compliance with the foregoing provision.

32            SEC. 26. Grant facility. — The SE Development Fund in Section 18 shall provide grants
33   to qualified social enterprises and resource institutions for the performance and conduct of their
34   mission toward poverty reduction, sustainable development, promotion of gender and social
35   justice, environmental conservation, climate action, and improvement in economic welfare of the

37           There will be three windows under this fund facility, namely: capacity-building grant; start

40           SEC. 27. Tax Credits. — SEDC will develop a system of valuation of social, economic
41   and ecological contributions of social enterprises and the social impact on the marginalized sectors
42   they serve, as bases for tax credits from the government.

31          SEC. 29. Insurance for Social Enterprises. — The Insurance Commission shall issue the
32   necessary rules and regulations and implement measures to ensure that the insurance industry shall
33   provide insurance products, both life and non-life, for social enterprises and their stakeholders
34   among the poor. Furthermore, SEMPS shall be assisted to become eligible and effective insurance
35   providers to their clients and constituents.

36           SEC. 30. Support for Social Investors and SE Resource Institutions. — The government
37   shall develop a program that fosters a community of social investors and resource institutions
38   supportive of social enterprises.

39           SEC. 31. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to implement the provisions of this
40   Act shall be charged against the current year's appropriation of the Office of the President.
41   Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in
42   the GAA. The budget requirements for the implementation of this Act for cooperating agencies
43   shall be incorporated in their respective budgets.

21   of this Act. It shall likewise determine inherent weaknesses in the law and recommend necessary
22   remedial legislation or executive measures.

23           The PRESENT Oversight Committee shall be composed of fourteen (14) members with
24   the chairpersons of the Committee on Trade and Industry of the House of Representatives, and the
25   Committee on Trade, Commerce and Entrepreneurship of the Senate as Co-Chairpersons; and six
26   (6) members from each House, to be designated by the Speaker of the House of Representatives,
