                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Section 4, Article XII of the 1987 Constitution directs that the
Congress shall, as soon as possible, determine by law the specific limits of
forest lands and national parks, marking clearly their boundaries on the
ground, and that thereafter such forest lands may not be diminished except
by law. That mandate is not merely an allocation of power; it is the only door
through which the situation this measure addresses can be resolved.
       In the Municipality of Kasibu, Province of Nueva Vizcaya, communities
have for generations built their homes, planted their rice, banana and
citrus, and raised their children on land that the State still carries on its
maps as forest. The Department's own field assessment describes the
covered areas as occupied by the community for residential, agricultural
and agro-forest purposes, containing scattered residential houses occupied
by farmers, with actual use recorded as agricultural, agroforest, rice field,
banana and citrus plantations. They cannot obtain a title. They cannot
pledge the land for a loan, pass it to their children with certainty, or be
secure against displacement. The local government cannot assess the land
for real property tax, so the municipality earns nothing from land it must
nonetheless serve with roads, water and schools. For the Province as a
whole, the Forest Management Bureau records that the conversion proposed
here would reduce forest land by about six hundredths of one percent,
leaving the provincial forest estate substantially intact.
      This bill reclassifies two (2) parcels of forestland in the Province of
Nueva Vizcaya, described by metes and bounds as certified by the
Department of Environment and Natural Resources, into alienable and
disposable public land, and opens them to disposition under the Public
Land Act. It deliberately does three further things. It carries forward, as
statutory exclusions, the protections of Section 16 of the Revised Forestry
Code — the twenty-metre riverbank strips, the ridge tops and plateaus from
which headwaters emanate, and the other areas needed for forest purposes
— so that reclassification does not become deforestation. It excludes from
coverage any land within an ancestral domain, ancestral land, Certificate of

                                   Page 1 of 9
Ancestral Domain Claim or Certificate of Ancestral Domain Title, and
requires certification by the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples
before any segregation survey may proceed, in recognition that land held by
indigenous cultural communities under native title has never been public
land at all. And it requires that the boundaries between the reclassified land
and the forestland that remains be marked on the ground with concrete
monuments and maintained there, which is what Section 4, Article XII
actually commands and what decades of paper-only classification have
failed to deliver.
      Considering the potential economic, social and developmental benefits
of the proposed measure, its immediate passage is respectfully and strongly
recommended.

  AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE RECLASSIFICATION AND CONVERSION
 OF CERTAIN PARCELS OF FORESTLAND IN THE PROVINCE OF NUEVA
      VIZCAYA INTO ALIENABLE AND DISPOSABLE PUBLIC LANDS
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:
       SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy
of the State to conserve and protect the nation's forest lands as part of its
patrimony, and, consistent with that duty, to determine by law the specific
limits of such forest lands and to release from them only those areas which,
by reason of long and actual human settlement, cultivation and use, no
longer serve forest purposes. It is further the policy of the State to promote
social justice and security of tenure for actual occupants and tillers of lands
of the public domain, to respect the rights of indigenous cultural
communities and indigenous peoples to their ancestral domains and
ancestral lands, and to ensure that the reclassification of forestland does
not impair watersheds, riverbanks, headwaters, or the ecological balance of
the areas concerned.
      SECTION 2. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
        (a) Alienable and disposable land refers to land of the public
            domain classified as agricultural land and declared available for
            alienation and disposition under Commonwealth Act No. 141,
            as amended, otherwise known as the Public Land Act;
        (b) Forestland refers to land of the public domain which has not
            been declared alienable and disposable, and which is subject to
            the system of land classification administered under
            Presidential Decree No. 705, as amended, otherwise known as
            the Revised Forestry Code of the Philippines;
        (c) Ancestral domain and ancestral land have the meanings
            assigned to them in Republic Act No. 8371, otherwise known as
            the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997;
        (d) Segregation survey refers to the ground survey conducted by the
            Department of Environment and Natural Resources to delimit,

             on the ground, the parcels reclassified under this Act and to
             separate them from the forestland that remains; and
         (e) Land classification map refers to the official map issued by the
             Department of Environment and Natural Resources delineating
             the classification of lands of the public domain.
      SECTION 3. Scope and Coverage. – Subject to Sections 4, 5 and 6 of
this Act, the two (2) parcels of land of the public domain situated in the
Province of Nueva Vizcaya, presently classified as forestland and more
particularly described hereunder, are hereby reclassified and converted into
alienable and disposable public land, and are declared agricultural land
open to disposition under the Public Land Act.
​
​     The aggregate area of the parcels reclassified under this Act shall be
that determined by the segregation survey conducted under Section 10
hereof, and shall be stated in the final land classification maps issued
thereunder.

​      SECTION 4. Areas Excluded from Coverage. – The following are
excluded from the operation of Section 3 of this Act, shall not be reclassified,
and shall be retained and maintained as permanent forestland
notwithstanding their location within the parcels described therein:
         (a) Twenty-metre (20 m) strips of land along the edge of the normal
             high waterline of rivers and streams with channels of at least
             five (5) metres wide;
         (b) Ridge tops and plateaus, regardless of size, found within or
             surrounded wholly or partly by forest lands where headwaters
             emanate;
         (c) Areas which have already been reforested;
         (d) Land fifty percent (50%) in slope or over;
         (e) Appropriately located road rights-of-way;
         (f) Areas needed for other public purposes, including national
             parks, national historical sites, game refuges and wildlife
             sanctuaries, forest station sites, and areas previously
             proclaimed by the President as forest reserves, national parks,
             game refuges, bird sanctuaries, national shrines or national
             historic sites;
         (g) Any land within an ancestral domain or ancestral land, or
             within an area covered by a Certificate of Ancestral Domain
             Claim or a Certificate of Ancestral Domain Title issued under
             Republic Act No. 8371; and
         (h) Any land within a protected area established under Republic
             Act No. 7586, as amended by Republic Act No. 11038, otherwise
             known as the Expanded National Integrated Protected Areas
             System Act of 2018, or within any watershed forest reserve
             established by law or by proclamation.
       The Department of Environment and Natural Resources shall delimit
the areas excluded under this Section in the course of the segregation
survey, and shall reflect them in the final land classification maps: Provided,
That the penal and regulatory provisions of Presidential Decree No. 705, as
amended, and of Republic Act No. 9175, otherwise known as the Chain Saw
Act of 2002, shall continue to apply to the areas so excluded.​
​
​      SECTION 5. Non-Application of Section 15 of Presidential Decree
No. 705. – Section 15 of Presidential Decree No. 705, as amended, insofar
as it prohibits the classification as alienable and disposable of land of the
public domain eighteen percent (18%) in slope or over, shall not apply to the
parcels described in Section 3 of this Act. This Section shall be construed
strictly and shall not be applied to any land other than the parcels described
in Section 3: Provided, That land fifty percent (50%) in slope or over remains
excluded from reclassification under Section 4(d) hereof: Provided, further,
That the Department of Environment and Natural Resources shall, in the
segregation survey, identify the portions of each parcel that are eighteen
percent (18%) in slope or over and shall prescribe, in the implementing rules
issued under Section 11 hereof, the soil and water conservation measures
required of occupants of those portions as a condition of disposition.​
​

​     SECTION 6. Conditions Precedent to Segregation. – No segregation
survey shall be commenced and no portion of the parcels described in
Section 3 shall be opened to disposition until the following have been
obtained and transmitted to the Secretary of the Department of
Environment and Natural Resources:
        (a) A certification by the National Commission on Indigenous
            Peoples that the parcel concerned does not overlap any
            ancestral domain, ancestral land, Certificate of Ancestral
            Domain Claim or Certificate of Ancestral Domain Title: Provided,
            That where an overlap is found, the overlapping portion shall be
            excluded under Section 4(g) hereof, and no disposition shall
            proceed as to that portion except upon the free and prior
            informed consent of the indigenous cultural community or
            indigenous people concerned, obtained in accordance with
            Republic Act No. 8371 and its implementing rules;
        (b) A certification by the Mines and Geosciences Bureau identifying
            any existing mineral agreement, financial or technical
            assistance agreement, exploration permit or other mining
            tenement overlapping the parcel concerned;
        (c) A certification by the Department of Environment and Natural
            Resources that no portion of the parcel concerned lies within a
            protected area established under Republic Act No. 7586, as
            amended by Republic Act No. 11038, or within a watershed
            forest reserve established by law or by proclamation; and
        (d) A resolution of the Sangguniang Bayan of the municipality
            concerned and of the Sangguniang Panlalawigan of the Province
            of Nueva Vizcaya interposing no objection to the reclassification,
            consistent with Sections 26 and 27 of Republic Act No. 7160,
            otherwise known as the Local Government Code of 1991.
      SECTION 7. Alienation and Disposition. – The land reclassified
under this Act shall be disposed of in accordance with Commonwealth Act
No. 141, as amended, particularly by Republic Act No. 11573: Provided,
That no person shall be granted an area exceeding twelve (12) hectares
under this Act: Provided, further, That for purposes of the agricultural free
patent under Section 44 of Commonwealth Act No. 141, as amended,
occupation and cultivation of the land by the applicant or through the
applicant's predecessors-in-interest prior to the effectivity of this Act shall
be credited as though the land had been alienable and disposable
throughout the period of such occupation and cultivation: Provided, finally,
That nothing in this Act shall be construed as conveying any right to
minerals, mineral oils, coal, petroleum or other mineral resources, which
remain the property of the State under Section 2, Article XII of the
Constitution.
      ​
No land acquired under this Act shall be sold, conveyed, encumbered or
otherwise alienated within ten (10) years from the issuance of the patent,
except in favour of the Government or of a government financial institution
as security for an agricultural production loan. Land acquired in violation of

this Section, or land which the patentee has ceased to occupy and cultivate
for two (2) consecutive years without lawful cause, shall revert to the State
upon proceedings instituted by the Solicitor General.
      ​
​      SECTION 8. Existing Rights and Tenurial Instruments. – Vested
rights, and rights acquired under a Certificate of Stewardship Contract, a
Community-Based Forest Management Agreement, a forest land use
agreement, a permit, a lease or any other tenurial instrument validly issued
over any portion of the parcels described in Section 3 and subsisting at the
effectivity of this Act, are hereby respected and shall continue in force
according to their terms: Provided, That the holder of such an instrument
who is a qualified applicant under Section 7 hereof may, at the holder's
option, surrender the instrument and apply for a patent over the area
covered by it, in which case the period of the holder's occupation under the
instrument shall be credited under Section 7: Provided, further, That no
such instrument shall be cancelled, and no holder shall be displaced, by
reason of the reclassification effected by this Act.
​
​     SECTION 9. Preservation of Ecological Balance and
Non-Diminution of the Remaining Forestland. – The forestland of the
Province of Nueva Vizcaya that remains after the reclassification effected by
this Act shall not be diminished except by law. The Department of
Environment and Natural Resources and the local government units
concerned shall protect and preserve the areas excluded under Section 4
hereof, and the removal of timber and other forest products therein shall
remain subject to forestry laws and regulations. No infrastructure project
shall be constructed within the areas so excluded except after an
environmental impact assessment conducted in accordance with
Presidential Decree No. 1586, and no additional human settlement shall be
permitted therein except as allowed by the Secretary of the Department of
Environment and Natural Resources.
​
​     SECTION 10. Segregation Survey, Issuance of Maps and
Establishment of Boundaries on the Ground. – Within twelve (12) months
from the effectivity of this Act, and upon compliance with Section 6 hereof,
the Department of Environment and Natural Resources shall conduct the
segregation survey of the parcels described in Section 3, shall issue the final
land classification maps covering the Province of Nueva Vizcaya, and shall
establish on the ground the boundary between the land reclassified under
this Act and the forestland that remains.
      ​
​     The boundary shall be marked with concrete monuments at every
corner and at intervals of not more than one (1) kilometre, with visible cut
lines, signboards and uniformly spaced distinctive trees between the
concrete monuments, and shall be maintained at all times. The Department
shall furnish certified copies of the final land classification maps to the
National Mapping and Resource Information Authority, the Land

Registration Authority, the Register of Deeds of Nueva Vizcaya, the Province
of Nueva Vizcaya and the municipality concerned.
      ​
​     SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within
ninety (90) days following the approval of this Act, the Secretary of the
Department of Environment and Natural Resources, in consultation with the
National Commission on Indigenous Peoples, the Department of Agrarian
Reform, the National Mapping and Resource Information Authority and the
local government units concerned, shall issue the implementing orders,
rules, regulations and circulars necessary to effectively implement the
provisions hereof.
      ​
​     SECTION 12. Report to Congress. – The Secretary of the Department
of Environment and Natural Resources shall, one (1) year after the effectivity
of this Act and annually thereafter until the disposition of the land
reclassified hereunder is completed, submit to the Speaker of the House of
Representatives, the President of the Senate and the chairpersons of the
appropriate committees of both Houses a report stating the area surveyed
and segregated, the number of monuments established and maintained, the
number of patent applications filed and approved, and the certifications
obtained under Section 6 hereof.
      ​
​    SECTION 13. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year's
appropriations of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources.
​
​     SECTION 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act or
the application of such provision to any person or circumstance is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or the application of such
provision to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected by such
declaration.
​
​     SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. – Section 15 of Presidential Decree
No. 705, as amended, is hereby rendered inapplicable to the parcels
described in Section 3 of this Act, to the extent and in the manner provided
in Section 5 hereof. All other laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations,
rules and regulations and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with
the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified
accordingly.
​
​     SECTION 16. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen
(15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of
general circulation.
