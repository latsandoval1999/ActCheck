AN ACT
MANDATING THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH (DOH) TO ENSURE THE
AVAILABILITY OF MEDICINES AND PROCUREMENT OF MEDICAL
EQUIPMENT IN ALL PUBLIC HOSPITALS THROUGH THE UTILIZATION
OF THE MEDICAL ASSISTANCE TO INDIGENT AND FINANCIALLY
INCAPACITATED PATIENTS (MAIFIP) FUND.

EXPLANATORY NOTE

The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to protect aud
promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness
among them. Despite this constitutional guarantee, many public hospitals
across the country continue to grapple with chronic shortages of essential
medicines and a lack of basic diagnostic equipment. This reality places a
heavy burden on indigent and financially incapacitated Filipinos who rely
entirely on government healthcare facilities for their survival and well-being.

This proposed measure seeks to address this critical gap by
institutionalizing a proactive and sustainable procurement mechanism. It
mandates the Department of Health (DOH) to ensure a continuous, adequate
supply of essential medicines and guarantees that all public hospitals are
equipped with baseline diagnostic tools like functional X-ray, ultrasound, and
ECG machines.

To ensure the viability of this mandate, the bill introduces a strategic
funding approach. It maximizes the existing Medical Assistance to Indigent
and Financially Incapacitated Patients (MAIFIP) Fund by explicitly earmarking
at least twenty-five percent (25%) of its annual allocation exclusively for the
procurement of essential medicines and medical equipment. By converting a
portion of direct financial assistance into tangible, readily available healthcare
resources at the point of care, the government can prevent stockouts, lower
overall healthcare costs through bulk procurement, and provide immediate
diagnostic and medical relief to marginalized patients.

Furthermore, the bill institutes safeguards for transparency, efficient
inventory management, and regular congressional reporting to ensure that
the funds are utilized effectively and reach the Filipinos who need them most.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

Introduced by REP. PHILIP CONRAD “DOK BONG” M. ACOP

AN ACT
MANDATING THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH (DOH) TO ENSURE THE
AVAILABILITY OF MEDICINES AND PROCUREMENT OF MEDICAL
EQUIPMENT IN ALL PUBLIC HOSPITALS THROUGH THE UTILIZATION
OF THE MEDICAL ASSISTANCE TO INDIGENT AND FINANCIALLY
INCAPACITATED PATIENTS (MAIFIP) FUND

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Universal
Medical Access and Equipment Act”,

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to promote and
protect the right to health of every Filipino by ensuring universal access to
essential medicines and quality healthcare services. In line with this, the State
shall mandate the Department of Health (DOH) to provide adequate medicines
and procure essential medical equipment for all government hospitals using
the Medical Assistance to Indigent and Financially Incapacitated Patients
(MAIFIP) Fund.

SEC 3. Definitions. — For the purposes of this Act, the following definitions
shall apply:

a) Essential Medicines - Refers to the list of medicines identified by the
DOH as essential for the treatment of prevalent diseases and health
conditions in the Philippines, as well as medicines included in the
Philippine National Formulary.

b) Essential Medical Equipment — Refers to X-ray machines, ultrasound
machines, ECG machines, and other diagnostic equipment deemed
necessary by the DOH for basic diagnostic services in public hospitals.

c) Public Hospitals — Refers to all hospitals owned and operated by the
national government, including, but not limited to, national hospitals,

===== [page 4] =====
regional hospitals, provincial hospitals, city hospitals, and municipal
hospitals.

d) MAIFIP Fund — Refers to the Medical Assistance to Indigent and
Financially Incapacitated Patients Fund.

SEC 4. Availability of Essential Medicines. — The Department of Health
(DOH) shall guarantee the continuous and adequate supply of essential
medicines in all public hospitals nationwide. To achieve this, the following
mechanisms and standards shall be strictly implemented:

a) The DOH shall establish an expedited and transparent procurement
process for essential medicines to ensure competitive pricing and timely
delivery. Bulk purchasing mechanisms and strategic partnerships with
pharmaceutical entities shall be utilized to maximize cost-effectiveness.
Subject to existing procurement laws, preference shall be given to
locally manufactured medicines that meet stringent quality standards.

b) All public hospitals shall implement efficient inventory management
systems to prevent stockouts, expiration, and wastage of medical
supplies. To support this, the DOH shall provide continuous technical
assistance and capacity-building training to hospital personnel on
inventory management best practices.

c) Public hospitals shall ensure the proper storage of all procured
medicines, strictly adhering to prescribed temperature, humidity, and
sanitation requirements to preserve their efficacy and safety.

d) The DOH shall promulgate standardized guidelines for the efficient and
equitable dispensing of medicines in public hospitals. Said guidelines
shall ensure that indigent and financially incapacitated patient-
beneficiaries are given utmost priority.

SEC 5. Provision of Essential Medical Equipment. — The Department of
Health (DOH) shall ensure that all public hospitals are adequately equipped
with essential medical equipment. To this end, every public hospital shall, at
a minimum, be equipped with the following functional diagnostic machines:

a) One (1) X-ray machine capable of basic radiographic examinations;
b) One (1) ultrasound machine; and
c) One (1) electrocardiogram (ECG) machine.
In addition to the foregoing, the DOH shall conduct a comprehensive
needs assessment to determine the necessity for other vital medical

equipment across public hospitals. The procurement of such additional
equipment shall be prioritized based on the findings of said assessment, with

===== [page 5] =====
paramount consideration given to the healthcare requirements of indigent
and financially incapacitated patients.

SEC 6. Funding. - The funds necessary for the procurement of essential
medicines and medical equipment under this Act shall be sourced primarily
from the Medical Assistance to Indigent and Financially Incapacitated
Patients (MAIFIP) Fund.

To ensure compliance with this mandate, at least twenty-five percent
(25%) of the annual MAIFIP Fund shall be strictly earmarked and exclusively
utilized for the procurement of essential medicines and medical equipment.
This allocation shall be prioritized and safeguarded against realignment or
other uses.

In addition to the MAIFIP Fund allocation, such sums as may be
necessary to supplement the funding requirements and ensure the long-term
sustainability of the program shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA).

The DOH is hereby authorized to accept grants, donations,
endowments, and other forms of financial assistance from domestic or foreign
sources to further supplement the funds allocated herein; Provided, That the
receipt and utilization of such funds shall strictly align with the objectives of
the MAIFIP and shall be subject to existing government accounting and
auditing rules and regulations.

SEC 7. MAIFIP Fund Management and Transparency. — The Department
of Health (DOH) shall promulgate clear and transparent guidelines governing
the utilization of the MAIFIP Fund for the purposes of this Act, thereby
ensuring strict accountability and preventing the misuse or misappropriation
thereof.

To this end, the DOH shall establish and implement a comprehensive
monitoring and evaluation system to track the disbursement and utilization
of MAIFIP funds designated for the procurement of medicines and medical
equipment. Said system shall likewise assess the impact of this Act on the
accessibility of healthcare services for indigent and financially incapacitated
patients.

Furthermore, the DOH shall publish periodic reports regarding the
utilization of the MAIFIP Fund. Such reports shall include, but not be limited
to, detailed accounts of the procured medicines and equipment, the total
number of patient-beneficiaries served, and an assessment of the overall
impact on public health outcomes.

SEC 8. Implementation and Monitoring. — The Department of Health (DOH)
shall serve as the lead implementing agency of this Act. Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH shall promulgate the necessary
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) to properly carry out its provisions.

===== [page 6] =====
Furthermore, the DOH shall conduct regular monitoring and evaluation
of the implementation of this Act to ensure its efficacy and faithful compliance
with its mandates.

The DOH shall likewise submit an annual report to Congress detailing
the progress of the implementation of this Act. Said report shall encompass,
but shall not be limited to, comprehensive data on the availability of medicines
and medical equipment in public health facilities, the utilization of
appropriated funds, the total number of indigent and financially incapacitated
patient-beneficiaries served, and any operational challenges encountered
during its execution.

SEC 9. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SEC 10. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, and rules
and regulations, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act, are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
