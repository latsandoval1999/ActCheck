                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE


         The Philippines is among the most disaster-prone countries in the world due to its
geographic location along the Pacific typhoon belt and the Pacific Ring of Fire. On average, the
country experiences about twenty (20) tropical cyclones every year, many of which cause severe
flooding, landslides, and widespread damage to communities and infrastructure. These disasters
result in significant social and economic losses that affect millions of Filipinos annually.

         Recent reports illustrate the magnitude of these impacts. In 2024 alone, natural hazard
events affected around 29 million persons or about eight (8) million families, while approximately
357,000 houses were damaged, including both partially and totally destroyed homes. Infrastructure
damage accounted for the largest share of losses, amounting to ₱316 million or about 68.3 percent
of total reported damage in medium-scale incidents.

        Strong typhoons and extreme weather events continue to inflict substantial losses on public
infrastructure. For example, recent tropical cyclones caused over ₱884 million in damage to roads,
bridges, and flood control systems, disrupting transportation networks and essential public services.
These figures highlight the vulnerability of critical government assets such as schools, hospitals,
roads, bridges, and flood control facilities, which are indispensable for public safety, economic
activity, and the delivery of government services.

         To address the country’s vulnerability to disasters, Congress enacted Republic Act No.
10121, otherwise known as the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010.
The law established a comprehensive disaster risk reduction and management framework that
emphasizes prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery. It also institutionalized
national and local disaster risk reduction mechanisms and required local government units to
allocate not less than five percent (5%) of their estimated revenues for the Local Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Fund to support disaster preparedness, mitigation, and recovery
initiatives.

        However, despite these institutional reforms, disasters continue to impose significant fiscal
pressures on the government. The cost of repairing and rebuilding damaged public infrastructure
often requires substantial government expenditure, which can strain national and local budgets and
delay the restoration of essential services. This underscores the need to complement disaster risk
reduction efforts with sustainable financial protection mechanisms.

        Under the Insurance Code of the Philippines, insurance is defined as a contract whereby an
insurer undertakes, for a consideration, to indemnify another against loss, damage, or liability
arising from an uncertain event. Provisions of the Code, including Article 154, establish the rules
governing the settlement of losses and the liability of insurers in cases where insured property
suffers damage. These provisions demonstrate the important role of insurance in providing
financial protection and facilitating recovery from unforeseen events, including disasters.

        In this context, disaster risk financing and insurance mechanisms are increasingly
recognized as essential tools in strengthening national resilience. These mechanisms enable
governments to manage fiscal risks, mobilize funds quickly after disasters, and ensure the prompt
rehabilitation and reconstruction of critical public infrastructure.

         This proposed measure seeks to strengthen Philippine disaster risk financing by
institutionalizing a Philippine Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Strategy for Public
Infrastructure Assets. The measure aims to establish a systematic and sustainable framework for
protecting government infrastructure against disaster-related losses through appropriate financial
instruments, including insurance and other risk-transfer mechanisms.

       By strengthening disaster risk financing mechanisms, the State can reduce the financial
burden of disasters, accelerate the rehabilitation of damaged infrastructure, and ensure the
continuous delivery of essential public services to affected communities.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

          AN ACT STRENGTHENING PHILIPPINE DISASTER RISK FINANCING,
         INSTITUTIONALIZING A PHILIPPINE DISASTER RISK FINANCING AND
         INSURANCE STRATEGY FOR PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE ASSETS, AND
                       APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

1           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Public Infrastructure
2    Financing and Insurance Act.”

3            SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall ensure a diverse and multi-layered
4    financing strategy to safeguard its public infrastructure assets from debilitating damage arising
5    from natural or man-made disasters and ensure the continuous and effective delivery of goods and
6    services.

7            To this end, the State shall coordinate all existing disaster responses in relation to the repair,
8    reconstruction, rehabilitation, and mitigation of public infrastructure assets, and shall develop and
9    implement a similar disaster risk financing and insurance (DRFI strategy with the private sector.

10           The DRFI strategy shall follow up-to-date internationally-accepted disaster risk financing
11   principles and standards, shall be information and data driven, and shall be anchored on a robust
12   public asset management system. The DRFI strategy shall also integrate and utilize up-to-date
13   technology, such as but not limited to artificial intelligence, to advance and optimize current
14   practices in its implementation.

15           SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
16   used as follows:

17               a) “Asset management” - defined under ISO 55000:2024 as a set of interrelated and
18                  interacting elements of an organization, whose function is to establish the asset
19                  management policy and asset management objectives, and the processes related to
20                  achieve those objectives. Elements of the Asset Management System include the
21                  policies, plans, business processes and information systems, which are integrated


                                                                                                             1
 1                  to give assurance that the asset management activities will be delivered in line with
 2                  organizational objectives.
 3               b) “Disaster” – a serious disruption of the functioning of a community or a society
 4                  involving widespread human, material, economic or environmental losses and
 5                  impacts, which exceeds the ability of the affected community or society to cope
 6                  using its own resources. Disasters are often described as a result of the combination
 7                  of: the exposure to a hazard; the conditions of vulnerability that are present; and
 8                  insufficient capacity or measures to reduce or cope with the potential negative
 9                  consequences. Disaster impacts may include loss of life, injury, disease and other
10                  negative effects on human, physical, mental and social well-being, together with
11                  damage to property, destruction of assets, loss of services, Social and economic
12                  disruption and environmental degradation.
13               c) “Disaster Risk” – the potential disaster losses in lives, health status, livelihood,
14                  assets and services, which could occur to a particular community or a society over
15                  some specified future time period.
16               d) “Government” – shall refer to the entire government structure and, as the context
17                  may require, shall include local government units, national government agencies,
18                  and government-owned and controlled corporations, whether considered
19                  collectively or individually and separately.

20          SEC. 4. Coverage and Scope. – This Act shall apply to all public infrastructure assets
21   owned by the Government, including those owned under Republic Act No. 6957 or the Build-
22   Operate-Transfer Law, as amended. Assets covered under this Section shall include:

23               a)   Buildings, including but not limited to, schools, hospitals, and government offices;
24               b)   Roads;
25               c)   Bridges;
26               d)   Airports;
27               e)   Ports;
28               f)   Railways;
29               g)   Power generation and transmission structures;
30               h)   Water resource management and supply structures; and
31               i)   Other similar structures necessary for effective public service delivery.

32           Insurance and non-insurance financing shall cover repair, reconstruction, and rehabilitation
33   or other similar activities to be undertaken as a result of disasters, natural or man-made in origin,
34   to ensure asset recovery and continuity of public service delivery.

35           SEC. 5. Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Council. – A council which shall be
36   known as the Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Council (DRFIC) is hereby created, which
37   shall be headed by the Secretary of the Department of Finance (DOF) as the Chairperson, with the
38   Secretary of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and the Secretary of the
39   Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) as Vice Chairpersons.

40           The DRFIC shall be further composed of the following members:

41               a) Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST);

27          SEC. 7. National Public Asset Insurance Consortium. – A National Public Asset
28   Insurance Consortium is established, hereby referred to as the Consortium, composed of all
29   insurance and reinsurance companies offering public asset insurance and shall be led by the
30   Government Service Insurance System (GSIS).

31           The Consortium shall develop the country's domestic local disaster risk insurance strategy,
32   and shall aid government-owned in the protection of public assets through the provision of
33   collective public asset insurance, and shall ensure rapid claims processing and asset recovery.

34          SEC. 8. Functions of the NPAIC. – The Consortium shall:

35              a) Issue insurance policies specifically for public assets annually;
36              b) Ensure collective risk sharing among its members;
37              c) Evaluate and approve insurance claims made by the Government in a timely
38                 manner;
39              d) Oversee claims processing and subsequently release approved insurance claims to
40                 the Government; and

1                e) Submit data regarding insurance claims and payments to the Consortium.

2            SEC. 9. National Disaster Response for Infrastructure Fund. – The existing financing
3    functions for infrastructure for repair, reconstruction, rehabilitation, or disaster mitigation of the
4    National Disaster Risk Reduction Management Fund is hereby transferred to the National
5    Disaster Response for Infrastructure Fund (NDRIF), hereby created under this Act.

6            The NDRIF shall be managed by the DBM, and its use shall be guided by the principles
7    of the DRFI Framework.

 8           Financing requests under the NDRIF shall be subject to the evaluation and approval of the
 9   DRFIC; Provided, That the DRFIC shall, to the greatest extent necessary, ensure that the NDRIF
10   shall not be the sole funding source for repair, reconstruction, rehabilitation, or disaster
11   mitigation.

12            SEC. 10. Tax Incentive For Members of the Consortium. - To spur investment into
13   Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance, a reduction of five percent (5%) on the prevailing
14   Corporate Income Tax on member companies of the Consortium shall be implemented for the
15   first five (5) years after its effectivity. Thereafter, Congress shall review the tax incentive policy
16   for remedial legislation.

17           SEC. 11. Appropriations. – For the purposes of this Act:

18               a) National Disaster Response for Infrastructure Fund – an initial and minimum
19                  fund of Twenty Billion Pesos (Php 20,000,000,000.00) shall be allocated for
20                  NDRIF for the first year of its implementation. Thereafter, regular appropriations
21                  for this purpose shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
22               b) Premiums for Insurance – the initial fund for the payment of insurance premiums
23                  shall be included in the annual appropriations of the Government as determined
24                  by the insurance policy approved by the DRFIC; Provided, that premium
25                  payments for GOCCs and LGUs shall likewise be included in the annual General
26                  Appropriations as national government subsidies.

27                   All other appropriations necessary for the implementation of this Act shall be
28                   determined by the Department of Budget and Management, and shall be included
29                   in the annual General Appropriations.
