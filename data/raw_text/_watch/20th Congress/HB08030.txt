                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Pursuant to Presidential Decree No. 1183, as amended, The Philippine government
imposes Philippine travel tax collected from most passengers departing the country.
While the travel tax has been historically justified as a revenue source, its administration
continues to generate practical burdens for Filipino travelers—particularly due to
payment validation issues, documentation requirements, and refund friction for those
entitled to exemptions or affected by offloading, rebooking, or cancellations. Public
commentary has likewise criticized the tax as burdensome and outdated, particularly as
it adds a fixed cost on top of already rising airfare and travel-related charges. These
realities are felt most sharply by ordinary Filipino travelers planning family visits,
educational trips, medical travel, and time-sensitive international travel, where added
fees and procedural uncertainty may discourage travel altogether or force last-minute
adjustments.

        This bill seeks to remove that additional burden by abolishing the travel tax and
prohibiting its further collection, while providing a transition mechanism for programs
previously funded by travel tax collections through appropriations in the annual GAA. By
effectively reducing the travel tax cost to zero, the measure is expected to make
international travel more affordable and predictable, lessen transaction friction at the
point of departure, and improve the overall ease of travel by removing a fee that is often
paid separately or verified through additional documentation. Moreover, a recent
Philippine input-output based study reported that eliminating the travel tax could
stimulate demand for travel, producing hundreds of thousands of additional trips and a
sizable increase in total economic output—effects consistent with the policy logic that
lowering travel costs can increase mobility, spending, and tourism-related activity.1

      by easing a direct cost imposed on travelers and simplifying travel-related
compliance, the bill promotes more accessible travel planning for Filipinos and supports

                                   AN ACT
                          ABOLISHING THE TRAVEL TAX

Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Travel Tax Abolition Act of
2026.".

Section 2. Repealing Clause. - The State hereby abolishes the travel tax as imposed
under Presidential Decree No. 1183, as amended, and Section 73 of Republic Act No. 9593,
otherwise known as the "Tourism Act of 2009". All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

Section 3. Prohibition and Refund. - No government agency or private entity shall
collect travel taxes upon the effectivity of this Act. For flights scheduled on or after the
date of effectivity, the collecting authority shall immediately refund any previously paid
travel taxes to the passenger.

Section 4. Budget and Alternative Funding. - To ensure the continuity of programs
previously funded by travel tax collections, the national government shall provide the
necessary funding for the following agencies through the annual General Appropriations
Act (GAA):

   1. The Tourism Infrastructure and Enterprise Zone Authority (TIEZA) for tourism
      development projects;
   2. The Commission on Higher Education (CHED) for tourism-related higher
      education development funds; and
   3. The National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA) for the National
      Endowment Fund for Culture and the Arts.
Section 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty (60) days from the
approval of this Act, the TIEZA, in coordination with the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the
efficient implementation of the tax refund and the transition to GAA-based funding.

Section 6. Separability Clause. - If any part or provision of this Act shall be held
unconstitutional or invalid, other provisions thereof which are not affected shall continue
to be in full force and effect.

Section 7. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in at least one (1) newspaper of general circulation, whichever
comes earlier.
