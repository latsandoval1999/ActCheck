                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Republic Act No. 11192 consolidated the public technical-vocational
institutions of the Cordillera Administrative Region into the Cordillera State
Institute of Technical Education (CSITE). While this integration was a significant
administrative achievement, the resulting institute continues to operate under
structural limitations that prevent it from fully meeting the region’s need for high-
level, industry-responsive technical education. The present organizational and
governance structure of the Institute is inadequate to meet the increasing demands
of higher education, research, innovation, and industry-responsive skills
development.
      This measure then seeks to amend Republic Act No 11192 to incorporate
critical reforms that will transform the institute into a true polytechnic institution,
to be known as the Cordillera State Polytechnic Institute (CSPI). The bill likewise
grants the Institute greater institutional and fiscal autonomy, establishes a Board
of Trustees as its highest policymaking body, and authorizes the collection and
retention of reasonable fees and other lawful income to strengthen its financial
sustainability and improve the delivery of academic and support services
      These reforms are intended to empower the Institute to pursue excellence in
technical, technological, professional, and applied higher education, research
extension, and innovation while ensuring accountability, transparency, and
responsiveness to the educational and developmental priorities of the Cordillera
Region and the nation. It is a deliberate and necessary shift in institutional
identity, academic scope, and societal aspiration.
      A polytechnic institute is distinguished by its comprehensive and multi-
disciplinary approach to technical education. It moves beyond a singular focus on
isolated trade skills toward the integration of theoretical knowledge with high-
level practical application across a broad spectrum of technologies, including
engineering technology, applied sciences, and industrial arts. The CSPI will offer
a seamless continuum of programs, from foundational skills training to advanced
technical diplomas, and foster an environment of applied research, innovation,
and technology incubation. The institute will not only produce skilled workers,
but technologist, supervisors, and innovators who can drive the region’s
economic development and attract higher-value industries, hence, ensuring the
Cordillera workforce to possess analytical capability, practical mastery, and
creative problem-solving required by the contemporary labor market.
     To operationalize and sustain this enhanced institutional identity, this
measure provides for a genuine fiscal autonomy. The present financial
management of the Institute being directly supervised and controlled by TESDA
hampers the timely maintenance of advanced polytechnic facilities, the
acquisition of modern industrial-grade equipment, and the swift implementation
of curricular improvements that require immediate funding. By establishing CSPI
as a corporate body with a dedicated revolving fund, it will be liberated from this
restrictive financial framework and empowered to retain and deploy its income
directly toward strategic institutional priorities, responding rapidly to industry
demands without wholly dependent on the annual national budget cycle.
Furthermore, the creation of an autonomous Board of Trustees composed of
multi-sectoral stakeholders replaces the exclusive supervision of the TESDA.
This governance body will bring together representatives from government,
industry, labor, and the local community to ensure that the Institute’s strategic
direction is deeply rooted in the socioeconomic realities and development
priorities of the Cordillera Region.
     Moreover, the bill establishes an unequivocal statutory basis for the
assessment and collection of reasonable fees for all program offerings. This will
provide a steady supplementary resource stream that will directly support the
continuous upgrading of curriculum, the professional development of its faculty-
trainers, and the provision of scholarships for the youth of the Cordillera,
particularly those from economically disadvantaged backgrounds, ensuring that
access to high quality polytechnic education remains equitable.
     This measure also addresses a critical gap in the current educational
architecture of the region by providing a clear statutory mandate for CSPI to
develop and implement ladderized diploma to degree programs. Under the
trifocalized education system, the articulation between technical-vocational
education and higher education has long been hampered by the absence of
enforceable mechanisms that require higher education institutions to recognize
and credit TVET qualifications. The Philippine Qualifications Framework and
the Ladderized Education Act provide the policy foundation for seamless
progression between TESDA and CHED qualifications, yet the implementation
of credit transfer arrangements remain largely dependent on the willingness of
individual institutions. By explicitly mandating CSPI to integrate its TESDA-
registered diploma programs with CHED-recognized baccalaureate degree
offerings, this bill creates a tangible, institution-based model for ladderization.
This provision established CSPI as a singular institution where a student may
enter a technical-vocational track, earn a TESDA diploma, and progress toward a
full baccalaureate degree within the same system, with the institute seeking the
appropriate recognition from each trifocal agency for its respective programs.
This approach does not disturb the trifocalized structure but instead
operationalizes it within a single, coherent institution, providing the youth of the
Cordillera with clear, accessible, and structured pathways for upward educational
and social mobility.
    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

  AN ACT AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11192, OTHERWISE
KNOWN AS THE “CORDILLERA STATE INSTITUTE OF TECHNICAL
    EDUCATION ACT” RENAMING THE INSTITUTE AS THE
    CORDILLERA STATE POLYTECHNIC INSTITUTE (CSPI),
    GRANTING IT GREATER INSTITUTIONAL AND FISCAL
   AUTONOMY, ESTABLISHING A BOARD OF TRUSTEES, AND
   PROVIDING FOR THE COLLECTION OF FEES AND OTHER
                      PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Cordillera State
Polytechnic Institute Charter Amendments Act”.
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
strengthen public higher education institutions by promoting academic
excellence, institutional autonomy, fiscal sustainability, research and innovation,
and industry-responsive technical and professional education. Towards this end,
the Cordillera State Institute of Technical Education shall be recognized and
strengthened as the Cordillera State Polytechnic Institute.
SEC. 3. Renaming of the Institute and Establishment of the Cordillera State
Polytechnic Institute (CSPI). The Cordillera State Institute of Technical
Education (CSITE) created under Republic Act No 11192 is hereby renamed and
established as the Cordillera State Polytechnic Institute (CSPI), hereinafter
referred to as the Institute. All references to the Cordillera State Institute of
Technical Education in Republic Act No 11192 and other laws, executive
issuances, rules and regulations shall hereafter refer to the Cordillera State
Polytechnic Institute.
     The Institute shall be a body corporate, possessing all the powers of a
corporation under the Revised Corporation Code of the Philippines, and shall
enjoy academic freedom, fiscal and institutional autonomy pursuant to Paragraph
2, Section 5, Article XIV of the 1987 Constitution. It shall integrate the CSITE
Main Campus, CSITE extension campuses at Abra, Ifugao, Mountain Province
and Kalinga, and all other LGU-run and State-run Provincial Training Centers in
the Cordillera Administrative Region.
SEC. 4. Polytechnic Identity and Objectives. – The Cordillera State
Polytechnic Institute is established as a comprehensive, multi-disciplinary
polytechnic institution. It shall:
     a. Integrate theoretical knowledge with high-level practical application
across engineering technology, applied sciences, industrial arts, and other
technical fields;
     b. Offer a seamless continuum of programs from foundational skills training
to advanced technical diplomas and as may be authorized, higher-level
credentials;
     c. Foster an environment of applied research, innovation, and technology
incubation in partnership with industry and local communities;
     d. Produce technologists, supervisors, and innovators capable of driving
regional economic development and adapting to the demands of the evolving
global economy;
      e. Serve as the apex instruction of the regional technical-vocational
education and training (TVET) system, ensuring that the Cordillera workforce
possesses analytical capability, practical mastery, and creative problem-solving
skills.
SEC. 5. Governing Board; Composition. – The governance of the Institute is
vested exclusively in a Board of Trustees, hereinafter referred to as the Board,
which shall replace the direct supervisory control of the TESDA. The Board shall
be composed of the following;
      a. The Director-General of the Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority (TESDA), as chairperson;
      b. The Executive Director of the Institute, as vice-chairperson;
     c. The Chairperson of the Senate Committee on Labor, Employment and
Human Resources Development, as member;
      d. The Chairperson of the House Committee on Higher and Technical
Education, as member;
     e. The Regional Director of TESDA- Cordillera Administrative Region, as
member;
      f. The Regional Director of the Department of Economy, Planning, and
Development (DEPDEV) – CAR, as member;
    g. The Regional Director of the Department of Science and Technology
(DOST) – CAR, as member;
      h. The President of the Cordillera Chamber of Commerce and Industry, or
a duly recognized regional business organization, as member;
     i. One (1) representative from the labor/workers sector;
     j. One (1) representative from the employer/industry sector; and
     k. One (1) representative from the Student Body Organization.
     The representatives from the labor and employer sectors, as well as the
students shall be appointed by the Board upon the recommendation of a Search
Committee. They shall serve for a term of two (2) years. The members of the
Committee shall serve without compensation but shall be entitled to honoraria
and reimbursement of actual and necessary expenses, subject to existing
accounting and auditing laws.
SEC. 6. Powers and Duties of the Board of Trustees. – In addition to its general
powers of administration and the exercise of corporate powers, the Board shall
have the following specific powers and duties;
     a. To promulgate and implement policies consistent with the polytechnic
character of the Institute and the national thrusts of TESDA under Republic Act.
No. 7796, otherwise known as the “Technical Education and Skills Development
Act of 1994”;
     b. To approve the curricula, instructional programs, and rules of discipline;
     c. To fix, assess, and collect reasonable fees for all program offerings as
provided in Section 12 of this Act, including tuition/training fees, and other
necessary school charges such as, but not limited to, matriculation fees,
graduation fees and other laboratory fees, as the Board may deem proper to
impose, after due consultation with the involved sectors and authorities and
concerned government agencies;
     d. To authorize the construction or repair of its buildings, machineries,
equipment, and other facilities and the purchase and acquisition of real and
personal properties, including necessary supplies, materials and equipment;
     e. To enter into Memorandum of Agreement/ Memorandum of
Understanding, locally and internationally, with business, industry, local
government units, and other educational institutions for the development and
management of the Institute’s economic assets and the enhancement of its
academic programs;
     f. To receive in trust legacies, gifts, and donations and to administer and
dispose of the same when necessary for the benefit of the Institute;
     g. To receive and appropriate all sums as may be provided for the support of
the Institute, in the matter it may determine in its discretion, to carry out the
purposes and functions of the Institute;
     h. To delegate any of its powers and duties to the Executive Director and
other officials of the Institute as it may deem appropriate, to expedite the
administration of the affairs of the Institute;
     i. To promulgate rules and regulations for the effective implementation of
this Act; and
    j. To exercise such other powers as may be necessary to carry out the
objectives of this Act
SEC. 7. Meetings of the Board. – The Board shall meet at least twice a year.
Whenever necessary, the Chairperson of the Board shall call special meetings
with written notices sent at least three (3) days prior to the date of the special
meeting.
      A quorum of the Board shall consist of the majority of all its members
holding office at the time of the meeting; Provided, That the Chairperson of the
Board or the Executive Director of the Institute is among those present in the
meeting.
      In the absence of the chairperson of the Board, the Director General of
TESDA shall designate a TESDA representative with all the rights and
responsibilities of a regular member; Provided, however, that during the said
meeting, the Executive Director of the Institute as the Vice Chairperson shall be
the presiding officer.
      The members of the Board shall not receive any salary but shall be entitled
to honorarium or reimbursement for actual and necessary expenses incurred,
either in their attendance to meetings of the Board or in connection with other
official business authorized by resolution of the Board, subject to existing laws
and regulations.
SEC. 8. – The Executive Director – The Institute shall be headed by an
Executive Director who shall render fulltime service and who shall be appointed
by the Board in accordance with the guidelines, qualifications and standards set
by the Board and Civil Service rules and regulations, upon recommendation of a
duly constituted search committee.
      In case of vacancy in the Office of the Executive Director by reason of
death, retirement, resignation, removal for cause or incapacity of the Executive
Director to perform the functions of the office, the Board shall designate an
officer-in-charge of the Institute pending the appointment of a new Executive
Director.
SEC. 9. – Directors for General Administration and Services and Academic
Affairs and Development – The Executive Director of the Institute shall be
assisted by the Directors for General Administration and Services and Academic
Affairs and Development. They shall render fulltime service and shall be
appointed by the Board upon recommendation of the Search Committee and the
Executive Director in accordance with the guidelines, qualifications and
standards set by the Board and Civil Service rules and regulations.
SEC. 10. Campus Sites and Directors. – The main campus and extension
campuses shall be headed each by a Campus Director who shall render fulltime
service. They shall be appointed by the Board upon recommendation of the
Search Committee and the Executive Director in accordance with the guidelines,
qualifications and standards set by the Board and Civil Service rules and
regulations.
SEC. 11. – Secretary of the Board. – The Board shall appoint a Secretary who
shall serve as such for both the Board and the Institute and who shall keep all
records and proceedings of the Board. The Secretary shall serve the appropriate
notice of Board meetings upon each member of the Board.
SEC. 12. Collection of Fees for Program Offerings; Revolving Fund. – The
Board of Trustees is expressly authorized to fix, assess, and collect reasonable
fees for all curricular and training program offerings, including but not limited to
tuition, training fees, laboratory fees, assessment fees, and other necessary school
charges. The proceeds from such fees shall be deposited in an authorized
government depository bank and shall constitute a Revolving Fund, which the
Institute may use exclusively for the construction of physical facilities,
improvement of instruction, applied research, extension services, maintenance
and upgrading of polytechnic facilities, acquisition of modern industrial-grade
equipment; professional development of faculty trainers, scholarship programs,
and other priority programs or projects of the Institute. The assessment and
collection of these fees shall be subject to the pertinent accounting, budgeting,
and auditing rules and regulations of the Department of Budget and Management
(DBM) and the Commission on Audit (COA). The Board shall ensure that the fee
structure remains accessible and equitable and shall provide for scholarship
programs and fee subsidies for economically disadvantaged but deserving
students. Any provision of existing laws, rules, or regulations to the contrary
notwithstanding, the income generated by the Institute from the collection fees
authorized under this section shall be retained by the Institute.
SEC. 13. Ladderized Diploma-to- Degree Programs. – In accordance with
Republic Act No. 10968, otherwise known as the “Philippine Qualifications
Framework Act”, and Republic Act No. 10647, otherwise known as the
“Ladderized Education Act of 2014, the Cordillera State Polytechnic Institute
(CSPI) is hereby mandated to develop and implement ladderized programs that
integrate TESDA-registered technical-vocational diploma programs with
baccalaureate degree offerings. Credits earned from the technical-vocational
component shall be recognized and credited toward relevant degree programs,
consistent with the Philippine Credit Transfer System. The Institute shall seek
appropriate recognition from TESDA for its diploma programs and shall be
authorized to confer the corresponding diploma and baccalaureate degree upon
completion of the respective requirements.
      The Board of Trustees shall ensure that these ladderized curricular
pathways are competency-based, industry-responsive, and aligned with the
Philippine Qualifications Framework. The specific guidelines governing the
development, implementation, and articulation mechanisms of the ladderized
programs, including the detailed credit transfer arrangements and coordination
protocols between CSPI, TESDA, and CHED, shall be prescribed in the
implementing rules and regulations of this Act.
SEC. 14. Duty and Tax Exemptions. – The provisions of any general or special
law to the contrary notwithstanding;
      a. All revenues and assets received by the Institute, of whatever source,
which are actually, directly, and exclusively used for instruction, research,
extension, production, or in furtherance thereof, as well as purchases and other
transactions entered into by the Institute through the Board, shall be exempt from
all kinds of taxes and duties, whether imposed by the National Government or
any of its agencies and instrumentalities, including government-owned or -
controlled corporations, and by local government units.
     b. All importation of technical, vocational, and scientific books and
publications, equipment, instruments, and apparatuses by the Institute, upon
certification by TESDA, shall be exempt from customs duties in accordance with
the provisions of Republic Act No. 10863, otherwise known as the “Customs
Modernization and Tariff Act (CMTA)”.
     c. All donations, bequests, and grants to the Institute shall be exempt from
donor’s tax and shall be considered allowable deductions from the gross income
of the donor for income tax purposes, in accordance with the provisions of the
National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.
SEC. 15. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of TESDA and the
integrated institutions. The amount for Capital Outlay shall be a continuing
appropriation. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
operation of the institute shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act.
SEC. 16. Transitory Provisions. Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of
this Act, the Board of Trustees shall be constituted. The Executive Director of
CSITE under Republic Act No. 11192 shall serve as the Acting Executive
Director of CSPI until a permanent appointment is made by the Board. All
existing contracts, agreements, and obligations of the former CSITE shall be
honored by CSPI.
SEC. 17. Retention of Existing Provisions. – All provisions of Republic Act No.
11192, otherwise known as the “Cordillera State Institute of Technical Education
Act,” which are not inconsistent with the provisions of this Act shall remain in
full force and effect.
SEC. 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Board of Trustees, in coordination with CHED,
TESDA and other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the implementing rules
and regulations necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act.
SEC. 19. Repealing Clause. – all laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act
are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
SEC. 20. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.
SEC. 21. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
