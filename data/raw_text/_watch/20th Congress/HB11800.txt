EXPLANATORY NOTE

Ube has long been part of Filipino food and culture. From traditional delicacies to modern
desserts and other food products, it has become increasingly popular both in the Philippines and
abroad. This growing demand presents an opportunity not only to strengthen the country's
agricultural and food industries, but also to create more jobs and livelihoods for Filipino workers.

Today, the Philippine ube industry continues to gain recognition both locally and
internationally, opening new opportunities for economic growth and employment. According to
the United Ube PH Federation, the global ube market was valued at approximately USDS95
million in 2025, while the Philippines accounted for only around USD3.06 million.! The
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) also described ube as a “rising star” export commodity,
particularly in markets such as the United States, Japan, and South Korea. Behind the growth of
this industry are Filipino farmers, workers, and communities whose livelihoods depend on the
opportunities it creates.”

While existing government initiatives and legislative proposals seek to increase ube
production, support farmers, develop processing facilities, and expand markets, equal attention
must be given to the people who will work in these growing enterprises. Beyond farming and
harvesting, the industry offers opportunities in food processing, manufacturing, packaging, quality
assurance, storage, logistics, and other related activities. Yet without the necessary skills, training,
and access to employment information, Filipino workers risk being left behind by the very industry
growing around them.

The government already implements programs supporting agricultural development,
technical-vocational training, enterprise development, and employment facilitation. These include
the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority's (TESDA) Agricultural Crops
Production NC II, Food Processing NC II, and Agro-Entrepreneurship NC II; the DTI's ProoSPER
Project; the Department of Agriculture's (DA) High-Value Crops Development Program; and the
Department of Labor and Employment's (DOLE) Integrated Livelihood Program. What is needed
is to ensure that these existing programs respond to the actual skills and employment needs of
workers as the ube industry expands.

This measure therefore seeks to integrate workforce development and employment
considerations into existing government initiatives for the Philippine ube and ube-based products
industry. It provides for the assessment of workforce requirements, alignment of training and
certification programs with industry needs, and facilitation of employment opportunities for
Filipino workers and jobseekers. Rather than creating new government programs, offices, or funds,

AN ACT
INTEGRATING WORKFORCE DEVELOPMENT, EMPLOYMENT PLANNING, AND
DECENT WORK CONSIDERATIONS INTO GOVERNMENT INITIATIVES FOR THE
PHILIPPINE UBE AND UBE-BASED PRODUCTS INDUSTRY, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Ube Workforce
Development Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote full, decent, and productive employment and to ensure that Filipino workers are able to
participate in and benefit from the growth of domestic industries.

Toward this end, the State shall promote the integration of workforce development and
employment considerations in the planning and implementation of programs for the development
of the Philippine ube and ube-based products industry, particularly in identifying employment
opportunities, addressing skills gaps, and improving workers’ access to jobs generated by industry
growth. In carrying out this policy, concerned government agencies shall, as far as practicable,
utilize and coordinate existing programs and mechanisms consistent with their respective
mandates.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
be defined as follows:

(a) “Ube” refers to Dioscorea alata, commonly known as purple yam;

(b) “Ube-based products industry” refers to economic activities involving the cultivation,
harvesting, post-harvest handling,processing, manufacturing, packaging, storage,
distribution, marketing, export, and other value-adding activities involving ube as a
principal or significant input;

(c) “Ube workforce” refers to workers, jobseekers, trainees, and other persons engaged in or
seeking employment in occupations directly associated with the ube and ube-based
products industry; and

(d) “Workforce development” refers to measures relating to skills identification and
development, competency assessment and certification, employment facilitation, career
information, enterprise-based education and training, and other interventions intended to
improve employability and productivity.

Industry Planning. - The Department of Agriculture (DA) and the Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI), in consultation with the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), the

industry. Such considerations shall include:

SECTION 5. Ube Workforce Assessment. — The DOLE and TESDA shall, in coordination
with the DA, DTI, industry stakeholders, workers and workers’ organizations, and other concerned
entities, periodically assess the workforce requirements of the ube and ube-based products
industry.

The assessment shall identify, as may be appropriate, existing and emerging occupations,
current and projected demand for workers, required competencies and qualifications, skills gaps,
geographic concentrations of employment opportunities, and barriers to the participation of
workers and jobseekers in the industry. Existing labor market information, administrative data,
industry studies, and other available government data shall be utilized for this purpose.

The results of such assessments shall inform relevant government programs and the
monitoring of employment outcomes, including jobs generated or supported, workers trained or
certified, and jobseekers referred or placed through existing government programs.

SECTION 6. Skills Development Response. — Based on the workforce needs identified
under this Act, TESDA shall review the responsiveness of applicable competency standards,
Training Regulations, qualifications, micro-credentials, scholarship programs, and enterprise-
based education and training arrangements to the skills requirements of the ube and ube-based
products industry.

Where gaps are identified, TESDA may, in accordance with its mandate and established
procedures and in consultation with industry and labor stakeholders, develop, review, or update
appropriate competency standards, qualifications, micro-credentials, or Training Regulations.
Workers and jobseekers may be referred to applicable technical-vocational education and training,
competency assessment and certification, scholarship, and enterprise-based education and training
programs, subject to existing laws, rules and regulations, eligibility requirements, and available
resources.

SECTION 7. Employment Facilitation. — The DOLE, through Public Employment
Service Offices (PESOs) and other existing public employment service mechanisms, shall
facilitate the dissemination of verified employment opportunities, job matching, referral, and
placement of qualified workers and jobseekers in the ube and ube-based products industry.

The DOLE and TESDA shall coordinate employment referral and skills development
services so that workers and jobseekers who lack the qualifications required for available
employment opportunities may be referred to appropriate existing training and certification
programs.

SECTION 8. Worker Participation and Labor Standards. — Workers and workers’
organizations shall be included, as appropriate, in consultations under this Act concerning
workforce requirements, skills development, employment opportunities, and working conditions
in the ube and ube-based products industry. Such consultations may likewise include employers,

SECTION 9. Integration with Existing Ube Industry Development Initiatives. — The
Department of Agriculture (DA) and the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), in coordination
with the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) and the Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority (TESDA), shall ensure that workforce assessments, identified skills
requirements, and employment opportunities under this Act are taken into account in the

SECTION 10. Non-Duplication of Government Programs. — The implementation of this
Act shall be undertaken through existing government programs, facilities, and service delivery
mechanisms, consistent with the respective mandates of the concerned agencies. Nothing tn this
Act shall be construed to require the establishment of a separate workforce development program,
office, council, or fund, or to duplicate existing government functions and services.

SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOLE, TESDA, DA, and DTI, in coordination with other concerned
agencies and after consultation with relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 12. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the existing appropriations of the concerned agencies. Thereafter,
such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 13. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force
and effect.

SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 15. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
