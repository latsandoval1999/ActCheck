                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

“Ang ina ang ilaw ng tahanan.” This is a famous Filipino phrase referring to the central
role of mothers as the heart and soul of their families. Filipino women are widely known
for their strength, resilience, and willingness to sacrifice for their families and loved
ones. However, behind their strength, many women quietly face personal struggles
that are unseen and unspoken.

One vulnerable period in a woman’s life is the postpartum stage. Not only are there
additional responsibilities of caring for a newborn, mothers also experience significant
physical and mental changes.1 Postpartum mental health refers to a woman’s well-
being after giving birth.2 A study on pregnant and postpartum women in the Philippines
found that at least three in every five pregnant or postpartum Filipino women
experienced clinically significant depressive symptoms.3 The same study found that

A number of studies have looked into the prevalence of postpartum depression among
mothers in specific hospitals in the country. Their results show a varying prevalence
of depression, with findings ranging from 14.5%,4 22.61%,5 and even as high as
32.8%.6

Despite the prevalence of these conditions, postpartum mental health disorders are
not often recognized or discussed in this country. Additionally, seeking help for mental
health issues entails financial costs and may cause additional strain on new mothers
and their families, making it even more difficult for postpartum women to receive the
necessary mental health support.7 Addressing these barriers would require increasing
public awareness about postpartum health conditions and improving access to
affordable mental health services.

Currently, the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation’s (PhilHealth) Maternity Care
Package and Normal Spontaneous Delivery Package only cover post-partum care
within 72 hours and 7 days after delivery.8 It does not cover postpartum mental health
screening or treatment of postpartum depression or similar disorders, especially those
that manifest or persist beyond the first week after childbirth.

While Philhealth provides benefit packages for general and specialty mental health
services, there are none targeted towards the screening and diagnosis of postpartum
mental health conditions in women. This gap is significant, especially because these
disorders are not often talked about or widely known in the country. It is essential that
Filipino mothers are not left to face these challenges alone.

                 APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR


Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Postpartum Mental Health
Benefits Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and promote
the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them. The
State also recognizes the role of women in nation-building and affirms the need to
promote their health and well-being. Towards this end, the State shall provide
accessible and affordable postpartum mental health services for Filipino women.

SEC. 3. Postpartum Mental Health Benefit Package. – Within one (1) year from the
effectivity of this Act, the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), in
consultation with the Department of Health (DOH), and key stakeholders, shall develop
a benefit package to support the postpartum mental health of Filipino women. The
benefit package shall cover routine screening, diagnosis, and treatment of postpartum
mental health disorders.

For purposes of this Act, postpartum mental health disorders include, but are not limited
to mood disturbances or disorders appearing shortly after birth, such as postpartum
blues, depression, or psychosis.



                                           4
SEC. 4. Information and Awareness Campaign. – The DOH and PhilHealth shall
conduct nationwide information and awareness campaigns on postpartum mental
health. These campaigns shall aim to educate Filipino mothers and families on the
importance of postpartum care and the availability of government support services,
including the benefit package provided under this Act.

SEC. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The Department of Health, in
consultation with key stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 6. Appropriations. - The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
charged against the total revenue of PhilHealth, composed of premium contributions
from direct contributors and premium subsidies of the government for the indirect
contributors.

SEC. 7. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part hereof is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provisions not otherwise affected shall
remain valid and subsisting.

SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. — Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive
order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation contrary to or
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed, modified, or amended
accordingly.

SEC. 9. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
