                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The 1987 Constitution of the Philippines provides an unequivocal
mandate: that the State shall promote social justice and give priority to the
needs of the underprivileged, the sick, the elderly, the disabled, and the
marginalized. The same living document affirms the dignity of every human
and guarantees full respect for human rights, mandating Congress to give
highest priority to enacting measures that protect our people by equitably
distributing wealth and public resources for the common good.

      Children with disabilities represent among the most vulnerable
members of Philippine society. Yet for too long, they and their families have
navigated their daily realities without adequate government support, denied
not by lack of law or rights but by absence of appropriate and accessible
public programs. Recognizing the opportunity to help the families of
children with disabilities, the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD) took the life-saving initiative by launching the
Community-based Holistic Early Response and Intervention for Children
with Disabilities, also known as Project CHERISH.
       Project CHERISH provides financial assistance, therapy access, and
rehabilitation support for children with disabilities and their families. Each
participating family-beneficiary receives a monthly disability support
package of up to Ten Thousand Pesos (P10,000), covering basic needs,
healthcare, therapy, and caregiver training. The program also provides a
graduated monthly disability allowance ranging from Two Thousand Pesos
(P2,000) to Ten Thousand Pesos (P10,000) based on assessed need, to
defray costs related to healthcare, therapy, education, and transportation.

      Social justice is neither a slogan nor an abstraction but a concrete
and operative directive requiring affirmative State action in favor of those
who are disadvantaged. The institutionalization of Project CHERISH as a
permanent national program is one such affirmative act aiming to transform
a commendable program into an enduring public commitment, ensuring
that benefits and beneficiaries are not contingent on budget cycles or
administrative discretion but guaranteed by law.

       A society that neglects its children with disabilities and the families
who sacrifice daily to care for them falls short of its own highest ideals. The
institutionalization of DSWD’s Project CHERISH will ensure that the
promise of an inclusive society is not merely dictated in law, but ultimately
realized in homes, barangays, and communities across the nation.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is
earnestly sought.

                         AN ACT​
INSTITUTIONALIZING PROJECT CHERISH OF THE DEPARTMENT OF
 SOCIAL WELFARE AND DEVELOPMENT, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                        THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

    SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Project
CHERISH Institutionalization Act"

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to uphold the rights and promote the welfare of children with
disabilities and their family caregivers. In consonance with the principles of
the Magna Carta for Persons with Disabilities (R.A. No. 7277), the
Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD), and the
Convention on the Rights of the Child, the State shall establish and sustain
a comprehensive, community-based disability support program that
provides financial assistance, therapy access, rehabilitation support, and
caregiver capacity-building. The State affirms that early intervention and
inclusive community participation are critical to the holistic development of
children with disabilities, and that family caregivers are essential partners in
realizing this mandate.

     SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall mean:

         a)​ Child with Disability — a person below eighteen (18) years of
             age with a long-term physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory
             impairment which, in interaction with various barriers, may
             hinder their full and effective participation in society. This
             includes, but is not limited to, children with autism spectrum
             disorder (ASD), cerebral palsy, Down syndrome, intellectual
             disabilities, hearing impairment, visual impairment, and other
             developmental or congenital conditions requiring special care
             and support.
         b)​ Project CHERISH — the Community-based Holistic Early
             Response and Intervention for Children with Disabilities
             launched by the DSWD is herein institutionalized as a
             permanent national program.
         c)​ Monthly Disability Support Package — the comprehensive
             financial assistance provided to eligible beneficiary families,
             covering costs related to basic needs, healthcare, therapy,
             education, transportation, and other disability-related expenses.
         d)​ Family Caregiver — a non-professional individual, typically a
             parent, sibling, grandparent, or other household member, who
             provides primary and full-time care to a child with a disability.
         e)​ Rehabilitation Services — medical, physical, occupational,
             speech, and other therapeutic interventions aimed at improving
             the functional capacity and quality of life of children with
             disabilities.
         f)​ Community-based Intervention — programs and services
             delivered at the barangay, municipal, or city level that promote
             inclusion, reduce stigma, and support children with disabilities
             within their own communities.
         g)​ PRPWD — Philippine Registry for Persons with Disability, the
             national database maintained by the NCDA for profiling and
             tracking persons with disabilities and services rendered to
             them.
       SEC. 4. Establishment of the National CHERISH Program. There
is hereby established the National CHERISH Program under the
administration of the Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD), in coordination with the National Council on Disability Affairs
(NCDA), the Department of Health (DOH), the Department of Education
(DepEd), and Local Government Units (LGUs). The Program shall operate
as a permanent, nationwide initiative modeled on the Project CHERISH
pilot and shall provide the following core services:

        a)​ Monthly Disability Support Package ranging from Two
            Thousand Pesos (P2,000) to Ten Thousand Pesos (P10,000)
            per beneficiary family per month, based on assessed need,
            household size, and regional cost-of-living.
        b)​ Access to Rehabilitation and Therapy Services, including
            physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy,
            behavioral intervention, and medical referrals to partner
            government hospitals and community health centers.
        c)​ Caregiver Capacity-Building, consisting of training and skills
            development programs for family caregivers on home-based
            care, disability management, therapeutic techniques, and
            livelihood opportunities compatible with caregiving.
        d)​ Community Engagement and Stigma Reduction, through
            awareness campaigns, inclusive barangay activities, and
            partnership with civil society organizations and faith-based
            communities.
        e)​ Data Management and Registry Integration, linking beneficiary
            data to Listahanan (National Household Targeting System for
            Poverty Reduction), the PRPWD, and other relevant national
            databases to ensure accurate targeting and efficient service
            delivery.

      SEC. 5. Eligibility and Coverage. To qualify as a beneficiary under
this Act, an applicant must satisfy the following criteria:

        1.​ Child Beneficiary: The child must be a Filipino citizen below
            eighteen (18) years of age with a documented physical, mental,
            intellectual, or sensory disability certified by a licensed
            physician,      developmental      pediatrician,  psychiatrist,
            psychologist, or the NCDA/PWDA.
        2.​ Family Caregiver: The primary family caregiver must be a
            Filipino citizen who assumes full-time or primary caregiving
             responsibilities for the child with disability, and as a result is
             unable to engage in full-time regular employment.
         3.​ Income Threshold: The total household income of the
             beneficiary family must not exceed the current poverty
             threshold for the relevant household size as determined by the
             Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA). The DSWD may adopt a
             needs-based scoring system to prioritize households in greatest
             need.
         4.​ Residency: The beneficiary family must reside in a participating
             LGU or barangay duly enrolled in the National CHERISH
             Program.

      The following categories of beneficiaries shall be presumed eligible
upon compliance with documentary requirements: (a) solo parent
caregivers of children with disabilities; (b) senior citizen grandparents
serving as primary caregivers; (c) sibling caregivers aged eighteen (18)
years or above who have foregone educational or employment
opportunities to care for a child with disability; and (d) families with multiple
children with disabilities.

      SEC. 6. Monthly Disability Support Package. Eligible beneficiary
families shall receive a Monthly Disability Support Package from the
government to defray expenses related to the child's disability. The base
amount shall range from Two Thousand Pesos (P2,000) to Ten Thousand
Pesos (P10,000) per family per month, to be determined by the DSWD
according to the following principles:

         a)​ Needs-Based Grading: Families with greater care demands,
             lower income, or multiple children with disabilities shall receive
             higher support within the range.
         b)​ Regional Differentiation: The DSWD may adjust amounts by
             region or province to account for differences in the cost of living
             and the availability of therapy services.
         c)​ Indexation: Support levels shall be reviewed annually and
             adjusted at least every three (3) years in line with inflation and
             changes in the PSA poverty thresholds.
         d)​ Duration: The support package is intended as long-term
             assistance for as long as the caregiving situation persists.
             Eligibility shall be revalidated at least once every two (2) years.
      The Monthly Disability Support Package shall be disbursed through
safe and accessible channels such as ATM debit cards, cash transfer
mechanisms, or electronic means. The package shall be tax-exempt and
shall not be counted as taxable income. Receipt of this package shall not
disqualify a beneficiary family from receiving other forms of government
assistance for which they are otherwise eligible.

      SEC 7. Rehabilitation and Therapy Access. The DOH, in
coordination with the DSWD and partner LGUs, shall ensure that every
beneficiary child has access to appropriate rehabilitation and therapy
services. Specifically:

        a)​ Government hospitals, rural health units, and community health
            centers shall be directed to prioritize children enrolled in the
            National CHERISH Program for therapy sessions, medical
            consultations, and assistive device issuance.
        b)​ The DSWD may enter into memoranda of agreement with
            accredited private therapy centers, NGOs, and faith-based
            organizations to expand the availability and reach of
            rehabilitation services at subsidized or no cost to Program
            beneficiaries.
        c)​ Where physical access to therapy facilities is limited, the
            Program shall promote and fund home-based and
            community-based therapy delivery, including through trained
            community health workers and volunteer therapists.

     SEC. 8. Caregiver Support Services. In addition to the Monthly
Disability Support Package, family caregivers enrolled in the National
CHERISH Program shall be entitled to the following support services:

        a)​ Capacity-Building and Skills Training: The DSWD, DOH,
            DepEd, and TESDA shall provide caregivers with training on
            home-based therapeutic care, disability management,
            autism-responsive parenting, and livelihood skills that can be
            pursued alongside caregiving duties.
        b)​ Mental Health and Psychosocial Support: Recognizing the
            psychological toll of full-time caregiving, the DOH shall ensure
            access to free or subsidized counseling, peer support groups,
            and stress management programs for family caregivers.
        c)​ Respite Care: The DSWD shall facilitate access to temporary
            respite services — including trained in-home substitute
            caregivers and community-based day care facilities — to allow
            primary caregivers periodic breaks from caregiving duties.
        d)​ PhilHealth Coverage: Family caregivers who are not otherwise
            covered shall be automatically enrolled as indirect contributors
            under PhilHealth, subsidized by the government.
        e)​ Social Security Continuity: The government shall establish a
            mechanism to subsidize SSS contributions of indigent
            caregivers who had to leave formal employment, so that
            caregiving years are credited as part of their social insurance
            record.

       SEC. 9. National Registry and Data Management. The DSWD, in
partnership with the NCDA and LGUs, shall establish and maintain a
National CHERISH Registry that records all enrolled beneficiary families,
profiles of children with disabilities, caregiving arrangements, services
received, and outcomes tracked over time. This registry shall be:

        a)​ Linked to and interoperable with the PRPWD, Listahanan, and
            the PhilHealth member database;
        b)​ Protected in accordance with the Data Privacy Act (R.A.
            10173), with personal identifiers secured and access limited to
            authorized personnel;
        c)​ Used for evidence-based policymaking, targeting of services,
            and evaluation of Program impact; and
        d)​ Coordinated with the PSA to include informal caregiving data in
            national time-use and disability surveys.

      SEC 10. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting. The DSWD shall
develop a Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Framework for this Act. Key
performance indicators shall include the number of children served,
average support package received, therapy utilization rates, caregiver
well-being measures, and developmental outcomes for children. The
DSWD shall submit annual reports to Congress beginning one (1) year
after effectivity. A comprehensive program review shall be conducted five
(5) years after effectivity to assess impact and guide legislative
enhancement.

     SEC. 11. Grievance Redress and Complaints Mechanism. The
DSWD shall establish a dedicated grievance redress system for the
National CHERISH Program, providing clear channels for complaints and
appeals related to denial of eligibility, delayed payments, inadequate
services, or misuse of funds. The mechanism shall be accessible via
hotline, online portal, and DSWD regional offices; shall allow remote filing
of complaints; and shall resolve grievances within thirty (30) days.
Caregivers who file complaints in good faith shall be protected against
retaliation.

      SEC. 12. Appropriations. The funds necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against current appropriations
of the DSWD and any available social program or contingency funds.
Thereafter, the amounts necessary for the continuous operation of the
National CHERISH Program shall be included in the Annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA), with a dedicated budget line under the DSWD.
The DOH, DepEd, TESDA, NCDA, PhilHealth, and relevant LGUs shall
likewise allocate funds within their respective budgets to fulfill their
mandates under this Act. The national government may provide financial
incentives to LGUs that champion local CHERISH program implementation.

       SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). Within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, as lead agency, in
consultation with the DOH, NCDA, DepEd, TESDA, Department of Finance
(DOF), Civil Service Commission (CSC), and representatives from
advocacy groups for persons with disabilities, shall promulgate the
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). The IRR shall detail eligibility
criteria, documentary requirements, subsidy determination formulas,
service delivery standards, inter-agency coordination mechanisms, and
timelines for phased implementation. The IRR shall be submitted to the
Committees on Social Services of both Houses of Congress for review.

      SEC 14. Harmonization with Existing Laws. This Act shall be read
in harmony with R.A. No. 7277 (Magna Carta for Persons with Disabilities),
R.A. No. 11861 (Expanded Solo Parents Welfare Act), R.A. No. 11223
(Universal Health Care Act), R.A. No. 11036 (Mental Health Act), and
House Bill No. 5088 (Family Caregiver Support and Welfare Act, if
enacted). Where a beneficiary qualifies under multiple programs, the
DSWD shall coordinate with concerned agencies to allow stacking of
complementary benefits or to ensure that the beneficiary receives the more
advantageous assistance, without unjust duplication. All laws, orders, and
issuances inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.

     SEC. 15. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


      SEC. 16. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
