                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The rapid advancement of digital technology has transformed the way economies
function, particularly in the movement of money and financial services. Digital payment
systems have become essential tools for improving financial efficiency, reducing
transaction costs, strengthening transparency, and expanding financial inclusion. For
governments, the adoption of digital payments also enhances accountability in the
handling of public funds and minimizes leakages associated with manual and cash-
based transactions.

      In the Philippines, while significant progress has been made in promoting digital
financial services, the adoption of digital payments across government institutions and
among merchants remains uneven. Many government collections and disbursements
are still conducted through traditional processes that are time-consuming, costly, and
vulnerable to inefficiencies. Likewise, many micro, small, and medium enterprises,
particularly in the informal sector, still lack access to safe and affordable digital
payment systems that would allow them to participate fully in the modern digital
economy.

       This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize the adoption of digital payments
in government transactions and promote their wider use among merchants and
enterprises. By mandating the use of secure and interoperable digital payment
systems for government disbursements and collections, the measure aims to improve
efficiency, transparency, and service delivery across public institutions. At the same
time, it encourages the development of a competitive and inclusive digital payments
ecosystem that enables merchants of all sizes, including micro and small enterprises,
to adopt digital payment capabilities without undue burden.

     The bill also emphasizes consumer protection, data privacy, and cybersecurity
safeguards to ensure that digital payment systems operate in a manner that protects
users while maintaining public trust. Furthermore, it supports interoperability among
payment platforms and promotes financial literacy and capacity-building initiatives to
assist both government entities and private merchants in transitioning toward digital
payment solutions.

country’s broader goal of building a more transparent, efficient, and inclusive financial
system, while accelerating the transition toward a resilient and cash-lite digital
economy.


     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                               AN ACT
     PROMOTING THE ADOPTION OF DIGITAL PAYMENTS FOR FINANCIAL
     TRANSACTIONS OF THE GOVERNMENT AND ALL MERCHANTS

         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1
 2        SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Electronic Payments
 3   for Merchants, Enterprises, and Nationwide Transactions (E-PAYMENT Act)."
 4
 5        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 6   recognize the vital role of information and communications technology, particularly
 7   financial technology, in nation-building, financial inclusion, and economic development.
 8
 9         To this end, the State shall promote the safe, secure, affordable, and efficient
10   use of digital payments in government and private sector transactions; institutionalize
11   digital payments in government collections and disbursements to enhance
12   transparency, accountability, and efficiency, encourage the inclusive and non-
13   burdensome adoption of digital payment capabilities by merchants; ensure consumer
14   protection, data privacy, and cybersecurity in accordance with law and Bangko Sentral
15   ng Pilipinas (BSP) regulations ;and foster a technology-neutral, competitive, and
16   interoperable digital payments ecosystem that promotes public trust.
17
18           SEC. 3. Objectives – This Act aims to increase the adoption and use of digital
19   payments in government and private sector transactions, reduce transaction costs,
20   inefficiencies, and leakage in the handling of public funds; promote interoperability and
21   fair competition among digital payment platforms; protect consumers, merchants, and
22   government entities from fraud, abuse and misuse of personal data; and support the
23   transition toward a cash-lite inclusive, and resilient digital economy.
24

 5          SEC. 5. Adoption of Digital Payments for Government. - Disbursements.
 6   All government entities, including but not limited to national government agencies
 7   (NGAs), government-owned or controlled corporations (GOCCS), foreign-based
 8   Philippine government units (LGUs), State Universities and Colleges (SUCs), and
 9   Local Universities and Colleges (LUCs) are hereby mandated to utilize safe, secure,
10   and efficient, digital payment systems for the disbursement of government funds,
11   including but not limited to salaries, wages, allowances, benefits, subsidies, and
12   financial assistance, subject to existing accounting and auditing rules.
13
14           Government entities may disburse funds directly to recipients transaction
15   accounts held with BSP-supervised financial institutions. For this purpose, said
16   government entities may use the automatic debit arrangement, interoperable
17   electronic fund transfer, or any other appropriate facility of its GSPSPS Accountable
18   officers of the government entities shall observe due diligence in ensuring the
19   accuracy of the recipient's or beneficiary's identity. All such transactions shall remain
20   transparent and subject to post-audit by the Commission on Audit (CDA).
21
22          Government entities shall be responsible for preparing the payment instruction
23   with the necessary details, including the recipient's or beneficiary's name, transaction
24   account details, and amount, among others, only after proper authorization in
25   accordance with established accounting and auditing rules and regulations.
26
27          In case of successful digital disbursement, there shall be a recovery mechanism
28   that will ensure the continuity of delivery of payment services: Provided, that in cases
29   of calamities or other emergencies, where payments through digital means may be
30   affected, non-digital pay-out channels may be explored.
31
32          SEC. 6. Adoption of Digital Payments for Government Collections. - All
33   government entities, including but not limited to NGAs, foreign-based Philippine
34   government agencies, GOCES, LGUS, SUCs, and LUCs, shall offer digital payment
35   options, in addition to cash, for the collection of taxes, fees, tolls, imposts, and other
36   revenues.
37
38           Government entities shall engage only secure, interoperable, and BSP-4
39   compliant digital payment solutions, and shall not be limited to government financial
40   institutions in selecting their PSPs.
41
42         SEC. 7. Interoperability and Open Finance. - The BSP accelerate the
43   adoption of interoperable digital payment systems, including the National Quick
44   Response (QR) Code Standard, to ensure seamless transactions across platforms.
45
46          The BSP, in coordination with relevant government agencies, shall likewise
47   promote the Open Finance Framework, subject to customer consent, data privacy,
48   and information security safeguards.

 1           SEC. 8. Digital Payment Capability of Merchants. – To accelerate the

19          SEC. 9. Consumer Protection, Data Privacy, and Cybersecurity. - All data
20   information and communications technology systems and networks used for digital
21   payments pursuant to the objectives of this Act shall be secured and protected at all
22   times. The DICT and the National Privacy Commission (NPC) shall, in consultation
23   with the BSP, define and prescribe the minimum information security and data
24   protection standards for compliance by PSPs covered by this Act that are not under
25   the oversight and regulatory authority of the BSP. Further, all digital payment systems
26   covered by this Act shall:
27
28       a) Comply with the Data Privacy Act of 2012, 8SP regulations, and NPC
29          standards;
30       b) Integrate security and privacy by design and by default;
31       c) Establish accessible dispute resolution and redress mechanisms; and
32       d) Implement appropriate safeguards against fraud, cybercrime, and
33          unauthorized transactions.
34
35          SEC. 10. Incentives and Support Measures. - to optimize the benefits of
36   digital payments, scale up financial inclusion, and promote sustainability, all
37   government entities shall prioritize the use of safe, secure, reliable, and efficient digital
38   payment in their financial transactions. Government entities may adopt incentive
39   frameworks to encourage digital payments, including reduced transaction fees for
40   micro-payments, administrative incentives, and financial and digital literacy programs.
41
42           Further, to enable the general public to adopt digital payments, micro-payment
43   transactions shall be subject to graduated pricing or be rendered free of any service
44   charge as determined by the BSP in consultation with the merchants and the payment
45   services industry, as may be represented by the BSP accredited payment system
46   management body. The DICT shall, consistent with its mandate and subject to
47   available resources, support initiatives that enhance connectivity necessary for the
48   effective implementation of this Act.

 1         SEC. 11. Transitory Provision. – Government entities shall ensure the orderly
 2   and progressive transition of their respective collection and disbursement procedures
 3   and policies to digital payments, in accordance with this Act and its implementing rules
 4   and regulations (IRR).

10           Digital collections shall likewise be implemented subject to a tiered and phased
11   transition framework, to be prescribed in the IRR, which shall provide differentiated
12   transition periods based on the readiness, capacity, and operational requirements of

14   (3) years from the effectivity of the IRR.

16          SEC. 12 Prohibited Acts. – The following acts shall be unlawful under this Act:
17
18          a) Willful refusal or deliberate failure, without lawful justification, of a public
19             officer or employee to implement digital payment policies, systems, or
20             directives duly issued pursuant to this Act or its implementing rules and
21             regulations, when such refusal or failure results in material prejudice to
22             public service, public funds, or government operations;
23          b) The knowing authorization, use, or maintenance of digital payment
24             arrangements for government transactions that are intended to conceal,
25             divert, delay, or misuse public funds, or to defeat transparency and
26             accountability requirements;
27          c) The intentional circumvention, suppression, or manipulation of audit trails,
28             records, reports, or data relating to digital payment transactions involving
29             public funds, for the purpose of avoiding post-audit, examination, or
30             verification by the Commission on Audit or other lawful authorities;
31          d) The knowing engagement, endorsement, or continued use of non-
32             compliant, fraudulent, or unregistered digital payment platforms or
33             arrangements for government transactions, despite the availability of
34             compliant and interoperable alternatives; or
35          e) The willful misrepresentation or false certification of compliance with
36             Interoperability, security, transparency, or regulatory requirements
37             prescribed under this Act or its implementing rules and regulations.

40   sanctions provided under Republic Act No. 11127, otherwise known as the "National

43   provision this Act, its implementing rules and regulations, or lawful directives or orders

28          SEC. 16 Interpretation. - Nothing in this Act shall be construed to exempt any
29   government funds in the possession of private entities from the constitutional authority
30   and duty of the COA to examine, audit, and settle all accounts pertaining to the
31   revenue and receipts of, and expenditures or uses of funds and property owned or
32   held in trust by, or pertaining to, the government. All digital payments involving
33   government transactions under this Act shall be subject to post-audit by the CDA, in
34   accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations. Accordingly, the COA may issue
35   or update such rules and guidelines as may be necessary to implement its audit
36   authority over transactions covered by this Act.
