                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to establish at least one (1) Provincial Soil Laboratory Facility in
every province of the Philippines under the Department of Agriculture (DA),
through the Bureau of Soils and Water Management (BSWM). It aims to provide
accessible, reliable, and comprehensive soil testing, analysis, and technical advisory
services for farmers and local government units.

        The agricultural sector continues to grapple with declining soil fertility,
inefficient fertilizer application, increasing production costs, and the growing
impacts of climate change. Although the BSWM currently provides laboratory
services, access remains limited, particularly in geographically isolated and
disadvantaged provinces. Many farmers rely on generalized fertilizer
recommendations or basic field-based Soil Test Kits that cannot adequately
determine nutrient deficiencies, soil organic matter, salinity, contamination, and
other critical indicators necessary for precision agriculture.

       The DA1 launched the country’s first Mobile Soil Laboratory in December
2024 to bring advanced soil testing closer to farming communities, acknowledging
that access to science-based soil analysis remains a challenge. The DA-BSWM’s
National Soil Health Program identifies accessible and accurate soil testing as a
cornerstone of sustainable agriculture and productivity. Additionally, the 2024 Status
of the World’s Soils2 reported that thirty-three percent (33%) of the global soils are
already moderately to highly degraded, threatening food production and
environmental sustainability. These realities highlight the urgent need to establish
permanent provincial laboratories capable of providing comprehensive diagnostics
to support informed fertilizer use, higher yields, and long-term food security.

micronutrient analysis, soil organic matter assessment, soil texture and structure
evaluation, salinity and contamination testing, and other diagnostic procedures
beyond conventional test kits. It integrates laboratory-generated data into fertilizer
assistance programs, agricultural extension services, and provincial development
planning through a mandatory Site-Specific Nutrient Management framework. The
bill also ensures affordable access to soil testing, prioritizes smallholder farmers,
promotes partnerships with state universities and research institutions, and
establishes a nationwide soil health monitoring system that will enable
evidence-based policymaking.

       Farmers have long carried the responsibility of feeding millions despite facing
declining soil quality and escalating production costs. Every harvest lost to poor soil
management weakens rural livelihoods, drives food prices upward, and threatens
national food security. Investing in provincial soil laboratories is an investment in
every farming family and Filipino household. Providing farmers with accurate soil
information empowers them to make better decisions, improve productivity, reduce
unnecessary expenses, and preserve the country’s most valuable agricultural
resource for future generations.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                         AN ACT
ESTABLISHING PROVINCIAL SOIL LABORATORY FACILITIES IN ALL
PROVINCES OF THE PHILIPPINES, STRENGTHENING SOIL HEALTH
MANAGEMENT AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Provincial Soil
Laboratory Development Act.”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
accelerate agricultural development by optimizing the use of resources such as soils
with the goal of attaining food security through science-based soil management. The
state shall ensure that farmers have accessible soil testing services to guide efficient
fertilizer use, improve soil fertility, and enhance climate-resilient agricultural
practices.

       SEC 3. Establishment of Provincial Soil Laboratory Facilities. - There shall be
established at least one (1) Provincial Soil Laboratory Facility in every province of
the Philippines to provide soil testing, analysis, and technical advisory services to
farmers and local government units.

      SEC. 4. Implementing Agency - The Department of Agriculture (DA), through
the Bureau of Soils and Water Management (BSWM), shall serve as the lead
implementing agency and shall:

          a. Establish, operate, and supervise provincial soil laboratories;

          b. Develop uniform soil testing standards and protocols;

          c. Provide technical assistance to provincial governments;

          d. Maintain quality assurance and laboratory accreditation systems;



                                           1
             e.​ Establish partnerships with state universities and colleges and research
         institutions; and

            f.​ Develop a nationwide soil health monitoring program.

         SEC. 5. Functions of Provincial Soil Laboratories. - Each provincial soil laboratory
shall:
            a.​ ﻿Conduct soil fertility and nutrient analysis;

            b.​ ﻿Provide crop-specific fertilizer and amendment recommendations;

            c.​ ﻿Support sustainable and climate-resilient farming practices;

            d.​ ﻿Assist in soil mapping and land suitability assessment;

            e.​ ﻿Provide training and extension services to farmers and agricultural
         technicians; and

            f.​ ﻿Generate soil data for agricultural planning and policy development.

       SEC. 6. Comprehensive Soil Testing and Advanced Analysis. - In addition to
standard soil testing services, Provincial Soil Laboratory Facilities established under
this Act shall be equipped and authorized to conduct comprehensive and advanced
soil analysis beyond the capabilities of conventional Soil Test Kits (STK).

         Such testing shall include, but shall not be limited to:

            a.​ ﻿Macro- and micronutrient analysis, including nitrogen, phosphorus,
         potassium, calcium, magnesium, sulfur, zinc, iron, manganese, copper, and
         boron;

            b.​ ﻿Soil organic matter and carbon content determination;

            c.​ Soil texture, structure, and water-holding capacity analysis;

            d.​ Salinity, sodicity, and cation exchange capacity assessment;

            e.​ Contaminant and heavy metal testing when necessary;

            f.​ Soil biological and microbial activity assessment, where applicable;
         and

            g.​ Crop- and site-specific nutrient management recommendations based
         on laboratory results.

     The Department of Agriculture, through the Bureau of Soils and Water
Management, shall ensure that provincial laboratories are provided with



                                              2
appropriate equipment, technical standards, and trained personnel to enable more
exhaustive and science-based soil evaluation compared to field-based Soil Test Kits.

       Results generated from comprehensive testing shall serve as the basis for
precision agriculture programs, fertilizer optimization initiatives, and sustainable
land management policies.

       SEC. 7. Site-Specific Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Optimization. - The
Department of Agriculture (DA), through the Bureau of Soils and Water
Management
(BSWM), shall adopt and implement a Site-Specific Nutrient Management (SSNM)
Framework utilizing data generated from Provincial Soil Laboratory Facilities
established under this Act. Accordingly:

         a.​ ﻿Fertilizer recommendations issued to farmers shall, as far as
      practicable, be based on laboratory-generated soil analysis and localized field
      conditions rather than uniform or blanket fertilizer application rates;

          b.​ ﻿Provincial Soil Laboratories shall generate crop-specific and
      location-specific nutrient management advisories to guide farmers in the
      efficient and sustainable use of fertilizers and soil amendments;

          c.​ ﻿The DA shall progressively integrate soil laboratory results into
      existing agricultural support programs, including fertilizer assistance, crop
      production programs, and extension services;

         d.​ ﻿Agricultural extension workers and local government unit personnel
      shall be trained to interpret laboratory results and assist farmers in adopting
      precision and climate-resilient nutrient management practices; and

          e.​ ﻿The DA shall promote reduced input waste, improved soil health,
      increased farm productivity, and minimized environmental impact through
      science-based fertilizer optimization.

       SEC. 8. Accessibility and Farmer Support. - Soil testing services shall be made
affordable and accessible, with subsidized rates prioritized for smallholder farmers,
agrarian reform beneficiaries, agricultural cooperatives, and farmers in
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

       Mobile soil testing services may be deployed to remote municipalities and
island provinces.

      SEC. 9. Personnel and Staffing. - The Department of Agriculture, in
coordination with provincial governments, shall ensure the hiring, training, and
continuous capacity development of soil scientists, laboratory technicians, and
support personnel necessary for effective operations.

        SEC. 10. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department of
Agriculture. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DA and BSWM, in consultation with concerned agencies
and stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations.

       SEC. 12. Separability Clause. - If, for any reason, any part or provision of this
Act shall be declared as unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining parts or
provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders
and rules and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act
are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 14. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
