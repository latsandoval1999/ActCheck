   CREATING THE HEALTH EMERGENCY AUXILIARY REINFORCEMENT
   TEAM UNDER THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH AND APPROPRIATING
                       FUNDS THEREFOR

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This measure seeks to establish the Health Emergency Auxiliary
Reinforcement Team (HEART) under the Department of Health as a pool of
trained and qualified health professionals, allied health workers, students,
retirees, and other volunteers who may be mobilized to assist government health
facilities during disasters, disease outbreaks, pandemics, and other public
health emergencies. The proposed HEART complements, rather than replaces,
the regular health workforce by providing surge capacity whenever existing
personnel become insufficient.

       The proposal is firmly anchored on the Constitution, which mandates the
State to protect and promote the people's right to health and adopt an integrated
and comprehensive approach to health development.1 It likewise supports the
objectives of Republic Act No. 11223, or the Universal Health Care Act, which
recognizes the importance of strengthening the health workforce as an essential
building block of an effective healthcare system.2 Furthermore, the bill aligns
with Republic Act No. 11332 by enhancing the government's capacity to respond

       Beyond domestic law, the measure advances the Philippines' commitment

(Good Health and Well-being), which calls on States to strengthen health systems
and improve preparedness for health risks and emergencies.4 By
institutionalizing a trained reserve force and establishing clear mechanisms for
recruitment, training, mobilization, protection, and coordination, the bill
ensures that the country is better prepared to respond swiftly and effectively to
future health crises while fostering the Filipino spirit of Bayanihan through
organized public service.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
  CREATING THE HEALTH EMERGENCY AUXILIARY REINFORCEMENT
  TEAM UNDER THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH AND APPROPRIATING
                      FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Health Emergency
Auxiliary Reinforcement Team (HEART) Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the people's right to health by maintaining a strong and resilient
healthcare system capable of responding effectively to public health emergencies.
Toward this end, the State shall strengthen its health workforce by mobilizing
skilled health professionals and volunteers through the Health Emergency
Auxiliary Reinforcement Team, fostering the spirit of Bayanihan and ensuring
adequate human resources during disasters, health crises, and other public
health emergencies.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

   1. Allied health professionals refer to professions who are involved with the
      delivery of health or related services such as the identification, evaluation
      and prevention of diseases and disorders, dietary and nutrition services,
      and rehabilitation and health systems management. Allied health
      professionals include, among others, dental hygienists, diagnostic medical
      sonographers, dietitians, medical technologists, occupational therapists,
   physical therapists, radiographers, respiratory therapists, and speech-
   language pathologists;

2. Bayanihan refers to the value of communal unity through helping others
   to achieve a certain goal without expecting anything in return;

3. Deployment refers to the actual movement of workforce to the assigned
   area within a prescribed period;

4. Health Emergency Auxiliary Reinforcement Team refers to a group of
   individual volunteers who are in the field of medicine, nursing, medical
   technology, and other allied health-related fields who are ready to be
   deployed to complement the health workforce in the event of a public
   health emergency and health threat;

5. Health threat refers to a condition, agent, or incident that greatly impacts
   health that leads to disease, accident, injury, and loss of life;

6. Mobilization refers to the process and procedures for activating,
   assembling, and transporting the needed health workforce to respond to a
   public health emergency and health threat;

7. Mobilization center refers to the establishment where the Health
   Emergency Auxiliary Reinforcement Team shall report upon receipt of the
   order for mobilization, coordinate their mission order and mobilization-
   related issues and concerns, and submit their report upon receipt of the
   order for demobilization;

8. Public health emergency refers to an occurrence that poses an imminent
   threat of an illness or health condition as defined in Republic Act No.
   11332, otherwise known as the Mandatory Reporting of Notifiable Diseases
   and Health Events of Public Health Concern Act, that:

      a. Is caused by any of the following:
            i. Bioterrorism;
           ii. Appearance of a novel or previously controlled or eradicated
               infectious agent or biological toxin;
          iii. A natural disaster;
          iv. A chemical attack or accidental release;
           v.  A nuclear attack or accident; or
          vi.  An attack or accidental release of radioactive materials; and

      b. Poses a high probability of any of the following:
          i. A large number of deaths in the affected population;
             ii.  A large number of serious injuries or long-term disabilities in
                  the affected population;
            iii. Widespread exposure to an infectious or toxic agent that poses
                  a significant risk of substantial harm to a large number of
                  people in the affected population;
             iv. International exposure to an infectious or toxic agent that
                  poses a significant risk to the health of citizens; or
              v.  Trade and travel restrictions
   9. Volunteers refer to those who freely register to be members of the HEART.

SECTION 4. Health Emergency Auxiliary Reinforcement Team. – There is
hereby established a HEART under the Department of Health (DOH) to augment
the health workforce in times of disasters, public health emergencies, and health
threats. The HEART shall be composed of the following individuals who shall
voluntarily register with the DOH:

   1. Licensed professionals in the fields of medicine, nursing, medical
      technology, and other allied health fields, including those who are retired
      and those who are no longer practicing their professions in a health facility
      setting. All health and allied health professional organizations may be
      tapped for the engagement of professionals who are not affiliated with any
      healthcare facility;
   2. Graduates of medicine and allied health courses and medical students
      who have completed a one-year medical internship and who have not yet
      been issued licenses to practice their respective professions, but due to
      the need for their services may be issued a limited and special
      authorization to render medical services during public health emergencies
      or health threats pursuant to Republic Act No. 2382, otherwise known as
      the Medical Act of 1959 or as may be amended hereafter; and
   3. Other health support workforce, including barangay health workers,
      whose services are necessary in times of disasters, public health
      emergencies, and health threats.

SECTION 5. The Health Emergency Auxiliary Reinforcement Team
Promotion Program. – There is hereby established the Health Emergency
Auxiliary Reinforcement Team Promotion Program, which shall refer to the
comprehensive set of objectives, targets, strategies and activities for the
promotion, recruitment and selection of a reserve force of highly skilled and
health-oriented professionals and volunteers. It shall include, but not be limited
to:
    1. Provision of initiatives that underscore the importance of volunteerism to
       improve community health and safety;
   2. Utilization of a medical reserves pool for emergency response and
      preparedness initiatives; and
   3. Building partnerships to ensure sustainability of the HEART and its
      capacity to meet healthcare system demands during emergencies.

SECTION 6. Creation of the HEART Board. – A HEART Board, hereinafter
referred to as the Board, is hereby created to ensure efficient and effective
mobilization of the HEART. It shall be chaired by the Department of Health (DOH)
and composed of representatives of the following, as members:

   1. Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);

   2. Department of National Defense (DND);

   3. Commission on Higher Education (CHED);

   4. Professional Regulation Commission (PRC);

   5. Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP);

   6. Office of Civil Defense (OCD); and

   7. One representative      from   a   non-governmental     health   professional
      organization.

The members of the Board shall designate a representative, with a rank not lower
than Assistant Secretary or its equivalent, to represent their respective offices in
the Board; provided that the representative must be fully authorized to decide
on behalf of the member agency.

The AFP shall designate a representative from the Office of the Surgeon General
with a rank not lower than Colonel as its Board representative.

The representative from a non-governmental health professional organization
shall have a two (2)-year term of office. The Board shall select the representative
based on the guidelines it shall promulgate: Provided, that the non-governmental
health organization is national in scope and has a good track record in providing
emergency health services.

The Board shall establish close coordination with the National Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Council and the Philippine National Volunteer
Service Coordinating Agency. It may call upon any government office or
instrumentality, including government-owned or controlled corporations and
local government units (LGUs), as necessary. The Board may likewise closely
coordinate and collaborate with non-governmental health organizations and the
private sector, especially those with actual programs in the delivery of emergency
health services.

SECTION 7. Powers and Functions of the Board. – The Board shall have the
following powers and functions:

   1. Define and develop the organization, management, mobilization,
      demobilization, and reporting mechanisms, policies, and guidelines for the
      HEART;

   2. Oversee the effective and efficient functioning of the HEART in responding
      to public health emergencies and health threats;

   3. Coordinate with concerned national government agencies and other
      stakeholders in responding to public health emergencies and health
      threats; and

   4. Conduct regular meetings every quarter or as often as may be necessary
      in times of disasters, during public health emergencies, or in the
      occurrence of health threats.

SECTION 8. Creation of a HEART Unit. – A HEART Unit shall be created by
the DOH which shall serve as the secretariat of the Board. It shall oversee
program implementation, including the maintenance of the database or
information system of the HEART.

The DOH shall determine the organizational structure and staffing pattern of the
HEART unit subject to the evaluation and approval of the Department of Budget
and Management (DBM) and in accordance with the civil service laws, rules and
regulations.

The regional counterparts of the HEART Unit shall likewise be allocated
additional staff and personnel to ensure the grassroots implementation of this
Act.

All LGUs shall ensure that a HEART Unit shall be operationalized under their
respective Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office, which shall
act as mobilization centers in times of disasters, public health emergencies, or
health threats.

SECTION 9. Registration and Training. – The Board shall prescribe a
comprehensive guideline for the recruitment and selection of a HEART volunteer.

The Board shall also prescribe a training program through written, practical, and
simulation activities on various aspects of health emergency management and
response in different health emergency scenarios. To this end, all volunteers
must undergo:

   1. Basic training on disaster and health emergency response; and

   2. Skill enhancement activities with relevant agencies in both public and
      private sectors.

Completion of the basic training or acceptable substitute or equivalent
certification shall be a requisite for admission to the HEART.

SECTION 10. Registry and Accounting of Members. – HEART Volunteers shall
be issued with individual registration numbers which shall serve as their
identification during deployment.

A registry or database that contains the names of the volunteers, their
registration numbers, address, contact details, and other personal information
as may be necessary, shall be maintained and regularly updated in accordance
with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the Data Privacy Act.

The HEART registry or database shall be integrated or linked to existing registries
such as the National Health Workforce Registry pursuant to Republic Act No.
11223, otherwise known as the Universal Health Care Act, Section 25 (c).

All volunteers covered under this Act are required to update their addresses and
contact details on file through the registry system or database as necessary.

SECTION 11. Mobilization. – The Board shall approve the prompt mobilization
of the HEART upon receipt of a request from municipal and city mayors and
provincial governors in connection with a public health emergency, threats to
public health, or a disaster, whether of local or national scale. The HEART may
be mobilized partially or in full, as may be necessary.

The Board shall promulgate a mechanism that shall ensure efficient
implementation of deployment, assignment areas, deployment periods, how
deployment orders are communicated to each volunteer, and to which
mobilization center the volunteers will report, taking into consideration Section
13 on “Accreditation, Mobilization, and Protection of Disaster Volunteers and
National Service Reserve Corps, CSOs, and the Private Sector” of Republic Act
No. 10121, otherwise known as the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Act of 2010.

SECTION 12. Deployment Order. – The President of the Philippines, upon the
recommendation of the HEART Board through a resolution, may order the
nationwide mobilization of the HEART to augment the health workforce in case
of a declaration of a state of public health emergency, state of calamity, and other
threats to public health.

Orders of deployment sent to the volunteers’ addresses and through the contact
details on file in the registry system or database shall be sufficient notice for
purposes of deployment.

SECTION 13. Mobilization Centers. – Mobilization Centers shall be established
in each province as needed, where volunteers shall report once deployment is
ordered.

A mobilization center may be any establishment that can adequately house the
volunteers, their equipment and supplies during the period of deployment,
including a multi-purpose hall, gymnasium, and other similar structures.

The Board shall, in consultation with the local executives, prescribe the location
of the mobilization centers. The concerned municipal and city mayors and
provincial governors shall disseminate information on the location of
mobilization centers to the widest extent possible.

SECTION 14. Essential Emergency Commodities. – The Board shall ensure
that the minimum essential individual and organizational commodities and
medical equipment and supplies shall be procured, stored, and managed to
enhance the rapid transition to readiness required for deployment of HEART
teams in the shortest possible time.

The Board shall ensure and maintain its capacity to scale up the procurement
of the equipment and supplies as needed during the period of deployment.

SECTION 15. Incentives. – In recognition and appreciation of the HEART
volunteers’ service rendered during the period of actual mobilization,
implementing agencies may provide volunteers with an allowance as may be
determined by the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the
Department of Budget and Management, subject to availability of funds, and
other existing civil service, budgeting, accounting and auditing rules and
regulations. Notwithstanding the foregoing, nothing in this Act shall be
construed as creating an employer-employee relationship between the
government and HEART volunteers.

SECTION 16. Legal Liability. – No volunteer shall be held liable for the death
or injury to any person or for the loss of, or damage to the property of any person
where such death, injury, loss, or damage was proximately caused by the
circumstance of an actual public health emergency or its subsequent conditions.
This Section shall not preclude liability for civil damages because of gross
negligence, recklessness, or willful misconduct.

SECTION 17. Public Health Emergency Insurance. – The DOH is mandated
to secure an insurance policy that would insure HEART volunteers from injury,
death, damage to, or loss of property, and for any legal liability asserted against
or incurred by the volunteer arising from the lawful performance of their duty.

SECTION 18. Termination of Deployment or Demobilization. – Upon the
declaration of the President, or as determined by the Board that the need for the
HEART is no longer necessary, or upon the expiration of the deployment or end
of mission, there being no approved extension thereof, volunteers who are
deployed pursuant to the declaration of a public calamity, a public health
emergency or health threat shall be discharged from the performance of HEART
duties.

Upon order of the demobilization of the HEART, the deployed volunteers shall be
reverted to inactive status. Mobilization Centers shall ensure reports on the
mission are duly submitted, and the certificate of services rendered pertaining
to the deployment is promptly issued.

SECTION 19. Annual Report. – The HEART Unit shall prepare and submit an
annual report to the Office of the President, through the DOH Secretary,
containing the list of accomplishments, status of the operations, and program
implementation of the HEART.

SECTION 20. Appropriations. – The funds necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the available funds of the
implementing agencies comprising the HEART Board. Thereafter, the funding
shall be included in the budget of the DOH under the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SECTION 21. Separability. If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

SECTION 21. Repeal. All orders, issuances, rules and regulations or parts
thereof that are contrary to, or inconsistent with, the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, modified or amended accordingly. This Act is consistent with
and shall not supplant the other streamlining initiatives of the government
covered by previously issued executive orders.

SECTION 22. Effectivity. Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the IRR, this Act
shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in the Official Gazette or in a
newspaper of general circulation.
