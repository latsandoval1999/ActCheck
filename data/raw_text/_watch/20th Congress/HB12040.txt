EXPLANATORY NOTE

In 2022, the Philippines enacted Republic Act No. 11934, otherwise known as
the Sim Card Registration Act, a landmark measure aimed at curbing cybercrime,
eliminating anonymous mobile usage, and instilling accountability among mobile
phone users and Public Telecommunications Entities. Despite this legislative
milestone, malicious actors have exploited loopholes—utilizing fraudulent
identification, purchasing pre-registered SIMs under fictitious names, or illicitly
transferring registered SIMs to bypass the law.’ A recent global anti-scam report
highlighted a 78.4% surge in scam calls in the country by October 2025, logging tens
of thousands of fraudulent calls and SMS phishing attempts.

To address this escalating threat, this bill institutes a Calling Name Presentation
(CNAP) system. The CNAP system requires telecommunication operators to
automatically transmit and display the registered name of the caller on the recipient's
device during call routing.

Crucially, this measure balances the State’s duty to protect public welfare with
the fundamental right to privacy. The bill mandates CNAP broadcasting exclusively for
juridical entities, registered telemarketers, bulk callers, and government agencies,
thereby stripping fraud networks and spam call centers of their shield of anonymity.
Conversely, recognizing the privacy rights of private citizens under Republic Act No.
10173 or the Data Privacy Act of 2012, the bill strictly requires the explicit, digital opt-
in consent of private individual subscribers before their registered names are
broadcasted.

By empowering consumers with real-time caller identification, equipping law
enforcement with superior tracing mechanisms, and instituting a cost-recovery
framework to prevent unfunded mandates on telecommunications providers, this bill
provides a pragmatic, rights-based solution to cybercrime.

SECTION 7. Device Agnostic Delivery and Network Upgrades. — All PTEs
operating in the Philippines are hereby mandated to upgrade their immediate network
nodes to ensure that CNAP data is delivered device-agnostically. The transmission of
the calling name shall occur at the network level during call set-up and must be
readable across all receiving mobile devices, including non-smartphones, without
requiring active internet connectivity or third-party applications.

SECTION 8. Cost Recovery Mechanism. — The National Telecommunications
Commission (NTC) shall formulate guidelines allowing PTEs to establish a cost-
recovery framework. This framework may include the imposition of premium service
fees upon juridical entities and telemarketers availing of the verified “Preferred Name”
CNAP display to offset the capital expenditure of inter-service provider database
lookups and network latency mitigation.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT), in coordination with the National Telecommunications
Commission (NTC), the National Privacy Commission (NPC), and the
telecommunications industry, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for
the effective implementation of this Act, specifically establishing the technical
standards for CNAP interoperability between competing PTE networks.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. — |f any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue
in full force and effect.

SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, administrative
orders, rules, and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 12. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
