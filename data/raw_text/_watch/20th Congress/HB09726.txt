                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Out-of-school youth (OSY) remain one of the most vulnerable yet
underutilized sectors in Philippine society. Despite existing programs under
the Alternative Learning System (ALS), Technical-Vocational Education and
Training (TVET), and various social protection mechanisms, significant gaps
persist in coordination, accessibility, and long-term reintegration. Many OSYs
continue to face systemic barriers such as poverty, lack of access to education,
unemployment, social exclusion, and exposure to exploitation and abuse.
Current interventions are largely fragmented across agencies, resulting in
duplication of efforts, inefficient delivery of services, and limited measurable
outcomes. More importantly, there is no comprehensive rights-based
framework that explicitly recognizes OSYs as a distinct sector entitled to
targeted protection, opportunities, and government support.

       This proposed measure seeks to address these gaps by institutionalizing
a Magna Carta of the Out-of-School Youth, anchored on human rights,
substantive equality, and inclusive development. It establishes a unified
policy framework that consolidates government efforts and mandates a
whole-of-government and multi-stakeholder approach toward the
empowerment of OSYs. The bill provides for the recognition and protection of
the rights of OSYs, including protection from discrimination, violence, and
exploitation; guaranteed access to education through ALS, TVET, and
pathways to formal tertiary education; expanded employment,
entrepreneurship, and livelihood opportunities through coordinated
programs of the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), Technical



                                                                                  1
Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and Department of
Trade and Industry (DTI); comprehensive social protection measures,
including health, insurance, and social services; special interventions for OSYs
in especially difficult circumstances; strengthened institutional mechanisms,
including a national database, localized profiling, and coordinated
implementation across national and local government units; and clear
accountability measures and penalties for violations of the rights of OSYs.

       By investing in the development of OSYs, the State not only fulfills its
constitutional mandate to promote social justice and human dignity, but also
unlocks a critical segment of the population that can significantly contribute to
national development. This measure ensures that no young Filipino is left
behind—not by circumstance, not by system failure, and not by lack of
opportunity. In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

 1                                        CHAPTER I
 2                                GENERAL PROVISIONS

 3             SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta
 4      of the Out-of-School Youth.”

 5             SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Article II, Section 11 of the Constitution
 6      provides that the State values the dignity of every human person and guarantees
 7      full respect for human rights. Pursuant to the aforementioned mandate, the State
 8      recognizes the rights of the out-of-school youth (OSY) to social protection and to
 9      the development of their full potential as partners in nation-building.
10             SEC. 3. Objectives. – The objectives of this Act are as follows:
11             a. To recognize, protect, and promote the rights of the OSYs;
12             b. To empower and encourage the OSYs to contribute to nation-building;




                                                                                        1
 1   c. To give full support to the improvement of the total well-being of the
 2      OSYs by providing learning and employment opportunities as well as
 3      the needed social services and interventions; and
 4   d. To recognize the important role of the private sector in improving the
 5      welfare of the OSY and to actively seek their partnership.

 7                                CHAPTER II
 8                         DEFINITION OF TERMS

 9   SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
10   a. Alternative Learning System (ALS) refers to a parallel learning system in
11      the Philippines that provides a practical option to the existing formal
12      instruction and equivalent pathways to basic education. It includes both
13      the non-formal and informal sources of knowledge and skills;
14   b. Local Social Welfare and Development Officer (LSWDO) refers to the
15      officer designated as such under the Social Welfare and Development
16      Offices in the local government unit (LGU);
17   c. Out-of-school youth refers to a member of the population aged 15 to 30
18      years old who is currently out of school, not gainfully employed, and
19      has not finished secondary or college education;
20   d. Social protection refers to policies and programs that seek to reduce
21      poverty and vulnerability to risks and enhance the social status and
22      rights of all OSY by promoting livelihood and employment
23      opportunities, including its major components such as labor market
24      interventions, social insurance, social welfare, and social safety nets;
25   e. Substantive equality refers to the fundamental aspect of human rights
26      law that is concerned with equitable outcomes and equal opportunities
27      for the disadvantaged and marginalized groups in society, including
28      protection against discrimination, marginalization, unequal distribution
29      of opportunities, and limited access to goods and services;




                                                                                   2
 1          f. Technical-Vocational Education and Training (TVET) refers to the
 2             education or training process designed at post-secondary and lower
 3             tertiary levels, officially recognized as non-degree programs aimed at
 4             preparing technicians, paraprofessionals and other categories of middle-
 5             level workers by providing them with a broad range of general
 6             education, theoretical, scientific and technological studies, and related
 7             job skills training; and
 8          g. TVET graduate refers to a student or trainee who has completed the
 9             requirements set for a Technical Education and Skills Development
10             Authority (TESDA)-registered TVET course or program.

12                                       CHAPTER III
13                  DUTIES RELATED TO THE HUMAN RIGHTS OF
14                                  OUT-OF-SCHOOL YOUTH

15          SEC. 5. State as the Primary Duty-Bearer. – The State shall, through the
16   National Youth Commission (NYC), Commission on Human Rights, and other
17   concerned youth-serving agencies, be guided by progressive developments on the
18   aspect of human rights of the OSY under international law and shall endeavor to
19   formulate policies, laws, and other regulatory measures to fulfill these duties. It
20   shall institute programs that will carry out the objectives of this Act and promote:
21          a. The protection of the rights of the OSYs against discrimination of any
22             kind by private corporations, entities, individuals, and from any
23             institution, public or private; and
24          b. The promotion of substantive equality on the rights of the OSY in all
25             aspects of growth and development.
26          SEC. 6. Duties of the State Agencies and Instrumentalities. – The duties
27   of the State as stated in Section 5 of this Act shall extend to all state agencies,
28   offices, and instrumentalities at all levels of government, including government-
29   owned and controlled corporations, subject to the Constitution and pertinent laws,
30   policies, and administrative guidelines that define specific duties of concerned
31   state agencies and entities.

 2                        RIGHTS AND EMPOWERMENT

 3          SEC. 7. Human Rights of Out-of-School Youth. – An OSY shall enjoy,
 4   without discrimination, all rights provided for in the Constitution and those rights
 5   recognized under international instruments duly ratified by the Philippines.
 6          SEC. 8. Protection from Violence. – The State shall ensure that all OSYs
 7   shall be protected from all forms of violence, physical abuse, sexual exploitation,
 8   and human trafficking as provided for under existing laws. The Department of
 9   Justice, Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), and other
10   concerned agencies shall gove priority to the defense and protection of the OSYs
11   and help them in attaining justice and social equality.
12          SEC. 9. Equal Treatment Before the Law. – The The NYC and other
13   youth-serving agencies shall take active steps to review and, when necessary,
14   submit proposals to amend or repeal existing laws that are discriminatory to the
15   OSYs, and submit legislative proposals, as far as practicable, at the start of every
16   Congress.
17          SEC. 10. Representation of Out-of-School Youth Issues and Concerns in
18   Media, Film, and Other Platforms. – The NYC and other concerned agencies
19   shallformulate policies and programs for the advancement of the OSYs in
20   collaboration with government and       non-government       organization      (NGO)
21   media-related organizations.
22          For this purpose, the State shall ensure the allocation of resources for the
23   production, publication, dissemination, and airing of all forms of information
24   materials on the rights of the OSYs and the various services and benefits provided
25   to them under this Act.
26          SEC. 11. Recognition and Preservation of Cultural Identity and Integrity.
27   – The rights of Moro and indigenous OSYs to practice, promote, protect, and
28   preserve their own culture, traditions, and institutions and to consider these rights

 1   in the formulation and implementation of national policies and programs shall be
 2   upheld. To this end, the National Commission on Muslim Filipinos and the
 3   National Commission on Indigenous Peoples shall, in consultation with the sectors
 4   concerned, protect their rights, indigenous knowledge system and practices,
 5   traditional livelihood, and other cultural manifestations, recommend legislation
 6   and other appropriate measures to promote and to respect them: Provided, That
 7   these cultural systems and practices do not discriminate against other OSYs who
 8   do not belong to the Muslim or Indigenous Peoples.
 9          SEC. 12. Right to Decent Work. – The          Department      of   Labor   and
10   Employment (DOLE) shall, in coordination with TESDA, ensure decent work
11   standards for OSYs who have graduated from TVET courses, including the
12   following minimum guarantees:
13          a. Local job generation and employment, economic opportunities, and

17             in wages, conditions of work, and employment opportunities in host
18             countries; and
19          c. Opportunities for work shall be productive and fairly remunerative as
20             family living wage, security of tenure in the workplace, and better
21             prospects for personal development and social integration.

 8                                   CHAPTER VI
 9                                    EDUCATION

10         SEC. 15. Technical Vocational Education. – It shall be the duty of the
11   State to provide technical or vocational education to the OSYs. The course shall be
12   provided and managed by TESDA subject to its rules and regulations: Provided,
13   That an officer of the TESDA shall be assigned as a TESDA adviser who shall
14   monitor the progress and development of the OSYs in the chosen technical
15   vocational course.
16         SEC. 16. Alternative Learning System. – An ALS of education shall be
17   made available to OSYs.
18         For OSYs who are under the care and custody of a home or an institution
19   managed or accredited by the DSWD, the latter shall coordinate with the DepEd
20   for the provision of an ALS within its premises.
21         SEC. 17. Promotion to Formal Tertiary Education. – The LSWDO may
22   recommend an OSY to be a recipient of a scholarship grant from the CHED under
23   either Republic Act No. 10931, otherwise known as the “Universal Access to
24   Quality Tertiary Education Act” or Republic Act No. 10687, otherwise known as
25   the “Unified Student Financial Assistance System for Tertiary Education
26   (UniFAST) Act.”

 1          An OSY who is a recipient of any CHED scholarship grant shall execute a
 2   scholarship bond, subject to the condition of rendering at least two (2) years of

 4          SEC. 18. Implementation. – The DTI shall, in coordination with TESDA
 5   and other relevant government agencies, conduct training programs to provide the
 6   OSYs     with   the   knowledge,     skills,   and   motivation    to    engage     in
 7   entrepreneurial endeavors in a variety of settings. These programs shall include
 8   small business management, new approaches to innovation, developing creativity
 9   or the introduction of new products or services to the markets and providing aid to

11          Both public and private higher education institutions shall set a mínimum

15   shall provide formal, non-formal, informal, technical and vocational education
16   services to the OSYs free of charge. It shall likewise provide materials,
17   instruments, and tools needed to carry out and complete said programs.

 1          SEC. 24. Punishable Acts and Penalties. – The following acts shall be
 2   deemed violations of this Act with the following corresponding penalties: (1)
 3   Discrimination against OSYs by private corporations, entities, and individuals; (2)
 4   Discrimination in wages, condition of work, and employment opportunities; and
 5   (3) Non-compliance with the mandatory coverage by employers of health,
 6   accident, and life insurance.
 7          a. For the first violation, imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but
 8             not more than two (2) years and a fine of not less than Fifty thousand
 9             pesos (P50,000.00) but not more than One hundred thousand pesos
10             (P100,000.00);
11          b. For any subsequent violation, imprisonment of not less than two (2)
12             years but not more than six (6) years and a fine of not less than One
13             hundred thousand pesos (P100,000.00) but not more than Two hundred
14             thousand pesos (P200,000.00);
15          c. For any person who avails of any of the benefits and privileges under
16             this Act but who is not qualified to avail of the same, imprisonment of
17             not less than six (6) months and a fine of not less than Fifty thousand
18             pesos (P50,000.00) but not more than One hundred thousand pesos
19             (P100,000.00); and
20          d. If the offender is a corporation, organization, or any similar entity, the
21             officials thereof directly involved in the violation or abuse shall be held
22             liable.
23
24             If the offender is an alien or a foreigner, the person shall be deported
25             immediately upon service of sentence without further proceedings.
