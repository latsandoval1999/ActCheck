   AN ACT PROMOTING URBAN FLOOD RESILIENCE AND RAINWATER
HARVESTING THROUGH THE ADOPTION OF A NATIONAL URBAN DRAINAGE
    AND RUNOFF MANAGEMENT CODE, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Urban flooding has become one of the most pressing public safety, economic,
and environmental concerns in the Philippines. Typhoons, monsoon rains, and
localized thunderstorms regularly overwhelm drainage systems, causing property
damage, business disruptions, displacement of families, and loss of life. In Metro
Manila alone, flood-related incidents cause an estimated ₱5 billion in annual economic
losses.

       The situation is aggravated by rapid urbanization, which has replaced natural
ground cover with impermeable surfaces such as concrete and asphalt. These
changes increase surface runoff while reducing natural infiltration, overwhelming
outdated drainage networks—many of which were designed for rainfall patterns that
no longer reflect current climate conditions. Improper solid waste disposal further clogs
waterways, reducing flow capacity.

       Current legal provisions addressing drainage and water management are
scattered across various statutes, such as the Local Government Code, the Water
Code of the Philippines, and certain environmental regulations. However, there is no
single, binding national standard for urban runoff management that integrates both
flood mitigation and water conservation.
         The proposed Urban Flood Resilience and Rainwater Harvesting Act seeks to
fill this gap by mandating the creation of a National Urban Drainage and Runoff
Management Code, to be developed by the Department of Public Works and
Highways (DPWH) in coordination with the Department of Environment and Natural
Resources (DENR), the Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development
(DHSUD), and other agencies.

       The measure requires the installation of on-site detention and retention
systems, permeable paving, and rainwater catchment facilities in all new
developments above a certain threshold, while promoting retrofitting in older districts.
Rainwater harvesting will serve the dual purpose of reducing peak runoff and
conserving water for non-potable uses such as irrigation, fire suppression, cooling,
and cleaning.

       Through uniform technical standards, strict compliance timelines for LGUs, and
capacity-building programs, this measure will provide a clear national framework to
address urban flooding while also promoting water sustainability. Incentives will be
available for LGUs and private developers that go beyond compliance through
innovative, climate-resilient solutions.

       By passing this bill, Congress will directly address urban flood risks, protect
lives and livelihoods, and help achieve the country’s climate adaptation and
sustainable development goals.

       In view of the urgent need to institutionalize a coherent and enforceable
framework for stormwater and rainwater management in urban areas nationwide, the
swift passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT PROMOTING URBAN FLOOD RESILIENCE AND RAINWATER
HARVESTING THROUGH THE ADOPTION OF A NATIONAL URBAN DRAINAGE
    AND RUNOFF MANAGEMENT CODE, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     Section 1. Title. – This legislative measure shall be known and cited as the
“Urban Flood Resilience and Rainwater Harvesting Act.”

      Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
safeguard the lives, health, and property of its citizens by strengthening the resilience
of urban communities to flooding, while promoting the sustainable use and
conservation of water resources. In pursuit of this policy, the State shall adopt and
implement a uniform set of technical standards and guidelines for the planning, design,
construction, operation, and maintenance of urban drainage and runoff management
systems.

      These standards shall incorporate climate-resilient infrastructure solutions,
mandate the installation of rainwater harvesting facilities, require the use of permeable
surfaces and green infrastructure, and ensure the retrofitting of outdated drainage
systems, particularly in high-risk urban districts.

       The State shall work in close partnership with local government units (LGUs),
relevant government agencies, civil society, and the private sector to achieve these
objectives.

       Sec. 3. Statement of Objectives. – This Act is enacted to achieve the following
objectives:
   1. To establish a binding and comprehensive National Urban Drainage and Runoff
      Management Code applicable to all urbanized areas, highly urbanized cities,
      component cities, and municipalities with high-density settlements throughout
      the country;
   2. To prescribe the mandatory incorporation of on-site detention and retention
      systems, permeable pavements, rain gardens, bioswales, and other forms of
      green infrastructure in all new developments and major renovations;
   3. To require the installation of rainwater catchment facilities in all newly
      constructed buildings, subdivisions, commercial complexes, industrial facilities,
      and government infrastructure projects;
   4. To mandate the retrofitting of drainage utilities and related infrastructure in older
      urban districts so that they meet modern engineering standards for climate
      resilience;
   5. To ensure that LGUs fully comply with the technical specifications to be
      promulgated by the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) under
      the National Urban Drainage and Runoff Management Code; and
   6. To integrate flood risk reduction strategies and water conservation measures
      into all urban development planning and implementation efforts.

      Sec. 4. Scope and Application. – The provisions of this Act shall apply to all
urbanized areas, highly urbanized cities, component cities, and municipalities with
high-density settlements, as determined by the Philippine Statistics Authority or as
may hereafter be defined by law.

      Sec. 5. National Urban Drainage and Runoff Management Code. – Within
one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, the DPWH, in coordination with the
Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), the Department of
Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD), and such other national
government agencies and stakeholders as may be necessary, shall develop,
promulgate, and adopt a comprehensive National Urban Drainage and Runoff
Management Code. This Code shall prescribe the technical standards,
methodologies, and procedures for the effective management of stormwater and
rainwater in urban areas.

        Sec. 6. Mandatory Compliance by Local Government Units. – All LGUs
falling within the scope of this Act shall, within six (6) months from the promulgation of
the National Urban Drainage and Runoff Management Code, adopt and integrate said
Code into their Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs), zoning ordinances, and
local development plans. Compliance shall be mandatory and enforceable, and LGUs
shall be required to align all urban drainage projects, approvals, and permits with the
provisions of the Code.
       Sec. 7. Stormwater and Runoff Infrastructure Requirements for New
Developments. – All new developments and redevelopment projects that will result in
the creation of more than five hundred (500) square meters of impervious surface area
shall:
   1. Install on-site detention or retention facilities sufficient to capture and regulate
       peak stormwater runoff volumes in accordance with the design standards of the
       DPWH;
   2. Incorporate permeable paving, rain gardens, bioswales, infiltration trenches, or
       other equivalent infiltration systems to promote groundwater recharge and
       reduce surface runoff; and
   3. Ensure full compliance with the minimum design and capacity standards for
       drainage systems as prescribed in the National Urban Drainage and Runoff
       Management                                                                   Code.

       Sec. 8. Rainwater Catchment Facilities. – All new buildings, subdivisions,
commercial complexes, industrial facilities, and government infrastructure projects
shall be designed, constructed, and equipped with rainwater catchment facilities
having storage capacity proportionate to their roof area and impervious surfaces, in
accordance with technical standards to be issued by the DPWH.

       The DPWH, in coordination with the Department of Science and Technology
(DOST), shall also formulate and implement programs to promote the retrofitting of
existing buildings and facilities with rainwater catchment systems. Such programs may
include the provision of incentives, technical assistance, and inclusion of rainwater
harvesting systems in government-led retrofitting projects.
All rainwater collected shall be intended for non-potable applications such as
landscape irrigation, fire protection, cooling systems, and cleaning purposes, subject
to water safety and quality standards to be issued by the appropriate regulatory
agencies.

       Sec. 9. Retrofitting of Existing Urban Districts. – The DPWH, in
collaboration with LGUs, shall conduct flood risk assessments and identify priority
urban districts for retrofitting of drainage and runoff systems, including the installation
of rainwater harvesting facilities, in order to meet the updated engineering standards
provided under this Act.

       Sec. 10. Monitoring and Enforcement. – The DPWH shall be the primary
agency responsible for ensuring national compliance with this Act, and shall establish
a monitoring framework for its enforcement. LGUs shall be tasked to perform regular
inspections and audits of urban drainage and rainwater harvesting facilities to ensure
operational efficiency and adherence to maintenance standards.
       Sec. 11. Penalties. – Any LGU, property developer, or facility owner who fails
or refuses to comply with the provisions of this Act, or with the standards set forth in
the National Urban Drainage and Runoff Management Code, shall be liable to
administrative fines and other penalties as may be prescribed in the Implementing
Rules and Regulations (IRR).

       Sec. 12. Incentives. – The national government may grant fiscal or non-fiscal
incentives to LGUs and private developers who adopt and implement innovative,
climate-resilient drainage and rainwater harvesting solutions that substantially exceed
the minimum requirements set forth in this Act.

      Sec. 13. Public Awareness and Capacity Building. – The DPWH, in
coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), shall
design and implement nationwide public education campaigns and capacity-building
programs to raise awareness on the importance of flood resilience and rainwater
harvesting. These initiatives shall target LGUs, developers, engineers, architects, and
communities.

       Sec. 14. Lead Agency. – The DPWH shall serve as the lead agency in the
implementation of this Act, in coordination with the DENR, DHSUD, DILG, and such
other agencies as may be deemed necessary.

      Sec. 15. Appropriations. – The amounts necessary to carry out the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the respective budgets of the
concerned agencies for the current fiscal year. Thereafter, such amounts as may be
necessary for the continuous implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

       Sec. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DPWH, in consultation with the concerned agencies,
shall promulgate the Implementing Rules and Regulations to operationalize its
provisions.

      Sec. 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
issuances, administrative orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof that are
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

        Sec. 18. Separability Clause. – Should any section, provision, or part of this
Act be declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining portions not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

       Sec. 19. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect immediately after its
publication in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
