                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

Financial literacy and entrepreneurship are increasingly recognized as indispensable life skills in this
fast-paced society, serving as cornerstones for economic empowerment, poverty reduction, and long-term
national development. In the Philippines, millions remain vulnerable to economic shocks and financial
mismanagement. Hence, it becomes of primordial importance to equip citizens with the knowledge to
make sound financial decisions and develop income-generating activities.

Despite the proliferation of digital financial technologies, which is fueled by the COVID-19 pandemic and
the rapid expansion of cashless transactions, the Philippines continues to face alarmingly low levels of
financial literacy. According to the World Bank, only 25% of Filipino adults possess basic financial
knowledge, a finding echoed by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP). This gap is especially concerning
given the growing complexity of financial products and the rising prevalence of digital lending, online
scams, and unsustainable consumer debt.

To address this gap, this bill proposes the inclusion of Financial Literacy and Entrepreneurship as core
subjects in the junior and senior high school curricula of both public and private schools. The proposed
measure aims to institutionalize financial education as part of the formal learning system—embedding it
early in the educational journey, at a time when lifelong habits and skills are formed.

This measure builds upon Section 4 of Republic Act No. 10922, or the Economic and Financial Literacy Week
Act, which mandates the promotion of financial literacy among Filipinos. It aligns with the need to
overhaul curriculum priorities and focus on foundational and future-ready skills to improve educational
outcomes and relevance.

When young Filipinos are empowered to start micro-businesses, manage capital responsibly, and
contribute to household or community income, they become agents of inevitable economic progress in
the country.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
REQUIRING THE INCLUSION OF FINANCIAL            LITERACY AND
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AS SEPARATE CORE SUBJECTS IN THE JUNIOR AND
SENIOR HIGH SCHOOL CURRICULA OF THE K-12 PROGRAM, AMENDING
FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10679, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
“YOUTH ENTREPRENEURSHIP ACT”

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:


       SECTION 1. Financial Literacy and Entrepreneurship Subjects. - Section 4 of
Republic Act No. 10679 is hereby amended to read as follows:

                  "SECTION 4. Youth Entrepreneurship and Financial Literacy
         Program. - The promotion of youth entrepreneurship and financial literacy
         program shall be inculcated in all levels of education nationwide. Consistent with
         Section 5 of Republic Act No. 10533, or the "Enhanced Basic Education Act of
         2013", the Department of Education (DepED) shall ensure that the K to 12
         curriculum shall be supported by programs on entrepreneurship and financial
         literacy: Provided, That at the elementary level, the programs referred to shall
         principally focus on the ideal values necessary to become successful
         entrepreneurs.

              THERE SHALL BE SEPARATE CORE SUBJECTS ON
         FINANCIAL LITERACY AND ENTREPRENEURSHIP IN THE
         JUNIOR AND SENIOR HIGH SCHOOL CURRICULA OF BOTH
         PUBLIC AND PRIVATE SECONDARY SCHOOLS NATIONWIDE.

                The Commission on Higher Education (CHED) and the Technical
         Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) shall ensure the
         promotion of programs on entrepreneurship and financial literacy.”

       SEC. 2. Development of Subject Content. - Section 6 of Republic Act No. 10679 is
hereby amended to read as follows:

"SEC. 6. Duties of the EEC. - The EEC shall:

a. Formulate a national plan of action for the implementation of this Act;


                                                                                                     1
b.​ Study and standardize all current programs of schools and institutions on entrepreneurship
    and financial literacy;
c.​ Research current government efforts in foreign countries with the end view of applying
    relevant practices in our education system;

   3.​ Set up enterprise incubation laboratories and creative spaces in schools and communities

       entrepreneurship activities; and

(E) CONDUCT A MULTI-SECTORAL CONSULTATION WITH EDUCATORS
FROM PUBLIC AND PRIVATE HIGH SCHOOLS, MEMBERS OF THE
NATIONAL CHAMBERS OF COMMERCE AND INDUSTRY, LEADING
ENTREPRENEURS AND THE PUBLIC AT LARGE FOR THE PURPOSE OF
DETERMINING THE APPROPRIATE CONTENT AND SCOPE OF THE
FINANCIAL LITERACY AND ENTREPRENEURSHIP SUBJECTS; AND

(e) (F) Exercise such other powers as may be necessary to carry out the purposes of this Act.”

         SEC 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days after the
effectivity of this Act, the DepEd Secretary, CHED Chairperson and the TESDA
Director-General, in coordination with the National Youth Commission, shall formulate the
rules and regulations necessary for the implementation of this Act.

        SEC 4. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations or other
issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed and
modified accordingly.

       SEC 5. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part hereof is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the same shall not affect the validity and effectivity of other provisions.

        SEC 6. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in any newspaper of general circulation.
