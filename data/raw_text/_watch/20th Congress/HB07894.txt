                           AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE GOVERNMENT INTERNSHIP PROGRAM
(GIP), PROVIDING FOR ITS IMPLEMENTATION, FUNDING, AND FOR
OTHER PURPOSES
                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The Constitution recognizes the State’s duty to promote the welfare and development
of the youth, ensuring that they are given opportunities to contribute meaningfully to nation-
building. In line with this, the Government Internship Program (GIP) was established to
provide young Filipinos with hands-on experience in public service, cultivate their skills, and
attract highly qualified individuals to careers in government.

        Recent data from the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) demonstrate that
the GIP has already made a significant impact on Filipino youth and their families. In 2019, a
total of 45,121 young Filipinos benefitted from the program. Regional reports from 2024
indicate continued expansion: Region 9 placed 1,485 interns, while Region 12 placed 1,556
interns, with a portion of these interns subsequently absorbed into government positions. These
figures underscore that GIP not only equips youth with valuable professional experience but
also provides financial support to their families, particularly those from underprivileged
sectors.

        The program addresses the persistent issue of youth underemployment, which,
according to the Philippine Statistics Authority, remains at 13.7% for the 15–24 age group, with
15.9% classified as NEET (not in employment, education, or training). By providing structured,
compensated internships in national and local government offices, GIP bridges the gap between
education and employment, fostering career pathways in the civil service while enhancing the
practical skills, work readiness, and employability of young Filipinos.

        A critical feature of the GIP is its structured introduction of interns to government and
administrative systems. Interns are exposed to the inner workings of government offices,
including organizational structures, administrative processes, public service protocols, and
policy implementation. Through supervised hands-on tasks, mentoring, and performance
evaluation, interns gain firsthand experience in administrative operations, interdepartmental
coordination, document processing, and citizen service delivery. This exposure ensures that
interns not only develop technical competencies but also cultivate an understanding of public
sector responsibilities, governance, and ethical standards, preparing them for future careers in
public service.

        Institutionalizing the GIP as a permanent national program ensures that these benefits
are accessible to all eligible youth across the country. It establishes a standardized framework
for selection, placement, and evaluation, strengthens pathways to civil service careers, and
further supports the economic well-being of young Filipinos and their families.

        Immediate passage of this bill is therefore earnestly sought to secure the continuity and
expansion of the Government Internship Program, ensuring that the youth remain active
participants in nation-building while gaining the knowledge, skills, and experience necessary
for meaningful careers in public service.

                           AN ACT

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

Section 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Government Internship Program Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to recognize the vital role of the
youth in nation-building and to promote their well-being, skills development, and access to
public service opportunities.

SEC. 3. Creation of the Government Internship Program (GIP). A national Government
Internship Program (GIP) is hereby institutionalized, to be implemented by all National
Government Agencies (NGAs), Local Government Units (LGUs), Government-Owned or
Controlled Corporations (GOCCs), and State Universities and Colleges (SUCs), collectively
referred to as “Host Institutions” (HIs).

SEC. 4. Objectives. — The internship program shall aim to:
  a) Provide young workers, particularly from underprivileged or marginalized sectors, with
       orientation in public service and opportunities for hands-on experience.
  b) Allow Host Institutions to identify, train, and recruit highly capable future government
       employees.
  c) Ensure interns receive fair compensation and benefits commensurate with their
       contributions.

SEC. 5. Qualifications of a GIP Intern. — Applicants must:
  a) Be a Filipino citizen;
  b) Be 18 to 30 years of age at the time of application;
  c) Be a high school graduate, vocational-technical graduate, or undergraduate/college
       student;
  d) Not have prior government work experience, except for school-required on-the-job
       training.
SEC. 6. Internship Period. — The internship shall last 3 to 6 months, and an individual may
participate only once.

SEC. 7. Compensation and Benefits. — GIP interns shall receive:
  a) Stipend. At least 75% of the minimum wage in the region of the Host Institution, based
       on actual attendance.
  b) Insurance. Coverage through the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS) or
       equivalent accident insurance for the duration of the internship.
  c) Certificate of Completion. Documenting the skills acquired, duties performed, and
       performance evaluation.

SEC. 8. Nature of Relationship. Participation in the GIP does not create an employer-employee
relationship. The internship is solely for training, education, and skills development.

SEC. 9. Preferential Hiring. GIP graduates who meet qualifications for permanent, temporary,
or contractual positions shall be given preference over other applicants, subject to Civil Service
laws and regulations.

SEC. 10. Role of the Implementing Agencies. For the purposes of this Act:
  a) Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE). Lead agency responsible for program
      supervision, nationwide monitoring, guidelines setting, and fund allocation to Host
      Institutions.
  b) Civil Service Commission (CSC) and National Youth Commission (NYC). Co-
      implementing agencies responsible for standardized learning plans, performance
      evaluation systems, and maintenance of a centralized database to enforce the one-time
      participation rule.

SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within 90 days from effectivity, the
DOLE, in consultation with the CSC and NYC, shall promulgate the necessary IRR for the
implementation of this Act.

SEC. 12. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to implement this Act, including stipends,
insurance, and related expenses, shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 13. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees, administrative
orders, rules, and regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 15. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect 15 days after publication in the Official
Gazette or in at least two newspapers of general circulation.
