                       AN ACT
STRENGTHENING THE CANCER REGISTRY SYSTEM IN THE
PHILIPPINES, MANDATING     THE   ESTABLISHMENT   AND
SUSTAINED FUNDING OF POPULATION-BASED AND HOSPITAL-
BASED CANCER REGISTRIES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES WITH
FUNDS APPROPRIATED THEREOF


                           EXPLANATORY NOTE


      This proposed bill seeks to establish and fund population-based and
hospital-based cancer registries to strengthen the cancer registry system mandated
by the National Integrated Cancer Control Act (NICCA).
       In the Philippines, cancer is the third leading cause of death. According to
the International Agency for Research on Cancer, over 188,000 new diagnoses
and over 113,000 deaths have been attributed to different forms of cancer. Such
data is only based on what was acquired from the Manila and Rizal Cancer
Registries.
      At the moment, the Philippines has four Population-Based Cancer
Registries (PBCR) situated in Manila, Rizal, Cebu, and Davao. As for Hospital-
Based Cancer Registries (HBCR), there are only forty-four known hospitals
adopting the CARE PH application, a software tool that functions as a hospital
cancer registry which is embedded into the hospital local area network maintained
by an organization of healthcare champions, patients, and their caregivers, with a
capacity to share their anonymized data with a secure and encrypted central
  database.
        Given this, cancer registration in the Philippines remains fragmented,
  underfunded, and geographically incomplete, covering only a fraction of the
  national population and hospital network. The lack of a fully institutionalized
  nationwide system continues to create significant gaps in cancer incidence,
  prevalence, survival, and mortality data.
         This bill seeks to strengthen the cancer registry system in the Philippines
  by mandating the establishment, accreditation, and sustained funding of both
  PBCRs and HBCRs. It further positions the National Cancer Registry as the
  official source of national cancer incidence and survival data, anchored on
  PBCRs as the backbone of population-level surveillance. It likewise reiterates the
  provision under NICCA that mandates that all hospitals, including clinics, shall
  have its own cancer registry. Moreover, it professionalizes the cancer registry
  workforce through a national certification program for Cancer Registrars to
  ensure data quality and interoperability, and enforce measures to practice secure
  and ethical data sharing in line with the Data Privacy Act of 2012.
         The proposed measure builds upon the NICCA by ensuring that cancer data
  systems are no longer fragmented and are institutionalized, sustainable, and
  aligned with global standards instead. Reliable data on cancer in the Philippines
  will not only empower policymakers and health workers but also deliver justice
  to patients and families by ensuring equitable allocation of resources, improved
  access to cancer care, and stronger public health interventions nationwide.

        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
  sought.

                                     AN ACT

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Cancer
Registry Act of 2025".


      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of
the State to protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
consciousness among them. In pursuit of this, the State shall establish a
comprehensive and responsive cancer data system through a well-supported and
technically sound cancer registry network. This will serve as the foundation for
cancer surveillance, planning, policy development, and evaluation of cancer
control strategies in the country. This will strengthen the implementation of the
National Integrated Cancer Control Act (NICCA).

   SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. For the purposes of this Act, the following
terms are hereby defined:

   a. Cancer Registrar refers to a trained health information professional
      responsible for collecting, abstracting, verifying, and managing cancer data
      in accordance with international standards.
   b. Cancer Registry refers to a database that contains information about people
      diagnosed with various types of cancer. The registry shall require
      systematic collection, storage, analysis, interpretation and reporting of data
      on subjects with cancer.
   c. Hospital-Based Cancer Registry (HBCR) refers to a registry that collects
      information on cancer patients diagnosed and/or treated in a specific
      hospital, primarily for clinical service evaluation, quality assurance, and
      hospital-based research.
   d. Population-Based Cancer Registry (PBCR) refers to a registry that
      collects data on all new cancer cases occurring in a defined population
      within a specific geographic area, regardless of the place of diagnosis or
      treatment. PBCRs provide incidence, survival, and mortality data to inform
      public health policies and research.
   e. WHO-IARC CanReg refers to an open-source software tool developed by
      the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), designed
      specifically to input, store, check, and analyze population-based cancer
      registry data to help track cancer incidence globally. It is a cornerstone tool
      for cancer registries worldwide aiming for quality and comparability in
      cancer data collection and analysis. It is widely adopted by cancer registries
      in Africa, Asia, the Middle East, Pacific Islands, Eastern Europe, and parts
      of Latin America for systematic, standardized cancer data collection and
      management.
   f. WHO IARC refers to the World Health Organization International Agency
      for Research on Cancer, which is the specialized cancer research agency of
      WHO. Its mission is to coordinate and conduct international research on
      the causes, prevention, and early detection of cancer.           IARC was
      established in 1965 as an intergovernmental agency and is headquartered
      in Lyon, France. Its membership has grown to 29 countries, and it works
      closely with WHO to translate research findings into global cancer
      prevention policies.
   g. National Cancer Registry refers to a population-based registry established
      to collect, store, analyze, interpret, and report data on persons with cancer
      in the Philippines.
   h. Cancer Repository Databank refers to a specialized database or data
      repository designed to systematically collect, store, manage, and share
      information about cancer patients, tumor characteristics, treatment
      outcomes, and/or genomic data for research, surveillance, and public health
      purposes.

   SECTION 4. Purpose of the Act. – The specific objectives of the Act are as
follows:


   a. Institutionalize a sustainable and high-quality cancer registry system
      composed of population-based and hospital-based cancer registries.
   b. Ensure the independent and uninterrupted operation of Population-Based
      Cancer Registries (PBCRs) as the backbone of the National Cancer
      Registry.
   c. Provide a clear framework for the roles, responsibilities, funding, and
      certification of cancer registrars.
   d. Establish mechanisms for coordination, data quality assurance, and ethical
      data sharing among registries and other relevant institutions.
   e. Promote the use of cancer registry data in national policy, planning, and
      research for cancer prevention and control.


   SECTION 5. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all Population-
Based Cancer Registries (PBCRs) and Hospital-Based Cancer Registries
(HBCRs) operating in the Philippines, whether managed by government
agencies, local government units, or private institutions. It shall also cover all
hospitals, cancer centers, and diagnostic facilities required to report cancer cases
as part of the national cancer surveillance system.


   SECTION 6. The National Cancer Registry. – The National Cancer Registry
shall serve as the official source of national cancer incidence, survival, and
mortality data and shall operate as a Population-Based Cancer Registry (PBCR).
It shall be developed, maintained, and managed under the supervision of the
Department of Health (DOH) Cancer Control Division, in coordination with the
Philippine Cancer Center Cancer Repository Data Bank and other relevant
stakeholders.

   The National Cancer Registry shall:

   a. Consolidate, integrate, and harmonize data submitted by accredited PBCRs
      to produce national estimates of cancer incidence, prevalence, survival, and
      mortality;
   b. Collect and encode PBCR data using the World Health Organization–
      International Agency for Research on Cancer (WHO–IARC) CanReg
      software, and operate in accordance with international standards set by
      WHO–IARC and other recognized authorities;
   c. Ensure that PBCRs remain the independent and primary data source for
      national cancer incidence statistics, and continue collecting and reporting
      data even in the absence or incompleteness of HBCR data;
   d. Guarantee data privacy and security in compliance with Republic Act No.
      10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”;
   e. Produce and disseminate regular cancer statistics and reports to guide
      national policy, research, and programs; and
   f. Serve as the official source of cancer data for submission to international
      bodies, including the World Health Organization (WHO) and the
      International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC).

   The current Metro Manila Philippine PBCRs DOH-Rizal Cancer Registry and
Philippine Cancer Society-Manila Cancer Registry contributing data to the
WHO-IARC GLOBOCAN shall continue to be part of the National Cancer
Registry. The newly established DOH - Davao Cancer Registry (DOH-DCR) and
the DOH - Cebu Cancer Registry (DOH-CCR) shall likewise continue to be part
of the National Cancer Registry, representing Mindanao and Visayas,
respectively. An additional Central Luzon PBCR shall be developed representing
Luzon. The active registrars of the DOH-RCR, PCS-MCR, DOH-DCR, and
DOH-CCR shall be recognized as the pioneer cancer registrars of the National
Cancer Registry.


   Hospital-Based Cancer Registries shall not be required for the validation or
continuation of PBCR operations. PBCRs shall operate and report independently
to ensure complete and representative population-level cancer data.


   SECTION 7. Establishment and Accreditation of PBCRs. – The Department
of Health shall ensure that accredited Population-Based Cancer Registries
(PBCRs) are strategically located to ensure equitable, regional, and population-
level representation across the country. The expansion of PBCRs shall be
progressive and guided by technical readiness, coverage needs, and data quality
standards.


   Each PBCR shall cover a defined geographic area and population, following
standardized protocols for data collection, coding, validation, and reporting.
PBCRs shall be accredited by the Department of Health based on criteria set in
accordance with international standards. The Department of Health shall provide
technical, financial, and capacity-building support to all accredited PBCRs.
PBCRs may be managed by government institutions, academic partners, or
independent organizations with proven capacity and integrity, subject to DOH
oversight.


   SECTION 8. Establishment of HBCRs. – All government and private
hospitals with cancer diagnostic or treatment services shall establish and maintain
Hospital-Based Cancer Registries (HBCRs). These registries shall collect data on
all cancer patients diagnosed and/or treated within their facilities to support
quality assurance, service planning, and hospital-based research.


   Hospitals shall designate trained and certified Cancer Registrars, and allocate
adequate resources for registry operations. Hospitals under the Department of
Health (DOH) and other national government agencies shall include cancer
registry positions in their staffing patterns, consistent with the standards
prescribed by the DOH and Civil Service Commission (CSC).


   The Philippine Cancer Center shall issue guidelines for the minimum
requirements, formats, and timelines for HBCR implementation, as well as
procedures for data submission, quality assurance, and integration with national
systems. Hospitals shall maintain or choose their own digital medical records
formats, from where the HBCR can be culled from. The HBCR shall be a hospital
data source of the PBCR.


   SECTION 9. Cancer Registrar Certification and Staffing. – The Department
of Health, in collaboration with relevant professional societies, academic
institutions, and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
(TESDA), shall develop and implement a national training and certification
program for Cancer Registrars. This program shall include core competencies in
cancer data abstraction, registry software use, international coding standards, data
security, and ethical reporting, in line with global standards such as those set by
the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC).


   TESDA shall be authorized to:


   a. Develop a dedicated national qualifications framework (NQF) level or
      certificate program for Cancer Registrars;
   b. Accredit training institutions and trainers to deliver modular or full-course
      instruction;
   c. Conduct national assessments and issue certificates of competency, in
      partnership with the Department of Health and relevant training partners.


   DOH hospitals, public tertiary and regional hospitals, cancer centers, and
PBCR host institutions shall include Cancer Registrar positions in their
organizational structure and staffing patterns, subject to Civil Service
Commission (CSC) rules and classification standards.


   The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and the Civil Service
Commission (CSC) shall ensure the timely creation and funding of plantilla items
for certified Cancer Registrars to professionalize the workforce and ensure
sustainability of cancer registration efforts.


   SECTION 10. Data Sharing, Privacy, and Security. – Cancer registries shall
implement secure and ethical data collection, storage, and sharing practices in
accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 or the Data Privacy Act of 2012.


To support comprehensive and accurate population-level cancer surveillance:


   a. All hospitals, both public and private, shall cooperate with Population-
      Based Cancer Registries (PBCRs) in the collection of essential cancer data.
      This includes sharing patient records, diagnostic details, and follow-up
      outcomes necessary for PBCR completeness, as authorized under existing
      health regulations and data-sharing protocols.
   b. The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) shall provide access to mortality
      data and civil registry records to the DOH and accredited PBCRs for the
      purposes of verifying patient vital status, generating survival statistics, and
      improving cancer mortality reporting.


The DOH shall also re-establish and establish protocols for:


   a. Secure data transmission and storage platforms for all cancer registries;
   b. Interoperability    between PBCRs,         HBCRs,   the Philippine Health
      Information Exchange (PHIE), and national registries such as PSA and
      PhilHealth;
   c. De-identified and anonymized data use for research and public health
      purposes; and
   d. Institutional review procedures for research and publication involving
      registry data.
   SECTION 11. Funding and Sustainability. – The amount necessary for the
initial implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current
appropriations of the Department of Health. Thereafter, such sums as may be
necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act (GAA).


The Department of Health shall:


   a. Provide annual grants to accredited PBCRs and qualified institutions for
      operations, training, equipment, digitalization, and staffing;
   b. Explore and implement mechanisms for public-private partnerships,
      external funding, and international technical cooperation;
   c. Issue sustainability and transition plans for registry host institutions.


   SECTION 12. Oversight and Monitoring. – An oversight mechanism for the
PBCR shall be established under the DOH-Cancer Control Division to:


   a. Monitor the implementation of this Act’s provision on PBCR;
   b. Review PBCR performance annually;
   c. Coordinate and collaborate with the Cancer Repository Databank under the
      Philippine Cancer Center;
   d. Recommend improvements in standards, training, and operations;
   e. Submit annual reports to the Secretary of Health and the Committee on
      Health of both Houses of Congress.


   An oversight mechanism for the HBCR shall be established under the
Philippine Cancer Center to:
   a. Monitor the implementation of this Act’s provision on HBCR;
   b. Review HBCR performance annually;
   c. Coordinate and collaborate with the PBCRs under the DOH-Cancer
      Control Division;
   d. Recommend improvements in standards, training, and operations;
   e. Submit annual reports jointly with the DOH-Cancer Control Division to
      the Secretary of Health and the Committee on Health of both Houses of
      Congress.


   SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health, in consultation
with the Philippine Cancer Center, registry experts, academic partners, and civil
society, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.


   SECTION 14. Separability Clause. – If any part, section, or provision of this
Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining parts not affected
thereby shall remain in full force and effect.


   SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.


   SECTION 16. Sunset Review. – A comprehensive review of the
implementation of this Act shall be conducted every five (5) years from its
effectivity by the Department of Health and relevant stakeholders. The findings
shall guide necessary amendments and policy directions to improve cancer data
systems in the country.
   SECTION 17. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
