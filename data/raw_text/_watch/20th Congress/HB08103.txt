ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL CANCER CENTER TO BE KNOWN AS THE NATIONAL

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Cancer is one of the leading causes of death in the Philippines and places a heavy
burden on patients, families, and the health care system. While the government
already has a national cancer program, many Filipinos still struggle to get timely
diagnosis, advanced treatment, follow-up care, and palliative support. Outside major
cities, access to specialized cancer doctors, modern equipment, and coordinated care
remains very limited, leading to delayed treatment and poorer outcomes.

This bill proposes the creation of a National Cancer Center of the Philippines to
serve as the country’s main referral hospital and center of excellence for cancer care.
The Center will provide highly specialized and multidisciplinary services, support
research, and train doctors, nurses, and other health workers in cancer care. It will
strengthen, not replace, the existing national cancer program by setting standards,
sharing expertise, and working with hospitals across the country to improve the
quality of care nationwide.

By investing in a dedicated national cancer center, this measure helps ensure that
Filipinos, regardless of where they live, can access quality cancer care. It promotes
better survival, more humane treatment, and stronger public accountability in the use
of health funds. This bill is a long-term investment in saving lives, improving care, and
building a stronger health system.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “National Cancer Center of
the Philippines Act.”

SECTION 2. Creation of the National Cancer Center of the Philippines. — There is
hereby created a specialty hospital and national referral, research, and training
institution to be known as the National Cancer Center of the Philippines, hereinafter
referred to as the “Center.”

The Center shall be attached to the Department of Health (DOH) for policy
coordination and shall support and complement the implementation of the National
Integrated Cancer Control Program.

SECTION 3. Corporate Powers; Nature of the Center. — The Center shall have
corporate powers and shall be administered by a Board of Trustees. For the
attainment and furtherance of its purposes and objectives, the Center, acting through
its Board, shall have the powers pertaining to a juridical person, including, among
others, the power to contract and be contracted with; acquire, hold, lease, mortgage,
and dispose of property; solicit and receive donations and endowments; open bank

accounts and disburse or invest funds as the Board may direct; adopt by-laws, rules,
and regulations not inconsistent with law; and do such other acts as may be necessary
or incidental to its purposes.

SECTION 4. Governing Board; Composition. — The Center shall be governed by a
Board of Trustees (Board), which shall direct, control, and manage its activities and
properties.

The Board shall be composed of seven (7) members:

   (a) The Secretary of Health, or a duly designated representative with a rank not
       lower than Assistant Secretary, as Chairperson, ex of icio;

   (b) The Secretary of the Interior and Local Government, or a duly designated
       representative, ex of icio;

   (c) The President and Chief Executive Of icer of the Philippine Health Insurance
       Corporation (PhilHealth), or a duly designated representative, ex of icio;

   (d) Two (2) medical doctors of good moral character and recognized probity with
       at least ten (10) years of active practice in oncology or cancer-related
       specialties, to be appointed by the President of the Philippines; and

   (e) Two (2) representatives from cancer-focused patient support organizations
       and advocacy networks, to be appointed by the President of the Philippines.

The foregoing composition is without prejudice to such further quali ications and
integrity requirements as may be provided in the Implementing Rules and
Regulations (IRR).

SECTION 5. Appointment; Term; Vacancies; Quorum. — The appointive members
of the Board shall be appointed by the President of the Philippines for a term of three
(3) years and may be reappointed for one (1) additional term, unless otherwise
provided in the IRR.

Any vacancy shall be illed in the same manner as the original appointment, and the
appointee shall serve only the unexpired portion of the term.

A majority of the members of the Board shall constitute a quorum to transact
business, and the vote of the majority of those present in a meeting where there is a
quorum shall constitute a valid and binding act of the Board.

SECTION 6. Medical Director; Chief Executive Of icer. — The Center shall be
headed by a Medical Director who shall serve as the Chief Executive Of icer and shall
be appointed by the Board. The Medical Director shall be responsible for the day-to-
day administration of the Center, subject to the supervision and control of the Board,
and shall have authority, among others, to appoint, discipline, and remove personnel,
and to ix compensation, subject to existing civil service, compensation, and other
applicable laws.

SECTION 7. Purposes and Objectives. — The Center shall have the following
purposes and objectives:

   (a) To establish, equip, maintain, administer, and operate a specialized hospital for
       the diagnosis, treatment, care, rehabilitation, and palliation of cancer patients;

   (b) To serve as a national referral center for complex cancer cases and specialized
       cancer services;

   (c) To promote and conduct cancer-related clinical and public health research,
       including the organization and sponsorship of congresses, conventions,
       seminars, and conferences;

   (d) To train and develop human resources for health, including physicians, nurses,
       medical technologists, pharmacists, social workers, and allied health
       professionals, in cancer prevention, diagnosis, treatment, survivorship, and
       palliative care;

   (e) To provide patient navigation, psychosocial support services, and counseling
       for patients and their families;

   (f) To undertake public information and education initiatives on cancer
       prevention, screening, early detection, and treatment; and

   (g) To generate, consolidate, and manage cancer-related data and information,
       consistent with applicable laws, in support of evidence-based policy and
       program development.

SECTION 8. Assistance and Coordination with Government Of ices. — The Center
may call upon any department, bureau, of ice, agency, or instrumentality of the
Government, including government-owned or controlled corporations, for such
assistance as it may need to carry out its purposes.

SECTION 9. Property of the Center. — The property of the Center shall consist of
such real, personal, mixed, and other property now owned or reserved for, or that may
hereafter be transferred, conveyed, donated, bequeathed, or otherwise acquired by
the Center from the Government, any of its instrumentalities, foreign governments,
and other entities or persons, domestic or foreign, in order to carry out its purposes
and objectives.

SECTION 10. Government Contribution; Site and Facilities. — The Government
shall contribute the necessary land, buildings, equipment, and facilities for the
establishment and operation of the Center, subject to existing laws on government
property and procurement.

SECTION 11. Donations; Tax Treatment. — Donations, contributions, and
endowments made to the Center shall be accepted in accordance with law. The tax
treatment of such donations, including donor incentives and exemptions, shall be in
accordance with the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended, and applicable
rules and regulations.

SECTION 12. Appropriations. — The amount of One Billion Pesos
(₱1,000,000,000.00) is hereby appropriated for the initial organization, construction,
and operation of the Center, to be charged against the current appropriations of the
DOH, subject to budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules. Thereafter, such sums as
may be necessary for the continued operation and maintenance of the Center shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 13. Auditing. — The books and accounts of the Center shall be subject to
audit by the Commission on Audit (COA).

SECTION 14. Annual Report; Congressional Oversight. — The Center shall submit
to the DOH an annual report of its activities, audited inancial statements, and such
recommendations as it may deem necessary. The DOH shall furnish the Senate and
the House of Representatives copies of the annual report for oversight and policy
review.

SECTION 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with PhilHealth and other

SECTION 16. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full force

SECTION 17. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
