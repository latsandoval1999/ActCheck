                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Electricity is an essential public service indispensable to daily life,
human       dignity,     economic      productivity, and national                development.
Households, schools, hospitals, and businesses depend on electricity. This is
why power costs are particularly burdensome for ordinary Filipinos.
Unfortunately, in recent years, consumer electricity bills skyrocketed in the
country. As of June 2026, the Department of Energy reported that the
Philippines recorded the highest average electricity rates in Southeast Asia
at P12.43 per kilowatt-hour, surpassing Singapore by less than a centavo.1
This is in no small part due to system loss charges. Based on Meralco’s 2025

       Under existing laws, specifically Section 10 of Republic Act No. 7832
or the “Anti-Electricity and Electric Transmission Lines/Materials Pilferage
Act of 1994”4 and Section 43(f) of the EPIRA Act of 2001,5 distribution
utilities and electric cooperatives are permitted to recover system losses
from consumers, subject to the caps prescribed by the Energy Regulatory
Commission. System loss refers to the difference between the energy
delivered to the distribution system and energy delivered to the users of the
distribution system, the amount of which is eventually measured and billed
to the consumers.6 It encompasses both technical losses (physical
dissipation of energy through lines and transformers) and non-technical
losses (electricity theft, illegal connections, meter tampering, and
administrative errors).7

       While certain technical losses may be expected in the operation of an
electric distribution system, the financial consequences of such losses form
part of the ordinary cost and risk of operating a public utility. Like
businesses in other industries, distribution utilities are expected to manage
these risks by maintaining efficient infrastructure, investing in modern
technologies, reducing technical losses, and preventing electricity theft.

Consumers, who have no control over the operation of the distribution
system, should not be required to bear these costs.

        The existing pass-through mechanism weakens incentives for utilities
to minimize system losses. If losses are simply recovered from consumers,
utilities have no pressure to modernize aging infrastructure, improve
operational efficiency, and strengthen anti-pilferage measures. As a result,
honest paying households, including those in the lower income brackets, are
forced to subsidize these inefficiencies answer for stolen electricity.

        This measure addresses these concerns by prohibiting the recovery or
pass-through of system losses to consumers while preserving the right of
distribution utilities to recover losses directly from persons responsible for
electricity pilferage, theft, meter tampering, fraud, vandalism, and other
unlawful acts through the remedies available under existing laws. The bill
likewise directs the Department of Energy, the National Electrification
Administration, and the Energy Regulatory Commission to assist electric
cooperatives through modernization programs, technical assistance,
capacity-building, and access to financing, particularly for those serving
geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas, to ensure that the
transition away from system loss charges does not compromise the
reliability of their electric services.

        The proposed measure is consistent with the Constitution, which
directs the State to promote a just and dynamic social order that will
improve the quality of life of all the people8 and protect consumers against
unfair business practices.9 It is likewise consistent with the principle that
public utilities, as holders of State-granted franchises, must provide safe,

adequate, efficient, and reasonably priced services.10 The ordinary costs and
risks of operating a public utility, including the responsibility to minimize
system losses, should be borne by the utility itself and not shifted to
consumers.

       Hence, this bill, which seeks to: (1) prohibit the recovery or pass-
through of system losses to electricity consumers; (2) strengthen the
accountability of distribution utilities in managing system losses; (3)
preserve the right of utilities to recover losses from the persons legally
responsible therefor; (4) provide technical and financial assistance to
electric cooperatives in reducing system losses; and (5) promote a more
efficient, accountable, and consumer-oriented electric power industry.

26   Section 4. Responsibility for System Losses. Distribution utilities shall
27   bear system losses as part of the ordinary costs and risks of providing
28   electric distribution services and shall not recover or pass on such losses to
29   consumers.

30   Nothing in this Act will prevent a distribution utility from recovering losses
31   directly from persons responsible for electricity pilferage, theft, meter
32   tampering, fraud, vandalism, negligence, breach of contract, or other
33   unlawful acts through the remedies available under existing laws.
