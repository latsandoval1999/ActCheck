  AN ACT MANDATING THE PROVISION OF A FIFTEEN THOUSAND PESOS
 (P15,000.00) PRODUCTION SUBSIDY TO THE FILIPINOS EMPLOYED IN THE
 FISHERIES AND AQUACULTURE SECTOR, TO STRENGTHEN PRODUCTION,
  SUPPORT SECTORAL RECOVERY, AND ADVANCE NATIONAL ECONOMIC
                  RESILIENCE AMID ECONOMIC CRISES

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

Among the country’s basic sectors, fisherfolk are among the poorest of the poor. According to
the Philippine Statistics Authority, the incidence of poverty remains higher among fisherfolk.
From 2018 to 2023, the number of poor fisherfolk increased from 287,070 to 353,190.

Subsequent interventions involving fisherfolk were largely limited to broad categories such
as assistance to micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and measures to ensure
supply chain continuity. In practice, fisherfolk were subsumed under a small allocation of
credit facilities—₱1 billion shared among approximately 40,000 small farmers and
fishers—primarily in the form of concessional loans. This approach contrasts sharply with
direct subsidies provided to other sectors, such as rice farmers. Furthermore, policies focused
on market access and supply chains primarily benefit intermediaries and traders, rather than
addressing the production challenges faced by small fisherfolk.

Notably, no substantial production subsidies were provided for the fisheries sector. This
omission reflects a failure to uphold the principles enshrined in Republic Act No. 8425,
including access to basic services, asset reform, sustainable resource development, and
participatory governance. Despite repeated warnings from international bodies and calls from
fisherfolk organizations such as Pambansang Lakas ng Kilusang Mamamalakaya ng Pilipinas
(PAMALAKAYA), the lack of targeted support has exacerbated the sector’s marginalization
and vulnerability.

Compounding these challenges is the recent surge in global oil prices, which has significantly
increased operational costs for fisherfolk. By early February, a typical fisher using 10 liters of
diesel per trip faced an additional ₱1,760 in monthly expenses (based on 16 fishing days),
increasing costs from ₱7,200 to ₱8,960. By March, this increase reached approximately
₱3,800 per month. As a result, some fisherfolk have reportedly ceased operations due to the
risk of financial losses.

Given these conditions, there is an urgent need to provide production subsidies for small
fisherfolk, alongside social amelioration for fish workers.

In response, the proposed measure seeks to provide P15,000 in production subsidy to
fisherfolk. This support aims to stimulate small-scale fishery production, contribute to
national food security, and facilitate economic recovery, while mitigating the disproportionate
impact of the crisis on already impoverished and vulnerable fisherfolk households.

The urgent passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in this
Congress assembled that:

SECTION 1. Short title. – This Act shall be known as the "P15,000 Fisherfolk Production
Subsidy Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policies. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
recognize the fisheries and aquatic resources sector as vital to national economic recovery
and the attainment of food security. The State likewise affirms the fisherfolk sector as a
frontliner in the food supply chain and shall ensure that it receives adequate support and
immediate relief, particularly in times of economic crisis, including but not limited to oil
price increases and other external shocks affecting livelihoods.

The State likewise recognizes the fisherfolk sector as among the most vulnerable and
marginalized sectors of society, having been disproportionately affected by the economic
crisis and continuing economic challenges. The State shall adopt timely and urgent support
interventions to safeguard their welfare and sustain their livelihoods.

SECTION 3. Beneficiaries. – The 2.7 million Filipinos registered fisherfolk shall directly
receive a production subsidy of Fifteen Thousand Pesos (P15,000.00), subject to the
following order of prioritization:
    a. Individuals belonging to households living below the poverty threshold;
   b.​ Municipal fishers and fish workers employed in, or operating, municipal fishing
       vessels, regardless of the status of their licensing or registration with the concerned
       local government unit;
   c.​ Fish workers employed in commercial fishing vessels and aquaculture operations,
       including but not limited to fish pens, fish cages, fish corrals or traps, fishponds,
       prawn farms, sea farms, salt beds, fish ports, fishing boats, and trawlers;
   d.​ Workers engaged in ancillary industries related to the fisheries sector, including the
       supply, construction, and maintenance of fishing vessels, gears, nets, and other fishing
       paraphernalia; fishery machine shops; and facilities such as hatcheries, nurseries, feed
       plants, cold storage and refrigeration facilities, processing plants, and other
       pre-harvest and post-harvest facilities; and
   e.​ Operators of small-scale commercial fishing vessels with a gross tonnage of 3.1 to 20.

The production subsidy shall be released on a quarterly basis: Provided, That in the event of a
state of calamity or state of national emergency, the production subsidy shall be released on a
monthly basis for the duration of such declaration.

The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) and the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources
(BFAR) shall conduct an annual census of fisherfolk and update the list of registered
fisherfolk.

SECTION 4. Functions of the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR). –
The Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR) shall:
   a.​ Administer and utilize the funds appropriated under this Act;
   b.​ Finalize the list of beneficiaries, guided by the criteria provided in the immediately
       preceding section, in close coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority and
       the concerned local government units;
   c.​ Formulate and implement the mechanisms for the direct distribution of the production
       subsidy to the beneficiaries;
   d.​ Ensure the prompt and efficient distribution of the production subsidy at the earliest
       possible time; and
   e.​ Regularly and publicly disclose the status of fund releases and distribution to ensure
       transparency and accountability.

SECTION 5. Appropriations. – For the implementation of this Act, the amount of Forty
Billion Five Hundred Million Pesos (P40,500,000,000) is hereby appropriated to the Bureau
of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR) to fund the provision of production subsidies
under this Act.

SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic
Resources (BFAR), in consultation with people’s organizations (POs) and non-government
organizations (NGOs) advocating for the rights and welfare of small-scale fisherfolk, shall
issue the implementing rules and regulations within fifteen (15) days from the effectivity of
this Act,
SECTION 7.​ Repealing clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
and other issuances not consistent with this Act are hereby repealed or amended as to
inconsistent parts.

SECTION 8.​ Separability clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared void, the
remaining parts shall remain in force and effect.

SECTION 9.​ Effectivity clause. – This Act shall take effect immediately after fifteen (15)
days from its publication in the Official Gazette.
