16           AN ACT RECOGNIZING BAMBOO AS A HIGH-VALUE AND
17   INDUSTRIAL CROP, FURTHER STRENGTHENING THE HIGH-VALUE CROPS
18      DEVELOPMENT ACT BY INSTITUTIONALIZING THE NATIONAL HIGH-
19    VALUE CROPS DEVELOPMENT AND BIOECONOMY PROGRAM, AMENDING
20   FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7900, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
21            “HIGH-VALUE CROPS DEVELOPMENT ACT OF 1995””
22
23                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE
24
25         Republic Act No. 7900, or the High-Value Crops Development Act of 1995,
26   established the national framework for the production, processing, marketing and
27   distribution of high-value crops. Three decades later, climate change, biotechnology,
28   plant-variety innovation, stricter product standards, supply-chain disruptions and
29   growing demand for renewable and bio-based materials require a more integrated,
30   science-based, market-oriented and institutionally sustained approach.
31
32         This bill expressly includes bamboo as a high-value crop and recognizes it as a
33   strategic industrial crop. Bamboo can provide food, fiber, biomass, engineered and
34   construction materials, furniture, packaging, bioenergy, biochar, biomaterials, and
35   other value-added products. Developed through appropriate agroforestry and multi-
36   storey farming systems, it can diversify rural livelihoods and support climate, soil,
37   water, and ecosystem objectives, subject to environmental, biodiversity, land-use,
38   agrarian reform and ancestral-domain laws.
39
1           The measure establishes within the Department of Agriculture the Office of the
2    Undersecretary for High-Value Crops Development, Innovation, and Bioeconomy. The
3    Office shall provide policy guidelines on technology management and innovation,
4    exercise direct oversight over the National High-Value Crops and Bioeconomy
5    Development Program, and supervise the functional roles, mandates and performance
6    of its component offices. The bill also proposes the creation of one (1) Assistant
7    Secretary position to support the Undersecretary and lead the agricultural statistics
8    and data science, knowledge management, market intelligence, geospatial services,
9    monitoring, evaluation and results reporting. A National High-Value Crops Program
10   Management Office will be created and headed by Director position to coordinate
11   implementation, provide technical services, farming systems, bamboo and industrial-
12   crop development, agribusiness, processing, standards, investment, and markets.
13
14          The bill further integrates research, innovation, biotechnology, genetic
15   resources, plant-variety development, quality planting materials, climate-resilient
16   production, integrated development areas, processing, enterprise development,
17   financing, standards, trade, and agricultural bioeconomy applications. It strengthens
18   the High-Value Crops Development Fund and permits the Department to propose an
19   indicative initial program envelope of Fifty Billion Pesos to support the five-year
20   implementation of the act, subject to the regular budget process and without creating
21   an automatic appropriation.
22
23            The proposed structure is designed as an integrating and coordinating
24   mechanism. It preserves the statutory mandates of the Philippine Statistics Authority
25   and other competent regulatory, research, standards, environmental, and local-
26   government institutions, while requiring compliance with government-optimization,
27   civil service, position-classification, budgeting, accounting and auditing laws.
28
29   In view of the foregoing, immediate approval of this measure is earnestly requested.

27         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Enhanced High-
28   Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy Act”.

30         SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
31   accelerate the sustainable, inclusive, science-based, climate-resilient, and market-
32   driven development of high-value crops as a principal engine of agricultural
33   modernization, agro-industrialization, rural employment, nutrition security, export
34   diversification, and a regenerative and circular bioeconomy. The State shall promote
35   productive landscapes and competitive enterprises that create value from biological
36   resources while conserving biodiversity, restoring ecosystems, improving soil and
37   water conditions, reducing waste, and increasing the incomes and resilience of
38   farmers, Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples, agrarian reform

1    beneficiaries, cooperatives, associations, workers, micro, small and medium
2    enterprises, and rural communities.

4          Towards this end, bamboo is expressly included as a high-value crop and
5    recognized as an industrial crop because of its renewable, multipurpose, and scalable
6    uses for food, feed, fuel, fiber, engineered materials, furniture, construction,
7    bioenergy, biochar, carbon sequestration, and other value-added products and
8    ecosystem services. The State shall strengthen the governance, financing, research
9    and innovation, production, processing, logistics, enterprise development, and
10   domestic and export market systems for all high-value crops through the National
11   High-Value Crops Development and Bioeconomy Office and the National High-Value
12   Crops Development and Bioeconomy Program.
