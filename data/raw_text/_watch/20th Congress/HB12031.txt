                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to prohibit the release, testing, processing, and sale of crops
and food products containing or derived from genetically modified organisms
(GMOs) in the Philippines.

       The Philippines has an established biosafety regulatory framework governing
genetically engineered crops and products, including procedures for risk
assessment, field testing, and commercial release. However, disputes over the
adequacy of these safeguards have raised continuing concerns about environmental
protection, public consultation, monitoring, and the long-term implications of
introducing genetically modified crops into local ecosystems.

       In April 2024, the Court of Appeals granted a Writ of Kalikasan concerning
Golden Rice and Bt eggplant, ordered restrictions on activities involving these crops,
and directed government agencies to strengthen risk assessment and monitoring
mechanisms. The ruling was subsequently amended in August 2024 to remove a
broader prohibition affecting other GMO applications1. This illustrates the
continuing policy debate over biosafety safeguards and the State’s responsibility to
protect public health and the environment.

       This measure strengthens safeguards by identifying existing GMO products,
conducting precautionary risk assessments, publishing an inventory, and
recommending their proper disposition. It seeks to replace the existing
product-approval approach, for activities within the bill’s scope, with a general
statutory prohibition intended to prioritize precaution and environmental
protection.

       Agriculture sustains millions of Filipinos, supports rural communities, and
contributes to the country’s food supply. Decisions involving agricultural
biotechnology therefore demand careful consideration of environmental risks,

                          AN ACT
PROHIBITING THE USE OF PRODUCTS CONTAINING GENETICALLY
MODIFIED ORGANISMS ON FOOD AND AGRICULTURE IN THE COUNTRY,
PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “GMO-Free Agriculture
Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared that the policy of the State
to protect and advance the right of the people to a safe and poison-free agriculture,
health and livelihood security, and a balanced and healthful ecology in accord with
the rhythm and harmony of nature.

       Towards this end, the State shall follow the precautionary principle in relation
to the field testing of genetically-engineered crops and the processing and
distribution of seeds, crops and food products consisting of, containing or derived
from genetically modified organisms (GMOs) to protect the people, the country’s
agriculture and environment for the sake of present and future generations. The
possible harmful effects of genetically modified crops and food on human health
and environment are irreversible. Thus, the burden of proof as to the safe use of
GMOs must be on the proponents of genetic engineering in agriculture.

       SEC. 3. Scope. – This Act shall cover the release, field testing, processing and
sale of crops and food products containing genetically modified organisms in the
country.

        SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

           a. Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) refers to a very long molecular structure,
        consisting of a string of units or genes that encode all the information
        regarding the structure and functioning of a living organism for its entire

       lifespan, as well as biological information that is passed on from one
       generation to the next;

          b.​ Field Testing refers to the deliberate release or intentional introduction
       of a GMO, or a combination of GMOs, into a controlled environment with
       provisions for containment such as physical barriers, or a combination of
       physical barriers together with chemical and/or biological barriers used to
       limit their contact with the general population and the environment;

          c.​ Genetically modified organism (GMO) means an organism in which
       the genetic material has been altered or modified in a way that does not occur
       naturally by mating and/or natural recombination;

          d.​ Genetically modified crops and products refer to the harvests and
       commodities consisting of, or containing a GMO, or a combination of GMOs;

           e.​ Genetic engineering, also called genetic modification, is the term given
       to the laboratory manipulation of a living organism’s genetic code involving
       the introduction of foreign DNA or synthetic genes into the said organism;

           f.​ National Committee on Biosafety of the Philippines (NCBP) refers to
       an attached agency of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST)
       created by virtue of Executive Order 430 (s. 1990) and tasked, among others,
       to identify potential risks associated with experiments involving GMOs and
       to recommend measures to minimize such risks;

          g.​ Organism means any biological entity capable of replication or of
       transferring genetic material;

          h.​ Precautionary principle is a precept that any action should not be
       pursued if the consequence is not certain or potentially dangerous;

          i.​ Processing means the breeding, preparation and other activities which
       through physical, chemical, or biological processes alter the exterior texture or
       form or inner substance of a product in such manner as to prepare it for
       special use to which it could not have put in its original form or condition;

           j.​ Precautionary risk assessment means to identify and evaluate the
       potential adverse effects of living modified organisms which the deliberate
       release of GMOs into the environment may pose. It should be carried out in a
       scientifically sound and transparent manner; and

          k.​ Sale refers to the act of selling and/or supplying and/or making a
       product available to other parties.

       SEC. 5. Prohibition. – The release, laboratory and field testing, processing, sale
and similar acts of crops and food products consisting of, containing or derived from
genetically modified organisms shall be prohibited in the country.

       SEC. 6. Exempting Clause. – Studies on crops and products containing
genetically modified organisms by an independent panel of scientists to be selected
and supervised by the NCBP and Food and Drugs Administration (FDA) together
with concerned non-government and people’s organizations shall be exempted from
the prohibition.

       SEC. 7. Existing GMO Products. – The NCBP, in coordination with the
Department of Agriculture (DA), the FDA, and relevant non-government
organizations, shall identify all existing GMO products currently under laboratory
testing, pilot testing, or multi-location testing, as well as those commercially
distributed in the Philippines prior to the effectivity of this Act. Upon completion of
this inventory, the NCBP shall publish a public list of these products, conduct a
precautionary risk assessment for each, and issue recommendations on their proper
disposition. The subsequent sale, processing, laboratory and field testing, release
and similar acts of such products in the list shall be prohibited indefinitely until such
time that said products have been declared free from genetically modified
organisms.

       SEC. 8. Monitoring. – A monitoring committee composed of the Department
of Trade and Industry (DTI), DA, Department of Health (DOH), Department of
Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), DOST, Bureau of Customs (BOC),
non-government organizations and people’s organizations, shall closely monitor the
sale, processing of products consisting of, containing or derived from genetically
engineered organisms. The monitoring committee shall recommend for disposition
any product found to be containing genetically-engineered organisms.

       SEC. 9. Penalties. – Any person or persons found violating the provisions of
this Act shall be penalized with imprisonment of not less than five (5) years but not
more than fifteen (15) years and a fine of not less than One hundred thousand pesos
(Php 100,000.00) but not more than One million pesos (Php 1,000,000.00), at the
discretion of the court depending on the gravity of the offense. Repetition of the
offense shall entail the maximum limit of the penalty and fine.

       SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act and after initiating a highly consultative process involving
sectoral representations, the NCBP, DA, DENR, and the FDA shall formulate and
publish the implementing rules and regulations for the effective implementation of
this Act.

       SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

       SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
