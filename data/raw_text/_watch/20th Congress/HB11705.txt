                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE
For decades, national infrastructure development has largely been concentrated on urban
infrastructure, including commercial centers, major transportation networks, road
expansions, and other public works. While these investments contribute to economic
growth, agricultural infrastructure has not received the sustained investment necessary to
address the needs of communities. As an agricultural country, the government must
recognize that agricultural infrastructure is most essential to national development.
The consequences of inadequate agricultural infrastructure are particularly felt by the
country's poorest communities. In 2023, the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported a
22.1% rural poverty incidence, with farmers and fisherfolk among the poorest sectors at
27.0% and 27.4%, respectively.
Yet despite their dependence on agriculture for livelihood, these communities continue to
face severe infrastructure deficiencies. The Department of Agriculture (DA) reported a
backlog of approximately 60,000 kilometers of farm-to-market roads, while agricultural
infrastructure still lacks a sufficiently integrated and sustained national flagship
development program. This neglect is evident in the Third District of Zamboanga del Norte,
which has one of the longest coastlines in Region IX, where the first cold storage facility has
only recently been introduced despite the longstanding dependence of its communities on
fishing. Without adequate infrastructure, farmers and fisherfolk remain trapped in poverty,
unable to fully realize the economic potential of their livelihoods.
This measure therefore seeks to correct this longstanding imbalance by institutionalizing
agricultural and fisheries infrastructure as a national investment priority, supported by a
mandatory budgetary allocation, multiyear planning, and safeguards for project completion
and maintenance. By directing sustained public investment toward rural and agricultural
communities, the State can alleviate poverty, strengthen agricultural productivity, and
advance national food security.
In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
   INSTITUTIONALIZING THE PRIORITIZATION, SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT,
     MAINTENANCE, AND PROTECTION OF AGRICULTURAL AND FISHERIES
INFRASTRUCTURE, ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL AGRICULTURAL INFRASTRUCTURE
      DEVELOPMENT PROGRAM, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Agricultural Infrastructure
Development and Protection Act."
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to recognize
agricultural and fisheries infrastructure as an essential component of national food
security, economic development, and rural poverty reduction.
Towards this end, the State shall:
(a) Accord priority to agricultural and fisheries infrastructure in national infrastructure
planning and investment;
(b) Ensure the equitable distribution of infrastructure investments, with particular
consideration for impoverished, geographically isolated, and underserved agricultural
communities;
(c) Promote integrated infrastructure development that supports agricultural production,
processing, storage, transportation, and market access;
(d) Ensure the timely completion, effective utilization, regular maintenance, and
rehabilitation of agricultural infrastructure; and
(e) Strengthen transparency, accountability, and the protection of public investments
throughout the useful life of infrastructure facilities.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:
(a) Agricultural and Fisheries Infrastructure refers to public physical facilities and
supporting systems primarily intended to improve agricultural or fisheries production,
post-harvest handling, processing, storage, transportation, distribution, and market access,
including farm-to-market roads, irrigation systems, fish ports, post-harvest and cold
storage facilities, and related utilities;
(b) Infrastructure Lifecycle refers to the successive stages of project identification,
planning, design, construction, commissioning, operation, maintenance, rehabilitation,
and, where necessary, replacement or decommissioning;
(c) Critical Infrastructure Deficiency refers to the absence, inadequacy, deterioration, or
inoperability of infrastructure that materially impairs agricultural productivity, food
security, market access, or the livelihoods of farmers and fisherfolk; and
(d) Implementing Agency refers to the national government agency, government-owned or
-controlled corporation, or other public entity legally responsible for undertaking an
infrastructure project covered by this Act.
SEC. 4. National Agricultural Infrastructure Development Program. — There is hereby
established a National Agricultural Infrastructure Development Program, hereinafter
referred to as the Program, to be administered by the Department of Agriculture (DA), in
coordination with the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev),
Department of Budget and Management (DBM), Department of Public Works and Highways
(DPWH), National Irrigation Administration (NIA), Philippine Fisheries Development
Authority (PFDA), and other concerned agencies.
The Program shall consolidate and harmonize existing agricultural and fisheries
infrastructure initiatives and shall prioritize:
(a) Construction, improvement, and              rehabilitation   of    farm-to-market     and
farm-to-coast-to-market roads and bridges;
(b) Development and rehabilitation of irrigation, drainage, and agricultural water
management systems;
(c) Establishment and upgrading of post-harvest, processing, storage, and cold-chain
facilities;
(d) Construction and improvement of fish ports, fish landing facilities, and fisheries-related
infrastructure;
(e) Provision of essential electricity, water, communications, and logistics infrastructure
directly supporting agricultural and fisheries activities; and
(f) Rehabilitation, modernization, and protection of existing agricultural and fisheries
infrastructure.
The Program shall complement and strengthen existing programs under Republic Act No.
8435 and Republic Act No. 10601, without duplicating their implementing structures or
diminishing the statutory mandates of concerned agencies.
SEC. 5. Mandatory Prioritization in Infrastructure Planning. — The DA, DBM, and
DEPDev shall ensure that agricultural and fisheries infrastructure receives preferential
consideration in the formulation of national infrastructure investment programs and
annual budget proposals, consistent with the constitutional priority accorded to education
and other applicable laws.
The DA shall identify and rank priority agricultural infrastructure projects according to the
needs-based criteria established under this Act. Concerned implementing agencies shall
incorporate the duly validated priority projects within their respective infrastructure
investment plans.
Where a higher-ranked, implementation-ready agricultural infrastructure project remains
unfunded while a lower-ranked project of comparable purpose is proposed for funding, the
concerned agency shall provide a written justification based on technical, fiscal, legal, or
other objective considerations.
SEC. 6. Needs-Based Infrastructure Prioritization. — The identification and ranking of
projects under the Program shall be governed by the following criteria:
(a) Severity of existing agricultural infrastructure deficiencies;
(b) Poverty incidence and socioeconomic conditions of the intended beneficiaries;
(c) Agricultural and fisheries production potential and contribution to food security;
(d) Number and needs of farmers, fisherfolk, and agricultural communities benefiting from
the project;
(e) Geographic isolation, market accessibility, and vulnerability to natural disasters;
(f) Historical levels of public infrastructure investment and the availability of existing
facilities; and
(g) Technical feasibility, cost-effectiveness, operational sustainability, and expected
socioeconomic benefits.
The DA shall establish and publish a uniform, weighted evaluation system incorporating
these criteria, with appropriate indicators for different categories of infrastructure.
No locality shall be excluded solely on account of low existing agricultural output,
population, or fiscal capacity where infrastructure investment may reasonably unlock
productive potential or address critical deprivation.
SEC. 7. Multiyear Infrastructure Development Plan. — The DA, in coordination with
concerned agencies, shall formulate and maintain a rolling five-year National Agricultural
Infrastructure Development Plan, which shall be reviewed and updated annually.
The Plan shall contain:
(a) A national inventory of agricultural infrastructure and identified deficiencies;
(b) Priority projects ranked according to Section 6 of this Act;
(c) Estimated project costs, implementation schedules, and responsible agencies;
(d) Projected requirements for operation, maintenance, rehabilitation, and replacement; and
(e) Measurable targets for agricultural productivity, market accessibility, infrastructure
utilization, and reduction of post-harvest losses.
The Plan shall serve as the principal sectoral reference for the preparation of annual
infrastructure budget proposals and shall be harmonized with the Philippine Development
Plan, Public Investment Program, and other applicable national and local development
plans.
Inclusion in the Plan shall not, by itself, constitute an appropriation or authority to incur
obligations.
SEC. 8. Integrated Infrastructure Development. — Projects under the Program shall,
whenever technically feasible and economically justified, be planned as components of
integrated agricultural production and distribution systems.
The DA shall assess whether proposed projects have adequate access to necessary
supporting infrastructure, including transportation networks, irrigation, electricity, water
supply, storage facilities, and markets.
Projects requiring complementary facilities shall identify the responsible agencies, funding
arrangements, and implementation schedules for such facilities before approval for
implementation.
A project shall not be disqualified solely because supporting infrastructure is presently
unavailable, provided that a feasible and funded or fundable plan for its development is
incorporated into the project implementation strategy.
SEC. 9. Project Readiness and Implementation Requirements. — No new infrastructure
project shall proceed to procurement for construction unless the concerned implementing
agency has established:
(a) A validated need and clearly identified intended beneficiaries;
(b) Technical feasibility and compliance with applicable engineering, environmental, and
safety standards;
(c) Required site availability, rights-of-way, permits, and clearances, or a lawful and
sufficiently definite plan for securing the same before the relevant work commences;
(d) A realistic implementation schedule and funding requirements;
(e) An identified entity responsible for operation and maintenance; and
(f) An operation and maintenance plan, including estimated lifecycle costs and proposed
funding sources.
The implementation of projects shall be governed by Republic Act No. 12009, or the New
Government Procurement Act, and other applicable laws. Nothing herein shall authorize
procurement or construction inconsistent with statutory project-readiness requirements.
SEC. 10. Completion and Rehabilitation Priority. — In preparing annual budget
proposals, implementing agencies shall give priority consideration to:
(a) Completion of ongoing and partially completed infrastructure projects;
(b) Rehabilitation of deteriorated, damaged, or nonoperational facilities; and
(c) Construction of new facilities addressing critical deficiencies.
The allocation of resources shall be based on technical urgency, socioeconomic benefits,
cost-effectiveness, and existing contractual and legal obligations.
No new project shall be preferred over the completion or rehabilitation of an existing facility
solely because it constitutes new construction.
SEC. 11. Mandatory Infrastructure Maintenance and Protection. — Every agricultural
infrastructure project covered by this Act shall have an approved operation and
maintenance plan identifying the responsible entity, maintenance activities, inspection
schedules, estimated costs, and funding sources.
The responsible implementing or operating agency shall:
(a) Conduct periodic inspections and condition assessments;
(b) Include necessary maintenance and rehabilitation requirements in its annual budget
proposals;
(c) Maintain records of inspections, repairs, and infrastructure condition;
(d) Undertake timely corrective measures against deterioration, safety hazards, and
operational deficiencies; and
(e) Establish appropriate measures to safeguard facilities against misuse, unauthorized
alteration, and preventable damage.
For infrastructure transferred to local government units, cooperatives, or other authorized
operators, the terms of turnover shall specify the respective responsibilities for operation,
maintenance, financing, and performance monitoring.
The DA shall provide technical assistance and, subject to available appropriations and
applicable laws, maintenance support to financially disadvantaged local government units
operating critical agricultural infrastructure.
SEC. 12. Operational Readiness and Turnover. — No completed infrastructure project
shall be certified as operational unless the concerned implementing agency has verified:
(a) Compliance with applicable construction and performance standards;
(b) Functionality of essential facilities, equipment, and utilities;
(c) Availability of qualified operators or appropriate management arrangements;
(d) Existence of an approved maintenance and financing plan; and
(e) Accessibility and suitability of the facility for its intended beneficiaries.
Where any requirement remains unmet, the agency shall identify the deficiency, responsible
party, and corrective action required.
Certification of operational readiness shall be separate from, and shall not replace,
inspection, acceptance, warranty, and other requirements under procurement and auditing
laws.
SEC. 13. Agricultural Infrastructure Registry and Public Monitoring. — The DA shall
maintain a centralized, publicly accessible Agricultural Infrastructure Registry, integrating
existing agency databases and geotagged monitoring systems to avoid duplication.
The Registry shall contain, at a minimum, the location, implementing agency, approved
cost, contractor where applicable, project status, completion date, operating entity,
condition, and utilization status of projects covered by this Act.
The Registry shall likewise identify delayed, abandoned, nonoperational, and substantially
underutilized facilities, together with the action being undertaken to address such
deficiencies.
Information shall be updated at least quarterly, subject to applicable restrictions under law.
SEC. 14. Performance Evaluation and Corrective Action. — The DA shall establish
performance indicators to evaluate the effectiveness of completed agricultural
infrastructure, including accessibility, utilization, reduction of production or transportation
costs, reduction of post-harvest losses, and contribution to agricultural productivity, as
appropriate to the project.
The responsible agency shall conduct an initial operational assessment within one (1) year
after commissioning and periodic assessments thereafter.
Where a project is found to be substantially underutilized, nonoperational, or defective, the
responsible agency shall prepare a corrective action plan within sixty (60) days from such
finding, specifying remedial measures, responsible officials, required resources, and
completion targets.
Unresolved material deficiencies shall be reported to the DA and the appropriate oversight
or investigative authorities, without prejudice to applicable contractual remedies and
administrative, civil, or criminal proceedings.
SEC. 15. Protection Against Abandonment, Misuse, and Unauthorized Disposition. —
Agricultural infrastructure funded under this Act shall be used and maintained for its
approved public purpose.
No such facility shall be abandoned, substantially altered, transferred, disposed of, or
converted to a use inconsistent with its approved purpose except upon a documented
determination by the competent authority that such action is lawful, necessary, and
justified by public interest.
Any proposed change of use shall consider the effect on intended farmer and fisherfolk
beneficiaries and, where feasible, provide for the continuity or replacement of the affected
public service.
Unauthorized disposition, diversion of project resources, fraudulent certification, and other
unlawful acts shall be investigated and prosecuted under applicable laws.
SEC. 16. Institutional Responsibilities. — The DA shall serve as the lead coordinating
and monitoring agency for the Program.
The DEPDev shall ensure consistency with national development and public investment
priorities. The DBM shall consider the Program in budget preparation and provide guidance
on appropriate funding requirements.
The DPWH, NIA, PFDA, and other concerned agencies shall retain their respective statutory
functions and technical responsibilities, subject to applicable laws and duly authorized
interagency implementation arrangements.
Local government units shall participate in project identification, implementation, and
maintenance consistent with the Local Government Code and existing laws. Nothing in this
Act shall diminish local autonomy or impose an unfunded mandatory transfer of national
government infrastructure responsibilities to local government units.
SEC. 17. Mandatory Allocation for Agricultural and Fisheries Infrastructure. — To
ensure sustained investment in agricultural development and national food security, the
State shall allocate not less than twenty percent (20%) of the total annual National
Government infrastructure appropriations to agricultural and fisheries infrastructure,
including the construction, completion, rehabilitation, modernization, and maintenance of
facilities covered by this Act.
For purposes of this Section, the allocation shall be computed based on the total
infrastructure appropriations funded under the annual General Appropriations Act,
excluding infrastructure expenditures financed by local government funds and projects not
principally intended for public infrastructure development. The DBM, in coordination with
the DA and the DEPDev, shall establish a uniform classification and accounting framework
to ensure the accurate identification of qualifying agricultural and fisheries infrastructure
expenditures.
Only projects directly and substantially supporting agricultural and fisheries production,
processing, storage, transportation, distribution, and market access shall qualify for
purposes of compliance with the mandatory allocation. Incidental or indirect agricultural
benefits shall not be sufficient.
The DBM shall ensure that the proposed National Expenditure Program reflects the
minimum allocation prescribed herein. Any deficiency in the proposed allocation shall be
expressly identified and justified in the accompanying budget documents submitted to
Congress.
No provision of this Act shall be construed as authorizing automatic appropriations,
releases, or disbursements without lawful budgetary authority.
SEC. 18. Annual Report to Congress. — The DA, in coordination with the DBM and
concerned implementing agencies, shall submit to Congress, within ninety (90) days after
the end of each fiscal year, an annual report containing:
(a) Total appropriations, obligations, and disbursements for projects covered by the
Program;
(b) Projects completed, ongoing, delayed, abandoned, or nonoperational;
(c) Infrastructure deficiencies remaining unaddressed;
(d) Maintenance and rehabilitation requirements and actions undertaken;
(e) Performance outcomes and identified implementation deficiencies; and
(f) Corrective actions and recommendations for succeeding fiscal years.
The report shall be published on the DA's official website.
SEC. 19. Accountability. — Public officers and employees responsible for implementing
this Act shall be accountable for the faithful performance of their duties.
Any willful violation of this Act, falsification of required project records, fraudulent
certification, unlawful diversion of project funds, or gross neglect resulting in substantial
prejudice to the government shall, where the elements of an offense or administrative
violation are present, be subject to applicable administrative, civil, and criminal laws.
The DA shall refer prima facie evidence of irregularities to the Commission on Audit, Office
of the Ombudsman, or other competent authorities, as appropriate.
Nothing in this Act shall impair the independent constitutional functions of the Commission
on Audit or authorize individual legislators to participate in procurement, project execution,
or disbursement of funds.
SEC. 20. Congressional Oversight. — The appropriate committees of the Senate and the
House of Representatives shall exercise oversight over the implementation of this Act,
particularly the equitable allocation, utilization, completion, and maintenance of
agricultural infrastructure investments.
Such oversight shall be exercised consistently with the separation of powers and shall not
authorize congressional participation in the implementation of individual projects.
SEC. 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty (180) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DA, in consultation with the DEPDev, DBM, DPWH, NIA,
PFDA, Department of the Interior and Local Government, and representatives of farmers
and fisherfolk, shall promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations.
Such rules shall prescribe the project prioritization methodology, planning and reporting
standards, minimum maintenance requirements, operational readiness procedures, and
performance evaluation mechanisms.
SEC. 22. Transitory Provisions. — Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, the
DA shall:
(a) Consolidate existing agricultural and fisheries infrastructure inventories;
(b) Complete an initial nationwide assessment of critical deficiencies and unfinished or
nonoperational projects;
(c) Publish the initial National Agricultural Infrastructure Development Plan and priority
project rankings; and
(d) Establish the Agricultural Infrastructure Registry using existing government information
systems to the extent practicable.
Existing infrastructure programs and ongoing projects shall continue under their respective
lawful appropriations, contracts, and implementation arrangements. They shall be
progressively integrated into the Program without impairing vested contractual rights or
interrupting essential services.
SEC. 23. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the available appropriations of the DA and concerned
implementing agencies, subject to existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
SEC. 24. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
SEC. 25. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.
SEC. 26. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
