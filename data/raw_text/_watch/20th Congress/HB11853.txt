                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

The quality of an education system is measured not only by the opportunities it provides to the
majority of learners, but also by how well it responds to those whose learning needs require
greater attention. For learners with disabilities and other special educational needs, access to
meaningful education depends considerably on the presence of teachers equipped with the
knowledge, patience, skills, and professional competence necessary to address their individual
circumstances.

Special Education (SPED) teachers occupy a distinct and indispensable place in the Philippine
public education system. Their responsibilities extend beyond the ordinary delivery of
classroom instruction. They must understand different disabilities and learning conditions,
develop and implement individualized instructional approaches, adapt teaching materials and
assessment methods, monitor developmental progress, and work closely with parents,
guardians, and other professionals involved in the education and welfare of their learners. These
responsibilities demand specialized preparation, continuing professional development, and a
considerable degree of professional commitment.

Despite the importance of their work, the compensation of SPED teachers must be given greater
legislative attention. The additional qualifications and specialized responsibilities required of
them should be adequately recognized in the government’s compensation structure. It is difficult
to speak of strengthening inclusive education while leaving insufficiently addressed the need to
attract, retain, and appropriately compensate the very professionals tasked with making it
possible.

The Constitution provides a clear foundation for this measure. Article XIV, Section 1 of the
1987 Constitution directs the State to protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality
education at all levels and to take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all.
More particularly, Article XIV, Section 2(5) of the 1987 Constitution recognizes the need to
provide persons with disabilities with appropriate training, while Article XIV, Section 5(5) of
the 1987 Constitution directs the State to assign the highest budgetary priority to education and
ensure that teaching attracts and retains its rightful share of the best available talents through
adequate remuneration and other means of professional fulfillment.

These constitutional commitments are given further expression in Republic Act No. 11650,
otherwise known as the “Instituting a Policy of Inclusion and Services for Learners with
Disabilities in Support of Inclusive Education Act.” The law institutionalizes inclusive education
and recognizes the importance of equipping teachers, school administrators, and other personnel
with the capabilities necessary to identify, address, and respond to the diverse educational needs
of learners with disabilities. It likewise provides for the establishment of Inclusive Learning
Resource Centers to facilitate the delivery of appropriate educational and related services.

The implementation of this law necessarily depends on a capable and adequately supported
teaching workforce. Buildings, learning materials, assistive devices, and institutional facilities,
however necessary, cannot independently accomplish the objectives of inclusive education. The
people who translate these policies into actual learning opportunities are the teachers who work
directly with the learners. Their professional contribution must therefore be matched by a
compensation system that recognizes the demands of their work.

Republic Act No. 4670, or the Magna Carta for Public School Teachers, likewise provides a
statutory basis for improving teachers’ economic and professional conditions. Section 1 declares
the policy of the State to promote and improve the social and economic status of public school
teachers, their living and working conditions, terms of employment, and career prospects.
Section 15 further provides that teachers’ salaries should compare favorably with those in other
occupations requiring equivalent qualifications, training, and abilities, and should recognize
positions involving higher qualifications and greater responsibilities.

The proposed upgrading of the minimum salary grade level of public-school SPED teachers
from Salary Grade 14 to Salary Grade 22 seeks to give concrete recognition to these
responsibilities. It is intended to establish a compensation framework that considers the
qualifications, competencies, and length of service of the teachers concerned, while ensuring
that the adjustment will not be diminished or prejudiced by general salary increases granted to
government personnel.

The proposed salary upgrading is therefore an investment in the people who make inclusive
education possible. By recognizing the specialized work of SPED teachers through an
appropriate compensation framework, the State takes a necessary step toward strengthening its
public education system and fulfilling its commitment to leave no learner behind.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT UPGRADING THE MINIMUM SALARY GRADE OF PUBLIC SCHOOL
   SPECIAL EDUCATION TEACHERS FROM SALARY GRADE 14 TO SALARY
                GRADE 22 AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Increase in the Minimum Salary Grade Level. - The present minimum salary
grade level of public-school Special Education (SPED) teachers in the elementary and
secondary schools shall be upgraded from Grade 14 to Grade 22: Provided, however, That the
salary upgrading shall be differentiated in accordance with the 5 qualifications and length of
service rendered by teachers and shall not be prejudiced by across-the-board salary adjustments.


SECTION 2. Priority in Budget Allocation. - The National Government shall appropriate such
amount, as may be necessary to carry out the objectives of this Act: Provided, That the salary
increase of public-school SPED teachers shall take priority over other non-educational and non-
agricultural budgetary allocations.


 SECTION 3. Appropriations. - The Department of Education (DepEd) shall come up with a
 specific programmed budget needed to cover the expenses for the upgrading in salary levels
 for all the corresponding SPED teacher Plantilla positions for a period of at least five (5)
years to allow the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) to make the necessary
budgetary adjustments to facilitate the smooth implementation of this Act.
The amount necessary to implement the provisions of this Act shall be included in the General
Appropriations Act for the year following the approval of this Act.


SECTION 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The DepEd and the DBM shall

promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.


SECTION 5. Repealing Clause. — All provisions of laws, orders, decrees, including rules and
regulations inconsistent herewith are hereby repealed and/or modified accordingly.


SECTION 6. Separability Clause. - If any part or provision of this Act shall be held
unconstitutional or invalid, other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby shall continue
to be in full force and effect.


SECTION 7. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the

Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
