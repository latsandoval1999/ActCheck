                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       At present, many provincial and district hospitals lack adequate medical
equipment, infrastructure, and specialized services, resulting in limited capacity to
respond to emergencies and address complex medical conditions. Thus, there is a
need to address these gaps through an upgrade of hospital facilities, including the
provision of modern diagnostic equipment, ambulances, and the establishment of
specialty units where it is needed most.

       The “Universal Health Facilities Enhancement Act” seeks to strengthen the
Philippine public health system by modernizing and upgrading provincial and
district hospitals across the country. It recognizes the State’s constitutional duty to
protect and promote the right to health of all citizens and aims to ensure that quality
healthcare services are accessible, efficient, and equitable, especially in underserved
and geographically isolated areas.



                                                                                       1
        To ensure effective implementation, the bill creates the Hospital Upgrade
 Commission (HUC), a multi-sectoral body composed of key government agencies,
 legislative representatives, and local government units. The HUC will be tasked with
 conducting a nationwide assessment of existing hospital facilities, developing a
 National Hospital Upgrade Agenda, and overseeing the prioritization, procurement,
 and implementation of hospital improvements.

       Overall, this measure seeks to build a more resilient, efficient, and equitable
 healthcare system by ensuring that all Filipinos—regardless of location—have
 access to modern, well-equipped, and responsive hospital services.

       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Universal Health
Facilities Enhancement Act”.


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote and protect the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness
among them. Towards this end, the State shall take appropriate measures to



                                                                                    1
modernize and upgrade the capacity of provincial and district hospitals, enhance
their capability to respond to medical emergencies, and ensure access to quality
health services nationwide.


SEC. 3. Objectives. - The objectives of this Act are:
   1. To upgrade all provincial and district hospitals with modern medical and
      diagnostic equipment;
   2. To provide adequate and well-equipped ambulance services for efficient
      patient transport and emergency response;
   3. To establish specialty units, including but not limited to trauma care, intensive
      care, maternal and neonatal care, and infectious disease units; and
   4. To ensure the equitable distribution of upgraded facilities and services across
      regions to improve health outcomes.


SEC. 4. Creation of the Hospital Upgrade Commission (HUC). - To implement
this Act, there is hereby created the Hospital Upgrade Commission (HUC), which
shall oversee and coordinate the upgrading of provincial and district hospitals in
cooperation with the Department of Health (DOH), local government units (LGUs),
and relevant stakeholders.


SEC. 5. Composition of HUC. - The HUC shall be composed of:


As Co-Chairpersons:
   1. Secretary of the Department of Health;
   2. Chairperson of the Senate Committee on Health and Demography;
   3. Chairperson of the House Committee on Health.


                                                                                      2
As Commissioners:
   1. Secretary of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM);
   2. Secretary of the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);
   3. President of the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth);
   4. Representative from the League of Provinces of the Philippines;
   5. Representative from the League of Cities of the Philippines;
   6. Representative from the League of Municipalities of the Philippines.


SEC. 6. Powers and Functions of HUC. - The HUC shall have the following
powers and functions:


         a. Conduct a nationwide assessment of existing provincial and district
            hospital facilities;
         b. Formulate and implement a roadmap for hospital upgrades, including
            procurement of equipment and ambulances;
         c. Maintain and regularly update a baseline criteria in identifying and
            prioritizing provincial and district hospitals for upgrading;
         d. Coordinate with LGUs and health agencies to establish specialty units
            in strategic locations;
         e. Monitor and evaluate the implementation of this Act, ensuring
            transparency and accountability;
         f. Submit annual reports to Congress on the status and impact of the
            upgrades.




                                                                                 3
SEC. 7. National Hospital Upgrade Agenda. - The HUC shall formulate,
implement, and update a National Hospital Upgrade Agenda to guide the
modernization and establishment of specialty units, subject to regular evaluation and
consultation with stakeholders. The Upgrade Agenda shall provide a baseline criteria
in identifying and prioritizing provincial and district hospitals for upgrading. Such
criteria shall include, but not be limited to, the following:


   a. Service Capability and Gaps — Hospital classification, range of services
      offered, and identified deficiencies relative to the health needs of the
      catchment population;


   b. Population Coverage and Demand — Population size, growth rate, and
      utilization rates of the hospital, including patient load and bed occupancy;


   c. Health Indicators and Burden of Disease — Prevalence of priority diseases
      and conditions within the hospital’s service area;


   d. Geographic Accessibility — Location of the hospital, including its
      accessibility to underserved, remote, or geographically isolated and
      disadvantaged areas (GIDAs);


   e. Infrastructure and Equipment — Structural integrity, safety, and adequacy of
      existing facilities, utilities, and medical equipment;


   f. Human Resources for Health — Availability, capacity, and distribution of
      medical, nursing, and allied health personnel;


                                                                                     4
   g. Local Government Capacity and Commitment — Demonstrated support of
      the concerned local government unit, including financial counter-parting and
      sustainability plans;


   h. Emergency Preparedness and Referral Role — Capacity of the hospital to
      respond to emergencies and its function within local and regional referral
      networks; and


   i. Equity Considerations — Prioritization of facilities serving vulnerable and
      underserved populations.


The Commission may refine, expand, and operationalize these criteria through the
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), including the adoption of a scoring,
weighting, or tiered prioritization system, consistent with national health policies
and standards.


SEC. 8. Audit, Inspection, and Accountability for Procured Facilities and
Equipment. - All facilities, infrastructure, medical equipment, and other assets
procured, constructed, or upgraded under this Act shall be subject to regular audit,
inspection, and post-procurement evaluation to ensure transparency, efficiency, and
intended utilization.


   a. Mandatory Audit. — The use of funds and the procurement, delivery,
      installation, and maintenance of facilities and equipment shall be subject to




                                                                                   5
   audit by the Commission on Audit (COA) in accordance with existing laws,
   rules, and regulations.


b. Inventory and Asset Registry. — All recipient hospitals and implementing
   entities shall maintain an updated inventory and asset registry of all procured
   items, indicating their condition, location, utilization status, and maintenance
   history, in accordance with COA and applicable government accounting and
   property management rules.


c. Inspection and Acceptance. — No facility or equipment shall be deemed
   accepted unless it has undergone proper inspection, testing, and certification
   by qualified personnel to ensure compliance with technical specifications and
   safety standards prescribed by the Department of Health (DOH) and other
   relevant agencies.


d. Post-Procurement Evaluation. — The Hospital Upgrade Commission, in
   coordination with the DOH and concerned local government units, shall
   conduct periodic evaluations to determine whether procured facilities and
   equipment are functional, properly maintained, and effectively utilized for
   their intended purpose.


e. Transparency and Reporting. — The Commission shall require implementing
   entities to submit regular reports on procurement, delivery status, utilization,
   and maintenance of facilities and equipment, which shall be made available
   to oversight agencies and, subject to applicable laws, to the public.




                                                                                  6
   f. Implementing Guidelines. — The Commission, in consultation with the COA,
      DOH, and other relevant agencies, shall prescribe detailed guidelines under
      the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), including audit protocols,
      inspection standards, reporting formats, and performance indicators.


SEC. 9. Accountability and Liability. — All officials, personnel, and entities
involved in the planning, procurement, delivery, installation, operation, and
maintenance of facilities and equipment under this Act shall be held to the highest
standards of accountability, transparency, and diligence.


Any public official or employee who, through act or omission, is found to have
caused undue injury to the government or to have given unwarranted benefits,
advantage, or preference to any party in the implementation of this Act shall be
subject to administrative, civil, and/or criminal liability in accordance with existing
laws, including but not limited to Republic Act No. 3019, otherwise known as the
Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act, and other applicable statutes.


The Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) shall provide detailed procedures
for reporting, investigation, adjudication, and enforcement of accountability
measures, including due process safeguards and grievance mechanisms.


SEC. 10. Whistleblower Protection. - The State shall ensure the protection of
individuals who, in good faith, report, disclose, or provide information on any act of
corrupt practices, misuse of funds, irregular procurement, or improper utilization of
facilities and equipment under this Act.




                                                                                      7
Protected disclosures shall include reports on fraud, corruption, collusion, gross
mismanagement, conflict of interest, violations of procurement laws and standards,
delivery of substandard or defective equipment, ghost deliveries, overpricing, or any
analogous acts prejudicial to the government.


Protection shall be afforded to any public officer or employee, contractor, supplier,
consultant, or private individual who makes a disclosure in good faith, whether
anonymously or otherwise, through authorized reporting channels.


Protection under this Section shall apply only to disclosures made in good faith and
with reasonable belief in the truth of the information provided. Any person who
knowingly makes false or malicious allegations shall not be entitled to protection
and may be subject to appropriate administrative, civil, or criminal liability.


SEC. 11. Standing Committees. - The HUC may establish standing committees
composed of members and experts to provide technical advice on infrastructure,
equipment standards, specialty units, training programs and other matters as may be
necessary to further implement this Act.


SEC. 12. Advisory Support Council. - An Advisory Support Council (ASC) shall
be created, composed of recognized experts from the health sector, academe,
business, civil society, and patient advocacy groups, to provide technical and
strategic advice to the HUC.




                                                                                   8
SEC. 13. Technical Secretariat and Executive Director. - A Technical Secretariat,
headed by an Executive Director (ED) with expertise in health systems management
and infrastructure development, shall support the HUC.


SEC. 14. Appropriations. - The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
included in the annual General Appropriations Act. Additional funds may be sourced
from government health programs, official development assistance, and other
allowable funding sources.


SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The DOH shall, in coordination
with the relevant agencies, promulgate the rules and regulations to implement the
provisions of this Act within sixty (60) days after its effectivity.


SEC. 16. Separability Clause. - If any part or provision of this Act is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.


SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders, issuances, decrees, rules
and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are deemed
amended, modified or repealed accordingly.


SEC. 18. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
