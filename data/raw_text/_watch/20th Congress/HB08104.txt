NUTRITION EDUCATION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Childhood overweight and obesity are rising in the Philippines and can lead to serious
health problems later in life, such as diabetes, heart disease, and mental health issues.
These conditions affect not only children and their families, but also place a growing
burden on the public health system. Many unhealthy habits begin early, and without
action, today’s children face higher medical costs and poorer quality of life as adults.

Schools are one of the best places to prevent this problem. Children spend much of
their day in school, eat food sold on or near campus, and follow daily routines that
shape lifelong habits. This bill builds on existing nutrition programs by creating
healthier school food environments, requiring daily physical activity, and
strengthening nutrition education for parents and communities. It limits the sale and
promotion of high-sugar food and drinks in public schools, encourages healthier
choices around school areas, and ensures that children are physically active every
school day.

By combining healthy food, regular exercise, and family involvement, this measure
helps children grow strong and healthy while supporting better learning and well-
being. It complements current feeding and nutrition programs and responds to the

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Childhood Obesity
Prevention Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right of children to health and proper nutrition. The State shall adopt
evidence-informed, school- and community-based interventions to prevent childhood
overweight and obesity and their long-term health consequences, through healthier
school food environments, regular physical activity, and strengthened nutrition
education, implemented through coordinated national and local mechanisms.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
de ined as:

   (a) “Child” refers to learners enrolled in public elementary and secondary
       schools.


                                                                           3|Page
   (b) “Public Schools” refers to all state-funded elementary and secondary schools.


   (c) “Physical activity” refers to structured or unstructured bodily movement that
       promotes physical itness, including physical education, supervised play,
       sports, and exercise.

   (d) “High-sugar food and beverage (HSFB)” refers to food or beverage products
       with added sugars exceeding thresholds to be determined by the Department
       of Health (DOH) in coordination with the National Nutrition Council (NNC) and
       the Food and Nutrition Research Institute (FNRI), using clear, evidence-based
       standards to be set in the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).

   (e) “School food outlets” refers to school canteens, cafeterias, kiosks, and other
       food service points operating within public school premises, including those
       operated through lease, concession, parents-teachers associations,
       cooperatives, or similar arrangements.

   (f) “School vicinity” refers to the area within one hundred (100) meters from the
       perimeter of a public school.

SECTION 4. Healthy School Food Standards and Restricted Products. —

   (a) Healthy school food standards. — The Department of Education (DepEd), in
       coordination with the DOH, the NNC, and the FNRI, shall adopt healthy school
       food standards to guide food service, procurement, and menu planning in
       public schools.

   (b) Restricted products. — The sale, distribution, and promotional marketing of
       HSFBs shall be prohibited within public school premises through school food
       outlets and during school activities, whether on-campus or off-campus, as may
       be de ined in the IRR.

   (c) School vicinity coordination. — DepEd and the concerned local government
       units (LGUs) shall coordinate to discourage and regulate, consistent with
       existing ordinances and applicable laws, the sale and promotional marketing
       of HSFBs within the school vicinity, including through permitting and licensing
       conditions of local authorities, as may be provided in the IRR.

SECTION 5. School-Based Nutrition and Health Measures. —

                                                                          4|Page
   (a) Nutrition information and learner health monitoring. — DepEd, in
       coordination with DOH, LGUs, and NNC, shall strengthen periodic learner
       health and nutrition monitoring and referral mechanisms.

   (b) Integration with existing nutrition programs. — Measures under this Act shall,
       as far as practicable, complement existing school-based nutrition and feeding
       initiatives, including standardized menu planning and nutrition monitoring
       systems.

SECTION 6. Mandatory Daily Physical Activity. — All public elementary and
secondary schools shall implement a minimum of thirty (30) minutes of daily physical
activity for learners, which may include structured physical education, supervised
play, organized sports, or equivalent activities suited to age, disability, health status,
weather, and local conditions, consistent with DepEd guidelines.

SECTION 7. Parent and Community Nutrition Education. —

   (a) Parent and caregiver sessions. — DepEd, in coordination with DOH and NNC,
       shall develop and implement a Parent and Community Nutrition Education
       Program to be conducted at least twice every school year, which shall include:

       1.   healthy meal planning and food preparation;
       2.   understanding food labels and added sugars;
       3.   risks of childhood overweight and obesity
       4.   practical family physical activity strategies;
       5.   referral pathways for learners needing clinical assessment.

   (b) Non-punitive approach. — Participation shall be encouraged through
       accessible scheduling, online/printed modules, and community-based
       delivery. No monetary penalty shall be imposed on parents or guardians for
       non-attendance. The IRR may provide incentives and non-punitive
       conditionalities consistent with child welfare and due process.

SECTION 8. Compliance, Monitoring, and Roles of Agencies. —

   (a) DepEd. — DepEd shall be the lead agency for school-level implementation,
       including compliance monitoring of school food outlets and the daily physical
       activity requirement.

   (b) DOH. — DOH shall provide technical assistance and develop standards for
       HSFB thresholds and nutrition education content in coordination with NNC
       and FNRI.

   (c) NNC. — Consistent with its mandate as the highest policy-making and
       coordinating body on nutrition, the NNC shall coordinate, monitor, and
       evaluate the nutrition components of this Act across agencies and LGUs, and
       may call upon agencies for assistance as necessary.

SECTION 9. Administrative Sanctions. —

   (a) School food outlets and operators. — Without prejudice to other applicable
       laws and contracts, DepEd may impose the following graduated administrative
       sanctions for violations of Section 4(b):

       1. First violation: written notice of violation and corrective action order, with
          mandatory re-orientation;
       2. Second violation: suspension of canteen/outlet operations for ifteen (15)
          to thirty (30) school days and/or administrative ine to be set in the IRR;
       3. Third violation: termination of concession/lease agreement or revocation
          of authority to operate within the school, and blacklisting from future
          school concessions for a period to be set in the IRR, subject to due process.

   (b) School of icials and personnel. — Violations committed by school of icials or
       personnel in the performance of of icial functions shall be dealt with under
       applicable civil service and administrative rules, without prejudice to other
       liabilities under existing laws.

SECTION 10. Reporting and Transparency. — DepEd, in coordination with DOH,
NNC, and LGUs, shall submit an annual report to the Of ice of the President and both
Houses of Congress on the implementation of this Act, including compliance
indicators, program coverage, and available aggregated data on nutrition status.

SECTION 11. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary to implement this Act shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. Funding shall cover, among
others, monitoring activities, capacity building, development of nutrition education
materials, and support for physical activity implementation.

SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from effectivity of this Act, DepEd and DOH, in coordination with the NNC, FNRI, and
