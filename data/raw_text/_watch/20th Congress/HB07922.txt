                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Reliable and affordable internet is no longer a luxury, it is a basic need for learning,
work, business, healthcare, and everyday participation in society. Yet many
Filipinos still suffer from slow speeds, dropped connections, and unclear or
misleading service promises. This hurts productivity, deepens the digital divide,
and holds back national growth. People deserve to know what they are paying for
and to receive internet service that actually works.

This bill addresses these problems by setting clear minimum standards for
internet quality, using one fair and nationwide way to measure performance, and
making results public in a form people can easily understand. It strengthens
consumer rights by requiring truthful service claims, better customer support, and
clear remedies when providers repeatedly fail to deliver. At the same time, it
ensures fairness by giving service providers due process before any penalties are
imposed.

By making internet performance measurable, transparent, and enforceable, this
measure promotes accountability and healthy competition based on service
quality, not just advertising. It also supports the expansion of internet services to
unserved and underserved areas through reasonable and time-bound rules.
Simply put, this bill helps ensure that Filipinos get the internet service they pay for
and need to fully participate in a modern digital economy.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the
undersigned respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                          AN ACT
ESTABLISHING   MINIMUM   QUALITY OF  SERVICE  STANDARDS,
MEASUREMENT, TRANSPARENCY, AND END-USER PROTECTIONS FOR
INTERNET ACCESS SERVICES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Internet Service Quality
Standards Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State recognizes the vital role of
communication and information in nation building and shall promote consumer
welfare by ensuring the quality, reliability, security, and interoperability of
telecommunications facilities and services. The State shall adopt measurable and
transparent quality of service standards for internet access services, ensure
regular monitoring and publication of service performance, and provide effective
redress for end users, consistent with due process and fair competition.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms
are de ined:

   (a) “Internet Access Service” refers to a service that provides end users the
       capability to transmit and receive data to and from the internet.

   (b) “Internet Service Provider (ISP)” refers to any entity authorized by the
       National Telecommunications Commission (NTC), whether as a public
       telecommunications entity or as a registered value-added service provider,

                                                                      2|Page
     to offer Internet Access Service to the public for a fee, subject to applicable
     laws on franchises, certi icates, and authorizations.

  (c) “Public Telecommunications Entity (PTE)” refers to any person, irm,
      partnership, corporation, government or private, engaged in the provision
      of telecommunications services to the public for a fee.

  (d) “Advertised Speed” refers to the download and upload speeds marketed
      or represented to end users in offers, service plans, or contracts.

  (e) “Download Speed” refers to the rate at which data is transferred from the
      network to the end user, expressed in bits per second.

  (f) “Upload Speed” refers to the rate at which data is transferred from the end
      user to the network, expressed in bits per second.

  (g) “Latency” refers to the time it takes for a data packet to travel from source
      to destination, expressed in milliseconds.

  (h) “Jitter” refers to the variation in latency within the same data low.

  (i) “Packet Loss” refers to the percentage of packets that fail to reach the
      destination.

  (j) “Service Availability” or “Uptime” refers to the percentage of time the
      service is available to the end user, net of outages attributable to the ISP,
      measured using the methodology prescribed by the NTC.

  (k) “Unserved and Underserved Areas” refer to areas identi ied by the
      Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) based
      on nationally accepted ICT/broadband coverage indicators and mapping.

SECTION 4. Minimum Quality of Service (QoS) Standards; Truthful
Marketing. —

  (a) NTC Standards. — Within six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act, the
      NTC, in coordination with the DICT and after public consultation, shall
      prescribe minimum QoS standards for Internet Access Service, which shall
      include, at a minimum:
      (i)    download speed;
      (ii)   upload speed;
      (iii) latency;
      (iv) jitter;

                                                                        3|Page
      (v)     packet loss; and
      (vi)    service availability.

   (b) Consistency with International and Regional Benchmarks. — The QoS
       standards shall, at a minimum, be aligned with international best practices
       and relevant regional network performance indices, and shall be reviewed
       at least annually. New or updated standards shall take effect at least thirty
       (30) days after publication.

   (c) Truthful Advertising and Plan Disclosure. — ISPs and PTEs shall not
       advertise or offer speeds or performance claims that they cannot
       reasonably and consistently deliver based on network capability and
       capacity planning. The NTC shall prescribe standard disclosure
       requirements, including typical performance ranges and material
       limitations (e.g., time-of-day congestion, data caps, and fair use policies),
       consistent with consumer protection.

   (d) Initial Minimum Service Levels. — Pending the issuance of the detailed QoS
       standards under subsection (a) the NTC shall, within six (6) months,
       prescribe interim minimum service levels for download speed and service
       availability by area classi ication, taking into account feasibility,
       affordability, and consumer welfare. Thereafter, the NTC may adjust these
       minimum service levels pursuant to subsection (b) but shall not lower
       consumer protection below the interim levels without clear and published
       justi ication based on substantial evidence.

   (e) Special Treatment for Unserved and Underserved Areas. — To encourage
       expansion, the NTC may adopt phased compliance, time-bound incentives,
       or calibrated minimum standards for newly served unserved or
       underserved areas, as identi ied by the DICT, provided that:

      (i)     the terms are transparent;
      (ii)    end users are clearly informed; and
      (iii)   basic consumer remedies under this Act apply.

SECTION 5. Measurement, Monitoring, and Publication. —

   (a) Measurement Methodology. — Within six (6) months from the effectivity
       of this Act, the NTC, in coordination with the DICT, shall adopt and publish
       a national methodology for measuring QoS parameters, including rules on:
       sampling, test frequency, test locations, peak/off-peak de initions,
       treatment of outages, and data integrity.


                                                                        4|Page
   (b) Public Reporting. — The NTC shall monitor and measure ISP/PTE
       performance on at least a quarterly basis and publish the results on its
       website in a consumer-readable format and an open-data format, subject
       to reasonable safeguards for security-sensitive information.

   (c) Independent Monitoring. — The NTC may engage an independent third-
       party monitoring irm, subject to applicable procurement rules, to assist in
       performance monitoring.

   (d) Consumer Diagnostic Tool. — The NTC shall require the availability of a
       secure, consumer-friendly mechanism for end users to run broadband
       diagnostics and to submit results as part of a complaint, subject to privacy
       and data protection laws.

   (e) Data Sharing with DICT. — The NTC shall furnish DICT with the raw or
       suf iciently granular performance datasets necessary for national planning
       and identi ication of unserved/underserved areas, subject to reasonable
       con identiality and cybersecurity safeguards.

SECTION 6. Responsibilities of the DICT. — The DICT shall:

   (a) Maintain and periodically update a national broadband and ICT assets
       index and/or mapping system, and publish summaries suf icient to guide
       public policy and investment;

   (b) Identify and publish the list of unserved and underserved areas for
       purposes of this Act, and update the same at least annually; and

   (c) Coordinate with the NTC on standards-setting and on policy measures that
       improve broadband deployment and consumer protection.

SECTION 7. End-User Rights and Minimum Service Standards in Customer
Care. — Every end user shall have the right:

   (a) To non-discriminatory internet access service consistent with the
       minimum QoS standards set by the NTC;

   (b) To clear and accurate disclosures of advertised speed, typical performance,
       pricing, lock-in period, data caps, and material service limitations;

   (c) To timely correction of billing or prepaid load errors and, where warranted,
       prompt rebates or refunds consistent with NTC rules;


                                                                       5|Page
   (d) To accessible complaints handling channels, including in-person, call, SMS,
       and online channels; and to a written or electronic reference number and
       status updates; and

   (e) To terminate service without pre-termination fees, and to receive
       proportionate refunds of unconsumed prepaid or advance payments, when
       the ISP/PTE is found by the NTC (or admitted by the provider in a manner
       prescribed by NTC rules) to have materially and repeatedly failed to meet
       minimum QoS standards, subject to fair procedures.

SECTION 8. Compliance Orders and Penalties; Due Process. —

   (a) Recti ication/Compliance Orders. — The NTC may, motu proprio or upon
       complaint, require an ISP/PTE to rectify non-compliance with QoS
       standards within a reasonable period set by the NTC, considering the
       nature and severity of the violation, and may require submission of a
       corrective action plan;

   (b) Administrative Penalties. — After due notice and hearing, and upon
       substantial evidence, the NTC may impose administrative penalties for
       violations of this Act, its IRR, and NTC QoS standards, including ines and
       other regulatory sanctions within the bounds of existing law and the
       ISP/PTE’s authorization;

   (c) Graduated Enforcement. — The NTC shall provide in the IRR a graduated
       penalty framework that considers: (i) severity; (ii) duration; (iii) number
       of affected subscribers; (iv) repeat offenses; (v) good-faith compliance
       efforts; and (vi) whether the violation occurred in unserved/underserved
       rollout phases;

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the NTC, in coordination with the DICT and after
public consultation, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations
(IRR) necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full
force and effect.

SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed
or modi ied accordingly.
