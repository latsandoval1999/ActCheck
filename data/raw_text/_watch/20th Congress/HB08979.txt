                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Batas Pambansa Blg. 36, enacted on September 7, 1979, imposes an
energy tax on the monthly electric power consumption of every residential
customer of electric utilities. The tax is structured based on consumption
levels:

      Consumption Bracket                            Tax imposed

          Not over 650kWh                               Exempt

       650kWh to 1,000kWh                P0.10 per kWh in excess of 650kWh

      1,000kWh to 1,500kWh              P35.00 plus P0.20 per kWh in excess

                                                     of 1,000kWh

           Over 1,500kWh               P135.00 plus P0.35 per kWh in excess

                                                     of 1,500kWh


       The primary objective of BP 36 was energy conservation and promoting
the efficient utilization of electric power. At the time of its enactment in 1979,
the Philippines was grappling with the global oil crisis, necessitating fiscal
measures to curb excessive energy consumption, particularly among high-
volume residential users. The law acted as a progressive "penalty" for higher
consumption to discourage waste.

      Almost fifty years had passed since the enactment of this law, and the
energy landscape has shifted to a complex, restructured market. The
imposition of a "conservation tax" on top of billing components is incompatible
in an era where the state aims to lower electricity costs for global
competitiveness.

      Today, end-users already bear the weight of a 12% Value-Added Tax
(VAT) on all components of the electric bill, system loss charges, universal
charges, among others. Furthermore, the thresholds set in 1979, where 650
kWh was considered the "luxury" ceiling, do not reflect modern consumption
patterns, where increased reliance on appliances and technology has made
such levels common.

      Repealing Batas Pambansa Blg. 36 is a necessary step to streamline the
Philippine electric power sector's tax structure, provide immediate relief to
consumers, and eliminate a token levy that no longer serves its original
purpose in a fully unbundled and competitive power market.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                              AN ACT
      REPEALING BATAS PAMBANSA BLG. 36, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS “AN
           ACT IMPOSING AN ENERGY TAX ON ELECTRIC POWER
                           CONSUMPTION”

     Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Republic
     of the Philippines in Congress assembled:
1           SECTION 1. Declaration of State Policies. – It is the policy of the
2    State to ensure the quality, reliability, security, and affordability of the supply
3    of electric power. The State likewise recognizes the need to protect the
4    consumers from excessive and unreasonable impositions that will unduly
5    provide an additional financial burden to the public.

6          SEC. 2. Repealing Clause. – Any provision of law to the contrary
7    notwithstanding, Batas Pambansa Blg. 36, entitled "An Act Imposing An
8    Energy Tax on Electric Power Consumption" is hereby repealed.

 9         SEC. 3. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15)
10   days following its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2)
11   newspapers of general circulation whichever is earlier.
