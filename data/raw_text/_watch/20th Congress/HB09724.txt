                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Across countries, retirement planning reflects a common goal: to
preserve one’s standard of living after leaving the workforce. It enables retirees
to be self-sufficient, ensuring that they have enough resources to maintain their
daily needs and maintain financial security.
      In the Philippines, government workers receive retirement benefits
through the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS), while self-employed
individuals, informal sector workers, Overseas Filipino Workers, Non-Working
Spouses, and private-sector employees receive them through the Social Security
System (SSS).
      Currently, eligibility for retirement benefits under the Government
Service Insurance System Act of 1997 depends largely on age and years of
service – a member must be at least sixty (60) years old, have rendered a
minimum of fifteen (15) years of government service, and must not be receiving
a monthly disability pension.
       This framework effectively requires individuals planning to retire at age
60 to enter government service no later than age 45 and to maintain continuous,
qualifying service thereafter. In reality, this pathway is not easily attainable.
Many Filipinos spend years securing eligibility through the civil service system
and obtaining stable government employment.




                                                                                   1
      As of September 2025, the Government Service Insurance System reports
over 2.77 million members and pensioners, yet only a fraction of active
members are likely to complete the 15-year requirement and qualify for
retirement benefits.
      In contrast, the Social Security System adopts a more flexible,
contribution-based approach. A member needs at least 120 monthly
contributions (equivalent to 10 years) to qualify for a monthly pension, with no
requirement of continuous employment. Those who fall short receive a lump
sum benefit instead. This allows workers with interrupted or non-linear career
paths to still qualify for retirement support.
      Given these differences, there is a compelling basis to revisit the current
GSIS framework. This bill proposes to reduce the minimum service
requirement from fifteen (15) to ten (10) years, aligning it more closely with SSS
standards and making retirement benefits more accessible to government
workers.
     In light of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is respectfully
sought.
