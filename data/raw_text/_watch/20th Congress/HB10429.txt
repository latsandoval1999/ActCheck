                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

The youth are among the nation's greatest assets and play a vital role in shaping the country's
future. Developing responsible, ethical, and service-oriented young leaders is essential to
strengthening democratic institutions, promoting civic participation, and fostering a culture of
volunteerism, good governance, and nation-building. However, opportunities for structured
leadership development remain uneven across the country, particularly for young people in
underserved and geographically isolated communities.

Although schools, youth organizations, and government agencies implement various
leadership and civic engagement programs, there remains a need for a more accessible and
institutionalized system that provides sustained leadership education and practical training
for young Filipinos.

Therefore, this bill seeks to establish Regional Youth Leadership and Nation-Building Training
Centers in all regions of the country. These centers shall provide leadership development,
civic education, governance awareness, volunteerism, public service training, and community
engagement opportunities for student leaders, youth leaders, and other qualified young
Filipinos.

By expanding access to quality leadership training and strengthening youth participation in
governance and community development, this bill seeks to empower Filipino youth to become
catalysts of positive change, innovation, and inclusive development in their respective
communities.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT ESTABLISHING REGIONAL YOUTH LEADERSHIP AND NATION-
 BUILDING TRAINING CENTERS FOR STUDENT LEADERS IN ALL REGIONS OF
              THE COUNTRY, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Youth Leadership and Nation-
Building Training Act".

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to foster the development of
responsible, ethical, and service-oriented youth leaders. Recognizing the role of young
people in shaping the nation’s future, the State shall provide structured and accessible
leadership training focused on democratic values, civic responsibility, nation-building, and
good governance. The State shall promote youth participation in nation-building,
democratic governance, volunteerism, social responsibility, and community development,
and shall ensure equal access to leadership development opportunities for all sectors of
the youth

SEC. 3. Objectives. – This Act aims to:

   a. Establish youth leadership and nation-building training centers in all regions of the
      Philippines;
   b. Develop a generation of student leaders who embody ethical leadership, critical
      thinking, and a commitment to public service;
   c. Promote civic education, democratic participation, and youth involvement in
      governance;
   d. Institutionalize regular leadership training programs in partnership with schools,
      government, and civil society organizations;
   e. Promote volunteerism and active citizenship among Filipino youth;
   f. Strengthen youth participation in public affairs and community development; and
   g. Develop future leaders committed to integrity, accountability, and national
      development

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms shall be defined as
follows:

   a. Youth – refers to persons aged fifteen (15) to thirty (30) years, consistent with the
      provisions of Republic Act No. 8044, as amended;
   b. Student Leader – refers to any elected or appointed officer of a recognized student
      government, student council, campus publication, student organization, or youth
      organization in an educational institution;
   c. Youth Leader – refers to a young person actively engaged in community, civic,
      volunteer, cultural, environmental, entrepreneurial, or public service initiatives and
      duly endorsed by a local government unit, educational institution, or recognized
      youth organization;
   d. Center – refers to a Regional Youth Leadership and Nation-Building Training
      Center established pursuant to this Act; and
   e. Nation-Building Training Program – refers to the leadership development, civic
      education, governance, volunteerism, and community engagement activities
      conducted under this Act.

SEC. 5. Establishment, Functions, and Program Components of the Regional Youth
Leadership and Nation-Building Training Centers. – There shall be established Regional
Youth Leadership and Nation-Building Training Centers in all regions of the country under
the overall policy coordination of the National Youth Commission (NYC), in partnership
with the Department of Education (DepEd), the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and other
concerned agencies.

Existing government training facilities, state universities and colleges, public educational
institutions, or other suitable government properties may be designated as Regional
Youth Leadership and Nation-Building Training Centers whenever practicable
.
The Centers shall serve as centers for leadership development, civic education, and
nation-building initiatives and shall:

   a. Conduct capacity-building programs for elementary, secondary, tertiary, and out-
      of-school youth participants through age-appropriate and developmentally
      responsive learning modules;
   b. Organize seminars, workshops, conferences, and training activities on leadership,
      citizenship, governance, public service, volunteerism, and community
      development;
   c. Facilitate inter-school, inter-regional, and multi-sectoral collaboration among youth
      leaders and organizations;
   d. Provide mentoring, leadership immersion, simulation exercises, and experiential
      learning opportunities; and
   e. Support youth-led community projects, innovation initiatives, and social
      development programs.

The training curriculum shall include, but not be limited to:

   1. Responsible and Ethical Leadership;
   2. Civic Responsibility and Democratic Participation;
   3. Philippine History, Constitution, Human Rights, and Rule of Law;
   4. Nation-Building, Peacebuilding, and Social Cohesion;
   5. Public Speaking, Communication, and Advocacy
   6. Project Development, Innovation, and Social Entrepreneurship;
   7. Anti-Corruption and Good Governance;
   8. Community Organizing and Volunteerism;
   9. Environmental Stewardship, Climate Action, and Sustainable Development; and
   10. Digital Citizenship, Media Literacy, and Responsible Use of Technology.

SEC. 6. Eligibility and Participation. – The program shall be open to:

   a. Student government leaders from public and private schools and universities;
   b. Officers and members of duly recognized youth organizations;
   c. Young leaders nominated by their local government units (LGUs), NGOs, or
      community organizations; and
   d. Out-of-school youth, indigenous youth, youth with disabilities, Muslim youth, and
      youth from geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs), subject to
      guidelines issued by the NYC.

SEC. 7. Role of the National Youth Commission. – The NYC shall:

   a. Serve as the lead coordinating and policy-making agency for the implementation
      of this Act;
   b. Work with DepEd, CHED, and the Department of the Interior and Local
      Government (DILG) to implement regional programs;
   c. Provide training modules, facilitator guidelines, and certification;
   d. Conduct national leadership summits and recognition for outstanding youth
      leaders;
   e. Establish standards, accreditation systems, and evaluation mechanisms for the
      Centers and training programs; and
   f. Monitor and assess program implementation and submit annual reports to
      Congress.

SEC. 8. Partnerships and Support. – The centers shall collaborate with:
   a. Local government units (LGUs);
   b. Civil society organizations and youth NGOs;
   c. Leadership institutes and training providers;
   d. The private sector, especially those with youth empowerment programs;
   e. State universities and colleges (SUCs);
   f. Professional organizations and academic institutions; and
   g. International development organizations and institutions, subject to existing laws
      and regulations

Such partnerships may include sponsorships, mentorship, logistical support, and access
to facilities.

The Centers may enter into memoranda of agreement with partner institutions for the
provision of training facilities, resource persons, technical assistance, scholarships,
internships, and other forms of support.

SEC. 9. Annual National Youth Leadership Congress. – A National Youth Leadership
Congress shall be held annually to:

   a. Showcase regional initiatives and success stories;
   b. Facilitate knowledge-sharing and collaboration among youth leaders; and
   c. Recognize outstanding youth-led nation-building projects.

SEC. 10. Integration with National Youth Commission Programs. – The Centers shall
coordinate with the National Youth Commission to ensure that leadership development
activities, policy consultations, and youth representation initiatives are aligned with
existing programs, including the National Youth Parliament and other youth participation
mechanisms established under law.

SEC. 11. Monitoring and Evaluation. – The NYC shall establish a monitoring and
evaluation system to assess the effectiveness, participation rates, outcomes, and impact
of the programs established under this Act and shall submit an annual report to Congress.

SEC. 12. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement the provisions of this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act, under the budgets of DepEd,
CHED, and the NYC. Additional funding may be sourced from donations, partnerships,
and development grants

SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, CHED, and NYC, in consultation with the DILG and
other stakeholders, shall issue the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.
SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders or issuances or any part thereof,
which are inconsistent herewith, are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
