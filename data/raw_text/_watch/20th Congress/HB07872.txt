                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

As an archipelagic country rich in marine and coastal resources, the Philippines relies
on its fisheries sector as an important component of its economy.1 Based on the Food
and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) FishStat database, in 2022,
the Philippines was the 11th largest fish-producing country in the world.2 In fact, the
value of fisheries production has increased from P281.6 million in 2019 to P328.7
million in 2023. Further, in 2023, the Agriculture, Forestry, and Fishing sector
accounted for 8.6% of the country’s total GDP.3

Millions of Filipinos remain dependent on the fishing industry. In 2023, 2.29 million
fisherfolk engaged in various fishing and aquaculture activities were registered with

Fisheries Resources, we heard firsthand the plight of our fisherfolk, and among their
persistent difficulties is the possibility of displacement and relocation, which affects
access to their livelihood.

The Philippine Fisheries Code of 1998, as amended, mandates the establishment of
fisherfolk settlement areas in coordination with concerned agencies of the
government, where certain areas of the public domain shall be reserved for the
settlement of the municipal fisherfolk. However, the law does not mandate a clear and
specific framework for the creation of said settlement areas, nor does it define the
roles of the relevant government agencies.

This proposed measure aims to strengthen the provisions of the Fisheries Code and
provide our fisherfolk with security and stability by ensuring they have suitable
settlement areas that have access to fishing grounds. This bill requires the
Department of Agriculture, Department of Human Settlements and Urban
Development, Department of Environment and Natural Resources, and the local
government units to establish settlement areas for fisherfolk, granting preference to
fisherfolk registered in the respective city or municipality who do not have real
property. To ensure a forward-looking approach, the bill also mandates LGUs to
integrate these settlement areas in their comprehensive land use plans.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Section 108 of Republic Act No. 8550, otherwise known as “The
Philippine Fisheries Code of 1998”, as amended by Republic Act No. 10654, is hereby
amended to read as follows:

Section 108. Fisherfolk Settlement Areas. - The Department shall establish and create
fisherfolk settlement areas in coordination with concerned agencies of the
government, where certain areas of the public domain, specifically near the fishing
grounds, shall be reserved for the settlement of the municipal fisherfolk. Nothing in
this section shall be construed to vest ownership of any resettlement area to a
municipal fisherfolk for whom said areas may have been reserved for or had been
actually granted to. THE DEPARTMENT OF AGRICULTURE, DEPARTMENT OF
HUMAN SETTLEMENTS AND URBAN DEVELOPMENT, DEPARTMENT OF
ENVIRONMENT AND NATURAL RESOURCES, AND THE LOCAL
GOVERNMENTS OF CITIES OR MUNICIPALITIES EXERCISING JURISDICTION
OVER COASTAL AREAS AND MUNICIPAL WATERS, SHALL IDENTIFY,
ESTABLISH, AND CREATE SETTLEMENT AREAS WHICH HAVE ADEQUATE


                                          3
ACCESS TO FISHING GROUNDS: PROVIDED, THAT IN PREVIOUSLY
IDENTIFIED OR ESTABLISHED SETTLEMENT AREAS WITH ADEQUATE
ACCESS TO FISHING GROUNDS, FISHERFOLK SHALL BE GRANTED
PREFERENCE IN THE AWARD OF SETTLEMENT RIGHTS THEREAT:
PROVIDED, FURTHER, THAT THE ESTABLISHMENT OF SETTLEMENT AREAS
SHALL BE IN FAVOR OF FISHERFOLK WHO ARE REGISTERED AS SUCH WITH
THEIR RESPECTIVE CITY OR MUNICIPALITY AND WHO ARE WITHOUT REAL
PROPERTY: PROVIDED, FINALLY, THAT LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNITS
EXERCISING JURISDICTION OVER COASTAL AREAS AND MUNICIPAL
WATERS ARE HEREBY MANDATED TO INTEGRATE SETTLEMENT AREAS FOR
FISHERFOLK IN THEIR RESPECTIVE COMPREHENSIVE LAND USE PLANS.

SECTION 2. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 3. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
letters of instruction, proclamations, or administrative orders, rules or regulations that
are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

SECTION 4. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
