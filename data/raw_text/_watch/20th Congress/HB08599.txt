                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Children deserve strong protection from abuse, exploitation, and discrimination. The
Special Protection of Children Against Child Abuse, Exploitation and
Discrimination Act (Republic Act No. 7610) was enacted to safeguard children
from harmful acts that threaten their safety and development. However, new and
evolving forms of exploitation, such as the use of children in obscene content, coercion
into illegal activities, and exposure to abusive environments, show that stronger
penalties are needed to ensure the law continues to protect children effectively.

This bill seeks to strengthen Republic Act No. 7610 by increasing the penalties and
 ines for acts that exploit, abuse, or endanger children. These include the use of
children in obscene publications or indecent shows, acts that place minors in
situations where abuse and exploitation are likely to occur, and the worst forms of
child labor already prohibited under Republic Act No. 9231. The measure also
increases penalties for discrimination against children from indigenous communities
and holds responsible not only individual offenders but also those who allow or
facilitate such abuses.

By strengthening penalties and accountability, the bill aims to deter those who exploit
children and to ensure that punishments re lect the serious and lasting harm caused

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Section 9 of Republic Act No. 7610, otherwise known as the “Special
Protection of Children Against Child Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act”, is
hereby amended to read as follows:

     “SEC. 9. Obscene Publications and Indecent Shows. – Any person who shall hire,
     employ, use, persuade, induce or coerce a child to perform in obscene
     exhibitions and indecent shows, whether live or in video, pose, or model in
     obscene publications or pornographic materials or to sell or distribute the said
     materials shall suffer the penalty of RECLUSION TEMPORAL in its MEDIUM
     period.

     “If the child used as a performer, subject or seller/distributor is TWELVE (12)
     YEARS OF AGE OR BELOW, the penalty shall be RECLUSION PERPETUA.

     “Any ascendant, guardian, or person entrusted in any capacity with the care of
     a child who shall cause, permit, or allow such child to be employed or to
     participate in any act punished under this section shall suffer the penalty of
     RECLUSION TEMPORAL in its MAXIMUM period.”

SECTION 2. Section 10 of the same Act as amended, is hereby amended to read as
follows:

     “SEC. 10. Other Acts of Neglect, Abuse, Cruelty or Exploitation and Other
     Conditions Prejudicial to the Child’s Development. –

     “(a) Any person who shall commit any other acts of child abuse, cruelty or
     exploitation, or be responsible for other conditions prejudicial to the child’s
     development, including those covered by Article 59 of Presidential Decree No.
     603, as amended, but not covered by the Revised Penal Code, as amended, shall
     suffer the penalty of RECLUSION TEMPORAL in its MINIMUM period.”

     “(b) Any person who shall keep or have in his company a minor, twelve (12)
     years of age or under, or who is ten (10) years or more his junior, in any public
     or private place, hotel, motel, beer joint, discotheque, cabaret, pension house,
     sauna or massage parlor, beach, tourist resort, or similar places, shall suffer the
     penalty of RECLUSION TEMPORAL in its MEDIUM period and a ine of not less
     than FIVE HUNDRED THOUSAND PESOS (₱500,000.00): Provided, That this
     provision shall not apply to any person related within the fourth degree of
     consanguinity or af inity, or under any bond recognized by law, local custom and
     tradition, or acts in the performance of a social, moral, or legal duty.”

     “(c) Any person who shall induce, deliver or offer a minor to any person
     prohibited by this Act to keep or have in his company a minor as provided in the
     preceding paragraph shall suffer the penalty of PRISION MAYOR in its
     MAXIMUM period and a ine of not less than FOUR HUNDRED THOUSAND
     PESOS (₱400,000.00): Provided, That if the perpetrator is an ascendant,
     stepparent, guardian, or caregiver having custody of the child, the penalty shall
     be RECLUSION TEMPORAL in its MEDIUM period and a ine of not less than FIVE
     HUNDRED THOUSAND PESOS (₱500,000.00), and the loss of parental authority
     or custody, as the case may be.

     “The court may, in addition, order the offender to undergo a child protection
     reorientation program to be designed and implemented by the Department of
     Social Welfare and Development (DSWD).”

     “(d) Any person, owner, manager, or one entrusted with the operation of any
     public or private place of accommodation, whether for occupancy, food, drink or
     otherwise, including residential places, who allows any person to take along

SECTION 3. Section 16 of the same Act, as amended, is hereby further amended to
read as follows:

     “SEC. 16. Penal Provisions. –

     (a) Any employer who violates Sections 12, 12-A and 14 of this Act, as amended,
     shall be penalized by imprisonment of one (1) year and one (1) day to six (6)
     years, or a ine of not less than ONE HUNDRED THOUSAND PESOS
     (₱100,000.00) but not more than FOUR HUNDRED THOUSAND PESOS
     (₱400,000.00), or both, at the discretion of the court.

     (b) Any person who violates Section 12-D of this Act, or the employer of the
     subcontractor who employs, or the one who facilitates the employment of a
     child in hazardous work, shall suffer the penalty of a ine of not less than TWO
     HUNDRED THOUSAND PESOS (₱200,000.00) but not more than ONE MILLION
     PESOS (₱1,000,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than twelve (12) years and
     one (1) day to twenty (20) years, or both, at the discretion of the court.

     (c) Any person who violates Sections 12-D(1) and 12-D(2) shall be prosecuted
     and penalized in accordance with SECTION 12 of Republic Act No. 10364
     (Expanded Anti-Traf icking in Persons Act of 2012): Provided, That the penalty
     shall be imposed in its maximum period.

     (d) Any person who violates Section 12-D(3) shall be prosecuted and penalized
     in accordance with Republic Act No. 9165 (Comprehensive Dangerous Drugs Act
     of 2002): Provided, That the penalty shall be imposed in its maximum period.

SECTION 4. Section 20 of the same Act is hereby amended to read as follows:

     “SEC. 20. Discrimination. – Children of indigenous cultural communities shall
     not be subjected to any and all forms of discrimination.

     “Any person who discriminates against children of indigenous cultural
     communities shall suffer the penalty of PRISION CORRECCIONAL in its MEDIUM
     period and a ine of not less than FIFTY THOUSAND PESOS (₱50,000.00) nor
     more than ONE HUNDRED THOUSAND PESOS (₱100,000.00).

     “The court may, in addition, order the offender to undergo an indigenous
     peoples cultural sensitivity program to be designed and implemented by the
     National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP).”

SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty
(180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, in coordination with the
Department of Justice (DOJ), the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) for
provisions involving child labor, the NCIP, and other relevant agencies, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to implement this Act. Such rules and
regulations shall take effect upon publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two
(2) newspapers of general circulation.

SECTION 6. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 8. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
