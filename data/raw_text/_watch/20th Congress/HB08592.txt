                            AN ACT
REQUIRING AIR CARRIERS AND PASSENGER SHIP OPERATORS TO PROVIDE
EMERGENCY INJECTABLE ALLERGY MEDICATION ON BOARD, PRESCRIBING
MINIMUM STANDARDS FOR STOCKING, STORAGE, TRAINING, AND REPORTING,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, can become life-threatening within
minutes if not treated immediately. During air or sea travel, access to hospitals or
medical professionals is often limited because passengers are far from land or lying
at high altitude. In such situations, the availability of emergency injectable allergy
medication, such as epinephrine, and trained crew members who can respond quickly
can mean the difference between life and death.

This bill seeks to improve passenger safety by requiring airlines and passenger ships
to carry emergency injectable allergy medication in their onboard medical kits. It also
requires crew members to receive proper training on how to recognize severe allergic
reactions and administer the medication safely during emergencies. The Department
of Health, together with the Department of Transportation, the Civil Aviation
Authority of the Philippines, and the Maritime Industry Authority, will establish clear
standards for the proper storage, handling, and use of these life-saving medicines.

This measure builds on the government’s existing safety oversight in the aviation and
maritime sectors. Agencies such as the Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines and
the Maritime Industry Authority already ensure compliance with passenger safety

to protect public health.

By requiring life-saving medication to be available on board and ensuring crew
readiness, the bill aims to prevent avoidable deaths and improve emergency response
during travel. By strengthening preparedness and protecting passengers in medical
emergencies, this measure promotes safer air and sea travel for all Filipinos.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Onboard Emergency
Allergy Care Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect the health,
safety, and welfare of passengers in air and sea travel. Recognizing that severe allergic
reactions, including anaphylaxis, may be rapidly fatal without immediate treatment,
the State shall ensure timely access to emergency injectable allergy medication on
board aircraft and passenger ships, supported by crew training, proper storage and
handling, and clear coordination with appropriate medical services.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) Air Carrier refers to any person, partnership, corporation, or entity engaged
       in the business of air transportation of passengers operating within Philippine
       jurisdiction, whether domestic or international.



                                                                             3|Page
   (b) Passenger Ship refers to any inter-island or international vessel engaged in
       the carriage of passengers, operating within Philippine jurisdiction.

   (c) Emergency Injectable Allergy Medication refers to epinephrine (adrenaline)
       in a dosage form for emergency treatment of anaphylaxis, including auto-
       injectors or ampules/vials with appropriate administration devices, and such
       other DOH-approved emergency injectable medication for severe allergic
       reactions as may be speci ied in the implementing rules and regulations (IRR).

   (d) Medical Emergency Kit refers to the set of medical supplies and medicines
       required to be carried on board by applicable rules of the Civil Aviation
       Authority of the Philippines (CAAP), the Maritime Industry Authority
       (MARINA), and other competent authorities, and as may be supplemented
       under this Act.

   (e) Trained Crew refers to crew members who have completed DOH-prescribed
       training on recognition of anaphylaxis, indications/contraindications,
       administration of emergency injectable allergy medication, and post-
       administration monitoring and referral procedures.

SECTION 4. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to:

   (a) All domestic and international air carriers operating lights within Philippine
       jurisdiction; and

   (b) All inter-island and international passenger ship operators operating voyages
       within Philippine jurisdiction.

SECTION 5. Mandatory Availability of Emergency Injectable Allergy Medication.
—
   (a) Stock Requirement. — Air carriers and passenger ship operators shall maintain
       on board, as part of the Medical Emergency Kit, a minimum stock of
       Emergency Injectable Allergy Medication.

   (b) Minimum Quantities and Con iguration. — Within six (6) months from the
       effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
       CAAP, the Department of Transportation (DOTr), and MARINA, shall prescribe
       minimum stock quantities, dosage con igurations (adult and pediatric where
       applicable), and any acceptable alternatives, taking into account passenger
       capacity, voyage/ light duration, and route characteristics.

                                                                         4|Page
   (c) Storage and Replacement. — Operators shall ensure proper storage conditions,
       temperature requirements, security, accessibility during emergencies, and
       regular inspection and replacement prior to expiry, in accordance with DOH
       standards and the IRR.

   (d) No Refusal to Carry. — Operators shall not refuse carriage or impose
       discriminatory conditions solely on the basis that a passenger has a known
       severe allergy, without prejudice to existing safety regulations and reasonable
       accommodations.

SECTION 6. Training and Readiness Requirements. —

   (a) Minimum Trained Crew. — Every light or voyage covered by this Act shall have
       at least two (2) Trained Crew members on duty.

   (b) Training Standards. — The DOH shall prescribe training content, duration,
       certi ication/recerti ication intervals, and competency standards, in
       coordination with CAAP and MARINA.

   (c) Coordination with Medical Support. — Operators shall adopt protocols for
       prompt coordination with ground/shore medical facilities, including
       procedures for diversion, emergency docking, or rapid disembarkation when
       medically indicated.

SECTION 7. Incident Documentation and Reporting. —

   (a) Record of Use. — Any administration of Emergency Injectable Allergy
       Medication shall be recorded in an incident report indicating the time of
       administration, circumstances, the crew member who administered, and post-
       event actions taken, subject to data privacy requirements.

   (b) Reporting. — Operators shall submit periodic anonymized reports to DOH and
       the appropriate regulator (CAAP for air carriers; MARINA for ships) for
       monitoring, compliance, and policy improvement, in the form and frequency
       to be prescribed in the IRR.

SECTION 8. Passenger Information and Public Awareness. — Operators shall
inform passengers of the availability of emergency allergy treatment on board


                                                                          5|Page
through safety brie ing materials, seatback/printed cards, or visual displays, and shall
indicate the procedure for requesting assistance.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within six (6) months from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with CAAP, DOTr, and MARINA, and
after consultation with stakeholders including carriers, maritime and aviation
medical professionals, and passenger groups, shall promulgate the IRR necessary to
implement this Act.

The IRR shall be germane to and in conformity with the standards and purposes of
this Act and shall include, among others:
    (i)    stock standards;
    (ii)   acceptable formulations and administration devices;
    (iii) storage parameters;
    (iv) training/certi ication standards;
    (v)    inspection and compliance auditing; and
    (vi) incident reporting templates.

SECTION 10. Compliance Monitoring. — CAAP and MARINA shall integrate
compliance checks under this Act into their existing inspection and audit processes.
The DOH shall provide technical standards and may conduct joint inspections as may
be provided in the IRR.

SECTION 11. Administrative Penalties. — Without prejudice to existing regulatory
powers and sanctions under applicable laws and rules, noncompliance with this Act
or its IRR shall subject the operator to the following administrative penalties, to be
imposed by the appropriate regulator:

   (a) First Offense — Fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos
       (₱100,000.00) and issuance of a compliance order with a speci ied cure
       period;

   (b) Second Offense — Fine of not less than Three Hundred Thousand Pesos
       (₱300,000.00) and enhanced inspection and monitoring;

   (c) Third and Subsequent Offenses — Fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand
       Pesos (₱500,000.00) and, when warranted by repeated willful noncompliance
       affecting passenger safety, suspension of the relevant permit or authority to
       operate, in accordance with due process and existing regulations.
