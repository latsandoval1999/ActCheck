                           AN ACT
PROVIDING FOR THE MAGNA CARTA OF THE OUT-OF-SCHOOL YOUTH,
STRENGTHENING GOVERNMENT SUPPORT MECHANISMS FOR THEIR
EDUCATION, EMPLOYMENT, SOCIAL PROTECTION, AND REINTEGRATION,
AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Constitution recognizes the vital role of the youth in nation-building
and mandates the State to promote and protect their physical, moral, spiritual,
intellectual, and social well-being. Despite this constitutional guarantee, millions of
Filipino children and youth remain out of school due to poverty, unemployment, family
problems, early pregnancy, lack of access to educational opportunities, and other
socio-economic barriers.

       Recent data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) and various
government studies continue to show that a significant number of Filipino youth
remain outside the formal education system. Many are forced to work at an early age
to help their families survive, while others discontinue schooling because of financial
hardship, displacement, mental health concerns, conflict, abuse, or lack of support
systems. As a result, many out-of-school youth become vulnerable to exploitation,
discrimination, criminality, trafficking, substance abuse, and long-term poverty.

        The problem of out-of-school youth is not merely an educational concern. It is
a social justice issue, an economic issue, and a national development issue. Every
Filipino youth who is denied the opportunity to study, develop skills, or secure decent
employment represents lost human potential and a setback to national progress.
       This measure seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive Magna Carta for the
Out-of-School Youth that will recognize, protect, and promote their rights and welfare.
It aims to strengthen the role of the government in ensuring accessible education,
technical-vocational training, employment opportunities, social protection, mental
health support, and reintegration programs for out-of-school youth across the country.

        The bill likewise adopts a whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach
by directing national government agencies, local government units, educational
institutions, the private sector, and civil society organizations to coordinate and
implement programs specifically designed for the needs of out-of-school youth. It
further recognizes the need for stronger interventions for youth in especially difficult
circumstances, including victims of abuse, trafficking, exploitation, armed conflict,
teenage pregnancy, and extreme poverty.

      Ultimately, this proposed measure seeks to empower out-of-school youth to
become productive, self-sufficient, and active participants in nation-building by giving
them genuine opportunities to complete their education, acquire skills, obtain decent
work, and improve their quality of life.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 3
 4         SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Magna Carta of
 5   the Out-of-School Youth Act.”
 6
 7           SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State recognizes the vital role of
 8   the youth in nation-building and shall promote and protect their physical, moral,
 9   spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being. Consistent with the Constitution, the State
10   shall ensure that out-of-school youth are afforded equal access to education, skills
11   development, employment opportunities, social protection, and government services
12   necessary for their development as productive members of society. The State shall
13   adopt a comprehensive, rights-based, gender-responsive, and inclusive approach in
14   addressing the concerns of out-of-school youth and shall remove barriers that prevent
15   young Filipinos from accessing quality education, livelihood, healthcare, and other
16   support systems essential to their growth and empowerment.
17
 1           Towards this end, the State shall recognize and protect the rights and dignity
 2   of out-of-school youth by ensuring their access to formal, non-formal, and alternative
 3   education programs, technical-vocational training, livelihood opportunities,
 4   employment facilitation, and responsive social protection and health services, while
 5   promoting their active participation in nation-building and community development
 6   and strengthening collaboration among government agencies, local government units,
 7   civil society organizations, educational institutions, and the private sector in the
 8   formulation and implementation of programs and interventions for out-of-school
 9   youth.
10
11          SECTION 3. Definition of terms. - For purposes of this Act:
12
13          (a) “Alternative Learning System (ALS)” refers to a parallel learning system that
14   provides practical and flexible educational opportunities outside the formal school
15   system;
16
17          (b) “Out-of-School Youth (OSY)” refers to persons aged fifteen (15) to thirty
18   (30) years old who are not enrolled in school, are not gainfully employed, and have
19   not completed secondary or post-secondary education;
20
21         (c) “Technical-Vocational Education and Training (TVET)” refers to education
22   and skills training programs designed to equip learners with practical and technical
23   competencies for employment and entrepreneurship;
24
25           (d) “Social Protection” refers to policies and programs that reduce poverty and
26   vulnerability by providing livelihood opportunities, insurance, welfare services, and
27   social safety nets;
28
29          (e) “Children at Risk of Becoming Out-of-School Youth” refers to minors
30   vulnerable to dropping out due to poverty, abuse, family problems, early pregnancy,
31   displacement, disability, or similar circumstances; and
32
33          (f) “Especially Difficult Circumstances” refer to situations involving abuse,
34   exploitation, trafficking, armed conflict, abandonment, mental health conditions,
35   substance abuse, child labor, homelessness, or conflict with the law.
36
37          SECTION 4. Rights of Out-of-School Youth. - Out-of-school youth shall
38   enjoy all rights guaranteed under the Constitution and existing laws without
39   discrimination based on socioeconomic status, gender, sexual orientation, disability,
40   ethnicity, religion, pregnancy, parenthood, or previous educational status.
41
42          They shall have the right to equal access to education, training, employment,
43   healthcare, social services, and government programs intended for youth development
44   and empowerment.
45
46         SECTION 5. Protection Against Discrimination and Exploitation. - The
47   State shall protect out-of-school youth from all forms of discrimination, violence,
 1   exploitation, trafficking, abuse, harassment, forced labor, and other acts that
 2   undermine their dignity and welfare.
 3
 4          Government agencies shall ensure that all programs and interventions under
 5   this Act are gender-responsive, inclusive, trauma-informed, and rights-based.
 6
 7           SECTION 6. Right to Decent Work. - Out-of-school youth shall be entitled
 8   to fair and decent working conditions consistent with labor standards and existing
 9   labor laws. The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), in coordination with
10   TESDA and other agencies, shall facilitate employment opportunities, skills matching,
11   apprenticeships, and job placement programs for qualified out-of-school youth.
12
13          SECTION 7. Education and Skills Development. - The Department of
14   Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and Technical
15   Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) shall establish and strengthen
16   programs that ensure accessible and affordable educational opportunities for out-of-
17   school youth.
18
19          Alternative Learning System programs, bridging programs, technical-vocational
20   education, digital literacy training, and community-based learning opportunities shall
21   be expanded nationwide, particularly in underserved and geographically isolated
22   areas.
23          TESDA shall provide free technical-vocational training, including assessment
24   and certification fees, and may provide transportation allowance, training materials,
25   starter toolkits, and other forms of educational support.
26
27          SECTION 8. Scholarship and Educational Assistance. - At least ten
28   percent (10%) of scholarship slots administered by CHED and other national
29   scholarship programs shall be allocated to qualified out-of-school youth applicants,
30   subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations.
31
32         Local government units are likewise encouraged to allocate scholarship grants
33   and educational assistance programs specifically for out-of-school youth in their
34   respective localities.
35
36          SECTION 9. Health and Mental Health Services. - The Department of
37   Health (DOH), in coordination with local government units and relevant agencies, shall
38   establish a comprehensive health program for out-of-school youth that includes
39   physical health services, mental health support, psychosocial interventions,
40   reproductive health education, substance abuse prevention, and counseling services.
41
42          SECTION 10. Social Protection and Reintegration. - The Department of
43   Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) shall develop reintegration and social
44   protection programs for out-of-school youth, including case management, temporary
45   shelter, family support interventions, financial assistance, after-care services, and
46   community reintegration programs.
47
 1         Special attention shall be given to out-of-school youth in especially difficult
 2   circumstances.
 3
 4           SECTION 11. Employment Facilitation. - The DOLE, Public Employment
 5   Service Offices (PESOs), TESDA, and the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI)
 6   shall establish employment facilitation and workforce development programs
 7   specifically for out-of-school youth.
 8
 9         These programs shall include career counseling, job fairs, skills matching,
10   apprenticeships, digital employment opportunities, and entrepreneurship support.
11
12          SECTION 12. Entrepreneurial Development. - The DTI, in coordination
13   with TESDA and relevant agencies, shall provide entrepreneurship training, mentoring,
14   startup assistance, market linkage programs, and financial literacy seminars for out-
15   of-school youth interested in establishing small businesses or community enterprises.
16
17           SECTION 13. Duties of Local Government Units. - Local government units
18   shall integrate programs for out-of-school youth into their local development plans
19   and youth development programs.

20          LGUs shall establish local databases of out-of-school youth, coordinate with
21   national agencies for service delivery, conduct community-based interventions, and
22   support barangay-level initiatives aimed at preventing school dropout and assisting
23   reintegration into formal or alternative education systems.
24
25          SECTION 14. Inter-Agency Coordinating Council. - An Inter-Agency
26   Coordinating Council on Out-of-School Youth is hereby created to ensure the effective
27   implementation of this Act. The Council shall be composed of representatives from the
28   DepEd, DOLE, TESDA, CHED, DSWD, DOH, DILG, NYC, DTI, and other relevant
29   agencies. Representatives from youth organizations and civil society groups may
30   likewise be included. The Council shall formulate a national action plan, monitor
31   implementation, establish a centralized database, recommend policies, and submit
32   annual reports to Congress.
33
34         SECTION 15. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
35   implementation of this Act shall be charged against available appropriations of
36   concerned agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in
37   the annual General Appropriations Act.
38
39         SECTION 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90)
40   days from the effectivity of this Act, the Inter-Agency Coordinating Council shall
41   promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its implementation.
42
43         SECTION 17. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
44   unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.
45
46          SECTION 18. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and
47   regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
