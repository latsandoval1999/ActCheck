                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

Senior citizens are increasingly vulnerable to fraud schemes that exploit mail,
telecommunication, and online platforms. Common tactics such as phishing, text scams,
and investment fraud not only cause financial losses but also bring emotional distress to
the elderly.

According to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (2021 Financial Inclusion Survey), only 2%
of respondents correctly answered all six basic financial literacy questions, showing
limited awareness that makes seniors more prone to scams. The Anti-Money Laundering
Council (2015 Strategic Analysis Report) further reported that cross-border investment
fraud involving the Philippines amounted to ₱4.66 billion over five years, underscoring the
scale of financial crimes that can affect vulnerable populations.

This measure mandates the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
through local government units, to regularly disseminate information on mail,
telemarketing, and internet fraud targeting senior citizens. It also requires coordination
with agencies such as the BSP, SEC, NTC, DICT, and NBI to provide updated fraud
prevention materials and reporting mechanisms.

By institutionalizing education, awareness, and reporting systems, this bill ensures that
senior citizens, their families, and caregivers are better equipped to recognize and avoid
fraudulent schemes. It strengthens government accountability and affirms Congress’
commitment to safeguarding the welfare of the elderly in the digital age.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT PROTECTING SENIOR CITIZENS FROM VARIOUS FORMS OF FRAUD
   THROUGH INFORMATION DISSEMINATION AND PROVIDING MECHANISMS
                            THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Senior Citizens Protection Against
Fraud Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to care for the elderly through
just programs of social security. To this end, the State shall establish mechanisms to
educate and keep the senior citizens informed in order to protect them from various forms
of fraud using mail, telecommunications and other modern means of communication and
ensure that they are protected from the ills and evils of modern technology.

SEC. 3. Education on Mail, Telemarketing, and Internet Fraud. — The Department of the
Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall, through the local government units, after
consultation and in coordination with the different government agencies, educate the
senior citizens and their families and caregivers on the following: a) General information
on mail, telemarketing, and internet fraud targeting senior citizens, including descriptions
of the most common fraud schemes; and b) Information on how and where to report fraud,
and how to prevent and avoid various forms of fraud targeting senior citizens.

SEC. 4. Information Gathering. — The DILG shall gather information for dissemination to
senior citizens from the following government agencies:

   a. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) — with respect to fraud and scams related to
      products and services of BSP-regulated financial institutions;
   b. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and Insurance Commission — with
      respect to fraud and scams related to lending and investment instruments and
      insurance products;
   c. National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) — with respect to text scams
      and subscriber identification module card fraud;
   d. Philippine Postal Corporation (PPC) — with respect to fraud and scams committed
      through mail;
   e. Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) — with respect to any reported fraud such
      as marketing scams; and
   f. National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), Philippine National Police (PNP), and
      Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC) — with respect to other
      fraudulent activities and cybercrimes.

SEC. 5. Role of the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT).
— The DICT shall provide the necessary technical assistance to the DILG, National
Commission on Senior Citizens (NCSC) and the concerned government agencies
mentioned in the preceding section, particularly on the development of a website and an
online platform to facilitate coordination and sharing of information.
The DICT shall also provide information on internet and telecommunications fraud
targeting senior citizens, including information on how to prevent and avoid these types
of fraud.


SEC. 6. Data Privacy and Protection. — All personal information collected from senior
citizens, their families, and caregivers in connection with fraud reporting, education, and
information dissemination shall be handled in strict compliance with the provisions of the
Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173).

The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), in coordination with the
National Commission on Senior Citizens (NCSC), the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), and other concerned agencies, shall ensure that:
a. Personal data is collected only for legitimate purposes directly related to fraud
prevention and protection of senior citizens;
b. Appropriate safeguards are in place to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and
availability of such data; and
c. Senior citizens are informed of their rights under the Data Privacy Act, including the
right to access, correct, and request deletion of their personal information.

SEC. 7. Other Mechanisms. — The DILG shall, in coordination with the NCSC and the
DICT, formulate and establish other mechanisms to fully protect senior citizens from any
fraud. These mechanisms include:
a. Referral of complaints to the PNP or NBI; and
b. Establishment of free hotline numbers and website for purposes of reporting mail,
telemarketing, and internet fraud.
SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the Secretary of the Interior and Local Government shall, in coordination with
the Chairperson of the NCSC, promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 9. Separability Clause. — If any portion or provision of this Act is subsequently
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof which are not affected thereby
shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, ordinances, administrative orders, rules
and regulations, or any part thereof, which are inconsistent with this Act are repealed or
amended accordingly.

SEC. 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette and at least two (2) newspapers of national circulation.
