                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This measure seeks to amend Republic Act No. 9439, otherwise known as,
“Anti-Hospital Detention Law”, through expanding the coverage and imposing more
stringent penalties to further protect the rights of patients confined in hospitals and
other medical facilities.

        The Anti-Hospital Detention Law was enacted to protect patients from unjust
detention by hospitals and medical clinics due to unpaid medical expenses. However,
despite its enactment, the law has been subject to continued scrutiny due to reported
practices wherein hospitals release the remains of deceased patients but withhold
death certificates and other pertinent documents until outstanding hospital bills are
fully settled. This practice raises concern as it is contrary to the intent of the law and
places an additional burden on the families of the deceased patients.

        This proposed measure grants priority to those deceased patients who are
indigents and all those individuals belonging to the fisheries and agriculture sectors.
Recognizing that indigents, farmers and fisherfolks are among the vulnerable sectors
in the country, they often encounter difficulty in securing hospital discharge due to
insufficiency of assets and no immediate person who can serve as a co-maker to avail
the promissory note agreement. The proposed bill expands the coverage of the Act to
include patients confined in similar medical facilities, whether public or private and
expressly prohibits the detention of patients and their relatives for non-payment of
professional fees. Patients admitted to private rooms shall likewise be covered when
no non-private rooms are available and when such admission is upon the attending
physician’s recommendation.

        This proposed measure also seeks to broaden the acceptable forms of
promissory notes to include mortgages over real or personal property, guarantee
letters from co-makers, deeds of assignment of loan benefits from the SSS or GSIS
and guarantee letters issued by the PCSO, DSWD, DOH, PAGCOR and PhilHealth.
       In the Philippines, access to healthcare is a fundamental right guaranteed by
the Constitution. However, financial constraints may give rise to situations where
patients or their families are unlawfully restrained in hospitals due to unpaid medical
expenses. This bill seeks to enhance the protection of patients’ rights by preventing
unlawful hospital detention, expanding coverage and strengthening enforcement
mechanisms under Republic Act No. 9439.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
