                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The law binds every Filipino. Under the Civil Code, ignorance of the law excuses
no one from compliance therewith.1 This rule is indispensable to public order, for no
legal system can function if citizens may evade compliance simply by claiming
unfamiliarity with the law. But the same rule carries an equally important obligation on
the part of the State: if the people are bound by law, the law must be reasonably
accessible, knowable, and understandable to them.

       The Supreme Court has long recognized that publication is essential to the
binding force of laws. In Tañada v. Tuvera, the Court held that publication is
indispensable because the people must be informed of the rules that govern them.2 The
1987 Constitution likewise recognizes the right of the people to information on matters
of public concern and guarantees access to official records, documents, papers
pertaining to official acts, transactions, or decisions, and government research data used
as a basis for policy development, subject only to limitations provided by law.3 These
principles reflect a deeper democratic premise: the law is not the private property of
lawyers, publishers, institutions, or government offices. It belongs to the people because
it governs the people.

       At present, however, public access to Philippine law remains fragmented. Official
legal texts may be available online, but they are often scattered across different
repositories, presented as originally enacted or promulgated, and not always

jurisprudence. A citizen attempting to understand a law amended several times may
have to locate the original statute, track each amendatory law, determine which
provisions remain in force, consult implementing rules, and search for relevant
jurisprudence. For lawyers and institutions with paid legal databases, this may be a
routine. For workers, farmers, fisherfolk, students, microentrepreneurs, barangay
officials, and ordinary citizens, this can be practically impossible.

       This difficulty is not merely technical. It is an access-to-justice problem. The
Philippines continues to face serious gaps in legal assistance, rule-of-law performance,
and digital inclusion. The World Justice Project has reported that the Philippines ranked
97th out of 143 countries in the 2025 Rule of Law Index, and 13th out of 15 in the East
Asia and Pacific region.4 Earlier legal-needs data also showed that many Filipinos
experiencing legal problems do not obtain formal legal help.5 At the same time, the
Philippine Statistics Authority reported that only 48.8% of households had internet
connection in 2024, confirming that digital public services must be designed for
low-bandwidth, mobile-first, and offline or assisted-access conditions.6

        This bill, therefore, seeks to establish the “Philippine Open Law System,” a free,
consolidated, continuously updated, searchable, accessible, and publicly available legal
information platform for Philippine laws, rules, regulations, jurisprudence, and other
legal issuances. This creates the public infrastructure necessary to help Filipinos locate
and understand the law they are already required to obey.

       This measure should be understood as part of the same transparency agenda
advanced by the proposed Senate Bill No. 1432, or the People’s Freedom of Information
Act. Freedom of information strengthens the citizen’s right to request and obtain
government records. This bill strengthens the State’s corresponding duty to proactively
supply the most basic public information in a democracy: the law itself. It ensures that
statutes, rules, regulations, jurisprudence, and other public legal materials are not
merely published in scattered form, but made available through an authenticated,
searchable, reusable, and accessible public system. In this sense, the proposed
Philippine Open Law System is the supply-side infrastructure that gives practical force
to the demand-side right protected by freedom of information.

support open and machine-readable formats, and progressively integrate local
ordinances and agency issuances. It shall also include accessibility measures for persons
with disabilities, low-connectivity communities, and citizens who rely on public
terminals, barangay facilities, libraries, schools, or local government offices for access to
digital services.

       The bill also anticipates the responsible use of artificial intelligence in public legal
information. Legal AI tools may assist citizens in searching, summarizing, translating,
or navigating legal texts, but they must not be allowed to replace lawyers, courts, or
competent legal authorities. Empirical studies have shown that even specialized legal
AI tools may produce inaccurate or fabricated legal outputs, making safeguards
indispensable.7 For this reason, the bill requires source citation, accuracy testing, human
oversight, hallucination-risk monitoring, public feedback mechanisms, data privacy
compliance, cybersecurity protection, and clear disclaimers stating that AI-generated
outputs are not legal advice or official interpretations of the law.

       The measure also carefully avoids displacing private legal publishers or legal
technology providers. The official text of the law must remain free and public, but
private entities may continue to offer annotations, commentaries, analytics, digests,
legal education materials, and value-added research tools. In this manner, the bill
protects the public character of legal texts while preserving legitimate innovation and
enterprise built around legal analysis.

       Other jurisdictions have already demonstrated that free public legal databases
are feasible and useful. The European Union operates EUR-Lex, which provides access
to EU law, consolidated texts, case law, and the Official Journal.8 India maintains the
India Code, a repository of central and state laws linked with subordinate legislation.9
Australia’s AustLII provides free and independent access to Australasian legal
materials.10 These models show that a modern legal system must not only publish laws,
but also organize them in a way that citizens can actually use.

essential condition for meaningful citizenship, accountable government, access to
justice, and the rule of law. A right that cannot be found, read, or understood is a right
diminished in practice. A duty imposed by an inaccessible law is a burden unevenly
carried by those least able to navigate the legal system.

        This bill gives practical effect to the constitutional right to information,
strengthens public legal literacy, supports access to justice, and builds digital public
infrastructure for the rule of law. It affirms a simple but fundamental principle: the law
is already the people’s. The State must finally put it in their hands.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE PHILIPPINE OPEN LAW SYSTEM, PROVIDING
    FOR A FREE, CONSOLIDATED, UPDATED, AND ACCESSIBLE PUBLIC
    DATABASE OF PHILIPPINE LAWS, RULES, REGULATIONS, AND
    JURISPRUDENCE,   INSTITUTIONALIZING    SAFEGUARDS     FOR
    AI-ASSISTED LEGAL INFORMATION SERVICES, APPROPRIATING
    FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives in the Philippines in Congress
assembled

        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “ Open Access to Law
Act.”

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
uphold the constitutional right of the people to information on matters of public
concern, promote access to justice, strengthen the rule of law, and ensure that the laws
binding the people are reasonably accessible, searchable, updated, and understandable.

       The State recognizes that the legal system cannot rest solely on the doctrine that
ignorance of the law excuses no one from compliance therewith, without a
corresponding public obligation to make the law knowable to the people. Laws, rules,
regulations, and jurisprudence are public legal materials issued in the name of the
Republic and shall be made available to the public without unreasonable financial,
technological, linguistic, or geographic barriers.

     The State further recognizes that the right to information has both a demand-side
and a supply-side dimension. While freedom of information mechanisms enable
citizens to request access to public records, the State must also proactively make
essential public legal information available in accessible, authenticated, searchable, and
reusable form. The law itself, being the foundation of public rights, duties, obligations,
and accountability, shall be treated as core transparency infrastructure.

       Towards this end, the State shall establish and maintain a free, consolidated,
continuously updated, and publicly accessible legal information system for Philippine
laws, rules, regulations, and jurisprudence, while preserving the independence of the
Judiciary, the constitutional powers of Congress, the rights of persons under data
privacy laws, and the legitimate role of private legal publishers in providing
commentary, analysis, and value-added services.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be
defined as follows:

   (a)​“An AI-assisted legal information service” refers to any artificial intelligence,
       machine learning, natural language processing, chatbot, or similar digital tool
       officially integrated into or linked with the Philippine Open Law System to assist
       users in searching, summarizing, translating, or navigating public legal
       information.

   (b)​ “Authenticated digital copy” refers to a digital copy made available through the
        System that has been verified as complete, genuine, and traceable to the official
        source text.

   (c)​ “Jurisprudence” refers to the decisions and final resolutions of the Supreme Court
        forming part of the legal system pursuant to Article 8 of the Civil Code.
        Decisions, resolutions, and issuances of lower courts, collegiate courts,
        quasi-judicial agencies, and constitutional bodies may be included in the System
        as public legal information, subject to arrangements with the issuing body and
        applicable rules on confidentiality, privacy, and publication.

   (d)​“Official source text” refers to the legal text officially enacted, issued,
       promulgated, filed, released, or certified by the competent government body.

   (e)​ “Public legal information” refers to the Constitution, statutes, presidential
        issuances, administrative rules and regulations, local ordinances, treaties and
        international agreements of public concern, jurisprudence, procedural rules, and
        other issuances of binding legal effect.

   (f)​ “Reference consolidation” refers to a public convenience version of a law, rule,
        regulation, or issuance that incorporates amendments, repeals, suspensions,
       effectivity dates, and other relevant changes based on available official source
       texts. A reference consolidation shall not constitute an official interpretation of
       law and shall not prevail over the official source text.

   (g)​“The Philippine Open Law System” refers to the official public legal information
       platform established under this Act.

        SEC. 4. Establishment of the Philippine Open Law System. – There is hereby
established the “Philippine Open Law System”, which shall serve as the official national
digital public infrastructure for access to Philippine legal information.

       The System shall provide the public with free access to authenticated digital
copies of official source texts and, where available, reference consolidations prepared
for public convenience. It shall be searchable, mobile-responsive, low-bandwidth
compatible, accessible to persons with disabilities, and designed to support use by
ordinary citizens, students, public officers, barangay officials, legal aid providers,
courts, lawyers, businesses, and civil society organizations.

        The System shall not replace existing publication, promulgation, filing, or
effectivity requirements under the Civil Code, the Administrative Code of 1987, the
Rules of Court, jurisprudence, and other applicable laws. Inclusion in or omission from
the System shall not, by itself, determine the effectivity, validity, or enforceability of any
law, rule, regulation, decision, or issuance.

       SEC. 5. Legal Effect of Official Source Texts and Reference Consolidations. –
Authenticated digital copies of official source texts made available through the System
shall serve as official digital reference copies for public access, subject to verification
protocols adopted under this Act.

       Reference consolidations shall be prepared solely as public legal information
tools. They shall not amend, repeal, modify, interpret, or determine the legal effect of
any law, rule, regulation, decision, or issuance. In case of conflict between an official
source text and a reference consolidation, the official source text shall prevail.

       No consolidation, summary, annotation, search result, metadata tag, explanatory
note, or AI-generated output appearing in the System shall be considered an official
interpretation of law, a legal opinion of the government, or a binding ruling, unless
separately issued by a competent authority in accordance with law.

     Each reference consolidation shall identify the official source texts used, the
amendments incorporated, the date of latest update, and any unresolved consolidation
issue requiring verification by the user, the issuing agency, counsel, or the competent
authority.

       SEC. 6. Philippine Open Law Council. – There is hereby created a “Philippine
Open Law Council,” which shall oversee policy direction, technical standards,
authenticity protocols, consolidation rules, open-data standards, accessibility measures,
AI safeguards, and inter-agency coordination under this Act.

       The Council shall be chaired by the Secretary of Justice and co-chaired by the
Secretary of Information and Communications Technology. It shall include
representatives from the office administering the Official Gazette, the University of the
Philippines Law Center through the Office of the National Administrative Register, the
National Library of the Philippines, the Department of the Interior and Local
Government, the Public Attorney’s Office, and such other agencies as may be necessary
under the implementing rules.

       The Senate, the House of Representatives, the Supreme Court, constitutional
commissions, the Integrated Bar of the Philippines, legal aid organizations, state
universities and colleges, and civil society organizations working on access to justice
may be invited as institutional partners or observers, subject to their respective
constitutional mandates and internal rules.

       Nothing in this Act shall authorize the Council or any executive agency to revise,
alter, suppress, or control official records belonging to Congress, the Judiciary,
constitutional commissions, or local government units, or independent bodies.

       SEC. 7. Coverage of the System. – The System shall progressively include
authenticated digital copies of official source texts and, where available, reference
consolidations of the Constitution, Republic Acts, Commonwealth Acts, Batas
Pambansa, Presidential Decrees, Executive Orders, Administrative Orders,
Proclamations, Memorandum Circulars, implementing rules and regulations,
administrative rules filed with the Office of the National Administrative Register
(“ONAR”), Supreme Court decisions and rules, treaties and international agreements of
public concern, and other public legal information determined by the Council to be
appropriate for inclusion.

       Local ordinances and local issuances shall be integrated in phases through
coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local Government and the
concerned local government units. The Council shall prescribe standards to ensure that
local legal materials uploaded to the System are authenticated, traceable, and clearly
identified as local issuances.
        SEC. 8. Relationship with ONAR and Duties of National Government Agencies.
– For administrative rules and regulations required by law to be filed with the Office of
the National Administrative Register, filing with ONAR shall remain the operative
filing requirement. The System shall, to the greatest extent practicable, ingest, index, or
link to administrative rules and regulations from ONAR in order to avoid duplicative
filing burdens on agencies.

        Where legal material is not required to be filed with ONAR, or where the
material is not otherwise available in authenticated digital form, the issuing agency
shall transmit to the System an authenticated digital copy in the format and within the
period prescribed by the Council.

       In case of unjustified failure to transmit, update, or correct covered public legal
information after written notice, the Council may issue a compliance directive.
Continued non-compliance shall be referred to the proper disciplining authority, the
Civil Service Commission, the Office of the Ombudsman, or other competent body, as
may be appropriate under existing laws and rules. The Council shall include material
non-compliance in its annual report to Congress.

       Failure to transmit an issuance to the System shall not dispense with publication
or filing requirements under existing law. However, the unjustified failure of a
responsible public officer to transmit covered issuances after notice and a reasonable
opportunity to comply shall constitute administrative neglect of duty, without prejudice
to other liabilities under applicable laws.

       SEC. 9. Consolidation, Version Control, and Legal Integrity. – The Council shall
adopt uniform rules for legal consolidation, citation, metadata, amendment tracking,
repeal notation, effectivity tagging, and version control. Each legal text in the System
shall preserve the relationship between the original law or issuance and subsequent
amendments, repeals, modifications, implementing rules, and relevant jurisprudence,
whenever available.

       The System shall maintain an archive of prior versions of laws and issuances to
preserve legal certainty, support historical research, and protect rights arising from past
transactions. Corrections to the System shall be transparent, time-stamped, and
traceable to the responsible office or official source.

        SEC. 10. Access for Geographically Isolated, Disadvantaged, and
Low-Connectivity Communities. – The System shall be designed for the actual
conditions of the Philippines, including areas with limited internet access, low digital
literacy, and limited access to lawyers.
       The Department of Information and Communications Technology, in
coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local Government, the
Department of Education, the Commission on Higher Education, the Public Attorney’s
Office, local government units, public libraries, and state universities and colleges, shall
establish reasonable access mechanisms such as low-bandwidth versions, downloadable
legal materials, offline access packets, public access terminals, mobile access points, and
barangay or municipal legal information kiosks.

       The System may include plain-language guides, frequently asked questions,
visual explainers, and translations into Filipino and major Philippine languages. These
materials shall be clearly marked as public legal information and shall not be treated as
controlling legal interpretations.

        SEC. 11. Verified Corpus Requirement Prior to AI Deployment. – No AI-assisted
legal information service shall be deployed to the public as part of, or officially linked
with, the System unless the Council has certified that the verified legal corpus necessary
for the intended service has been sufficiently established, authenticated, indexed, and
tested.

       The certification shall identify the categories of legal materials covered by the
AI-assisted service and the limitations of the corpus. Any AI-assisted service shall be
technically constrained to retrieve from, cite to, and operate only upon the verified
corpus or other official sources approved by the Council.

       This section shall not prevent the System from offering ordinary keyword search,
metadata search, citation search, or non-AI indexing tools before the deployment of
AI-assisted services.

      SEC. 12. AI-Assisted Legal Information Services. – The State may develop or
authorize AI-assisted legal information services as part of the System, provided that
such tools are used only to assist public access to legal information and not to replace
lawyers, courts, prosecutors, public attorneys, judges, quasi-judicial bodies, or
competent legal authorities.

       Any AI-assisted legal information service officially integrated into the System
shall cite the official source texts relied upon, disclose that it does not provide legal
advice, preserve user access to the underlying legal text, and provide referral
information to the Public Attorney’s Office, legal aid offices, courts, or appropriate
government agencies when the query appears to involve an actual legal dispute or
urgent rights-based concern.
       The Council shall establish minimum safeguards before any AI-assisted legal
information service is deployed to the public, including accuracy testing,
hallucination-risk testing, source citation requirements, bias assessment, cybersecurity
review, human oversight, public feedback mechanisms, and periodic independent
audits.

       The Council shall immediately suspend, correct, or withdraw any AI-assisted
legal information service or public-facing tool that presents a substantial risk of legal
misinformation, discrimination, privacy violation, cybersecurity breach, or public harm.

       SEC. 13. Safeguards on Accuracy, Bias, and Public Harm. – The Council shall
establish minimum safeguards before any AI-assisted legal information service is
deployed to the public. These safeguards shall include accuracy testing,
hallucination-risk testing, source citation requirements, bias assessment, cybersecurity
review, human oversight, public feedback mechanisms, and periodic independent
audits.

      The System shall not collect personal or sensitive personal information from
users except when strictly necessary for a lawful and declared public purpose.
Anonymous access shall remain available for general legal research. Any processing of
personal data shall comply with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and other applicable laws.

       The Council shall immediately suspend, correct, or withdraw any AI-assisted
legal information service or public-facing tool that presents a substantial risk of legal
misinformation, discrimination, privacy violation, cybersecurity breach, or public harm.

       SEC. 14. Open Standards and Public Use. – Public legal information made
available under this Act shall be provided in open, searchable, interoperable, and
machine-readable formats, subject to reasonable safeguards for cybersecurity, system
integrity, privacy, and protection against malicious interference.

       Official source texts of statutes, rules, regulations, judicial decisions,
administrative issuances, and other legal materials of binding legal effect shall not be
subject to copyright protection and may be freely accessed, copied, reproduced,
distributed, indexed, searched, quoted, linked, transformed into machine-readable
formats, and used for public, educational, research, civic, technological, commercial, or
non-commercial purposes, subject to the limitations provided by law.

       Consistent with Section 176 of the Intellectual Property Code, no copyright shall
subsist in any work of the Government of the Philippines and no prior approval or
conditions shall be required for the use, for any purpose, of statutes, rules and
regulations, and certain public addresses or materials rendered before courts,
administrative agencies, deliberative assemblies, and meetings of public character.

       Nothing in this Act shall prevent private publishers, law firms, universities, legal
technology developers, or civic organizations from producing annotations,
commentaries, digests, analytics, educational tools, citation tools, or other value-added
services, provided that they do not falsely represent such materials as official
government texts or exclusive sources of law.

       SEC. 15. Protection of Judicial Independence and Congressional Prerogatives. –
The integration of jurisprudence, court rules, and judicial issuances into the System
shall be undertaken with due respect for the independence and fiscal autonomy of the
Judiciary, and under such arrangements as may be agreed upon with the Supreme
Court.

       The integration of bills, legislative records, enrolled bills, Republic Acts, and
related congressional materials shall be undertaken with due respect for the
constitutional powers, internal rules, and records-management authority of the Senate
and the House of Representatives.

       Nothing in this Act shall be construed to authorize the Executive Department to
determine the meaning of judicial decisions, alter legislative records, or interfere with
the internal proceedings of Congress or the Judiciary.

      SEC. 16. Cybersecurity and Authenticity. – The Department of Information and
Communications Technology shall ensure that the System is protected by appropriate
cybersecurity, authentication, encryption, backup, disaster recovery, and continuity
measures. The System shall include audit trails sufficient to identify when a legal text
was uploaded, corrected, updated, archived, or modified, and by whom.

       The Council shall establish digital authentication protocols so that users may
verify whether a legal text, a consolidated version, or a downloadable document
originated from the official System.

       SEC. 17. Good-Faith Reliance, Corrections, and Non-Liability. – Reference
consolidations, summaries, translations, annotations, metadata, search results, and
AI-assisted outputs made available through the System are provided as public legal
information tools and shall not constitute official legal advice, official legal
interpretation, or binding rulings.
      No civil, administrative, or criminal liability shall attach to the government, the
Council, participating agencies, or their officers and employees for good-faith errors,
omissions, delays, or inaccuracies in reference consolidations, summaries, translations,
annotations, metadata, search results, or AI-assisted outputs, provided that reasonable
standards for authentication, consolidation, review, correction, and public notice have
been observed.

       This section shall not apply to gross negligence, willful misconduct, bad faith,
fraud, corruption, malicious alteration, suppression, or falsification of official source
texts or authenticated digital copies.

        The Council shall maintain a clear public correction mechanism. Corrections
shall be time-stamped, traceable, and accompanied by notice sufficient to inform users
of the nature of the correction. This section shall be read together with the rule that
official source texts prevail over reference consolidations and other public legal
information tools.

        SEC. 18. Prohibited Acts. – It shall be unlawful for any person to knowingly and
without lawful authority alter, delete, corrupt, suppress, replace, disable, or interfere
with an official source text, authenticated digital copy, authentication mechanism, audit
trail, archive, or core integrity feature of the System in a manner that affects or is
reasonably capable of affecting the authenticity, availability, completeness, or integrity
of public legal information.

        It shall likewise be unlawful for any person to knowingly upload, introduce,
certify, or cause the publication in the System of a false, forged, altered, or fabricated
legal text as an official source text or authenticated digital copy.

       It shall further be unlawful for any person, for profit, public deception, or
unlawful advantage, to falsely represent a private database, edited legal text,
AI-generated output, unofficial consolidation, or private legal information product as
an official source text, authenticated digital copy, or official output of the Philippine
Open Law System.

        Public officers who knowingly cause the unauthorized alteration, suppression, or
falsification of legal information in the System shall be subject to administrative, civil,
and criminal liability under applicable laws.

      Good-faith citation, republication, commentary, annotation, teaching, research,
legal advice, legal representation, criticism, news reporting, civic technology
development, or private value-added legal publishing shall not constitute a prohibited
act under this section, provided that the material is not falsely represented as an official
source text, authenticated digital copy, or official output of the System.

       SEC. 19. Penalties. – Any person who commits unauthorized tampering,
corruption, suppression, substitution, deletion, disabling, or interference under the first
paragraph of the preceding section shall, upon conviction, suffer the penalty of
imprisonment of six (6) years and one (1) day to twelve (12) years, and a fine of not less
than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00) but not more than Two Million Pesos
(₱2,000,000.00), when the act affects or is reasonably capable of affecting the
authenticity, availability, completeness, or integrity of an official source text,
authenticated digital copy, authentication mechanism, audit trail, or archive of the
System.

       Any person who falsely represents a private database, unofficial consolidation,
AI-generated output, edited legal text, or private legal information product as an official
source text, authenticated digital copy, or official output of the System shall, upon
conviction, suffer the penalty of imprisonment of six (6) months and one (1) day to two
(2) years and four (4) months, or a fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos
(₱50,000.00) but not more than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00), or both, at
the discretion of the court.

      If the offender is a public officer who takes advantage of official position, the
penalty shall be imposed in its maximum period, with the accessory penalty of
disqualification from public office, without prejudice to administrative, civil, and other
criminal liability under existing laws.

      Where the act is punishable under the Revised Penal Code, the Cybercrime
Prevention Act of 2012, the Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act, the Data Privacy Act
of 2012, or other special laws with a higher penalty, the higher penalty shall apply.
Prosecution under this Act shall not bar prosecution for distinct offenses arising from
the same act, subject to constitutional protections against double jeopardy.

      SEC. 20. Public Legal Literacy Program. – The Department of Justice, Public
Attorney’s Office, Department of Education, Commission on Higher Education,
Department of the Interior and Local Government, and local government units shall
promote public awareness and use of the Philippine Open Law System.

      The public legal literacy program shall prioritize workers, farmers, fisherfolk,
micro and small entrepreneurs, students, barangay officials, persons deprived of liberty,
overseas Filipino workers and their families, indigenous cultural communities, persons
with disabilities, and geographically isolated and disadvantaged communities.

       SEC. 21. Phased Implementation. – The implementation of this Act shall be
undertaken in phases. The first phase shall prioritize the Constitution, Republic Acts,
major presidential issuances, and administrative rules and regulations of national
application. The second phase shall prioritize jurisprudence, procedural rules,
quasi-judicial issuances of public significance, and agency-level regulatory issuances.
The third phase shall provide for the integration of local ordinances and other local
issuances in coordination with local government units.

       The Council shall publish an implementation roadmap within one hundred
eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act.

       SEC. 22. Annual Report to Congress. – The Council shall submit an annual report
to Congress on the status of implementation, agencies connected to the System, number
of legal texts consolidated, number of users served, access gaps, cybersecurity incidents,
AI performance audits if applicable, public feedback, budget utilization, and
recommendations for further legislation.

      The annual report shall be made publicly available through the System.

       SEC. 23. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department of
Justice, the Department of Information and Communications Technology, and other
participating agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       The Council may accept grants, technical assistance, and partnerships from
domestic or international institutions, subject to existing laws on procurement, auditing,
cybersecurity, data privacy, national security, and conflicts of interest. No grant,
partnership, or technical assistance arrangement shall grant any private entity exclusive
control over public legal information.

       SEC. 24. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred eighty
(180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Justice and the Department
of Information and Communications Technology, in coordination with the members of

        SEC. 25. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

       SEC. 26. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

      SEC. 27. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
