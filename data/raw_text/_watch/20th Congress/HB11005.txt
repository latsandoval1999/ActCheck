                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to institutionalize a Sustainable Schools Program which
mandates the installation of solar energy systems in all public elementary and
secondary schools. It promotes the use of renewable energy to improve learning
environments, strengthen climate resilience, and reduce electricity costs in public
schools.

        Many public schools continue to experience inadequate ventilation and rising
indoor temperatures due to extreme heat, which adversely affect student health,
concentration, and learning outcomes. At the same time, electricity costs consume a
significant portion of school operating expenses, while schools in geographically
isolated and disadvantaged areas continue to face unreliable or limited access to
electricity. The transition to renewable energy presents an opportunity to address
these challenges while supporting the country’s clean energy and climate
commitments.

       This measure institutionalizes a nationwide solarization program for public
schools. It prioritizes schools with the highest heat indices, off-grid locations, and
high student populations. The bill also establishes phased energy capacity targets,
provides for maintenance funding and technical training, promotes net-metering for
grid-connected schools, and ensures that procurement follows life-cycle cost and
energy efficiency standards. These measures are intended to improve energy
security, lower operating costs, and provide a more conducive environment for
teaching and learning.

      Investing in renewable energy for public schools is an investment in both
education and sustainable development. This bill promotes quality education while
advancing energy security, environmental sustainability, and climate resilience.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                         AN ACT
MANDATING THE INSTALLATION OF SOLAR ENERGY SYSTEMS IN ALL
PUBLIC SCHOOLS, ESTABLISHING THE SUSTAINABLE SCHOOLS
PROGRAM, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREOF

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled.

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Solar–Powered Schools
Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote the use of renewable energy in public infrastructure to ensure climate
resilience and a conducive learning environment. The State recognizes that extreme
heat impacts student performance and seeks to modernize public schools through
sustainable and cost–effective energy solutions. Towards this end, the State shall
institutionalize a nationwide school solarization program to lower electricity
expenses, guarantee reliable power access, and foster a safe, modern, and conducive
environment for teaching and learning.

      SEC. 3. The Sustainable Schools Program. – The Department of Education
(DepEd), in coordination with the Department of Energy (DOE) and the Department
of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), shall implement a program to install solar
energy systems in all public elementary and secondary schools.

        The program shall prioritize:​

           a. Schools located in areas with the highest recorded heat indices;

           b. Off–grid schools with no existing access to electricity; and

           c. Schools with the highest student population density.

       SEC. 4. Implementation and Capacity Targets. – The solar energy systems shall be
designed to power essential academic facilities, including classrooms, laboratories,
and libraries, with a priority on cooling and ventilation systems.

         a.​ Initial Phase: Within two (2) years, systems must cover at least twenty
      percent (20%) of the school’s average monthly utilization (AMU).​

         b.​ Expansion Phase: Within five (5) years, capacity shall be increased to
      cover at least fifty percent (50%) of the AMU.

       SEC. 5. Maintenance and Sustainability. – To ensure the long–term practicality
of the systems, the DOE shall provide technical training to school facility
coordinators on the basic operation and maintenance of solar equipment. DepEd
shall include a “Solar Maintenance Fund” in its annual budget to ensure the
replacement of batteries, inverters, and other components as needed.

        SEC. 6. Net–Metering Option. – For schools already connected to the main
electrical grid, the installation of “On–Grid” or “Hybrid” systems shall be
encouraged to allow for Net–Metering. Any credits or savings realized from selling
excess energy back to the grid shall be retained by the school for the maintenance of
the solar systems and other facilities.

        SEC. 7. Procurement. – The procurement of solar energy systems under this
Act shall be in accordance with Republic Act No. 12009, otherwise known as the
“New Government Procurement Act”. The DepEd, as the procuring entity, shalI
prioritize the Most Economically Advantageous and Responsive Bid (MEARB),
taking into consideration the life–cycle costs and the long–term energy efficiency of
the systems.

        SEC. 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s available appropriations of the
DepEd.

      For the first two (2) years of implementation, the program shall be funded
through the following sources:

          a.​ The Basic Education Facilities (BEF) Fund of the DepEd;​

         b.​ The Renewable Energy Trust Fund (RETF) administered by the DOE;
      and​

        c.​ Savings or unprogrammed funds as identified by the Department of
      Budget and Management (DBM).

      Thereafter, the funds necessary for the continued operation, maintenance, and
expansion of the Solar–Powered Schools Program shall be included in the regular
budget of the Department of Education in the annual General Appropriations Act
(GAA).

        SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOE, DepEd, and DPWH shall promulgate the rules and
regulations necessary for the effective implementation of the provisions of this Act.

       SEC. 10. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is held
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall
remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

      SEC. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
