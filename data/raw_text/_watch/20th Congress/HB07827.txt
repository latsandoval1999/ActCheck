                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Article II, Section 17 of the 1987 Constitution mandates the State to
give priority to education, science and technology, arts, culture, and sports in
order to foster patriotism and nationalism, accelerate social progress, and
promote total human development. Complementarily, Article XIV, Section 1
provides that the State shall protect and promote the right of all citizens to
quality education at all levels and shall take appropriate steps to make such
education accessible to all.

       In recent years, the Philippine education system has undergone rapid
digital transformation. While this transition accelerated during periods of
national disruption, the shift toward technology-enabled learning has
continued beyond emergency conditions. Public schools increasingly rely on
blended and digital learning platforms, online repositories, electronic
modules, and remote access tools. However, access to learning materials
remains uneven and fragmented, particularly in geographically isolated and
disadvantaged areas.

      The absence of a centralized, government-maintained digital repository
of academic resource materials has resulted in duplication of effort,
inconsistent quality of materials, and inequitable access across regions. Many
schools continue to depend on printed modules with limited circulation, while
others rely on disparate online sources that are not uniformly vetted,
standardized, or aligned with the national curriculum.

      At the same time, the country continues to confront persistent learning
gaps, declining literacy and numeracy benchmarks, and disparities in
educational outcomes. Addressing these challenges requires systemic reform
not only in pedagogy but in infrastructure and resource accessibility.

      Digitization of academic materials offers a scalable and cost-efficient
solution. Properly implemented, a centralized digital library would:
   1. Ensure simultaneous and nationwide access to curriculum-aligned
      materials for learners and teachers;
   2. Reduce recurring printing, storage, and logistics costs associated with
      physical modules;
   3. Standardize and quality-assure instructional content;
   4. Preserve educational resources against physical deterioration, disaster-
      related loss, and obsolescence; and
   5. Support blended and hybrid learning models consistent with modern
      educational practices.

       This bill seeks to establish the Philippine Online Library, a centralized
digital platform that will compile, curate, and provide access to digitized
academic resource materials for public primary and secondary schools
nationwide. The measure also provides for the necessary infrastructure
support to ensure equitable access, particularly for underserved schools.

      By institutionalizing a national digital learning repository, the State
strengthens educational resilience, promotes equal access to quality
materials, and advances its constitutional mandate to make education truly
accessible to all.

      Based on the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                          AN ACT
 DIGITIZING ALL BOOKS NECESSARY FOR PUBLIC EDUCATION AND
ESTABLISHING THE PHILIPPINE ONLINE LIBRARY, PROVIDING FUNDS
             THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:


      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine
Online Library Act of 2026.”

        SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of
the State to protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education
at all levels and to take appropriate steps to make such education accessible
to all. Thus, the State shall endeavor to introduce measures that will allow its
citizens to catch up with the increasingly digitized approach to education and
remove barriers to access to necessary online educational materials.
       SECTION 3. Philippine Online Library. – The Department of Education
(DepEd) shall create digitized copies of all textbooks and reference books as it
deems necessary for the public education of Philippine primary and secondary
school students. These digitized copies shall be compiled in the Philippine
Online Library which shall be jointly managed by the DepEd, the Department
of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), and the Intellectual
Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL).

      SECTION 4. Provision for Access to the Digitized Copies of Textbooks. –
The Department of Education (DepEd) shall ensure access to the digitized
copies of textbooks by providing computers, laptops, or appropriate
wherewithal to all primary and secondary public schools nationwide.

      SECTION 5. Additional Online Educational Materials. – The Department
of Education (DepEd) is recommended to tap the National Library of the
Philippines to supplement the Philippine Online Library’s digital repository of
textbooks with additional materials that are representative of Filipino culture
and literature. DepEd shall also be allowed to add other online materials that
it deems necessary for expanding the learning opportunities of students.

       SECTION 6. Custody Over Digitized Copies of Textbooks. – The
Department of Education (DepEd) and the National Library of the Philippines
shall have joint custody over digitized copies of the textbooks. The Department
of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) in consultation with
the IPOPHL shall likewise ensure the technological management of the digital
repository to ensure the integrity of the system and its compliance with
intellectual property laws.

      SECTION 7. Appropriations. – Such amount as may be necessary for
the immediate implementation of this Act shall be drawn from and managed
by the Department of Education (DepEd) and Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT). Thereafter, such amount as may be
necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of
Education (DepEd), the Department of Information and Communications
Technology (DICT), and the National Library of the Philippines shall have joint
responsibility for the formulation and promulgation of the Implementing
Rules and Regulations necessary for the effective execution and enforcement
of this Act. They shall do so in close coordination and consultation with the
Department of Science and Technology, IPOPHL, and other stakeholders. The
Implementing Rules and Regulations shall be released within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act.

      SECTION 9. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be
declared unconstitutional or invalid, such declaration shall not invalidate
other parts thereof which shall remain in full force and effect.

       SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders,
presidential decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders,
rules, and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 11. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
