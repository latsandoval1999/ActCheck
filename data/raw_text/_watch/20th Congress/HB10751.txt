                                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Sa bawat butil ng kanin na ating kinokonsumo, sa bawat isda o yamang dagat na ating
nilalasap, may magsasakang tinitiis ang matinding init ng araw at may mangingisdang
hinaharap ang mapanganib na alon ng dagat…

        Ngunit sa kabalintunaan, ang mga magsasaka at mangingisda ang mga sektor na
nanatiling pinakaduha sa Pilipinas.

        Filipino farmers and fisherfolk hold the primary role in ensuring national food security
in the country. They are the most indispensable yet most vulnerable sectors of the Philippine
economy. Through their hard work, they feed millions of Filipino families every day despite
unstable incomes, high production costs, climate-related disasters, and limited social
protection.

        The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) research showed that farmers and fisherfolk
consistently have the highest poverty incidence among all basic sectors in the country. It further
reported that in 2021, the poverty incidence among fisherfolk stood at 30.6%, higher than the
national poverty incidence.1 This means that nearly one (1) in every three (3) Filipino farmers
and fisherfolk continues to live below the poverty threshold despite their vital contribution to
the country’s food supply.

        Critical sources of livelihood and employment still come from the sectors of agriculture
and fishing. Around 22.36% of total employment in the Philippines in 2023 was generated by
agriculture-related work, proving the continued dependence of millions of Filipino families on
this sector for survival.2 The fisheries sector alone directly and indirectly supports the
livelihoods of approximately 2.29 million Filipinos, including those engaged in capture

        Filipino farmers and fisherfolk remain economically insecure, especially the elderly
ones, despite their indispensable role in the country. Most agricultural workers belong to the
informal sector and often lack adequate retirement savings, pension security, insurance
protection, and stable access to social security benefits. Many continue to work even in old age
because retirement is simply unaffordable and not an option. Studies and government reports
indicate that the average Filipino farmer is now in their mid-50s, an indication of an urgent
demographic concern over the sustainability of food production and the declining interest of
younger generations in agriculture.5

        Without a comprehensive and dedicated pension mechanism for agricultural workers,
not only perpetuates poverty but also discourages younger Filipinos from entering the
agriculture and fisheries sectors. Without institutional support, farming and fishing become
occupations associated with poverty.

        This proposed measure seeks to address this longstanding inequity through the
establishment of the Farmers and Fisherfolk Social Security and Pension Program, a
comprehensive pension and social protection system designed to provide income security and
retirement protection to farmers and fisherfolk through access to sickness, maternity, disability,
retirement, death, and funeral benefits under the Social Security System (SSS), in coordination
with the Department of Agriculture (DA) and the Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation
(PCIC).

        Recognizing that poverty among farming and fishing households is often
intergenerational, this bill further introduces an educational scholarship component for children
of registered farmers and fisherfolk. Through this bill, the State empowers the next generation
to break cycles of poverty while still honoring and uplifting families whose labor sustains the
nation.

        Higit sa lahat, ang panukalang batas na ito ang nagpapamulat sa isang simpleng
katotohanan: Na ang mga taong naghihirap at nagbabanat ng buto upang may makain ang
ating bayan ay nararapat lamang na hindi magutom, maabuso at makalimutan. Napapanahon
na upang magkaroon ng isang komprehensibo at matibay na social protection mechanism para
sa ating mga magsasaka at mangingisda na malaki ang sakripisyo para sa ating bansa.

      Similar bills were filed such as in the 18th Congress authored by Rep. Florencio Gabriel

        Given the considerations mentioned, this Representation respectfully seeks the passage
of this proposed measure.

      AN ACT CREATING AN AGRICULTURAL PENSION PROGRAM TO PROVIDE
          SOCIAL SECURITY AND PENSION BENEFITS TO FARMERS AND
                              FISHERFOLK


     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Pensyonadong Magsasaka at
 2   Mangingisda Act.”

 3          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to:

 4              a. Revitalize the agriculture industry towards sustainability and create the necessary
 5                 conditions to end hunger, achieve food security, and improve the general health and
 6                 nutrition of Filipinos;
 7              b. Recognize the important role and give proper value to the contributions of farmers
 8                 and fisherfolk in our daily survival, national development, and in ensuring long-
 9                 term food security;
10              c. Protect and promote the welfare of farmers and fisherfolk by putting in place
11                 measures that would reduce their socio-economic risks and vulnerabilities, thereby
12                 improving their quality of life; and
13              d. Establish and institutionalize a comprehensive pension and social security program
14                 for the benefit of agricultural workers.

15           SEC. 3. The Farmers and Fisherfolk Social Security and Pension Program. - There is
16   hereby established the Farmers and Fisherfolk Social Security and Pension Program, herein
17   referred to as the Program, which shall provide agricultural workers a substantial and reliable
18   source of income security and secured retirement in their old age. The Program shall consist of the

                                                                                                      1
 1   comprehensive set of objectives, targets, and holistic approach in the provision of sickness,
 2   maternity, disability, retirement, death, and funeral benefits under the Social Security System (SSS)
 3   and relevant programs of the Department of Agriculture (DA), and the Philippine Crop Insurance
 4   Corporation (PCIC).

 5          SEC. 3-A. Scholarship Benefits for Dependents. – In recognition of the invaluable role of
 6   farmers and fisherfolk in national development, and to uplift their families from intergenerational
 7   poverty, the Program shall likewise provide educational scholarships to the legitimate and legally
 8   adopted sons and daughters of registered farmer and fisherfolk beneficiaries.

 9           The scholarship shall cover tuition, miscellaneous fees, and a reasonable allowance for
10   books, supplies, and other academic needs. Qualified dependents shall be entitled to a scholarship
11   grant from the time of admission until completion of a college degree or technical-vocational course
12   in any state university, college, or public technical-vocational institution.

13          In cases where slots and resources permit, qualified dependents may also avail of assistance
14   when enrolled in accredited private educational institutions, subject to guidelines prescribed by the
15   Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).

16       SEC. 4. Implementing Agencies. – The PCIC, an attached agency of the DA, and the SSS shall
17   be the primary implementing agencies of the provisions of this Act. To fulfil the goals of this Act,
18   they shall perform the following duties and functions:

19       a) The SSS and PCIC shall tap the expertise of other relevant government offices in the
20          formulation of the objectives, targets, and approaches under the Program;
21       b) The PCIC shall manage the registration system of eligible agricultural workers under the
22          Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA) and maintain the required
23          operational reports;
24       c) The SSS shall manage the funds allocated and the social security and pension scheme of
25          the Program and maintain the required actuarial and financial reports;
26       d) The PCIC and the SSS shall perform other duties and functions necessary in the efficient
27          and effective implementation of this Act.
28       e) The Department of Agriculture (DA), in coordination with the Commission on Higher
29          Education (CHED) and the Technical Education Skills Development Authority (TESDA),
30          shall administer and supervise the scholarship component of this Act. The DA shall ensure
31          that the selection of scholarship-beneficiaries is merit-based, transparent, and inclusive,
32          prioritizing dependents of indigent farmers and fisherfolk. The DA, CHED, and TESDA
33          shall submit an annual report to Congress on the number of scholarship recipients, their
34          fields of study, and their performance.
35
36           The DA shall ensure that the objectives of the Program are met and shall evaluate and
37           monitor its contribution to the productivity of the agricultural sector and the improvement
38           of the quality of life of farms, fisherfolk, and their families.

39       SEC. 5. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the initial implementation of this
40   Act shall be drawn from the savings of the national government. Thereafter, the funds necessary
41   for the continuous implementation of the program shall be sourced from ten percent (10%) of the

                                                                                                        2
 1   total annual duties collected from the importation of agricultural products. Of this amount, five
 2   percent (5%) shall be earmarked exclusively for the scholarship component established under
 3   Section 3-A of this Act. The supplemental amount necessary for its implementation shall be
 4   included in the annual appropriations of the DA.

 5       SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the date of
 6   effectivity of this Act, the PCIC shall, in consultation with the DA, SSS, the Department of Budget
 7   and Management (DBM), and the Department of Finance (DOF), formulate the rules and
 8   regulations for the proper implementation of this Act.

 9       SEC. 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
10   unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in full force and
11   effect.

12       SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive and administrative orders, rules, and
13   regulations inconsistent with the foregoing provisions are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

14       SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
15   Official gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation in the Philippines.

16           Approved,
