EXPLANATORY NOTE

Every child possesses an inherent dignity and a fundamental right to a
quality of life that allows them to achieve their fullest potential. However, for
Children with Special Needs (CSN)—including those with Autism Spectrum
Disorder, Down Syndrome, Cerebral Palsy, and other developmental,
intellectual, or sensory impairments—accessing these basic rights often involves
navigating profound systemic barriers.

In the Philippines, families of CSN frequently bear overwhelming financial
and emotional burdens. The high costs of specialized medical care,
developmental therapies, and assistive tools isolate low-income families and
restrict their children’s progress. Furthermore, the lack of equipped Special
Education (SPED) centers in underserved areas, combined with persistent
societal stigma, continuously marginalizes these children and prevents their full
integration into society.

This proposed measure seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive, whole-
of-society support system to protect and promote the rights of CSN.

Specifically, this bill aims to achieve the following:

1. Expand Educational and Therapeutic Access: Mandates the
establishment of SPED centers in underserved areas and strengthens
inclusive education programs to integrate CSN into mainstream
classrooms wherever appropriate.

===== [page 2] =====
2. Provide Meaningful Financial Relief: Alleviates the economic strain on
families through therapy subsidies, specialized vouchers, expanded
PhilHealth coverage, and the exploration of tax incentives for parents and
guardians.

3. Empower Families and Caregivers: Establishes community centers for
CSN to serve as hubs for free or subsidized therapies, while providing
parental training and emotional support groups to equip caregivers with
necessary skills.

4, Eradicate Stigma: [nstitutionalizes national public awareness campaigns
and integrates special needs awareness into educational curricula at all
levels to foster a culture of empathy and acceptance.

5. Ensure Cohesive Governance: Creates the Inter-Agency Coordinating
Council for Children with Special Needs (IACCSN) to unify the efforts of
the DSWD, DOH, DepEd, NCDA, PhilHealth, and the private sector in
implementing a national plan for CSN welfare.

A truly inclusive nation is measured by how it cares for its most vulnerable
populations. By enacting this bill, the State fulfills its constitutional mandate to
protect the rights of children and provide them with the necessary interventions
to lead dignified, productive lives.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Inclusive
Development and Well-Being of Children with Special Needs Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the rights of children with special needs, ensuring their full
and inclusive development. The State recognizes that children with special needs
possess inherent dignity and right to a quality of life that enables them to achieve
their fullest potential. To this end, the State shall:

a} Prioritize and integrate the needs of children with special needs into all
relevant legislative efforts, policies, programs, and services;

b) Ensure access to quality, comprehensive, and appropriate intervention,
therapeutic, and educational services;

c) Alleviate the financial and emotional burdens on families of children with
special needs through various mechanisms; and

d) Foster a society that embraces inclusivity, free from stigma and
discrimination against children with special needs and their families. e}
Strengthen inter-agency collaboration and establish a comprehensive
support system for children with special needs and their families.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. ~ As used in this Act:

a) Children with Special Needs (CSN) refers to individuals below eighteen
{18) years of age who have developmental] delays, disabilities, or conditions
that require specialized interventions, including but not limited to, Autism
Spectrum Disorder, Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder, Down
Syndrome, Cerebral Palsy, intellectual disability, specific learning
disabilities, sensory impairments (visual or hearing), and speech and
language disorders. This also include those who have long-term physical,
mental, intellectual or sensory impairments which in interaction with
various barriers may hinder their full and effective participation in society
on an equal basis.

b) Early Intervention Services refers to a range of services provided to
infants and toddlers with developmental delays or disabilities and their
families to promote the child’s development and minimize the effects of the
disability.

c} Therapeutic Services refers to professional interventions such as, but
not limited to, occupational therapy, physical therapy, speech and
language therapy, behavioral therapy, and other related services designed
to improve the functional abilities and well-being of CSN.

d) Special Education (SPED) refers to the educational program designed to
meet the unique needs of CSN, including SPED centers, inclusive
classrooms, and home-based programs.

e) Underserved Areas refers to geographical regions, localities, or
communities where access to specialized services for CSN is limited or
non-existent.

f}) Assistive Tools and Medical Care refers to devices, equipment,
technologies, medications, and healthcare services that assist CSN in their
daily living, communication, mobility, and overall health management.

(DepEd), in coordination with the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD) and the Department of Health (DOH), shall establish
and expand SPED schools and centers in underserved areas, ensuring
adequate facilities and qualified SPED teachers and therapists.

SEC. 7. Support Systems for Families and Caregivers. — The State shall
establish and strengthen support systems for families and caregivers of CSN:

a) Community Centers for CSN. The DSWD, in coordination with the Local
Government Units (LGUs), shall endeavor to establish or support existing
community centers in their jurisdiction. The support may include any of
the following:

i. Free or subsidized therapy services;
ii. Parental training, and/or,

iii. Emotional support groups for families of CSN. These centers shall
also serve as hubs for information dissemination and referral to
other relevant services.

b) Parental Training and Empowerment Programs. The DSWD and DOH
shall, in coordination and cooperation with the Technical Education and
Skills Development Authority (TESDA), develop and implement
comprehensive training programs for parents and caregivers of CSN.

These training programs shall focus on equipping them with the
knowledge and skills necessary to support their child’s development and manage
challenging behaviors.

SEC. 8. Stronger Policies for Inclusion. — The State shall ensure the full
inclusion of CSN in all aspects of society:

a) Inclusion in Schools. The DepEd shall formulate and implement guidelines
for inclusive education, mandating reasonable accommodations, and non-
discriminatory practices for CSN in both public and private schools; and

b) Accessibility in Public Spaces. All government agencies and private
establishments shall ensure that public spaces, facilities, and services are
accessible to CSN, in compliance with existing accessibility laws and
regulations.

SEC. 9. Inter-Agency Coordinating Council. —- There is hereby created an Inter-
Agency Coordinating Council for Children with Special Needs (IACCSN)
(hereinafter referred to as the “Council”), to ensure the effective implementation
of this Act. The Council shall be composed of representatives from the DSWD,
DOH, DepEd, National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA), PhilHealth, and at
least two representatives from the private sector, which directly implement
programs relevant and related to the needs of the CSNs. The Council shall:

===== [page 7] =====
a} Formulate and oversee the implementation of a comprehensive national
plan for the welfare of CSN;

b) Coordinate the programs and activities of various government agencies
and non-government organizations pertaining to CSN;

c) Monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of policies and programs addressing
the needs of CSN; and

d) Recommend legislative and policy reforms to further enhance the rights
and well-being of CSN.

SEC. 10. Appropriations. —-The amount necessary to carry out the provisions
of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, in coordination with the DOH, DepEd,
NCDA, PhilHealth, and in consultation with other relevant agencies and
stakeholders, shall formulate and promulgate the necessary ruies and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 12. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part hereof is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provision not otherwise
affected shall remain valid and subsisting.

SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, rules and regulations,
or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.

SEC. 14. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
