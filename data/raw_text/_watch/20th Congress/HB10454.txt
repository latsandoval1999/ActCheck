                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to institutionalize the grant of risk welfare benefits to nurse
educators assigned to supervise nursing students during clinical and
community-based Related Learning Experiences (RLEs) in public and private
institutions. The measure recognizes the occupational risks inherent in clinical
instruction and establishes a mandatory risk welfare benefit to strengthen the
recruitment, retention, and professional welfare of nurse educators, thereby
supporting the country's health workforce development and the implementation of
universal health care.

       The Philippines continues to face significant challenges in building and
sustaining an adequate nursing workforce. World Health Organization (WHO)1
identifies sustained investment in nursing education, faculty capacity, workforce
retention, and decent working conditions as essential to achieving universal health
coverage and resilient health systems. The report further emphasizes that
strengthening the nursing workforce requires not only increasing the number of
practicing nurses but also ensuring the availability and protection of qualified nurse
educators who prepare future generations of healthcare professionals.

       Despite their indispensable role, nurse educators remain outside the statutory
coverage of the hazard pay and related benefits provided under Republic Act No.
7305, otherwise known as the “Magna Carta of Public Health Workers”. While
supervising students in hospitals, rural health units, laboratories, and community
health facilities, nurse educators are routinely exposed to infectious diseases,
biological agents, hazardous substances, workplace violence, ergonomic hazards,
and other occupational risks substantially similar to those encountered by frontline
nurses. The absence of a comparable statutory benefit creates an inequitable
situation that fails to recognize the risks assumed by those responsible for educating
and training the country's future nursing workforce.

        The proposed measure addresses this policy gap through requiring

employers to provide risk welfare benefits based on the actual monthly hours of
clinical or community-based exposure. The graduated schedule of benefits is
patterned after the exposure-based hazard pay framework under the Magna Carta
of Public Health Workers and adapted to the clinical supervision workload of nurse
educators. The corresponding workday classifications are converted into equivalent
monthly hours of clinical exposure to reflect the workload of nurse educators
supervising Related Learning Experiences. The bill likewise establishes standards
for the certification of exposure hours, prescribes employer responsibility for the
payment of benefits, and provides mechanisms for compliance monitoring,
complaint resolution, and enforcement to ensure the effective implementation of the
measure.

       Nurse educators carry the responsibility of preparing every generation of
Filipino nurses who will serve in hospitals, communities, and healthcare institutions
across the country and around the world. They perform this duty while confronting
many of the same occupational hazards faced by frontline healthcare workers, yet
the law has long denied them the protection and recognition afforded to others
exposed to similar risks. Such disparity can no longer be ignored. Extending risk
welfare benefits to nurse educators is a matter of fairness, dignity, and sound public
policy. Investing in those who educate and train the nation's future nurses
strengthens the nursing profession, secures the country's healthcare workforce, and
protects the quality of care that every Filipino deserves.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
GRANTING RISK WELFARE BENEFITS TO NURSE EDUCATORS ENGAGED
IN CLINICAL OR COMMUNITY-BASED RELATED LEARNING EXPERIENCES
IN THE PUBLIC AND PRIVATE SECTORS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Risk Welfare Benefits of
Nurse Educators Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State upholds the vital importance of
achieving universal health coverage for our people and, in this regard, seeks to
address the shortage of nurses and nurse educators. In pursuit of national health
security, the State recognizes the lack of commensurate and mandatory benefits for
nurse educators. Towards this end, it shall institutionalize the grant of risk welfare
benefits as part of the recruitment and retention packages for the said educators.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act,

           a. Health facility refers to hospitals, infirmaries, rural health units,
       birthing centers, laboratories, clinics, and other health facilities licensed by
       the Department of Health (DOH);

          b. Hours of Exposure shall refer to the aggregate number of actual hours
       of clinical or community-based supervision rendered by a nurse educator
       during a calendar month;

          c. Nurse educator refers to a registered nurse who assesses, plans,
       implements, and evaluates nursing education and professional development
       programs, and is assigned to supervise nursing students during clinical or
       community-based Related Learning Experiences (RLEs). A nurse educator
       must:

                i.​ Be a registered nurse in the Philippines with a current valid
              Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) Identification (ID) Card;

                ii.​ Have attained a Master’s degree in Nursing from a duly
              recognized college or university by the Commission on Higher
              Education; and

               iii.​ Have a minimum of at least three (3) years of clinical
              experience;

          d.​ Risk refers to occupational exposure to biological, chemical, physical,
       ergonomic, psychosocial, environmental, and other hazards arising from the
       supervision of RLEs; and

          e.​ Risk welfare benefit refers to a financial benefit for nurse educators
       performing preceptorship in intensive nursing practice or RLEs. It is a
       payment to cover actual or potential threats or dangers associated with
       carrying out RLE activities.

       SEC. 4. Grant of Risk Welfare Benefits. – Schools or health facilities that employ
nurse educators, regardless of employment status, whether permanent, temporary,
contractual, part-time, or full-time, shall grant them risk welfare benefits for every
month of service, based on the aggregate monthly hours of exposure, based on the
following rates of risk welfare benefits:

 HOURS OF EXPOSURE             LEVEL OF EXPOSURE                      RATE
      96 hours or more                   High             25% of monthly basic salary
    48 hours - 95 hours               Moderate            14% of monthly basic salary
     Less than 48 hours                  Low               8% of monthly basic salary

        The actual hours of exposure shall be jointly certified by the Dean or
authorized academic head and the head of the receiving clinical facility based on
official duty assignments and RLE records.

     The employer of the nurse educator shall be primarily responsible for the
payment of the risk welfare benefit regardless of the location of the RLE assignment.

        Employers shall maintain records of RLE assignments, exposure hours,
certifications, and benefit payments for at least five (5) years and shall make them
available upon request by the implementing agencies.

       SEC. 5. Disbursement of Risk Welfare Benefits. – Risk welfare benefits shall be
paid monthly together with the regular salary of the nurse educator and shall not be
deferred, withheld, or released in lump sum except in cases authorized by law or as
may be necessary to correct administrative or payroll errors under the
Implementing Rules and Regulations.


                                            2
       Government institutions shall charge the payment against their respective
appropriations. Private employers shall release the benefit directly to qualified
nurse educators and maintain records subject to inspection and compliance
monitoring by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) and the Department
of Labor and Employment (DOLE), and by the DOH when the RLE is conducted in
a health facility.

        In the event that the area of exposure of the nurse educator entails increased
risk, the implementing agencies shall prescribe additional risk welfare benefits or
enhanced rates for extraordinary exposure in the Implementing Rules and
Regulations.

       SEC. 6. Non-Diminution of Benefits. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
reduce any allowance and benefit under existing laws, decrees, issuances, executive
orders, and contracts or agreements between nurse educators and their employers.

        SEC. 7. Complaint Resolution and Grievance Mechanism. – Any nurse educator
who is denied or deprived of the risk welfare benefits provided under this Act may
file a verified complaint before the appropriate CHED Regional Office, which shall
have primary jurisdiction to receive, investigate, and resolve complaints arising
from the implementation of this Act.

       The CHED Regional Office shall constitute a Grievance Panel, in coordination
with the DOH and DOLE, to hear and resolve complaints involving the grant,
computation, payment, or non-payment of risk welfare benefits. The Panel may
require the submission of documents, conduct investigations, summon the parties,
and issue appropriate recommendations or compliance orders in accordance with
the Implementing Rules and Regulations.

       Where the complaint involves a government higher education institution or a
state-run health facility, the CHED Regional Office shall coordinate with the
appropriate government agency concerned. Where the complaint involves a private
higher education institution or private health facility, the CHED Regional Office
shall coordinate with the DOLE and other appropriate agencies, as necessary.

      Nothing in this Act shall preclude an aggrieved nurse educator from
pursuing other administrative, civil, or criminal remedies available under existing
laws.

       SEC. 8. Implementing Agency. – The Commission on Higher Education
(CHED) shall serve as the lead implementing agency for this Act, in coordination
with the DOH, DOLE, and PRC.

       SEC. 9. Prohibition Against Circumvention. – No employer shall reduce the
salary, benefits, workload credits, honoraria, or other existing benefits of a nurse
educator to offset the payment of the risk welfare benefit provided under this Act.



                                          3
       SEC. 10. Compliance Monitoring. – The CHED shall, in coordination with the
DOH and DOLE, conduct periodic inspections and compliance audits in compliance
with this Act and may require educational institutions and health facilities to submit
reports and records necessary to verify compliance.

       SEC. 11. Annual Reporting. – The CHED shall submit to Congress an annual
report containing the number of beneficiaries, amount released, number of
participating   institutions, compliance rate, complaints received, and
recommendations not later than March 31 of every year.

       SEC. 12. Funding. – The risk welfare benefits of nurse educators employed in
State Universities and Colleges (SUCs), Local Universities and Colleges (LUCs),
other government higher education institutions, and state-run health facilities shall
be charged against the respective appropriations of the employing agency or
institution. Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.

       Nurse educators employed by private higher education institutions shall
receive the risk welfare benefits from their respective employers as part of the
mandatory employment benefits provided under this Act. Private higher education
institutions that comply with this Act may be granted fiscal incentives in accordance
with existing tax laws and subject to the approval, rules and regulations issued by
the Department of Finance and the Bureau of Internal Revenue.

      The implementing agencies shall utilize their respective existing
appropriations for the administrative expenses necessary to carry out their
responsibilities under this Act. Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary shall
be included in their respective annual budget proposals, subject to the usual
budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.

       SEC. 13. Penal Provision. – Any person, institution, or agency that violates any
provision of this Act or its implementing rules and regulations shall, upon
conviction, be punished with a fine of not less than Two hundred fifty thousand
pesos (Php 250,000.00) but not more than Five hundred thousand pesos (Php
500,000.00), or imprisonment of not more than two (2) years, or both, at the
discretion of the court.

       If the offender is a corporation, partnership, association, educational
institution, hospital, or any other juridical entity, the penalty shall be imposed upon
its president, chief executive officer, administrator, dean, director, or any other
responsible officer who authorized, directed, or knowingly permitted the
commission of the violation. The judicial entity shall likewise be subject to the
applicable fine and other sanctions provided under this act.

       If the offender is a government agency or public institution, the responsible
public official or employee who authorized, directed, or knowingly permitted the
violation shall be subject to the penalties provided in this Act, in addition to any
administrative, civil, or criminal liability that may arise under existing laws.

       The imposition of penalties under this Act shall not prevent an aggrieved
nurse educator from pursuing the recovery of unpaid risk welfare benefits and other
monetary claims through the appropriate administrative, quasi-judicial, or judicial
proceedings.

       CHED may impose administrative sanctions on private higher education
institutions that repeatedly fail to comply with this Act, without prejudice to other
penalties provided by law.

       SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days after
the effectivity of this Act, CHED, as the lead implementing agency, shall promulgate
the implementing rules and regulations to carry out the provisions of this Act in
coordination with DOH, DOLE, and PRC; Provided, That the non-promulgation of
the rules and regulations shall not prevent the immediate implementation of this
Act upon effectivity.

       SEC. 15. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

       SEC. 16. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 17. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
