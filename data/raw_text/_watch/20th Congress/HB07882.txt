                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippine tourism sector remains a vital pillar of the national economy, notwithstanding the
sector’s stiff competition from neighboring Asian countries that offer more seamless shopping
experiences to tourists. While Republic Act No. 12079, or An Act Creating a Vat Refund Mechanism
for Non-Resident Tourists, Adding a New Section 112-A to The National Internal Revenue Code 0f 1997
introduced a VAT refund system, the Act’s reliance on airport-based kiosks creates significant
friction, including long queues and the potential for tourists to miss refunds due to tight flight
schedules.

It is in this regard that the proposed bill seeks to modernize the system by introducing the Direct
Point-of-Sale (POS) Refund System, shifting the primary refund touchpoint from the airport
terminal to the retail storefront. By adopting a Direct Deduction model, the Philippines follows
the highly successful precedent set by Japan. According to the Japan Tourism Agency (JTA), the
transition to at-store VAT exemptions was a primary catalyst for the surge in tourist
consumption, which reached a record ¥5.3 trillion in 2023. The same study provides that unlike
the traditional refund-later model, providing the discount immediately at the cashier encourages
re-spending as tourists are more likely to use those immediate savings to purchase additional
local goods and services before leaving the country.

Moreover, in ensuring fiscal integrity, the proposed measure mandates a real-time digital link
between Accredited Merchants, the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), and the Bureau of
Immigration (BI). The infrastructure allows for the instantaneous verification of a tourist’s
eligibility through a passport scan, preventing domestic residents from abusing the exemption.
Furthermore, the integration of Electronic Tax-Free Sales Invoices guarantees that every peso of
exempted VAT is tracked digitally, providing the government with granular data on tourist
spending patterns that were previously unavailable.

Addressing the concerns of the retail sector, the proposed Bill introduces an Automated Tax
Credit mechanism for merchants in which retailers can automatically credit the VAT they
deducted at the point of sale against their own monthly or quarterly tax liabilities, thereby
protecting the cash flow of Filipino businesses while incentivizing them to join the program. In
fact, the Global Blue (2023) data suggests that countries with in-store refund options see a thirty
percent (30%) higher merchant adoption rate compared to those with airport-only systems.

        SEC 3. Establishment of the Direct Point-of-Sale (POS) Tax-Exempt Purchase System.
– In addition to the existing refund mechanisms at international ports of exit, a Direct
Point-of-Sale (POS) Tax-Exempt System is hereby established, which shall introduce the
following features:

   (a)​Eligibility for Direct Deduction. – A non-resident tourist, upon presentation of a valid
       foreign passport and a digital or physical arrival stamp, shall be entitled to an immediate
       12% VAT deduction at the cashier. The minimum purchase requirement for a
       single-receipt transaction under this system shall be Three Thousand Pesos (₱3,000.00).

   (b)​Merchant Obligations and Digital Verification. – Accredited merchants shall utilize a
       real-time digital verification system linked to the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) and
       the Bureau of Immigration (BI). The merchant must:

         (i)​   Scan the tourist's passport to verify non-resident status and length of stay;
        (ii)​   Issue an Electronic Tax-Free Sales Invoice which serves as the official record of
                the VAT-exempt transaction;
       (iii)​   Securely pack the goods with a "Tax-Free" seal or identification, which must
                remain intact until the tourist reaches the port of departure.

   (c)​Automated Tax Credit for Merchants. – The VAT amount deducted at the point of sale
       shall be automatically credited to the merchant’s digital tax account. These credits can be
       used to offset the merchant's monthly or quarterly VAT liabilities, ensuring that the
       retailer's cash flow is not adversely affected by providing immediate refunds to tourists.

   (d)​The Export Validation Safeguard. – To prevent the domestic diversion of tax-free
       goods, the Bureau of Customs (BOC) shall maintain a digital ledger of all POS
       Tax-Exempt purchases. Upon departure, the tourist may be required to present the
       sealed goods at a Customs Validation Counter. If the goods are found to have been
       consumed or sold within the Philippines, the BOC shall be authorized to collect the
       unpaid VAT and applicable penalties from the tourist prior to boarding.

       SEC. 4. Merchant Incentives. – Accredited merchants who utilize the Direct POS
Refund System shall be entitled to a tax credit or a fast-tracked deduction against their quarterly
VAT liabilities for the amount of tax-free sales conducted.

        SEC 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) calendar days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance shall, after due consultation with the
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Transportation (DOTr), Department
of Tourism (DOT), National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA), Bureau of
Internal Revenue (BIR), and Bureau of Customs (BOC), promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations to faithfully implement the intent and provisions of this Act.

        SEC. 6. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to cover the initial implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA) of the Department
of Trade and Industry (DTI), Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), Bureau of Immigration (BI),
and Bureau of Customs (BOC). Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).

       SEC. 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional,
the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force and effect.

        SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations or parts
thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 9. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
