                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The 1987 Constitution declares that the family is the foundation of the
nation. It likewise mandates the State to strengthen the family's solidarity
and actively promote its total development. In fulfillment of this
Constitutional principle, government measures must not only protect the
rights of children with disabilities but also empower the families who bear
the primary responsibility for their care, development, and well-being.

      Republic Act No. 7277, otherwise known as the Magna Carta for
Disabled Persons, as amended, affirms the State's commitment to the
rehabilitation, self-development, self-reliance, and integration of persons
with disabilities into mainstream society. These laws emphasize that the
realization of the rights of children with disabilities is not the sole
responsibility of the government, but of the family, the community and the
society as a while.

      However, while the law recognizes the indispensable role of families,
it does not provide sufficient employment support to parents and legal
guardians who must regularly accompany their children to medical
consultations, therapy sessions, rehabilitation programs, educational
assessments, and other interventions essential to their development.

      Raising a child with disability requires extraordinary time,
commitment, and financial resources. Parents and legal guardians
often face the difficult choice between fulfilling their employment
obligations and attending to the specialized needs of their children.
Frequent medical appointments, rehabilitation therapies, individualized
educational planning, and government transactions related to disability
services commonly require the physical presence of a parent or
primary caregiver. In the absence of dedicated leave benefits, many
caregivers are compelled to exhaust their existing leave credits, forego
income, or even risk their employment in order to fulfill these
responsibilities.

       Recognizing these realities, this measure seeks to
institutionalize a ten (10)-day paid Caregiver's Leave for qualified
parents and legal guardians of children with disabilities in both the
public and private sectors. The proposed leave shall be separate from
existing leave privileges and may be availed of to accompany the child
during medical treatment, rehabilitation, therapy, educational
interventions, and other analogous circumstances requiring the
caregiver's presence.

       The proposed Caregiver's Leave is an investment in the welfare
and development of children with disabilities with their families. By
enabling parents and legal guardians to actively participate in their
children's care without jeopardizing their employment, the State
reinforces the constitutional recognition of the family as the primary
institution responsible for nurturing every Filipino child while advancing
the objectives of inclusion, equal opportunity, and social justice.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is
earnestly sought.

                          AN ACT​
    PROVIDING ADDITIONAL LEAVE BENEFITS TO PARENTS AND
      LEGAL GUARDIANS OF CHILDREN WITH DISABILITIES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Caregiver’s
Leave Act.”

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to recognize the indispensable role of parents and primary caregivers
in promoting the health, development, rehabilitation, education, and social
inclusion of children with disabilities. Consistent with the principles
embodied in Republic Act No. 7277, the State shall strengthen families by
providing appropriate support mechanisms that enable them to effectively
respond to the special needs of children with disabilities.

      The State further recognizes that the continuous care, therapy,
medical treatment, educational intervention, and other developmental
services required by children with disabilities often necessitate the physical
presence and active participation of their parents or primary caregivers.
Toward this end, the State shall provide reasonable leave benefits to
qualified employees who are parents or primary caregivers of children with
disabilities, without prejudice to existing leave privileges provided by law.

      SEC. 2. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act:

     (a) Parent or Primary Caregiver refers to the biological parent,
adoptive parent, legal guardian, foster parent, or any person exercising
parental authority or legal custody over a child with disability and who is
primarily responsible for the child's daily care, treatment, rehabilitation,
education, and welfare.

       (b) Child with Disability refers to any person below eighteen (18)
years of age, or such age as may be provided under existing laws, who has
a long-term physical, mental, intellectual, psychosocial, or sensory
impairment which, in interaction with various barriers, may hinder his or her
full and effective participation in society on an equal basis with others, and
who is duly certified by the appropriate government authority.

      (c) Caregiver's Leave refers to leave benefits granted to a qualified
parent or primary caregiver to enable him or her to perform parental duties
and responsibilities requiring his or her physical presence in relation to the
medical care, rehabilitation, therapy, education, assessment, intervention,
or other developmental needs of a child with disability.

       SEC. 3. Caregiver’s Leave. In addition to leave privileges under
existing laws, a qualified parent or primary caregiver of a child with
disability shall be entitled to not more than ten (10) working days of paid
caregiver's leave every calendar year, provided that the employee has
rendered at least one (1) year of continuous or aggregate service within the
last twelve (12) months, whether in the public or private sector.

       The leave may be availed of, in whole or in part, for the purpose of
accompanying or attending to the child during medical consultations,
hospitalization, therapy sessions, rehabilitation programs, educational
assessments, individualized educational planning, government transactions
relating to disability benefits, or other analogous circumstances requiring
the physical presence of the parent or primary caregiver.

      Availment of the caregiver's leave shall be subject to reasonable
notice and such documentary requirements as may be prescribed by the
appropriate implementing agency, taking into consideration the need to
prevent undue burden on both the employee and the employer.

      The caregiver's leave provided under this Act shall be separate and
distinct from, and shall not diminish or prejudice, other leave benefits
granted under existing laws, collective bargaining agreements, employment
contracts, or company policies.

     SEC. 4. Implementing Rules and Regulation. The Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE), and the Civil Service Service Commission
(CSC), in coordination with the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD) shall issue the implementing rules and regulations
as may be necessary to carry out the purpose of this Act.

      SEC. 5. Separability Clause. If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not
affected shall remain in full force and effect.

     SEC. 6. Repealing Clause. Any laws, decrees, rules or regulations
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

      SEC. 7. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
