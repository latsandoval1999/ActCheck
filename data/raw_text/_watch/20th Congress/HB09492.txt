                       AN ACT
ENSURING THE WELFARE AND PROTECTION OF BUSINESS
PROCESS OUTSOURCING (BPO) WORKERS IN THE PHILIPPINES


                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) industry has emerged as a major
driver of economic growth in the Philippines and is widely recognized as one of
the primary pillars of the national economy. Over the past two decades, the sector
has positioned the country as a global hub for customer service, information
technology services, finance and accounting support, healthcare information
management, and other knowledge-based services.

      The continued expansion of the BPO sector has generated substantial
foreign exchange earnings, stimulated regional development, and created
employment opportunities for a young and highly skilled workforce. However,
the nature of BPO operations—largely servicing multinational corporations
across different time zones—requires round-the-clock operations and shifting
work schedules. Consequently, a significant portion of the workforce is assigned
to night-shift work and extended irregular hours, exposing employees to
heightened health risks, workplace stress, safety concerns, and long-term
physiological effects associated with shift work.

      Given the scale of employment in the sector and the unique occupational
conditions faced by its workforce, there is a compelling need to institutionalize
  additional protections tailored specifically to BPO workers. While existing labor
  laws provide general safeguards, the distinct circumstances of night-shift and
  shifting work arrangements necessitate enhanced standards to ensure their health,
  safety, security, and overall welfare.

        This proposed measure seeks to protect and promote the welfare of workers
  in the BPO industry through the establishment of sector-specific employment
  standards and occupational health and safety policies. Among its salient features
  are the mandated regularization of BPO workers upon completion of six (6)
  months of employment, including those engaged as trainees or under
  probationary training; the prohibition of discrimination; the institutionalization of
  workplace health programs; and the provision of full health insurance coverage
  for employees.

        By strengthening employment security, promoting occupational health,
  and ensuring equitable workplace protections, this bill affirms the State’s
  commitment to safeguarding the rights and well-being of BPO workers who play
  a vital role in sustaining the country’s economic stability and global
  competitiveness.

        Finally, we recognize Rep. Gus Tambunting of the 2nd District of
  Parañaque for filing a similar bill in the Nineteenth Congresses.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “BPO Workers’
Welfare and Protection Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the rights and welfare of workers in the Business Process
Outsourcing (BPO) industry, taking into full account the unique nature and
operational demands of the sector.


      Toward this end, the State shall adopt and implement measures to enhance
the social and economic well-being of BPO workers, improve their living and
working conditions, ensure fair and just terms of employment, and promote
opportunities for professional growth and career development.


SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall cover all employees in the Business
Processing Outsourcing industry.


SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall be understood as follows:


      (a) Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) is defined as the delegation of
         service-type business processes to a third-party service provider. It is
         generally divided into the following sectors: call centers, back office
         services,   data transcription,      animation,   software development,
         engineering development, and game development. Most BPO
         companies engage in shift work to complete their 24-hour cycle.
      (b) Call Center also known as contact center, refers to a central customer
         service operation where agents—whatever their designation—handle
         business-related telephone calls, and other IT-related non-voice
         activities, on behalf of a client.
      (c) Employer includes any person or enterprise acting directly or indirectly
         in the interest of an employer, about an employee, and shall include
         government-owned or controlled corporations and institutions, as well
         as non-profit private institutions or organizations. Indirect employees
         or contractors shall also be considered “employers” in this Act.
      (d) Employee shall mean any person hired, permitted, or suffered to work
         by an employer.
      (e) Health shall connote a sound state of the body and mind of the worker,
         which enables him to perform his job normally, in a state of well-being.

                          CHAPTER 1
                GENERAL EMPLOYMENT STANDARDS

SEC. 5. Standard of Treatment. – The employer and the supervisors must, at all
times, treat the BPO worker in a just and humane manner and ensure and provide
that the rights and benefits of BPO workers be accorded as mandated by the Labor
Code. Abusive language, physical violence, or any act which debases the dignity
of a person shall not be used against the employee.


SEC. 6. Access to Relevant Information. – The State shall ensure the BPO
companies allow their workers and employees access to relevant information to
                                                                                 4
make them understand their rights, benefits, obligations, conditions, and realities
attending to their profession. BPO companies shall not restrain their employees
from organizing or attending activities, such as, but are not limited to, workshops
and symposia that seek to equip BPO workers with relevant information about
their rights, welfare, and conditions.


SEC. 7. Protection from Understaffing or Overloading. – There shall be no
understaffing or overloading of BPO workers. The ratio of BPO worker to client
quota or quantitative targets shall be such as to reasonably effect a sustained
quality of service at all times without overworking the worker and over-extending
his/her services beyond what is stipulated in the employment contract, or what is
allowed as the worker’s regular hours of work. The employer may request the
employee to perform tasks beyond the duties stipulated in the employment
contract: Provided, That the employee consents and that such tasks are duly
compensated by the employer with an additional payment of not less than 25%
of the regular rate per hour.


SEC. 8. Regularization. – All BPO workers shall be considered regular
employees upon the completion of their sixth month of employment as trainee or
apprentice, or upon the completion of a maximum probationary training period
of six months.


SEC. 9. Right to Self-Association. – It shall be unlawful for any person, entity,
or employer to interfere with, restrain, or coerce BPO workers in the exercise of
their right to self-organization, including the right to form, join, or assist labor
organizations of their own choosing, to engage in collective bargaining, and to
participate in discussions and decision-making processes affecting their rights,
interests, and welfare.

                                                                                   5
SEC. 10. Freedom from Excessive Company Bond. – It shall be unlawful for
any person or company to compel a BPO worker to commit a company bond,
imposing an unreasonable fee to be paid by the employee upon leaving the
company before a specified length of time.


SEC. 11. Protection from Discrimination. – BPO workers shall be protected
from discrimination because of sex, sexual orientation, age, political or religious
belief, civil status, pregnancy, physical characteristics or disability, or ethnicity.


SEC. 12. Hours of Work. – The hours of work for BPO workers shall be
governed by the provisions of the Labor Code, as supplemented by other
applicable laws as well as this Act.


SEC. 13. Work on Holidays. – BPO workers may be required to work on days
designated as non-working holidays in the Philippines given the nature of their
profession; Provided, That the employer shall duly compensate the employee’s
work on such days by existing laws about holiday pay.


SEC. 14. Leave Benefits. – BPO workers shall be entitled to all leave benefits
and privileges, such as but not limited to maternity, paternity, vacation, and sick
leaves, as provided for under existing laws: Provided, That upon separation of
the employee from service, they shall be entitled to all accumulated leave credits
with pay. No employee may be terminated based solely on unapproved leaves
without affording the employee due process in an administrative proceeding.


SEC. 15. Transportation Benefits. – In the absence of safe and adequate public
transportation services at the place of work of BPO workers, the employer shall

                                                                                     6
provide safe transportation services to such workers between the hours of 9:00
p.m. and 6:00 a.m. Such transportation services shall include, but shall not be
limited to, shuttle services to and from the workplace and designated drop-off
points, to ensure the safe commute of employees during said hours.


SEC. 16. Security of Tenure. – No employee may be terminated except for just
causes as may be provided by the Labor Code and other existing laws, and after
due process in an administrative proceeding as provided for under the law.


                      CHAPTER 2
     OCCUPATIONAL HEALTH AND SAFETY STANDARDS AND
                     REGULATIONS

SEC. 17. Employers’ Obligation. – Pursuant to Article 106 of the Labor Code,
labor-only contracting is prohibited. In cases where labor-only contracting is
established, the principal shall be deemed the direct employer of the contractor’s
employees and shall be responsible for all obligations arising from the employer-
employee relationship.


      In cases of legitimate job contracting or subcontracting, the principal shall
be solidarily liable with the contractor for any violations of labor standards
committed against the contractor’s employees, in accordance with law.


      Accordingly, any employer or principal who engages the services of a
contractor or subcontractor shall ensure compliance with applicable labor
standards and occupational safety and health requirements under the Labor Code,
this Act, and other relevant laws and regulations. The principal shall likewise
ensure that the workplace or worksite under its control is maintained in a safe and
healthful condition for all workers therein, including employees of contractors or
subcontractors.
                                                                                  7
      Failure to comply with the foregoing obligations shall subject the employer
or principal to liability as provided under existing laws.


SEC. 18. Occupational Health and Safety Standards and Regulations for BPO
Workers. – Under its mandate, the Department of Labor and Employment is
tasked to establish Occupational Health and Safety Standards (Standards) for
BPO work. The Standards shall adhere to the prevailing standards of health and
safety for BPO work and other similar employment. The minimum provisions in
the   Standards    must       meet   the   International     Labor   Organizations’
recommendations. The Standards should be reviewed annually by the agencies, a
Workplace Occupational Health and Safety Officer, and registered interested
parties, and must include provisions related to the enforcement officers and the
authorized representatives.


SEC. 19. Nature of BPO Night-Shift Work. – Establishments operating on a
twenty-four (24)-hour work cycle through shifting schedules expose employees
assigned to night work to increased health and security risks, as well as
physiological and psychological stress arising from work during biologically
irregular hours. Accordingly, night-shift work in the BPO sector is hereby
recognized as hazardous for purposes of this Act.


SEC. 20. Compliance with the Standards. – All establishments operating within
the Philippines shall strictly comply with the provisions of the Standards.
Compliance shall be mandatory and subject to monitoring, inspection, and
enforcement by duly authorized officers or representatives, in accordance with
procedures set forth in the Standards.



                                                                                  8
SEC. 21. Workplace Occupational Health and Safety Officer. – From among
the employees, a Workplace Occupational Health and Safety Officer (WOHSO)
shall be elected to serve as a consultant and in-house monitor of compliance with
the Standards. The WOHSO shall review measures implemented to safeguard the
health, safety, and welfare of employees and shall investigate and endeavor to
resolve any matters that may pose a risk to health and safety in the workplace.


      Employees may elect more than one WOHSO representative, subject to the
approval of the Department. The powers, functions, and procedure for the
election of the WOHSO shall be prescribed in the Standards, subject to the
following guidelines: (a) All employees shall be entitled to vote in the election of
the WOHSO; and (b) The powers of the WOHSO shall include, but shall not be
limited to, inspecting any area of the workplace and requiring the establishment
of a health and safety committee.


SEC. 22. Workplace Policy on Occupational Health and Safety. – Each
establishment shall develop and implement an occupational health and safety
policy that, at a minimum, complies with the Standards set forth in Section 18 of
this Act. Establishments may incorporate additional safety measures appropriate
to the nature of the local work environment, subject to the approval of the Bureau.
The policy shall be reviewed annually by the employer, the Workplace
Occupational Health and Safety Officer (WOHSO), and a duly authorized
enforcement officer or representative.


      All employees shall be informed of the occupational health and safety
policy. Informational materials, including posters, brochures, or other similar
devices, shall be provided to employees free of charge.



                                                                                   9
SEC. 23. Health Program. – The company physician shall, in addition to his
duties stated in the Labor Code, develop and implement a comprehensive
occupational health program for the benefit of all employees.


SEC. 24. Health Insurance. – All employees shall be entitled to a free
comprehensive medical examination upon commencement of employment and
subsequently on an annual basis for the duration of their employment.


The establishment shall provide full health insurance coverage for its employees,
the terms and scope of which shall be mutually agreed upon between the
employer and the employees.


SEC. 25. Work-Related Injuries, Sickness, and Death Compensation. – All
employees and their dependents shall be entitled to compensation for injuries,
medical complications, illnesses, disabilities, or death arising out of or in the
course of employment, in accordance with existing laws, labor policies, rules, and
regulations.


      The Employees’ Compensation Commission (ECC) shall promptly process
and resolve any claim filed by or on behalf of injured, disabled, or deceased
employees or their qualified dependents.


SEC. 26. Prohibition against Elimination or Diminution of Benefits. – Nothing
in this Act shall be construed to eliminate or diminish, in any way, existing
benefits being enjoyed by BPO employees at the time of the effectivity of this
Act, or benefits beyond the minimum standards set forth by this Act.




                                                                                10
SEC. 27. Penal Provision. – Any person or company who violates the provisions
of this Act shall be punished with a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand
Pesos (P100,000.00) and/or imprisonment of not less than two (2) months but not
more than one (1) year, or both, at the discretion of the Court.


SEC. 28. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act shall be held
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not otherwise affected shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 29. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders or issuances or any part
thereof, which are inconsistent herewith, are hereby repealed or amended
accordingly.


SEC. 30. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
