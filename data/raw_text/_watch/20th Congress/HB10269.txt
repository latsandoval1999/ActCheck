                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The rapid advancement of digital technology has transformed the financial
services landscape in the Philippines. Through smartphones, mobile applications,
digital wallets, and online platforms, millions of Filipinos now enjoy unprecedented
access to financial products and services, including loans, credit facilities, and cash
advances. These technological developments have significantly expanded financial
inclusion, particularly among individuals who have traditionally been underserved by
conventional banking institutions.

       In recent years, however, the country has witnessed the proliferation of online
lending applications and digital credit platforms that offer instant loans with minimal
documentary requirements. While many of these platforms operate lawfully and
provide valuable financial assistance to consumers, numerous reports have emerged
concerning abusive, coercive, and unethical debt collection practices employed by
certain lending applications and their agents.

       Borrowers have reported receiving threatening, intimidating, harassing, and
humiliating messages through text messages, social media platforms, electronic mail,
mobile applications, and other digital communication channels. Some collection agents
have resorted to threats of imprisonment, public shaming, disclosure of personal
information, unauthorized contact with family members, friends, employers, and
colleagues, and the use of offensive, degrading, or coercive language in an effort to
compel payment.

        The increasing sophistication of technology has likewise enabled certain entities
to automate these abusive practices through mass messaging systems, artificial
intelligence-powered communication tools, caller identification spoofing, and other
technological means that amplify harassment and intimidation. These practices not
only undermine the dignity and privacy of borrowers but also erode public confidence
in the country's digital financial ecosystem.
        While existing laws provide certain protections against privacy violations and
unfair business practices, there remains a need for a comprehensive legal framework
specifically prohibiting the use of threats, intimidation, harassment, coercion, and
digital abuse in the collection of debts by online lending applications, digital credit
providers, and other similar entities.

       The State recognizes the right of lenders to collect legitimate obligations and
to employ lawful means to recover debts. However, debt collection must be conducted
in a manner that respects the dignity, privacy, rights, and welfare of borrowers.
Financial innovation must not come at the expense of fundamental rights and
protections guaranteed under the Constitution and laws of the Republic.

       This measure seeks to establish clear standards for ethical digital debt collection
practices, prohibit abusive collection methods, provide effective remedies for
aggrieved borrowers, strengthen regulatory oversight, and impose appropriate
penalties upon violators.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                  AN ACT
     PROHIBITING THREATENING, HARASSING, COERCIVE, AND ABUSIVE DEBT
     COLLECTION PRACTICES BY ONLINE LENDING APPLICATIONS, DIGITAL
     CREDIT PROVIDERS, FINANCING COMPANIES, LENDING COMPANIES, AND
     OTHER SIMILAR ENTITIES, PRESCRIBING PENALTIES THEREFOR, AND FOR
     OTHER PURPOSES

 1   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
 2   Congress assembled:
 3
 4         SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Anti-Digital Debt
 5   Collection Harassment Act."
 6
 7          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State recognizes the vital role of
 8   digital technology and financial innovation in promoting financial inclusion and
 9   expanding access to credit and other financial services. While lending companies,
10   financing companies, online lending applications, digital credit providers, and similar
11   entities possess the legitimate right to collect debts and enforce lawful obligations,
12   such right must be exercised in a manner consistent with the Constitution and the
13   laws of the Republic. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect borrowers
14   from threatening, coercive, harassing, abusive, and unethical debt collection practices,
15   particularly those perpetrated through digital and electronic means. The State shall
16   safeguard the dignity, privacy, reputation, security, and personal information of
17   individuals against intimidation, public humiliation, unauthorized disclosure of personal
18   data, and other forms of abuse arising from debt collection activities. Toward this end,
19   the State shall promote responsible lending, ethical collection practices, accountability
20   among financial service providers, and the responsible use of technology, while
21   ensuring that advancements in digital finance contribute to economic development
22   without compromising the fundamental rights and welfare of consumers. Furthermore,
23   the State shall establish effective mechanisms for regulation, enforcement, and
24   redress to ensure that debt collection activities are conducted fairly, transparently,
25   and with due respect for human dignity and the rule of law.

 2   SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act:
 3
 4   (a) Debt Collection refers to any act intended to secure payment, settlement,
 5   or recovery of a financial obligation.
 6
 7   (b) Digital Communication refers to communication conducted through text
 8   messages, emails, social media platforms, instant messaging applications,
 9   mobile applications, voice-over-internet communications, automated
10   messaging systems, artificial intelligence systems, and similar technologies.
11
12   (c) Harassment refers to repeated, unwanted, unreasonable, or abusive
13   communications intended to annoy, intimidate, embarrass, alarm, or coerce a
14   borrower.
15
16   (d) Threatening Communication refers to any statement, message, image,
17   audio recording, video, or communication that directly or indirectly threatens
18   physical harm, legal action without basis, imprisonment, public humiliation,
19   reputational damage, employment consequences, disclosure of personal
20   information, or any other adverse consequence intended to coerce payment.
21
22   (e) Digital Lending Platform refers to any application, website, software, or
23   electronic system through which loans, credit facilities, or cash advances are
24   offered or administered.
25
26   (f) Borrower refers to any individual who obtains or applies for a loan, credit
27   facility, or cash advance.
28
29   (g) Regulatory Authority refers to the Securities and Exchange Commission,
30   Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, National Privacy Commission, Department of
31   Information and Communications Technology, or any other agency having
32   jurisdiction over covered entities.
33
34   SECTION 4. Scope and Coverage. - This Act shall apply to:

36
37   (b) Financing companies;
38
39   (c) Online lending applications;
40
41   (d) Digital credit providers;
42
43   (e) Buy-now-pay-later service providers;
44
45   (f) Loan servicing entities;
46
47   (g) Third-party debt collection agencies;
 1   (h) Collection agents and representatives acting on behalf of any covered
 2   entity; and
 3
 4   (i) Any natural or juridical person engaged in the collection of debts through
 5   electronic or digital means.
 6
 7   SECTION 5. Prohibited Acts. - The following acts are prohibited:
 8
 9   (a) Sending messages that threaten arrest, imprisonment, detention, criminal
10   prosecution, deportation, or other legal consequences when such threats are
11   false, misleading, or intended primarily to intimidate;
12
13   (b) Threatening physical harm, violence, injury, or damage to property;
14
15   (c) Using profane, obscene, discriminatory, degrading, insulting, humiliating, or
16   abusive language;
17
18   (d) Publicly shaming borrowers through social media, websites, online forums,
19   group chats, or any digital platform;
20
21   (e) Threatening to publish, distribute, or disclose photographs, videos, personal
22   information, financial records, contact lists, or other private information;
23
24   (f) Contacting family members, relatives, friends, employers, co-workers,
25   clients, classmates, or other third parties for the purpose of embarrassing,
26   humiliating, or coercing a borrower;
27
28   (g) Misrepresenting oneself as a government official, law enforcement officer,
29   lawyer, court personnel, or representative of any public agency;
30
31   (h) Using caller identification spoofing, fabricated legal notices, fake court
32   orders, or simulated government documents;
33
34   (i) Sending communications designed to create a false impression that criminal
35   proceedings have already been initiated;
36
37   (j) Making repeated or excessive communications that constitute harassment;
38
39   (k) Using automated systems, artificial intelligence tools, bots, or mass
40   messaging technologies to send threatening or harassing communications;
41
42   (l) Accessing, processing, or using a borrower's contact list, photographs,
43   messages, or other personal data for debt collection purposes without lawful
44   authority;
45
46   (m) Threatening adverse employment consequences, academic sanctions,
47   immigration consequences, or professional disciplinary actions without legal
48   basis;
 1
 2         (n) Creating social media accounts, websites, or digital content intended to
 3         ridicule, shame, or expose borrowers;
 4
 5         (o) Any other act that a reasonable person would consider threatening,
 6         coercive, intimidating, humiliating, or harassing.
 7
 8         SECTION 6. Fair Debt Collection Standards. - Covered entities shall:
 9
10         (a) Conduct debt collection in a professional, respectful, and lawful manner;
11
12         (b) Identify themselves truthfully in all communications;
13
14         (c) Provide accurate information regarding the debt;
15
16         (d) Maintain records of collection communications for at least five (5) years;
17
18         (e) Establish internal mechanisms for receiving and resolving consumer
19         complaints;
20
21         (f) Adopt safeguards against abusive debt collection practices.
22
23         SECTION 7. Borrower's Rights. - Borrowers shall have the right to:
24         (a) Be treated with dignity and respect;
25
26         (b) Receive truthful and accurate information regarding their obligations;
27
28         (c) Be free from threats, intimidation, harassment, and public humiliation;
29
30         (d) File complaints before appropriate regulatory authorities;
31
32         (e) Obtain copies of collection records relating to their accounts;
33
34         (f) Seek damages and other legal remedies for violations of this Act.
35
36          SECTION 8. Duty to Implement Compliance Programs. - All covered
37   entities shall establish compliance programs, employee training systems, monitoring
38   mechanisms, and consumer protection policies to ensure compliance with this Act
39

47         (c) Administrative fines ranging from Five Hundred Thousand Pesos

 6          SECTION 10. Criminal Liability. - Any person who violates Section 5 of this

27          (d) Attorney's fees and litigation expenses;
28
