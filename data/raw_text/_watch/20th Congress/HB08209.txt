                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The 1987 Constitution upholds social justice, human dignity, and the
reintegration of individuals into society. While the penal system exists to impose
accountability for criminal offenses, it also recognizes rehabilitation as a core objective
of punishment. Reintegration into the workforce is one of the most decisive factors in
preventing recidivism and promoting long-term public safety.

        In the Philippines, formerly incarcerated individuals frequently encounter
significant barriers to employment. Standard hiring procedures often require clearance
from the Philippine National Police and the National Bureau of Investigation. While such
clearances serve legitimate public interest functions, they may also reinforce stigma that
limits employment prospects, even for individuals who have fully served their sentences
or have been lawfully released.

        Recent data from correctional authorities indicate that tens of thousands of
persons are released annually from national prisons, jails, and community-based
corrections programs. However, post-release employment placement mechanisms
remain limited and largely dependent on informal networks. International studies
consistently show that unemployment among formerly incarcerated individuals remains
significantly higher than the general population, particularly within the first year of
release—a period strongly correlated with recidivism risk.

        The economic landscape has evolved in recent years, with overall employment
levels recovering from the pandemic period. Nonetheless, labor market competition has
intensified, and vulnerable sectors, including persons with criminal records, remain
disproportionately excluded. Without structured reintegration support, former
prisoners face diminished earning capacity, financial instability, and increased social
marginalization.

       This measure seeks to promote the employment of qualified former prisoners
through structured incentives and reintegration mechanisms. By encouraging private
sector participation and facilitating access to lawful employment opportunities, the
State affirms its commitment to restorative justice. Meaningful employment enables
returning citizens to rebuild identity, restore family stability, contribute productively to
the economy, and significantly reduce the likelihood of reoffending.

      Rehabilitation cannot end at the prison gates. Sustainable reintegration requires
access to dignified work. By supporting employment pathways for qualified former
prisoners, this measure advances public safety, economic inclusion, and social justice.

       It is for these reasons that this bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
  ENCOURAGING THE EMPLOYMENT OF FORMER PRISONER IN THE PRIVATE
            SECTOR AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Bagong Laya, Bagong
Buhay Act.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to aid former
prisoners to continue their reformation and help them reintegrate into society after they
have been released from prison.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act:

          a) Business establishments refer to private companies that employ former
             prisoners.

          b) Committee refers to the Committee on Employment Opportunities for
             Former Prisoners under the Department of Justice.

          c) Former prisoners refer: a) to those convicted by the final judgment and
             who have been released on probation, parole, pardon; b) served the
             sentence imposed or has been lawfully released through parole, executive
             clemency, or completion of sentence; c) has been issued a Certificate of
             Release or Completion of Sentence by the appropriate correctional
             authority.

        SECTION 4. Tax Incentives for Private Business Establishments. – Upon the
effectivity of this Act, business establishments that will employ former prisoners as
employees shall be entitled to an additional deduction from their gross income,
equivalent to twenty percent (20%) of the total amount paid as salaries, wages, and
health benefits to former prisoners, subject to the provision of Section 34 of the NIRC,
as amended: Provided, however, That such employment shall continue for a period of at
least six (6) months.

       SECTION 5. Administrative Coordination. – The Department of Justice (DOJ),
through the Bureau of Corrections (BuCor) and the Parole and Probation Administration
(PPA), shall establish a verification mechanism for purposes of certifying eligible Former
Prisoners. The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall ensure compliance
with labor standards laws. No new permanent office shall be created under this Act.

       SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Secretary of the
Department of Justice and the Department of Labor and Employment shall, within
thirty (30) days from the effectivity of this Act, issue such rules and regulations
necessary for the proper implementation of the provisions of this law.

       SECTION 7. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of
this Act shall be included in the General Appropriations Act of the year following its
enactment into law and thereafter. An initial amount of One Hundred Million Pesos
(P100,000,000.00) shall be allocated for the first year of its implementation.

       SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, executive issuances,
or letters of instructions, or any part thereof, inconsistent with or contrary to the
provisions of this Act, are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 9. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any section or provision of
this Act shall be declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other parts thereof not affected
shall remain valid.

      SECTION 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
