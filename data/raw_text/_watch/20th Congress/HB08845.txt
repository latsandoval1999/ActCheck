                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The United Nations Educational, Scientific, and Cultural Organization
(“UNESCO”) recognizes traditional sports and games as part of humanity’s intangible
cultural heritage that must be safeguarded and transmitted to future generations. These
traditional forms of play embody the values, skills, and cultural expressions of
communities, and serve as important vehicles for social cohesion, education, and cultural
continuity.

        Indigenous sports and games refer to the traditional sports, games, or physical
activities inherent to indigenous cultural communities, reflecting their customs,
traditions, and way of life, which have been handed down from generation to generation.
These include, among others, Arnis, Sipa, Palo-sebo, Sungka, Luksong Tinik, Patintero,
Siato, and Tumbang Preso. Collectively, these activities represent a rich repository of
cultural knowledge and practices that form an integral part of the nation’s intangible
cultural heritage.

       For generations, indigenous sports and games have played a vital role in shaping
the formative years of Filipino youth. Beyond recreation, these traditional activities instill
important values such as teamwork, discipline, resilience, respect, and camaraderie. They
help build character, enhance social interaction, promote physical fitness, and provide
meaningful avenues for cultural expression and community engagement.

       In recent years, however, the practice and transmission of indigenous sports and
games have steadily declined. The growing prevalence of electronic gadgets, mobile
devices, and console-based entertainment has increasingly displaced traditional forms of
play. While technological advancement brings undeniable benefits, it is imperative to
strike a healthy balance by encouraging physical activity, direct social interaction, and
deeper engagement with the country’s cultural and natural environment. Without
deliberate intervention, many indigenous sports and games risk being forgotten,
resulting in the gradual erosion of an important aspect of Filipino cultural identity.

       In this regard, this bill seeks to protect, preserve, and promote indigenous sports
and games as part of the nation’s living heritage. The proposed measure mandates the
National Commission for Culture and the Arts, in coordination with other concerned
government agencies, to initiate programs and policies for the documentation,
preservation, and promotion of traditional sports and games. It further mandates the
Philippine Sports Commission to institutionalize an annual regional and national
indigenous sports competition to encourage participation, ensure continuity of practice,
and raise public awareness of these traditional games.

       By institutionalizing government support for indigenous sports and games, this
measure aims to promote cultural pride, strengthen community participation, foster
healthy and active lifestyles among the youth, and ensure that these time-honored
traditions remain a vibrant and enduring part of Philippine heritage.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

       AN ACT PRESERVING THE TRADITIONAL AND INDIGENOUS SPORTS
       AND GAMES OF THE PHILIPPINES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Laro ng Lahi
Preservation Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — Article II, Section 22 of the 1987 Constitution
provides that the State shall recognize and promote the rights of Indigenous Cultural
Communities within the framework of national unity and development. Furthermore,
Article 31.1 of the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples
enjoins all State signatories to take effective measures to recognize and protect the
exercise of the rights of Indigenous Peoples, including the maintenance and development
of their cultural heritage, traditional knowledge and traditional expressions, oral
traditions, literature, designs, sports, and traditional games, among others.

       Pursuant to these mandates, it is incumbent upon the State to lay down specific
policy directives to promote the interests of Indigenous Peoples and strengthen the
country’s cultural heritage and historical roots. The State shall take the necessary steps to
preserve traditional and indigenous sports and games, which serve as avenues of self-
expression and promote peace, harmony, goodwill, and camaraderie among
communities.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

          (a) Traditional and Indigenous Sports and Games (“TISG”) - refer to the sports,
              games, or physical activities rooted in the traditions, customs, and cultural
              practices of Indigenous Cultural Communities and other local communities
             in the Philippines, which have been transmitted from generation to
             generation and form part of the country’s intangible cultural heritage.
             These include, but are not limited to, tribal archery, blowpipe, sibat, dug-
             out canoe race, rock balancing, sumpit, sungka, patintero, tumbang preso, siato,
             and other games distinct to particular communities.

          (b) Indigenous Cultural Communities (“ICCs”) - shall refer to communities as
              defined under Republic Act No. 8371, otherwise known as the Indigenous
              Peoples’ Rights Act (“IPRA”).

       SEC. 4. National Registry of Traditional and Indigenous Sports and Games. – The
National Commission for Culture and the Arts (“NCCA”), in coordination with the
Philippine Sports Commission (“PSC”), the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples
(“NCIP”), the National Commission on Muslim Filipinos (“NCMF”), and other relevant
agencies, shall establish and maintain a National Registry of Traditional and Indigenous
Sports and Games.

      The National Registry shall:
         (a) Identify and document traditional and indigenous sports and games
             practiced by various ICCs and local communities across the country;
         (b) Record the historical, cultural, and technical aspects of each sport or game;
         (c) Serve as the official reference for programs, competitions, and preservation
             initiatives under this Act.

       The National Registry shall be reviewed and updated every three (3) years, or as
often as may be necessary.

      SEC. 5. Establishing an Annual Regional and National Traditional and
Indigenous Sports and Games Competition. – The PSC, in coordination with local
government units (“LGUs”), shall conduct annual regional and national traditional and
indigenous sports and games competitions.

       The host LGU, in consultation with the concerned ICCs and cultural institutions,
shall determine which sports will be included in the competitions.

      SEC. 6. Accreditation Standards for Traditional and Indigenous Sports and
Games. — The NCCA, in coordination with the PSC, NCIP, NCMF, and relevant
stakeholders, shall establish accreditation standards and guidelines for traditional and
indigenous sports and games recognized under the National Registry.

      Such standards shall include:
         (a) Criteria for recognizing official events and competitions;
         (b) Guidelines for preserving cultural authenticity and traditional practices;
         (c) Safety standards for the conduct of competitions; and
         (d) Mechanisms for accrediting organizations, cultural groups, and institutions
             promoting such sports and games.

      SEC. 7. Integration into the Educational Curriculum. — The Department of
Education (“DepEd”), in coordination with the NCCA, PSC, NCIP, and NCMF, shall
ensure the integration of traditional and indigenous sports and games into the basic
education curriculum, particularly in Physical Education and culture-based learning
modules.

       The Commission on Higher Education (“CHED”) shall likewise encourage higher
education institutions to incorporate traditional and indigenous sports and games in
relevant academic and athletic programs.

       SEC. 8. Incentives for Host Local Government Units. — Local government units
that host regional or national traditional and indigenous sports competitions may be
granted incentives by the national government, subject to applicable laws, including:

   (a) Priority access to national sports development grants;
   (b) Technical and logistical support from the PSC and relevant agencies;
   (c) Recognition programs for LGUs that demonstrate exemplary efforts in promoting
       traditional and indigenous sports and games.

      The PSC may likewise extend financial assistance to host LGUs to support the
conduct of such competitions.

      SEC. 9. Other Means of Preserving Traditional and Indigenous Sports and Games.
— The NCCA, NCIP, and NCMF, in coordination with the DepEd, CHED, and Philippine
Information Agency (“PIA”), shall initiate measures to preserve traditional and
indigenous sports and games in the country.

      These measures may include, but are not limited to:
         (a) Inclusion of such games in educational curricula;
         (b) Production of documentaries and educational materials;
         (c) Conduct of exhibitions, demonstrations, and cultural festivals featuring
             traditional and indigenous sports and games;
         (d) Demonstrations during the Palarong Pambansa and other national sporting
             events.

      The NCCA shall likewise conduct research on the various sports traditionally
played by different indigenous groups to ensure their preservation and development.
      SEC. 10. Funding. — The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the National Commission for
Culture and the Arts and the Philippine Sports Commission.

       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       The PSC may also utilize available funds from the National Sports Development
Fund established under Republic Act No. 6847, as well as receive grants, donations, and
other lawful funding support from government or private sources for purposes
consistent with this Act.

       SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The NCCA and PSC, as lead
agencies, in coordination with NCIP, NCMF, DepEd, CHED, PIA, and the LGUs, shall
promulgate the rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act within
ninety (90) days from its effectivity.

        SEC. 12. Separability Clause. – If any part or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

      SEC. 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, issuances, decrees, rules,
and regulations inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this Act are hereby
amended, modified, or repealed accordingly.

      SEC. 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
