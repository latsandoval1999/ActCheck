                                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Philippines is one of the most vulnerable countries to climate change impacts,
regularly confronting intensified typhoons, shifting rainfall patterns, and extreme weather
temperatures.

         According to the Philippine Atmospheric Geophysical and Astronomical Services
Administration or PAGASA, more tropical cyclones enter the Philippine Area of
Responsibility (PAR) than anywhere else in the world, with an average of twenty tropical
cyclones passing through the PAR annually. Extreme weather events like super typhoons
are expected to become more frequent and stronger, while temperatures are projected to
rise further.1

       As one of the sectors most severely impacted, agriculture suffers annual losses
from flooded farmlands and destroyed crops, directly threatening livelihoods and the
country’s food security. One of the ways by which we can mitigate the effects of climate
change is by institutionalizing the use of crop climate calendars.

        While traditional crop calendars, which are available at the local agriculturists'
offices, are widely available, it only provides standard planting and harvesting dates but
lack critical data on local weather and climate conditions.

        In contrast, a crop climate calendar is a visual tool that aligns the phenology and
cultivation practices with the cropping season timeline. Unlike traditional calendars, it
integrates real-time local weather and climate data, identifying specific risks faced by
crops at each growth stage.2

                           AN ACT
      ENHANCING THE RESILIENCY AND ADAPTABILITY OF FARMERS
         TO ADVERSE WEATHER AND CLIMATE EVENTS BY
   INSTITUTIONALIZING THE USE OF CROP CLIMATE CALENDARS,
  EQUIPPING FARMERS FOR CLIMATE SENSITIVE DECISION-MAKING,
  AND PROVIDING ACCESS TO LOCALIZED WEATHER AND CLIMATE
                       INFORMATION
      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
       Section 1. Short Title.- This Act shall be known as the “Climate Resilience in
Agriculture Act”

        Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - In recognition of the importance of agriculture
in the country’s economic development, the State shall enhance the resiliency and
adaptability of the sector against the adverse effects of weather disturbances and climate
change that endanger productivity levels and harvest yield. Climate resiliency shall be a
priority government intervention for the agricultural sector.

       Section 3. Definition of Terms.- As used in this Act, the following terms shall be
defined as follows:

    a.   Crop climate calendar refers to a visual tool that presents the phenology and
         cultivation practices of a crop against the timeline of the cropping season,
         containing information on the weather and climate states that occur in the
         locality and the risks faced by the crop per growing stage in relation to these;

    b.   Climate-sensitive decision refers to decisions that hinge on weather and climate
         states with final outcomes and even decision alternatives which are varying
         depending on the prevailing weather phenomena; and

    c.   Localized weather and climate formation refers to information on weather and
         climate phenomena that are particularized down to the city or municipal level.

        Section 4. Crop Climate Calendars.- The Department of Agriculture (DA) shall
establish and promote the use of crop climate calendars throughout the Philippines. It
shall work with municipal, city, and provincial agriculturists, farmers and farmer’s
organizations, academe, civil society organizations, and representatives from indigenous
peoples to formulate, use, and interpret crop calendars that are tailor-made for each
specific locality or community incorporating indigenous practices, knowledge and
strategies. The use of These crop climate calendars shall be distributed free-of-charge to
all farmers and farmer’s organizations and be made available online.

        Section 5. Climate-Sensitive Decision- Making.- The DA, through the
Agricultural Training Institute (ATI), shall provide regular appropriate training and
capacity-building for farmers and farmer’s organization in the practice of climate-
sensitive decision-making and the use of relevant tools and applications. The ATI shall
formulate and develop clear, concise and easily understandable charts, tables, diagrams,
briefers, and similar documents on climate sensitive decision-making for distribution to
farmers and farmer’s organizations.

        Section 6. Localized Weather and Climate Information.- The Philippine
Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA) shall
provide regular and up-to-date localized weather and climate information to city,
municipality, and provincial agriculturist’s offices and directly to farmers themselves
through all available means of communication and information dissemination including
internet, social media, television, and radio. This information shall be in simple English,
Filipino. Or other regional dialect as necessary and must be presented in a clear, concise
and easily understandable form.

       Section 7. Appropriations.- The amount necessary for the implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations for the year following its
enactment into law and thereafter.

        Section 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations.- Within sixty (60) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DA and PAGASA, in coordination with other relevant
government agencies, farmer’s organizations and other stakeholders, shall issue the
necessary rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

       Section 9. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations, or any part thereof, which are inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

        Section 10. Separability Clause.- If any provision of this Act, or the application
thereof to any person or circumstances, is held unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder
of this Act shall not be affected thereby.

       Section 11. Effectivity.- This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
