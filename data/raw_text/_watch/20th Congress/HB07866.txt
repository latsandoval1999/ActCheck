                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

The 1987 Constitution mandates the State to protect and promote the right to health
of all Filipinos.1 It likewise provides that the State shall adopt an integrated and
comprehensive approach to health development to make health and other social
services available to all.2 These constitutional principles underscore the need for a
healthcare system that is efficient, accessible, and responsive to the needs of the
Filipino people.

At present, medical records in the country are mostly paper-based, and stored with
fragmented health care providers. Patients who transfer from one hospital to another,
or who see multiple doctors, therefore have to repeatedly submit the same information
in order to provide an accurate medical history. However, this process is not efficient,
prone to error, or susceptible to data loss as some critical information may be forgotten
or left out.

This measure aims to establish a unified Electronic Medical Records System for all
Filipinos in order to provide a secure and centralized digital platform to record and
store patient health information. This bill seeks to cover all public health facilities under
the DOH, local government units, government-owned or controlled health institutions,

This type of system has also been adopted in other countries.3 For instance, New
Zealand recently released a Health Digital Investment Plan, a 10-year roadmap to
modernize their health sector, including having a single Electronic Medical Record
system that will allow medical information to flow securely between doctors and
hospitals.4

Under this measure, not only will health care providers be able to ensure efficient and
coordinated medical services and treatment plans, patients can also easily view their
electronic health records, grant or revoke consent for access to specific categories of
sensitive data, and designate representatives to access their records. This way,
patients will have ready access to their health information.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT ESTABLISHING A UNIFIED ELECTRONIC MEDICAL RECORDS FOR
        ALL FILIPINOS AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Unified Health Records
Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health of all Filipinos and instill health consciousness among
them. Towards this end, the State shall ensure the seamless, efficient, and
coordinated delivery of health services, and security of patient health information
through the establishment of a unified electronic medical records system.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall be defined as follows:

   (1) Electronic Medical Records (EMR) refer to the digital compilation of a patient’s
       medical profile, background, and history, including but not limited to,
       diagnoses, medications and prescriptions, allergies, treatment plans,
       immunization dates, laboratory results. It shall come in a standard electronic
       format;
   (2) Health Facility refers to any institution, whether public or private, that delivers
       health services, including hospitals, clinics, rural health units, barangay health
       stations, laboratories, virtual care entities, and other health service providers;
       and




                                            3
   (3) Interoperability refers to the ability of health information systems, software, and
       applications to communicate, exchange, and use health data effectively and
       securely.

SECTION 4. Establishment, Maintenance, and Uploading of Electronic Medical
Records. - The Department of Health (DOH), with the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT) and the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
(PhilHealth) shall establish a unified Electronic Medical Records (EMR) System that
shall be the centralized digital infrastructure for managing electronic health records of
all citizens of the Philippines.

The DOH and DICT shall be supported by:

   (a) The Philippines Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) for financial claims,
       benefits, and fraud prevention mechanisms;
   (b) The National Privacy Commission (NPC) for data protection and compliance,
       and overseeing breach management;
   (c) The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) for the
       coordination with local government health units; and
   (d) The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) to ensure identity authentication and
       portability.

Notwithstanding all provisions of existing laws to the contrary, health facilities shall
establish and maintain electronic medical records, which shall be electronically
uploaded on a regular basis. Health facilities shall likewise maintain hard copies of the
electronic records to be printed and stored as back-up records.

SECTION 5. Coverage and Compliance. -This EMR System shall apply to:

   (a) All public health facilities under the DOH, LGUs, and government-owned or
       controlled health institutions, which shall fully integrate into the system within
       three (3) years from the effectivity of this Act; and
   (b) All private hospitals, clinics, diagnostic centers, and other health service
       providers, which shall integrate into the system within five (5) years, subject to
       phased accreditation and support mechanisms.

All digital health platforms and software vendors that seek interoperability with public
systems shall comply with standards prescribed by the DOH and the DICT.

SECTION 6. Data Protection, Access Controls, and Privacy Safeguards. - The
EMR System shall be governed by the principles of privacy, security, transparency,
accountability, and data minimization. All personal and sensitive health data
processed under this Act shall be protected in accordance with applicable privacy and
data protection laws, regulations, and issuances. Data collected through the EMR

                                            4
System shall be used solely for lawful health care delivery, public health surveillance,
health systems management, or policy development. No part of a patient's health
record may be accessed, shared, or used for purposes beyond the scope of this Act,
unless with the patient's explicit consent or as otherwise provided by law. The DOH,
in coordination with the DICT and NPC, shall ensure full compliance with privacy
standards and cybersecurity protocols in the design, implementation, and ongoing
operation of the EMR System.

SECTION 7. Access and User Registry. – Access to the EMR System shall be
limited to verified and approved personnel under accredited health facilities.

   (a) Only accredited health facilities shall be permitted to access the EMR System.
       Each accredited entity shall declare to the DOH the names, roles, and
       professional credentials of its personnel authorized to access the system.
   (b) No individual shall be granted access to the EMR system unless their role and
       credentials have been approved by the DOH or its designated accrediting body.
       The DOH shall maintain a secure registry of EMR System users, including their
       declared roles, facility affiliation, and access privileges.
   (c) Health facilities shall update this list regularly and immediately notify the DOH
       of any change in employment, reassignment, or disqualification. For health
       professionals with multiple affiliations, system access shall be granted per role
       and per entity, and shall reflect only the level of access appropriate to each
       facility's scope and the user's approved responsibilities therein.
   (d) Access to the EMR System shall be limited to what is necessary to perform
       one's lawful and officially declared functions. Only licensed health professionals
       may create or modify clinical records, whether directly or through supervised
       encoding by support personnel.

SECTION 8. Patient Access. – Patients shall have access to their own electronic
health records.

Patients shall have the right to:
   (a) Securely view their complete electronic health record through an official EMR
       System portal or application;
   (b) Grant or revoke consent for access to specific categories of sensitive data,
       subject to legally recognized exceptions such as medical emergencies or
       mandatory reporting;
   (c) Designate representatives or next of kin to access their records;
   (d) Request access to a system-generated audit log of interactions, subject to
       reasonable procedures and safeguards to be defined in the IRR.

Consent to access or disclose sensitive health data shall be informed, voluntary, and
revocable at any time, without prejudice to ongoing care. No person shall be

compelled to unlock or disclose protected data beyond what is medically or legally
required.

SECTION 9. Contextual and Emergency Access. -The EMR System shall allow
conditional access for handling role-specific, contextual, and emergency situations.
   (a) Default Data Filtering. The EMR System shall present to each user only the
       subset of information relevant to their role and the specific reason for system
       access, such as a diagnosis, referral, or laboratory test request. Broader
       access to historical or unrelated data shall require additional justification or
       patient consent, except in medical emergencies.
   (b) Emergency Override Protocols. In life-threatening situations or public health
       emergencies, authorized providers may override access restrictions subject to
       system-logged justification and post-event review.

SECTION 10. Training and Capacity Building - The DOH shall allocate funds for
training healthcare personnel, including physicians, nurses, administrative staff, and
IT support teams, on the use and maintenance of the EMR system. This shall include
online modules, in-person workshops, and continuous learning support.

SECTION 11. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DOH, the DICT,
and other concerned government agencies. Thereafter, the funds necessary for the
continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SECTION 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The Department shall create
a special committee to draft the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) governing
this Act. Within three (3) months from the effectivity of this Act, the said special
committee shall submit the IRR to the Secretary of the Department who shall approve
the same within thirty (30) days.

SECTION 13. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder thereof not otherwise affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 14. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
letters of instruction, proclamations, or administrative orders, rules or regulations that
are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or modified
accordingly.

SECTION 15. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

Approved,
