                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Eversley Childs Sanitarium and General Hospital (ECSGH), situated in
Mandaue City, Cebu, stands as one of the most enduring and historically significant
public health institutions in the country. Established nearly a century ago, ECSGH
was originally founded as a sanitarium for the treatment and care of patients afflicted
with Hansen’s Disease (leprosy)—a bold and compassionate response to a
stigmatized and neglected public health crisis during that era. Through decades of
dedicated service, it not only provided life-saving medical care, but also dignity and
rehabilitation to thousands of individuals marginalized by their condition.

       Over time, ECSGH evolved from a specialized sanitarium into a full-service
general hospital, expanding its mandate to meet the increasingly complex health care
needs of Cebu’s growing population. Today, it serves as a lifeline for thousands of
patients annually, particularly those from underserved, indigent, and geographically
isolated communities across Northern Cebu and neighboring provinces such as
Bohol, Leyte, and Negros Oriental.

       Its transformation over the decades reflects a deep institutional commitment
to public service, inclusivity, and medical advancement. From its humble beginnings
as a facility for the forgotten to its current role as a hub for general and specialized
care, ECSGH has embodied the values of compassion, resilience, and excellence in
public health.

      In recent years, ECSGH has seen a significant increase in patient admissions,
expansion of medical services, and enhancement of its capabilities. Despite these
advancements, its current institutional classification and structural limitations no
longer reflect the actual scope and complexity of the health care services it delivers.

       In recognition of its growing regional significance and in pursuit of a higher
standard of care, ECSGH has set its vision to become the Apex Hospital of Northern
Cebu—a premier government-run referral institution that will lead in the delivery of
multi-specialty, evidence-based, and patient-centered healthcare. This vision is
driven by the hospital’s unique position as the primary catchment facility for
complex medical cases in Northern Cebu and as a vital referral point for nearby
provinces. Becoming an Apex Hospital will enable ECSGH to strengthen health
system integration, enhance specialty and subspecialty services, and serve as a
training ground for future healthcare professionals.

       Upgrading ECSGH into a full-fledged medical center is not merely a matter
of nomenclature; it is a recognition of the hospital’s critical function as a primary
catchment facility for patient care across Northern Cebu and neighboring localities.
As one of the few government hospitals in the region equipped to manage both
primary and specialized cases, the institutional conversion will formalize its role as
a referral, teaching, and training hospital—thereby attracting a broader pool of
skilled healthcare professionals and enabling more comprehensive, efficient, and
multidisciplinary care.

        Furthermore, the upgrade will significantly strengthen ECSGH’s capacity to
fulfill its core public health mandate—to deliver accessible, high-quality, and
comprehensive healthcare services, especially to the most vulnerable sectors of
society. By aligning the hospital’s infrastructure, workforce, and service delivery

with the standards of a tertiary-level medical center, this measure ensures that
ECSGH is properly positioned to respond to the increasingly complex and evolving
health needs of the region, solidifying its place as the cornerstone of the Northern
Cebu health system.

       This proposed legislation seeks to formally convert ECSGH into the Eversley
Childs Memorial Medical Center (ECMMC), a tertiary-level government hospital,
thereby establishing the legal and institutional foundation to support its continuous
growth and transformation. It will likewise empower the Department of Health to
prioritize the allocation of necessary resources for infrastructure, equipment, and
human capital consistent with the operational and service requirements of a modern
medical center.

      Ultimately, this institutional upgrade is both a culmination of ECSGH’s nearly
century-long legacy of service and a strategic step forward to ensure that ECSGH—
and soon, ECMMC—continues to fulfill its mission of providing accessible,
equitable, and high-quality healthcare to the people of Cebu and the Visayas, for the
next hundred years and beyond.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                        AN ACT
       CONVERTING THE EVERSLEY CHILDS SANITARIUM
    AND GENERAL HOSPITAL IN MANDAUE CITY, CEBU INTO A
           MEDICAL CENTER TO BE KNOWN AS THE
       EVERSLEY CHILDS MEMORIAL MEDICAL CENTER,
           AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. This Act shall be known as the "Eversley Childs Memorial
Medical Center Act."

       SECTION 2. The Eversley Childs Sanitarium and General Hospital, located
in Mandaue City, Province of Cebu, is hereby converted into a medical center with
all the necessary personnel, equipment and facilities, and renamed as the Eversley
Childs Memorial Medical Center, hereinafter referred to as the "Medical Center."



4|Page
      This conversion reflects the institution’s expanded mandate, elevated
capacity, and enhanced role as a tertiary-level health care provider in the region.
Accordingly, this Act expressly amends Republic Act No. 11273 and supersedes all
provisions therein that are inconsistent with the intent and purpose of this law.

        SECTION 3. The Department of Health (DOH) shall ensure the
comprehensive upgrading and modernization of the Eversley Childs Memorial
Medical Center’s infrastructure, facilities, service delivery capabilities, and human
resource complement, in accordance with the standards and requirements of a
tertiary-level medical center. Toward this end, the DOH shall implement measures
that shall include, but shall not be limited to, the acquisition and installation of state-
of-the-art medical equipment, the construction or expansion of essential healthcare
facilities, and the establishment or enhancement of specialized medical and
diagnostic services.

        In furtherance of its mandate, the Medical Center shall be allocated such
plantilla positions and staffed with the appropriate number of medical,
administrative, technical, and support personnel as may be necessary to ensure its
effective, efficient, and continuous operation. The Department of Health (DOH)
shall determine the staffing pattern of the Medical Center, subject to the review and
approval of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and shall appoint
its officials and employees in accordance with existing civil service laws, rules, and
regulations, and applicable guidelines of the DOH and DBM.

       SECTION 4. For purposes of the initial implementation of this Act, and
consistent with the standards for a Level III tertiary hospital, the Medical Center
shall commence with a bed capacity of not less than four hundred (400) beds, subject
to periodic review and adjustment based on service demand, population growth, and
regional health care needs. Any future increase in the authorized bed capacity, health
care facilities, service capability, and human resource complement of the Medical
Center shall be based on a comprehensive hospital development plan to be
formulated by the Department of Health (DOH) and approved in accordance with
applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

       SECTION 5. The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act
shall be included in the General Appropriations Act (GAA) of the year following its
enactment into law and thereafter. The DOH shall also allocate from its existing
budget the funds necessary to support the Medical Center’s transition and upgrading
requirements.


5|Page
        SECTION 6. Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH
shall, in coordination with the DBM and other concerned agencies, promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

      SECTION 7. All laws, decrees, executive orders, proclamations, rules,
regulations, or parts thereof that are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

        SECTION 8. If any provision of this Act is held invalid or unconstitutional,
the remainder of the Act or any provision not affected shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SECTION 9. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
