EXPLANATORY NOTE

Unmanned systems, such as drones and other operated or autonomous devices, are
becoming more common in our daily lives. They are now being used for many purposes,
including transportation, agriculture, business, public safety, photography, and other
activities.

While these technologies provide many benefits, their improper or irresponsible use can
also pose risk to people, property, and public safety. Therefore, there is a need a nced to
ensure that unmanned systems are operated safely and responsibility.

This proposed measure seeks to establish clear rules on the possession and operation of
unmanned systems. It aims to require owners and operators to follow safety standards and
to be accountable for the proper use of their equipment.

The bill also provides for mandatory insurance coverage to help protect individuals and
property from possible damage, injury, or other losses caused by the operation of unmanned
systems. It likewise provides appropriate penalties for violations to ensure compliance with
the law.

This measure is intended not to prevent the use and development of unmanned technology,
but to promote its safe, responsible, and beneficial use. By putting reasonable regulations in
place, we can enjoy the advantages of these technologies while protecting the safety and
welfare of the public

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

ARTICLE I: GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Unmanned Systems Regulation
and Safety Act of 2026".

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the declared policy of the State to promote and
govern the use of technology for national development while safeguarding the life, liberty,
property, and security of its citizens. The State recognizes the immense potential of drones,
formally known as Unmanned Systems (UMS), for commercial, recreational, scientific, and
public service applications. However, the State also acknowledges the inherent risks they
pose to public safety, national security, and individual privacy, including the risk of bodily
injury, death, property damage, and economic loss arising from their operation.

The State further recognizes that Unmanned Systems or drones have increasingly been
exploited for criminal, illegal, and terrorist activities, including but not limited to smuggling,
illegal drug trafficking, surveillance for criminal operations, and deployment of explosives
or hazardous materials for bombing and attacks against persons, infrastructure, and public
order.

To address these threats, the State establishes:

e A mandatory law enforcement registration system analogous to the regulation of
motor vehicles by the PNP Highway Patrol Group (HPG) and firearms by the PNP
Firearms and Explosives Office (FEO), to enable positive identification, tracking,

===== [page 3] =====
and accountability of every drone through its unique serial number, and to pre-empt
or trace the use of such systems in the commission of any unlawful act;

A mandatory insurance framework to ensure financial responsibility, prompt
compensation for victims, and accountability for damages or injuries caused by
unmanned systems.

Therefore, the State shall establish a comprehensive regulatory framework to ensure that the
importation, manufacture, sale, ownership, and operation of drones are conducted
responsibly, safely, and with financial accountability. This framework shall balance
innovation with security and protection of public welfare, empower law enforcement
agencies, and prescribe clear penalties to deter unlawful use and exploitation.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. As used in this Act, the following terms shall mean:

a.

Agricultural Operation - refers to the use of an Unmanned System for agricultural
purposes, including but not limited to, crop dusting, seeding, pest control. crop
health monitoring, and livestock management.

CAAP Regulations - Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines rules, including
Civil Aviation Regulations (CAR) Part 11 and Part 14 and CAAP Memorandum
Circular No. 29-15, governing UAS operations.

Commercial Operation - refers to any authorized use of a drone for work, business,
or in connection with any form of compensation or hire.

Comprehensive Insurance - an insurance policy covering loss of, damage to, or
theft of the unmanned system itself, together with coverage for equipment, payload,
and operational interruption, as prescribed under this Act.

Controlled Airspace - refers to airspace of defined dimensions within which Air
Traffic Control service is provided.

CTPL or Compulsory Third-Party Liability Insurance — a mandatory no-fault
or liability insurance policy providing minimum statutory coverage for death, bodily
injury, or property damage inflicted upon third parties arising from the operation of
an unmanned system, without requiring proof of fault, as prescribed under this Act.

Critical Infrastructure - refers to assets, systems, and networks so vital to the
Republic of the Philippines that their incapacitation or destruction would have a
debilitating impact on national security, economic stability, public health, or safety.

Drone Operator or Remote Pilot - refers to any natural or juridical person who
commands, controls, operates, and manipulates the flight controls of an Unmanned
System.

Drone Owner - refers to the natural or juridical person in whose name the drone is
registered.

Enhanced Managing Police Operations (EMPO) - a program strategy by the
Philippine National Police (PNP) focused on improving anti-criminality efforts by
providing targeted interventions based on prevalent crime rates. It encompasses
various facets of police work, including patrol strategies, community engagement,
and intelligence gathering, all with the overarching goal of enhancing the overall
efficiency and efficacy of the police force.

. Law Enforcement Agency (LEA) - refers to the Philippine National Police (PNP),
National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency
(PDEA), and other government agencies authorized by law to conduct law
enforcement functions.

Law Enforcement Operation - refers to the broad range of activities undertaken by
government agents to enforce the law. This encompasses the practical, on-the-
ground duties and responsibilities performed by the PNP and other law enforcement
agents to maintain public order, prevent and detect crime, and apprehend offenders.

. Modification - refers to any alteration to a drone's hardware or software that changes
its flight capabilities, payload capacity, or operational range from its original
manufacturer specifications.

. PNP Drone Registry — refers to the centralized database maintained by the
Philippine National Police (PNP), through the PNP Air Group, Maritime Group, and
Highway Patrol Group, which serves as the official repository of all registered
unmanned systems, including their serial numbers, ownership records, operational
details, and transfer history. The Registry shall function similarly to the motor
vehicle registry maintained by the PNP Highway Patrol Group and the firearms
registry maintained by the PNP Firearms and Explosives Office, for purposes of
identification, monitoring, regulation, and law enforcement.

Unmanned System by a government entity for public service purposes, such as but
not limited to search and rescue, law enforcement, reconnaissance, tactical
operations, disaster response, and other purposes deemed necessary to promote law
and order, safety, and security.

. Recreational Operation - refers to the authorized use of an Unmanned System for
personal interest, hobby, or amusement purposes, with no commercial or
professional intent.

r. Restricted Zone - refers to a designated area over which drone flight is subject to
specific conditions or limitations.

s. Unmanned Aircraft System (UAS) - refers to an unmanned system designed for

t. Unmanned Ground-Based System (UGS) - refers to an unmanned system

SECTION 4. Categories and Classifications of Drones. All drones shall be classified
based on their Maximum Take-Off Weight (MTOW) and/or operational risk as follows:

a. Based on Weight:

1. Category 1 (Micro): Less than 250 grams. Exempt from registration if used
recreationally but not from CTPL requirements where applicable.

2. Category 2 (Small): 250 grams to 7 kilograms. Requires registration and
licensing and mandatory insurance.

3. Category 3 (Standard): Over 7 kg but not exceeding 25 kg. Requires stricter
certification and higher insurance limits.

4. Category 4 (Large): Over 25 kg. Regulated similarly to manned aircraft, with
corresponding insurance coverage as prescribed by the CAAP.

b. Based on Operational Risk:

1. Class 1 (Low-Risk): Recreational flights in approved areas. Subject to CTPL as
required by weight category.

===== [page 6] =====
2. Class 2 (Medium-Risk): Commercial operations within VLOS, such as aerial
photography or mapping. Requires both CTPL and Comprehensive Insurance.

3. Class 3 (High-Risk): Specialized operations like Beyond Visual Line of Sight
(BVLOS) flights, nighttime operations, or drone deliveries, which require special
permits and enhanced insurance limits.

ARTICLE IT: GOVERNANCE, REGISTRATION, AND LICENSING

SECTION 5. Inter-Agency Council on Unmanned Systems (IAC-US). To ensure a
holistic and harmonized regulatory approach, an Inter-Agency Council on Unmanned
Systems is hereby created. It shall be chaired by the Secretary of the Department of
Transportation (DOTr) and composed of representatives from the DND, DILG, DTI, DICT,
DOST, DA, the National Security Adviser, CAAP, AFP, PNP, BFP and the Philippine Coast
Guard (PCG). The IAC-US shall be the primary body responsible for policy-making and
overseeing the implementation of this Act, including the setting of insurance coverage limits
and standards in coordination with the Insurance Commission.

SECTION 6. Mandatory Unmanned System Registration. All Unmanned Systems,
except for UAS weighing less than 250 grams used for recreational purposes, must be
registered in the Philippine Unmanned Systems Registry. The IAC - US shall establish this
central registry. The CAAP shall manage the registration of UAS. The PCG shall manage
the registration of UMS. The DOTr, in coordination with the PNP, shall manage the
registration of UGS. Registration shall be renewable every three (3) years, and renewal shall
be conditional upon proof of subsisting mandatory insurance coverage.

SECTION 6-A. Mandatory Registration with the PNP/PNP Air Group.

a) Requirement — In addition to the registration requirements under Section 6, all
drones and Unmanned Systems offered for sale, sold, transferred, or owned
within the territory of the Philippines shall be registered with the PNP Air Group
prior to delivery, transfer, or operation. This requirement applies to all categories
and classes of Unmanned Systems, including those previously exempted, to
establish a complete, accountable record of every unit.

b) Purpose and Rationale — This registration system is established by analogy to
the regulation of motor vehicles by the PNP Highway Patrol Group (HPG) and
firearms by the PNP Firearms and Explosives Office (FEO). It shall serve as the
official law enforcement database to: (1) record the unique serial number, make,
model, technical specifications, ownership details, and insurance status of every
drone; (2) enable immediate identification, tracking, and tracing of drones
recovered, seized, or involved in accidents, incidents, or crimes; (3) pre-empt the
use of unregistered or unaccounted drones in illegal activities, smuggling, drug
trafficking, surveillance operations, terrorism, bombing, or other threats to
national security and public safety; and (4) ensure that all units entering or
circulating in the country are documented, traceable, and financially accountable
at all times.

ARTICLE Il: RULES OF OPERATION

SECTION 9. General Operating Rules. All unmanned system operations shall adhere to
the following unless otherwise authorized:

a.

Operate within prescribed operational boundaries (e.g., altitude for UAS, depth for
UMS, geographical limits for UGS);

Maintain continuous visual line of sight (VLOS) with the system, unless specifically
authorized for BVLOS or autonomous operations;

For UAS, fly at a maximum altitude of 400 feet (122 meters) above ground level;

Do not fly over crowds, schools, hospitals, or public gatherings without
authorization and enhanced insurance coverage;

Do not operate a drone from a moving vehicle;
Yield the right-of-way to all manned vehicles, vessels, or aircraft;

Do not operate over crowds, critical infrastructure, or designated public gatherings
without authorization;

SECTION 10. Prohibited Acts. The following acts are declared unlawful:

a.

Operating a drone without a valid registration and/or operator's license, or required

Import, manufacture, lease, and sell drones, parts, and their peripherals without

Sell or lease a drone to any individual without a valid Drone Operator's License or

Weaponizing a drone or equipping it with any dangerous or hazardous material,

Using a drone for surveillance, harassment, or the violation of any individual's right

Using a drone to aid or commit any crime, including but not limited to smuggling,
illegal drug trafficking, and reconnaissance for criminal or terrorist activities;

Interfering with the operations of law enforcement, emergency response, or disaster
relief personnel;

Hacking, spoofing, jamming, or illegally interfering with the command, control, or
navigation signal of a registered drone;

Operating a drone while under the influence of alcohol or prohibited drugs;

Similar and analogous acts through the use of drones in the commission of illegal
activities;

Allowing insurance coverage to lapse or providing false or fraudulent proof of
insurance to evade requirements.

SECTION 11-A. EXEMPTION FOR LAW ENFORCEMENT AND PUBLIC
SAFETY OPERATIONS. The prohibitions contained in Section 11 shall not apply to the
Philippine National Police (PNP), the Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP), the Philippine
Coast Guard (PCG), and other duly authorized law enforcement agencies when operating
unmanned systems in the lawful performance of their duties and responsibilities and
fulfillment of their functions for public safety, and security:

Provided, that such operations are conducted:

1. Inthe course of legitimate law enforcement, counter-terrorism, intelligence, border
control, disaster response, search and rescue, or public safety missions;

2. With prior coordination or clearance as may be prescribed in the IRR, except in
cases of imminent threat or emergency where delay would endanger public safety
or national security;

3. In accordance with existing rules of engagement, standard operating procedures,
and protocols;

4. Inamanner that minimizes interference with civil aviation and legitimate private
activities; and

5. Properly documented and reported as required by the agency’s internal
accountability mechanisms.

Nothing in this provision shall be construed to authorize the use of unmanned systems for
purposes other than those specified by law or agency mandate.

SECTION 12. Protection of Privacy. The use of a drone for surveillance, eavesdropping,
or capturing images, videos, or audio of any person or private property without explicit
consent is strictly prohibited and shall be punishable under this Act, in addition to penalties
provided under Republic Act No. 10173, or the "Data Privacy Act of 2012."

SECTION 13. Regulation on Drone Modification. Any modification to a drone that alters
its flight performance, weight, or communication systems must be declared and approved
by the CAAP. The weaponization of a drone, by attaching firearms, explosives, biological
agents, hazardous materials, or any lethal or non-lethal weapon, is absolutely prohibited.

ARTICLE IV: ROLE OF GOVERNMENT AGENCIES AND SPECIAL
OPERATIONS

SECTION 14. Role of Local Government Units. Consistent with the Local Government
Code, LGUs may enact ordinances to regulate the operation of drones in areas of local
jurisdiction, such as public parks, plazas, and heritage sites. Such ordinances shall be
supplementary to this Act and its IRR and must not contradict the national policies and
standards set by the CAAP and other national government agencies.

SECTION 15. Use for National Security and Law Enforcement. — Duly constituted
government agencies, including the Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) and the
Philippine National Police (PNP), may operate unmanned systems for legitimate national
security, intelligence, law enforcement, border control, and counter-terrorism operations.
The use of such systems, including the employment of counter-unmanned system
technology, shall be governed by their existing rules of engagement and protocols
established in the IRR. The PNP Air Group shall maintain and manage the national database
of all registered drones, including insurance status, and shall have the authority to verify
ownership, trace serial numbers, and conduct inspections to ensure compliance with
registration requirements, in the same manner and with the same powers exercised by the
PNP-HPG over motor vehicles and the PNP FEO over firearms.

a. Free Licensing and Registration for PNP Personnel. — Qualified personnel of
the Philippine National Police (PNP) authorized to operate unmanned systems for
official law enforcement, public safety, disaster response, intelligence, and related
police operations shall be exempt from payment of licensing. registration, and
certification fees imposed by the Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines
(CAAP), subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations.

b. Streamlined Processing of Special Permits for Police Operations. — The CAAP
shall establish a simplified and expedited procedure for the issuance of special
permits and flight clearances requested by the PNP for lawful law enforcement
operations, public safety activities, disaster response, search and rescue,
intelligence monitoring, and other police operations, especially during emergencies
and time-sensitive situations.

c. Training, Maintenance, and Capability Development. — The PNP shall establish
and maintain a continuing program for the training, certification, maintenance, and
operational readiness of unmanned systems and drone operators. Funding
necessary for the implementation of this program, including procurement,
maintenance, and capability enhancement, shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA).

d. Training Authority of the PNP Air Group - The Philippine National Police,
through the PNP Air Group, is hereby authorized to develop, conduct, and
administer Remotely Piloted Aircraft (RPA) training programs for PNP personnel
and personnel of other government agencies for official use. Such training
programs shall be eligible for recognition by the Civil Aviation Authority of the

===== [page 13] =====
Philippines (CAAP) upon compliance with the appropriate Certificate of
Authorization requirements issued by the CAAP or memorandum of agreement.
For this purpose, the CAAP shall prescribe separate, appropriate requirements and
standards distinct from those applicable to commercial training facilities, taking
into consideration that the training is intended exclusively for official government
use and not for commercial purposes.

SECTION 16. Use for Disaster and Emergency Response. — Government agencies such
as the NDRRMC, PHIVOLCS, BFP, NAMRIA, and PCG are authorized to deploy and
operate unmanned systems as a critical component of disaster risk assessment, monitoring,
search and rescue, and post-disaster recovery operations. They may apply for exemptions
from standard operating rules during a state of calamity, provided safety is ensured through
coordination with the IAC-US.

SECTION 17. Use for Research and Development. - The State shall encourage the use
of unmanned systems for scientific research and technological development. The
Department of Science and Technology (DOST), in coordination with the IAC-US, shall:

a. Establish designated special-use areas for safe testing and experimentation.

b. Develop a streamlined process for issuing experimental or research permits to
accredited academic and scientific institutions.

c. Promote partnerships between the government, academe, and industry to advance
local unmanned systems technology.

SECTION 18. Use of Drones for Agricultural Purposes. The use of drones for
agricultural activities is hereby recognized as a vital tool for enhancing food security and
modernizing the agricultural sector. Such use shall be governed by special permits,
certification, and enhanced liability insurance due to the nature of payloads and operations,
to include the following:

a. Authorization and Scope. The State shall support and regulate the use of
agricultural drones for purposes including, but not limited to:

1) Precision Dispensing in applying fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides, seeds, or
water.

2) Crop Management in conducting crop health monitoring, soil analysis, irrigation
assessment, and yield estimation through multispectral or thermal imaging.

3) Livestock Monitoring to oversee livestock location, health, and grazing patterns.

b. Special Permit and Operator Certification. The operation of a drone for
dispensing any chemical, biological, or agricultural substance shall require a Special
Agricultural UAS Permit from the Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines
(CAAP).

===== [page 14] =====
c. Operators must obtain a Special Agricultural Drone Operator Certificate in
addition to their Remote Pilot License. This certification requires specialized
training in the safe handling and application of agrochemicals, spray drift mitigation,
and the operation of drone-mounted dispersal systems, conducted by training
organizations accredited by the Department of Agriculture (DA) and the Fertilizer
and Pesticide Authority (FPA).

d. Operational Safety and Coordination. All agricultural drone operations involving
chemical dispensing must adhere to safety protocols established by the FPA and the
DA, which shall include:

1) Buffer Zones: Maintaining mandatory buffer zones from residential areas,
schools, bodies of water, and other sensitive areas to prevent contamination.

2) Public Notification: Providing prior notification to the local government unit or
barangay before conducting large-scale spraying operations.

3) Extended Visual Line of Sight (EVLOS): To facilitate operations over large
plantations, flight operations beyond the direct sight of the remote pilot may be
allowed, provided that trained visual observers are utilized to keep the drone in
sight and maintain communication with the pilot, subject to CAAP approval.

ARTICLE V: ENFORCEMENT, COUNTER-DRONE MEASURES, AND
DISPOSAL

SECTION 19. Enforcement Authority. The PNP and other deputized law enforcement
agencies are hereby authorized to enforce the provisions of this Act. They shall have the
authority, but not limited to:

a. Demand the presentation of the drone's registration, operator's license, and insurance
certificate during any inspection or stop;

b. Order the immediate landing and grounding of any drone believed to be operating
in violation of this Act, including where insurance has lapsed or is invalid;

c. Seize, confiscate, and impound any drone operated in violation of this Act or used
in the commission of a crime without prejudice to the handling and processing of
the drone and all data saved therein of evidentiary value.

d. Employ counter-drone technology in situations where a drone poses an imminent
threat to public safety, national security, or the security of a high-value installation
or event, subject to existing rules of engagement protocols and standard operational
procedures.

===== [page 15] =====
SECTION 20. Counter-Drone Measures. The Philippine National Police (PNP), the
Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP), and other authorized national security agencies are
hereby authorized to use reasonable and necessary force, including approved counter-drone
technologies (e.g., jammers, net guns, directed energy systems), to detect, identify, disable,
or neutralize or take control of any Unmanned System that poses an imminent threat to
public safety, critical infrastructure, national security, or the life and safety of persons on
the ground.

The deployment and use of such counter-drone measures shall be governed by strict
protocols to be established in the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), which shall
include safeguards to prevent interference with legitimate communication, aviation, and
public services.

SECTION 21. Disposal and Decommissioning Procedures. The following procedures
shall govern the voluntary surrender, turnover, custody, decommissioning, and final
disposition of Unmanned Systems, their frames, and components, consistent with due
process, accountability, and environmental protection:

a. Voluntary Turnover and Surrender:

1) Any owner or lawful possessor may voluntarily surrender an Unmanned
System or its frame bearing a serial number to the Philippine National Police
(PNP)/PNP Air Group, the Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines
(CAAP), or other authorized office for proper disposal, decommissioning, or
transfer. Surrender shall be acknowledged in writing through an official receipt
clearly stating the unit’s make, model, serial number, physical condition, and
the full name, address, and contact details of the surrendering party.

2) Voluntary surrender does not absolve the person from liability for prior
unlawful use of the unit, but shall be considered a mitigating circumstance in
the imposition of penalties.

3) All surrender and disposal procedures shall be conducted consistently with
Article III, Section 1 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution (protection against
deprivation of property without due process), applicable Commission on Audit
(COA) rules on government property custody, and environmental laws.

b. Custody and Inventory:

1) Upon voluntary turnover, surrender, or lawful seizure, the unit shall be

ARTICLE VI: PROHIBITED ACTS

SECTION 22. Prohibited Acts and Schedule of Penalties. Any natural or juridical person
found in violation of the provisions of this Act shall be subject to the following penalties.
The PNP and other deputized law enforcement agencies are hereby empowered to impose
the following administrative fines and sanctions for violations of its regulations
promulgated in accordance with this Act:

a. Any person operating an unregistered drone required to be registered, or for failure
to mark the drone with its registration number, shall be fined from Fifty Thousand
Pesos (P50,000) to One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000) and temporary
suspension of the operator's license. Including drones not registered with the PNP
Air Group as required.

b. Any person operating a drone without a valid Remote Pilot License shall be fined
One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000) to Two Hundred Thousand Pesos
(P200,000) and disqualified from applying for a license for a period of one (1) year.

c. Any person violating the General Operating Rules under Section 8 shall be fined
Twenty Thousand Pesos (P20,000) to Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000) per violation.

d. Any person who failed to comply with the accreditation requirements for importers,
manufacturers, and sellers shall be fined from Three Hundred Thousand Pesos
(P300,000) to One Million Pesos (P1,000,000) and suspension or revocation of the
entity's business permit and accreditation. Including failure to register units with the
PNP Drone Registry/PNP Air Group prior to sale, or failure to submit required serial
number and ownership records to the concerned PNP unit.

===== [page 18] =====
Operating without valid CTPL Insurance — Fine of P30,000 to P100,000;
suspension of registration and operator’s license until coverage is secured; and
mandatory attendance in insurance liability seminar.

Operating without required Comprehensive Insurance — Fine of Fifty Thousand
Pesos (P50,000) to One Hundred Fifty Thousand Pesos (P150,000); suspension of
registration until coverage is secured.

. Fraudulent or false proof of insurance — Fine of One Hundred Thousand Pesos
(P100,000) to Three Hundred Thousand Pesos (P300,000); revocation of registration
and license; and criminal prosecution under the Revised Penal Code for falsification.

. Any person who willfully operates a drone in a Prohibited Zone or in a manner that
violates the conditions of a Restricted Zone shall be punished by prisidn correccional
in its medium and maximum periods (2 years, 4 months, and 1 day to 6 years) and a
fine of Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (P200,000) to Five Hundred Thousand Pesos
(P500,000).

Any person who uses a drone to commit acts of surveillance or recording in violation
of Section 10 shall be punished by prision correccional in its minimum and medium
periods (6 months and 1 day to 4 years and 2 months) and a fine of One Hundred
Thousand Pesos (P100,000) to Three Hundred Thousand Pesos (P300,000). This is
without prejudice to any separate liability under Republic Act No. 10173 (Data
Privacy Act of 2012) or Republic Act No. 10175 (Cybercrime Prevention Act of
2015).

Weaponization of a drone - attachment of any firearm, explosive, chemical,
biological, radiological, or any other deadly weapon or hazardous material to a drone
shall be punished by reclusién temporal in its maximum period to reclusion perpetua
and a fine of One Million Pesos (P1,000,000) to Two Million Pesos (P2,000,000).

. Any person who uses a drone to intentionally interfere with the operations of law
enforcement officers, fire fighters, or other emergency responders shall be punished
by prisiOn correccional in its maximum period (4 years, 2 months, and 1 day to 6
years) and a fine of Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000).

Any person who uses drone to unlawfully gather, collect, record, or transmit
information, images, or electronic data from any military installation, critical
infrastructure, or government facility for purposes of espionage or to aid any foreign
power or entity hostile to the Republic of the Philippines shall be punished by
reclusion perpetua (20 years and 1 day to 40 years) and a fine of Five Million Pesos
(P5,000,000) to Ten Million Pesos (P 10,000,000).

. If the offender is a public officer or employee, the penalty of perpetual absolute
disqualification from public office shall be imposed in addition to the imprisonment
and fine.

n. If the offender is a foreign national, they shall be prosecuted accordingly and
deported immediately after serving the sentence without further proceedings.

SECTION 22-A. EXEMPTION FROM PENALTY FOR LAWFUL OFFICIAL USE.
The provisions of this Section prescribing penalties shall not apply to duly authorized
personnel of the PNP, AFP, PCG, and other law enforcement agencies when operating
unmanned systems in the lawful performance of their duties and responsibilities and
fulfillment of their functions for public safety and security.

Provided, That such operations are conducted in accordance with the conditions set forth in
Section 11-A and applicable agency rules and protocols.

SECTION 23. Use of a Drone as a Special Aggravating Circumstance. In any crime
punishable under the Revised Penal Code or any special penal law, the use of a drone in the
planning, commission, facilitation, or concealment of said crime shall be considered a
special aggravating circumstance. When this circumstance is present, the penalty to be
imposed shall be the maximum period of the penalty prescribed by law for the offense
committed.

ARTICLE VII: IMPLEMENTING RULES AND REGULATIONS
SECTION 24. Authority to Promulgate Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).
The Department of Transportation (DOTr), through the Civil Aviation Authority of the
Philippines (CAAP), as the lead implementing agency, is hereby mandated to formulate, in
public consultation with relevant stakeholders, the rules and regulations necessary for the
effective implementation of this Act.

The IRR shall be prepared in coordination with the following government agencies:
a. Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);

b. Department of National Defense (DND);

c. Department of Trade and Industry (DTI);

d. Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT);

e. Department of Agriculture (DA);

f. Department of Environment and Natural Resources DENR);

g. National Privacy Commission (NPC);

h. Philippine Nationa! Police (PNP);

i. Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP); and

===== [page 20] =====
j. Other agencies as may be deemed necessary.

SECTION 25. Scope and Content of the IRR. The IRR shall be promulgated within ninety
(90) days from the effectivity of this Act and shall provide detailed procedures and standards
for the following matters:

a.

Registration and Marking: The procedure for drone registration, renewal, transfer
of ownership, de-registration, fees, and the specific requirements for displaying
registration markings. Including the procedures, forms, fees, and database
management for registration with the PNP Drone Registry/PNP Air Group, aligned
with the systems used for motor vehicles and firearms;

Insurance: CTPL and Comprehensive Insurance coverage limits, schedules of
premiums, eligible insurers, claim procedures, and evidence of loss reporting
protocols, coordinated with the Insurance Commission;

Licensing and Training: The curriculum, standards, and procedures for the
aeronautical knowledge examination, practical skills assessment, and issuance of
Remote Pilot Licenses for various categories and classes of operations. It shall also
establish standards for accredited drone pilot training organizations.

Airspace and Flight Rules: The precise definition and publication of Prohibited
and Restricted Zones, and the process for establishing Temporary Flight Restrictions
(TFRs) for emergencies or special events.

Special Operations Permits: The application process and safety requirements for
Class 3 (High-Risk) operations, including Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS),
nighttime operations, and drone delivery services.

Law Enforcement Protocols: The rules of engagement and standard operating
procedures for the PNP and other law enforcement agencies in the inspection,
seizure, and disposition of drones, as well as the authorized use of counter-drone
technologies.

Governmental Use Protocols: The specific guidelines for drone use by the BFP,
PNP, AFP, and other government agencies, including inter-agency coordination
during joint operations.

Privacy and Data Protection: The specific guidelines to ensure compliance with
the Data Privacy Act, particularly for drones equipped with advanced surveillance
and data-capturing technologies.

Accreditation and Sales Monitoring: The detailed requirements and procedures
for the accreditation of drone importers, manufacturers, and sellers, and the
mechanism for reporting sales data.

===== [page 21] =====
j. Fines and Penalties: A detailed schedule of administrative fines and a clear
procedure for the adjudication of administrative offenses.

SECTION 26. Retroactivity and Transitional Provision. All drone owners who have not
yet registered their drones or obtained permits to operate their drones are given six (6)
months from the effectivity of the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of this Act to
register, obtain licenses or permits, and secure required insurance coverage without fear of
sanction. This Act shall otherwise have retroactive effect only to the extent that it does not
prejudice vested rights.

SECTION 27. Separability Clause. If for any reason, any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, such parts not affected thereby shall remain in full force and
effect.

SECTION 28. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions
of this Act shall are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 29. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation in the
Philippines.
