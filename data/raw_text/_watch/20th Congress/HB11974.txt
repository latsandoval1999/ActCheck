EXPLANATORY NOTE

From 1971! to 1982, the State exacted levies upon every kilo of copra sold by coconut
farmers and invested the collections in, among others, a bank, oil mills, and corporate
shareholdings. In a series of final and executory decisions, notably Republic v. COCOFED
(2001) and COCOFED v. Republic (2012), the Supreme Court settled the character of these
assets: the coconut levy funds are public funds impressed with a trust, and the assets acquired
therewith shall be used only for the benefit of the coconut farmers and for the development of
the coconut industry.

Pursuant to this doctrine, Congress enacted Republic Act No. 11524, which created the
Coconut Farmers and Industry Trust Fund and directed that all coconut levy assets, including
the six oil mill companies comprising the CIIF Oil Mills Group (CIIF-OMG), be sold within
five (5) years from the law’s effectivity, with the proceeds accruing to the Trust Fund. The satd
period lapsed in March 2026 without any disposition having been completed, and the forced
sale of the mills — several of which are non-operating — into a limited market of prospective
buyers would in all likelihood yield depressed proceeds for the Trust Fund, displace workers
in coconut-producing regions, and dismantle the State’s only direct instrument for supporting
farmgate copra prices.

It bears emphasis that the CIIF-OMG is the sole government-owned or -controlled
corporation engaged in coconut milling and export, that approximately eighty percent (80%)
of its outpul is sold to foreign buyers, and that its nationwide network of copra buying stations
accounts for an estimated twenty-five to thirty percent (25%~30%) of national copra purchases.
In March 2019, when mill-gate copra prices collapsed to as low as twelve pesos (P12) per kilo
in certain areas, the Government, acting through the CLIF-OMG, established a buying floor of
twenty pesos (P20) per kilo, thereby arresting the decline in farmgate prices for the benefit of
coconut farmers. The CIIF-OMG has, morcover, sustained its operations without any
appropriation from the National Government, financing its requirements through self-
liquidating working capital.

The President, in his 2025 State of the Nation Address, called upon Congress to amend
Republic Act No. 11524, and the measure has since been included as a priority in the Common
Legislative Agenda of the Legislative-Executive Development Advisory Council for the
Twentieth Congress. In the public hearing conducted by the Senale Committee on Agriculture,

Food and Agrarian Reform in May 2026, the reconsideration of the mandatory disposition of
coconut levy assets was identified as among the consensus amendments, with farmer
organizations and the Presidential Commission on Good Government expressing support for
the retention of the oi] mills.

This bill therefore seeks to re-charter the Coconut Industry Investment Fund Oil Mills
Group as an independent government-owned or -controlled corporation with an original
charter, pursuant to Article XII, Section 16 of the 1987 Constitution, under which the six oil
mill companies shall operate as wholly-owned subsidiaries. The bill likewise provides for: (a)
a nine-member Board of Directors, all appointed by the President of the Philippines under
Statutory qualifications and fixed, staggered terms of office, three (3) seats of which are
reserved tor coconut farmer representatives from T.uzon, the Visayas, and Mindanao: (b) a
commercial operations regime, with appropriate safeguards, governing the purchase of copra
at prevailing market prices and the sale of the Corporation’s products, including export sales;
(c) the remittance to the Trust Fund of at least fifty percent (50%) of the Corporation’s annual
net earnings: and (d) the continued audit jurisdiction of the Commission on Audit and the
supervision of the Governance Commission for Government-Owned or -Controlled
Corporations.

In view of the foregoing considerations, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

significantly below the prevailing regional market prices, the Corporation shall act as

SECTION 11. Funding. — The Corporation shall be self-liquidating and shall finance
its operations, rehabilitation, and expansion from its own revenues, borrowings, and internally
generated funds. Consistent with the established operating practice of the CIIF-Oi! Mills
Group, the working-capital requirements of the Corporation shall be financed through
revolving, self-liquidating facilities: short-term loans, credit tines, export packing credits, and
similar working-capital accommodations contracted under Section 16-D(e) of Republic Act
No. 11524, as amended, to fund copra purchases and mill operations, repaid from the proceeds
of sales, and renewed against succeeding operating cycles. No appropriation fram the National
Treasury or under the General Appropriations Act shall be required, made, or received for the
operations, working capital, or capitalization of the Corporation: Provided, That, as an
investment of the Trust Fund in its wholly-owned Corporation and not as a grant or subsidy,
the Coconut Farmers and Industry Trust Fund may, upon request of the Board and with the
approval of the Trust Fund Management Committee, subscribe to additional shares of the
Corporation or extend an initial seed facility or standby credit line as provided in Section 16-
C of Republic Act No. 11524, as amended. The Trust Fund Management Expense authorized
under Section 10 of Republic Act No. 11524 shall continue to be sourced from the Trust Fund
as provided therein, and shall not be charged against the operations of the Corporation, except
for the cost of Board-approved services actually rendered by the Corporation for the Trust
Fund.

unconstitutional or invalid, all other provisions not affected thereby shall remain valid.

and rules and regulations or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act, including the

pertinent provisions of Republic Act No. 11524, except as otherwise herein amended, are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
