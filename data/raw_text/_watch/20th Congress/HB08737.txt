                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

         Article II, Sec. 23 and Art. XIII, Sec. 15 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution affirm the
people’s right to participate in governance. These provisions recognize the vital role of Civil Society
Organizations (CSOs), People’s Organizations (POs), and non-governmental organizations (NGOs)
as partners of the State in nation-building. They also affirm that democratic governance flourishes
when the voice of citizens is heard, when their organizations are allowed to grow, and when local
institutions open their doors to genuine participation.

        In practice, however, citizen participation has often been limited to consultation without
clear structures for sustained engagement. Mechanisms for organized involvement remain
fragmented, and partnerships between local government units (LGUs) and civil society are not
always institutionalized. This gap weakens the capacity of communities to contribute to policy-
making and oversight in matters that directly affect them.

        This proposed measure seeks to bridge that gap. It establishes a framework for the
registration and accreditation of CSOs and Pos at the local level, ensuring that their presence is both
recognized and safeguarded. It grants them the rights and privileges necessary to take part
meaningfully in governance – whether in shaping policies, implementing programs, or monitoring
projects.
        At the heart of this bill is the creation of Local People’s Councils, bodies designed to give
organized citizens a permanent seat at the table of local governance. By amending the composition
of local boards, councils, and committees, the measure secures representation for People’s
Organizations and strengthens the system of checks and balances at the local level.

       This bill also places safeguards against partisanship. It ensures that People’s Councils
remain independent from political influence, thereby preserving their integrity and keeping their
focus on the welfare of communities rather than the interest of parties or candidates.

        Through this Act, citizen participation will no longer be confined to symbolic consultation
but will be woven into the very fabric of local governance. It will strengthen accountability, enhance
transparency, and foster inclusive development – so that governance is not merely exercised for the
people, but carried out with them

         AN ACT CREATING A SYSTEM OF PARTNERSHIP BETWEEN LOCAL
        GOVERNMENTS AND CIVIL SOCIETY ORGANIZATIONS THROUGH THE
           ESTABLISHMENT OF A PEOPLE’S COUNCIL IN EVERY LOCAL
          GOVERNMENT UNIT, PRESCRIBING ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

1           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “People Empowerment Act.”

 2           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to ensure participation of
 3   citizens in all avenues of local governance. Likewise, the State acknowledges the value of creating
 4   a mechanism where its citizen’s views and opinions in different areas of governance may be heard
 5   and considered. Furthermore, pursuant to Article II, Section 23 of the Constitution, the State
 6   recognizes the value of non-governmental, community-based, or sectoral organizations in nation
 7   building and creating an avenue to ensure their growth and development. The State also recognizes
 8   that partnerships with People’s Organizations and Civil Society Organizations will spur
 9   development in local government units (LGUs) as envisioned by the Local Government Code of
10   1991.

11          SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

12               a) Civil Society Organization or CSO - refers to any organized citizens’ group
13                  including People’s Organizations and Non-Government Organizations (NGOs)
14                  such as community groups, labor unions, indigenous groups, charitable
15                  organizations, faith-based organizations, professional associations, and
16                  foundations;
17               b) Local Sanggunian - refers to the applicable local legislative body, specifically
18                  either the Sangguniang Panglungsod for cities or the Sangguniang Bayan for
19                  municipalities;
20               c) People’s Organization or PO refers to a bona fide cooperative or association of
21                  citizens organized for purposes not contrary to law with demonstrated capacity to
22                  promote the public interest and with identifiable leadership, membership, and

1                    structure with none of its officers and members occupying elective government
2                    positions.

3            SEC. 4. Registration of CSOs. – Any CSO in active operation for at least one year may be
4            registered in their respective city or municipality where they conduct their operations and
5            where majority of their members reside. Registration will be approved by the Local
6            Sanggunian, upon submission or compliance of the following requirements:

 7               a) Proof of existence and operation in the city or municipality they are applying in for
 8                  at least one year prior to filing the application for registration;
 9               b) Proof of activities held in pursuit of development objectives or of organizational
10                  activities concluded;
11               c) Program of activities planned for one year following the date of application for
12                  registration;
13               d) Copies of its constitution, by-laws and/or articles of incorporation;
14               e) Lists of its officers and members in good standing and their respective addresses;
15               f) Financial statements and declaration to register and participate under the Act.

16               A CSO whose application for registration has been approved shall be issued a certificate
17               of registration containing, among others, the terms and conditions for the maintenance
18               of its registered status.

19               The Local Sanggunian, however, may from time to time, and in consultation with the
20               Local People’s Council, impose such other requirements and conditions for registration
21               as it may deem appropriate to best adhere to the principles of this Act.

22               Any CSO already accredited by the LGU prior to the effectivity of this Act need not
23               register for purposes of this Act, unless its accreditation has in the meantime been
24               withdrawn.

25           SEC. 5. Registration Deemed Inactive. – A registered CSO is required to submit, by
26   January 31 of every year, a list of accomplishments of the previous year and its plans for the current
27   year. Any CSO who fails to comply for two (2) consecutive years, shall automatically have its
28   registration deemed inactive.

29           Any CSO whose registration has been deemed inactive may not avail of the rights and
30   privileges of registered CSOs contained in Section 7 of this Act. A CSO may reactivate its
31   registration upon submission of the documents it lacks.

32          SEC. 6. Committee on Registration of CSOs. – There shall be a Committee on Registration
33   of CSOs in each Local Sanggunian of all cities and municipalities. It is mandated to perform the
34   following functions:

35               a) Receive and process applications for registration;
36               b) Monitor compliance with the conditions for registration; and
37               c) Initiate legislative measures for the effective performance of its tasks.

3           SEC. 7. Rights and Privileges of Registered CSOs. – Registered CSOs may enjoy the
4    following rights and privileges:

 5               a) Enter into joint ventures or other forms of undertaking with Local Sanggunian to
 6                  engage in the delivery of certain basic services, capacity-building, and livelihood
 7                  projects;
 8               b) Develop local enterprises designed to improve productivity and income, diversity
 9                  agriculture, spur rural industrialization, promote ecological balance, and enhance
10                  the economic and social well-being of the people within the framework of equitable
11                  and sustainable development;
12               c) Receive assistance from the Local Sanggunian for economic, socially-oriented,
13                  environmental, good governance, or cultural projects to be implemented within
14                  their territorial jurisdiction;
15               d) Join and become part of the Local People’s Council.

17           SEC. 8. Creation of Local People’s Councils. – A Local People’s Council, composed of
18   accredited CSOs, shall be created. For its purpose, the Committee on Registration of each Local
19   Sanggunian shall, not later than ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, call for a
20   convention of all accredited CSOs to facilitate the formal organization of the Local People’s
21   Council. Thereafter, such group shall file a petition for recognition with the Local Sanggunian.

22           A Local People’s Council shall be formally created upon approval by the Local Sanggunian
23   of the petition for recognition.

24           SEC. 9. Structure and Rules of the Local People’s Council. – Each Local People’s
25   Council shall determine its own organizational structures and internal rules, but shall at all times
26   provide for adequate consultation mechanisms for purposes of obtaining the views and suggestions
27   of all political parties or movements, government employees’ organizations, other non-accredited
28   but legitimate CSOs, and accredited CSOs which are not members of the Council; Provided, that
29   no political part, government employees’ organization or non-registered CSOs may be nominated
30   by the Council for membership in the LGU’s special bodies nor may they be granted the rights and
31   privileges of accredited CSOs under this Act.

32           SEC. 10. Recognition of the Local People’s Council by the Local Sanggunian. – Only
33   one Local People’s Council shall be recognized by each Local Sanggunian. The Local Sanggunian
34   is mandated to award recognition to a People’s Council within thirty (30) days from the filing of a
35   petition for recognition.

36            SEC. 11. Withdrawing Recognition of the Local People’s Council. – The Local
37   Sanggunian may withdraw the recognition granted to the Local People’s Council only upon a
38   verified petition and based on the following grounds: (a) that the Local People’s Council has
39   allowed itself or any of its member organizations to engage primarily in, or to be used primarily
40   for, partisan political activities; and (b) such other grounds as may be provided in the Implementing


                                                                                                        3
1    Rules and Regulations of this Act. The Local Sanggunian shall have no jurisdiction over internal
2    and/or inter-organizational conflicts within the Local People’s Council.

3           SEC. 12. Powers and Responsibilities of the Local People’s Council. – Upon recognition,
4    the Local People’s Council may, in accordance with its policies and internal rules, exercise the
5    following powers and responsibilities:

 6                a) Elect or appoint, from among its member organizations, its representatives to all
 7                   LGU bodies, boards, councils, committees, task forces special government bodies
 8                   and other similar work groups which the laws may hereinafter create. Member
 9                   organizations elected or appointed to represent the Local People’s Council shall
10                   have the sole prerogative to choose from among their bona fide members, the
11                   persons who shall sit in the boards, councils, committees, task forces and/or special
12                   bodies concerned. Unless otherwise provided herein, such representatives shall not
13                   exceed twenty-five (25) percent of the membership of the board, council,
14                   committee, task force or special body;
15                b) Participation of the Local People’s Council or any of its member organization in
16                   the conception, implementation and evaluation of government activities and
17                   functions shall be without compensation or remuneration. The LGU, however,
18                   shall provide for the necessary office spaces, facilities and/or equipment for said
19                   participation of the Council;
20                c) Representatives of the Local People’s Council may observe, vote and participate
21                   in the deliberation, conceptualization, implementation and evaluation of projects,
22                   activities and programs of the LGU, propose legislations and participate and vote
23                   at the committee level of the Local Sanggunian;
24                d) The Local People’s Council shall also elect a representative for the provincial
25                   level.

26           SEC. 13. Creation of a Provincial People’s Council. – A Provincial People’s Council shall
27   be created and recognized by the Sangguniang Panlalawigan. The Provincial People’s Council shall
28   be composed of all representatives to the provincial level from the Local People’s Councils within
29   the province’s jurisdiction.

30            The Provincial People’s Council shall elect, from within its ranks, a representative who will
31   sit on the Sanggunian Panlalawigan and shall exercise the duties and powers of a Provincial Board
32   member.

33           SEC. 14. Non-Partisan Nature of the Local and Provincial People’s Council. – The Local
34   or Provincial People’s Council shall not engage in, or allow itself or its member organizations to
35   be used for purposes of partisan politics and shall adopt such measures to ensure that it is adequately
36   shielded from any political partisanship or influence.

37           For purposes of this Act, partisan politics shall refer to any activity statement or
38   manifestation which solely or primarily serves to campaign for or against any particular political
39   party or any candidates for any elective public office.

1             SEC. 15. Amendment of the Composition of LGUs. – The existing composition of the
2    committees, boards, councils, task forces, special bodies of all LGUs are hereby amended and
3    modified to accommodate the membership and participation therein of the Local People’s Council
4    or its representatives as herein mandated.

 5           SEC. 16. Right to Self-Organization. – Pursuant to Article XIII, Section 15 of the
 6   Constitution, CSOs are recognized as independent and autonomous self-help organizations. They
 7   are encouraged to organize into formal cooperatives, labor union, interest groups, non-government
 8   organizations, sectoral organizations and/or people’s organization in furtherance of their
 9   constitutionally mandated role of enabling the people to pursue and protect, within the democratic
10   framework, their legitimate and collective interests and aspirations through peaceful and lawful
11   means.

12         Furthermore, the Department of Local and Interior Governance (DILG) and LGUs are
13   mandated to assist, and support efforts of the people towards self-organization to address their
14   common concerns, to promote their common welfare, and/or to serve their localities or their
15   communities and interests

16           SEC. 17. Funding. – The funds needed for the capacity building of CSOs shall be included
17   in the annual budget of the DILG. The funds needed for the capacity building of CSOs shall be
18   included in the annual budget of the DILG. The funds necessary for the maintenance and
19   operational expenses of Local People’s Council, in relation to participation in local governance,
20   shall be included in the annual budgets of respective LGUs.

21           SEC. 18. Information Boards and Suggestion Boxes. – Information boards and suggestion
22   boxes shall be provided, maintained and controlled by the LGU in each barangay, at the public
23   plazas, city hall compound, public markets, schools, government offices, and at such public places
24   accessible to the people, which boards and boxes shall be other than those maintained by the
25   barangays and the national government, and shall be controlled solely by the LGU.

26           LGUs are mandated to respond within fifteen (15) working days to the suggestions filed by
27   citizens filed by citizens in suggestion boxes.

28          SEC. 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred twenty (120) days
29   from the effectivity of this Act, the DILG, LGUs, in consultation with CSOs that have experience
30   working with LGUs, shall determine effective ways and methods for the implementation of this
31   Act and craft the implementing rules and regulations of this Act.

32           SEC. 20. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
33   unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such declaration shall remain in full force and
34   effect.

35          SEC. 21. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, administrative orders, and rules
36   and regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

37           SEC. 22. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
38   publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
