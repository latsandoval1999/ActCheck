                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Constitution mandates that "Congress shall give highest priority to
the enactment of measures that protect and enhance the right of all the people to
human dignity, reduce social, economic, and political inequalities, and remove cultural
inequities by equitably diffusing wealth and political power for the common good”
(Article XIII, Section 11).

        In line with the 1987 Constitutional directive to promote a just and dynamic
social order and prioritize the needs of the underprivileged, the State is mandated to
ensure that social services are not only available but accessible to all. The hallmark of
an effective government is its ability to respond to these needs with both speed and
compassion, ensuring that the dignity of every citizen is upheld during times of crisis.
When the bureaucracy acts as a bridge rather than a barrier, it fulfills its primary duty
of serving the people with utmost efficiency and social justice. Currently, the
Philippines’ primary social protection programs, including the Pantawid Pamilyang
Pilipino     Program      (4Ps),    the    Tulong     Panghanapbuhay          sa    Ating
Disadvantaged/Displaced Workers (TUPAD), and the Assistance to Individuals in Crisis
Situations (AICS), serve as the backbone of the national safety net. The success of
these programs is reflected in Social Weather Stations (SWS) data, showing that a
robust 80% to 90% of Filipinos find these programs helpful. However, a significant
disconnect persists between the availability of these funds and the ease with which a
citizen in crisis can access them. For many, the process of seeking these social
protection assistance is plagued with bureaucratic red tape by requiring excessive
documentary demands, and grueling long queues and waiting time. These
administrative inefficiencies disproportionately burden the poor and marginalized, who
often lack the resources or time to navigate a fragmented bureaucracy while dealing
with urgent personal emergencies.

       This bill seeks to institutionalize a streamlined and standardized system for
accessing government financial aid. By establishing a clear, minimum set of
requirements, limited to essential documents like a valid government ID, a billing
statement, and a social worker referral, this measure aims to eliminate unnecessary
hurdles. The goal is to ensure that in times of grief or illness, the State acts as a helper
rather than a gatekeeper, delivering aid in the easiest and fastest way possible.

       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall                         be    known      as   the
 2   “Direkta at Agad na Lingap at Interbensiyon (DALI) Act.”
 3
 4        Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State shall promote a just and dynamic social
 5   order and to prioritize the needs of the underprivileged, sick, elderly, disabled, women, and
 6   children, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote the general welfare of the
 7   Filipino people by ensuring that individuals and families in difficult situations are provided
 8   with timely, dignified, and accessible financial assistance. To this end, the State shall adopt
 9   proactive measures to eliminate excessive documentary requirements and institutionalize
10   streamlined, standardized procedures for availing aid from all government agencies.
11
12       Sec. 3. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all national and local
13   government agencies, government-owned or -controlled corporations (GOCCs),
14   government financial institutions (GFIs), and other instrumentalities—including their
15   respective social responsibility arms and corporate social responsibility (CSR) programs—
16   that provide medical, burial, and similar forms of financial assistance. Including but not
17   limited to the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Department of
18   Health (DOH), Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO), and Philippine Insurance
19   Corporation (PhilHealth).
20
21      Sec. 4. Standardized Requirements. – To avail of immediate financial assistance,
22   beneficiaries shall be required to submit only the following documents:
23              a. Valid government-issued identification (ID);
24              b. Billing statement from the hospital, clinic, funeral parlor or related entities;
25          and
26              c. Original or certified true copy of a medical certificate or medical abstract with
27          the date of issuance, complete name, signature and license number of the attending

                                                   1
 1          physician, or death certificate issued by the hospital or the city/municipal health
 2          office, whichever is applicable. Hospitals, clinics and other authorized medical
 3          facilities issuing such medical certificates or medical abstracts shall, in coordination
 4          with the DOH, ensure that the foregoing information is clearly indicated therein.
 5
 6              Where the request for assistance is made through a representative, such
 7          representative shall present an authorization duly signed by the beneficiary, or where
 8          the beneficiary is unable to sign due to age, illness, disability, or illiteracy, such other
 9          reasonable proof of authorization, along with a valid government-issued ID of both
10          the beneficiary and the representative. The authorized representative must be a
11          relative of the beneficiary within the fourth degree of consanguinity or affinity, or
12          legal guardian: Provided, That no individual shall act as a representative for more
13          than two (2) beneficiaries.
14
15            Sec. 5. Digital Request for Government Aid. – The Department of Information
16   and Communications Technology (DICT) shall be primarily tasked to develop and use
17   the eGov PH Super App or other appropriate digital systems or platforms as a mechanism
18   for Filipinos to avail aid from government agencies. The online or mobile application shall
19   be utilized to facilitate requests for medical, burial, and similar forms of
20   financial assistance.
21
22          Sec. 6. Streamlined Processing. – All concerned government agencies shall
23   process requests for assistance within the day, subject to availability of funds:
24   Provided, That complete documentary requirements are submitted during official working
25   hours. Requests submitted on weekends, holidays or outside official working hours shall be
26   processed on the next working day.
27
28           Sec. 7. Coordination with the Anti-Red Tape Authority. – The DSWD and all
29   implementing agencies shall coordinate with the Anti-Red Tape Authority (ARTA) to ensure
30   full compliance with Republic Act No. 9485, as amended by Republic Act No. 11032 or the
31   “Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018”. They shall
32   likewise work with ARTA to address complaints involving undue delays, excessive
33   documentary requirements, or other forms of non-compliance with the provisions of this
34   Act.
35        Sec. 8. Separability Clause. If any of the sections or provisions of this Act is held
36   invalid, all other provisions not affected thereby shall remain valid.
37
38       Sec. 9. Repealing Clause. All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, letters
39   of instructions, proclamations, and administrative regulations that are inconsistent with
40   the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.
41
42       Sec. 10. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect immediately following the completion
43   of its publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
