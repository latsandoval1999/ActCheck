                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The protection and promotion of public health is a fundamental duty of the
State. “The State shall protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill
health consciousness among them.1” Consistent with this constitutional mandate, the
State has adopted taxation policies that discourage the consumption of harmful
products such as tobacco and alcohol, while generating revenues to support health
programs and services. Over the past decade, landmark reforms2 have strengthened
the country’s fiscal capacity to finance health services and have supported the
implementation the Universal Health Care (UHC) Act.

       Notwithstanding these reforms, significant public health challenges persist.
Tobacco use and harmful alcohol consumption continue to impose a heavy burden on
the country’s health system, contributing substantially to preventable morbidity and
mortality. In the Philippines, tobacco use alone is estimated to cause more than 112,000
deaths annually, accounting for approximately 17.5% of total deaths nationwide and
more than one-fifth of deaths from non-communicable diseases (NCDs) such as
cardiovascular diseases, cancer, and chronic respiratory illnesses. When combined
with the harmful use of alcohol and emerging nicotine products such as vaping, these
risk factors contribute to approximately 115,000 preventable deaths each year and
result in the loss of an estimated 3.9 million years of healthy life among Filipinos.3
These conditions continue to drive the growing prevalence of non-communicable
diseases, including liver disease and other chronic illnesses, placing sustained
pressure on the country’s public health system.

        Given the magnitude of these health and economic burdens, effective policy
interventions are necessary to reduce the consumption of these harmful products and
mitigate their impact on public health. Excise taxation remains among the most
effective policy instruments for reducing harmful consumption while generating
public revenues. By increasing retail prices, higher excise taxes discourage the use of
tobacco and alcohol—particularly among youth and other price-sensitive
consumers—while providing a sustainable funding stream for health system
strengthening. Evidence from the Philippines demonstrates the effectiveness of this
approach: following the enactment of the 2012 Sin Tax Reform Law, cigarette sales
volume declined by approximately 23% between 2012 and 2017 as a result of higher
prices, accompanied by a corresponding reduction in tobacco and alcohol
consumption.4

       This bill seeks to further restructure and increase excise tax rates on alcohol and
tobacco products under the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended,
while providing annual adjustments to preserve the real value of health taxes. The
measure aims to discourage harmful consumption and generate sustainable revenues
for public health priorities. It also introduces an additional revenue set-aside under
Section 288-A to support the implementation of Universal Health Care, strengthen the
health workforce through scholarships, training, compensation support, and
incentives for service in underserved areas, expand health promotion and disease
prevention programs, and assist tobacco farmers in transitioning to alternative
livelihoods. Moreover, the bill strengthens government efforts to combat illicit trade
in excisable products by mandating an integrated system that includes digital tax
stamps, electronic track-and-trace mechanisms, and automated production
monitoring. These reforms seek to sustain the gains of prior health tax reforms,
improve tax administration, and ensure the long-term sustainability of Universal
Health Care.

          In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

       SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.
Consistent with Article II, Section 15 of the Constitution, the State shall adopt effective
measures to reduce the consumption of tobacco and alcohol products through taxation
policies that discourage harmful consumption, generate sustainable revenues for
universal health care, and support health workforce development.

      SEC. 8. Anti-Illicit Trade Measures. — The Secretary of Finance, upon
recommendation of the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, shall establish and
implement an integrated anti-illicit trade system for excisable alcohol products and
tobacco products, including heated tobacco products and vapor products, which shall
include, at a minimum:

          (a) digital tax stamps or unique identification markings;

          (b) an electronic track-and-trace system from manufacture or importation
              to removal and distribution; and

          (c) automated production volume monitoring for manufacturers and
              importers.

       The Secretary of Finance shall, upon recommendation of the Commissioner of
Internal Revenue, promulgate the implementing rules and regulations for this Section
within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act. The
implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe uniform technical and security
standards, registration or accreditation requirements, data reporting and retention
rules, audit access protocols, interoperability with Bureau of Internal Revenue
systems, and transition periods: Provided, That transition periods shall not exceed
twelve (12) months from the effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations,
except for good cause shown.

        Noncompliance with this Section and its implementing rules and regulations
shall be subject to the applicable civil, administrative, and criminal penalties under
the National Internal Revenue Code and other applicable laws.
The Secretary of Finance and the Commissioner of Internal Revenue shall submit to
the Joint Congressional Oversight Committee on Illicit Trade on Excisable Products
created under Section 290-A of this Code semi-annual reports on the implementation
of this Section.
