                              AN ACT
EXEMPTING MICRO AND SMALL BUSINESSES FROM THE MINIMUM CORPORATE
INCOME TAX, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE SECTION 27 OF THE NATIONAL
INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Micro and small businesses are the backbone of the Philippine economy. They create
jobs, provide livelihoods, support local communities, and encourage
entrepreneurship. However, many of these businesses operate with limited capital
and small pro it margins, making them more vulnerable to economic downturns,
rising costs, and unexpected business losses. During dif icult times, every peso
matters, and unnecessary tax burdens can make it even harder for them to survive
and grow.

Under existing tax laws, corporations may be required to pay the Minimum Corporate
Income Tax (MCIT) beginning on their fourth year of operations if it is higher than
their regular income tax. Unlike the regular corporate income tax, which is based on
taxable income or actual pro it, the MCIT is based on gross income. As a result, even
businesses that earn very little or are struggling inancially may still be required to
pay the MCIT, placing additional pressure on their cash low and limiting their ability
to recover and continue operating.

This bill seeks to exempt quali ied micro and small businesses from paying the MCIT.
The exemption is limited only to businesses that fall within the classi ications set by
law and recognized by the Bureau of Internal Revenue. It does not remove their
obligation to register with the BIR, ile tax returns, keep proper books of accounts, or

pay the regular corporate income tax and all other applicable national and local taxes.
Instead, it simply ensures that small businesses are not required to pay a minimum
tax despite having little or no taxable income.

By allowing micro and small businesses to retain more of their working capital, this
measure will help them sustain operations, protect jobs, and invest in future growth.
It promotes a fairer tax system by recognizing the inancial realities faced by smaller
enterprises while maintaining their responsibility to comply with tax laws. In view of
the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Micro and Small Business
MCIT Exemption Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote the
growth, sustainability, and competitiveness of micro and small businesses, which play
an important role in employment generation, local economic development, and
inclusive growth.

The State recognizes that the imposition of the minimum corporate income tax may
impose an undue burden on micro and small businesses, particularly during periods
of low pro itability, business reverses, or economic disruption. Accordingly, the State
shall provide a targeted exemption from the minimum corporate income tax while
maintaining the obligation of quali ied businesses to register, keep proper books and
records, ile tax returns, and pay all other taxes imposed by law.

SECTION 3. Section 27(E) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as
amended, is hereby further amended to read as follows:

SECTION 4. Coverage of the Exemption. —

The exemption under this Act shall apply to domestic corporations that qualify as
micro or small businesses under Section 21(B) of the National Internal Revenue Code
of 1997, as amended, and other applicable laws and regulations.

The exemption shall apply beginning with the taxable year following the effectivity of
this Act. A quali ied corporation shall remain exempt for as long as it satis ies the
applicable classi ication requirements during the taxable year in which the exemption
is claimed.

A corporation that no longer quali ies as a micro or small business shall be subject to
the minimum corporate income tax beginning with the taxable year in which it ceases
to qualify, subject to the applicable provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code
of 1997, as amended.

SECTION 5. Tax Compliance Not Excused. —

The exemption granted under this Act shall not exempt a quali ied corporation from:
   1. Registration with the Bureau of Internal Revenue;
   2. The iling of income tax returns and other required tax returns;
   3. The maintenance of books of accounts and other required records;
   4. The payment of regular corporate income tax, when applicable; and
   5. The payment of all other national and local taxes, fees, and charges imposed
      by law.

SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Finance, upon the
recommendation of the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, shall promulgate the rules
and regulations necessary for its effective implementation.

The implementing rules and regulations shall, among others, prescribe:
   1. The documentary and reporting requirements for claiming the exemption;
   2. The manner of determining whether a corporation quali ies as a micro or small
      business;
   3. The treatment of corporations that exceed the applicable classi ication
      thresholds during the taxable year;
   4. The procedures for monitoring compliance; and
   5. Safeguards against the improper splitting, merger, or restructuring of
      businesses solely to obtain or retain the exemption.

The implementing rules and regulations shall not impose conditions that are
inconsistent with the exemption granted under this Act.

SECTION 7. Non-Diminution of Existing Bene its. — Nothing in this Act shall be
construed as diminishing or impairing any exemption, incentive, privilege, or relief
granted under the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, or under any
other law, unless expressly provided by law.

SECTION 8. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 10. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
