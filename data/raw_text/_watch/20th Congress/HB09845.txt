                       AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE HIGHER EDUCATION-INDUSTRY
PARTNERSHIP FRAMEWORK, CREATING FOR THE PURPOSE THE
TRIPARTITE COUNCIL FOR TERTIARY EDUCATION, AND
STRENGTHENING STRUCTURED COLLABORATION BETWEEN
HIGHER EDUCATION AND INDUSTRY FOR WORKFORCE
RELEVANCE, INNOVATION, AND NATIONAL DEVELOPMENT

                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive and coherent framework
for sustained collaboration between higher education institutions and industry in
the Philippines, in recognition of their shared responsibility in developing a
skilled, adaptive, and competitive Filipino workforce.

       Despite existing policies and programs on education, training,
qualifications, and workforce development, persistent challenges remain,
including graduate unemployment and underemployment, job–skills mismatch,
uneven regional development, and weak linkages between learning, research,
innovation, and productive economic activity. These challenges underscore the
need for a more systematic, structured, and institutionalized mechanism to align
higher education outcomes with labor market needs and national development
priorities.
       The proposed measure establishes the Higher Education-Industry
Partnership Framework (IHEPF) as a national coordination and governance
architecture to guide regular engagement, joint planning, labor market and skills
intelligence exchange, and shared accountability among higher education
institutions, industry partners, and relevant government agencies. The
Framework is expressly designed to function as a coordination and facilitation
mechanism only and does not confer regulatory, supervisory, or enforcement
powers, nor does it interfere with academic freedom, institutional autonomy, or
existing quality assurance and accreditation systems.

       To support the implementation and continuous refinement of the
Framework, the bill creates a Tripartite Council for Tertiary Education (TCTE)
composed of representatives from government, higher education, and industry.
The Council shall serve as the principal national advisory, coordinating, and
intelligence body, tasked with monitoring labor market and skills trends,
generating and analyzing workforce and innovation intelligence, and
recommending policy measures to strengthen higher education–industry
alignment. The Council is consultative in nature and does not supplant or
diminish the statutory mandates of existing agencies such as CHED, TESDA,
DOLE, DepEd, and DTI.

      The bill further provides for an Annual General Assembly to serve as a
national convergence platform for dialogue, assessment, and collective learning
among stakeholders, as well as the possible creation of Regional and Sectoral
Advisory Boards to ensure that local and sector-specific contexts, priorities, and
labor market conditions are adequately reflected in national policy discussions.

     Recognizing institutional diversity and autonomy, the measure
encourages—but does not mandate—higher education institutions to establish
Higher Education–Industry Partnership Units to manage and coordinate
engagement with industry partners in a transparent, ethical, and systematic
manner.

       Overall, this bill seeks to strengthen the relevance, responsiveness, and
future-readiness of the Philippine tertiary education system by fostering
structured, inclusive, and sustained partnerships with industry, while fully
respecting academic freedom, institutional autonomy, and existing legal
mandates. In doing so, it aims to enhance graduate employability, innovation
capacity, and national competitiveness in support of inclusive and sustainable
development.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

PARTNERSHIP FRAMEWORK, CREATING FOR THE PURPOSE THE

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Title — This Act shall be known as the “Higher Education-Industry
Partnership Framework Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote the sustained development of a skilled, adaptive, and competitive
Filipino workforce and to ensure that higher education remains relevant and
responsive to national development goals.
The State recognizes the vital role of higher education institutions in producing
professionals, scientists, educators, managers, engineers, health workers, and
other high-level human resources necessary for economic growth, social
progress, and national competitiveness. The State likewise recognizes the role of
industry as a principal driver of employment, productivity, innovation, and
enterprise development, and as a key stakeholder in ensuring the relevance and
applicability of higher education outcomes.


Notwithstanding existing efforts, challenges such as unemployment and
underemployment among graduates, job–skills mismatch, uneven regional
development, and weak linkages between learning, research, innovation, and
productive economic activity persist, thereby underscoring the need for closer,
more systematic, and more institutionalized coordination between higher
education and industry. While existing laws govern education delivery,
enterprise-based education and training, qualifications frameworks, and lifelong
learning, the State recognizes the need for a unified framework to guide regular
engagement, joint planning, labor market and skills intelligence exchange, and
shared accountability between higher education and industry in the tertiary
education sector.


Accordingly, the State shall institutionalize a Higher Education-Industry
Partnership Framework and shall create for this purpose a Tripartite Council for
Tertiary Education to serve as the principal coordinating and advisory body to
strengthen collaboration between higher education and industry, consistent with
academic freedom, institutional autonomy, and existing statutory mandates.

SEC. 3. Scope and Application — This Act shall apply to the following:
   a. Public and private higher education institutions, duly recognized by the
      Commission on Higher Education (CHED), including state universities
      and colleges, local universities and colleges, and private higher education
      institutions offering degree and non-degree programs within the tertiary
      education system;
   b. Industry partners, including enterprises, employer organizations,
      chambers of commerce, professional associations, cooperatives, industry
      clusters, social enterprises, and other entities engaged in economic activity,
      innovation, and employment generation that may contribute to or benefit
      from higher education industry collaboration; and
   c. Relevant government agencies, including those involved in higher
      education, workforce development, skills formation, innovation, labor,
      trade, and economic planning, which shall participate within their
      respective mandates, functions, and areas of responsibility.


Provided, That nothing in this Act shall be construed to mandate participation by
any institution, organization, or entity; alter existing accreditation, quality
assurance, licensing, or regulatory requirements; impose curriculum content,
academic standards, or program offerings; interfere with institutional governance
or academic freedom; or confer regulatory, supervisory, or enforcement powers
not otherwise provided by law.


SEC. 4. Institutionalization of the Higher Education — Industry Partnership
Framework — There is hereby institutionalized an Higher Education-Industry
Partnership Framework (IHEPF) which shall serve as the national policy,
coordination,


                                                                                  3
and governance architecture for structured, sustained, and systematic
collaboration between industry and higher education institutions in the tertiary
education sector, with the objective of strengthening relevance, responsiveness,
and alignment with national development priorities.


The Framework shall:
   a. Define the guiding principles, permissible modalities, institutional roles,
      responsibilities, and boundaries of industry engagement in higher
      education, taking into account institutional autonomy, academic freedom,
      and differentiated institutional missions;
   b. Institutionalize regular, structured, and sustained dialogue, joint planning,
      consultation, and feedback mechanisms between higher education
      institutions and industry partners at the national, regional, and sectoral
      levels;
   c. Promote shared responsibility among higher education institutions,
      industry partners, and relevant government agencies for graduate
      relevance, skills utilization, research translation, innovation uptake, and
      workforce readiness, consistent with national and sectoral priorities;
   d. Enable evidence-informed decision-making, planning, and policy
      development through the systematic generation, consolidation, and use of
      labor-market information, skills intelligence, graduate outcomes data, and
      innovation indicators relevant to the tertiary education sector; and
   e. Ensure coherence, alignment, and mutual reinforcement between higher
      education outcomes, workforce development objectives, innovation
      agendas, and national, regional, and sectoral development strategies:




                                                                                 4
Provided, That the Framework shall function solely as a coordination, facilitation,
and alignment mechanism and shall not exercise regulatory, supervisory, or
enforcement powers;


Provided, further, That it shall not regulate academic content, prescribe
curriculum standards, confer academic or professional credentials, or interfere
with the internal governance, academic decision-making, or quality assurance
processes of higher education institutions;


Provided, finally, that all academic authority, including authority over curriculum
design,   program     approval,   admissions,    assessment,    grading,   faculty
appointments, and the conferral of academic degrees, shall remain vested in
higher education institutions and the appropriate regulatory bodies pursuant to
existing laws.


SEC. 5. Guiding Principles of the Partnership Framework — The
implementation of the Higher Education–Industry Partnership Framework shall
be guided by the following principles:
   a. Institutionalized Engagement – Engagement between higher education
      institutions and industry partners shall be structured, sustained, and
      embedded in institutional policies, systems, and governance processes,
      rather than limited to ad hoc, project-based, or short-term arrangements;
   b. Co-Creation and Shared Problem-Solving – Higher education institutions
      and industry partners shall jointly identify challenges, opportunities, and
      priorities, and shall collaboratively design, implement, and review
      initiatives that enhance the relevance of learning outcomes, research
      activities, and innovation efforts;


                                                                                  5
   c. Mutual Accountability – Higher education institutions, industry partners,
      and relevant government agencies shall recognize and uphold shared
      responsibility for outcomes relating to graduate relevance, skills
      utilization, employability, research translation, and workforce readiness,
      subject to their respective roles and mandates;
   d. Respect for Academic Freedom and Institutional Autonomy – All
      partnerships shall uphold academic freedom, protect institutional
      autonomy, and respect the authority of higher education institutions over
      academic matters, governance, and internal decision-making processes;
   e. Inclusivity and Equitable Participation – The Framework shall promote
      inclusive participation by public and private higher education institutions,
      including regional, emerging, and resource-constrained institutions, as
      well as by diverse industry sectors, including small and medium enterprises
      and industries outside major urban centers;
   f. Regional Balance and Development Sensitivity – Engagement
      mechanisms shall take into account regional development priorities, labor
      market conditions, and local industry contexts, and shall support balanced
      growth across regions; and
   g. Future-Readiness and Adaptability – Partnerships shall anticipate
      technological change, emerging occupations, evolving industry needs, and
      long term development trends, and shall promote adaptability, continuous
      learning, and innovation within the tertiary education system.


SEC. 6. Modes of Higher Education-Industry Partnership — Consistent with
the Higher Education–Industry Partnership Framework, collaboration between
higher education institutions and industry partners may be undertaken through
the following modes, mechanisms, and arrangements, subject to applicable laws,
rules, and regulations:

                                                                                6
a. Structured advisory arrangements, including participation of industry
   representatives in consultative or advisory bodies, committees, or forums
   established by higher education institutions for the purpose of providing
   inputs on program relevance, emerging skills requirements, occupational
   trends, and industry developments, without encroaching upon academic
   decision-making;
b. Joint planning and coordination for work-integrated learning, including
   internships, practicums, apprenticeships, on-the-job training, and other
   forms of supervised workplace exposure, undertaken in coordination with
   existing enterprise-based education and training and lifelong learning
   frameworks,      and   designed   to   support   graduate     transition   and
   employability;
c. Collaborative research, development, and innovation initiatives,
   including joint or co-funded research projects, applied research activities,
   technology development, innovation challenges, and knowledge-transfer
   activities that respond to industry needs and societal priorities, consistent
   with institutional research policies and ethical standards;
d. Faculty exposure, immersion, and professional development programs,
   including opportunities for faculty members to engage with industry
   settings for purposes of updating professional knowledge, strengthening
   industry awareness,     and enhancing the relevance of instruction and
   research, subject to institutional policies and guidelines;
e. Shared access to facilities, laboratories, equipment, and innovation
   spaces, including arrangements for the joint use, development, or
   enhancement of physical or digital infrastructure that support learning,
   research, and innovation, subject to agreed terms and applicable
   regulations; and


                                                                                7
   f. Coordinated participation in regional and sectoral development
      planning, including engagement in industry roadmaps, workforce
      development plans, innovation strategies, and other development
      initiatives that require inputs from higher education institutions and
      industry partners.


Provided, That participation in any of the foregoing modes of partnership shall
be voluntary and shall be entered into by mutual agreement of the parties
concerned;


Provided, finally, That such partnerships shall not substitute for, preempt, or
circumvent accreditation, quality assurance, program approval, licensing, or other
regulatory processes required by law or by the appropriate government
authorities.


SEC. 7. Creation of the Tripartite Council for Tertiary Education — There is
hereby created a Tripartite Council for Tertiary Education (TCTE) which shall
serve as the principal national coordinating, advisory, and intelligence body for
the implementation, monitoring, evaluation, and continuous refinement of the
Higher Education–Industry Partnership Framework, with the objective of
strengthening coherence, relevance, and responsiveness of higher education-
industry collaboration in the tertiary education sector.


The Council shall function as the primary institutional mechanism for facilitating
structured dialogue, joint assessment, and strategic coordination among higher
education institutions, industry partners, and relevant government agencies, and
shall provide a permanent forum for the generation, consolidation, and


                                                                                 8
interpretation of labor market, skills, and innovation intelligence relevant to
tertiary education, consistent with national development priorities.


Provided, That the Council shall be consultative and coordinative in nature and
shall exercise its functions through recommendation, facilitation, and advisory
processes only;


Provided, further, That the Council shall not exercise regulatory, accrediting,
licensing, supervisory, or enforcement powers, nor issue binding directives
affecting academic programs, institutional governance, or regulatory decisions;


Provided, finally, That the Council shall not supplant, diminish, or assume the
statutory mandates, powers, or functions of the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED), the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA),
the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), the Department of Education
(DepEd), the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), or any other government
agency, but shall operate strictly within the bounds of coordination, advisory
support, and policy recommendation as provided in this Act.


SEC. 8. Composition, Leadership, and Secretariat of the Council — The
Tripartite Council for Tertiary Education shall be composed of representatives
from government, higher education, and industry, reflecting a balanced and
inclusive tripartite structure to ensure that diverse perspectives inform the
implementation and continuous refinement of the Higher Education–Industry
Partnership Framework.
   a. Government Representation. — The government sector shall be
      represented by the following officials, or their duly authorized permanent
      representatives, serving in an ex officio capacity:

                                                                                  9
  1. Chairperson of the Commission on Higher Education (CHED);
  2. Director-General of the Technical Education and Skills Development
     Authority (TESDA);
  3. Secretary of the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE);
  4. Secretary of the Department of Education (DepEd); and
  5. Secretary of the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI).
b. Higher Education Representation. — The higher education sector shall
  be represented by six (6) members, to be designated from among nationally
  recognized federations of public and private higher education institutions
  and accreditation bodies, as may be determined in accordance with
  guidelines issued by CHED, to ensure broad representation across
  institutional types, geographic regions, and governance structures.
c. Industry Representation. — The industry sector shall likewise be
  represented by six (6) members, to be designated from among nationally
  recognized employer, business, professional, and sectoral federations,
  including representatives from priority, emerging, and innovation-driven
  industries, as may be identified in accordance with guidelines issued by
  CHED in consultation with relevant stakeholders.
d. Chairperson and Vice-Chairperson. — The Chairperson of the
  Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall serve as Chairperson of
  the Council and shall preside over its meetings, set the agenda in
  consultation with the members, and ensure the effective discharge of its
  functions. The Director-General of the Technical Education and Skills
  Development Authority (TESDA) shall serve as Vice-Chairperson and
  shall assist the Chairperson and perform such functions as may be assigned
  by the Council. In the absence or incapacity of the Chairperson, the Vice-
  Chairperson shall act as Chairperson, and the Chairperson may delegate
  functions to such members or officers of the Council as may be necessary.

                                                                         10
   e. Secretariat. — The Tripartite Council for Tertiary Education shall be
      attached to the Commission on Higher Education for administrative and
      budgetary purposes. CHED shall provide a permanent Secretariat to the
      Council, which shall be responsible for providing technical, administrative,
      and logistical support, including the preparation of meeting materials,
      documentation of proceedings, coordination with members and
      stakeholders, management of data and reports, and monitoring of follow-
      through on Council recommendations. The Secretariat shall be manned by
      organic personnel of CHED and may be augmented by personnel detailed
      or seconded from member-agencies of the Council, subject to existing civil
      service laws, rules, and regulations.
   f. Compensation and Meetings. — Members of the Council, except those
      serving in an ex officio capacity, may receive per diem or reasonable
      allowances for meetings actually attended, subject to existing budgeting,
      accounting, and auditing rules and regulations. The Council shall meet on
      a regular basis, at least quarterly, and may convene special meetings as
      may be necessary for the effective performance of its functions.


Provided, That the composition and operations of the Council shall at all times
respect institutional autonomy, academic freedom, and the statutory mandates of
the participating agencies and organizations;


Provided, further, That the detailed procedures for appointment, representation,
convening, and internal operations of the Council shall be prescribed in the
implementing rules and regulations to be issued pursuant to this Act.


SEC. 9. Powers and Functions of the Council — The Council shall have the
following powers and functions:

                                                                               11
a. Monitor domestic and international labor-market trends affecting tertiary
   education,     including   employment     and      unemployment    patterns,
   occupational shifts, emerging industries, technological developments, and
   global workforce movements, insofar as these have implications for higher
   education relevance, graduate outcomes, and long-term human capital
   development;
b. Conduct periodic inventories and assessments of skills, competencies, and
   job requirements relevant to higher education graduates, including the
   identification of current and emerging skills gaps, priority competencies,
   and evolving occupational standards across sectors and regions;
c. Generate, consolidate, analyze, and interpret labor-market, skills, and
   innovation intelligence, drawing from available government data, industry
   inputs, academic studies, and other credible sources, for the purpose of
   informing higher education–industry collaboration, planning, and policy
   formulation;
d. Recommend short-term, medium-term, and long-term policy measures to
   the President of the Philippines, Congress, and relevant government
   agencies on matters affecting higher education–industry alignment,
   workforce relevance, innovation capacity, and graduate employability,
   consistent with national development priorities;
e. Spearhead the implementation, periodic review, and updating of the Higher
   Education–Industry Partnership Framework, including the identification of
   implementation issues, good practices, and areas for improvement, and the
   formulation of strategic directions for sustained collaboration;
f. Receive, synthesize, and evaluate inputs, recommendations, and
   assessments submitted by Regional and Sectoral Advisory Boards
   established pursuant to this Act, and integrate such inputs into national-
   level analysis, policy advice, and framework refinement;

                                                                            12
   g. Hold regular meetings, at least once every quarter, and such special
      meetings as may be necessary for the effective performance of its
      functions, and adopt internal procedures consistent with this Act and its
      implementing rules and regulations; and
   h. Perform such other functions as may be necessary, incidental, or related to
      the attainment of the objectives of this Act and the effective
      implementation of the Higher Education–Industry Partnership Framework.


SEC. 10. Annual General Assembly — The Tripartite Council for Tertiary
Education shall convene an annual General Assembly of higher education
institutions, industry partners, relevant government agencies, accreditation
bodies, and other stakeholders involved in or affected by higher education–
industry collaboration, which shall serve as the primary national convergence
mechanism under the Higher Education-Industry Partnership Framework.


The annual General Assembly shall be convened at least once every calendar year
and shall provide a structured venue for broad-based consultation, exchange of
information, and collective assessment of developments affecting higher
education relevance, workforce alignment, innovation, and graduate outcomes at
the national, regional, and sectoral levels.


Provided, That the annual General Assembly shall serve as the principal forum
for national dialogue, assessment, and collective learning on the implementation
of the Higher Education–Industry Partnership Framework, including the
identification of emerging trends, implementation challenges, good practices, and
priority areas for policy attention; Provided, further, That the Annual Higher
Education–Industry Partnership Report,


                                                                              13
together with relevant analytical briefs, data summaries, and thematic
assessments prepared by or for the Council, shall be formally presented and
discussed during the Assembly to facilitate transparency, informed discussion,
and stakeholder feedback;


Provided, further, That the Assembly may include plenary sessions, thematic
discussions, sector-focused or region-focused consultations, and other structured
activities as may be necessary to elicit meaningful inputs from participating
institutions and stakeholders;


Provided, finally, That the proceedings, outcomes, conclusions, and policy
recommendations arising from the Annual General Assembly shall be duly
documented by the Secretariat and transmitted to the Council, and shall be taken
into consideration by the Council in the formulation of policy recommendations,
strategic directions, and updates to the Higher Education–Industry Partnership
Framework.


SEC. 11. Regional and Sectoral Advisory Boards — Regional and Sectoral
Industry-Higher Education Advisory Boards may be constituted, as may be
necessary and appropriate, to provide localized, sector-specific, and context-
sensitive inputs in support of the implementation and continuous refinement of
the Higher Education-Industry Partnership Framework.


Such Advisory Boards shall serve as platforms for structured consultation,
dialogue, and joint problem identification among higher education institutions,
industry stakeholders, and relevant government entities within a defined
geographic region or economic sector, taking into account local labor market


                                                                              14
conditions, industry composition, development priorities, and institutional
capacities.


The Advisory Boards may assist in identifying region-specific and sector-specific
skills needs, emerging workforce trends, graduate transition issues, research and
innovation opportunities, and other matters relevant to strengthening higher
education–industry collaboration, for consideration at the national level.


SEC. 12. Institutional Partnership Units — Higher education institutions are
encouraged to establish or designate Higher Education-Industry Partnership Units
within their respective organizational structures to serve as focal points for the
planning, coordination, and management of engagement with industry partners,
consistent with the objectives of the Higher Education-Industry Partnership
Framework.


Such Partnership Units may be responsible for facilitating sustained and
structured interaction between academic units, research offices, administrative
offices, and industry stakeholders; coordinating institutional participation in
partnership activities, including advisory engagements, work-integrated learning
arrangements, research and innovation initiatives, and faculty exposure
programs; and supporting the alignment of such activities with institutional
missions, development plans, and quality assurance systems.


The Partnership Units may likewise ensure the observance of ethical standards,
transparency requirements, and conflict-of-interest safeguards in the conduct of
higher   education-industry   partnerships,   including   the   development    or
implementation of internal guidelines governing partnership arrangements, data


                                                                               15
sharing, intellectual property considerations, and the protection of academic
integrity.


Further, the Partnership Units may be tasked with documenting, monitoring, and
evaluating partnership activities and outcomes for purposes of institutional
learning, continuous improvement, and internal planning, including the
preparation of reports, case studies, and other records that may inform decision-
making at the institutional level and contribute to evidence-based refinement of
partnership practices.


Provided, That the establishment or designation of such Partnership Units shall
not be mandatory and shall be undertaken at the discretion of the higher education
institution concerned, taking into account its size, mandate, resources, and
institutional context.


SEC. 13. Annual Report to Congress — The Council shall submit to Congress
an Annual Higher Education–Industry Partnership Report, which shall include,
among others, assessments of graduate employment and employability outcomes,
labor market and workforce trends, current and emerging skills requirements, and
policy recommendations to strengthen higher education–industry collaboration,
workforce relevance, and innovation capacity, consistent with national
development objectives and priorities.


SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall
promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations.




                                                                               16
SEC. 15. Appropriations — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Commission
on Higher Education (CHED). Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


SEC. 16. Separability Clause — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 17. Repealing Clause — All laws, rules, regulations, and issuances
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


SEC. 18. Effectivity — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
