                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to establish a ten kilometer (10 km) municipal fishing
buffer zone beyond the existing fifteen kilometer (15 km) limit of municipal
waters. It amends Section 4 and 18 of Republic Act No. 8550, otherwise
known as the “Philippine Fisheries Code of 1998”, as amended, to expand the
spatial protection accorded to small-scale fishers and to ensure that fishing
activities within this buffer zone primarily benefit local fishing communities.

       The 2017 National Stock Assessment Program of the National Fisheries
and Research Development Institute reported eighty seven percent (87%) of
Philippine marine fisheries stocks are now classified as overfished1. A recent
fisheries assessment further shows a sustained decline in the country’s
fisheries from 2010 to 2023, with total losses reaching 591,136 metric tons of
fish2. This reflects resource depletion in nearshore waters, where municipal
fisherfolk depend most. It compels them to venture into deeper and more
hazardous waters to maintain their catch, while intensifying conflict with
commercial operators that possess greater capacity to exploit marine
resources.

       This bill ensures that the proposed buffer zone prioritizes municipal
fisherfolk, who rely on fishing for subsistence rather than large-scale profit. It
reinforces equitable access to fishing grounds and supports the role of
municipal fisheries in sustaining local food systems and coastal economies.

       The establishment of this buffer zone also serves as a climate adaptation
measure for coastal communities. Healthy coral reefs and seagrass beds
within these areas function as natural barriers against storm surges and as
critical habitats for fish reproduction. As climate risks intensify, protecting
these ecosystems strengthens ecological resilience and secures the long-term
productivity of municipal fishing grounds. It also preserves the traditional
knowledge of sustainable practices of artisanal fisherfolk, which remain
essential to the country’s maritime heritage.

     This vital piece of legislation affirms that municipal waters must remain
primarily for small-scale artisanal fishers, whose livelihoods and welfare
depend on fair and sustained access to maritime resources.

ESTABLISHING A TEN-KILOMETER MUNICIPAL FISHING BUFFER
ZONE BEYOND MUNICIPAL WATERS, PROTECTING MUNICIPAL
FISHING AND ARTISANAL FISHERFOLKS, AMENDING FOR THE
PURPOSE SECTIONS 4 AND 18 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8550,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS “THE PHILIPPINE FISHERIES CODE OF
1998”, AS AMENDED, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Section 4 of Republic Act (RA) No. 8550, otherwise known as
the “Philippine Fisheries Code Of 1998”, as amended by RA No. 10654, is hereby
further amended to read as follows:

            “Sec. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Code, the following
      terms and phrases shall mean as follows:

             “x x x

            “57. Municipal fishing – refers to fishing within municipal waters
      OR WITHIN MUNICIPAL WATERS BUFFER ZONE using fishing
      vessels of three (3) gross tons or less, or fishing not requiring the use of
      fishing vessels.

             “58. Municipal waters - x x x

          “58-A. MUNICIPAL WATERS BUFFER ZONE. – MARINE
      WATERS INCLUDED BETWEEN TWO (2) LINES DRAWN
      PERPENDICULAR TO THE GENERAL COASTLINE FROM
      POINTS    WHERE   THE  BOUNDARY    LINES  OF  THE

      SEC. 2. Section 18 of the same Act is hereby amended to read as follows:

            “Sec. 18. Users of Municipal Waters. – All fishery related activities
