                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE


        The Philippine textile industry is one of the country's industries with significant potential
for growth. Neo-ethnic textiles not only promote and preserve our culture but also support local
weavers and artisans whose craftsmanship has been passed down through generations. The
intricate designs, vibrant colors, and indigenous patterns found in Philippine textiles reflect the
richness of our history and embody the unique identity of the Filipino people.


        In the Philippines, native textiles have become an integral part of many aspects of our
lives. They are prominently featured in academic regalia, where their beautiful patterns and
colors represent the identity and traditions of our educational institutions. Likewise, more
Filipinos are incorporating neo-ethnic textiles into formal attire and other occasions,
demonstrating a growing appreciation for clothing made from indigenous materials and native
fabrics. This increasing demand highlights the vast opportunities for the Philippine clothing and
textile industry to flourish while preserving our cultural heritage.


        This bill seeks to promote and institutionalize the development of the neo-ethnic
Philippine textile and handloom weaving industry by providing the necessary support for local
weavers, encouraging innovation, and creating greater market opportunities for Philippine-made
textiles. By strengthening this industry, the State not only preserves an important part of the
nation's cultural heritage but also generates sustainable livelihoods and contributes to inclusive
economic growth.


        In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                              AN ACT
     PROMOTING AND INSTITUTIONALIZING THE DEVELOPMENT OF THE NEO-
   ETHNIC PHILIPPINE TEXTILE AND HANDLOOM WEAVING INDUSTRY, AND FOR
                                        OTHER PURPOSES


        Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Philippine Neo-Ethnic Textile
Development Act."

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to instill
patriotism and nationalism among the Filipino people, with emphasis on the youth, by fostering
an abiding sense of loyalty to the Republic and deep appreciation of the nation's unique cultural
heritage. The State recognizes the essential role of the education system in inculcating the values
of responsible citizenship, national identity, and cultural pride, and shall endeavor to promote
the preservation, development, and propagation of Philippine traditions as part of the formation
of civic virtues.

        Pursuant to this policy, the State shall undertake appropriate measures to preserve,
promote, and support indigenous artistic expressions — including, but not limited to, handloom
weaving and the production of neo-ethnic Philippine textiles — as integral components of the
national cultural patrimony. These textiles, which reinterpret traditional designs for
contemporary use while preserving their cultural roots, shall be recognized as vital expressions
of Filipino identity and as tools for cultural continuity.

        In furtherance thereof, the preferential use of locally manufactured goods — particularly
those utilizing indigenous materials, traditional knowledge systems, neo-ethnic design
innovations, and local technologies shall be encouraged in both public and private spheres.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are hereby
defined as follows:

           (a)​ Handloom - refers to a device used in weaving fabrics by holding the warp
               threads under tension to facilitate the interweaving of the weft threads;
           (b)​ Handloom weaving industry - composed of the interrelated and interconnected
               component supply chain realizing the production of textiles through the
               handloom;
           (c)​ Neo-ethnic Philippines textiles - refers to textiles made of or containing natural or
               indigenous materials, sourced and/or produced in the Philippines using updated,
               relevant, and green scientific and technological approaches and innovations;
               while integrating and/or retaining traditional patterns, designs, icons, motifs
               and/or approaches, and thus, contribute to livelihood generation and in
               sustaining the local handloom weaving and textile heritage.

​      SEC. 4. Objectives – This Act shall have the following objectives:

           (a)​ To recognize and support the handloom weaving industry and neo-ethnic textile
               production as essential elements of the country's intangible cultural heritage;
           (b)​ To ensure the sustainability and economic viability of traditional weaving
               communities through training, access to markets, financing, and infrastructure
               support;
           (c)​ To promote cultural innovation through the integration of traditional and
               neo-ethnic designs in commercial, ceremonial, and institutional settings;
           (d)​ To create a national registry of recognized weaving communities, patterns, and
               techniques, in collaboration with the National Commission for Culture and the
               Arts (NCCA), the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), and concerned
               agencies;
           (e)​ To integrate the appreciation and use of Philippine indigenous and neo-ethnic
               textiles in education, government functions, and national events; and
           (f)​ To protect indigenous intellectual property and promote ethical collaboration
               between designers, brands, and cultural communities.

​      SEC. 5. Establishment of the National Neo-Ethnic Textile Development Council. – The
National Neo-Ethnic Textile Development Council, hereinafter referred to as the Council, shall be
composed of the following agencies:
           (a)​ Department of Science and Technology;
           (b)​ Department of Trade and Industry;
           (c)​ National Commission for Culture and the Arts;

              (d)​ National Commission on Indigenous Peoples;
              (e)​ Commission on Higher Education;
              (f)​ Technical Education and Skills Development Authority;
              (g)​ Philippine Fiber Industry Development Authority;
              (h)​ Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines; and
              (i)​ Three (3) representatives of accredited handloom weaving and textile industry
                  associations;


          SEC. 6. The National Neo-Ethnic Textile Development Policy. — The Council shall
    formulate the National Neo-Ethnic Textile Development Policy within one (1) year from its
    creation. The Policy shall include, but not be limited to, the following:
              (a)​ Formulation of the Neo-Ethnic Textile Industry Roadmap;
              (b)​ Innovations in weaving design and engineering;
              (c)​ Technical support mechanism for sustainable weaving activities;
              (d)​ Development and promotion of neo-ethnic Philippine textiles;
              (e)​ Development of Philippine Standards for Handloom Woven Textiles;
              (f)​ Intellectual property protection framework for neo-ethnic Philippine textiles;
              (g)​ Development of a database of neo-ethnic Philippine textiles;
              (h)​ Development of textile-related technical and skills and vocational programs; and
              (i)​ Education and training.


          SEC. 7. Academic Regalia from Neo-Ethnic Philippine Textiles. - As a means to elevate
    the dignity and visibility of Philippine culture within formal institutions, the use of neo-ethnic
    Philippine textiles in the academic regalia of all State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) and
    Local Universities and Colleges (LUCs) is hereby prescribed. The SUCs and LUCs shall
    promote the continuing development of culture-based industries, sustain the livelihoods of
    local artisan communities, and advance inclusive economic growth by generating employment
    rooted in cultural heritage.


          SEC. 8. Appropriations. — The amount of Ten Million Pesos (Php 10,000,000.00) is
    hereby authorized to be appropriated from the National Treasury for the initial implementation
    of this Act. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the implementation of this Act shall
    be appropriated as a distinct and separate item.


          SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days from the
    effectivity of this Act, the Department of Science and Technology and the Department of Trade
    and Industry shall promulgate implementing rules and regulations of this Act.


​         SEC. 10. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional,
the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.
