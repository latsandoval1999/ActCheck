                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The rapid integration of Artificial Intelligence (Al) into educational settings presents
a transformative opportunityto enhance learning outcomes and operational efficiency.
However, the swift and unregulatedadoptionof these technologies carries significant
risks, including threats to student data privacy, the potentialfor algorithmic bias that may
disadvantage vulnerable learners, and the deepening of the digital divide between well-
resourced and remoteor low-incomeschools. Current national policies, such as Republic
Act No. 11927 or the Philippine Digital Workforce Competitiveness Act, rightfully promote
digital upskilling but lack specific safeguards for the ethical and equitable deployment of
Ai within the sensitive context Of basic and higher education ciassrooms.

      This legislative gap is concerning as Al tools can process vast amounts of sensitive
student data—from academic performance and behavioral records to biometric
information—creatingattractive targets for data breaches and misuse. Furthermore,
                                procurement and use Of                1              may
inadvertently rely on tools built on foreign datasets that do not reflect Philippine cultural
and linguistic contexts, potentially marginalizing local knowledge. The challenge is to craft
governance that protects learners withoutstifling innovation, moving beyond reactionary
bans to create adaptable, principledframeworks.

       This bill, therefore, seeks to establish a comprehensive regulatory framework for
the safe, ethical, and inclusive use of Al in all Philippine educational institutions. It amends
Republic Act No. 11927 to expand the mandate of the Inter-Agency Council for
Development and Competitiveness of the Philippine Digital Workforce, tasking it with
overseeing Al in education and developing essential guidelines on ethics, data privacy,
and accessibiiity. The biii mandates institutionaiAi Governance Committees, rigorous
impact assessments for high-risk Al tools, and robust parental consent mechanisms. By
setting clear standards for transparency, equity, and accountability, this Act aims to
harness the benefits of Al for education while firmly safeguarding the rights and welfare
of all Filipino learners, ensuring no student is left behind in the digital future.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

       AN ACT PROVIDING FOR A FRAMEWORK FOR THE ETHICAL, TRANSPARENT,
          AND EQUITABLE USE OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE IN EDUCATIONAL
      INSTITUTIONS, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11927, AND

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Responsible Artificial
 2   Intelligence in Education Act of 2026.”

 3   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to recognize the
 4   transformative potential of digital technologies and artificial intelligence (AI) in enhancing
 5   the quality, accessibility, and inclusivity of Philippine education, as aligned with the goals
 6   of developing a competitive digital workforce. The State shall ensure that the integration
 7   of AI in educational institutions is conducted in a manner that:

 8          (a) Upholds the highest standards of ethics, safety, and data privacy;

 9          (b) Promotes transparency, fairness, and accountability in algorithmic decision-
10          making;

11          (c) Actively bridges the digital divide and ensures equitable access to AI’s benefits
12          for all learners, including those in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas,
13          persons with disabilities, and other marginalized sectors;

14          (d) Protects the fundamental rights of students, educators, and personnel, with
15          special consideration for children as a vulnerable population; and

16          (e) Fosters an ecosystem of innovation that is responsive to Philippine educational
17          goals and cultural contexts.

18   The State further recognizes that effective governance of AI in education requires a
19   coordinated, multi-stakeholder, and adaptable approach that can evolve alongside the
20   technology.

21   SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms shall mean:
 1         (a) Artificial Intelligence (AI) System refers to a machine-based system that, for
 2         explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate
 3         outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can
 4         influence physical or virtual environments. This includes, but is not limited to,
 5         machine learning, automated reasoning, and generative AI models.

 6         (b) Educational Institution refers to both public and private establishments
 7         providing basic, tertiary, technical-vocational, and special education, as defined by
 8         the Department of Education (DepEd), the Commission on Higher Education
 9         (CHED), and the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA).

10         (c) High-Risk AI System refers to an AI system deployed in an educational context
11         that poses significant potential threats to the rights, safety, or well-being of students.
12         This includes, but is not limited to, systems used for:

13                (1) Automated grading or high-stakes academic evaluation;

14                (2) Behavioral prediction, monitoring, or emotional recognition;

15                (3) Proctoring and examination integrity;

16                (4) Personalized learning pathways that significantly influence curriculum;
17                and

18                (5) Admissions, scholarship, or placement screening.

19         (d) Synthetic Data refers to data generated through AI systems that mimics the
20         statistical properties and patterns of real personal data but does not relate to an
21         identified or identifiable natural person, thereby mitigating privacy risks.

22         (e) Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) refers to a systematic evaluation
23         conducted prior to the deployment of a high-risk AI system to identify its potential
24         benefits, risks, biases, and impacts on equity, privacy, and educational outcomes.

25   SECTION 4. Amendment to Republic Act No. 11927. – Section 6 of Republic Act No.
26   11927 is hereby amended by adding new subsections (l), (m), and (n) to read as follows:

27         “SEC. 6. Powers and Functions. – The Inter-Agency Council shall have the following
28         powers and functions:

29         …

30         (l) Act as the Central Oversight Body for AI in Education: Formulate, issue, and
31         periodically update a National Framework for Ethical AI in Education. This
32         framework shall establish minimum standards for procurement, development,
33         deployment, and monitoring of AI systems in educational institutions, with particular
34         focus on ethics, equity, accessibility, and data privacy.

35         (m) Develop Sector-Specific Guidelines: In collaboration with DepEd, CHED,
36         TESDA, the National Privacy Commission, and relevant academic and industry
37         stakeholders, develop and disseminate detailed implementation guidelines,
38         including model protocols for Algorithmic Impact Assessments, informed consent,
39         and the use of privacy-enhancing technologies like synthetic data.

40         (n) Establish a Public Registry and Feedback Mechanism: Maintain a public registry
41         of certified AI tools suitable for educational use and establish a mechanism for
42         students, parents, and educators to report concerns related to AI system
43         performance or ethics.”
 1   SECTION 5. Institutional AI Governance Committee. – All Educational Institutions
 2   procuring, developing, or deploying AI systems shall establish an AI Governance
 3   Committee within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act. This
 4   committee shall be composed of representatives from the administration, faculty, IT and
 5   data privacy officers, student government (for tertiary levels), and where applicable,
 6   parents’ representatives. The Committee shall be responsible for:

 7         (a) Reviewing and approving all high-risk AI system deployments based on
 8         completed Algorithmic Impact Assessments;

 9         (b) Ensuring all AI use complies with this Act, the National Framework, the Data
10         Privacy Act of 2012, and other relevant laws;

11         (c) Overseeing the institution’s AI literacy programs for educators, staff, and
12         students; and

13         (d) Serving as the internal point for addressing grievances related to AI use.

14   SECTION 6. Mandatory Requirements for AI Deployment.

15         (a) Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA): No high-risk AI system shall be deployed
16         in any Educational Institution without a satisfactory AIA conducted by the provider
17         and reviewed by the institution’s AI Governance Committee. The AIA must
18         specifically evaluate risks related to bias, discrimination, data privacy, and
19         psychological impact on students.

20         (b) Transparency and Explainability: All interactions with AI systems by students
21         must be clearly identifiable as such. For any AI-driven decision that significantly
22         affects a student’s academic path (e.g., grading, placement), the institution must
23         ensure there is a capacity for human review and a simple explanation of the primary
24         factors involved in the decision.

25         (c) Data Privacy and Protection: The collection and processing of student data by
26         AI systems shall adhere to the principles of data minimization, purpose limitation,
27         and storage limitation. Educational Institutions are strongly encouraged to adopt
28         privacy-enhancing technologies, such as synthetic data generation, for system
29         training and testing where feasible to minimize exposure of real personal data.

30         (d) Prior and Informed Consent: For the use of AI systems that process personal
31         data beyond basic administrative functions, Educational Institutions must obtain
32         prior, informed, and written consent from parents/guardians (for minors) or from the
33         students themselves (if of legal age). Consent forms must be in clear language,
34         specifying the type of data processed, the purpose, and the rights of the data
35         subject.

36   SECTION 7. Equity and Digital Inclusion. – In line with the State’s policy on digital
37   inclusion, the Department of Education (DepEd) and the Commission on Higher Education
38   (CHED), in coordination with the Inter-Agency Council and the Department of Information
39   and Communications Technology (DICT), shall ensure that programs for AI integration
40   actively work to bridge the digital divide. This includes:

41         (a) Prioritizing AI tools and solutions that are functional in low-bandwidth
42         environments and available in major Philippine languages;

43         (b) Ensuring that procurement policies do not favor solutions that would only benefit
44         urban or well-resourced schools, thereby exacerbating existing inequalities; and
 1          (c) Exploring and promoting AI applications that reduce administrative burdens for
 2          teachers and support system-level efficiency, particularly in under-resourced areas.

 3   SECTION 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
 4   this Act shall be charged against the current fiscal year's appropriations of the member
 5   agencies of the Inter-Agency Council, particularly the DepEd, CHED, and DICT.
 6   Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued execution shall be included
 7   in the annual General Appropriations Act.

 8   SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – The Inter-Agency Council
 9   established under R.A. 11927, in consultation with key stakeholders, shall promulgate the
10   necessary IRR within ninety (90) days after the effectivity of this Act.

11   SECTION 10. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
12   unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue in full force
13   and effect.

14   SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
15   regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
16   repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

17   SECTION 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete
18   publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
