                                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State affirms labor as a primary social and economic force and is
committed to promoting full, productive, and freely chosen employment. Central
to long-term development planning is the national vision articulated in AmBisyon
Natin 2040, which reflects the aspirations of Filipinos for a stable, comfortable
and stable life, including access to quality employment opportunities within the
country.

      Despite sustained economic recovery in recent years, structural challenges
in the labor market persist. Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA)
show that as of April 2026, the unemployment rate stood at approximately 4.3%,
equivalent to around 2.2 million unemployed Filipinos. Meanwhile,
underemployment—defined as employed persons seeking additional hours of
work or better employment – remained elevated at about 11.7%, indicating
continued job quality concerns1.

      A critical issue underlying these figures is the persistent mismatch
between available jobs and the skills of the workforce. Employers continue to
report difficulty in filling positions due to gaps in technical competencies, digital

skills, and job readiness, while many jobseekers face prolonged periods of
unemployment or precarious employment. This mismatch disproportionately
affects young workers, displaced employees, and those transitioning between
jobs.

      To address these gaps, this bill proposes the establishment of a Labor
Employment Assistance Program, a targeted intervention designed to support
workers during periods of job transition. The program aims to : (1) provide
temporary income support to displaced or transitioning workers at a level not
lower than the applicable minimum wage ; (2) offer skills upgrading, retooling,
and job-matching services aligned with industry demand; and (3) facilitate faster
reintegration of workers into the labor market through coordinated public
employment services.

      By institutionalizing a responsive and integrated support mechanism, the
proposed measure seeks to reduce frictional unemployment, improve job
matching efficiency, and enhance the overall employability of Filipino workers.
Ultimately, it strengthens the country’s human capital and ensures that
economic growth translates into meaningful and inclusive employment
opportunities.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
  ESTABLISHING A LABOR EMPOWERMENT ASSISTANCE PROGRAM,
AMENDING PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 442, AS AMENDED, OTHERWISE
 KNOWN AS THE LABOR CODE OF THE PHILIPPINES, APPROPRIATING
          FUNDS THEREFOR AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Labor Empowerment
Assistance Program (LEAP) Act.”

SECTION 2. Amendment. – A new Article 107-A of the Labor Code of the
Philippines, as amended, is hereby provided to read as follows:

     “ARTICLE 107-A. LABOR EMPOWERMENT ASSISTANCE PROGRAM. –
WITH CONTRIBUTORY FUNDING FROM THE REGISTRATION OF
CONTRACTORS OR SUBCONTRACTORS, TEN PERCENT (10%) OF THE GROSS
CONTRACT COST LEVY FROM THE PRINCIPAL EMPLOYER FOR EVERY
OUTSOURCING CONTRACT ENTERED INTO, AND BUDGET ALLOCATION
FROM THE GENERAL APPROPRIATIONS ACT (GAA), A LABOR EMPOWERMENT
ASSISTANCE PROGRAM (LEAP) SHALL BE CREATED. THE LEAP SHALL:

      (A) SERVE AS A THREE-MONTH BRIDGING PROGRAM FOR WORKERS
          IN BETWEEN JOB PERIODS AND IS LINKED TO TESDA SKILLS
          TRAINING OR UPGRADING DURING THE UNEMPLOYMENT PERIOD;
      (B) THE AMOUNT OF FINANCIAL SUPPORT SHALL NOT BE LOWER THAN
          THE APPROPRIATE MINIMUM WAGE IN THE FORM OF A MONTHLY
         CASH CARD OR STORED VALUE CARD FOR BASIC NECESSITIES
         LIKE RICE, INFANT FORMULA, MEDICINES, ELECTRICITY AND
         WATER UTILITIES; AND

      (C) LABOR ORGANIZATIONS IN THE NATIONAL TRIPARTITE INDUSTRIAL
          PEACE    COUNCIL    (NTIPC) SHALL   CO-ADMINISTER    THE
          DISTRIBUTION OF THE CARDS AND MONITORING OF COMPLIANCE
          BY THEIR BENEFICIARY-MEMBERS WITH THE REQUIRED TESDA
          SKILLS TRAINING OR UPGRADING.”

SECTION 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Secretary of Labor
and Employment shall promulgate the necessary implementing rules and
regulations within thirty (30) days from the effectivity of this Act.

SECTION 4. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, rules, and regulations or
parts thereof, which are contrary to or inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 5. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this law or the
application thereof to any person or circumstance, is held invalid, the remainder
of this law, or the application of such provision or part to other persons of
circumstances, shall not be affected thereby.

SECTION 6. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
