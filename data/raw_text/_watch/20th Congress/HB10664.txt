                           AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE EQUITABLE, TRANSPARENT, AND
INCLUSIVE DISTRIBUTION OF PHILHEALTH BENEFIT SHARES AMONG
PERSONNEL OF PUBLIC HEALTH INSTITUTIONS, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution mandates the State to protect life, liberty, and property under Article II,
Section 5, and to promote social justice in all phases of national development under Article
XIII. It likewise upholds the protection of workers and the equitable distribution of benefits,
principles that extend to all personnel in public health institutions who collectively sustain the
delivery of essential healthcare services to the Filipino people.

With the implementation of the Universal Health Care (UHC) Act (Republic Act No. 11223),
the Philippine health financing system has shifted toward pooled PhilHealth reimbursements,
global budgeting, and facility-level financial autonomy through mechanisms such as Special
Health Funds. While intended to improve efficiency and service delivery, Commission on Audit
(COA) observations on Department of Health (DOH) hospitals, together with DOH
administrative issuances, indicate that the utilization of PhilHealth-derived revenues and
related personnel benefit shares is largely implemented at the institutional level, where hospital
management exercises significant discretion in distribution.

Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) research on UHC implementation further
notes that decentralization of hospital financial management has contributed to uneven and
inconsistent application of incentive and benefit-sharing systems across health facilities. As a
result, concerns have been raised regarding unequal distribution, lack of standardized formulas,
delayed releases, and limited participation of non-medical, non-clinical, and non-permanent
personnel, including job order and contract of service workers who nonetheless play essential
roles in hospital operations.

In practice, disparities exist both across and within hospital systems, particularly between
tertiary and lower-level facilities and between plantilla and non-permanent personnel. World
Health Organization (WHO) health workforce studies and PIDS policy research consistently
show that income-derived incentive systems tend to favor clinical and higher-ranked personnel,
while non-clinical workers such as administrative staff, utility personnel, and support service
workers often receive lower shares or are excluded depending on institutional policy. The Civil
Service Commission (CSC) likewise recognizes that job order and contract of service personnel
are not automatically covered by standard government incentive schemes unless explicitly
included in agency guidelines, reinforcing structural inequities in benefit access.

Delays in the release of PhilHealth Benefit Share allocations are also frequently reported. COA
audit findings on hospital income utilization note that administrative processing requirements,
pooling mechanisms under the UHC framework, and the absence of uniform statutory timelines
contribute to delayed distribution of benefits to personnel. These delays adversely affect
workforce morale, financial predictability, and perceptions of fairness in public health
institutions.

These systemic issues are compounded by the growing reliance of public hospitals on
PhilHealth reimbursements as a major source of operational funding. However, in the absence
of a uniform statutory framework, implementation remains fragmented, institution-dependent,
and vulnerable to discretion-driven variations, resulting in inconsistent treatment of similarly
situated personnel across facilities and weakening equity in public sector compensation
systems.

In light of these conditions, there is a compelling need to institutionalize a standardized,
transparent, and inclusive framework for the distribution of PhilHealth Benefit Shares. The
proposed bill seeks to ensure that all personnel, regardless of employment status, are fairly
included in benefit-sharing mechanisms, while establishing safeguards against delay, diversion,
and discriminatory allocation.

The bill further strengthens governance by mandating uniform distribution rules, timely release
of benefits, transparency and public reporting requirements, protection against retaliation for
whistleblowers, and enhanced inter-agency coordination among the Department of Health
(DOH), Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), Civil Service Commission (CSC), and Commission on Audit (COA). In
doing so, it promotes fairness, strengthens workforce morale and retention, and ensures that
public health financing translates into equitable benefits for all who contribute to healthcare
delivery.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE EQUITABLE, TRANSPARENT, AND
INCLUSIVE DISTRIBUTION OF PHILHEALTH BENEFIT SHARES AMONG
PERSONNEL OF PUBLIC HEALTH INSTITUTIONS, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “PhilHealth Benefit Share Equity
and Transparency Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote social justice, protect
and advance the welfare of health workers, and ensure the equitable distribution of benefits
derived from public healthcare services. The State recognizes the indispensable contributions
of all personnel in public health institutions, including clinical, non-clinical, permanent,
temporary, casual, contractual, job order, contract of service, coterminous, and other non-
permanent personnel, in the delivery of healthcare services and the generation of PhilHealth
reimbursements.

Toward this end, the State shall establish a transparent, accountable, equitable, and standardized
system for the distribution of PhilHealth Benefit Shares, ensure their timely release, prevent
arbitrary and discriminatory allocation, and protect such funds from diversion or misuse.

SEC. 3. Coverage. — This Act shall apply to all government-owned and government-operated
hospitals, Department of Health hospitals and medical centers, provincial, city, municipal,
district, and local government-operated hospitals, specialty hospitals, sanitaria, infirmaries,
rural health units participating in integrated health systems, and other public health institutions
accredited by the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) and receiving
PhilHealth reimbursements or payments.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act the following terms shall be defined:

   a) PhilHealth Benefit Share (PBS) refers to the portion of PhilHealth reimbursements,
      global budget payments, capitation payments, network contracting funds, and other
      PhilHealth-derived revenues legally allocable for personnel benefit sharing;
   b) Public Health Institution refers to any government-owned or government-operated
      health facility covered by this Act;
   c) Personnel refer to all individuals rendering services in a covered institution regardless
      of employment status;
   d) Non-Permanent Personnel refers to personnel holding temporary, casual, contractual,
      coterminous, job order, contract of service, part-time, or similar appointments;
   e) Equitable Distribution refers to a fair allocation based on objective criteria including
      workload, responsibility, risk exposure, performance, length of service, and
      contribution to health service delivery;
   f) Special Health Fund refers to the pooled fund established under Republic Act No.
      11223 or the Universal Health Care Act.

SEC. 5. Inclusion of All Personnel. — All personnel who have rendered actual service during
the applicable benefit-sharing period shall be eligible to receive PhilHealth Benefit Shares. No
personnel shall be excluded solely on the basis of employment status, source of compensation,
appointment classification, or nature of engagement.

SEC. 6. Ring-Fencing of Personnel Shares. — Notwithstanding any provision of Republic Act
No. 11223, otherwise known as the Universal Health Care Act, and its implementing rules and
regulations, the personnel component of PhilHealth reimbursements, global budget payments,
capitation payments, network contracting funds, and other PhilHealth-derived revenues
intended for personnel benefit sharing shall be segregated, ring-fenced, and exclusively
reserved for distribution to qualified personnel under this Act.

Such funds shall not be diverted, pooled for non-personnel purposes, reallocated, withheld, or
absorbed into the general funds of local government units or any other account unrelated to
personnel benefit sharing.

SEC. 7. Standardized Distribution Framework. — Within one hundred twenty (120) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in consultation with PhilHealth,
the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), the Civil Service Commission (CSC), the
Commission on Audit (COA), employee organizations, and other stakeholders, shall
promulgate a standardized distribution framework.

The framework shall establish a uniform benefit-sharing system composed of:
   a) A Base Equity Component constituting fifty percent (50%) of the distributable
       personnel benefit pool, to be distributed equally among all qualified personnel;
   b) A Position and Responsibility Component constituting thirty percent (30%) of the
       distributable personnel benefit pool, to be allocated based on objective criteria
       including level of responsibility, professional qualifications, risk exposure, and scope
       of work; and
   c) A Performance and Productivity Component constituting twenty percent (20%) of the
       distributable personnel benefit pool, to be allocated based on measurable performance
       indicators, attendance, and contribution to service delivery.

The framework shall ensure that all qualified personnel, regardless of employment status,
participate in each component of the distribution system.

SEC. 8. Equity Safeguards and Allocation Parameters. — To prevent favoritism,
discrimination, and excessive concentration of benefits:
   a) No qualified personnel shall be excluded from any component of the distribution
      formula solely on account of employment status;
   b) The highest individual PhilHealth Benefit Share shall not exceed three (3) times the
      lowest share received by a full-time qualified personnel member during the same
      distribution period;
   c) Distribution formulas shall be based on objective, measurable, and transparent criteria;
   d) No personnel category shall receive preferential treatment except as may be justified
      by the standardized criteria established under this Act;
   e) The allocation system shall ensure meaningful participation of non-clinical personnel
      and non-permanent personnel in the overall benefit-sharing mechanism.

SEC. 9. Mandatory Period for Distribution. — All PhilHealth Benefit Shares shall be
distributed within thirty (30) calendar days from the date of receipt or crediting of the
corresponding PhilHealth reimbursement, global budget payment, capitation payment, network
contracting fund, or other PhilHealth-derived revenue.

Failure to distribute within the prescribed period without lawful justification shall constitute
prima facie evidence of administrative neglect of duty.

SEC. 10. PhilHealth Benefit Share Committee. — Every covered institution shall establish a
PhilHealth Benefit Share Committee composed of representatives from management,
physicians, nurses, allied health professionals, administrative personnel, non-clinical
personnel, non-permanent personnel, and recognized employee organizations.

The Committee shall monitor compliance, review allocations, receive complaints, and
recommend corrective measures.

SEC. 11. Transparency and Public Disclosure. — Every covered institution shall prepare and
publish an Annual PhilHealth Benefit Share Transparency Report containing:

   a) Total PhilHealth revenues received;
   b) Total personnel benefit pool;
   c) Distribution formula utilized;
   d) Number of beneficiaries by personnel category;
   e) Aggregate distributions per personnel category.

The report shall be posted on the institution’s official website, if available, and in conspicuous
places accessible to employees and the public.

SEC. 12. Grievance and Appeals Mechanism. — The DOH shall establish a grievance and
appeals mechanism for personnel who allege unlawful, inequitable, delayed, or discriminatory
distributions.

Complaints shall be resolved within sixty (60) days from receipt.

SEC. 13. Protection Against Retaliation. — No personnel shall be dismissed, terminated,
reassigned, subjected to adverse performance evaluations, denied promotion, subjected to non-
renewal of contract, harassed, intimidated, or otherwise prejudiced for filing a complaint,
participating in an investigation, or providing information concerning violations of this Act.
Any adverse action taken within one (1) year from the filing of a complaint shall create a
rebuttable presumption of retaliation.

Where prima facie evidence of retaliation exists, the appropriate disciplinary authority may
place the responsible official under preventive suspension pending investigation.

SEC. 14. Prohibited Acts. — The following acts are prohibited:

   a) Excluding qualified personnel from benefit sharing solely because of employment
      status;
   b) Diverting, withholding, delaying, misallocating, or unlawfully reducing PhilHealth
      Benefit Shares;
   c) Utilizing personnel shares for purposes other than those authorized under this Act;
   d) Falsifying records relating to PhilHealth collections or distributions;
   e) Granting preferential treatment without legal basis;
   f) Retaliating against complainants, witnesses, or cooperating personnel.

SEC. 15. Administrative Liability. — Any public officer or employee found liable for violating
this Act shall be subject to administrative sanctions under applicable civil service laws, rules,
and regulations, without prejudice to criminal, civil, or other liabilities.

SEC. 16. Penal Provisions. — Any person who willfully diverts, withholds, misallocates,
unlawfully delays, or excludes qualified personnel from receiving PhilHealth Benefit Shares
shall, upon conviction, suffer:

   a) Imprisonment of not less than six (6) months and one (1) day but not more than six (6)
      years;
   b) A fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00) but not more than
      One Million Pesos (₱1,000,000.00); or
   c) Both such fine and imprisonment, at the discretion of the court.

Where the offender is a public officer or employee, the penalty of perpetual disqualification
from public office shall likewise be imposed.

SEC. 17. Relation to the Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act. — Any willful diversion,
withholding, misallocation, unauthorized use, or discriminatory allocation of PhilHealth
Benefit Shares resulting in undue injury to qualified personnel shall, where applicable,
constitute a violation of Section 3(e) of Republic Act No. 3019, otherwise known as the Anti-
Graft and Corrupt Practices Act, without prejudice to prosecution under other applicable laws.

The Office of the Ombudsman shall have concurrent jurisdiction over complaints involving
public officials and employees under this Act.

SEC. 18. Appropriations. — The administrative and operational expenses necessary for the
implementation of this Act, including the establishment of PhilHealth Benefit Share
Committees, monitoring systems, and grievance mechanisms, shall be charged against the
appropriations of the Department of Health and concerned agencies, subject to existing
budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.
The PhilHealth Benefit Shares distributed under this Act shall not require separate
appropriations and shall be sourced exclusively from PhilHealth reimbursements, global
budget payments, capitation payments, network contracting funds, and other PhilHealth-
derived revenues legally allocable for personnel benefit sharing.

SEC. 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred twenty (120) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with PhilHealth, DBM, CSC, COA, and
other concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations.

SEC. 20. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 21. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, administrative issuances, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 22. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
