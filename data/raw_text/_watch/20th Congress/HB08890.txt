                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to protect and
   promote the right to health of the people (Article II, Section 15) and to undertake
   appropriate health human resource development responsive to the country’s
   needs and problems (Article XIII, Section 12).

         Occupational therapists (OTs) play a vital role in the health care system
   by promoting recovery, rehabilitation, and functional independence among
   individuals affected by injury, illness, or disability. They provide patient-
   centered interventions that enable individuals to regain, develop, or maintain
   the skills necessary for daily living, education, and productive employment.
   Their work is essential in hospitals, rehabilitation centers, community-based
   programs, and other healthcare settings, particularly for persons with
   disabilities, the elderly, and those requiring long-term care.

          Despite their indispensable contributions, occupational therapists in
   government service remain comparatively disadvantaged in terms of
   compensation relative to the complexity, specialization, and impact of their
   work. The current salary structure does not adequately reflect the professional
   demands placed upon them, nor does it provide sufficient incentive to retain
   skilled practitioners in the public sector.

         At the same time, the growing global demand for occupational therapists
   has contributed to the continued outmigration of Filipino health care
   professionals seeking better compensation and career opportunities abroad.
   This trend exacerbates the shortage of rehabilitation professionals in the
   country and undermines the capacity of the public healthcare system to deliver
   comprehensive and sustained care.

         This measure seeks to upgrade the minimum entry-level salary grade of
   government occupational therapists from Salary Grade 11 to Salary Grade 15.
   The proposed adjustment aims to ensure equitable compensation, improve
   workforce retention, and align their remuneration with similarly situated
   healthcare professionals.
      Ultimately, this measure affirms the State’s commitment to uphold
decent work standards in the public health sector and to strengthen the
delivery of accessible, inclusive, and quality healthcare services for all Filipinos.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                               “AN ACT
            UPGRADING THE MINIMUM SALARY GRADE OF GOVERNMENT
            OCCUPATIONAL THERAPISTS FROM SALARY GRADE 11 TO
            SALARY GRADE 15”

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Government
Occupational Therapists' Salary Upgrading Act of 2026. "

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to ensure adequate and just compensation for government occupational
therapists in order to attract and retain competent professionals in the public health
sector, consistent with the State’s mandate to provide accessible and quality
healthcare services to all Filipinos.

     SECTION 3. Upgrading the Government Occupational Therapists'
Minimum Salary Grade Level. - The minimum salary grade of government
occupational therapists occupying entry-level positions (Occupational Therapist I) is
hereby upgraded from Salary Grade 11 to Salary Grade 15.

       The salary grades of occupational therapists occupying higher positions shall
be correspondingly adjusted in accordance with existing compensation and position
classification systems.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all occupational therapists
employed in public health facilities and other health-related institutions owned and
operated by the national government, local government units, and other government
instrumentalities.

     SECTION 5. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s available
appropriations of the Department of Health (DOH) and other concerned agencies.
       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the Civil Service
Commission (CSC), shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its
effective implementation.

     SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

      SECTION 9. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
