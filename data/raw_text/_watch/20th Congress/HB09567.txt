     AN ACT ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL INTEGRATED PSORIASIS

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        According to The Lancet, a peer-reviewed general medical journal and one of
the world's oldest and best-known medical publications, psoriasis is a common,
lifelong inflammatory skin disease that can appear at any age and poses significant
personal and societal burdens. It is associated with serious comorbidities such as
depression, psoriatic arthritis, and cardiometabolic disorders.

       Although psoriasis cannot yet be cured, optimal management requires early
intervention, prevention of comorbidities, lifestyle modifications, and individualized
therapy to lessen both its physical and psychological impact.

        In 2014, Member States of the World Health Organization (WHO) recognized
psoriasis as a serious noncommunicable disease (NCD) through a World Health
Assembly resolution. The resolution emphasized that many people around the world
suffer needlessly from psoriasis due to incorrect or delayed diagnosis, inadequate
treatment options, insufficient access to care, and social stigmatization. Likewise, a
2025 global study shows a significant rise in psoriasis cases, with the number of people
affected increasing by 86%-from 23.06 million in 1990 to 42.98 million in 2021. The
total number of psoriasis-related disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs), which
represent the overall disease burden, increased from 2 million in 1990 to 3.7 million
in 2021. In the Philippines, the estimated number of individuals with psoriasis is about
2 million, although some organizations believe the actual figure may be higher due to
undiagnosed cases.

       In this light, and consistent with the recommendations of the Global Report on
Psoriasis to:




                                           1
          a) Ensure that people with psoriasis have access to professional medical
             care and comprehensive, individualized treatment;
          b) Provide patients with medicines listed in the WHO Model List of Essential
             Medicines, including systemic therapies;
          c) Cover the cost of psoriasis treatments through universal health coverage
             schemes;
          d) Make optimal treatment for psoriasis and its comorbidities available;
          e) Ensure that health services for psoriasis are responsive to patient
             preferences and delivered in a manner that is safe, effective, timely,
             efficient, and of acceptable quality;
          f) Urge governments and nongovernmental organizations to support
             education on common chronic skin conditions for health-care
             professionals, including in undergraduate medical and nursing curricula
             and in-service primary care training; and
          g) Encourage governments to support psoriasis research and to reduce
             stigma and discrimination.

This bill seeks to address the unmet needs of Filipinos living with psoriatic disease by
establishing a national integrated program for the prevention and treatment of
psoriasis as a public health concern.



       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1          SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “National Integrated
 2   Psoriasis Care and Treatment Program Act".
 3
 4          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to protect and
 5   promote the right to health of the people, including the right of persons suffering from
 6   psoriasis to survival and full and healthy development as individuals through access
 7   to timely health information and adequate professional medical care. In pursuit of
 8   such policy, and in recognition that psoriasis is a serious noncommunicable disease
 9   (NCD) with no clear cause or cure, the State shall institutionalize a system that is
10   people-centered, comprehensive, integrative and sustainable and shall facilitate
11   collaboration among government and non-government agencies and organizations at
12   the national and local levels, private sector, professional health organizations,
13   academic institutions, communities and families towards the provision of early and
14   sustainable care of persons afflicted with psoriasis. The State recognizes the crucial
15   role of research in defining health programs and activities to address the needs of
16   patients with psoriasis. The State also recognizes that an effective public education
17   program is vital in helping ensure the early diagnosis and treatment of psoriasis and
18   in preventing those afflicted with them from being the subject of ridicule and
19   stigmatization.

 7         Sec. 4. National Integrated Psoriasis Prevention and Treatment
 8   Program. There is hereby established a National Integrated Psoriasis Prevention and
 9   Treatment Program (NIPPTP) under the Department of Health (DOH), which shall
10   serve as the framework for all psoriasis-related activities of the government. The
11   program shall have the following objectives:
12         (a) Promote and provide mechanisms for early identification, screening, and
13         diagnosis of persons suffering from psoriasis;
14         (b) Establish an extensive and sustainable health care system integrated within
15         the public health care delivery system for early and sustainable care for patients
16         suffering from psoriasis through the provision of comprehensive care from
17         multidisciplinary teams of specialists, including dermatologists,
18         rheumatologists, psychologists, psychiatrists, pediatricians, cardiologists and
19         others;
20         (c) Improve the access of psoriasis patients to professional medical care, and
21         comprehensive, individually adapted treatment;
22         (d) Provide referral services to patients for appropriate medical expertise and
23         to identify medical facilities suited to the needs of patients;
24         (e) Maintain an updated list of health care institutions and practitioners
25         concerned with the treatment of psoriasis;
26         (f) Facilitate the manufacture and importation of affordable drugs, especially
27         those included in the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines, including systemic
28         therapies;
29         (g) Provide regulatory and fiscal incentives to support research and
30         development studies on psoriasis particularly on the epidemiology, etiology,
31         treatment and ways to improve health care for psoriasis patients and
32         collaborate with other institutions for the conduct of research activities
33         pertaining to the disease; (h) Provide an avenue for the sharing of best
34         practices on issues relating to early detection, prevention, cure, treatment, and
35         rehabilitation of persons diagnosed with psoriasis;
36         (i) Promote an advocacy discouraging bias against and stereotyping of persons
37         afflicted with psoriasis;
38         (j) Establish sustainable support networks and orientation and counselling
39         services for families of persons diagnosed with psoriasis;
40         (k) Network with other organizations, institutions, and professionals working
41         on public programs for persons diagnosed with psoriasis;
42         (1) Integrate public educational and informational campaigns in the current
43         programs to identify persons afflicted with psoriasis and help the public
44         understand the special needs of such persons;
45         (m) Facilitate the regular collaborative activities among stakeholders regarding
46         the realization of the objectives of this Act;
47         (n) Publish a newsletter on current developments and research on psoriasis, as
48         well as other reading materials on the early detection, prevention, and

13   efficiency in the implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the NIPPTP and in the

16          Sec. 6. Development of Medical Guidelines for Psoriasis Treatment. -
17   The DOH, in consultation with relevant agencies of government, psoriasis focused
18   professional societies, and patients' organizations, shall spearhead the development
19   of medical guidelines regarding the diagnosis of psoriasis and its treatment and shall
20   implement standards relating to medical care for psoriasis patients such as
21   establishment of objectives of care and treatment plan, adequate assessment of
22   progress of therapy, using uniform tools to assess the severity of the disease and
23   patient quality of life. Sec. 7. Psoriasis Patients as Persons with Disabilities. Persons
24   living with psoriasis, shall be considered as persons with disability (PWDs) in
25   accordance with Republic Act No. 7277, as amended, otherwise known as the "Magna
26   Carta for Disabled Persons".
27
28          Sec. 7. Rights and Privileges. - Persons living with psoriasis shall be
29   accorded the same rights and privileges as PWDs and the Department of Social
30   Welfare and Development (DSWD) shall ensure that social welfare and benefits
31   provided under Republic Act No. 7277, as amended, are granted to them. Further, the
32   Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) shall adopt programs which promote
33   work and employment opportunities for persons with psoriasis.
34
35         Sec. 8. Non-discrimination. - The Commission on Higher Education (CHED),
36   Department of Education (DepEd), Technical Education and Skills Development
37   Authority (TESDA), DOLE, and other relevant government agencies, shall ensure that
38   people living with psoriasis are free from any form of discrimination in school,
39   workplace and community.

25          Sec. 10. PhilHealth Benefits for Psoriasis. - The Philippine Health
26   Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) shall expand its benefit packages to include
27   primary care screening, detection, diagnosis, treatment assistance, supportive care,
28   management and follow-up care for all types and severity of psoriasis. It shall also
29   develop innovative benefits such as support for community-based models of care to
30   improve psoriasis treatment journey and reduce costs of care. The development or
31   expansion of any PhilHealth benefit shall go through a proper, transparent and
32   standardized prioritization setting process, such as the Health Technology Assessment
33   and actuarial feasibility study, to avoid inequitable allocation of funds for health care
34   services.
35
36   The Fund and PhilHealth benefits shall be made available in public and private DOH
37   licensed facilities. The DOH and PhilHealth shall prescribe, in consultation with
38   stakeholders, the coverage rates and applicable rules on options to charge co-payment
39   for services rendered. Processes to avail of such funding shall be streamlined to ensure
40   timely provision of psoriasis care.

14           Sec. 12. Psoriasis and Related Supportive Care Medicines. The DOH,
15   and other concerned government agencies, shall implement reforms supporting early
16   access to essential medicines, innovative medicines and health technologies, to ensure
17   highest possible quality of life among people with psoriasis. The reforms include
18   facilitating quick access to drugs for compassionate use and developing a more
19   responsive system for effectively addressing emergency cases.
20   The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) shall create a dedicated and streamlined
21   process, not exceeding one (1) year, for the licensing of innovator and generic
22   psoriasis medication, subject to appropriate quality checks and compliance with
23   minimum standards, such as, but not limited to, being approved and used for psoriasis
24   treatment in other countries.
25   The DOH shall ensure sufficient supply of medicines for psoriasis-related care and
26   management that are available at affordable prices. Further, the DOH shall formulate
27   a monitoring system to check that psoriasis medications are safe and administered in
28   correct dosages.

30          Sec. 13. National Survey and Research. - The DOH shall conduct an annual
31   survey to determine the extent and total incidence and prevalence of persons with
32   psoriasis in the country for the government to provide the necessary health
33   interventions to address the needs faced by persons with psoriasis.
34
35          Sec. 14. Integrated Approach to Research in Cooperation with

30           Sec. 19. Health Education and Promotion in Schools, Colleges, and
31   Universities. - The CHED and the DepEd, in coordination with the DOH, shall develop
32   policies and provide technical guidance to academic institutions and administrators to:
33           (a) Promote and facilitate integration of age appropriate and gender sensitive
34           key messages on psoriasis risk factors, early warning signs and symptoms of
35           psoriasis including lifestyles and healthy diets in their curriculum, health and
36           wellness programs, and co-curricular activities;
37           (b) Undertake mainstreaming of practical supportive care and psychosocial
38           support programs for people living with psoriasis, and their family members,
39           especially those who act as carers for psoriasis patients; and
40           (c) Adopt initiatives that minimize or eliminate stigma and discrimination in
41           schools, colleges, and universities that are experienced by people with
42           psoriasis, psoriasis survivors and their families.
43
