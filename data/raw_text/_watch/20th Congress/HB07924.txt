                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Many Filipinos, especially those in remote, poor, and disaster-prone barangays,
struggle to access safe and affordable medicines. Distance from pharmacies, high
prices, and the risk of counterfeit drugs often force families to delay treatment or rely
on unsafe alternatives. This situation directly affects health outcomes and places the
most vulnerable communities at greater risk of preventable illness.

This bill institutionalizes the Botika sa Barangay program to bring essential
medicines closer to communities in a safe, regulated, and affordable manner. It
establishes clear rules on licensing, pharmacist supervision, and quality standards to
ensure that medicines sold at the barangay level are genuine, properly stored, and
responsibly dispensed. By prioritizing underserved areas and allowing lexible
governance models with strong oversight, the measure balances wider access with
patient safety and professional accountability.

By strengthening community-level access to essential medicines, this measure gives
practical meaning to the constitutional right to health. It empowers barangays,
supports rational drug use, and helps ensure that no Filipino is denied basic treatment
simply because of where they live or how much they earn.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                              AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING BARANGAY DRUGSTORES, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
“BOTIKA SA BARANGAY,” PROVIDING FOR THEIR LICENSING, OPERATION, AND
PHARMACIST SUPERVISION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Botika Sa Barangay Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right to health and to improve access to safe, effective, and affordable
essential medicines at the community level, particularly in geographically isolated
and disadvantaged areas and economically depressed barangays.

The State shall institutionalize Botika sa Barangay (BSB) outlets as community-based
access points for essential medicines and health promotion, while ensuring
compliance with standards on pharmaceutical practice, licensing, quality assurance,
and patient safety.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) Botika sa Barangay (BSB) refers to a community-based pharmaceutical
       outlet established, operated, or accredited under this Act and licensed in
       accordance with applicable FDA/DOH rules.

   (b) Pharmacist refers to a duly registered and licensed pharmacist with a valid
       Certi icate of Registration and Professional Identi ication Card.

   (c) Pharmacy support personnel includes pharmacy assistants, aides, or other
       medicine handlers as recognized under applicable laws and regulations.

   (d) Core list/National Drug Formulary refers to the list of essential medicines
       authorized for dispensing in BSB outlets under DOH policy and the National
       Drug Formulary system, subject to updating rules.

SECTION 4. National Program; Coverage; Phased Implementation. —

   (a) Program. The Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with the Food and
       Drug Administration (FDA), local government units (LGUs), and other relevant
       agencies, shall implement a national program for the establishment and/or
       accreditation of BSB outlets.

   (b) Phased implementation. Within three (3) years from effectivity, the DOH
       shall prioritize barangays based on population, poverty incidence, access to
       health facilities, geographic isolation, disaster risk, and other health equity
       indicators.

   (c) Reasonable timeline. The DOH shall issue a realistic implementation
       schedule; the thirty (30)-day period in the original draft is retained only
       for program planning and issuance of initial guidelines, not for
       establishing BSB in every barangay.

SECTION 5. Establishment Priority; Local Consultation. — Prior to establishing or
accrediting a BSB, the DOH and the concerned LGU shall consult the Municipal/City
Development Council and the barangay council. Highest priority shall be given to
economically depressed barangays and geographically isolated and disadvantaged
areas.

SECTION 6. Licensing; Regulatory Oversight; Compliance. —

   (a) Licensing required. No BSB shall operate without the appropriate
       license/authority issued pursuant to FDA and DOH rules and regulations.




                                                                          3|Page
   (b) Regulatory purpose. The FDA shall exercise inspection, licensing, and
       monitoring powers over BSB outlets consistent with the State policy of
       maintaining an effective health products regulatory system.

   (c) Minimum standards. The IRR shall prescribe minimum standards on:

      1. Sourcing and procurement of medicines;
      2. Storage conditions and inventory controls;
      3. Prevention of counterfeit/substandard medicines;
      4. Dispensing protocols and patient counseling;
      5. Pharmacovigilance, adverse drug reaction reporting, and referral
         mechanisms; and
      6. Recordkeeping and reporting.

SECTION 7. Medicines to be Made Available. —

   (a) Scope of medicines. BSB outlets shall sell and dispense only medicines
       included in the core list/essential medicines list and other medicines as may
       be speci ically allowed by DOH/FDA rules for the BSB category.

   (b) Generic transparency. The BSB shall display to the public the generic names,
       brand/trade names (when applicable), strengths, and prices of medicines
       carried, in accordance with applicable rules.

SECTION 8. Pharmacist Supervision; Role of the Botika sa Barangay Aide. —

   (a) Mandatory       pharmacist     supervision. The     procurement, storage,
       distribution, and dispensing of medicines in BSB outlets operated by or in
       coordination with LGUs shall be conducted only under the supervision of a
       duly registered and licensed pharmacist, consistent with law.

   (b) Botika sa Barangay Aide. A BSB may be staffed by a trained aide or other
       pharmacy support personnel to assist in operations, provided that such aide
       shall work under the supervision and accountability framework prescribed by
       the DOH and consistent with pharmacy practice regulations.

   (c) Training and certi ication. The DOH, in coordination with the FDA and
       appropriate professional bodies, shall develop standardized training modules
       for BSB aides on medicine safety, storage, dispensing assistance, basic

       counseling scripts, recordkeeping, and referral. Aides shall not perform
       functions reserved by law for licensed pharmacists.

SECTION 9. Governance Models; Partnerships. —

   (a) The DOH may implement BSB through any of the following models, subject to
       IRR safeguards:
       1. DOH-supported and LGU-operated outlets;
       2. LGU-operated outlets accredited under national standards; or
       3. Partnership/accreditation of quali ied cooperatives or community
          organizations, subject to licensing and pharmacist supervision
          requirements.

   (b) The DOH shall ensure that BSB design does not undermine existing licensed
       pharmacies, but complements access in underserved areas, consistent with
       public welfare objectives and police power regulation principles.

SECTION 10. Public Information and Health Promotion. — The DOH shall conduct
a sustained public information campaign on rational drug use, antibiotic stewardship,
medicine safety, and the proper use of BSB services, in coordination with LGUs,
barangay health workers, and civil society organizations.

SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Guidelines. — Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the DOH, in coordination with the FDA, and after consultation with LGUs
and stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations (IRR) to
carry out the provisions of this Act.

SECTION 12. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with this Act are
hereby repealed or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 14. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
