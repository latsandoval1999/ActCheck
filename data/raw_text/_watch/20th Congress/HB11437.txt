18                                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE
19
20   The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is one of the country’s most important natural sources.
21   Stretching for more than 500 kilometers, it runs north-to-south along the entire eastern
22   coast of Luzon, from Cagayan down to Quezon province.1 It is home to diverse wildlife,
23   rivers, and a critical sanctuary for thousands of unique species. It is relevant not only to
24   those areas near this mountain range, but also to the entirety of Luzon.
25
26   Perhaps the Sierra Madre's most well-known role is its function as a natural barrier and the
27   country’s first line of defense against destructive tropical cyclones coming from the Pacific
28   Ocean.2 As storms approach, the mountain range helps slow down and weaken their winds
29   before they reach inland areas. This natural shield helps reduce the destructive effects of
30   typhoons, protecting lives, infrastructure, ecosystems, and livelihoods. It also helps
31   regulate water that supports communities and agricultural areas across the region.3
32
33   However, the “Backbone of Luzon” is in urgent need of protection. Despite its importance,
34   the Sierra Madre continues to face severe environmental degradation. Every year, the
35   mountain range reportedly loses roughly 9,000 hectares of forest cover due to human
36   activities, including illegal logging, agricultural expansion, mining, and other forms of

 2   regulate water flow, and reduce the impact of strong winds is also diminished. As forest
 3   degradation continues, communities in Central Luzon and Metro Manila may face greater
 4   risks of flooding and landslides.

 5   This proposed measure therefore seeks to establish the Sierra Madre Mountain Range
 6   Authority (SMMRA), which will lead the formulation and implementation of a
 7   comprehensive and long-term plan for the conservation, protection, and sustainable
 8   development of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range. It will also coordinate the efforts of
 9   concerned national government agencies and local government units to ensure a unified
10   and sustained approach to the protection and development of the mountain range.

11   The bill also proposes the creation of the Sierra Madre Ecological Integrity and
12   Sustainable Development Master Plan, a comprehensive, science-based, 25-year plan that
13   will establish ecological limits, management zones, no-go areas, permitted and prohibited
14   uses, restoration priorities, measurable outcomes, investment requirements, and
15   implementation responsibilities. The Master Plan will provide a common framework for
16   harmonizing national, regional, local, ancestral-domain, and sectoral plans affecting the
17   Sierra Madre.

18   This measure also promotes sustainable livelihoods, including agroforestry, ecotourism,
19   and sustainable non-timber forest products. It also provides avenues for Indigenous
20   Peoples to participate in conservation and restoration activities.

21   To support the long-term implementation of the law, the bill establishes a Sierra Madre
22   Fund and provides for lawful and transparent financing mechanisms, including national
23   government appropriations, environmental charges, and grants and technical assistance.
24   The Fund is limited to the SMMRA’s statutory functions and is subject to government
25   accounting, auditing, transparency, and disclosure requirements.

26   The measure also strengthens accountability by establishing ecological standards,
27   monitoring systems, environmental liability mechanisms, and stronger prohibitions
28   against destructive activities. It prohibits, among others, unauthorized land conversion,
29   destructive extractive activities, introduction of invasive species, unauthorized
30   commencement of covered works, and the use of false compliance documents.

31   The proposed legislation therefore seeks to move Sierra Madre governance from
32   fragmented management toward an integrated, science-based, and investment-oriented
33   system. It recognizes that protecting the Sierra Madre is an investment in the country’s
34   overall well-being, which include food and water security, biodiversity, climate resilience,
35   and disaster-risk reduction.

22                                          General Provisions
23   SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Sierra Madre Mountain Range
24   Protection, Conservation, and Sustainable Development Act.”
25
26   Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. Consistent with Article II, Section 16 of the 1987 Constitution,
27   which mandates that the State shall protect and advance the right of the people to a
28   balanced and healthful ecology, it is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect,
29   conserve, restore, and sustainably manage the Sierra Madre Mountain Range as a
30   nationally significant ecological, climate, water-security, and disaster-resilience system.
31   The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is one of Luzon’s principal ecological systems. Its
32   topography influences tropical-cyclone winds and rainfall. The range includes the Northern
33   Sierra Madre, which Republic Act No. 9125 recognizes as the widest remaining tropical
34   rainforest in Luzon, together with other forest, watershed, riverine, and coastal
35   ecosystems, some of which are fragmented or degraded. These ecosystems store and
36   sequester carbon, sustain biodiversity and ecological connectivity, and act as a regulator
37   of climate and water flow. Protecting the Sierra Madre is thus indispensable to biodiversity
38   conservation, climate resilience, water security, and disaster risk reduction. Their
39   protection, restoration, and sustainable management are therefore of national
40   importance.
41

 1   To achieve these ends, the State shall adopt a science-based, ecosystem-based, and
 2   landscape-scale approach to management, integrating conservation, restoration, and
 3   sustainable development. This includes applying ridge-to-reef planning, recognizing the
 4   ecological and hydrological links among uplands, lowlands, rivers, and coastal areas, and
 5   using nature-based solutions where appropriate to strengthen resilience.
 6
 7   The State shall adopt a Nature-Based Solutions-First and resilience-investment approach
 8   in addressing climate change, disaster risk, flooding, landslides, drought, water insecurity,
 9   and other climate-related risks affecting the Sierra Madre Mountain Range and its
10   downstream beneficiary areas. Public and private investments shall, where scientifically
11   and technically appropriate, prioritize the protection, restoration, and enhancement of
12   natural infrastructure, including forests, watersheds, wetlands, riparian areas, floodplains,
13   mangroves, ecological corridors, and other natural systems that provide climate and
14   disaster-risk reduction services.
15
16   The State shall likewise mobilize investments from beneficiaries of Sierra Madre
17   ecosystem services and establish mechanisms through which verified ecological and
18   resilience outcomes may be financed through Nature Credits, blended finance, public-
19   private partnerships, climate finance, sustainable livelihood investments, and other lawful
20   market-based mechanisms.
21
22   The State recognizes that degradation persists because existing economic and
23   governmental decision systems permit land conversion, extraction, and conventional
24   development to generate immediate and identifiable financial returns, while ecological,
25   climate, water, and disaster costs are transferred to the public. Institutions, Indigenous
26   Peoples, local communities, and lawful tenure holders responsible for protection,
27   restoration, and long-term stewardship do not receive reliable revenues proportionate to
28   the ecosystem services they maintain. Fragmented authority enables this misalignment
29   and prevents ecological limits and economic responsibilities from being applied
30   consistently across the mountain range.
31
32   Accordingly, the State shall: (a) establish binding ecological limits, no-go areas, decision
33   principles, and consistency requirements governing covered plans, zoning ordinances,
34   permits, projects, procurement, and public investment; (b) account for the extent,
35   condition, service flows, beneficiaries, and economic contribution of Sierra Madre
36   ecosystems in accordance with Republic Act No. 11995, or the Philippine Ecosystem and
37   Natural Capital Accounting System Act; (c) require Covered Enterprises to satisfy Nature
38   Credit Purchase Obligations proportionate to their measured dependency on and benefit
39   from Sierra Madre ecosystem services; (d) require persons causing environmental damage
40   to avoid harm and fully restore affected ecosystems, independently of and in addition to
41   compliance with the Philippine Environmental Impact Statement System; and (e) direct



                                                                                                5
 1   Sierra Madre Nature Credit revenues to lawful rights holders, registered nature-project
 2   developers, and ecosystem stewards in accordance with existing tenurial instruments,
 3   customary rights, and verified performance.
 4
 5   The avoidance hierarchy, precautionary principle, polluter-pays principle, beneficiary-pays
 6   principle, ecological connectivity, intergenerational equity, and the more protective
 7   applicable standard shall govern implementation. No purchase, credit, or payment under
 8   this Act shall authorize prohibited activity, excuse noncompliance with an Environmental
 9   Compliance Certificate, or compensate for harm to an irreplaceable ecosystem that should
10   have been avoided.
11
12   The Authority established under this Act shall harmonize, interface, and rationalize the
13   mandates, programs, and functions of national agencies, local government units, and
14   other stakeholders, thereby ensuring a coherent, integrated, and ecosystem-scale
15   governance framework. All policies and actions shall be evidence-based, incorporating the
16   latest scientific data on biodiversity, hydrology, and climate impacts. The State shall
17   likewise strengthen enforcement and penalties against illegal activities like illegal logging,
18   mining, wildlife trafficking, land conversion, and the like, to deter destruction of this critical
19   landscape.
20
21   In line with Republic Act No. 8371, known as the Indigenous Peoples Rights Act (IPRA) of
22   1997 and the Constitution, the State recognizes and protects the rights of Indigenous
23   Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) within the Sierra Madre. All
24   management plans and projects shall respect ancestral domains, recognize customary
25   laws, and secure Free and Prior Informed Consent (FPIC) from affected communities.
26   Indigenous and local communities shall have meaningful participation in decision-making
27   and benefit-sharing, as stewards of the environment. Nothing in this Act shall diminish the
28   lawful powers of the DENR, LGUs, PAMBs, the NCIP, or Indigenous governance
29   institutions; such powers shall be exercised consistently with the ecological floors, conflict
30   rules, and decision procedures established herein.
31
32   Sec. 3. Scope and Coverage. This Act applies to the entire Sierra Madre Mountain Range,
33   the longitudinal mountain chain from Cagayan Province in the north to Quezon Province
34   in the south, including all existing and future protected areas within it. It covers all
35   forestlands, watersheds, river basins, and coastal zones up to their tidal limits that lie
36   within the range. Buffer zones, ecological corridors, and ridge-to-reef linkages necessary
37   to maintain connectivity and integrity are included. All components of the National
38   Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS), as amended, within Sierra Madre shall be
39   governed by this integrated framework alongside their existing management bodies.

 1   Sec. 5. Creation of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority. There is hereby created the
 2   Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority (SMMRA), hereinafter referred to as the
 3   Authority, as a government-owned or -controlled corporation with an original charter
 4   under Republic Act No. 10149, vested with the corporate and regulatory powers expressly
 5   granted by this Act. It shall be attached to the Office of the President for policy
 6   coordination and administrative supervision, and shall be subject to the oversight of the
 7   Governance Commission for GOCCs and other applicable laws. For policy and technical
 8   alignment, the Authority shall closely coordinate with the Department of Environment and
 9   Natural Resources (DENR) and other relevant national agencies. The Authority shall be the
10   lead policy, planning, coordinating, and regulatory body, and the range-wide ecological
11   governance and nature-market authority, tasked with managing the Sierra Madre
12   landscape as a whole.
13
14   Its principal mandate is to change the governmental and economic decision systems
15   affecting the mountain range by: (a) establishing and enforcing the Sierra Madre Master
16   Plan and binding ecological limits, and administering the Sierra Madre Master Plan
17   Conformity Certificate process; (b) compiling operational ecosystem, enterprise-
18   dependency, and sector-dependency accounts in coordination with the agencies
19   responsible under Republic Act No. 11995; (c) delineating and periodically updating
20   Ecosystem-Service Catchments and identifying the cities, municipalities, economic zones,
21   industrial estates, utility service areas, locators, and other beneficiary areas within them;
22   (d) establishing methodologies, safeguards, and integrity requirements for Sierra Madre
23   Nature Credits; (e) administering project and developer registration, credit issuance,
24   ownership, transfer, purchase, retirement, and the Sierra Madre Nature Credit Registry;
25   (f) determining and enforcing Nature Credit Purchase Obligations of Covered Enterprises;
26   (g) assessing ecological loss and enforcing restoration performance; (h) enabling private,
27   community, Indigenous, and public project proponents to access the standards,
28   information, lawful tenure pathways, and regulated demand needed to finance
29   protection, restoration, and long-term stewardship; and (i) integrating the exercise of the
30   lawful mandates of the DENR, LGUs, PAMBs, the NCIP, and other institutions through the
31   decision rules and procedures established by this Act.
32
33   The Authority shall not originate, sponsor, design, develop, construct, implement, own,
34   operate, manage, finance, invest in, underwrite, or guarantee any nature, restoration,
35   infrastructure, or other project that is eligible or expected to seek registration, Sierra
36   Madre Nature Credits, a Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate, or another
37   decision from the Authority. It shall not act as project proponent, developer, investment
38   adviser, arranger, broker, trader, buyer, or beneficial owner of Sierra Madre Nature
39   Credits.

 1   Sec. 13. Delineation of Powers and Functions. The Authority shall operate as a coordinative
 2   and integrative body, and shall not duplicate or supersede the existing mandates of
 3   national agencies, LGUs, Protected Area Management Boards (PAMBs), or Environmental
 4   Impact Committees (EICs) established under existing laws, rules, and regulations. It shall

 8   (EMB), other agencies, or local government units. It shall not exercise customary
 9   governance or determine ownership of ancestral domains. Nothing in this Act shall be
10   construed to diminish, modify, or supplant the Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)
11   System and the functions of the Environmental Impact Committee (EIC) in the evaluation
12   of projects requiring an Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC). Likewise, it shall not
13   override LGU autonomy, except to the extent necessary to enforce consistency with
14   national environmental laws and the Sierra Madre Master Plan. Instead, the Authority’s
15   role is to harmonize policies, set scientific standards, integrate plans, provide technical and
16   scientific inputs, where appropriate, to the EIA process, and coordinate enforcement
17   across jurisdictions, thereby enhancing a unified, ecosystem-based management of the
18   Sierra Madre Mountain Range.

21   Act, including adoption and enforcement of the Sierra Madre Master Plan and ecological
22   floors; issuance of Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificates; operational

24   safeguards; project and developer registration; issuance and retirement of Sierra Madre
25   Nature Credits; assessment of Nature Credit Purchase Obligations; maintenance of the
26   Sierra Madre Nature Credit Registry; ecological-loss and restoration assessment; and
27   market-integrity enforcement.

26   Sec. 23. Formulation and Adoption. Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act, the
27   Authority shall formulate, adopt, and commence the implementation of a Sierra Madre
28   Master Plan covering a period of twenty-five (25) years. The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall
29   serve as the comprehensive, science-based, ecosystem-oriented, and climate-resilient
30   blueprint for the conservation, protection, restoration, and sustainable development of
31   the Sierra Madre Mountain Range.
32
33   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall integrate, among others, land use and development
34   planning; biodiversity conservation; watershed and ridge-to-reef ecosystem management;
35   climate change adaptation and mitigation; disaster risk reduction and management;
36   sustainable livelihoods; infrastructure compatibility; and the traditional ecological
37   knowledge, customary governance systems, and sustainable resource management
38   practices of ICCs/IPs, consistent with RA 8371, otherwise known as the "Indigenous
39   Peoples' Rights Act of 1997." The Authority shall likewise recognize ICCAs and support
40   indigenous co-management approaches consistent with applicable laws.
41

 1   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall harmonize and integrate national, regional, and local
 2   development and land use plans affecting the Sierra Madre Mountain Range, including
 3   Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs), Regional Development Plans (RDPs), ADSDPPs,
 4   protected area management plans, and other relevant sectoral plans, to ensure policy
 5   coherence and an integrated ridge-to-reef management approach.
 6
 7   In formulating the Sierra Madre Master Plan, the Authority shall build upon existing
 8   management plans prepared under the NIPAS, as amended, utilize the best available
 9   scientific information and ecological data, and conduct meaningful consultations with
10   concerned national government agencies, local government units, FICCs/IPs, local
11   communities, the academe, civil society organizations, and other stakeholders.
12
13   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall expressly identify binding ecological limits, no-go areas,
14   permissible uses, restoration priorities, and decision rules. Harmonization shall be
15   governed by ecological integrity, precaution, avoidance before mitigation, polluter pays,
16   beneficiary pays, connectivity, intergenerational equity, the more protective applicable
17   standard, and respect for ancestral domains, customary law, tenurial rights, and FPIC.
18
19   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall be reviewed at least once every three (3) years and may
20   be updated earlier, whenever necessary, to incorporate advances in scientific knowledge,
21   respond to fortuitous events and environmental emergencies, address emerging threats,
22   or reflect significant changes in ecological, climatic, or socio-economic conditions.

28   zoning system shall guide conservation, protection, restoration, sustainable resource use,
29   settlement, development, and disaster-risk management throughout the Sierra Madre
30   Mountain Range.
31

33   Protection and Irreplaceable Ecosystem Zones; (2) Ecological Corridor and Connectivity
34   Zones; (3) Watershed and Catchment Protection Zones; (4) Restoration and Recovery
35   Zones; (5) Sustainable Production and Community Stewardship Zones; and (6) Existing
36   Settlement and Compatible Development Zones. The Sierra Madre Master Plan may create
37   subzones only within these classes and for the purposes stated in this Act. The Master Plan
38   shall likewise identify the corresponding broad management grouping and applicable
39   subzone for each geographically delineated area.

10   Sec. 49. Sierra Madre Protected Area Network (SMPAN). All protected areas, protected
11   landscapes and seascapes, critical habitats, and other conservation areas within the Sierra

13   Network (SMPAN) under a landscape-scale conservation approach to strengthen
14   ecological connectivity, biodiversity conservation, and climate resilience. The
15   management regime for the Network shall promote the interconnectivity of ecological
16   corridors among protected areas and other key biodiversity areas to ensure the long-term
17   ecological integrity of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range. Coordinated planning, zoning,
18   monitoring, and enforcement shall be undertaken to prevent conservation leakage and to
19   enhance the effectiveness of protected area management.
20

24               government units, ICCs/IPs, and other stakeholders, shall establish, restore,
25               and maintain a network of ecological corridors linking fragmented protected
26               areas and other critical habitats to facilitate wildlife movement, gene flow, and
27               climate-induced species migration, including those of threatened and endemic
28               species such as the Philippine Eagle; and
29
30        (b)   The Authority shall utilize geospatial technologies, biodiversity assessments,
31              and scientific data to identify ecological bottlenecks and habitat fragmentation
32              between the northern, central, and southern Sierra Madre ranges, and shall
33              prioritize the restoration and reconnection of these ecological corridors
34              through appropriate conservation and habitat restoration measures.

12   Sec. 72. Prohibited Acts. In addition to the acts prohibited under existing environmental
13   and natural resources laws, the following acts are likewise prohibited within the Sierra
14   Madre Mountain Range:
15
16       1.   Unauthorized Land Conversion. The conversion of forest lands to agricultural or
17            other non-forest uses without the required authority under existing laws and,
18            where applicable, without first securing a Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity
19            Certificate;
20
21       2.   Destructive Extractive Activities. The conduct of mining or commercial or non-
22            commercial quarrying in a manner that causes significant or irreversible
23            environmental damage, including, but not limited to, the destruction of old-
24            growth forests, critical habitats, or watersheds, and the pollution or
25            contamination of rivers and other water bodies;
26
27       3.   Introduction of Invasive Species. The deliberate introduction, release, or
28            propagation of non-native or invasive species of flora or fauna that harm native
29            species, biodiversity, or ecosystem integrity;
30
31       4. Unauthorized Commencement of Covered Works. Beginning, financing,
32          directing, or causing site clearing, tree cutting, grading, excavation, road opening,
33          land development, construction, installation, expansion, or other physical works
34          for a covered project before obtaining a valid Sierra Madre Master Plan
35          Conformity Certificate or other Authority document expressly required by this
36          Act, together with the ECC, PAMB approval, FPIC, tenure instrument, LGU permit,
37          or sector authorization otherwise required by law. A permit issued by one
38          institution shall not excuse the failure to obtain a separate certification, approval,
39          or consent required by this Act or another law;

 1       5.   Continuation after Notice or Order. Continuing covered works after receipt of a
 2            lawful stop-work, cease-and-desist, suspension, or restoration order issued by the
 3            Authority, the DENR, a PAMB, an LGU, a court, or another competent body, or
 4            removing an official seal or obstruction placed to enforce that order; and
 5
 6       6. False Compliance Evidence. Knowingly submitting, using, altering, or causing the
 7          submission or use of a false Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity Certificate,
 8          Nature Credit Compliance Clearance, registry record, electronic confirmation
 9          record, or other Authority document to obtain a permit, financing, procurement
10          award, disbursement, or authority to proceed.

18   Sec. 73. Penalties.
19       (a) Administrative Compliance Violations. Failure to file a required report, maintain
20            an accurate account, timely purchase and retire a final assessed requirement, or
21            comply with a procedural or Registry duty, without fraud or material ecological
22            harm, shall be subject only to the administrative sanctions expressly provided in
23            this Section after notice and hearing.

25            Authority certificate, compliance clearance, Registry record, verification report,
26            or settlement confirmation; intentionally causes double issuance or double
27            claiming; manipulates the Registry; or diverts credit revenues shall, upon
28            conviction, be punished by imprisonment of two (2) years and one (1) day to six
29            (6) years and a fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00)
30            nor more than Ten Million Pesos (₱10,000,000.00), or three (3) times the
31            economic benefit obtained, whichever is higher, without prejudice to prosecution
32            under another law carrying a higher penalty.
