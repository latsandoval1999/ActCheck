                              AN ACT
  ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE NATIONAL FRAMEWORK FOR SAFE,
   SECURE, RESILIENT, AND SUPPORTIVE EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS,
  INSTITUTIONALIZING SCHOOL-BASED VIOLENCE PREVENTION, THREAT
     ASSESSMENT, EMERGENCY PREPAREDNESS, CRISIS RESPONSE,
 RECOVERY, MENTAL HEALTH EARLY INTERVENTION, AND CONFIDENTIAL
  SAFETY REPORTING MECHANISMS, PROVIDING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND
                       FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Schools must be places of learning, development, and security. Yet verified
data show that bullying remains a serious concern. According to the Organisation for
Economic Co-operation and Development's Programme for International Student
Assessment 2022 country note for the Philippines, 43% of participating girls and 53%
of participating boys reported experiencing acts of bullying at least a few times a
month. The Department of Education recorded 2,500 bullying cases in the National
Capital Region in School Year 2024–2025, up from 2,268 the previous school year.
These are reported incidents only, but they demonstrate the continuing need for
stronger prevention, reporting, response, and accountability systems.

       While Republic Act No. 10627 (Anti-Bullying Act of 2013) mandates school anti-
bullying policies, disciplinary measures, and law enforcement referrals, it lacks a
unified national framework for broader safety threats across basic, higher, and
technical-vocational institutions. School safety demands a proactive approach
encompassing prevention, threat assessment, emergency medical response, prompt
parental notification, evidence preservation, mental health support, recovery, and
inter-agency coordination. Institutional delays or lack of accountability are
unacceptable when student safety is compromised.
      Constitutional mandates under Article II, Section 13 (youth well-being), Article
XIV, Sections 1 and 4 (quality education and institutional regulation), and Article XV,
Section 3 (child protection) provide clear grounds for legislation. Reinforced by RA No.
10121 (disaster reduction), RA No. 11036 (mental health), and RA No. 12080 (school
mental health), this measure consolidates these duties into one framework.

       The principal justifications and effects of the measure are as follows:

          1. Establishes a unified National Safe Schools Framework covering
             prevention, protection, preparedness, response, and recovery across
             public and private basic, higher, and technical-vocational education
             institutions, jointly formulated by DepEd, CHED, and TESDA.
          2. Requires covered schools to conduct annual safety risk assessments
             and maintain an updated School Safety and Emergency Preparedness
             Plan addressing crisis management, medical response, inclusive
             arrangements, and learning continuity.
          3. Mandates behavior-based, multidisciplinary Threat Assessment and
             Intervention Teams; assessments do not determine liability, and
             protective traits or lawful conduct cannot solely define dangerousness.
          4. Creates confidential reporting channels, bars retaliation against good-
             faith whistleblowers, and prevents penalties based strictly on unverified
             anonymous claims.
          5. Compels immediate protection, medical checks, evidence tracking,
             victim care, and child-sensitive probes for serious violence, requiring
             parent notification within fifteen minutes of school awareness.
          6. Prohibits leaving injured learners unattended or unassessed, requires
             formal medical transfer, mandates screening for hidden head trauma,
             and explicitly bans using token corrective activities as the sole
             institutional remedy.
          7. Prohibits tampering with relevant incident records under legal notice,
             and subjects severe violations—including fatalities, weapon use, and
             cover-ups—to external reviews by education authorities.

       Victims and their parents or guardians must be informed of available referral
mechanisms, consulted on safety measures, informed of the general status of the
investigation subject to privacy and due-process limitations, and allowed to submit
evidence and concerns, with reasonable accommodation for learners with disabilities
or special educational needs. Implementation will be coordinated through a National
Safe Schools Council and supported by a Safe Schools Innovation and Assistance
Fund.

       A school cannot provide quality education when warning signs are ignored,
parents are informed too late, injured learners are not immediately assessed, or
serious violence is reduced to nominal discipline.
      Ang ligtas na paaralan ay institusyong handang umiwas sa panganib, tumugon
agad, at managot sa bawat batang ipinagkatiwala rito.

      In view of the foregoing, the swift passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

28         Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Safe Schools and
29   Violence Prevention Act of 2026.”

31          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
32   protect the right of every learner, teacher, school personnel, parent, and lawful visitor
33   to a safe, secure, inclusive, child-sensitive, and resilient educational environment.
34
35           The State recognizes that school safety requires an integrated and preventive
36   approach that addresses the full continuum of risks affecting educational institutions,
37   including violence, bullying, cyberbullying, threats, unauthorized weapons, dangerous
38   intrusions, hostage incidents, terrorism, fires, earthquakes, severe weather events,
39   chemical and biological hazards, public health emergencies, and other natural or
40   human-induced emergencies.

13           Sec. 3. Objectives. – This Act shall have the following objectives:
14           (a) Establish minimum national standards for school safety, security,
15   emergency preparedness, crisis response, and post-incident recovery;
16           (b) Institutionalize preventive systems for identifying, assessing, and
17   addressing credible threats and concerning behavior before such conduct escalates
18   into violence or serious harm;
19           (c) Strengthen the capacity of educational institutions to respond to armed
20   attacks, dangerous intrusions, bomb threats, hostage incidents, fires, disasters,
21   hazardous-material incidents, and other emergencies;
22           (d) Improve access to age-appropriate, culturally responsive, and evidence-
23   informed mental health and psychosocial support services;
24           (e) Establish safe, confidential, accessible, and accountable channels for

26   serious safety concerns;
27           (f) Require coordinated action among educational institutions, law-enforcement
28   agencies, emergency responders, health authorities, social-welfare offices, local
29   government units, parents, learners, and communities;
30           (g) Promote risk-based improvements in school infrastructure, access control,
31   emergency communication, and campus security;
32           (h) Establish reliable national data for evidence-based school safety policies
33   while protecting personal information and the rights of children; and
34           (i) Provide mechanisms for continuity of learning, family reunification, trauma-
35   informed recovery, and support for affected learners and personnel after emergencies.
36
37          (j) Establish time-bound, documented, and enforceable duties for the medical
38   response, parent notification, evidence preservation, external reporting, victim
39   protection, and institutional review of serious school violence.

13          Sec. 5. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
14          (a) “Behavioral intervention” refers to a structured, non-punitive, and
15   individualized process for addressing conduct, circumstances, or needs that may
16   place a person or the school community at risk;
17          (b) “Credible threat” refers to a communication, act, plan, or pattern of conduct
18   that, based on reasonably available information and an objective assessment,
19   indicates a genuine possibility of violence, serious injury, self-harm, or other grave
20   danger;
21          (c) “Crisis response team” refers to the trained school-based group responsible
22   for coordinating immediate action during and after an emergency;
23          (d) “Educational institution” or “school” refers to any institution covered under
24   Sec. 4 of this Act;
25          (e) “Emergency” refers to an actual or imminent event that threatens life, health,
26   safety, property, school operations, or continuity of education, whether arising from
27   natural or human-induced causes;
28          (f) “Family reunification” refers to the controlled and documented process of
29   releasing learners to authorized parents, guardians, or responsible adults following an
30   emergency;
31          (g) “Mental health and psychosocial support” refers to services and
32   interventions that protect or promote psychosocial well-being and address mental
33   health needs before, during, and after emergencies;
34          (h) “School safety” refers to the policies, systems, infrastructure, personnel,
35   programs, and practices intended to protect members of the school community from
36   violence, injury, abuse, disasters, emergencies, and other serious risks;
37          (i) “Threat assessment” refers to a fact-based and multidisciplinary process for
38   determining whether a reported threat or concerning behavior presents a risk of harm
39   and identifying appropriate protective, supportive, administrative, or lawful
40   interventions;
41          (j) “Threat assessment and intervention team” refers to the multidisciplinary
42   school-based or cluster-based body constituted under this Act to assess and manage
43   reported threats or concerning behavior; and

28         Sec. 6. National Safe Schools Framework. – The Department of Education, in
29   coordination with the Commission on Higher Education and the Technical Education
30   and Skills Development Authority, shall formulate and periodically update a National
31   Safe Schools Framework.
32
33          The Framework shall establish nationally consistent but risk-based standards
34   covering the following pillars:
35          (a) Prevention, including child protection, anti-bullying measures, mental health
36   support, behavioral intervention, threat assessment, and safe reporting;
37          (b) Protection, including campus access control, school infrastructure,
38   emergency communication, visitor management, and security personnel standards;
39          (c) Preparedness, including risk assessment, emergency planning, drills,
40   training, continuity arrangements, and coordination with responders;
41          (d) Response, including incident command, communication, emergency
42   medical action, evacuation, lockdown, shelter-in-place, accounting for learners and
43   personnel, and family reunification; and
44          (e) Recovery, including psychosocial support, victim assistance, continuity of
45   learning, facility restoration, after-action review, and institutional improvement.

 5           Sec. 7. School Safety and Emergency Preparedness Plan. – Every educational
 6   institution shall prepare, maintain, implement, and annually review a School Safety
 7   and Emergency Preparedness Plan appropriate to its size, location, learner
 8   population, facilities, identified hazards, available resources, and level of risk.
 9
10           The Plan shall include:
11           (a) A school safety profile and annual risk and vulnerability assessment;
12           (b) Procedures for preventing, identifying, reporting, and responding to threats
13   and emergencies;
14           (c) Incident command and succession arrangements;
15           (d) Evacuation, lockdown, shelter-in-place, emergency dismissal, and family
16   reunification protocols;
17           (e) Procedures for armed attacks, dangerous intrusions, hostage situations,
18   bomb threats, suspicious packages, fires, earthquakes, severe weather, chemical or
19   biological hazards, disease outbreaks, and other identified risks;
20           (f) Emergency communication and redundant notification systems;
21           (g) Procedures for accounting for learners, personnel, contractors, and visitors;
22           (h) Emergency medical assistance and coordination with hospitals, emergency
23   medical services, and local health authorities;
24           (i) Measures for learners and personnel with disabilities, medical conditions,
25   language barriers, or other access and functional needs;
26           (j) Continuity-of-learning and temporary relocation arrangements;
27           (k) Trauma-informed recovery and psychosocial support measures;
28           (l) Data protection and confidentiality protocols;
29           (m) Assigned responsibilities of school officials and personnel;
30           (n) Contact information and coordination arrangements with local authorities
31   and emergency responders; and
32           (o) A schedule for training, drills, evaluation, and corrective action.

12         Sec. 10. Threat Assessment Procedures and Due Process. – The
13   appropriate education agencies shall establish uniform procedures and minimum
14   standards for threat assessment and intervention.
15
16          Such procedures shall include:
17          (a) Prompt initial screening of reports;
18          (b) Determination of whether an immediate emergency exists;
19          (c) Referral to law-enforcement, medical, mental health, child-protection, or
20   social-welfare authorities when warranted;
21          (d) Collection and verification of relevant information from lawful and reliable
22   sources;
23          (e) Assessment of the nature, specificity, credibility, capability, access to
24   means, imminence, and protective factors associated with the reported threat;
25          (f) Preparation of a proportionate safety and intervention plan;
26          (g) Periodic review and closure of cases;
27          (h) Appropriate notice to and participation of parents or guardians, subject to
28   lawful exceptions involving immediate safety, abuse, neglect, or other circumstances
29   where prior notice may increase risk;
30          (i) Protection of confidential and privileged information;
31          (j) Documentation of the factual basis for material decisions; and
32          (k) Procedures for administrative review and correction of materially inaccurate
33   records.
34
35          A threat assessment shall be preventive and protective in character and shall
36   not, by itself, constitute a finding of criminal or administrative liability. Disciplinary
37   proceedings shall remain subject to applicable laws, institutional rules, notice, and
38   opportunity to be heard.

38         Sec. 24. Mandatory Response to Serious School Violence. – Upon actual or
39   constructive knowledge of serious school violence, the educational institution shall
40   immediately:
41         (a) Stop the incident and secure the victim;
42         (b) Separate the persons involved and prevent retaliation or further contact;
43         (c) Provide first aid and obtain emergency medical assessment when
44   warranted;
45         (d) Notify the victim’s parent or guardian in accordance with Sec. 21 of this Act;


                                                                                            15
 1          (e) Notify the appropriate school safety, child-protection, regulatory, social-
 2   welfare, medical, or law-enforcement authorities as required by the nature of the
 3   incident;
 4          (f) Preserve all relevant physical, documentary, digital, and testimonial
 5   evidence;
 6          (g) Prepare a written and time-stamped incident chronology;
 7          (h) Implement an individualized victim safety and support plan;
 8          (i) Conduct a prompt, impartial, and child-sensitive investigation consistent with
 9   due process;
10          (j) Impose proportionate disciplinary, corrective, and rehabilitative measures;
11   and
12          (k) Conduct an after-action review to identify and correct supervision, policy,
13   training, communication, or institutional failures.
14
15          Internal disciplinary action, settlement, apology, restorative intervention,
16   withdrawal of complaint, or parental agreement shall not substitute for any medical,
17   reporting, evidence-preservation, child-protection, or regulatory duty imposed by this
18   Act.

20          Sec. 25. Mandatory Medical Response. – When a learner is struck in the head,
21   loses consciousness, exhibits confusion, vomiting, dizziness, bleeding, impaired
22   speech, unusual behavior, severe pain, or other indications of possible serious injury,
23   the school shall:
24          (a) Immediately remove the learner from further danger;
25          (b) Cause the learner to be assessed by qualified health personnel;
26          (c) Immediately contact emergency medical services or transport the learner to
27   an appropriate health facility when medically indicated;
28          (d) Notify the parent or guardian without delay;
29          (e) Continuously supervise the learner pending transfer to the parent, guardian,
30   or medical personnel; and
31          (f) Document all observations, actions, referrals, and communications.
32
33         The absence of visible external injury shall not be a ground for withholding
34   medical assessment when head trauma is reasonably suspected.
35
36          A learner who may have suffered serious physical injury shall not be left
37   unattended, returned to class, required to continue an activity, or released without
38   appropriate supervision, medical assessment, and documented transfer to an
39   authorized adult or health professional.
40
41          Sec. 26. Interim Protective and No-Contact Measures. – Upon receipt of a
42   credible report of serious school violence, the school shall immediately implement
43   proportionate interim protective measures, which may include:
44          (a) Physical separation of the learners involved;
45          (b) No-contact directives;
46          (c) Increased adult supervision;
47          (d) Temporary adjustment of schedules, classes, transportation, or activities;
48          (e) Restriction from identified areas or activities; and
49          (f) Other measures necessary to prevent retaliation or further harm.

 4           Sec. 27. Evidence Preservation and Incident Chronology. – Upon actual or
 5   constructive notice of serious school violence, the school shall immediately preserve
 6   all reasonably available evidence, including:
 7           (a) Closed-circuit television recordings;
 8           (b) Photographs, audio recordings, and videos;
 9           (c) Electronic communications and school-platform records;
10           (d) The object or instrument allegedly used;
11           (e) Attendance, class, duty, activity, and supervision records;
12           (f) Clinic, health, and incident records;
13           (g) Visitor and access logs;
14           (h) Witness identities and initial accounts; and
15           (i) Communication logs concerning parent and authority notification.
16
17          No relevant recording or record shall be deleted, overwritten, altered,
18   concealed, or destroyed after the school receives actual or constructive notice of an
19   incident that may result in administrative, civil, child-protection, or criminal
20   proceedings. Such evidence shall be retained for at least one (1) year and, when a
21   proceeding is pending or reasonably anticipated, until final termination thereof, subject
22   to applicable privacy and records-management laws.
23
24           For every serious school violence incident, the school shall prepare a verified
25   chronology stating the time and location of the incident; when each responsible school
26   official became aware of it; when the victim was secured and medically assessed;
27   when the parent or guardian, emergency responders, and authorities were contacted;
28   when evidence was preserved; what interim protective measures were imposed; and
29   the reasons for any material delay.
30
31           The victim’s parent or guardian shall be furnished a copy of the chronology,
32   subject to lawful redaction of confidential information concerning other minors.
33           Sec. 28. Disciplinary, Corrective, and Rehabilitative Response. – Disciplinary
34   and corrective measures for serious school violence shall be proportionate to the
35   nature and severity of the act, the injury caused or risk created, the use of a weapon
36   or dangerous object, whether the head or another vital body part was targeted,
37   repetition, planning, group participation, concealment, retaliation, prior related
38   conduct, and the age, maturity, needs, and circumstances of the learner concerned.
39
40           The school shall adopt a coordinated response that includes, as appropriate,
41   disciplinary action, behavioral intervention, counseling, parent participation, increased
42   supervision, rehabilitation, reintegration planning, and measures to protect the victim
43   and the school community.
44
45           The exemption of a child from criminal liability under applicable juvenile justice
46   laws shall not relieve the school from its duties to protect the victim, manage the safety
47   risk, provide intervention and rehabilitation, report the incident when required, and
48   prevent recurrence.

 5          Sec. 38. Rights-Based Implementation and Prohibited Practices. – In

26          Sec. 43. Designation of Lead Agencies. – The Department of Education shall
27   be the lead implementing agency for basic education institutions.

34
35         Each lead agency shall establish or designate a permanent office or unit

39          Sec. 44. Compliance, Monitoring, and Corrective Action. – The lead agencies
40   shall establish proportionate monitoring and compliance mechanisms for institutions
41   under their respective jurisdiction.
42
43          When an educational institution fails to satisfy a material requirement under this
44   Act, the appropriate lead agency shall ordinarily provide:

                                                                                           24
 1         (a) Written notice of the deficiency;
 2         (b) Technical assistance where appropriate;
 3         (c) A reasonable corrective-action period based on the seriousness of the risk
 4   and the resources required; and
 5         (d) Follow-up assessment.
 6
 7         Immediate interim protective measures may be required when a deficiency
 8   presents a clear and serious danger.
 9
10           Administrative sanctions may be imposed only in accordance with existing laws,
11   applicable regulatory authority, due process, and the implementing rules and
12   regulations. No public school head or personnel shall be held administratively liable
13   for the non-completion of a capital-intensive infrastructure requirement when the
14   necessary funds, procurement authority, or implementing support have not been made
15   available, unless the person concerned misused available funds, refused without
16   lawful cause to implement feasible interim measures, or committed another actionable
17   violation.
18
19          The implementing rules and regulations shall identify, subject to existing laws
20   and due process, the following as material administrative violations:
21          (a) Failure to provide required emergency medical assistance;
22          (b) Unjustified delay in parent or guardian notification;
23          (c) Deliberate failure to report serious school violence;
24          (d) Destruction, alteration, concealment, or unlawful withholding of relevant
25   evidence;
26          (e) Retaliation against a victim, reporter, or witness;
27          (f) Falsification or material omission in an incident report or chronology;
28          (g) Coercing a victim or family into withdrawal, waiver, settlement, mediation,
29   or reconciliation;
30          (h) Imposition of a sham or nominal sanction to avoid regulatory reporting or
31   review;
32          (i) Failure to implement reasonable interim protective measures; and
33          (j) Repeated failure to act on documented bullying or violence.

 4          Sec. 46. Research and Periodic Review. – The National Safe Schools Council

 7   technology, school climate, and recovery.

16          Sec. 47. Construction with Existing Laws. – This Act shall be implemented in
17   harmony with existing laws. Nothing in this Act shall be interpreted to reduce any
18   higher standard or stronger protection established under existing law.

20           Sec. 48. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation

23   Development Authority, and other concerned agencies.
