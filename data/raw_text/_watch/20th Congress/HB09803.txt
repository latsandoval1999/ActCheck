                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Every child deserves the chance to grow up safe, healthy, educated, and hopeful. Yet
many Filipino children continue to suffer from poverty, hunger, neglect, violence, and
lack of support. Too often, children who stop attending school or struggle at home are
only noticed when they are already in serious trouble. This bill seeks to strengthen
youth development and child welfare programs so that children receive help early,
before they fall deeper into crisis.

Although existing laws already protect children, many communities still lack enough
counselors, social workers, nutrition programs, and support services. As a result,
many young people become disconnected from school, exposed to abuse, or pushed
toward conflict with the law. This measure strengthens coordination among schools,
barangays, local governments, and national agencies to ensure that children are
guided, supported, and protected rather than neglected or punished.

The bill promotes practical and compassionate solutions such as school attendance
monitoring, feeding and nutrition programs, counseling services, and community-
based intervention for children at risk. By helping children stay in school, addressing
family and mental health concerns early, and providing stronger support systems,
this measure gives every child a better chance to succeed and build a brighter future.

SECTION 1. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to recognize the vital
role of the youth in nation-building and to promote and protect their physical, moral,
spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being. The State shall likewise defend the right
of children to assistance, including proper care and nutrition, and to special
protection from all forms of neglect, abuse, cruelty, exploitation, and other conditions
prejudicial to their development.

Consistent with these constitutional mandates, and in furtherance of the State policy
of special protection for children, this Act strengthens school- and community-based
programs that prevent children from being at risk and from coming in conflict with
the law and ensures timely referral to appropriate social welfare interventions.

This Act shall be implemented in a manner consistent with the restorative and child-
centered framework of the juvenile justice system, including the coordinated inter-
agency implementation mechanism under the Juvenile Justice and Welfare Council
(JJWC).

SECTION 2. School Attendance and Truancy Prevention; Designation of
Barangay Truancy Focal Person. —

   (a) Designation. Every barangay shall designate a Barangay Truancy Focal
       Person from among the members of the Sangguniang Barangay or any
       qualified barangay personnel, who shall coordinate school attendance
       interventions in partnership with the school head and the Barangay Council
       for the Protection of Children (BCPC).

   (b) Functions. The Barangay Truancy Focal Person shall:

       1. Coordinate with schools on reports of habitual absenteeism;
       2. Conduct home visits jointly with the Barangay Social Welfare and
          Development Officer under Section 5 of this Act;
       3. Facilitate family conferencing and referral to community-based services;
          and
       4. Submit periodic reports to the City/Municipal Social Welfare and
          Development Office (C/MSWDO) and to the Local Council for the
          Protection of Children (LCPC), consistent with local child protection
          planning.

   (c) Definition of truancy. For purposes of this Act, “truancy” refers to habitual
       absenteeism as defined in DepEd rules and policies, and in the absence
       thereof, absence without valid cause for at least five (5) school days within
       a month, whether consecutive or not.

   (d) Referral and case management. A child identified as truant shall be referred
       to the C/MSWDO for assessment and case management. Referral
       to alternative care (including foster care or licensed child-caring
       institutions) shall be governed by existing child welfare laws and DSWD
       regulations, and shall not be automatic or punitive.

SECTION 3. Local Truancy and Child Attendance Monitoring Desk (TCAMD). —

   (a) Creation. The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), in
       coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd) and the Department
       of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), shall issue guidelines for the
       creation, in every city and municipality, of a Truancy and Child Attendance
       Monitoring Desk (TCAMD) within the LCPC Secretariat or such office as the
       LGU may designate.

                                                                           4 | Page
   (b) Functions. The TCAMD shall:

       1. Serve as the LGU’s consolidated data and referral desk for school
          attendance concerns;
       2. Ensure privacy and confidentiality of child data;
       3. Coordinate referrals to social welfare services; and
       4. Support the LCPC in planning and budgeting child protection and juvenile
          intervention measures.

   (c) Coordination on parental neglect. When warranted by social case study and
       after appropriate intervention efforts, the C/MSWDO may recommend to
       appropriate authorities action under applicable laws on parental neglect and
       child protection, consistent with due process and the best interests of the
       child.

SECTION 4. Guidance Counselors in Schools. —

   (a) Minimum standard; phased compliance. DepEd, in coordination with the
       Civil Service Commission and relevant professional regulatory bodies, shall
       set minimum guidance counselor-to-learner ratios and implement a
       phased compliance plan prioritizing schools with high-risk indicators
       (dropout, violence, substance abuse, bullying, and the like).

   (b) Functions. Guidance counselors shall provide counseling and developmental
      guidance services and coordinate with child protection committees and local
      social welfare offices for early intervention.

SECTION 5. Strengthening Social Welfare Presence at the Barangay Level. —

   (a) Barangay Social Welfare and Development Focal Person. Every barangay
       shall have a designated Barangay Social Welfare and Development Focal
       Person, preferably a registered social worker, who shall operate under the
       technical supervision of the C/MSWDO.

   (b) Functions. The Barangay Social Welfare and Development Focal Person shall
       conduct home visits, counseling, child-rights education, and case conferencing,
       and shall assist in community-based intervention for children at risk and
       children in conflict with the law.

   (c) Compensation. LGUs shall provide honoraria or compensation subject to
       applicable budgeting, civil service, and auditing rules.

SECTION 6. Expanded School-Based Feeding and Nutrition Support. —

   (a) Program. DepEd shall strengthen and expand school-based feeding and
       nutrition support, prioritizing undernourished and food-insecure learners,
       consistent with available appropriations and program standards.

   (b) Food donations. Donations of apparently wholesome food to schools for
       feeding programs shall be governed by the Food Donation Act and its
       implementing rules.

SECTION 7. School Health Services (Nurses and Dentists). —

   (a) Standard-setting. The Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
       DepEd, shall establish minimum standards for school health staffing and
       service delivery, with phased implementation prioritizing high-need areas.

   (b) Employment arrangements. LGUs and relevant agencies may adopt shared
       service arrangements consistent with local autonomy and existing public
       health staffing mechanisms.

SECTION 8. Child Witness Assistance. — The Department of Justice (DOJ), in
coordination with DSWD, shall strengthen child witness assistance throughout
criminal proceedings, including psychosocial support, referrals, and coordination
with schools and local social welfare offices, consistent with applicable rules and
confidentiality protections.

SECTION 9. Juvenile Reformatory Centers. — The DSWD, in coordination with the
Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG), shall establish, fund, and
manage 24-hour child-caring institution providing residential care for children in
conflict with the law who commits parricide, murder, infanticide, kidnapping, and

or rape, destructive arson, rape, or carnapping where the driver or occupant is killed
or raped or offenses under Republic Act No. 9165, otherwise known as the
“Comprehensive Dangerous Drugs Act of 2002,” punishable by more than twelve (12)
years of imprisonment.

SECTION 20. Joint Congressional Oversight Committee. — There is hereby created
a Joint Congressional Oversight Committee to oversee, monitor, and evaluate the
implementation of this Act and other related laws. The Oversight Committee shall be
composed of five (5) members each from the Senate and from the House of
Representatives, including the Chairpersons of the Senate Committee on Justice and
Human Rights, to act as Chairperson of the Senate Panel, and the House Committee
on Justice, to act as Chairperson of the House Panel. The membership of the
Committee for every House shall have at least one (1) minority member.

The Oversight Committee shall conduct a mandatory review of this Act every three
(3) years from its approval.

The DSWD and DepEd are required to report to the Oversight Committee, copy
furnished the Senate President and the Speaker of the House of Representatives, on a
monthly basis relevant data on school enrollment.

SECTION 21. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.
