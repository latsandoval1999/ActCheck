                           AN ACT
GOVERNING THE REGISTRATION OF SHIPS FOR INTERNATIONAL TRADE
     UNDER THE PHILIPPINE FLAG AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

      International maritime transport is fundamental to the Philippine
economy and to global commerce. The United Nations Conference on Trade and
Development (UNCTAD) reports that more than 80 percent of the volume of
international trade in goods is carried by sea, with the proportion generally
higher for developing countries.1 The Philippines likewise has a substantial
maritime labor force: according to the Maritime Industry Authority’s 2024
Statistical Report, 504,057 Filipino seafarers were deployed in 2024, following
578,626 in 2023 and 385,239 in 2022.2

       The importance of the maritime industry in creating employment is not
localized in any specific part of the country. For example, Quezon City is well-
positioned to benefit from a strong maritime industry because it has an
established base of maritime and engineering education that can supply skilled
workers not only for international shipping but also for shipbuilding, ship repair,
maintenance, and related maritime industries.

       As of May 2025, MARINA’s list of recognized Maritime Higher Education
Institutions identifies two institutions in Quezon City offering Bachelor of
Science in Marine Transportation (BSMT) and/or Bachelor of Science in Marine

Engineering (BSMarE).3 This maritime education base is complemented by a
much broader engineering ecosystem in the city, with higher-education
institutions offering fields directly relevant to shipbuilding and maritime
industrial activity, including mechanical, electrical, electronics, civil, computer,
industrial, environmental, and sanitary engineering. By attracting more vessels,
shipowners, maritime enterprises, and potentially shipbuilding and repair
investments to the Philippine registry, the proposed law can therefore create a
larger local market for Quezon City’s maritime and engineering graduates,
strengthen technical employment opportunities, and encourage the development
of ancillary maritime businesses in the city.

      A stronger Philippine flag registry can therefore complement the country’s
established pool of maritime professionals by encouraging more ships and
maritime business activities to be placed under Philippine jurisdiction.

       Despite the country’s strong position as a source of maritime labor, the
benefits associated with ship ownership, ship registration, management,
financing, and related maritime services can be further developed. The proposed
measure addresses this gap by creating a dedicated legal framework for ships
engaged in international trade under the Philippine flag. It establishes a
comprehensive Philippine Ship Registry under MARINA, provides for permanent,
provisional, and temporary registration, and expressly permits the registration
or reflagging of qualifying foreign-registered ships subject to ownership, safety,
environmental,     crew-welfare,    insurance,    and    international-convention
requirements. It also provides a framework for recognizing and recording ship
mortgages, maritime liens, and encumbrances, thereby improving legal certainty
for shipowners, financiers, charterers, and other maritime stakeholders.

       To make the Philippine registry commercially competitive while advancing
national objectives, the bill proposes targeted fiscal and regulatory incentives.
These include a seven-year income tax holiday, a five-percent special corporate
income tax rate thereafter, an optional tonnage-tax regime, exemptions from
specified import duties and taxes, VAT incentives, and personnel tax incentives
for Philippine-registered ships engaged in international trade.4 Additional
incentives are provided for ships employing Filipino officers and crew and for
certified “Green Ships,” while government financial institutions are directed to
develop financing programs for MARINA-accredited maritime enterprises. At the

      The measure thus seeks to combine competitiveness with responsible flag-
State regulation, allowing the Philippines to capture a greater share of the
economic activity generated by international shipping while sustaining the
country’s role as a major provider of qualified maritime personnel.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly

                           AN ACT
GOVERNING THE REGISTRATION OF SHIPS FOR INTERNATIONAL TRADE
     UNDER THE PHILIPPINE FLAG AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine
International Ship Registration and Fiscal Incentives Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to strengthen and modernize the Philippine ship registry for international trade
by establishing a competitive, transparent, efficient, and internationally
compliant registration system that will attract Philippine and foreign shipowners
to register under the Philippine flag; promote the growth and competitiveness of
the domestic maritime industry; generate investment, employment, and
government revenues; expand opportunities for Filipino seafarers; and ensure
that Philippine-registered ships operate in accordance with applicable national
laws and international standards on safety, security, environmental protection,
labor, and maritime liability.

Toward these ends, the State shall provide a predictable regulatory framework,
appropriate fiscal and financing incentives, streamlined registration and
administrative procedures, and effective mechanisms for the protection of
maritime liens, mortgages, and other legitimate interests, while strengthening
the Maritime Industry Authority (MARINA) in the exercise of the Philippines’
responsibilities as a flag State.
SECTION 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all ships registered under the
Philippine flag for international shipping operations.

SECTION 4. Implementing Agency. – The Maritime Industry Authority
(MARINA) shall be vested with powers and duties to effectively implement and
enforce the provisions of this Act and its implementing rules and regulations.

SECTION 5. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, terms not defined
herein shall, unless otherwise indicated herein, have the meanings ascribed to
such terms under relevant international instruments:

   1. Arrest refers to any detention or restriction on removal of a ship by order
      of a Court to secure a maritime claim, but does not include the seizure of
      a ship in execution or satisfaction of a judgment or other enforceable
      instrument;
   2. Bareboat charter refers to a contract for the lease of a ship, for a stipulated
      period of time, by virtue of which the lessee has complete possession and
      control of the ship, including the right to appoint the master and crew of
      the ship, for the duration of the lease;
   3. Bareboat charterer or charterer may be used interchangeably and refers to
      any person, commercial partnership or corporation which bareboat
      charters a Philippine flag vessel from another Philippine national, or a
      vessel of compatible foreign registry, for its own use;
   4. Certificate of Philippine Registry refers to the document of registration of a
      ship registered in accordance with Section 21 of this Act;
   5. Claimant refers to any person asserting a maritime claim;
   6. Foreign-registered ship refers to a ship which is registered under the laws
      of a country other than the Philippines and which is authorized to fly the
      flag of that country;
   7. Foreign registry refers to the registry of ships of a country other than the
      Philippines;
   8. Maritime claim refers to any of the claims provided in this Act;
   9. Maritime lien refers to a charge that attaches to a ship which arises from a
      maritime claim against the ship, its owners, charterers, managers, or
      operators that results in a demand for indemnity or remuneration from
      the ship or its owners, charterers, managers, or operators, and is
      enforceable by judicial action;
   10.       Mortgagee refers to the person, whether natural or juridical, in
      whose favor a ship mortgage is created to secure a principal obligation;
   11.      Mortgagor refers to a ship owner who constitutes a ship mortgage
      over a vessel or a vessel under construction registered in its name in order
      to secure the fulfillment of a principal obligation;
   12.      Recognized Organization refers to an organization that has been
      assessed by the MARINA and found to comply with the Recognized
      Organization Code.

                                    TITLE II
                                 SHIP REGISTRY

SECTION 6. The Philippine Ship Registry. – The State, through the MARINA,
shall maintain a Registry which shall confer nationality, exercise its jurisdiction,
protect and enforce private rights, and undertake the national and international
responsibilities of a flag State over all ships engaged in international trade flying
the Philippine flag.

The Registry shall hold the Register of Ships and the Record of Ship Mortgages
and Encumbrances, which shall be maintained by the MARINA in accordance
with the provisions of this Act, and the rules and regulations that may be issued
pursuant thereto.

                                 CHAPTER I
                           REGISTRATION OF SHIPS

SECTION 7. One Ship, One Registry Rule. – A ship shall be registered with
only one ship registry at any one time. Before entering the ship in the Register,
the MARINA shall ensure that the ship has been deleted from its previous
registry.

In the case of a foreign-registered ship under a bareboat charter, the MARINA
shall ensure that the right to fly the flag of the former flag State is suspended for
the duration of its registration with the Philippine registry. A Certificate of
Registry shall be issued only upon the production of evidence indicating the
suspension of its previous registration and nationality under the former flag
State, and indicating the particulars of any registered encumbrances entered in
the previous registry.

It is the duty of the owner, master, or agent of every ship in the Register to inform
the MARINA of any change in the facts pertaining to the ship and entered therein.

SECTION 8. Documentation. – The MARINA shall issue and recognize, in
appropriate form, certificates clearly indicating whether a vessel is registered and
ensure such documentation meets all applicable national and international
requirements.

SECTION 9. Register of Ships. – The MARINA shall establish, maintain, and
update a comprehensive Register of Ships for the Philippines which shall
constitute the official public record of all ships registered under the Philippine
flag in international voyages.

SECTION 10. Contents of the Register. – The Register of Ships shall contain,
at minimum, the following information for each registered vessel:

   1. The name, official number, and call sign of the vessel, if assigned;

   2. The port of registry;

   3. The type, dimensions, tonnage, and propulsion of the vessel;

   4. The date and place of built and the name of the builder;

   5. Full particulars of the ownership including the name, nationality, and
      address of the registered owner;

   6. Full particulars of any bareboat charter arrangement;

   7. The date and place of registration and any subsequent changes;

   8. Records of all mortgages, liens, and other encumbrances affecting the
      vessel, if any; and

   9. The registration status as domestic or international pursuant to the dual
      registry system.

SECTION 11. Digital Format. – The Register shall be maintained in secure
digital format, with appropriate backup systems, and shall incorporate
necessary technological safeguards to ensure data integrity and protection
against unauthorized access or alteration.

SECTION 12. Public Access. – The Register of Ships shall be open to public
inspection during ordinary business hours, in the offices of the MARINA or its
official website. All documents and information provided in connection with the
registration of a ship are considered public documents. Upon request and
payment of reasonable fees, authenticated copies of any document or instrument
required to be filed under this Act and pertinent rules and regulations, or
extracts thereof, shall be furnished to any person, subject to the requirements
of Republic Act No. 10173 or the Data Privacy Act of 2021 and provisions of
Executive Order No. 02 or the Freedom of Information and its subsequent
amendments.

SECTION 13. Certification. – The MARINA shall be authorized to issue certified
extracts from the Register, which shall constitute prima facie evidence of the
facts contained therein.

SECTION 14. Updates. – The owner, operator, master, or authorized
representative of a registered vessel shall promptly notify MARINA of any change
in the registered particulars of the vessel within thirty (30) days of such change.

SECTION 15. Who may Register. – Any person, whether an individual,
corporation, or partnership, including corporations duly organized under
Philippine law that are one hundred percent (100%) foreign-owned, subject to
compliance with all applicable laws, rules, and regulations, may register
convention-sized ships, except transient foreign flag ships subject to such
regulations as the MARINA may prescribe, with the Philippine registry: Provided
that they meet the conditions for the grant of registration imposed by this Act
and the regulations pursuant hereto.

SECTION 16. Effects of Registration. – Any ship registered under the
Philippine registry shall be entitled to the following:

   1. Conferment of the nationality and status of a Philippine ship;

   2. Entitlement to the protection of the Philippine flag;

   3. Entitlement to the right to invoke the diplomatic, consular, and naval
      protection of Philippine authorities and the protection of the flag of the
      Philippines;

   4. Imposition of the duty to fly the Philippine flag;

   5. Obligation to abide by all applicable laws, decrees, orders, rules, and
      regulations of the Philippines;

   6. Entitlement to the privilege to engage, consistently with the limitations
      provided by law, in international trade in accordance with corollary
      permits and certificates that may be issued to the ship; and

   7. Entitlement to the right to invoke the exercise of jurisdiction and control
      by Philippine government authorities over all persons found on board the
      ship, or over any incident involving the penal or disciplinary responsibility
      of the master or any member of the crew whether the same arises from a
     crime or quasi-delict committed on board the ship or from any collision or
     other incident of navigation concerning the ship.

SECTION 17. Conditions for Registration. – No ship shall be registered unless
the following conditions are present, as determined by the MARINA under this
Act and regulations issued related thereto:

  1. Proof of Ownership. There must be satisfactory proof of ownership and/or
     management by qualified persons.

  2. Seaworthiness. A ship must comply with the general requirements for age,
     safety, security, navigation, manning, and protection and preservation of
     the marine environment in accordance with the national and international
     standards for ships.

  3. Manning. A ship must be manned by a crew with such level of skills and
     competencies as may be necessary in accordance with applicable
     international maritime conventions, rules, and standards as well as
     national law and regulations.

  4. Labor and social conditions. A ship must meet the labor and social
     standards required by applicable international labor conventions, rules,
     and standards as implemented by the competent authority.

  5. Management. A ship must be managed by a person or entity that is under
     the effective jurisdiction of the State.

  6. Payment of fees and dues. The applicant for ship registration must pay the
     corresponding registration fees and tonnage dues as may be imposed by
     the MARINA.

SECTION 18. Types of Registration. – Registration of a ship may be
permanent, provisional, or temporary in nature.

Permanent Registration. Except as provided in the succeeding sections, the
registration of a ship and issuance of a Certificate of Philippine Registry is
deemed to be permanent until the ship is actually deleted from the Philippine
Register of Ships;

Provisional Registration. Ships constructed and/or acquired abroad, before
being brought to the Philippines, may be issued a Provisional Certificate of
Philippine Registry and a provisional tonnage certificate for conduction
purposes.
Temporary Registration. The following may be issued a Temporary Certificate
of Philippine Registry:

   1. Any ship about to be built, or under construction in the Philippines; and
   2. Any ship owned and/or operated by an entity duly registered with the
      Board of Investment (BOI) under pertinent investment laws, rules, and
      regulations, to be used exclusively for the purpose of transporting raw
      materials and/or finished products within the Philippine waters as an
      essential element and operation in the manufacturing, processing, or other
      business activity or project registered by such entity with the BOI:
      Provided, that such temporary registration applies only for the duration of
      such business activity or project, and shall not exceed one year, renewable
      for another year. Provided further, that the type of ship used or service
      undertaken cannot be provided by a Philippine ship owner.
   3. Any ship that is operated and managed under a bareboat charter.
      Provided, that in case of a foreign-registered ship bareboat-chartered by a
      qualified person for operation in Philippine waters, the ship may be
      temporarily registered with the Registry for the duration of the charter.
      Provided further, that for the duration of temporary registration with the
      Philippines, any registration with a foreign flag State is suspended.
      Provided finally, that no ship under a bareboat charter shall be registered
      if, after the effectivity of its charter, it has undergone extensive structural
      modification in a manner that affects its safety and seaworthiness.

SECTION 19. Transfer or Re-flagging of Ships. – Any foreign-registered ship
may be registered under the Philippine flag, provided that the owner or operator
submits to the MARINA complete documentation establishing ownership or title
to the ship, evidence of deletion from the previous registry, and proof of
compliance with applicable international maritime conventions; provided further
that the ship passes inspection by authorized Philippine maritime authorities
and/or recognized organizations, confirming adherence to safety, security,
environmental, and crew welfare standards as prescribed by Philippine law and
international agreements to which the Philippines is a party.

The owner or operator shall demonstrate financial capacity to maintain the ship
in seaworthy condition and shall provide evidence of adequate marine insurance
coverage.

Upon compliance with these requirements, the MARINA shall issue a Certificate
of Philippine Registry, and the ship shall thereafter be considered a Philippine-
registered ship entitled to the privileges and subject to the obligations attendant
thereto under Philippine law and applicable international conventions.

SECTION 20. Effect of Charter Agreement. – Ships of a foreign registry that
are covered by a bareboat charter may be registered under the Philippine flag.
Provided that their registration under the foreign flag of primary registration
shall not be abandoned, but shall be deemed suspended for the duration of the
ship’s registration under the Philippine flag.

When a ship of Philippine registry is bareboat chartered out, the registration
shall be temporarily suspended from the Philippine registry.

SECTION 21. Issuance of Certificate of Philippine Registry. – A Certificate
of Philippine Registry shall only be approved and issued upon proper evaluation
that all the conditions and documents required and mandated for registration
under this Act or rules and regulations issued hereto have been complied with.
The MARINA, its recognized organizations, or its deputized representative, shall
inspect the ship and its documents, and ensure that it is qualified for registration
under this Act.

A Certificate of Philippine Registry shall be continuously renewed for a similar
period, provided that all the requirements for registration or its renewal are met.

In the case of a bareboat chartered ship, the Certificate of Philippine Registry
shall be valid for not more than five (5) years or the period of the bareboat
charter, whichever is shorter.

SECTION 22. Notice of Any Change in the Ship. – The owner, master, or
authorized representative of any ship registered under this Act shall notify
MARINA, in writing, of any modification to the ship within fifteen (15) days prior
to the commencement of such modification, or immediately upon the knowledge
of such modification.

SECTION 23. Suspension, Cancellation or Revocation of Ship Registration.
– The MARINA shall, motu proprio or upon verified complaint, after due notice
and hearing, revoke the Certificate of Philippine Registry and order the deletion
from the Registry if any ship is found to be in violation of the conditions of the
ship registration set forth by the MARINA through implementing rules and
regulations or in its subsequent issuances.

SECTION 24. Deletion of Ships. – A ship that is or has been registered in
another State shall not be eligible for permanent registration in the Philippines,
unless:
   1. A certificate has been issued by the former flag State to the effect that the
      ship has been deleted; or

   2. A certificate has been issued by the former flag State to the effect that the
      ship has been deleted on the day such new registration is effected.

Except in cases of forced sale, a Philippine-registered ship shall not be deleted
without the written consent of all holders of registered mortgages.

If the deletion of the ship is obligatory in accordance with this section, all holders
of registered mortgages shall be notified by the MARINA of the pending deletion
in order to enable such holders to take appropriate action to protect their
respective interests.

If the consent of the holders is not obtained, deletion shall not be implemented
earlier than three (3) months after notice to such holders of registered mortgages
is given.

SECTION 25. Fees and Charges. – For purposes of this Act, fees and charges
shall be fixed in such reasonable amounts as determined by the MARINA.

                            CHAPTER II
           RECORD OF SHIP MORTGAGES AND ENCUMBRANCES

SECTION 26. Record of Ship Mortgages and Encumbrances. – The MARINA
shall maintain a Record of Ship Mortgages and Encumbrances containing the
details and documents regarding all transfers, mortgages, encumbrances, or
other documents proving or affecting the title to any ship registered in the
Register of Ships. All documents, certificates, receipts, or acknowledgments
canceling or satisfying any such obligation in whole or in part shall likewise be
recorded. The date and time of recording shall be expressly stated.

A ship’s electronic certificate bearing security features, including all the records
of the ship mortgages and encumbrances, as well as electronic recordings of
maritime liens, shall be accepted by the banks; Provided, That it complies with
rules and regulations set forth by the BSP and the Electronic Commerce Act of
2000.

SECTION 27. Effect of Recording. – In order to be valid and enforceable, every
mortgage, encumbrance, or other right over a Philippine-registered ship, whether
permanent or temporary in nature, in favor of a person other than the registered
ship owner or his agent, must be entered into the record. A mortgage registered
prior in time shall have preference over those registered later in time.
SECTION 28. Rights Over Ships Under Construction. – Any right in respect
to a ship under construction in the Philippines may be entered and recorded
after the keel has been laid or equivalent construction work has been performed.

Ships under construction contracted by a Philippine national may be registered
in accordance with the provisions of this Act. For the ships to be the subject of
registration under this Act, it is necessary that the ship’s keel must have been
laid and a keel number assigned by the shipyard at the time of application.

                                  TITLE III
                             FISCAL INCENTIVES

                             CHAPTER I
                 INCENTIVES FOR FILIPINO SHIPOWNERS

SECTION 29. Tax Incentives for Registered Ships. - Any ship registered under
the Philippine flag pursuant to this Act shall be entitled to the following tax
incentives:

   1. Income Tax Holiday. A MARINA-accredited shipowner, operator,
      manager, or bareboat charterer shall be granted a seven (7) year income
      tax holiday from the date of registration, during which the shipowner,
      operator, manager, or bareboat charterer shall be exempt from income tax
      on earnings derived from the operation of the registered ship;
   2. Special Corporate Income Tax Rate. After the expiration of the income
      tax holiday, a special corporate income tax rate of five percent (5%) on
      gross income earned from the operation of the registered ship, in lieu of
      the regular corporate income tax rate;
   3. Tonnage Tax Option. In lieu of income tax, MARINA-accredited
      shipowners, operators, and ship managers may opt for an annual tonnage
      tax based on the net tonnage of the ship, computed at rates to be
      prescribed by the Secretary of Finance in consultation with the Bureau of
      Internal Revenue and MARINA.

SECTION 30. Import Duty and Tax Exemptions. - The following shall be
exempt from import duties, taxes, and other charges:

   1. The importation of ships for registration under the Philippine flag;
   2. Spare parts, engines, machinery and equipment directly used for the
      construction and repair, steel plates, and other materials reasonably
      needed for the construction, repair, refurbishment, maintenance, or
      operation of registered ships. If the above machinery, equipment,
      materials, and spare parts are used for other purpose or sold to non-tax-
      exempt persons or entities, the corresponding duties and taxes shall be
      paid by the original importer;
   3. Navigation and communication equipment, safety equipment, and
      pollution prevention devices required for the registered ship;
   4. Fuel, lubricants, and other consumables for the exclusive use of registered
      ships engaged in international voyages.

SECTION 31. Value-Added Tax (VAT) Incentives. – Philippine-Registered ships
shall enjoy, within ten (10) years from the effectivity of this Act, the following VAT
incentives:

   1. VAT Zero-Rating on the purchase of goods and services directly related to
      ship operations;

   2. VAT Exemption on the importation of items covered under Section 4 of this
      Act;

   3. VAT Zero-Rating on charter fees, freight charges, and other income derived
      from ship operations in international trade.

SECTION 32. Personnel Tax Incentives. - All officers and crew members
employed on Philippine-registered ships engaged in international trade shall be
entitled to:

   1. Income Tax Exemption on salaries, wages, and other compensation
      received by reason of their employment on the registered ship;

   2. Withholding Tax Exemption on payments made to such personnel by the
      shipowner, operator, or manager.

SECTION 33. Registration Fee Incentives. - MARINA may provide reduced
registration fees for ships that:

   1. Are newly built and meet international environmental standards
      consistent with Section 9 hereof;

   2. Employ one hundred percent (100%) Filipino officers and crew; or

   3. Are engaged in routes that serve the national interest as determined by
      MARINA.
SECTION 34. Green Ship Registry Fee Incentives. - Philippine-registered
ships engaged in international trade certified as “Green Ships” shall be entitled
to avail the following registration benefits:

   1. Waived registration fees for the first five (5) years of registration;

   2. Priority processing of all regulatory applications and renewals; and

   3. Reduced inspection fees for safety and environmental compliance
      inspections.

Philippine-registered ships shall be entitled to tax deduction to be determined by
the MARINA, for skills training and research and development for green jobs
creation in conserving and preserving the environment as per RA 10771 or the
Green Job Act.

                              CHAPTER II
                  INCENTIVES FOR FOREIGN SHIPOWNERS

SECTION 35. Annual Tonnage Fee for Transfer or Reflagging of ships. - An
annual tonnage fee shall be paid by the foreign shipowner, manager or bareboat
charterer of the ship in order for the issuance and continued possession of a
valid and subsisting Certificate of Philippine Registry. The annual tonnage fee
shall be paid to MARINA and computed at the rate to be determined by the
Department of Finance, in consultation with the MARINA. The shipowner,
manager, or agent shall, however, pay the tonnage fee in Philippine Peso.

SECTION 36. Additional Incentives for Foreign-Owned Ships. - Foreign-
owned ships registered under the Philippine flag that employ full Filipino officers
and crew shall be entitled to an additional two (2) year extension of the income
tax holiday provided under Section 3(a).

                                 CHAPTER III
                            NON-TRANSFERABILITY

SECTION 37. Transferability and Assignment. - The incentives granted under
this Act shall be personal to the registered shipowner and shall not be
transferable or assignable to any other person or entity, except in cases of
legitimate sale or transfer of ship ownership with prior approval of MARINA.
                                 CHAPTER IV
                                USE OF FUNDS

SECTION 38. Use of funds. - Fifty per centum (50%) of all moneys earned by
MARINA from the collection/levy of any and all such fees, charges, dues,
assessments, fines and penalties it may be empowered to collect/levy under this
Act, and other existing and future laws shall be used solely to fund the promotion
and expansion of the international shipping industry. The utilization of any
funds coming from the collection and/or levy of MARINA shall be subject to the
examination of the Commission on Audit.

                                CHAPTER V
                           FINANCING PROGRAMS

SECTION 39. Overseas Shipping Financing Programs. – Government
financial institutions shall develop attractive financing programs for MARINA-
accredited shipping companies, owners, operators, and other maritime
registered enterprises, and shall provide financial assistance, training, and
consultancy services that will promote, develop, and foster a sustainable and
progressive maritime industry.

                               TITLE IV
                  MARITIME LIENS AND ENCUMBRANCES

                                 CHAPTER I
                               MARITIME LIENS

SECTION 40. Maritime Liens. – The maritime liens shall be attached to the
ship notwithstanding any change in ownership, legislation, or flag, except in case
of forced sale of the ship.

SECTION 41. Claims Secured by Maritime Liens. – Each of the following
claims against the owner, demise charterer, manager or operator of the vessel
shall be secured by a maritime lien on the vessel:

claims for wages and other sums due to the master, officers and other members
of the vessel's complement in respect of their employment on the vessel,
including costs of repatriation and social insurance contributions payable on
their behalf;

   1. claims in respect of loss of life or personal injury occurring, whether on
      land or on water, in direct connection with the operation of the vessel;
   2. claims for reward for the salvage of the vessel;

   3. claims for port, canal, and other waterway dues and pilotage dues;

   4. claims based on tort arising out of physical loss or damage caused by the
      operation of the vessel other than loss of or damage to cargo, containers
      and passengers' effects carried on the vessel.

SECTION 42. Claims Not Secured by Maritime Liens. – No maritime lien shall
attach to a vessel to secure claims as set out in subparagraphs (b) and (e) of
Section 41 which arise out of or result from:

   1. Damage in connection with the carriage of oil or other hazardous or
      noxious substances by sea for which compensation is payable to the
      claimants pursuant to international conventions or national law providing
      for strict liability and compulsory insurance or other means of securing
      the claims; or

   2. The radioactive properties or a combination of radioactive properties with
      toxic, explosive or other hazardous properties of nuclear fuel or of
      radioactive products or waste.

SECTION 43. Priority of Maritime Liens. – The maritime liens set out in
Section 30 shall take priority over registered mortgages, "hypotheques" and
charges, and no other claim shall take priority over such maritime liens or over
such mortgages, "hypotheques" or charges, except as provided in paragraphs 3
and 4 of Section 44.

The maritime liens set out in Section 41 shall rank in the order listed, provided
however that maritime liens securing claims for reward for the salvage of the
vessel shall take priority over all other maritime liens which have attached to the
vessel prior to the time when the operations giving rise to the said liens were
performed.

The maritime liens set out in each of subparagraphs (a), (b), (d) and (e) of Section
41 shall rank equally as between themselves.

The maritime liens securing claims for reward for the salvage of the vessel shall
rank in the inverse order of the time when the claims secured thereby accrued.
Such claims shall be deemed to have accrued on the date on which each salvage
operation was terminated.
SECTION 44. Maritime Liens in case of Salvage, Wreck Removal, or
General Average Acts. – Maritime liens securing claims for salvage, wreck
removal, and contribution to general average shall take priority over all other
maritime liens which have been attached to a ship prior to the time when the
operations giving rise to the said liens were performed.

Such liens shall rank in the inverse order of the time when the claims secured
thereby accrued. Claims for contribution to general average shall be deemed to
have accrued on the date on which the general average act was performed, while
claims for salvage shall be deemed to have accrued on the date on which the
salvage operation was terminated.

In the event of a forced sale of the wreck of a ship, following its removal by a
government accredited salvage company by a government-accredited salvage
company, the costs of such removal shall be paid out of the proceeds of the sale
before all other claims secured by maritime lien on the ship.

If at the time of the sale, the ship is in the possession of a ship-builder or a ship-
repairer who enjoys a right of retention, the latter shall surrender possession of
the ship to the purchaser but is entitled to obtain satisfaction of a claim out of
the proceeds of the sale after the satisfaction of the claims of holders of maritime
liens mentioned in Section 41 and in this section.

SECTION 45. Extinguishment of Maritime Liens. – Maritime liens shall be
extinguished after a period of one (1) year from the time when the claims secured
thereby arose, unless prior to the expiration of such period, the ship has been
arrested and the ownership thereof was transferred through a forced sale ordered
by a competent court or tribunal.

The period of one (1) year shall not be subject to suspension or interruption;
provided, that the time shall not run if the person enforcing the lien is prevented
from arresting and the ownership thereof transferred through a forced sale
ordered by a competent court or tribunal.

Maritime liens that arose before the effectivity of this Act shall be extinguished
one (1) year after the date of such effectivity.

SECTION 46. Retention Rights for Shipbuilders and Ship Repairers. – A
shipbuilder who is in possession of a ship or ship that is under construction,
and who wants to secure claims arising out of the construction of, or other work
carried out in relation to the ship, shall have the right to retain such ship or ship
under construction, without the need of a court order.
However, when in possession of a ship or ship that is under repair, a ship
repairer shall need to secure a court order in order to have the right to retain
such ship or ship under repair for the purposes of claiming a claim arising out
of the repair of, including the modification, conversion, or reconstruction of the
ship, or other work carried out in relation to the ship, affected during such
possession.

SECTION 47. Extinguishment of Rights of Retention. – The rights of retention
under the preceding section shall be extinguished when:

   1. The claim in respect of which said right has been exercised is met or is
      otherwise discharged;

   2. The ship ceases to be in the possession of the ship-builder or ship repairer
      as the case may be; or

   3. The competent court orders the release of the ship.

SECTION 48. Assignment or Subrogation. – The assignment of a maritime
claim by a claimant or the subrogation of a third party to the rights of a claimant,
shall entail the simultaneous assignment of, or subrogation to, the maritime lien
securing such claim. However, any claimant holding a maritime lien shall not be
subrogated to compensation payable to the owner of a ship arising from an
insurance contract.

SECTION 49. Exclusions. — The provisions of this Title shall not apply to:

   1. A claim for the enforcement of a maritime lien that is already the subject
      of a pending case and which is sought to be enforced under the provisions
      of Presidential Decree No. 1521, or the Ship Mortgage Decree, at the time
      of the effectivity of this Act; or

   2. A maritime lien which arose prior to the effectivity of this Act, unless such
      lien has already been prescribed.

                                  CHAPTER II
                               SHIP MORTGAGES

SECTION 50. Application. – The provisions of this Chapter shall apply to every
mortgage wherever executed in respect of:

   1. a ship that is registered in the Philippines; or

   2. a ship that is under construction in the Philippines.
SECTION 51. Notification of Deletion of a Ship Subject of Mortgage. –
Except in the case of a forced sale upon order of a competent court or tribunal,
the deletion of a ship shall not be permitted unless all registered ship mortgages
are first canceled or the written consent of all holders of such mortgages is
obtained.

Where the deletion of the ship is obligatory according to the provisions of this
Act or its implementing regulations, the MARINA shall notify the holders of
registered ship mortgages of the pending deletion to enable such holders to take
appropriate action to protect their respective interests. Unless the said holders
consent to a shorter period, the deletion shall not be implemented until the lapse
of a reasonable time from notification in writing, which shall not be less than
three (3) months.

There shall be no other requirements for formal validity except as stated in the
preceding paragraphs.

                              CHAPTER III
                       ENFORCEMENT OF MORTGAGE

SECTION 52. Grounds to Enforce a Ship Mortgage. – A Mortgagee shall be
entitled to enforce the security represented by the ship under a mortgage in the
following cases:

   1. The mortgagor defaults in the discharge of the indebtedness or other
      obligations secure by the ship mortgage;

   2. the mortgagor or any person in possession of the mortgaged property
      substantially prejudices the security of the mortgagee by any act or
      omission; and

   3. The mortgagor commits any breach of the mortgage contract which, by the
      terms of the contract, entitles the mortgagee to enforce the security.

SECTION 53. Remedies of Mortgagees. – The enforcement of the security
under the mortgage may be carried out by:

   1. Arresting the ship subject of the mortgage and applying for an order for its
      forced sale by a competent court; or

   2. Foreclosing the mortgage; or

   3. Any other manner stipulated in the mortgage contract.
Section 54. Arrest and Forced Sale of a Ship. – A mortgagee entitled to enforce a
mortgage, or a person exercising a maritime lien, may bring a proceeding in rem
against the ship which is the subject of the mortgage or to which the maritime
lien attaches, as the case may be, before a competent court or tribunal having
jurisdiction over the ship.

The rules on preliminary attachment under the Rules of Court shall have
suppletory application.

The petitioner shall be entitled to apply with the court an ex parte motion for an
order for the arrest of said ship. The petitioner's claim shall be supported by an
affidavit stating the circumstances that shall be the basis to enforce the mortgage
or lien. An affidavit executed abroad must be duly authenticated. If the affidavit
satisfactorily establishes the petitioner's claim, the competent court shall order
the arrest of the ship.

The court may require the petitioner to post a bond or other security before
issuing the order of arrest. If so required, the bond or security shall not exceed
the amount of the mortgage debt or lien. The court shall after judgment, order
the forced sale of the ship to satisfy the claim, unless the ship has been released
in accordance with the next succeeding section.

The rules on preliminary attachment under the Rules of Court shall not apply in
the arrest of ships.

SECTION 55. Release of Arrested Ship. – The court shall not release the
arrested ship prior to rendition of judgment unless the mortgagor, the owner of
the ship, or any person authorized to act on his behalf or of any other person
interested in the ship:

   1. Applies to the court for an order releasing the ship from arrest, by making
      a cash deposit, and if the same is not possible upon presentation of
      competent evidence, by filing a counter-bond or security in an amount
      double the value of the claim, to secure the payment of any judgment that
      the mortgagee may recover in the action; or

   2. Proves that the arrest was improperly or irregularly made.

SECTION 56. Notice of Forced Sale of a Ship. – Prior to the forced sale of a
ship, the court shall give, or cause to be given, at least thirty (30) days prior
notice of the time and place of such sale, either in customary or electronic form,
to the following:
   1. All holders of registered mortgages;

   2. Such holders of maritime liens whose claims have been notified to the
      court and have not expired;

   3. All other persons having an interest in the ship, whose interests have been
      notified to the court;

   4. The authority in charge of the register in the State of registration; and

   5. The registered owner of the ship.

SECTION 57. Effect of Forced Sale of a Ship. – In the event of the forced sale
of a ship, all mortgages except those assumed by the purchaser with the consent
of the holders and all liens and other encumbrances of whatsoever nature shall
cease to attach to the ship but shall attach to the proceeds of sale: Provided,
that at the time of the sale, the ship is in the jurisdiction of the Philippines and
the sale has been effected in accordance with the laws of the Philippines.

Any charter party or contract for the use of the ship in existence at the time of
the forced sale shall be deemed terminated without prejudice to any claim the
charterer may have against the owner of the ship at the time of the sale.

SECTION 58. Bankruptcy Does Not Affect Ship Mortgage. – The mortgage of
a ship is not affected by the bankruptcy or rehabilitation of the mortgagor after
the date of the registration of the mortgage.

SECTION 59. Distribution of the Proceeds. – The costs awarded by the
competent court and expenses arising out of the arrest and sale of the ship shall
be paid first out of the proceeds of such sale. Thereafter, the following claims
shall be satisfied out of the balance of the proceeds of the sale in the order herein
enumerated, observing also the preference among each category of claims,
established in Sections 41, 42, and 44 hereof on maritime liens:

   1. Maritime liens;

   2. Registered mortgages; and

   3. Rights of retention.

In case of deficiency, the mortgagee shall be entitled to file a suit in personam
against the mortgagor.

SECTION 60. Certificate of Release. – When a ship registered in the
Philippines has been the object of a forced sale in the Philippines, the competent
court or tribunal having jurisdiction shall, at the request of the purchaser, issue
a certificate to the effect that the ship is sold free of all mortgages, maritime liens,
and other encumbrances, except those assumed by the purchaser. Upon the
production of the above-mentioned certificate, the MARINA shall delete all
registered mortgages from the Registry, except those assumed by the purchaser,
or issue a certificate of deletion for the purpose of registration.

SECTION 61. Effect of the Forced Sale of a Ship in Foreign States. – The
forced sale in a foreign state of a ship registered in the Philippines shall be
recognized and effective in the Philippines; Provided that, the procedure for such
forced sale in that foreign state substantially corresponds to the appropriate
procedure in Section 57, including the time and form.

                                CHAPTER IV
                       FOREIGN LIENS AND MORTGAGES

SECTION 62. Foreign Maritime Liens. — A claim governed by the law of a State
other than the Philippines and recognized by such law as giving rise to a
maritime lien or right of a similar nature against the owner, or against the demise
or other charterer, manager or operator of a ship, shall be recognized by and
enforceable in the Philippines. Provided, that said claim constitutes or
corresponds substantially to a claim set out in this Act. The provisions of this
Act relating to maritime liens, including priority, ranking of liens between
themselves, and enforcement, shall also apply to such claims.

SECTION 63. Foreign Mortgages. — A foreign mortgage or any mortgage or
“hypotheque” which relates to a ship of foreign registry shall be recognized and
enforceable in the Philippines. Provided, that:

   1. Such mortgage has been effected and registered or otherwise recorded in
      accordance with the law of the State where the ship is registered;
   2. Such register and any instrument or document which are required to be
      deposited are open to public inspection, and that extracts of the register
      and copies of such instruments are obtainable from the registrar, and
   3. Either the register or the loan agreement specifies:
         a. The name and address of the person in whose favor the mortgage
            has been effected or that it has been issued to bearer;
         b. The amount secured by the mortgage or the manner of calculating
            said amount;
         c. The date of the mortgage; and
         d. Such other particulars which according to the law of the State where
            the ship is registered, determine the rank in regard to other
            registered mortgages.

All matters relating to the procedure for enforcement of foreign mortgages shall
be governed by this Act.

SECTION 64. Priority and Ranking of Foreign Mortgages. — Foreign
mortgages shall be subordinate to maritime liens as set out in this Act. The
ranking of foreign registered mortgages as between themselves, and their effect
as regard to third parties, shall be governed by the law of the State where the
ship is registered.

                                   TITLE V
                           LIMITATION OF LIABILITY

                                 CHAPTER I
                          RIGHT TO LIMIT LIABILITY

SECTION 65. Application. - The following may invoke the right to limit their
liability in accordance with the rules provided for in this Title for the claims set
out in the succeeding section:

   1. Ship owners;

   2. Ship operators;

   3. Bareboat charterers;

   4. Ship managers;

   5. Salvors with respect to claims set forth in Section 67 hereof;

   6. Any person whose act, neglect, or default the ship owner, ship operator,
      bareboat charterer, ship manager or salvor is responsible for, and

   7. Insurers who assume the liability of any ship owner, ship operator,
      bareboat charterer, ship manager, or salvor.

They may invoke their right to limit liability against any claimant in a judicial
action who has sought the arrest of their ships in order to enforce a claim which
is subject to limitation.
SECTION 66. Claims which are Subject to Limitation. — The following claims
made against the persons set forth in the preceding section or against the ship,
whatever the basis of liability may be, shall be subject to the limitation of liability:

   1. Claims in respect of loss of life or personal injury, or loss of or damage to
      property, including damage to harbor works, basins, waterways, and aids
      to navigation, occurring on board or in direct connection with the
      operation of the ship or with salvage operations and consequential loss
      resulting therefrom;

   2. Claims in respect of loss resulting from delay in the carriage by sea of
      cargo, passengers, or their luggage;

   3. Claims in respect of other loss resulting from the infringement of rights,
      other than contractual rights, occurring in direct connection with the
      operation of the ship or salvage operations;

   4. Claims in respect of loss or damage arising from the raising, removal,
      destruction or the rendering harmless of a ship which is sunk, wrecked,
      or abandoned, including anything that is or has been on board such ship;

   5. Claims in respect of loss or damage resulting from the removal,
      destruction, or the rendering harmless of the cargo of the ship; and

   6. Claims in respect of measures taken in order to avert or minimize loss for
      which the person liable may limit his liability in accordance with this Title,
      and any further loss caused by such measures.

SECTION 67. Claims Not Subject to Limitation. — The following claims shall
not be subject to any limitation of liability in accordance with this Title:

   1. Claims arising from the contract of salvage or from any salvage operation
      rendered in accordance with relevant Philippine law on salvage or any
      applicable international salvage convention, including any claim for
      special compensation payable under a contract of salvage, or any
      contribution in general average;

   2. Claims for oil pollution damage within the meaning of the applicable
      convention covering the civil liability for oil pollution damage or any
      amendment thereto which is in force;

   3. Claims subject to any international convention governing or prohibiting
      the limitation of liability for nuclear damage;
   4. Claims against the ship owner of a nuclear ship for nuclear damage; and

   5. Claims brought by employees against the shipowner, ship operator,
      bareboat charterer, ship manager, or salvor, where the duties of such
      employees are connected with the ship or with the salvage operations, as
      the case may be, or their heirs, successors, or assigns, if under the law
      governing the contract of employment, the ship owner, ship operator,
      bareboat charterer, ship manager, or salvor is not entitled to limit his
      liability in respect of such claims, or if the law governing the contract of
      employment permits a limit of liability greater than that provided for in
      this Act.

SECTION 68. Conduct Barring Limitation. — Any shipowner, ship operator,
bareboat charterer, ship manager or salvor, or any person acting on their behalf,
shall not be entitled to limit liability if it is proven that the loss resulted from the
personal act or omission of such owner, ship operator, bareboat charterer, ship
manager, or salvor, or any person acting on their behalf, and such act or
omission was committed with the intent to cause such loss, or it was done
recklessly and with the knowledge that such loss would probably result.

SECTION 69. Counterclaims, Right to Set Off. — When a ship owner, ship
operator, bareboat charterer, ship manager, or salvor, who is entitled to limit his
liability, has a counterclaim against the claimant arising out of the same
occurrence, their respective claims shall be set off against each other, and the
limits of liability shall only apply to the balance of the claim, if any. The right of
set-off can be invoked only if both parties are entitled to limit their respective
liabilities and their respective claims do not fall within the provisions of sections
67 and 68 of this Act.

                                   CHAPTER II
                               LIMITS OF LIABILITY

SECTION 70. General Limits. — The general limits of liability for claims other
than for passenger claims, arising from any distinct occasion shall be calculated
in accordance with the International Convention on the Limitation of Liability for
Maritime Claims.

SECTION 71. Aggregation of Claims for Loss of Life and Personal Injury
and for other Claims. — The limits of liability for loss of life and personal injury
and for other claims determined in accordance with Section 65 hereof shall apply
to the aggregate of all claims that arise on any distinct occasion against:
   1. The ship owner, ship operator, bareboat charterer, or ship manager and
      any person whose act, neglect, or default such ship owner, ship operator,
      bareboat charterer, or ship manager is responsible for; or
   2. The ship owner, ship operator, bareboat charterer, or ship manager of a
      ship rendering salvage services from a ship or the salvor or salvors
      operating from a salvage tug, and any person whose act, neglect or default
      the ship owner, ship operator, bareboat charterer, ship manager or salvor
      is responsible for; or
   3. The salvor who is not operating from a ship or salvage tug or who is
      operating solely on the ship to, or in respect of, which the salvage services
      are rendered and any person whose act, neglect or default the salvor is
      responsible for.

SECTION 72. Ships Below the Specified Tonnage Limits. – The MARINA is
hereby authorized to issue rules and regulations regarding the limitation of
liability for ships below the previously specified tonnage limits. Such rules and
regulations shall take into consideration the availability of providers of financial
security policies and the prevailing financial capacities of shipowners.

                                  CHAPTER III
                               LIMITATION FUND

SECTION 73. Creation of a Limitation Fund. - Any person entitled to limit his
liability in accordance with Section 65 hereof and who is alleged to be liable for
a claim provided in Section 70 hereof, may, at any time during the hearing of the
case or after the arrest of his ship, constitute a fund with the court in which legal
proceedings have been instituted for the satisfaction of the claim subject to
limitation.

The fund shall be in an amount equivalent to the limits of liability set forth in
the rules and regulations issued by the MARINA in accordance with the
immediately preceding section.

Any fund created shall be available only for the payment of claims in respect of
which limitation of liability can be invoked.

SECTION 74. Insurance Indemnity Cover. — Every shipowner or bareboat
charterer shall be required to maintain adequate insurance coverage or
protection and indemnity coverage to meet the financial responsibility for any
liability which such shipowner or bareboat charterer may incur for loss of or
injury to human life, damage to property, and to the marine environment. In
determining the limits for mandatory insurance coverage for seagoing ships, the
MARINA shall take into account and be guided by the applicable limits of liability
provided in relevant international conventions. In setting the limits for
mandatory insurance coverage for coastwise ships, the MARINA shall consider
the prevailing local, social, economic, and other conditions in the Philippines.

SECTION 75. Form of the Fund. — The fund may be created either by
depositing the sum with a bank or other financial institution designated by the
court, or by providing a bond, or other security acceptable to the court. The fund
constituted by any of the persons mentioned in Section 65, subsections (a), (b),
(c), (d), or (e) shall likewise be deemed constituted in favor of the persons
mentioned in subsections (f) and (g) of that section.

SECTION 76. Distribution of the Fund. — Subject to the provisions of Sections
65 and 66 hereof, the limitation fund shall be distributed among the claimants
in proportion to their claims against the fund as determined by the court.

SECTION 77. Subrogation of Rights. — If, at any time before the fund is
distributed, the ship owner, ship operator, bareboat charterer, ship manager, or
salvor, or their respective insurers, settles a claim made against and payable by
the fund, such person shall, up to the amount paid, acquire by subrogation the
rights which the claimant would have enjoyed under this Title.

SECTION 78. Contingency Fund for Expected Claim. - Where the ship owner,
ship operator, bareboat charterer, ship manager, or salvor, or their respective
insurers, establishes that it may compelled to pay a claim, at a later date,
whether in whole or in part, and because of such payment, the ship owner, ship
operator, bareboat charterer, ship manager or salvor, or their respective
insurers, would have enjoyed a right of subrogation if the claim had been paid
before the fund was distributed, the court having jurisdiction over the claim and
in which the fund has been constituted may order that a sufficient sum from the
fund be provisionally set aside to enable the person who has been subrogated to
the rights of the claimant to enforce his claim against the fund at such later date.

SECTION 79. Bar to other Actions. — Where a limitation fund has been
constituted in accordance with Section 73 hereof, any person having made a
claim against the fund shall be barred from enforcing such claim against any
other assets of the persons mentioned in Section 65 hereof and on whose behalf
the fund has been constituted.
SECTION 80. Release from Arrest after the Constitution of the Limitation
Fund. — After a limitation fund has been constituted in accordance with Section
73 of this Act, any ship or other property, belonging to a shipowner, ship
operator, bareboat charterer, ship manager, or salvor on whose behalf the fund
has been constituted, which has been arrested or attached in accordance with
Title III of this Act in order to satisfy a claim which is enforceable against the
fund or any other bond or security given, may be released from arrest at the
discretion of the court. Such release shall, however, be mandatory if the
limitation fund has been constituted:

   1. At the port where the occurrence took place;

   2. If the occurrence took place out of port, at the first port of call of the ship
      after such occurrence;

   3. At the port of disembarkation in respect of claims for loss of life or personal
      injury;

   4. At the port of discharge in respect of damage to cargo; or

   5. At the port where the arrest is made.

The provisions of this section and of the immediately preceding section shall
apply only if the claimant may bring a claim against the limitation fund before
the court administering that fund, and the fund is available and freely
transferable to the claimant in respect of the claim.

                                  TITLE VI
                             OPERATION OF SHIPS

SECTION 81. Ship management. – Any person or entity may engage in ship
management services in the Philippines, which shall include but not be limited
to technical management, crew management, commercial management, and
administrative services for ships, provided that the ship management company
shall be duly registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission and holds
a valid license issued by the MARINA. Provided further that the company
maintains qualified personnel with appropriate maritime credentials and
certifications as prescribed by the Authority.
The company shall demonstrate financial capability and shall maintain
professional indemnity insurance coverage adequate to the scope of management
services provided.

All management activities shall comply with applicable Philippine maritime laws,
international maritime conventions to which the Philippines is a party, and the
requirements of the ship’s flag state.

The company shall be subject to periodic inspection and audit by the MARINA to
ensure continued compliance with safety, security, environmental, and
operational standards.

SECTION 82. Power to Grant Special Permit. – The MARINA is hereby
authorized to grant Philippine-registered ships permission to operate in both
domestic and international trade routes.

It shall have the power to issue special permits allowing Philippine-registered
vessels to shift operations between domestic and international trading routes
without requiring re-registration or change in vessel classification, provided such
ships maintain compliance with all applicable safety, security, and
environmental protection standards.

SECTION 83. Qualification Requirements. – To qualify for the special permit,
vessel owners or operators must:

   1. Hold valid registration and all necessary certifications for both domestic
      and international operations;

   2. Demonstrate compliance with international maritime conventions
      applicable to international trade, including but not limited to SOLAS,
      MARPOL, STCW, and MLC;

   3. Maintain appropriate insurance        coverage   for   both   domestic   and
      international operations; and

   4. Satisfy any additional requirements as may be prescribed by MARINA
      through appropriate circulars or memoranda.

SECTION 84. Application for Special Permit. – MARINA shall establish a
streamlined application process for special permits, which shall include:
   1. Submission of a comprehensive operations plan detailing intended trade
      routes;

   2. Documentation of compliance with           all   applicable   domestic   and
      international maritime regulations;

   3. Evidence of financial capability to sustain operations in both trading
      environments; and

   4. Payment of prescribed fees as determined by MARINA.

SECTION 85. Operational Requirements – Vessels granted special permit shall
provide advance notification to MARINA of any intended shift between domestic
and international operations.

They shall likewise maintain separate records of domestic and international
voyages for regulatory and taxation purposes;

Grantee ships shall comply with crew certification and manning requirements
applicable to the relevant trading area and submit to special inspections as may
be required by MARINA to verify continued compliance with safety and security
standards.

SECTION 86. Taxation and Fees for Special Permit. The Department of
Finance, in coordination with MARINA, shall establish appropriate taxation
policies for vessels engaged in both domestic and international operations,
ensuring equitable treatment while preventing unfair competition.

SECTION 87. Cabotage Considerations. Nothing in this provision shall be
construed as exempting foreign vessels from existing cabotage laws governing
domestic trade operations.

SECTION 88. Ship Construction. Every new building to be registered for
international voyage under Philippine laws shall be constructed in accordance
with standards for ship construction set forth in international maritime
conventions and implemented through government regulations. Ship
construction shall be under the supervision of the MARINA, or a recognized
organization appointed for the purpose. The recognized organization shall
oversee the construction of the ship and ensure that the construction regulations
provided in international maritime conventions and those issued by the MARINA,
covering the structural strength and integrity of all parts of the ship's hull and
its appendages, as well as the safety and reliability of the main propulsion,
steering systems, and auxiliary systems of the ship, including the installation of
navigational and communication equipment and equipment to prevent marine
pollution, are followed. The shipowner shall immediately notify the MARINA and
the relevant recognized organization, in writing, of any modification, alteration,
or conversion introduced thereon.

SECTION 89. Navigation and Communications Equipment. Every Philippine-
registered ship shall have on board such navigation and communication
equipment and apparatus required by international maritime conventions for the
safe navigation of ships. Every equipment and apparatus required to be installed
on board shall be consistent with the type, tonnage, power, means of propulsion,
and trading limitations of the ship.

SECTION 90. Equipment to Prevent Marine Pollution. - Every Philippine-
registered ship shall have on board such navigation and communication
equipment and apparatus required by international maritime conventions for the
safe navigation of ships. Every piece of equipment and apparatus that shall be
required to be installed onboard shall be suited for the type, tonnage, power,
means of propulsion, and trading limitations of the particular ship

SECTION 91. Inspections and Surveys. To ensure the proper maintenance of
the ship's hull, machinery, and equipment, its compliance with international
conventions and government regulations, and for its safe operation, every
Philippine-registered ship shall be subject to inspections and surveys as required
under applicable international conventions and national regulations.

SECTION 92. Scope of Inspection and Surveys. - The inspections and surveys
shall be conducted in accordance with international maritime conventions and
government regulations, and shall include an inspection and survey of the ship’s:

   1. Hull structure;

   2. Machinery and equipment;

   3. Life-saving equipment;

   4. Fire-fighting equipment;

   5. Radio and communications installations;

   6. Such other facilities and equipment as may be prescribed by MARINA.

SECTION 93. Extent of Authority of Recognized Organization. — The
MARINA, in appointing a recognized organization or classification society to
conduct the inspection and survey of ships, shall inform the recognized
organization of the specific responsibilities and conditions of the authority
delegated to it and exercise a comprehensive oversight thereof.

SECTION 94. Issuance of Safety Certificates. — The recognized organization,
pursuant to the functions delegated to it by the MARINA, shall have the authority
to issue ship certificates to every Philippine-registered ship and for this purpose,
the ship certificates issued by such recognized organization shall be deemed to
have met the requirements of this Act. Copies of the ship certificates shall be
submitted to MARINA, which shall, upon verification of compliance, issue a
certificate of inspection in favor of the complying ship confirming the findings of
the recognized organization.

SECTION 95. Availability of Ship Certificates. — The ship certificates
mentioned in the immediately preceding section shall be readily available on
board the ship for examination at all times.

                              TITLE VII
                    ENFORCEMENT AND INVESTIGATION

SECTION 96. Report of any Incident Involving a Philippine Registered
Ship. - When any Philippine-registered ship sustains an accident or causes any
incident involving the loss of life, material loss of property, or serious injury to
any person, or receives any damage affecting its seaworthiness or its
efficiency, or if it should cause any pollution or damage to the marine
environment, regardless of where such accident or incident may occur, the ship
owner, ship operator, bareboat charterer, ship manager, agent or master of
such ship shall immediately, after the occurrence of the accident or incident,
send a report of the accident or incident to the MARINA:

   1. The name of the ship;

   2. The port to which the ship belongs;

   3. The place where the incident occurred;

   4. The nature and probable cause of the incident;

   5. The number and names of those who perished; and

   6. The estimated amount of loss or damage to the ship, the cargo, or the
      marine environment.

The specific procedures for the submission of reports, will be detailed in the
implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of this Act. The ship owner, ship
operator, charterer, ship manager, agent, or master may be required to furnish
such other information as shall be called for.

                               TITLE VIII
                   CASUALTY OR SAFETY INVESTIGATION

SECTION 97. Marine Casualty or Safety Investigation. — Following the
occurrence of an accident or incident involving a Philippine-registered ship
resulting in the loss of life, material loss of property, or serious injury to any
person, or which results in damage affecting its seaworthiness or efficiency, or
causes any pollution or damage to the marine environment, regardless of where
such accident or incident may occur, the safety investigation shall be conducted
by the MARINA. MARINA shall undertake these investigations unless another
law expressly provides for the transfer of this mandate to another entity.

Furthermore, MARINA shall ensure that all investigations comply with the
operational requirements set forth in the International Convention for the Safety
of Life at Sea (SOLAS) and other relevant international maritime regulations. The
MARINA shall maintain its role in conducting such investigations and ensure
that all safety measures and procedures align with both national and
international standards for maritime safety.

SECTION 98. Nature of the Investigation. — The investigation undertaken in
accordance with this Chapter shall not apportion blame or determine liability
but shall be conducted to determine the cause of the accident and the
identification of measures to prevent the occurrence of similar marine casualties,
accidents, or incidents in the future.

SECTION 99. Power of Investigators to Investigate. - Maritime casualty or
safety investigators appointed by MARINA shall have full powers to board the
Philippine-registered ship, regardless of where it may be found, interview the
master and the crew and any other person involved, and acquire such
evidentiary material for purposes of undertaking and completing the marine
safety investigation.

SECTION 100. Amendment of Safety Regulations. - Following completion of
the safety investigation and upon a complete study of the safety investigation
report, the MARINA shall take measures to amend its safety regulations to
implement the recommendations made in the report.

SECTION 101. Providing Reports to the International Maritime
Organization. - The MARINA, as competent authority, shall provide a copy of
the report to the International Maritime Organization to enable a wide
dissemination of information and to assist the international maritime
community to address safety issues.

                                 TITLE IX
                          MANNING REQUIREMENTS

SECTION 102. Citizenship of Crew. A ship registered under the Philippine flag
for international trade shall employ competent and qualified management level
officers who may be foreign nationals. Operational and support level, including
other members of the crew not falling under any of the categories mentioned,
must be Filipino citizens.

SECTION 103. Crew Competence. - Every member of the crew of a Philippine-
registered ship shall possess the appropriate qualification and competence to
serve in the capacity and perform the functions involved at the level of
responsibility, for the position held, for the type, tonnage, power, means of
propulsion, and trading limitations of the ship concerned.

Documents evidencing such qualifications and competence, as well as any
endorsements thereto and any renewals thereof, shall be issued by MARINA.

                                 TITLE X
                           REQUISITION OF SHIPS

SECTION 104. Requisition of Ships. - The President of the Philippines may, in
times of war and other national emergency, when the public interest so requires,
order the requisition of any and all ships of the Philippine registry. The
Government shall pay the owner or operator of the ship, based on normal
conditions at the time of requisition:

   1. The fair market value, if the ship is taken absolutely; or

   2. The fair charter value, if the ship is taken temporarily, for the period
      commencing from the takeover by the Government to the date the ship is
      returned to its owner or operator.

                                  TITLE XI
                              FINAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 105. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90)
days after the effectivity of this Act, the MARINA shall issue the implementing
rules and regulations as may be necessary to implement the provisions of this
Act.

SECTION 106. Green Lane on Procedural System. – To ensure the effective
implementation of this Act, the Department of Foreign Affairs, Department of
Finance, Department of Labor and Employment, Department of Health,
Department of Interior and Local Government, Department of Migrant Workers,
Securities and Exchange Commission, Bureau of Immigration, Board of
Investments, Bureau of Customs, Bureau of Internal Revenue, Philippine Ports
Authority and other port authorities, Philippine Coast Guard, Bureau of
Fisheries and Aquatic Resources, Bureau of Quarantine, Anti-Red Tape
Authority, and other relevant offices and agencies shall develop, introduce, and
promote in their respective policies and procedures streamlining of processes on
the acquisition, registration and operation of ships; the hiring, deployment,
change, and repatriation of crew, the construction, repairs, maintenance and
recycling of ships, and other government and business processes concerning
ships in the Philippine Registry to support the country’s competitiveness and
promote trade facilitation.

SECTION 107. Administrative Offenses and Penalties. - To ensure faithful
compliance with the rules and regulations issued in accordance with the
preceding section, the MARINA, through summary administrative proceedings,
may identify, prohibit, and penalize such acts and activities that may reduce the
efficiency and reliability of the Philippine Ship Registry, impede the effectiveness
of the exercise of Philippine jurisdiction and control over Philippine-registered
ships, or otherwise interfere with its duties and responsibilities as a flag State
and the implementation of this Title.

For this purpose, the MARINA is authorized to recommend to the Philippine
Coast Guard the detention of a ship and is further authorized to impose
administrative fines as well as the penalty of suspension, cancellation, or
revocation of the relevant certificates, for violations of this Act and such rules
and regulations that it may issue pursuant to this Act.

The range of fines shall be subject to review and revision every five (5) years from
the date of effectivity of this Act.

SECTION 108. Appropriations. – The MARINA shall immediately include in its
programs the operationalization requirement of this Act. The funds necessary to
effectively carry out the provisions of this Act during the first year of
implementation shall be sourced from the available funds of MARINA. Thereafter,
such funds as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act
shall be included in the annual General Appropriation Act.

SECTION 109. Transitory Provisions. - Every ship currently registered under
the laws of the Philippines and granted the privilege to fly its flag shall be deemed
to be registered under this Act insofar as its registration is not inconsistent with
the provisions of this Act and such registration shall continue to be valid until
its expiration unless sooner revoked by the MARINA.

Any ship mortgage entered into in accordance with the provisions of the Ship
Mortgage Decree or the Chattel Mortgage Law shall continue to be valid and
effective, and its registration shall be transferred to the MARINA in accordance
with the provisions of this Act. Nothing in the transfer shall change or in any
way alter or affect any preference a registered mortgage may enjoy or the original
date on which such mortgage was entered in the registry

SECTION 110. Separability Clause. - If, for any reason, any section,
subsection, clause, or term of this Act is held to be illegal, invalid, or
unconstitutional, such parts not affected by such declaration shall remain in full
force and effect.
