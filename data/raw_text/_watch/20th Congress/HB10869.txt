                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The 1987 Constitution provides that the State shall recognize the role of
women in nation-building1 and shall protect and promote the right to health of
the people and instill health consciousness among them.2 Furthermore, the
Constitution also provides that the State shall protect working women by
providing safe and healthful working conditions, taking into account their
maternal functions, and such facilities and opportunities that will enhance their
welfare and enable them to realize their full potential in the service of the nation.3

      Menstruation is the recurring discharge of blood and tissue from the inner
lining of the uterus as part of the menstrual cycle. Menstruation is usually
experienced by individuals with a uterus, including cisgender women and
transgender men. Some individuals may experience pain and discomfort during
menstruation due to dysmenorrhea, the severe and frequent cramps during the
menstruation period. It was reported that more than half of women who
menstruate have some pain for 1 to 2 days each month4, affecting between 45
and 95% of all women5.

The pain during menstruation is normal, but for some individuals, the pain and

pain may be due to hormonal activity during the menstrual cycle or may be an
associated phenomenon of an underlying medical condition.

       In light of the constitutional mandate and the factual backdrop
surrounding menstrual health, this bill proposes that a menstrual health
privilege be made available to employees in the public and private sectors who
experience pain and discomfort during menstruation.

       The menstrual health privilege shall be in the form of either a two-day paid
leave or an option for a work-from-home arrangement during their menstruation
period, if applicable to the nature of their duties and responsibilities. The option
for a two-day paid leave, distinct and separate from the legally mandated
sickness benefit under existing laws, is made available to allow the employee to
recuperate in extreme and severe cases of painful menstruation.

      On the other hand, the option for a work-from-home arrangement is made
available to allow the employee to maintain productivity in less-severe cases of
painful menstruation. The menstrual health privilege aims to promote the
general well-being of employees who suffer from painful and burdensome
menstruation and alleviate the distress that they experience.

       The menstrual health privilege proposed by this bill should not be seen as
a special treatment for a particular class of employees. Menstruation and its
associated pain and discomfort are both health issues and medical concerns. It
is high time that we recognize the plight of employees who experience these
realities and give them our support as they go through this recurring struggle.

            Based on the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT PROVIDING MENSTRUAL HEALTH PRIVILEGE TO EMPLOYEES IN
               THE PUBLIC AND PRIVATE SECTORS

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Menstrual
Health Privilege Act of 2026”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the role of women
employees and employees who were assigned as females at birth in nation-
building. Article XIII, Section 14 of the 1987 Constitution provides that the State
shall protect working women by providing safe and healthful working conditions,
considering their maternal functions, and such facilities and opportunities that
will enhance their welfare and enable them to realize their full potential in the
service of the nation. The State shall promote their general health and well-being
and shall aid them to maintain their productivity as essential members of the
labor force.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall mean:

      (a) Menstrual Cycle – the repetitive, recurring changes in hormone
          production and structure of the uterus and ovaries of the female
          reproductive system.

      (b) Menstrual Health – the state of physical, mental, emotional, and social
          well-being of an individual in relation to the menstrual cycle.

      (c) Menstruation – the discharge of blood and tissue from the inner lining
          of the uterus as part of the menstruation cycle, which may or may not
         have associated discomfort and pain, occurring typically every four (4)
         weeks and lasting three (3) to five (5) days.

       SECTION 4. Menstrual Health Privilege. – Employees in the public and
private sectors, regardless of the type of their employment and whenever
applicable, shall have the privilege of availing either a two-day paid leave under
Section 6 of this Act or a work-from-home arrangement option under Section 7
of this Act during their menstruation period.

       An employer may require or opt not to require an employee seeking to avail
the privilege to present a medical certificate as proof of their special medical
circumstance entitling them to the privilege; Provided, that such medical
certificate, if so required by the employer, may be complied with in advance and
in anticipation of the menstruation period; Provided further, that such medical
certificate as proof of special medical circumstance, if so required by the
employer, shall be valid for twelve (12) months for purposes of this Act.

       SECTION 5. Coverage of the Privilege. – Employees who experience
difficulties, pain, and discomfort during their menstruation period, regardless of
their sexual orientation, gender identity and expression (SOGIE), and type of
employment, are entitled to the menstrual health privilege provided under
Section 4 of this Act.

       SECTION 6. Menstrual Two-day Paid Leave. – Employees in the public and
private sectors entitled to the menstrual health privilege under Section 5 of this
Act may opt to avail of a two-day paid leave during their menstruation period
separate and distinct from the legally-mandated sickness leave benefit under
existing laws, rules, and regulations. Provided, that such leave is not convertible
to cash if unused.

      SECTION 7. Option for a Work-From-Home Arrangement. – Employees in
the public and private sectors entitled to the menstrual health privilege under
Section 5 of this Act may opt to avail of a work-from-home arrangement for two
(2) days during their menstruation period; Provided, that such work-from-home
arrangement is available only to employees whose nature of duties and
responsibilities allows them to work remotely.

      SECTION 8. Security of Tenure. - Any female worker who avails of the
benefits under this Act, whether in government service or the private sector, and
regardless of employment status, including probationary, casual, contractual,
project-based, or job-order employment, shall be assured of security of tenure.
The exercise of this right shall not be used as a ground for dismissal, non-
renewal of contract, demotion, suspension, or reduction in rank or pay, nor shall
any employer impose new performance conditions or alter any compensation or
incentive scheme with the effect of penalizing a worker for such availment. In
any complaint alleging a violation of this Section where an adverse employment
action is taken against an employee within a reasonable period following their
availment of the benefits under this Act, the burden shall be on the employer to
prove that the action was taken for a valid and legitimate reason unrelated to
such availment.

      SECTION 9. Non-Discrimination. - No employer shall discriminate against
any individual, whether a prospective or existing employee, on the basis of their
menstrual health condition, or on account of their availment, intended
availment, or eligibility to avail of the menstrual health privilege granted under
this Act. Discrimination under this Section includes, but is not limited to:
refusing to hire an applicant, or imposing different terms or conditions of hiring
an applicant, on account of the applicant’s potential future availment of the
benefits under this Act; dismissing, demoting, suspending, or otherwise
disadvantaging an employee, or refusing to renew a contract, on account of such
availment as a condition for hiring, continued employment, or promotion; and
imposing on employees eligible for this privilege less favorable terms of
employment or opportunities for training and advancement than those enjoyed
by other employees similarly situated. Nothing in this Section shall be construed
to prevent an employer from imposing performance standards or taking
personnel actions based on just and legitimate grounds unrelated to the
availment of the privilege under this Act.

      SECTION 10. Construction of this Act. – When the need arises, this Act
shall be construed in favor of the employee seeking to avail of the menstrual
health privilege with due regard and consideration to the right to due process of
employers.

      SECTION 11. Just Cause for Termination of Employment. –
Misrepresentation of any fact or circumstance by an employee who is not entitled
to the menstrual health privilege shall be a just cause for termination of
employment.

      SECTION 12. Penalized acts. – For purposes of this Act, the following acts
are penalized:

         (a) Discrimination against an employee. – Any person, corporation,
             partnership, or association who shall commit any form of
             discrimination, as defined under Section 10, against individuals in
             relation to the menstrual health privilege, including prospective and
             existing employees, shall be penalized with a fine not less than one
             hundred thousand pesos (Php 100,000.00) or imprisonment of not
             more than six (6) months. If the employer is a corporation,
             partnership, or association, the penalized act under this item is
             without prejudice to the civil, criminal, or administrative liability
             that may be incurred by its responsible officers.
         (b) Refusal to honor the menstrual health privilege. – Any person,
             corporation, partnership, or association who shall refuse to honor
             the menstrual health privilege of an employee entitled to the same
             shall be penalized with a fine not less than one hundred thousand
             pesos (Php 100,000.00) or imprisonment of not more than six (6)
             months. If the employer is a corporation, partnership, or
             association, the penalized act under this item is without prejudice
             to the civil, criminal, or administrative liability that may be incurred
             by its responsible officers.

       SECTION 13. Access to medical professionals. – The Department of Health
(DOH) is hereby mandated to formulate policies and mechanisms that will
provide free and equitable access to licensed medical professionals in
government and private hospitals and facilities for purposes of securing a
medical certificate to be used in availing the menstrual health privilege. Such
policies and mechanisms shall form part of the implementing rules and
regulations.

       SECTION 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE) and the Civil Service Commission (CSC), in
coordination with the Department of Health (DOH), Department of Economy,
Planning, and Development (DEPDev), and other relevant government agencies,
shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act within sixty
(60) days from its effectivity.

      SECTION 15. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act, or part
hereof, is held invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or provision
not otherwise affected shall remain valid and subsisting.

      SECTION 16. Repealing Clause. – All provisions of laws, presidential
decrees, letters of instruction, and other presidential issuances that are
incompatible or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby deemed
amended or repealed.

      SECTION 17. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

      Approved,
