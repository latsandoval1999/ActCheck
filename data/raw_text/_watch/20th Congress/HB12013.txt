                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

As Filipino families increasingly recognize companion animals—such as dogs,
cats, and other domestic pets—as treasured members of the household, the bond
between humans and their pets has deepened significantly. With this deep
emotional connection comes the inevitable and painful reality of pet loss. Millions
of pet owners across the country face the grieving process daily, seeking dignified,
humane, and respectful after-life care services for their deceased animal
companions, including proper handling, transportation, cremation, and burial
services.

However while proper treatment of animals is anchored on Republic Act No. 8485
(The Animal Welfare Act of 1998, as amended by Republic Act No. 10631, it lacks
specific, comprehensive regulations governing pet after-life care industries. The
absence of a standardized regulatory framework has left pet owners vulnerable to
unregulated operators, substandard and unsanitary cremation practices, illegal
dumping of animal remains, exorbitant pricing without service transparency, and
environmental hazards arising from improper disposal methods.

This Bill seeks to bridge this critical regulatory gap by establishing a robust
framework for the protection, handling, cremation, burial, and overall after-life
care services for companion animals. Specifically, this measure requires all pet
after-life care providers (mortuaries, crematories, memorial parks, and transport
services) to secure proper accreditation and licenses from the Bureau of Animal
Industry (BAI) and relevant local government units (LGUs).

This measure also protects grieving pet owners from predatory pricing, fraudulent
service claims, unauthorized commingling of ashes, and improper disposal
practices. A strong penal sanctions, including heavy fines and imprisonment, for
establishments and individuals engaging in illegal disposal, maltreatment of
deceased remains, fraud, or operating without a valid license is also provided.
By dignifying the passing of companion and pet animals and ensuring
accountability among service providers, this measure upholds public health,
environmental safety, and humane treatment standards that respect the dignity
of life and loss.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this Bill is earnestly sought.

       AN ACT ESTABLISHING A REGULATORY FRAMEWORK FOR THE
PROTECTION, HANDLING, CREMATION, BURIAL, AND OTHER AFTER-LIFE
 CARE SERVICES FOR COMPANION AND PET ANIMALS, PROVIDING FOR
       THE REGISTRATION AND LICENSING OF SERVICE PROVIDERS,
PRESCRIBING STANDARDS AND SAFEGUARDS, AND IMPOSING PENALTIES
                         FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Companion and Pet
Animal After-Life Care and Dignity Act of 2026."
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.
It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect and promote animal welfare,
public health, and environmental safety. The State recognizes that companion
animals occupy a special place in the Filipino household and society, and that
their owners are entitled to access safe, transparent, ethical, and dignified after-
life care services upon their demise. Towards this end, the State shall regulate all
commercial and non-commercial entities engaged in companion animal after-life
care to prevent fraud, environmental degradation, and disrespect toward animal
remains.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms.
As used in this Act:

   1. "Companion/Pet Animal" refers to domesticated animals traditionally kept
      for companionship in or near the household, including but not limited to
      dogs, cats, pet birds, rabbits, and similar domesticated species.
   2. "After-Life Care Services" refer to any commercial or institutional service
      dealing with the retrieval, handling, refrigeration, transport, cremation,
      burial, taxidermy, or memorialization of deceased companion animals.
   3. "Pet Crematory" refers to any facility equipped with specialized incineration
      machinery (cremator) designed exclusively or certified for the thermal
      destruction or reduction of animal remains.
   4. "Pet Memorial Park / Cemetery" refers to a parcel of land duly zoned,
      permitted, and dedicated exclusively or partially for the interment or
      entombment of companion animal remains.
   5. "Individual Cremation" refers to a process where a single companion animal
      is placed into the cremation chamber alone, and the entirety of its ashes is
      recovered and returned to the owner.
   6. "Communal Cremation" refers to a process where multiple companion
      animals are cremated together without separation of individual ashes, the
      disposal of which is managed by the service provider in a sanitary and
      lawful manner.
   7. "BAI" refers to the Bureau of Animal Industry under the Department of
      Agriculture (DA).

SEC. 4. Scope and Application.


This Act shall apply to all individuals, partnerships, cooperatives, corporations,
veterinary clinics, animal hospitals, shelters, and entities engaged or offering to
engage in the business or operation of companion animal after-life care services
within the territorial jurisdiction of the Philippines.


SEC. 5. Registration, Licensing, and Accreditation.


   a. No person, partnership, or corporation shall operate a pet mortuary, pet
      crematory, pet cemetery, or specialized pet transport and after-life service
      without first securing a Certificate of Registration and License to Operate
      (LTO) from the Bureau of Animal Industry (BAI), in coordination with the
      Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), the
      Department of Health (DOH), and the respective Local Government Unit
      (LGU).
   b. Licenses shall be valid for a period of three (3) years and shall be subject
      to periodic inspection and renewal.
   c. Operating standards, sanitary requirements, and accreditation fees shall
      be determined by the BAI through the Committee on Animal Welfare.
SEC. 6. Standards for Handling, Storage, and Transportation.
  a. Humane and Respectful Handling: Deceased companion animals must be
     handled with dignity at all times. Tossing, improper stacking, or
     disrespectful disposal of animal remains is strictly prohibited.
  b. Temporary Storage and Refrigeration: Facilities holding deceased companion
     animals prior to cremation or burial must maintain dedicated refrigeration
     units operating at appropriate low temperatures to prevent decomposition
     and health hazards.
  c. Safe Transportation: Vehicles used for transporting deceased pets must be
     sanitary, leak-proof, segregated from live animals and food transport, and
     adequately disinfected after every trip.
SEC. 7. Regulation of Cremation and Environmental Standards.


  a. Cremation Compliance: All pet crematories must comply with environmental
     emission standards set by the DENR under the Philippine Clean Air Act.
     Cremators must be equipped with secondary combustion chambers and
     pollution control devices.
  b. Verification of Ashes: In cases of requested individual cremation, providers
     must maintain a strict chain-of-custody tracking system to guarantee that
     the ashes returned to the pet owner belong exclusively to their companion
     animal.
  c. Commingled Ashes Disposal: Ashes from communal cremations must be
     disposed of in an eco-friendly manner, such as scattering in designated
     memorial gardens or legal green burial sites, in compliance with LGU zoning
     and sanitary regulations.


SEC. 8. Issuance of Death Certificates and Memorial Documentation.
  a. Upon request of the owner, a licensed veterinarian or an authorized after-
     life care provider shall issue a Certificate of Companion Animal Passing /
     Death Certificate, detailing the date, time, estimated cause of death,
     microchip number (if applicable), and disposition method.
  b. Providers must maintain transparent service agreements and itemized
    pricing schedules made accessible to clients prior to rendering services.
SEC. 9. Prohibited Acts.
It shall be unlawful for any person, establishment, or entity to:

   1. Operate an after-life care service, crematory, or pet cemetery without a valid
      LTO from the BAI and permits from the LGU and DENR.
   2. Arbitrarily dump, abandon, or illegally dispose of deceased companion
      animals in public spaces, waterways, vacant lots, or municipal garbage
      systems.
   3. Conduct communal cremation while misrepresenting it as an individual
      cremation, or intentionally substituting or tampering with animal ashes.
   4. Subject deceased animal remains to unauthorized commercial exploitation,
      mutilation, or irreverent treatment.
   5. Impose fraudulent, hidden, or predatory fees that exploit grieving pet
      owners.
   6. Fail to maintain sanitary conditions and environmental pollution controls
      in crematories and mortuaries.

SEC. 10. Penalties and Sanctions.
Any person or entity found guilty of violating the provisions of this Act shall suffer
the following penalties:
   a. For Operating Without a License / Illegal Dumping: Imprisonment ranging
      from six (6) months to two (2) years and/or a fine ranging from One
      Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00) to Five Hundred Thousand Pesos
      (₱500,000.00), at the discretion of the court.
   b. For Fraudulent Cremation Practices (e.g., false individual cremation / ash
      tampering): Imprisonment ranging from one (1) year to three (3) years and
      a fine ranging from Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱200,000.00) to One
      Million Pesos (₱1,000,000.00), alongside immediate revocation of the
      establishment's license and permanent disqualification of the
      owner/operator from engaging in animal-related businesses.
   c. For Minor Sanitary or Administrative Violations: Suspension of license for
      six (6) months and a fine ranging from Fifty Thousand Pesos (₱50,000.00)
      to One Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00).
   d. If the offense is committed by a corporation, partnership, or association,
      the penalty shall be imposed upon the officers, directors, or managers who
      are responsible for or consented to the violation. Furthermore, foreign
      nationals convicted under this Act shall be deported immediately after
      serving sentence without need of further deportation proceedings.
SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR).
Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of
Agriculture, through the Bureau of Animal Industry and the Committee on
Animal Welfare, in consultation with the DENR, DOH, DILG, and registered
animal welfare NGOs, shall formulate and issue the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.
SEC. 12. Appropriations.
The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be charged
against the current year's appropriations of the Department of Agriculture.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
SEC. 13. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 15. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
