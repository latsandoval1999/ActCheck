                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The Philippines continues to face mounting economic pressures
brought about by rising prices of fuel, transportation, electricity, food, and
other essential goods and services. These increases have been aggravated
by ongoing global economic uncertainties, geopolitical tensions, disruptions
in international supply chains, and volatility in global oil markets, all of
which have directly affected the cost of living for ordinary Filipino families.
Recent reports likewise indicate that inflationary pressures have intensified
anew due to rising global fuel prices linked to instability in the Middle East.1

       As an oil import-dependent country, the Philippines remains
particularly vulnerable to fluctuations in global energy prices and external
economic shocks. The Philippine Statistics Authority reported that headline

including the excise tax component, government VAT collections rise
mechanically as prices rise. The current crisis has therefore produced a
fiscal windfall: The national government is collecting additional VAT
revenue as a direct consequence of the price increases that are burdening
Filipino households.

       At a time when Filipino households are struggling with the rising cost
of basic necessities, the government must adopt responsive and people-
centered measures that can immediately ease the burden on consumers. A
reduction in the VAT rate, which is the single broadest consumption tax in
the country, would directly address this burden by lowering the tax
component embedded in the price of almost every good and service.

       The VAT system, by its nature, is a consumption tax that
disproportionately affects low- and middle-income households, as a larger
portion of their income is spent on daily necessities. While existing VAT
exemptions on raw food and other basic necessities provide partial relief,
the bulk of household consumption such as fuel, electricity, transport,
processed food, and household goods all remain subject to VAT. Reducing
the VAT rate from twelve percent (12%) to ten percent (10%) will provide

temper inflationary pressures, increase household purchasing power,
stimulate consumer spending, and support economic activity during a
period of global uncertainty.

       Moreover, a lower VAT rate can provide relief not only to consumers
but also to small businesses and enterprises that continue to recover from
economic disruptions and weakened consumer demand. By easing the
overall tax burden on transactions, the proposed measure seeks to promote
a more equitable and sustainable economic recovery. Unlike a suspension of
fuel excise taxes, a VAT reduction reaches every household through every
taxable transaction, is verifiable by consumers on every receipt, and can be
offset through progressive revenue measures to mitigate or neutralize the
fiscal loss.

       This bill therefore seeks to amend Sections 106 (A), 107 (A), and 108
(A) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, by reducing
the VAT rate from twelve percent (12%) to ten percent (10%) on the sale of
goods and properties, importation of goods, and services, including digital
services and the use or lease of properties.

                                 CHAPTER I

                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

     Section 1. Short Title. – This Act will be known as the "VAT
Reduction Act.”

     Sec. 2. Section 106 (A) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997,
as amended, is hereby further amended to read as follows:

     “Section 106. Value-Added Tax on Sale of Goods or Properties. –

     (A)      Rate and Base of Tax. – There shall be levied, assessed and
           collected on every sale, barter or exchange of goods or properties, a
           value-added tax equivalent to [TWELVE PERCENT (12%)] TEN
           PERCENT (10%) of the gross sales of the goods or properties sold,

      Sec. 3. Section 107 (A) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997,
as amended, is hereby further amended to read as follows:

      “Section 107. Value-Added Tax on Imported Goods. –

      (A)      In General. – There shall be levied, assessed and collected on
            every importation of goods a value-added tax equivalent to
            [TWELVE PERCENT (12%)] TEN PERCENT (10%) based on the
            total value used by the Bureau of Customs in determining tariff
            and customs duties plus customs duties, excise taxes, if any, and
            other charges, such tax to be paid by the importer prior to the
            release of such goods from customs custody: Provided, That where
            the customs duties are determined on the basis of the quantity or
            volume of the goods, the value-added tax shall be based on the
            landed cost plus excise taxes, if any.

      Sec. 4. Section 108 (A) of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997,
as amended, are hereby further amended to read as follows:

      “Section 108. Value-Added Tax on the Sale of Services, Including
Digital Services, and the Use or Lease of Properties. –

      (A)      Rate and Base of Tax. – There shall be levied, assessed and
            collected, a value-added tax equivalent to [TWELVE PERCENT
            (12%)] TEN PERCENT (10%) of the gross sales derived from the
            sale or exchange of services, including digital services, and the use
            or lease of properties.

      Sec. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance, in upon the
recommendation of the Bureau of Internal Revenue, must promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

      Sec. 6. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act or the
application thereof to any person or circumstance is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, other provisions of this Act that are not affected will
continue to be in full force and effect.

      Sec. 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees or
issuances, executive orders, letters of instruction, administrative orders,
rules, or regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, modified, or amended accordingly.

      Sec. 8. Effectivity. – This Act takes effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
