20                                              EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   Meaningful participation of citizens and civil society organizations in local governance
23   is essential to responsive, transparent, and accountable government. While existing
24   laws recognize the role of civil society in local development, there remains a need for a
25   clearer and more consistent mechanism through which organized citizens can
26   participate in local decision-making and work with local governments.
27
28   Hence, this bill seeks to address this need by establishing a People’s Council in every
29   local government unit and providing a clear framework for the accreditation,
30   organization, recognition, and participation of civil society organizations in local
31   governance.
32
33   The measure provides People’s Councils with a formal role in local planning, policy-
34   making, program implementation and evaluation, as well as representation in local
35   boards, councils, committees, task forces, and other similar bodies. The objective of
36   this measure is clear – that is, to ensure that local policies and programs reflect the
37   lived experiences of the communities they serve.
38
39   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

20                                  AN ACT
21             CREATING A SYSTEM OF PARTNERSHIP BETWEEN LOCAL
22         GOVERNMENTS AND CIVIL SOCIETY ORGANIZATIONS THROUGH THE
23            ESTABLISHMENT OF A PEOPLE'S COUNCIL IN EVERY LOCAL
24        GOVERNMENT UNIT, PRESCRIBING ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS AND
25        PROVIDING FOR ITS PARTICIPATION IN THE LOCAL SANGGUNIAN AND
26        LOCAL BOARDS, COUNCILS, COMMITTEES, TASK FORCES OR SIMILAR
27                               WORK GROUPS
28
29   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
30   Congress assembled:
31
32       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "People
33   Empowerment Act.”
34
35           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared a policy of the State to
36   ensure participation of citizens in all avenues of local governance. Likewise, the State
37   acknowledges the value of creating a mechanism where its citizens' views and opinions
38   in different areas of governance may be heard and considered. Furthermore, pursuant
39   to Article II, Section 23 of the Constitution, the State recognizes the value of non-
40   government, community-based, or sectoral organizations in nation building and in
41   creating an avenue to ensure their growth and development. The State also recognizes
42   that the partnership with people's organizations (POs) and civil society organizations
43   (CSOs) will spur development in local communities as envisioned by the Local
44   Government Code of 1991.
45
46          SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:
47
48          (a) Civil society organization refers to any organized citizens' group including POs
49          and non-government organizations such as community groups, labor unions,
50          indigenous groups, charitable        organizations,    faith-based     organizations,
51          professional associations, and foundations;
52
53          (b) Local sanggunian refers to the local legislative body specifically the
 1         Sangguniang Panlungsod for cities, and the Sangguniang Bayan for
 2         municipalities;
 3
 4         (c) Non-government organization refers to an independent private and non-
 5         profit organization formed primarily to promote social and economic
 6         development with none of its officers or members occupying an elective position;
 7
 8         (d) People's council refers to a body composed of representatives of
 9         registered or accredited CSOs and POs which shall be created in every city or
10         municipality and provincial levels as mandated in Section 8 of this Act; and
11
12         (e) People's organization refers to a bona fide cooperative or association of
13         citizens organized for purposes not contrary to law, with demonstrated capacity
14         to promote the public interest and with identifiable leadership, membership,
15         and structure whose officers and members do not occupy any elective
16         government position.
17
18         SEC. 4. Accreditation of CSOs. – To be duly recognized and allowed to
19   participate in local governance, a CSO shall have actively operated for at least one
20   (1) year in the city or municipality where majority of its members reside, and must apply
21   for accreditation with the local sanggunian. An application for accreditation shall be
22   approved by the local sanggunian, upon submission of the following requirements:
23
24        (a) Proof of existence and operation in the respective city or municipality it is
25        applying in for at least one (1) year prior to the application for registration;
26
27        (b) Proof of activities held in pursuit of developmental objectives or of
28        organizational activities conducted;
29
30        (c) Program of activities planned for the year following the date of application
31        for registration;
32
33        (d) Copies of its Constitution, By-laws and/or Articles of Incorporation;
34
35        (e) Lists of its officers and members of good standing and their respective
36        addresses;
37
38        (f) Financial Statement and Declaration of Assets and Liabilities; and
39
40        (g) Board Resolution manifesting a decision to register and participate under this
41        Act.
42
43        A CSO whose application for accreditation has been approved shall be
44   issued a certificate containing the terms and conditions for accreditation.
45
46         A local sanggunian, however, may from time to time, and in consultation with
47   the organized People's Council, impose such other requirements and conditions for
48   registration as it may deem appropriate to best adhere to the principles of this Act.
49
50        Any CSO already accredited by a local government prior to the effectivity of this
51   Act need not register for purposes of this Act, unless its accreditation has in the
 1   meantime been withdrawn.
 2
 3         SEC. 5. Accreditation Deemed Inactive. – An accredited CSO shall submit, by
 4   January 31 of every year, a list of accomplishments of the previous year and its plans
 5   for the current year. The failure to submit these requirements for two (2) consecutive
 6   years shall render the CSO's accreditation inactive and thus disqualifies the CSO
 7   from availing of the rights and privileges thus vested upon registration mandated in
 8   Section 7 of this Act.
 9
10         A CSO may apply for the reactivation of its accreditation after submitting the
11   required list of accomplishments and plans for the current year.
12
13        SEC. 6. Committee for the Accreditation of CSOs. – There shall be a
14   Committee for the Accreditation of CSOs in all provincial, city and municipal
15   sanggunians. It shall perform the following functions:
16
17          (a) Receive, evaluate and process applications for registration;
18
19          (b) Monitor compliance with the conditions for accreditation; and
20
21          (c) Initiate legislative measures for the effective performance of its tasks.
22
23          The People's Council created under Section 8 hereof, once formally organized,
24   shall be entitled to a two (2)-seat representation in the Committee.
25
26          SEC. 7. Rights and Privileges of Accredited CSOs. – An accredited CSO
27   shall enjoy the following rights and privileges:
28
29          (a) Enter into joint ventures and other cooperative undertakings with the
30          local government to engage in the delivery of certain basic services, capability-
31          building and livelihood projects;
32
33          (b) Develop local enterprises designed to improve productivity and income,
34          diversify agriculture, spur rural industrialization, promote ecological balance,
35          and enhance the economic and social well-being of the people within the
36          framework of equitable and sustainable development;
37
38          (c) Receive assistance from the local government for economic, socially-
39          oriented, environmental, good governance, or cultural projects to be
40          implemented within their territorial jurisdiction; and
41
42          (d) Automatic membership in the People's Council.
43
44          SEC. 8. Creation of People's Councils. – A People's Council composed of
45   accredited CSOs shall be created in each local government unit (LGU). For this
46   purpose, the Committee for the Accreditation of CSOs of each local sanggunian shall,
47   not later than ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, call for a convention of
48   all accredited CSOs to facilitate the formal organization of the People's Council.
49
50        SEC. 9. Structure and Rules of the People's Council. – Each People's
51   Council shall determine its own organizational structure and internal rules, but shall at
 1   all times provide for adequate consultation mechanisms for purposes of obtaining the
 2   views and suggestions of all political parties or movements, government employees'
 3   organizations, other non-accredited but legitimate CSOs, and accredited CSOs which
 4   are not members of the People's Council: Provided, That no political party,
 5   government employees' organization or non-registered CSOs may be nominated by the
 6   Council for membership in the LGUs special bodies nor granted the rights and privileges
 7   of accredited CSOs under this Act: Provided further, That no provision herein shall be
 8   interpreted to prohibit the Council from changing its name or from being organized
 9   for purposes other than those indicated in this Act.
10
11          SEC. 10. Recognition of the People's Council by the Local Sanggunian. –
12   Only one (1) People's Council shall be recognized by each local sanggunian. Each
13   local sanggunian is hereby mandated to issue a certificate of recognition for the People's
14   Council within thirty (30) days from the filing of a petition for recognition and shall
15   pass a Sanggunian Resolution embodying the rights and privileges of the recognized
16   People's Council.
17
18          SEC. 11. Withdrawal of Recognition of the People's Council. – A local
19   sanggunian can withdraw the recognition granted to a People's Council only upon
20   action of a petitioner, and based on the following grounds:
21
22          (a) That the People's Council allowed itself or any of its member organizations
23          to engage primarily in, or to be used primarily for, partisan political activities; and
24
25          (b) Other grounds to be provided for in the rules and regulations to be
26          issued to implement this Act.
27
28         The local sanggunian shall not recognize internal or inter-organizational
29   conflicts within the People's Council.
30
31          SEC. 12. Powers and Responsibilities of the People's Council. – Upon
32   recognition, the People's Council may, in accordance with its policies and internal
33   rules, exercise the following powers and responsibilities:
34
35          (a) Elect or appoint, from among its member organizations, its representatives
36          to all local government bodies, boards, councils, committees, task forces
37          special government bodies and other similar work groups which the local
38          government or national laws may hereinafter create. Member organizations
39          elected or appointed to represent the people's Council shall have the sole
40          prerogative to choose, from among their bona fide members, the persons
41          who shall represent the People's Council in the respective boards, councils,
42          committees, task forces or special bodies. Unless otherwise provided herein,
43          such representatives shall not exceed twenty-five percent (25%) of the
44          membership of the board, council, committee, task force or special body;
45
46          (b) Participate in the conceptualization, implementation and evaluation of
47          government activities: Provided, That these functions shall be exercised without
48          compensation or remuneration. The concerned local government shall, however,
49          provide for the necessary office spaces, facilities and equipment for the use of the
50          People's Council;
51
 1         (c) Observe, and participate in the deliberation, conceptualization,
 2         implementation and evaluation of projects, activities and programs of the LGU,
 3         propose legislation and participate at the committee level of the local sanggunian;
 4         and
 5
 6         (d) Elect a representative to the provincial People's Council.
 7
 8           SEC. 13. Creation of a Provincial People's Council (PPC). – A PPC shall be
 9   created and recognized by the Sangguniang Panlalawigan and shall be composed of all
10   representatives from the municipal and city People's Councils within a province's
11   jurisdiction.
12
13         The PPC shall elect, from within its ranks, a representative who shall sit as a
14   member of the Sanggunian Panlalawigan and shall exercise the duties and powers of a
15   Provincial Board member
16
17         SEC. 14. Non-Partisan Nature of the People's Council. – The People's
18   Council shall not engage in, or allow itself or its member organizations to be used for
19   purposes of partisan politics and shall adopt such measures to ensure that it is
20   adequately shielded from any political partisanship or influence.
21
22          As used in this Act, partisan politics shall refer to any activity, statement,
23   or manifestation which solely or primarily serves to campaign for or against any
24   particular political party or any candidate for any elective public office.
25
26           SEC. 15. Right to Self-Organization. – Pursuant to Article XIII, Section 15
27   of the Constitution, all CSOs are recognized as independent and autonomous self-help
28   organizations. They are encouraged to organize into formal cooperatives, labor unions,
29   interest groups, non-government organizations, sectoral organizations and people's
30   organizations in furtherance of their constitutionally mandated role of enabling the
31   people to pursue and protect, within democratic framework, their legitimate and
32   collective interests and aspirations through peaceful and lawful means.
33
34          Furthermore, the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and
35   all LGUs are mandated to assist and support efforts of the people towards self-
36   organization to address common concerns, and to promote common welfare and
37   interests.
38
39          SEC. 16. Information Boards and Suggestion Boxes. – Information boards
40   and suggestion boxes shall be provided, maintained and controlled solely by each
41   barangay, to be strategically situated in public plazas, government buildings and
42   offices, public markets, schools, and at such public places accessible to the people,
43   which boards and boxes shall be used other than those maintained by the barangays
44   and the national government for other
45   purposes.
46
47          LGUs are mandated to respond within fifteen (15) working days to the suggestions
48   filed by citizens in suggestion boxes.
49
50          SEC. 17. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of
51   this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
 1
 2          SEC. 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within one hundred twenty
 3   (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DILG and LGUs shall, in consultation with
 4   accredited CSOs who have previously engaged in concerned undertakings with
 5   local governments, determine effective ways and methods to ensure the success of
 6   the objectives of this Act and craft the rules and regulations to implement it.
 7
 8         SEC. 19. Sunset Review. – Five (5) years from the effectivity of this Act,
 9   Congress shall review and recommend amendments thereto, after evaluating the
10   problems and issues in its implementation.
11
12          SEC. 20. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
13   or unconstitutional, the other provisions, not affected thereby, shall remain valid and
14   subsisting.
15
16          SEC. 21. Repealing Clause. – All other laws, presidential decrees, executive
17   orders, proclamations and administrative regulations which are inconsistent with the
18   provisions of this Act are hereby amended, modified, superseded, or repealed
19   accordingly.
20
21          SEC. 22. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
22   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
