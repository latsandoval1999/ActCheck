                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE
      Access to food remains an urgent issue for a large percentage of the
Philippine population. As recently as March 2026, a Social Weather Station
(SWS) survey1 showed that almost a quarter of the country’s population
experienced involuntary hunger (23.2%), of which 5.5% experienced severe
hunger. Such issues arise from the precarious state of the nation’s food
security and the inadequate access to safe, reliable, and nutritious food
sources.
      Tackling food waste goes hand-in-hand with helping alleviate food
insecurity in the country and reducing greenhouse gas emissions. As part of
global and national actions to end hunger and curb carbon pollution, an Act
that mandates food establishments to donate edible food surplus can help
tackle the twin problems of food security and reduced emissions.
      Although the Philippines is making progress in fulfilling its commitments
to SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) and SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and
Production), more rigorous legislation is needed to sustain this progress and
divert food waste towards food banks. Child stunting, for instance, remains
prevalent, with as many as 23.6 of the population experiencing stunting due to
malnutrition2.
      The ongoing 2026 fuel crisis brought on by the conflict in West Asia is
also having negative impacts on the nation’s inflation, which has detrimental

      There is a very pressing need to establish clear guidelines for

bill effectively aims to tackle the pressure points that prevent a large
percentage of the population from accessing safe and nutritious food. By
mandating food establishments to donate their excess edible food and
distributing it to the marginalized via food banks, we can slowly solve
malnutrition, childhood stunting, and hunger in our Philippine islands.
         In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is urgently sought.

     AN ACT TO REDUCE FOOD WASTE BY REQUIRING EDIBLE FOOD
      SURPLUS TO BE DONATED TO FOOD BANKS AND CHARITIES
       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:
     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Anti Food
Waste Act of 2026”
       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes the universal
right to access safe and edible food. It is the purview of the State to provide the
necessary conditions not only for the survival of its citizens, but of their safety
and comfort, and this begins at the assurance that every Filipino has food on
their table. The high number of food waste is both a tragedy and a solution.
This Act intends to divert such waste, which may by all standards, still fit for
human consumption and be given to those that live in hunger. Towards this
end, the State shall adopt a system to promote, facilitate, and ensure the
reduction of food waste through redistribution, diversion, and repurposing.
      SECTION 3. Definitions. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms
shall mean:
      (a) Food waste is defined as food removed from the human food supply
      chain, including all the crop and livestock human-edible food that,
      directly or indirectly, completely exit the human food supply chain by
      being discarded, incinerated or otherwise, and do not reenter in any
      other utilization (such as animal feed, industrial use, etc.), up to, and
      excluding, the retail level. Losses that occur during storage, transport
      and processing, also of imported quantities, are therefore all included;
     (b) Food refers to any raw, cooked, processed, or prepared edible
     substance, ice, beverage, or ingredient used or intended for use in whole
     or in part for human consumption. “Food” includes drink, and any
     substance that has been used in the manufacture, preparation or
     treatment of food. Therefore food waste includes both;
     (c) Edible food surplus refers to wholesome edible food that is unsold,
     unserved, or unused but still fit for human consumption based on
     standards set by the National Nutrition Council, the Food and Drugs
     Administration, the Department of Agriculture, and other relevant
     government agencies;
     (d) Inedible food surplus refers to components associated with a food
     that are not intended to be consumed by humans such as bones, rinds
     and pits/stones;
     (e) Wholesome edible food refers to food that meets all quality and
     labeling standards imposed by pertinent laws and administrative
     regulations regardless if it may not be readily marketable due to
     appearance, age, freshness, grade, size, surplus, or other conditions. It
     does not include milk products as defined and covered under Executive
     Order No. 51, otherwise known as the Milk Code, which prohibits
     donation of milk products of any kind;
     (f) Food insecure is defined as the failure to access necessary food needs
     for human consumption, nutrition, and survival;
     (g) Food Service Sector encompasses private and public establishments
     where food is manufactured or sold in substantial quantities, in either
     retail or wholesale settings. This could include large subsectors such as
     hotels, cafes, and bars; caterers, eateries, and cafeterias; retailers,
     grocery stores, supermarkets, and convenience stores;
     (h) Food banks encompass non-profit, non-stock organizations operating
     to serve social, public, and charitable causes and directly involved in
     storing and distributing donated food to feeding programs, social welfare
     missions, and other charitable organizations;
      SECTION 4. Covered Establishments in the Food Service Sector. – The Act
applies to establishments within the Food Service Sector. These include:
        (a)​ Food manufacturers involved in producing raw food materials to
             wholesome, edible food. Provided that the discarded food is labeled
             wholesome and edible and does not go against pertinent laws
             related to food manufacturing and food safety;
        (b)​Retailers (markets,      major     grocery   stores,   and     formal
            supermarkets);
        (c)​ Food establishments (restaurants, fast food chains, cafes, bars,
             canteens, and catering services); and
        (d)​Hospitality (hotels, resorts, and inns).
      SECTION 5. Mandatory Food Donation Mechanisms. – Establishments
and manufacturers within the Food Service Sector are mandated to donate
their edible food surplus to accredited food banks, government social welfare
programs, and to LGU-facilitated redistribution schemes:
        (a)​ Waste Segregation. The owners and operators of food
             manufacturers must segregate their surplus to edible and inedible
             food. To facilitate distribution, food manufacturers may perform
             segregation at the retailers/supermarkets that sell their products;
        (b)​Those deemed edible food, upon inspection of a duly-accredited
            Health Inspector from the LGU, in accordance with NNC and FDA
            standards, can be donated;
        (c)​ Food that are edible and certified for donation can be distributed to
             food banks as determined by the DSWD;
        (d)​Food banks, in coordination with DSWD and LGUs, will distribute
            the edible food to the food insecure;
        (e)​ Establishments may opt to post their surplus at the close of
             business operations, where DSWD-recognized charities and
             non-profit organizations may claim the surplus for distribution to
             the food insecure; and
        (f)​ Barangay-Facilitated Redistribution. LGUs may mandate the
             formation of barangay food distribution systems for rapid
             deployment of edible food surpluses. Establishments may report
             their daily surplus to the barangay, which deploys trained LGU
             health inspectors to verify food safety prior to immediate dispatch
             to food-insecure households.
      SECTION 6. Food Waste Reduction Mechanisms. – Food Service Sector
establishments are hereby required to:
        (a)​ Submit their respective reports to the DSWD and DENR that
             contains data on the amount (in tonnes) of its edible and inedible
           food waste in the past year, organized according to the manner of
           disposal, including donation, composting or discarding;
        (b)​Enter into a contract with food banks to redistribute edible food
            waste to the food insecure;
        (c)​ Ensure that the edible food waste is unadulterated and in good
             condition upon arrival at the food bank's distribution center;
        (d)​Enter into a contract with waste management and recycling
            enterprises to recycle inedible food waste into fertilizer or compost;
        (e)​ Enter into contract with animal industries and farms to divert food
             scraps to animal food; and
        (f)​ Reach and maintain food waste levels at the target set by the
             DENR.
      SECTION 7. National Anti-Food Waste Framework. – DSWD, serving as
the primary coordinating agency between food service sector establishments
and food banks, shall be mandated to:
        (a)​ Formulate comprehensive guidelines and minimum standards
             governing the safe collection, warehousing, and redistribution of
             edible surplus food directed to food banks;
        (b)​Ensure that food businesses have executed formal agreements
            with accredited food banks or designated barangay-level initiatives,
            and issue corresponding certificates of compliance to participating
            establishments;
        (c)​ Promote linkages between food banks and LCDs to create a
             community-based food distribution system for the food insecure;
             and
        (d)​Institute a Self-Sufficiency Program that will provide the food
            insecure with skills training on managing food banks and
            livelihood programs to mitigate the sole dependence on food
            donations.
      SECTION 8. Household and Local Government Unit Waste Reduction
Strategy. – LGUs are hereby required to:
        (a)​ Submit an annual report that contains data on the amount (in
             tonnes) of inedible food waste that can be recycled as raw material
             for fertilizers or compost to the DENR;
         (b)​Initiate waste segregation efforts per household through local
             campaigns;
         (c)​ Enter into a contract with waste management and recycling
              enterprises to recycle inedible food waste from households into
              fertilizer or compost;
         (d)​Shoulder the costs of transporting inedible food waste from
             collection areas to waste management sites; and
         (e)​ Reach and maintain inedible food waste levels at the target set by
              DENR.
      SECTION 9. Training and accreditation of Health Inspectors. – The DOH,
in coordination with the FDA, shall conduct seminars and provide training to
LGU health inspectors regarding the proper segregation and identification of
wholesome edible food surplus and inedible food surplus.
      SECTION 10. Tax Incentive. - Food Service Sector-related businesses
that donate edible food to food banks shall be exempt from donor's tax and the
same shall be considered as an allowable deduction from their gross income, in
accordance with the provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC),
as amended.
       SECTION 11. Penalties. - The penalty of a fine amounting to one
hundred thousand pesos (Php 100,000.00) will be imposed on any individual,
private or public, who deliberately makes food loss unfit for consumption for
the first offense and escalating fines and mandatory closure for succeeding
offenses, without prejudice to other applicable laws. The same penalty is
applicable to private or public actors who prevent the redirection of food loss to
food distributing institutions or food waste to waste management and recycling
enterprises.
      SECTION 12. Liability of Damage from Food Donations. – A donating food
service sector establishment shall not be held liable for damages arising from
the nature, age, packaging, or condition of apparently wholesome food that is
donated in good faith for charitable purposes, and as deemed edible by
duly-accredited health inspectors. This shall not apply, however, to an injury or
death of an ultimate beneficiary of the donated food that results from an act or
omission of a constituting gross negligence or intentional misconduct.
      SECTION 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DSWD, in
coordination with the DOH, the NNC; DENR, DOST, DA, FDA, and other
relevant government agencies, recognized non-government organizations and
private entities shall formulate and issue the necessary rules and regulations
for the implementation of this Act within ninety (60) days after the effectivity of
this Act.
       SECTION 14. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.
      SECTION 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
      SECTION 16. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
