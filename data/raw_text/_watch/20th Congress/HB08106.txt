AND APPROPRIATIONS

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The early years of a child’s life are critical. When delays in speech, movement,
learning, or behavior are not detected early, they can lead to lasting dif iculties in
school, health, and daily life. But when children are screened early and given the right
support, they are more likely to develop well, be ready for kindergarten, and live more
independent and healthy lives. Early action also reduces stress on families and lowers
long-term costs for government services.

Our laws already recognize this need. Existing policies require the government to ind
children with special needs early and provide timely assessment, referral, and
intervention through coordinated health and education services. However, many
families still face confusion and delays. Parents often do not know where to go for
screening, referrals between health centers and schools are weak, and services are
uneven, especially in poor and remote communities. As a result, many children are
identi ied too late, when help is already more dif icult and costly.

This bill turns existing commitments into real, working systems at the community
level. It sets up a clear and child-friendly pathway for early screening, assessment,
intervention, and smooth transition to kindergarten, with shared responsibility

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Early Childhood Special
Needs Screening and Intervention Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to ensure the healthy
growth and development of children, promote inclusive development and education,
and ensure that children with disabilities and other special needs are identi ied
early, assessed properly, and provided timely interventions and transition
services through accessible, coordinated, and community-based mechanisms.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   (a) “Children with Disabilities / Learners with Disabilities” shall refer to
       children covered by the inclusive education framework and related laws, and
       shall include children with developmental delays and disabilities requiring
       early identi ication, intervention, and transition support.


                                                                           3|Page
   (b) “Children with Special Needs” refers to children eighteen (18) years old and
       below who, due to disability, developmental delay, or other conditions that
       require additional support, need early identi ication, assessment,
       intervention, and transition services; Provided, that this term shall be
       interpreted in a manner consistent with inclusive education and ECCD
       standards.

   (c) “Child Find System (CFS)” refers to the coordinated mechanism for
       identi ication, location, evaluation, referral, and facilitation of access to
       appropriate services for children with disabilities and other special needs,
       including those not yet in school.

   (d) “Early Intervention Program” refers to educational, therapeutic, medical,
       nutritional, or family support services designed to reduce the effects of
       disabilities or prevent the occurrence of learning and development problems
       for children from birth to ive (5) years, consistent with existing law.

   (e) “Transition Program” refers to a coordinated set of activities to support
       movement from early childhood and school into post-school activities
       including vocational training and integrated employment, consistent with
       existing law.

   (f) “Inclusive Learning Resource Center (ILRC)” refers to the service hub
       established under existing law that delivers free support and related services,
       multidisciplinary assessments, referral systems, and IEP support for learners
       with disabilities.

SECTION 4. Strengthened Early Identi ication and Intervention Program. — The
Department of Education (DepEd), the Department of Health (DOH), and the ECCD
Council, in coordination with local government units (LGUs), shall strengthen and
operationalize a national program providing comprehensive, accessible, and
affordable early identi ication, referral, intervention, and transition services for
children with disabilities and other special needs.

For children below ive (5) years old, the program shall be implemented through
and consistent with the ECCD System.

For children ive (5) years old and above, the program shall be implemented in
coordination with DepEd and the ILRC service delivery system under inclusive
education law.

SECTION 5. Objectives. — The Program shall have the following objectives:

   (a) To increase public awareness and reduce barriers to early screening,
       assessment, diagnosis, referral, and evidence-based interventions;

   (b) To build and sustain a quali ied workforce and service network capable of
       delivering multidisciplinary services;

   (c) To develop and implement a continuous public information campaign on early
       childhood development, disability inclusion, and service access;

   (d) To establish a quality assurance and shared accountability system, including
       outcome indicators and data tracking consistent with privacy and child
       protection standards; and

   (e) To strengthen coordinated transition planning for children with disabilities
       and other special needs, including transition to school, community
       participation, and, where appropriate, vocational pathways.

SECTION 6. Service Delivery Mechanism; Role of ILRCs and Local Pathways. —

   (a) ILRC as Hub for Assessment and Referral. Consistent with existing law, the
       ILRC shall serve as the primary hub in each city/municipality/school district
       for education-related assessment, IEP-related functions, referrals, and support
       services for learners with disabilities.

   (b) LGU Service Pathway. Each LGU shall establish and publish a local Child Find
       and Early Intervention Pathway specifying: (i) where and how children may
       be screened; (ii) referral points to ILRCs, DOH facilities, and accredited
       providers; (iii) turnaround timelines for assessment and referrals; and (iv)
       available support services.

   (c) No Duplication of Centers. LGUs may establish special needs centers or
       support units, but such shall be designed to complement—not duplicate or
       con lict with—the ILRC functions under existing law.

SECTION 7. Inter-Agency Coordination and Implementation. —



                                                                          5|Page
   (a) The DepEd bureau responsible for learning delivery shall coordinate with
       implementing partner agencies through an effective mechanism for
       implementation and enforcement of laws for learners with disabilities.

   (b) The DOH shall ensure linkage of the Program to health screening,
       developmental surveillance, diagnostics, habilitation/rehabilitation services,
       and appropriate referral networks.

   (c) Coordination with LGUs shall include training, technical assistance, and shared
       monitoring tools.

SECTION 8. Appropriations. —

   (a) A dedicated budget line item shall be included in the annual appropriations of
       DepEd and other implementing partner agencies for the delivery of services,
       including assessment and diagnosis, transportation and support services, and
       assistive devices and learning materials, consistent with existing inclusive
       education budgeting.

   (b) The amount necessary for initial implementation shall be charged against
       existing appropriations of DepEd, DOH, and other partner agencies, and
       thereafter included as separate line items in the annual General
       Appropriations Act, consistent with existing law.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
effectivity, the DepEd, DOH, and the ECCD Council, in coordination with the DILG and
LGUs and in consultation with relevant private sector stakeholders and organizations
of persons with disabilities, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations
(IRR) to carry out this Act, harmonized with existing inclusive education and ECCD
regulations.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modi ied accordingly.
