                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The State shall protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at
all levels and shall take appropriate steps to make such education accessible to all.1 At
the local level, this commitment is manifested through Section 272 of the Local
Government Code of 1991 (“LGC”). Section 272 allocates one percent of real property tax
collections to the Special Education Fund (“SEF”). When the LGC was enacted the
enumeration of allowable SEF expenditures corresponded to the education programs that
existed at that time. More than three decades have passed, and numerous new education
programs, not included in the previous list, have come into existence.

       Local school boards are now expected to implement Early Childhood Care and
Development (“ECCD”), the Alternative Learning System (“ALS”), and inclusive
education for learners with disabilities – programs that Section 272 does not authorize
the SEF to fund. This mismatch has produced a detrimental fiscal consequence. The
Second Congressional Commission on Education documented approximately fifteen
billion pesos in unspent SEF collections in 2022 alone, which could have been used to
address the education crisis.2

       In the 2022 Programme for International Student Assessment, the Philippines
placed among the five lowest-performing countries out of 81 participants. Only 16
percent of Filipino students reached minimum proficiency in mathematics, 24 percent in
reading, and 23 percent in science.3 As of the end of School Year 2024 to 2025, the
Comprehensive Rapid Literacy Assessment revealed that 48.76 percent of learners in
Grades 1 to 3 are still not reading at their respective grade levels.4 This bill, by expanding
the enumerated programs for which SEF Fund can be used for, seeks to prevent billions
of pesos worth of fiscal firepower to remain unutilized when it could have been used to
help solve the education crisis.

      Aligned with the wisdom of this initiative, the executive branch already
responded through DILG-DepEd-DBM-DOF Joint Circular No. 1, series of 2025,
expanding allowable SEF uses to cover all three programs. By including said expansion

       This bill amends Section 272 of the Local Government Code to permanently
authorize SEF expenditures on ECCD, the ALS, and inclusive education for learners with
disabilities. The sole purpose is to align the statute with the education system it was
always meant to support.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
AMENDING SECTION 272 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7160, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
THE "LOCAL GOVERNMENT CODE OF 1991," TO EXPAND THE ALLOWABLE
USES OF THE SPECIAL EDUCATION FUND, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Amendment of Section 272. Section 272 of Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise
known as the Local Government Code of 1991, is hereby amended to read as follows:


       “Section 272. Application of Proceeds of the Additional One Percent SEF Tax. –
       The proceeds from the additional one percent (1%) tax on real property accruing
       to the Special Education Fund (SEF) shall be automatically released to the local
       school boards: Provided, that in case of provinces, the proceeds shall be divided
       equally between the provincial and municipal school boards: Provided, however,
       that the proceeds shall be allocated [for the operation and maintenance of public
       schools, construction and repair of school buildings, facilities and equipment,
       educational research, purchase of books and periodicals, and sports development]
       AS DETERMINED AND APPROVED BY THE LOCAL SCHOOL BOARD, FOR
       ANY OF THE FOLLOWING PURPOSES:


               (A) OPERATION AND MAINTENANCE OF PUBLIC SCHOOLS,

               (B) CONSTRUCTION, REPAIR, AND MAINTENANCE OF SCHOOL
               BUILDINGS,         LEARNING          CENTERS,         LIBRARIES,        SCHOOL

SECTION. 2. Inter-Local Cooperation for Education. A new Section 272-A is hereby
inserted after Section 272 of Republic Act No. 7160, to read as follows:


       “SEC. 272-A. INTER-LOCAL COOPERATION FOR EDUCATION. — LOCAL
       GOVERNMENT UNITS MAY, THROUGH APPROPRIATE ORDINANCES,
       ENTER INTO INTER-LOCAL COOPERATION AGREEMENTS FOR THE
       POOLING, SHARING, OR JOINT IMPLEMENTATION OF PROGRAMS
       FUNDED THROUGH THE SEF: PROVIDED, THAT PRIORITY SHALL BE
       GIVEN TO ARRANGEMENTS BENEFITING FOURTH, FIFTH, AND SIXTH
       CLASS MUNICIPALITIES WHOSE SEF COLLECTIONS ARE INSUFFICIENT TO
       SUSTAIN QUALITY ECCD, ALS, OR INCLUSIVE EDUCATION PROGRAMS:
       PROVIDED, FURTHER, THAT ALL SUCH AGREEMENTS SHALL SPECIFY
       THE PARTICIPATING LGUS, THE AMOUNT AND PURPOSE OF POOLED
       FUNDS,       GOVERNANCE        ARRANGEMENTS,          AND     ACCOUNTABILITY
      MECHANISMS, SUBJECT TO THE GUIDELINES TO BE ISSUED PURSUANT TO
      SECTION 5 OF THIS ACT.”


SECTION. 3. Local Education Office or Local School Board Secretariat. A new Section
272-B is hereby inserted after Section 272-A of Republic Act No. 7160, to read as follows:


      “SEC. 272-B. LOCAL EDUCATION OFFICE OR LOCAL SCHOOL BOARD
      SECRETARIAT. — EVERY LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNIT IS HEREBY
      AUTHORIZED TO ESTABLISH A LOCAL EDUCATION OFFICE OR, WHERE
      RESOURCES ARE LIMITED, A LOCAL SCHOOL BOARD SECRETARIAT, TO
      COORDINATE, MONITOR, AND REPORT ON EDUCATION PROGRAMS AND
      PROJECTS FUNDED THROUGH THE SEF. THE ORGANIZATIONAL
      STRUCTURE AND STAFFING PATTERN SHALL REFLECT THE LGU’S
      SERVICE REQUIREMENTS AND FINANCIAL CAPABILITY, SUBJECT TO
      MINIMUM        STANDARDS         PRESCRIBED       BY     THE     CIVIL    SERVICE
      COMMISSION.”


SECTION. 4. Reporting and Transparency. A new Section 272-C is hereby inserted after
Section 272-B of Republic Act No. 7160, to read as follows:


      “SEC. 272-C. REPORTING AND TRANSPARENCY. — ALL LOCAL SCHOOL
      BOARDS SHALL SUBMIT AN ANNUAL REPORT ON THE COLLECTION,
      ALLOCATION, AND UTILIZATION OF THE SPECIAL EDUCATION FUND TO
      THE BUREAU OF LOCAL GOVERNMENT FINANCE OF THE DEPARTMENT
      OF FINANCE (DOF-BLGF), WITH COPIES FURNISHED TO THE LOCAL
      SANGGUNIAN, THE REGIONAL OFFICES OF THE DEPARTMENT OF THE
      INTERIOR AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT (DILG), THE DEPARTMENT OF
      EDUCATION (DEPED), AND THE DEPARTMENT OF BUDGET AND
      MANAGEMENT (DBM), NOT LATER THAN THE THIRTY-FIRST (31ST ) DAY
      OF MARCH OF THE SUCCEEDING YEAR.


      THE DOF-BLGF SHALL SUBMIT A CONSOLIDATED REPORT ON SEF
      COLLECTIONS AND UTILIZATION TO THE SENATE COMMITTEES ON
      LOCAL GOVERNMENT AND ON EDUCATION, ARTS AND CULTURE, AND
      TO THE HOUSE COMMITTEES ON LOCAL GOVERNMENT AND ON BASIC
      EDUCATION AND CULTURE, NOT LATER THAN THE THIRTIETH (30TH)
      DAY OF JUNE OF EACH YEAR.


      ALL SEF REPORTS SHALL BE POSTED ON THE OFFICIAL WEBSITES OF THE
      CONCERNED LOCAL GO VERNMENT UNITS AND ON THE FULL
       DISCLOSURE POLICY PORTAL ADMINISTERED BY THE DILG, AND IN AT
       LEAST THREE (3) CONSPICUOUS PUBLIC PLACES IN THE LOCALITY.”


       SECTION. 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations. The DILG, DepEd, DOF, and
DBM shall jointly promulgate the implementing rules and regulations necessary to carry
out this Act within ninety (90) days from the date of its effectivity.


       SECTION. 6. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.


       SECTION. 7. Repealing Clause. All laws, orders, rules, and regulations contrary
to or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


       SECTION. 8. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
