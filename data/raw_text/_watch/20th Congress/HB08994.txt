                              EXPLANATORY NOTE


      The right to travel is a fundamental liberty afforded to every Filipino citizen under
Section 6, Article III of the 1987 Constitution. While the State has a duty to enforce
immigration laws and combat human trafficking, such authority must be exercised in
a manner that is fair, transparent, and respectful of other individual rights. In practice,
however, numerous Filipino passengers have experienced deferred or denied
departures due to extended immigration inspections, often resulting in missed flights
and financial losses, even in cases where no violation of law is ultimately established.
Concerns have been raised regarding the inconsistent, opaque, and at times arbitrary
implementation of outbound immigration procedures, particularly in cases involving
the denial of departure or so-called “offloading” of Filipino travelers.

     At present, there exists no clear statutory mechanism to provide relief to
passengers who incur financial harm as a result of such deferred departures. This gap
places an undue burden on law-abiding travelers, effectively penalizing them despite
the absence of any wrongdoing. The lack of accountability mechanisms also risks
undermining public confidence in immigration processes.

     This proposed measure seeks to address this gap by establishing a
reimbursement mechanism for Filipino passengers whose international departure is
deferred or denied by the Bureau of Immigration, in cases where such denial is not
based on lawful or clearly established grounds. By limiting reimbursement to the cost
of the outbound travel ticket and imposing reasonable eligibility conditions, the bill
ensures that relief is targeted, fair, and fiscally responsible.

       At the same time, the measure carefully preserves the State’s authority to
prevent human trafficking, illegal recruitment, and other transnational crimes. It
explicitly excludes cases involving fraudulent documents, misrepresentation,
trafficking indicators, or failure to comply with established legal requirements. In doing
so, the bill strikes a necessary balance between protecting individual rights and
maintaining effective border control.

     The measure further introduces clear procedures for filing, evaluating, and
resolving reimbursement claims, thereby promoting administrative efficiency,
transparency, and accountability. It also mandates reportorial requirements to ensure
oversight and proper use of public funds.

      Ultimately, this bill affirms that while the State may regulate the movement of
persons for legitimate purposes, it must also take responsibility when such regulation
results in unwarranted financial harm to its citizens. By institutionalizing a system of
redress, the measure strengthens both the integrity of immigration enforcement and
the protection of Filipino travelers.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                 AN ACT
     MANDATING THE REIMBURSEMENT OF TRAVEL EXPENSES INCURRED BY
     FILIPINO PASSENGERS WHOSE INTERNATIONAL DEPARTURE IS DEFERRED
     OR DENIED BY THE BUREAU OF IMMIGRATION, SUBJECT TO ANTI-
     TRAFFICKING SAFEGUARDS, AND PRESCRIBING THE CONDITIONS
     THEREFOR

         Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1        SECTION 1. Short Title - This Act shall be known as the "Passenger Protection
 2   and Reimbursement for Deferred Departures Act".
 3
 4        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 5   protect the rights, welfare, and dignity of Filipino travelers, consistent with their
 6   constitutional right to travel, while ensuring effective immigration enforcement.
 7
 8          Towards this end, this Act establishes a mechanism to provide financial relief to
 9   Filipino passengers whose international travel is delayed or deferred due to extended
10   immigration checks, without diminishing the authority of the Bureau of Immigration to
11   enforce immigration and anti-trafficking laws.
12
13        SEC. 3. Definition of Terms –
14
15        a. Court Order refers to a directive issued by a Philippine court of competent
16           jurisdiction preventing any attempt by a Filipino passenger from leaving the
17           Philippines.
18
19        b. Declared Sponsor refers to a person or organization that meets the
20           qualifications to sponsor a passenger's travel, per qualifications provided by
21           the IACAT Guidelines on Departure Formalities for International-Bound
22           Passengers;

 7           regardless of the travel purpose, whether for temporary or permanent
 8           migration, whose travel has been deferred by Immigration Officers due to

41        SEC. 4. Eligibility Criteria for Reimbursement Claims. – Any Filipino
42   passenger whose international departure from the Philippines is deferred or denied by
43   the Bureau of Immigration without a court order may be eligible to claim
44   reimbursement of travel expenses, subject to the conditions and limitations provided
45   under this Act.
46
47         Eligibility for reimbursement shall arise when the deferral or denial of departure
48   results from immigration inspection conducted in the course of immigration
49   assessment, verification, or clearance procedures.
50

 1        No reimbursement shall be granted where the deferral or denial of departure is
 2   due to any of the following:
 3
 4        a. Failure to present an Allow Departure Order (ADO) for passengers with an
 5           active Court Order or who are accused and released on bail;
 6        b. Failure to present a duly issued Travel Authority for international travel for
 7           government employees, as required under existing regulations;
 8        c. Failure to present required documents under the Inter-Agency Council
 9           Against Trafficking (IACAT) Guidelines on Departure Formalities for
10           International-Bound Filipino Passengers (Guidelines) and other government-
11           required supporting documents for international travels;
12        d. Any passenger who is identified to have fraudulent, falsified, or tampered
13           travel documents, or identified as a potential trafficked person, or has
14           misrepresented their travel purpose;
15        e. Passengers who are assessed as suspected illegal recruiters/traffickers
16           under RA No. 9208, as amended;
17        f. Travel/deployment restrictions imposed by the Department of Foreign Affairs
18           (DFA), the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW), and other authorized
19           government agencies; and
20        g. Failure to board the flight for causes other than the result of immigration
21           inspection.
22
23        SEC. 5. Filing of Reimbursement. – A claim for reimbursement of travel
24   expenses shall be filed personally by the passenger with the BI's International Port of
25   Entry and Exit Management Office (IPEE-MO) where the passenger was deferred for
26   departure, within thirty (30) calendar days from the date of the deferred departure.
27
28          SEC. 6. Evaluation of Reimbursement Claims. – The Bureau of Immigration
29   (BI), through its International Port of Entry and Exit Management Office (IPEE-MO) at
30   the international airport or seaport of exit where the passenger's departure was
31   deferred, shall receive and evaluate claims for reimbursement of travel expenses and
32   shall endorse the same to the Department of Justice-Inter-Agency Council Against
33   Trafficking (IACAT) for determination.
34
35         The IACAT shall assess the eligibility of the passenger based on records
36   generated during immigration inspection and shall resolve the reimbursement claim
37   within thirty (30) working days from receipt of the BI's endorsement.
38
39        SEC. 7. Notice of Decision and Finality. – The claimant shall be notified of the
40   decision on the reimbursement claim through the fastest means available, and a copy
41   thereof shall be furnished to the Bureau of Immigration. The determination of eligibility
42   and the amount of reimbursement shall be final and executory.
43
44        SEC. 8. Limitations for a Reimbursement Claim. –
45        a. Eligible passengers shall be entitled to only one (1) reimbursement claim per
46           year regardless of the number of deferred departures.
47        b. Only travel expenses that a passenger or a declared sponsor personally paid
48           for will be considered for reimbursement.
