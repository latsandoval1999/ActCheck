    AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE FILIPINO FAMILY MAINSTREAMING
    FRAMEWORK, ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR THE
  FILIPINO FAMILY (NCFF), MANDATING FAMILY IMPACT ASSESSMENTS IN
 PUBLIC POLICYMAKING AND GOVERNANCE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                              THEREFOR

                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Constitution gives the Filipino family a place of special constitutional
protection. Article II, Section 12 recognizes the sanctity of family life and directs the
State to protect and strengthen the family as a basic autonomous social institution.
Article XV further recognizes the Filipino family as the foundation of the nation and
mandates the State to strengthen its solidarity and actively promote its total
development.

       This constitutional command is now confronted by measurable social and
demographic realities. The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported that
registered marriages decreased from 414,213 in 2023 to 371,825 in 2024, while the
crude marriage rate declined from 3.7 to 3.3 marriages per thousand population. The
2025 National Demographic and Health Survey reported a total fertility rate of 1.7
children per woman aged 15 to 49 for the three-year period preceding the survey,
down from 1.9 in 2022 and below the replacement fertility level of 2.1 children per
woman. PSA likewise recorded 1,358,989 registered live births in 2024, 6.2 percent
fewer than the 1,448,522 registered in 2023. Of the 2024 registered live births,
808,965, or 59.5 percent, were non-marital births. This last figure does not, by itself,
establish poor family functioning; rather, it underscores the range of family
circumstances in which government must support children, parents, and caregivers.
        These demographic figures describe changing patterns of marriage, fertility,
and childbearing; they do not, standing alone, measure family functioning or parental
care. The case for family mainstreaming rests on the fact that child welfare and family
resilience are shaped across multiple policy domains. A child’s welfare is affected by
health, education, housing, labor, digital safety, local government capacity, and
household economic resilience. A parent’s ability to raise and protect a child is likewise
affected by employment conditions, childcare support, public safety, social protection,
transport systems, housing affordability, and access to reliable services. The condition
of the Filipino family is therefore a governance issue that cuts across agencies and
sectors.

       The risks facing Filipino children further demonstrate the need for a coordinated
family policy framework. UNICEF Philippines, citing the 2022 Disrupting Harm study
conducted by UNICEF Office of Research, INTERPOL, and ECPAT International,
reported that two in ten, or around two million, internet-using children aged 12 to 17 in
the Philippines had experienced online sexual abuse and exploitation in the preceding
year, while fewer than three percent reported such incidents to authorities. This study-
based estimate highlights the need for stronger coordination among family support,
child protection, law enforcement, education, digital safety, and community-based
intervention systems.

       The proposed Filipino Family Mainstreaming Act responds to these realities by
making family well-being a deliberate and measurable consideration in governance. It
does not create a new large bureaucracy. Instead, it establishes the National
Commission for the Filipino Family (NCFF), attached to the Office of the President for
policy coordination, with the Department of Social Welfare and Development serving
as Secretariat. It requires agencies to designate existing senior officials as Family
Mainstreaming Focal Points, thereby avoiding unnecessary creation of new plantilla
positions. It also integrates family mainstreaming into existing planning, budgeting,
monitoring, and performance systems.

        The measure also introduces Family Impact Assessments (FIAs) as a policy
discipline. Under Section 11, an FIA is mandatory for covered Executive department
and agency measures, including nationally significant executive issuances,
development plans, regulations, and major government programs reasonably
expected to have substantial effects on Filipino families. For proposed legislation,
however, the NCFF is directed to develop and make available an FIA methodology
and technical assistance which either House of Congress, its committees, or
legislative offices may use in accordance with their respective rules. FIAs are not
another permit or clearance; they are intended to help decision-makers assess effects
on child welfare, parental responsibilities, caregiving, household resilience, work-
family balance, vulnerable family sectors, implementation costs, measurable
outcomes, and less adverse policy alternatives.
        The proposal also addresses a recurring weakness of social legislation: the
absence of measurable outcomes. It directs the National Commission for the Filipino
Family, in coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority and concerned agencies,
to adopt National Family Well-being Indicators covering child development,
educational participation and completion, family economic resilience, health and
nutrition, housing conditions, caregiving, child protection, violence affecting family
members, and work-life balance. These indicators will allow Congress, the Executive,
local governments, researchers, and the public to evaluate family policy on the basis
of evidence and measurable results.

        The bill also contains targeted obligations intended to give practical effect to the
mainstreaming framework. In basic education, it requires advance curriculum
transparency for defined Sensitive Topics, parental opt-out procedures, and specified
parental consent and notification rules. In government procurement, it disqualifies
natural or juridical entities with final administrative, civil, or judicial convictions for
violations of specified laws protecting life, women, children, and family welfare,
consistent with Republic Act No. 12009 or the New Government Procurement Act. In
agency budgeting, it requires identification of family mainstreaming activities and
recognizes qualifying programs that strengthen family stability, foster spousal
communication, and prevent domestic breakdown as possible components of the
Gender and Development budget under Republic Act No. 9710. These sector-specific
provisions complement the bill’s broader system of planning, assessment,
coordination, and accountability.

        The Filipino family does not need rhetoric alone. It needs a coherent
governance framework that can translate constitutional protection into planning,
budgeting, policy review, data systems, and accountability. A government that
measures inflation, employment, poverty, classroom shortages, health outcomes, and
infrastructure gaps should also be able to measure how its major policies affect the
family, the first and most enduring institution of Filipino life.

        The Filipino Family Mainstreaming Act is not a substitute for parental
responsibility, moral formation, employment, education, housing, or child protection. It
is the mechanism that allows these concerns to be viewed together. It is a framework
for government to ask, before it acts: Will this policy strengthen or weaken the Filipino
family? Will this program support parents and children, or make their burdens heavier?
Will this public investment improve the conditions in which families live, work, learn,
care, and grow?

       In view of the foregoing, the swift passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

    AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE FILIPINO FAMILY MAINSTREAMING
    FRAMEWORK, ESTABLISHING THE NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR THE
  FILIPINO FAMILY (NCFF), MANDATING FAMILY IMPACT ASSESSMENTS IN
 PUBLIC POLICYMAKING AND GOVERNANCE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                              THEREFOR

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Filipino Family
Mainstreaming Act.”

       Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
recognize the Filipino family as the foundation of the nation pursuant to Article II,
Section 12 and Article XV of the 1987 Constitution. The State shall strengthen family
solidarity, preserve family autonomy, support responsible parenthood, promote child
welfare, respect the rights and duties of parents, and actively promote the total
development of the Filipino family.

      The State further recognizes that strong families contribute to national
development, poverty reduction, educational attainment, public health, economic
productivity, community resilience, and social stability.

        Towards this end, the State shall institutionalize family mainstreaming as a
guiding principle in governance by ensuring that major public policies, legislation,
development plans, programs, and investments appropriately consider their impacts
on Filipino families.

      Sec. 3. Objectives. This Act aims to:
       (a) institutionalize family mainstreaming throughout the Executive branch and
local governments;
       (b) strengthen coordination among government agencies on family-related
concerns;
       (c) improve evidence-based policymaking affecting Filipino families;
       (d) integrate family well-being into national and local development planning;
       (e) support parents and families while respecting existing constitutional rights
and statutory mandates;
       (f) improve accountability for family-related public policies through measurable
indicators; and
       (g) promote efficient governance by integrating family considerations into
existing planning, budgeting, and evaluation systems without creating unnecessary
regulatory burdens.

       Sec. 4. Guiding Principles. Implementation of this Act shall be guided by the
following principles:
       (a) Constitutional fidelity;
       (b) Respect for family autonomy;
       (c) Best interests of children;
       (d) Evidence-based policymaking;
       (e) Whole-of-government coordination;
       (f) Partnership with civil society, academe, faith-based organizations, and the
private sector;
       (g) Transparency and accountability;
       (h) Subsidiarity and local participation;
       (i) Administrative efficiency; and
       (j) Non-discrimination consistent with the Constitution and existing laws.

       Sec. 5. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act:
       (a) “Family Mainstreaming” refers to the systematic integration of family
considerations into the formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of
major government policies, legislation, programs, regulations, development plans, and
budgets.
       (b) “Family Impact Assessment” refers to a structured analysis of the
reasonably foreseeable effects of a proposed measure on family well-being, parental
responsibilities, caregiving, child welfare, family economic resilience, and vulnerable
family members.
       (c) “Family Well-being Indicators” refer to measurable indicators adopted under
this Act to monitor the condition and development of Filipino families.
       (d) “Major Government Policy” refers to nationally significant legislation,
executive issuances, development plans, or programs meeting thresholds prescribed
under this Act or its implementing rules.
       (e) “Family Mainstreaming Focal Point” refers to the senior official designated
by an agency to oversee implementation of this Act.
       (f) “Best Interests of the Family” refers to a holistic standard encompassing:

          1. The preservation of marriage, family relations, unity, and stability,
             including the life and optimal development of every family member;

          2. The promotion of the physical, emotional, psychological, moral, spiritual,
             and economic well-being of all family members, including the conceived
             but unborn;

          3. The primacy of parental rights and duties balanced with the rights of
             children for nation-building;

          4. The protection of children’s best interests as paramount in all family-
             related decisions;

          5. The equitable consideration of the rights and welfare of parents, elderly,
             and dependent family members; and

          6. The prevention of family separation due to poverty, migration, or lack of
             social protection.

       Family preservation and reunification shall be promoted only insofar as
consistent with the best interests, safety, dignity, and legally protected rights of each
family member, particularly children and other vulnerable persons.

      (g) Sensitive topic – refers to any topic regarding which a reasonable parent
could be expected to set age limits on discussion and be equipped in handling the
consequences when discussion takes place, including but not limited to:

          a) Reproduction and reproductive health, including any discussion relating
             to reproductive anatomy and physiology, puberty, pregnancy, childbirth;
          b) Sexuality, sexual attraction, sexual pleasure, sexual activity, and
             exposure to any material on the same;
          c) Contraception;
          d) Sexually transmitted diseases, including HIV/AIDS;
          e) Romance, dating, marriage, and parenthood;
          f) Forms of violence and abuse including family violence, dating violence,
             gang violence, sexual violence, and abortion;
          g) Mental health issues;
          h) Trafficking, including sex trafficking; and,
          i) Any addiction including pornography, drugs, smoking, alcohol, gambling,
             and any other risky behavior; and values pertaining to all the above.

       (h) “Sexual Risk Avoidance” refers to primary prevention programs that promote
the elimination of risks associated with premature sexual activity by emphasizing
whole-person development, character formation, healthy decision-making, and
personal goal-setting.
         (i) “Pro-life” includes the upholding of the fundamental legal right to life and
dignity of persons, starting from conception and their continuous protection and
support after birth, alongside a commitment to the flourishing of mothers and children,
including the unborn child under R.A. 11148, as well as fathers, until the natural end
of life.

        (j) “Pro-family” refers to the upholding of the following: (1) the importance of
marriage as an institution that protects the best interests of the family; (2) the value of
intact families, in accordance with the Convention on the Rights of the Child, for the
flourishing of children and society provided that solo-parents, adoptive foster kinship-
care and separated households, are not disadvantaged in accessing support under
this Act; (3) the significance of sexual risk avoidance to achieve this end; (4) the duty
of society to orient itself around the best interests of families and children; (5) the
benefits of family growth for parents, children, national survival, and national
prosperity; and (6) pro-life principles.

        (k) “Health” refers to the bio-psycho-social-spiritual (BPSS) health framework
that affects every area of life starting at conception and thereafter. To achieve optimum
BPSS health, the promotion of physical and mental well-being, family stability, and
respect for values for proper societal integration must also be achieved.

       (l) “Household” a group of people who are domiciled in a housing unit and share
burdens, rights, duties, and living expenses. A person is domiciled in a particular
residence or with a particular group of people even if he or she does not regularly
reside there but has the intention of returning and making such residence or group of
people his or her permanent home and mutual support system. This term excludes
those who are living in facilities and institutions whose nature, rights, and duties are
defined elsewhere by law.

      Sec. 6. National Family Mainstreaming Framework. Family mainstreaming is
hereby adopted as a national governance framework.

       All national government agencies, government-owned or controlled
corporations, government financial institutions, state universities and colleges, and
local government units shall integrate family considerations into their planning,
policymaking, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation functions in accordance
with this Act.

      Implementation shall utilize existing government planning, budgeting,
monitoring, and performance management systems whenever practicable.

      Sec. 7. National Commission for the Filipino Family (NCFF). The National
Committee on the Filipino Family (NCFF) under EO No. 238 Series of 2003 is hereby
reconstituted as such under this Act as the National Commission for the Filipino Family
(NCFF) and attached to the Office of the President for policy coordination. The NCFF
shall serve as the principal inter-agency and public-private coordinating body for family
mainstreaming and national family policy.

       The NCFF shall not exercise regulatory powers over agencies beyond those
expressly provided by law and shall not diminish or supersede the statutory mandates
of existing government agencies.

      Sec. 8. Composition of the National Commission for the Filipino Family. The
NCFF shall be chaired by the Executive Secretary or his or her duly authorized
representative.

       Its members shall include the Secretaries or Heads of:
       (1) Department of Social Welfare and Development;
       (2) Department of Health;
       (3) Department of Education;
       (4) Department of Labor and Employment;
       (5) Department of Justice;
       (6) Department of the Interior and Local Government;
       (7) Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development;
       (8) Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev);
       (9) Department of Budget and Management (DBM)
       (10) Philippine Statistics Authority;
       (11) Commission on Population and Development;
       (12) Council for the Welfare of Children;
       (13) Philippine Commission on Women;
       together with:
       (14) one representative from accredited local government leagues;
       (15) two representatives from SEC-registered civil society organizations
engaged in family development;
       (16) one representative from the academe;
       (17) three (3) representatives from faith-based organizations; and
       (18) one representative from the business sector with demonstrated experience
in family-friendly workplace policies.

       Representatives from the private sector, including faith-based organizations,
shall be on a voluntary, contributory, and commitment basis, and the Commission shall
ensure that there is sufficient participation from the private sector to carry out the
objectives of this Act.

      Sec. 9. Secretariat. The Department of Social Welfare and Development shall
serve as Secretariat of the NCFF.
       The Secretariat shall utilize existing plantilla positions and organizational
structures as far as practicable. Creation of new permanent positions shall be subject
to existing civil service, budget, and compensation laws.

      Sec. 10. Powers and Functions of the NCFF. The NCFF shall:
      (a) formulate and periodically update the National Family Development
Framework;
      (b) recommend policies that strengthen Filipino families;
      (c) prescribe standards for Family Impact Assessments;
      (d) develop national Family Well-being Indicators;
      (e) coordinate family-related policies among agencies;
      (f) monitor implementation of this Act;
      (g) prepare an annual State of the Filipino Family Report for submission to the
President and Congress;
      (h) promote research, programs, and policy innovations for pro-family, health,
and pro-life objectives;
      (i) facilitate partnerships with civil society, educational institutions, faith-based
organizations, professional organizations, employers, and local governments;
      (j) recommend legislative and administrative reforms; and
      (k) perform such other functions as may be necessary to implement this Act.

       The NCFF shall primarily exercise coordinating, advisory, and monitoring
functions and shall avoid duplicating the operational responsibilities of existing
agencies.

        Sec. 11. Family Impact Assessment. A Family Impact Assessment shall be
undertaken by Executive departments and agencies for proposed nationally significant
executive issuances, development plans, regulations, and major government
programs reasonably expected to have substantial effects on Filipino families. For
proposed legislation, the NCFF shall develop and make available an FIA methodology
and technical assistance which either House of Congress, its committees, or
legislative offices may utilize in accordance with their respective rules.

       The FIA shall be integrated into existing policy development and regulatory
review processes and shall not constitute a separate permit, clearance, or licensing
requirement.

        Routine administrative issuances, internal management policies, procurement
activities, personnel matters, and other actions determined by the Implementing Rules
and Regulations to have no material impact on families shall be exempt.

      Sec. 12. Minimum Components of a Family Impact Assessment. Every Family
Impact Assessment shall evaluate, where applicable:
      (a) the likely effects on family stability and family functioning;
        (b) child welfare and development;
        (c) parental responsibilities and caregiving;
        (d) family economic resilience and household financial security;
        (e) work-family balance;
        (f) effects on older persons, persons with disabilities, solo parents, overseas
Filipino worker families, indigenous peoples, and other vulnerable family sectors;
        (g) anticipated implementation costs;
        (h) measurable outcomes and indicators;
        (i) marital or parent-to-parent dynamics; and
        (j) alternative policy options that may achieve the same objectives with fewer
adverse effects on Filipino families.

        The NCFF shall prescribe proportional assessment standards to ensure that
the scope of the assessment is commensurate with the significance of the proposed
measure. It shall likewise recommend appropriate corrective actions in cases of non-
compliance. The assessment shall, where applicable, examine funding requirements
and availability, financial obligations, actual disbursements, delays in service delivery,
causes of underspending, and time-bound corrective measures, in order to assist
Congress in evaluating whether appropriated resources are translating into timely and
effective program implementation.

      Sec. 13. Family Mainstreaming Focal Point System. Every national government
agency, government-owned or controlled corporation, government financial institution,
and state university or college shall designate an existing senior official as its Family
Mainstreaming Focal Point.

       The designation shall not require the creation of new plantilla positions.

       The Focal Point shall:
       (a) oversee implementation of this Act within the agency;
       (b) coordinate preparation of the agency's Family Mainstreaming Plan;
       (c) ensure compliance with applicable Family Impact Assessment
requirements;
       (d) monitor family-related performance indicators; and
       (e) submit annual accomplishment reports to the NCFF.

        Sec. 14. Agency Family Mainstreaming Plans. Within one (1) year from the
effectivity of this Act, every covered agency shall prepare a Family Mainstreaming Plan
consistent with its statutory mandate.

       The Plan shall identify:
       (a) family-related objectives;
       (b) priority programs;
       (c) measurable targets;
       (d) performance indicators;
       (e) responsible offices;
       (f) monitoring mechanisms; and
       (g) estimated resource requirements.

       Agency plans shall be integrated into existing strategic planning and
performance management systems. No separate planning document shall be required
where the matters required under this section can be incorporated into an agency’s
existing strategic or operational plans.

       Sec. 15. Integration into National Development Planning. The Department of
Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), shall incorporate family well-being
considerations into the preparation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of the
Philippine Development Plan and other national development frameworks, where
appropriate.

      Family-related indicators shall complement, and not replace, existing economic
and social development indicators.

        Sec. 16. National Family Well-being Indicators. Within one (1) year from the
effectivity of this Act, the NCFF, in coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority
and other concerned agencies, shall adopt a National Family Well-being Indicator
Framework.

       The Framework may include indicators relating to:
       (a) child development;
       (b) educational participation and completion;
       (c) family economic resilience;
       (d) access to health and nutrition;
       (e) housing conditions;
       (f) family caregiving;
       (g) child protection;
       (h) violence affecting family members;
       (i) work-life balance; and
       (j) other measurable indicators determined appropriate by the Commission.

       Indicators shall be evidence-based, periodically reviewed, and aligned with
available national statistical systems.

       Sec. 17. Research, Statistics, and Evidence-Based Policymaking. Government
agencies shall, to the extent practicable, formulate family-related policies using reliable
Philippine data.
      Where local evidence is unavailable, internationally recognized research may
be considered, taking into account Philippine legal, cultural, demographic, and socio-
economic conditions.

      The NCFF shall encourage policy experimentation, impact evaluation, and
independent academic research relating to Filipino families.

       Sec. 18. Local Government Family Mainstreaming. Every province, city,
municipality, and barangay shall integrate family mainstreaming into existing local
planning and development processes, including, where applicable:
       (a) the Comprehensive Development Plan;
       (b) the Comprehensive Land Use Plan;
       (c) the Local Development Investment Program;
       (d) the Annual Investment Program; and
       (e) other local development plans prescribed by law.

      Nothing in this Act shall diminish the constitutional autonomy of local
government units.

        Sec. 19. Family-Friendly Public Service Delivery. National government
agencies and local government units are encouraged, consistent with their respective
mandates and available resources, to develop programs that strengthen family
resilience, responsible parenthood, caregiving support, family literacy, parenting
education, child protection, healthy ageing, work-family balance, and community-
based family support systems.

      Furthermore, all respective government agencies involved in the coordination
and case-build up for cases involving marital relations, family disputes, child abuse,
OSAEC, violence against women and their children, and other cases wherein a
vulnerable family or household member is aggrieved shall have streamlined
procedures to ensure that justice and recovery are done.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed as creating new individual entitlements
unless expressly provided by law.

        Sec. 20. Family Welfare Disqualification in Government Procurement. —
Consistent with Republic Act No. 12009 (New Government Procurement Act), no
national or local government procurement contract shall be awarded to any natural or
juridical entity with a final administrative, civil, or criminal decision or conviction for
violation of national statutes protecting life, women, children, and family welfare,
including but not limited to Republic Act No. 7610, Republic Act No. 9262, and related
anti-trafficking laws.

    Sec. 21. Community Lay Peer-Support Volunteers. — To address gaps in
community-based mental health and family support, the Department of Social Welfare
and Development (DSWD) and Department of Health (DOH), in partnership with SEC-
registered family and faith-based civil society organizations, shall establish a
standardized framework to train, certify, and supervise Lay Community Peer-Support
Volunteers.

        These volunteers shall provide bio-psycho-social-spiritual support, community-
level facilitation, and referral services to assist licensed social workers and behavioral
health professionals in executing intervention plans for vulnerable families.

         Sec. 22. Communication Infrastructure for Detained Family Members.
Detention Communication Infrastructure. The BuCor, BJMP, and local detention
facilities shall, subject to reasonable security, disciplinary, operational, judicial, data
privacy, and correctional requirements, provide appropriate means for persons
deprived of liberty to maintain lawful family contact and, where authorized, participate
remotely in judicial, administrative, mediation, or family-related proceedings.

       Sec. 23. Family Alternative Dispute Resolution. The DOJ, through the OADR,
may establish guidelines for voluntary family alternative dispute resolution
mechanisms for civil and intra-family disputes suitable for consensual settlement, with
the objective of facilitating fair, voluntary, durable, and rights-respecting resolution.

       No mediation or other consensual process shall be required or encouraged
where there are reasonable grounds to believe that domestic violence, child abuse,
sexual abuse, trafficking, coercive control, intimidation, serious power imbalance, or
imminent risk to any party would render such process unsafe or inappropriate. Neither
shall mediation include matters that cannot be legally compromised under existing law
such as civil status, marriage validity, grounds for legal separation and criminal liability,
among others.

      Sec. 24. Overseas Workers and Families Left Behind. LGUs, through their
respective CSWDOs or MSWDOs, shall, within the Parent Effectiveness Service
Program established under Republic Act No. 11908 and subject to available
resources, develop appropriate modules and interventions responsive to families of
overseas Filipino workers and other families experiencing prolonged parental
absence.

       Sec. 25. Parent Participation and Transparency. Government agencies
implementing programs directly affecting children and families shall, whenever
practicable and consistent with existing laws, provide mechanisms for meaningful
consultation with parents, guardians, caregivers, civil society organizations,
professional associations, and other stakeholders.

      Nothing in this Act shall diminish the rights and responsibilities of parents
recognized under the Constitution and existing laws.

       Sec. 26. Educational Transparency and Parental Rights in Basic Education. —
Pursuant to the constitutional mandate recognizing the natural and primary right and
duty of parents in rearing the youth:
       (a) Advance Curriculum Transparency. All public and private basic educational
       institutions shall provide parents or legal guardians advance notification of at
       least sixty (60) days prior to the instruction or presentation of modules covering
       Sensitive Topics or non-standard supplemental social programs; provided, that
       the advance notice requirement shall not apply where immediate instruction,
       information, counseling, or intervention is reasonably necessary to address an
       urgent child-protection, safety, public-health, or other comparable risk, or where
       such instruction or intervention is required by existing law or competent
       authority. In such cases, parents or legal guardians shall be informed as soon
       as reasonably practicable, unless notification is restricted or prohibited by law.

       (b) Parental Opt-Out Rights. Except those covering basic protective education
       such as teaching children how to recognize abuse and seek help and other
       exceptions already established by law, a parent or legal guardian shall retain
       the right to excuse their minor child from specific instruction, presentations, or
       surveys on Sensitive Topics by submitting a written Parental Sensitivity
       Exemption Form to the school head. Students excused under this section shall
       be provided alternative, non-punitive learning activities and shall not suffer
       academic penalties, administrative prejudice, or social discrimination.

       (c) Medical and Counseling Consent. Unless otherwise provided by law, explicit
       parental consent shall be required prior to administering non-emergency
       medical interventions or diagnostic health procedures on minors in educational
       settings. Schools shall ensure parental notification and involvement in student
       counseling interventions, except when there is a reasonable ground not to do
       so for the best interest of the minor .

       (d) Parental Notification Duty. Educational institutions shall immediately inform
       parents or legal guardians regarding instances of serious bullying, medical
       emergencies, severe behavioral issues, or requests by a minor to officially alter
       institutional records of identity; provided that such notification may be withheld
       when the institution has reasonable grounds to do so for the best interest of the
       minor.

        Sec. 27. Family Mainstreaming in Budget Preparation. Covered agencies shall
identify and integrate family mainstreaming programs, activities, and projects within
their annual plans and budget proposals whenever relevant to their respective
mandates, including initiatives relating to family resilience, caregiving, child welfare
and development, responsible parenthood, family economic security, work-family
balance, protection of vulnerable family members, and other family-related concerns
under this Act.

      Family-related programs, activities, and projects may be attributed to or funded
under the mandatory Gender and Development (GAD) budget pursuant to Republic
Act No. 9710, otherwise known as the “Magna Carta of Women,” only when they
address identified gender issues, advance women’s empowerment or gender equality,
and comply with applicable GAD planning, budgeting, implementation, monitoring,
evaluation, accounting, and audit requirements.
       This Act shall not require mandatory budget earmarks or fixed expenditure
percentages for family mainstreaming beyond those otherwise required by law.
Implementation shall, as far as practicable, utilize existing appropriations, programs,
personnel, and government systems, unless additional funding is otherwise provided
by law.

       Sec. 28. Regulatory Burden Limitation. Implementation of this Act shall adhere
to the principles of efficient and responsive governance.

       No agency shall impose unnecessary permits, certifications, documentary
requirements, approvals, or compliance obligations solely by reason of family
mainstreaming.

       Family Impact Assessments shall, as far as practicable, be incorporated into
existing planning, regulatory, or policy review processes to avoid duplication,
administrative delay, or unnecessary costs to government or stakeholders.

       Sec. 29. State of the Filipino Family Report. The NCFF shall submit to the
President of the Philippines and to both Houses of Congress, not later than March 31
of every year, a State of the Filipino Family Report containing:
       1. an assessment of the condition of Filipino families based on the National
Family Well-being Indicators;
       2. significant demographic, social, economic, and family-related trends;
       3. the implementation status of this Act;
       4. accomplishments of national government agencies and local government
units;
       5. for major family-related programs and measures, the amounts appropriated
or otherwise made available, obligations incurred, actual disbursements, utilization
rates, significant variances between programmed and actual spending, causes of
underspending or delayed disbursement, and the corresponding effects on service
delivery;
       6. identified delays, implementation bottlenecks, unmet targets, and corrective
actions, including responsible agencies and time-bound deadlines for compliance;
       7. identified policy gaps and implementation challenges;
       8. recommendations for legislative, administrative, budgetary, and
programmatic action; and
       9. such other information as the NCFF may deem necessary.
       The Report shall distinguish, where practicable, between deficiencies arising
from inadequate funding and those arising from delayed obligation, procurement,
disbursement, administrative inaction, implementation capacity, or other causes.
       The Report shall be made publicly available through appropriate government
platforms, subject to existing laws on data privacy, confidentiality, and official statistics.
       Sec. 30. Monitoring and Evaluation. The NCFF shall establish and maintain
a monitoring and evaluation system to measure the effectiveness, efficiency, and
actual implementation of family mainstreaming across government.

       The system shall focus on measurable outcomes and actual service delivery
rather than procedural compliance alone, and shall utilize existing government
planning, budgeting, financial management, audit, and reporting systems whenever
practicable.

       For major family-related programs and measures, the monitoring system shall
track, where applicable:
       1. appropriations, allotments, releases, obligations, and actual disbursements;
       2. utilization and disbursement rates;
       3. delays in procurement, implementation, or service delivery;
       4. reasons for underspending, delayed obligations, delayed disbursements, or
unmet physical targets;
       5. the relationship between financial performance and actual outputs and
outcomes for families;
       6. corrective measures, responsible agencies or offices, and time-bound
deadlines for compliance; and
       7. the status of implementation of such corrective measures.

       The NCFF shall use the results of the monitoring system to identify recurring
implementation bottlenecks, recommend corrective administrative or budgetary
action, and support congressional oversight, budget review, and future policy
development.

        Sec. 31. Independent Five-Year Review. Within five (5) years from the
effectivity of this Act, and every five (5) years thereafter, the NCFF shall commission
an independent evaluation to determine:
        (a) whether family mainstreaming has improved public policymaking;
        (b) the effectiveness of Family Impact Assessments;
        (c) measurable improvements in family well-being;
        (d) implementation costs and administrative efficiency;
        (e) unintended consequences arising from implementation; and
        (f) recommendations for legislative amendments.

      The results shall be transmitted to Congress and made available to the public.

        Sec. 32. National Family Research Program. The NCFF shall promote research
on Filipino families by encouraging collaboration among government agencies, higher
education institutions, research organizations, civil society organizations, and
development partners.
      Priority research areas may include:
      (a) family resilience;
      (b) responsible parenthood;
      (c) child development;
      (d) caregiving;
      (e) demographic trends;
      (f) work-family balance;
      (g) migration and family life;
      (h) fertility and mortality rates;
      (i) digital technologies and family relationships; and
      (j) other emerging issues affecting Filipino families.

      Research findings shall inform future policymaking and shall not be construed
as creating legally binding standards unless adopted pursuant to law.

         Sec. 33. Partnerships with Civil Society and the Private Sector. The NCFF may
establish partnerships with civil society organizations, faith-based organizations,
academic institutions, professional organizations, employers, labor organizations,
cooperatives, and community organizations to strengthen family development
initiatives.

      Such partnerships shall remain voluntary and shall not diminish governmental
accountability for implementing this Act.

       Sec. 34. Recognition and Incentive Program. Subject to existing laws and
available appropriations, the NCFF may establish a voluntary recognition program for
government agencies, local government units, educational institutions, private
employers, and civil society organizations demonstrating exemplary initiatives that
promote family well-being.

      Recognition under this Act shall not create monetary entitlements unless
expressly authorized by law.

       Sec. 35. Non-Duplication of Government Functions. Nothing in this Act shall be
construed to diminish, transfer, repeal, modify, or supersede the statutory mandates,
powers, or functions of existing government agencies, including but not limited to the
Department of Social Welfare and Development, Department of Health, Department
of Education, Department of Labor and Employment, Department of Justice, Council
for the Welfare of Children, Commission on Population and Development, Philippine
Commission on Women, National Youth Commission, National Commission of Senior
Citizens, and local government units.
      The NCFF shall perform a coordinating, advisory, monitoring, and policy
development role and shall not duplicate operational programs already vested in other
government agencies by law.

       Sec. 36. Rule on Statutory Construction. Nothing in this Act shall be interpreted
to:
      (a) impair constitutionally protected rights and liberties;
      (b) modify rights and obligations established under the Family Code, Civil Code,
Labor Code, Local Government Code, or other existing laws, except as expressly
provided herein;
      (c) create new causes of action or civil liabilities;
      (d) expand regulatory authority beyond that expressly granted by law; or
      (e) authorize governmental interference in lawful family decisions except as
otherwise provided under existing laws for the protection of children and other
vulnerable persons.

       Sec. 37. Data Privacy and Confidentiality. All collection, processing, sharing,
and publication of information pursuant to this Act shall comply with the Data Privacy
Act of 2012 and other applicable laws governing confidentiality and official statistics.

       Personally identifiable information shall not be disclosed except as authorized
by law.

       Sec. 38. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within one hundred eighty (180)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the NCFF, through its Secretariat and in
consultation with concerned government agencies, local government units, civil
society organizations, academic institutions, professional organizations, faith-based
organizations, and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary Implementing
Rules and Regulations.

      The Rules shall ensure that implementation remains evidence-based,
proportionate, efficient, and consistent with existing laws.

        Sec. 39. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department of
Social Welfare and Development and participating agencies.

      Thereafter, such amounts as may be necessary for the continued
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

      Implementation shall maximize the use of existing government personnel,
systems, and resources to minimize additional fiscal requirements.
       Sec. 40. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue
to be in full force and effect.

       Sec. 41. Repealing Clause. All laws, executive orders, administrative rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       Sec. 42. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

       Approved.
