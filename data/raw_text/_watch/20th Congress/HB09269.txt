                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

Malnutrition remains a persistent and multifaceted public health challenge in the
Philippines, affecting millions of Filipinos—particularly children, pregnant women, and
other vulnerable sectors. Despite decades of government intervention under
Presidential Decree No. 491, the country continues to face a “triple burden of
malnutrition,” characterized by the stunting and wasting, micronutrient deficiencies,
and rising rates of overweight and obesity1.

Recent data underscore the urgency of strengthening the country’s nutrition
governance framework. According to the 2023 Expanded National Nutrition Survey of
the Food and Nutrition Research Institute (FNRI), 23.6% of Filipino children under
five years old are stunted, indicating chronic malnutrition that adversely affects
physical and cognitive development2. Meanwhile, 5.6% of children under five suffer
from wasting, a life-threatening form of acute malnutrition, while 15.1% are
underweight3.

In absolute terms, the burden remains substantial. It is estimated that around
630,000 Filipino children are affected by wasting, reflecting the continuing scale of

Food insecurity further exacerbates the situation. Data show that 31.4% of Filipino
households experience moderate to severe food insecurity, limiting access to
adequate and nutritious food and reinforcing cycles of malnutrition6.

Despite some progress, improvements in nutrition outcomes have been slow and
uneven. The persistence of malnutrition is driven by structural factors such as poverty,
inequality, limited access to health and nutrition services, and weak coordination
among government agencies and local government units. As noted in governance
assessments, nutrition indicators in the Philippines have shown minimal improvement
over the years, highlighting systemic gaps in implementation7.

Presidential Decree No. 491, enacted in 1974, created the National Nutrition Council
(NNC) as the country’s primary policy-making and coordinating body on nutrition.
While it has contributed significantly to advancing nutrition programs, the increasing
complexity, scale, and persistence of malnutrition necessitate a stronger, more
empowered, and better-resourced institution.

This proposed measure seeks to create the National Nutrition Commission, a
restructured and strengthened body that will replace the existing council. The
proposed Commission shall serve as the central authority for nutrition governance,
with expanded mandates to formulate policies, ensure effective implementation, and
enforce accountability across all levels of government.

The Commission will be tasked to:
   • Formulate and implement a comprehensive and integrated national nutrition
      policy;
   • Strengthen coordination among national government agencies, local
      government units, and stakeholders;
   • Establish robust monitoring and evaluation systems to track outcomes and
      ensure accountability;

By repealing Presidential Decree No. 491 and establishing the National Nutrition
Commission, this bill aims to modernize and institutionalize nutrition governance in
the Philippines. It seeks to ensure that nutrition policies are effectively implemented,
adequately funded, and responsive to the evolving needs of the population.

Ultimately, the enactment of this measure is a critical step toward reducing
malnutrition, improving health outcomes, enhancing human capital development, and
securing a healthier and more productive future for all Filipinos.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known and referred to as the "National
 2   Nutrition Commission Act."
 3
 4   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes nutrition as a basic human
 5   right and guarantees the right to adequate food, which should be implemented by the
 6   government in an integrated manner.
 7
 8   SEC. 3. National Nutrition Commission. — There is hereby created a National
 9   Nutrition Commission, ("Commission"), which shall be attached to the Office of the
10   President. The Commission shall be the primary policy-making, coordinating,
11   implementing and advisory body on nutrition and food concerns.
12
13   SEC. 4. Powers and Functions of the Commission. – The Commission shall have
14   the following powers and functions:
15      a) Formulate national food and nutrition policies, programs, strategies, activities,
16           and projects, and serve as the policy, coordinating and advisory body on food,
17           nutrition and health concerns;
18      b) Supervise, coordinate, monitor, and evaluate the implementation of its
19           programs, projects, and activities undertaken by all agencies and
20           instrumentalities of both the government and private sector concerning
21           nutrition and food.
22      c) Recommend to Congress measures to effectively promote the full realization of
23           the right to nutrition and adequate food, and the harmonization of existing laws
 1         and issuances addressing food, nutrition and health concerns through the
 2         review of relevant administrative and legislative frameworks;
 3      d) Conduct investigations upon all matters relating to the enforcement of the right
 4         to nutrition, health, and adequate food, and facilitate access for individuals and
 5         groups to legal assistance through coordination with appropriate government
 6         and non-government legal aid agencies to better assert the progressive
 7         realization of their abovementioned rights;
 8      e) Call on any department, bureau, office, agency and other instrumentalities of
 9         the government for assistance in the form of personnel, facilities and resources
10         as the need arises;
11      f) Provide technical assistance to increase competencies and capabilities of
12         stakeholders through the conduct of public education, capacity building, skills
13         development and advocacy;
14      g) Coordinate the release of funds, loans, and grants from government
15         organizations and non-government organizations;
16      h) Provide monetary and non-monetary incentives to nutrition champions by
17         establishing rewards and recognition system;
18      i) Establish a continuing program for information dissemination and awareness;
19         and
20      j) Exercise such other powers and functions as are necessary for the
21         implementation of this Act.
22
23   SEC. 5. Composition of the Commission. – The Commission shall be headed by a
24   Chairperson and two (2) Commissioners, all of whom shall be appointed by the
25   President for a term of six (6) years, without prejudice to one reappointment. They
26   shall continue to hold office until their successors have been appointed. Appointments
27   to a vacancy that occurs before the expiration of the term of a Chairperson or
28   Commissioner shall cover only the unexpired portion of the term of the predecessor.
29   At the expiration of the term of the Chairperson or in case of vacancy, the more senior
30   of the Commissioners shall temporarily assume and perform the duties and functions
31   of the Chairperson until a permanent Chairperson is appointed by the President.
32
33   The Commission shall appoint its officers and employees in accordance with the Civil
34   Service law, rules and regulations.
35
36   SEC. 6. The Chairperson and the Commissioners. – The Chairperson and the
37   Commissioners of the Commission must be Filipino citizens, with at least ten (10) years
38   of experience in the fields of food security, good nutrition promotion, and mitigation
39   of hunger, and malnutrition, at least thirty-five (35) years of age, and of good moral
40   character. The Chairperson, together with at least one (1) commissioner, must hold a
41   valid licensed to practice medicine in the Philippines.
42
43   The Chairperson and Commissioners shall receive annual compensation and shall be
44   entitled to other benefits and privileges provided under existing laws for a Department
45   Secretary and Department Undersecretaries, respectively. They shall not, during their
46   tenure, hold any other office or employment, or in the active management or control
47   of any business which, in any way, may affect the functions of their office.

32   SEC. 8. Composition of the Secretariat. - The Commission shall establish a
33   secretariat to be headed by an Executive Director, who must be a natural born citizen
34   of the Philippines; at least thirty-five (35) years of age on the day of the appointment;
35   holder of a college degree; with at least five (5) years of executive and management
36   experience; with considerable exposure to food security, good nutrition promotion,
37   and mitigation of hunger, and malnutrition; and, a person of good moral character:
38   Provided, That he or she shall be a career officer.

40   SEC. 9. Nutrition Offices in Local Government Units. — To ensure the effective,
41   sustainable, and coordinated implementation of nutrition programs at the sub-national
42   level, every province, city, and municipality shall establish or designate a Nutrition
43   Office within its existing local government structure and organizational framework.
44   Local government units (LGUs) shall ensure the provision of adequate institutional,
45   financial, technical, and human resource support necessary for the operation of such
46   offices.
 1   The Provincial Nutrition Office shall be headed by a Provincial Nutrition Action Officer
 2   (PNAO), with the assistance of a Provincial Nutrition Coordinator and such other
 3   personnel as may be necessary. The City/Municipal Nutrition Office shall be headed
 4   by a City/Municipal Nutrition Action Officer (C/MNAO), supported by an adequate staff
 5   complement.
 6
 7   In organizing and staffing said offices, LGUs shall integrate and maximize existing
 8   personnel, facilities, and administrative infrastructure, and shall ensure that sufficient
 9   resources are made available for the performance of nutrition functions and delivery
10   of nutrition services.

11   Plantilla positions and qualification standards for personnel composing said offices
12   shall be determined by the appropriate Commission in consultation with the Civil
13   Service Commission and the Department of Budget and Management, and shall be
14   funded through the General Appropriations Act and/or local government budget
15   allocations.
16
17   SEC. 10. Role of Barangay Nutrition Scholars. – Barangay Nutrition Scholars
18   (BNS) shall form part of the local nutrition human resource development and
19   workforce strengthening strategy. LGUs shall provide mechanisms for the continuing
20   engagement, training, career development, and professional advancement of BNS as
21   part of the local nutrition system.
22
23   To strengthen continuity and institutional capacity in nutrition governance, LGUs, in
24   coordination with the appropriate national government agencies, shall establish a
25   ladderized development program for Barangay Nutrition Scholars, including
26   opportunities for competency upgrading, scholarship support, technical certification,
27   and professional education that may qualify them for appointment or promotion to
28   municipal, city, or provincial nutrition positions, including City/Municipal Nutrition
29   Action Officer and Provincial Nutrition Action Officer positions, subject to existing civil
30   service laws, rules, and regulations.
31
32   Sec. 11. Research and Policy Development Support. — The Food and Nutrition
33   Research Institute of the Department of Science and Technology, as well as other
34   agencies of government and private entities which are performing nutrition research,
35   shall cooperate with the Commission for research and policy development.
36
37   SEC. 12. Donations. - The Commission shall be authorized to receive donations,
38   grants, or gifts in whatever form and from whatever sources: Provided, That said
39   grants, gifts or donations shall be administered, obligated and disbursed in accordance
40   with the terms of the grant or donation and in such manner as the Commission may
41   in its discretion determine.
