                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

          Palliative and hospice care are essential components of a
    compassionate and comprehensive health care system. Patients suffering
    from serious, life-threatening, life-limiting, chronic, or complex illnesses
    often experience significant pain and other physical, psychosocial, and
    emotional burdens that require care beyond the treatment of the underlying
    disease.

           While the Philippine health care system has made significant progress
    in expanding access to health services, access to organized and affordable
    palliative and hospice care remains uneven. Many patients and their families
    continue to face difficulties in obtaining appropriate pain and symptom
    management, home-based care, psychosocial support, continuity of care,
    and end-of-life services.

            This measure seeks to integrate palliative and hospice care into the
    Philippine health care system and the framework of Universal Health Care,
    ensuring that such services are available across hospitals, primary care
    facilities, communities, and appropriate home-based settings.

            The bill establishes standards for quality and accreditation,
    strengthens referral and continuity-of-care networks, promotes education
    and training of health professionals and volunteers, supports continuing
    research, and directs the development of appropriate PhilHealth benefit
    packages for inpatient, outpatient, and home-based services. It likewise
    facilitates support for indigent patients and recognizes the important role of
    family caregivers.

          Ultimately, this measure seeks to ensure that every Filipino facing
    serious or end-of-life illness may receive accessible, affordable, quality, and
    compassionate care that protects dignity, relieves suffering, and improves
    quality of life for both patients and their families.

          In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
    earnestly sought.

                                “AN ACT
           INTEGRATING PALLIATIVE AND HOSPICE CARE INTO THE
           PHILIPPINE HEALTH CARE SYSTEM”

           Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
    Philippines in Congress assembled:

         SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Palliative
    and Hospice Care Act.”

          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - The State recognizes the right of
    every person to health and shall endeavor to make essential health and social
    services available to all people at affordable cost. Consistent with Article II,
    Section 15 of the Constitution and Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known
    as the “Universal Health Care Act,” it is the policy of the State to adopt an
    integrated and comprehensive approach to health development and to
    ensure access to quality, affordable, and appropriate health services
    throughout the life cycle, including palliative and hospice care for persons
    with serious, life-threatening, or life-limiting illnesses.

           SECTION 3. Palliative and Hospice Care Defined. - As used in this
    Act:

           a. “Palliative care” refers to an approach that improves the quality of
              life of patients and their families facing serious, life-threatening,
              life-limiting, complex, or chronic illnesses, through the prevention
              and relief of suffering by means of early identification, assessment,
              and management of pain and other physical, psychosocial, and
              spiritual symptoms. Palliative care may be provided together with
              disease-directed or curative treatment and shall not be limited to
              patients who are terminally ill or have a specific life expectancy.

           b. “Hospice care” refers to specialized, person-centered care provided
              to patients approaching the end of life, together with support for
              their families and caregivers, with emphasis on comfort, dignity,
              quality of life, symptom management, psychosocial and spiritual
              support, and continuity of care, in accordance with applicable
              clinical standards and regulations.

           SECTION 4. Accreditation. - Hospitals, hospice institutions, and 2
    palliative and hospice care programs and service providers shall comply with
    the accreditation, certification, licensing, and other regulatory requirements
prescribed by the Department of Health (DOH). The professional licensing
and regulatory authority of the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC)
and other competent government agencies over health professionals shall
remain unaffected.

      The DOH, in consultation with relevant government agencies,
professional organizations, health institutions, patient and caregiver
organizations, and other stakeholders, shall formulate the rules and
guidelines for accreditation and certification to ensure safe, accessible,
equitable, and quality palliative and hospice care services.

      SECTION 5. Quality Assurance. - Key elements necessary to ensure
quality palliative and hospice care services in accredited hospitals and
hospices include the following:

      a. Adequate number        of   appropriately   trained   multi-specialty
         personnel;

      b. Appropriate financing and mechanisms for the delivery of health,
         supportive, and custodial services, consistent with applicable laws
         and benefit programs;

      c. Clear and practical standards for facilities and services;

      d. Appropriately designed and equipped facilities;

      e. Regular and systematic supervision and reporting to the DOH;

      f. Appropriate assessment and management of pain and other
         symptoms;

      g. Psychosocial, spiritual, and bereavement support for patients and
         their families, as appropriate; and

      h. Appropriate referral, discharge planning, continuity of care, and
         coordination among health care providers.

       SECTION 6. Mandatory Palliative and Hospice Service. - All
government and private hospitals shall provide basic palliative care services
or ensure timely access to such services through an appropriate referral
system. Hospitals with the capacity and accreditation to provide specialized
palliative or hospice care shall provide such services in accordance with
standards established by the DOH.
       Hospitals are required to link with a referral and aftercare network
within their respective local health systems and health care provider
networks that is organized and made functional by all provincial, city, and
municipal governments consistent with Republic Act No. 11223 and other
applicable laws, and under the guidance and monitoring of the DOH.

       Rural health units, health centers, and health offices shall, within
their capacity and in accordance with DOH standards, facilitate or provide
community- and home-based palliative care programs in coordination with 3
government-owned and privately-owned hospices and other accredited
palliative care providers in the local government units.
      The DOH shall establish a national referral and continuity-of-care
framework linking primary care facilities, hospitals, specialized palliative
care providers, hospices, home-based care providers, and other appropriate
health and social service providers.

       SECTION 7. Leave Benefits. - Immediate family members who are
employed in the public or private sector and who are providing care to a
relative receiving palliative or hospice care may utilize available leave benefits
granted under existing laws, rules, regulations, collective bargaining
agreements, employment contracts, or company policies, subject to the
applicable conditions and requirements governing such leave benefits.

      The DOH, in coordination with the Civil Service Commission, the
Social Security System, the Government Service Insurance System, and the
Department of Labor and Employment, shall be tasked to formulate the
necessary guidelines in the implementation of Section 7 of this Act.

       SECTION 8. Education and Training of Health Care Professionals
and Volunteers. - The DOH, in partnership with relevant professional
organizations, health institutions, patient and caregiver organizations, and
other accredited stakeholders, shall develop the education and training
modules for health care professionals and workers and volunteers engaged
in palliative and hospice care.

      The Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall coordinate with
the DOH and relevant professional regulatory bodies to develop and adopt
appropriate policies for the integration of courses on the principles and
practice of Palliative Care and Hospice Care into the curriculum of Medicine
and Nursing, as well as in all appropriate paramedical and allied health
courses.

      SECTION 9. Continuing Research. - The DOH, in coordination with
the Philippine Council for Health Research and Development of the
Department of Science and Technology, shall ensure continuing research
and collection of data on palliative and hospice care and availability of funds
for this purpose, subject to applicable laws, ethical standards, and data
privacy and confidentiality requirements.

     SECTION 10. Implementing Agency. - The DOH shall be the lead
implementing agency of this Act. The Secretary of Health shall designate the
appropriate bureau or office of the Department to oversee and coordinate its
implementation, in coordination with other relevant offices and agencies.

      The DOH shall perform the following functions:

      a. Promote palliative care in the Philippines through advocacy and
         social marketing;

      b. Formulate policies and develop standards on quality palliative and
         hospice care;
                                                                                     4
      c. Monitor the enforcement of standards and implementation of the
         program on palliative and hospice care;
      d. Mobilize and generate resources for sustainability of operation;

      e. Network with international hospice associations and other relevant
         international organizations;

      f. Coordinate research undertakings with other institutions and
         agencies;

      g. Serve as repository of database for policy-making and establish and
         maintain a national palliative and hospice care registry, subject to
         applicable data privacy and confidentiality laws;

      h. Organize and develop continuing training programs for physicians,
         nurses, physical therapists, and other professional health workers
         and volunteer workers in the field of palliative care;

      i. Serve as the coordinating center of a national palliative and hospice
         care network located in the different regions of the country; and

      j. Develop, in consultation with relevant professional regulatory
         bodies and organizations, ethical guidelines and standards in the
         practice and delivery of palliative and hospice care.

       SECTION 11. PhilHealth Benefit Package. - The Philippine Health
Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) shall develop and implement appropriate
benefit packages covering palliative and hospice care, including inpatient
palliative care, outpatient hospice care, and home-based palliative care,
subject to applicable laws, accreditation requirements, actuarial studies,
and availability of funds.

       SECTION 12. Funding Support. - All non-profit, DOH-accredited
palliative and hospice care institutions which are serving indigent patients
may qualify as institutional beneficiaries under the Philippine Charity
Sweepstakes Office (PCSO) Institutional Financial Assistance Program,
subject to applicable eligibility, accreditation, documentary, and other
requirements of the said Program.

       SECTION 13. Tax Incentives. - Any donation or bequest made to the
DOH that is intended for palliative and hospice care shall be entitled to the
tax treatment and deductions allowed under the National Internal Revenue
Code of 1997, as amended, and applicable laws: Provided, that such
donations or bequests shall be used exclusively for purposes consistent with
this Act.

       Donations or bequests made to qualified non-stock, non-profit
organizations or institutions accredited by the DOH and engaged in the
delivery of palliative and hospice care may likewise be entitled to applicable
tax deductions or incentives under existing laws, subject to the requirements
of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.

      SECTION 14. Appropriations. - The initial amount necessary to
implement the provisions of this Act shall be charged against the current 5
appropriations of the DOH. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for
the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the Annual
General Appropriations Act, subject to applicable budgeting, accounting,
auditing, and procurement laws, rules, and regulations.

      SECTION 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty
(60) days from the approval of this Act, the Secretary of Health, after
consultation with relevant government agencies, local government units,
professional organizations, health institutions, patient and caregiver
organizations, and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and
regulations implementing the provisions of this Act.

       SECTION 16. Separability Clause. - In case any provision of this Act
is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions hereof which are
not affected thereby shall continue in full force and effect.

      SECTION 17. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of
this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 18. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.

      Approved.
