                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Article II, Section 16 of the 1987 Constitution provides that, “[t]he State shall
protect and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology in
accord with the rhythm and harmony of nature.” Republic Act No. 7586, otherwise
known as the “National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS) Act of 1992,”
provides the framework for protecting the country’s most ecologically significant areas.
Among its initial components is the Libmanan Caves National Park (LCNP) in
Libmanan, Camarines Sur, a 19.4-hectare game refuge and bird sanctuary proclaimed
as a national park on February 6, 1934, under Presidential Proclamation No. 654.1 To
date, the LCNP is the only proclaimed protected area for caves in the Bicol Region.2

        The park is a forest-over-limestone landscape with ten caves and three rock
shelters.3 It is home to 138 species of flora and 136 species of fauna, including two
newly described tarantula species.4 Five caves adjacent to the protected area
boundary and twenty-two caves within the proposed expansion area have also been
identified.5

        Despite its unique biophysical characteristics, the LCNP remains under
pressure. Farming, grazing, poaching, firewood gathering, and tree cutting continue in
and around it,6 and the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR)
itself cites the need for immediate legislation, additional personnel, and a site
development plan.7 Legislation is therefore necessary to secure the protection of the
park and the caves around it.

       Recognizing these concerns, the DENR conducted assessments in 2018 and
2022 and, after public consultations and the endorsement of the Protected Area
Management Board (PAMB), recommended establishing the park and its surroundings
as a protected landscape covering 274.5776 hectares.8

        This bill seeks to establish the Libmanan Caves Protected Landscape under
the NIPAS by expanding the protected area, defining its boundaries, authorizing the
designation of buffer zones, and requiring a management plan. Furthermore, it vests
the management of the protected area in a PAMB, with a Protected Area Management
Office (PAMO) headed by a Protected Area Superintendent (PASu), and creates a
trust fund to finance its projects and activities. By doing so, this bill seeks to safeguard
an important site of ecotourism and biodiversity in the country and reaffirm the State’s
commitment to building a sustainable future for Filipinos through a harmonious
coexistence with nature.

       In the 20th Congress, a similar bill was filed by Representative Terry Ridon. This
bill was filed upon the request of the PAMO of the Libmanan Caves National Park.

       Early approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

 7          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – Mindful of the significant effects of human
 8   intervention on all components of the natural ecosystem, it is the policy of the State to
 9   secure for the Filipino people of the present and future generations the perpetual
10   existence of biological diversity through the establishment of protected areas under
11   the National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS), classified as national parks
12   as provided in Article XII, Section 3 of the 1987 Constitution.
13
14          Considering the rich biodiversity of the flora and fauna that are endemic and
15   unique in the Libmanan Caves National Park and the adjacent highland ecosystem,
16   as well as the series of natural caves and their aesthetic, educational, socio-cultural,
17   paleontological, archaeological, economic, and ecological importance, it is necessary
18   to declare the 274.5776-hectare area a protected area under the category of protected
19   landscape, to be known as the Libmanan Caves Protected Landscape (LCPL).

26          SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall
27   be defined as follows:
28
29          (a) Biological diversity or biodiversity refers to the variability among living
30   organisms from all sources including, inter alia, terrestrial, marine, and other aquatic
31   ecosystems and the ecological complexes of which they are part; this includes
32   diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems;
33
34          (b) Buffer zones refer to identified areas outside the boundaries of and
35   immediately adjacent to designated protected areas that need special development
36   control to avoid or minimize harm to the protected area;
37
38          (c) Caves mean any naturally occurring void, cavity, recess, or system of
39   interconnected passages beneath the surface of the earth or within a cliff or ledge and
40   which is large enough to permit an individual to enter, whether or not the entrance,
41   located in either private or public land, is naturally formed or man-made. The term
42   includes any natural pit, sinkhole, or other feature which is an extension of the
43   entrance, and cave resources therein, but excludes any vug, mine tunnel, aqueduct,
44   or man-made excavation;
45
46         (d) Cave resources include any material or substance occurring naturally or
47   found in caves, such as animal life, plant life, paleontological and archaeological
48   deposits, cultural artifacts or products of human activities, sediments, minerals,
49   speleogems, and speleothems;
50
51            (e) Conservation refers to any act or acts of preservation and sustainable
52   utilization of natural resources, or the maintenance, restoration, and development of
53   related habitats;
54
55         (f) Delineation refers to the actual ground survey of the boundaries of protected
56   areas and their buffer zones and management zones using the global positioning
57   system (GPS) or other applicable survey instruments and technologies to produce a
58   map of the area;
59
60          (g) Demarcation refers to the establishment of the boundaries of protected
61   areas and their buffer zones using visible markers, monuments, buoys in case of
62   marine areas, and known natural features and landmarks, among others, based on
63   the actual ground delineation;
64

65          (h) Endemic species refer to the species or subspecies of flora and fauna which
66   are naturally occurring and found within specific areas in the country;
67
68           (i) National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS) refers to the
69   classification and administration of all designated protected areas to maintain essential
70   ecological processes and life-support systems, to preserve genetic diversity, to ensure
71   sustainable use of resources found therein, and to maintain their natural conditions to
72   the greatest extent possible;
73
74          (j) Protected area refers to identified portions of land and/or water set aside by
75   reason of their unique physical and biological significance, managed to enhance
76   biological diversity and protected against destructive human exploitation;
77
78          (k) Protected landscapes refer to areas of national significance characterized
79   by the harmonious interaction of people and nature, and which provide opportunities
80   for public enjoyment through recreation, tourism, and other economic activities;
81
82          (l) Tenured migrants refer to protected area occupants who have been actually,
83   continuously, and presently occupying a portion of the protected area for five (5) years
84   immediately before the proclamation or law establishing the same as a protected area,
85   and are solely dependent therein for subsistence; and
86
87         (m) Wildlife refers to the wild forms and varieties of flora and fauna, in all
88   developmental stages, including those which are in captivity or are being bred, fed, or
89   propagated.

319          SEC. 12. Libmanan Caves Protected Landscape Integrated Protected Area
320   Fund. – There is hereby established a trust fund to be known as the Libmanan Caves
321   Protected Landscape Integrated Protected Area Fund (LCPL-IPAF) for sustainably
322   financing the projects and activities of the protected area under the NIPAS. The LCPL-
323   IPAF shall be sourced from fees and charges for the availment or enjoyment of the
324   resources and facilities of the protected area; contributions from industries and
325   establishments directly benefiting from the protected area; and other fees and income
326   derived from the operation of the protected area. The use of the IPAF shall be subject
327   to existing accounting, budgeting, and auditing rules and regulations: Provided, That
328   the LCPL-IPAF shall not be used to cover personal services expenditures.
