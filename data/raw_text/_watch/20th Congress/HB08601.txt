                             AN ACT
PROVIDING A DIRECT ELECTRICITY SUBSIDY FOR QUALIFIED LOW-INCOME
HOUSEHOLDS, HARMONIZING THE LIFELINE RATE MECHANISM WITH A
GOVERNMENT-FUNDED SUBSIDY, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Electricity is a basic necessity for Filipino families. It powers homes, keeps food safe,
allows children to study at night, and supports small businesses and daily work.
However, rising electricity costs place a heavy burden on poor and near-poor
households. Many families are forced to reduce their power use to unsafe levels or
sacri ice other basic needs such as food, medicine, and education just to pay their
electric bills. This situation calls for stronger and more direct government support to
ensure that essential electricity remains affordable for those who need it most.

Existing laws already recognize the need to protect low-income electricity consumers.
The Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001 (EPIRA) created the lifeline rate
to give discounted electricity prices to marginalized households. Congress later
strengthened this protection through Republic Act No. 10150 and Republic Act No.
11552, which extended the program and improved the rules for identifying quali ied
bene iciaries, including households under the government’s Pantawid Pamilyang
Pilipino Program (4Ps). While these measures provide relief through lower
electricity rates, the assistance is mainly delivered through pricing adjustments
within the power sector.

This bill proposes a complementary solution through a direct electricity subsidy for
quali ied low-income households. Instead of relying solely on pricing mechanisms, the

government will provide transparent and targeted support that will appear directly
in the bene iciaries’ electricity bills. By working with the Department of Social
Welfare and Development, the Department of Energy, and the Energy Regulatory
Commission, the program will ensure accurate targeting, clear accountability, and
proper oversight. In doing so, the Tulong-Kuryente Act aims to make electricity more
affordable for vulnerable families while strengthening existing protections under the
law.

respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Tulong-Kuryente Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote equitable
access to electricity, protect low-income households from undue electricity cost
burdens, and ensure that social assistance is delivered transparently and ef iciently.
Toward this end, the State shall provide a direct electricity subsidy to quali ied
bene iciaries, and shall rationalize subsidy delivery to minimize leakages and
promote fairness in electricity pricing.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) Quali ied Bene iciaries refer to quali ied marginalized end-users as
       recognized under Section 73 of Republic Act No. 9136, as amended, including:
       (i) quali ied household-bene iciaries under Republic Act No. 11310 (4Ps
       Act) as regularly submitted by DSWD; and (ii) other marginalized end-users
       certi ied and validated pursuant to criteria determined by the Energy
       Regulatory Commission (ERC), taking into account, among others, the poverty
       threshold set by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA).

                                                                           3|Page
   (b) Distribution Utility (DU) refers to any electric cooperative, private
       corporation, or government-owned or -controlled entity engaged in the
       distribution of electricity to end-users for a fee.

   (c) Direct Electricity Subsidy refers to the government-funded payment applied
       to a Quali ied Bene iciary’s electricity bill in accordance with this Act.

SECTION 4. Creation of the Direct Electricity Subsidy Program. — There is hereby
created a Direct Electricity Subsidy Program for Quali ied Bene iciaries with
residential service connections.

The subsidy shall be applied as a deduction from the Quali ied Bene iciary’s monthly
electricity bill, subject to eligibility rules, consumption or bill thresholds, and
safeguards under this Act and its implementing rules.

SECTION 5. Bene iciary Identi ication and Credentialing. —

   (a) DSWD Lead for Social Targeting. The DSWD shall be the lead agency for
       bene iciary identi ication and credentialing and shall primarily utilize the 4Ps
       list and other DSWD social protection databases, consistent with Section 73 of
       RA 9136, as amended.

   (b) Use of PSA Data. The PSA shall maintain and regularly update relevant
       poverty statistics. The DSWD and DOE shall utilize PSA data, consistent with
       the statutory directive that PSA data be primarily used in determining
       thresholds for lifeline eligibility and consumption levels.

   (c) Meters Not in Bene iciary’s Name. The program shall include rules for cases
       where the meter or service connection is not registered in the bene iciary’s
       name, consistent with the mandate for guidelines under Section 73 of RA 9136,
       as amended.

SECTION 6. Subsidy Design and Amount; Application to Bills. —

   (a) Subsidy Amount. The subsidy shall cover up to One Thousand Pesos
       (P1,000.00) per month of the Quali ied Bene iciary’s electricity charges, or
       such other amount as may be determined under subsection (d).

   (b) Eligibility Threshold. The subsidy shall apply only to Quali ied Bene iciaries
       whose monthly electricity bill does not exceed Five Thousand Pesos

                                                                           4|Page
      (P5,000.00), or such other threshold as may be determined under subsection
      (d).

   (c) Billing Statement Disclosure. DUs shall clearly indicate the subsidy as a
       separate line item in the billing statement, showing the gross bill, subsidy
       amount, and net amount due.

   (d) Authority to Adjust Parameters. Upon joint recommendation of the DSWD
       and DOE, and after ERC consultation on rate/billing impacts, the subsidy
       amount and eligibility thresholds may be adjusted based on in lation, regional
       tariff conditions, PSA poverty statistics, and program evaluation
       results: Provided, That the adjustment shall be consistent with the objectives
       of equitable subsidy targeting.

SECTION 7. Payment Mechanism; DU Reimbursement. —

   (a) Remittance to DUs. The DSWD shall remit subsidy payments to DUs on behalf
       of Quali ied Bene iciaries based on veri ied billing data and agreed
       reconciliation procedures.

   (b) Reconciliation and Audit Trail. DUs shall submit monthly subsidy claims
       supported by billing records and bene iciary identi iers, subject to post-audit
       by the Commission on Audit (COA).

SECTION 8. Oversight, Transparency, and Program Evaluation. —

   (a) ERC Monitoring. The ERC shall monitor compliance by DUs on billing
       transparency and proper application of subsidies consistent with its
       regulatory authority under EPIRA.

   (b) Periodic Evaluation. To prevent leakages and ensure equitable distribution
       of bene its, the DOE, in coordination with DSWD and ERC, shall conduct
       periodic program evaluations, taking guidance from the EPIRA requirement of
       comprehensive evaluation of lifeline implementation every two (2) years.

   (c) Reporting. The DOE shall submit an annual report to Congress on program
       implementation, including bene iciary counts, iscal outlays, and evaluation
        indings.



                                                                          5|Page
SECTION 9. Harmonization with the Lifeline Rate; Transition. —

   (a) Non-impairment of Existing Lifeline During Transition. During the
       transition period under subsection (c), Quali ied Bene iciaries shall continue
       to be entitled to the lifeline rate under Section 73 of RA 9136, as amended.

   (b) Rationalization. Within the transition period, the DOE, in coordination with
       DSWD and ERC, shall recommend to Congress or implement within existing
       authority such measures as may be necessary to harmonize the lifeline rate
       mechanism with the Direct Electricity Subsidy Program, including whether the
       lifeline rate should be retained for residual cases, narrowed, or used as a
       backstop.

   (c) Transition Period. A period of one (1) year from effectivity shall be provided
       to fully operationalize direct subsidy payments nationwide.

SECTION 10. Value-Added Tax (VAT) Treatment. — Electricity sales to Quali ied
Bene iciaries may be granted VAT relief subject to coordination with the Department
of Finance and the Bureau of Internal Revenue, and subject to applicable tax
administration rules.

SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90)
days from effectivity, the DSWD and DOE shall jointly issue the implementing rules
and regulations, in coordination with the ERC, COA, PSA, and
stakeholders: Provided, That the delineation of DOE policy issuance and ERC
implementation shall be observed.

This implements the doctrine that DOE formulates implementing rules while ERC
implements, consistent with jurisprudence.

SECTION 12. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
DSWD and such other available sources as may be identi ied in the General
Appropriations Act. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 13. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.
