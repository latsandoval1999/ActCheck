                                    AN ACT
    INSTITUTING A NATIONAL LAND USE AND MANAGEMENT POLICY, PROVIDING THE
         IMPLEMENTING MECHANISMS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES



                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The 1987 Philippine Constitution, under Article XII, Section 2, mandates that all lands of the
public domain and natural resources belong to the State. It further charges the State with the
responsibility of ensuring that the use of land bears a social function and contributes to the
common good. Despite this constitutional mandate, the Philippines remains without a
comprehensive national law that governs the classification, management, and utilization of
its finite land and water resources. For decades, land use in the country has been managed
through a fragmented, sectoral approach, resulting in overlapping jurisdictions among
government agencies, the rapid loss of prime agricultural lands, and the proliferation of human
settlements in high-risk disaster zones.

The necessity of this measure is underscored by the alarming rate of land conversion and
environmental degradation. Official data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA),
specifically the 2022 Census of Agriculture and Fisheries (CAF), reveals a staggering decline
in the nation’s agricultural base. From a peak of 9.97 million hectares in 1991, the total area
of agricultural farms has plummeted to 6.16 million hectares as of 2022. This represents a
loss of 3.81 million hectares—or approximately 38.2% of our total agricultural land—in just
three decades. This rapid disappearance of farmlands, primarily due to unchecked land
conversion for residential and industrial purposes, poses a direct and existential threat to the
nation’s food security.1

Furthermore, the urgency for a unified spatial framework is intensified by our vulnerability to
climate change. The Department of Environment and Natural Resources – Mines and
Geosciences Bureau (DENR-MGB) has consistently reported that approximately 60% of the
Philippines' total land area is highly susceptible to multiple geo-hazards, including landslides
and flooding. Despite these scientific warnings, the lack of a national land use law has
permitted continued settlement in these "danger zones." Compounding this is the pressure of
rapid urbanization; the World Bank projects that by 2050, nearly 7 in 10 Filipinos will reside in
urban areas, a shift that will inevitably lead to further "urban sprawl" and ecological collapse
if not managed through the national strategy proposed in this Act.2

This bill seeks to institutionalize a National Land Use and Management Policy by categorizing
land into four major functional uses: Protection, Production, Settlements, and Infrastructure.
By clearly delineating these areas, the State ensures that critical watersheds, key biodiversity
areas, and prime agricultural lands are protected from encroachment in perpetuity. It also
establishes a rationalized system for "Production Land Use," where economic activities such
as mining, energy development, and industry are balanced against ecological stability.

A salient feature of this Act is the creation of the National Land Use Policy Commission
(NLUPC) under the Office of the President. The NLUPC shall serve as the highest policy-
making body, tasked to resolve conflicting land use policies between national agencies and
local government units (LGUs). It harmonizes the "bottom-up" approach of the Local
Government Code with national development goals, ensuring that Comprehensive Land Use
Plans (CLUPs) at the municipal level are data-driven and resilient to climate change.

Moreover, this measure grants significant recognition to the rights of Indigenous Cultural
Communities (ICCs) and Indigenous Peoples (IPs). By mandating the integration of Ancestral
Domain Sustainable Development and Protection Plans (ADSDPPs) into local and national
physical framework plans, the bill upholds the principle of self-determination while ensuring
that ancestral lands are protected from unauthorized alienation.

In view of the gravity of the issues addressed and the long-standing need for a unified land
policy, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

10    SECTION 5. Basic Land Use Considerations. – Various land uses as categorized in the
11   succeeding section shall be determined in a manner that promotes the policies and principles
12   defined in Section 2 of this Act and shall consider the following elements of the framework
13   therefor, among others:
14       (a) Protection land use category as defined in Section 6 shall prevail over existing
15           production land use category. The State shall undertake the necessary action in
16           changing the classification of these production areas;
17       (b) Preservation, conservation, rehabilitation and protection to perpetuity of permanent
18           forestlands, critical watersheds, key biodiversity areas, environmentally-critical and
19           ecologically-fragile areas and prime agricultural lands from any other land use,
20           conversion, disposition, intrusion, utilization and development aside from their
21           determined use and limits;
22       (c) Protection of natural forests and natural resources ensures environmental stability,
23           conserve biological diversity, improve ecosystem functions and provide long-term
24           ecological and economic benefits. Designated restoration areas are designed to revive
25           the ecosystem functions and services of forests as well as improve the economic and
26           ecological benefits of local communities;
27       (d) Protection of forest and wetlands from infrastructure development to preserve the
28           ecological services they provide which are essential to economic development. These
29           activities include, but not limited to river channelization and dredging, large scale
30           irrigation and river diversions which reduce riverine habitat and alter flood patterns and
31           natural flow regimes, reduce downstream water availability for agriculture and
32           contribute to salinization through saltwater intrusion in coastal areas;
33       (e) Resolution of land use conflicts which are life-threatening or threatening to public
34           safety, sustainability of key production resources or employment activities and the
35           delivery of basic services which are harmful or destructive to protected areas, flora,
36           fauna, and other protected natural resources. Greater consideration shall be given to
37           the human and tenurial rights of vulnerable groups in resolving land use conflicts;
38       (f) Identification of geohazards (earthquakes, volcanic activity, landslides, soil erosion,
39           ground-related issues, and human-induced hazards) and corresponding measures to
40           reduce risks and enhance resilience.
41       (g) Respect for existing customary rights and traditional land use of ICCs/IPs;
42       (h) Identification of settlement areas to check the demand for land and establish the
43           location of employment-generating industries and basic services; and
44       (i) The availability of natural resources including indigenous energy resources for energy
45           security and self-sufficiency. However, infrastructure energy projects such as dams
46           with large reservoirs in natural ecosystems shall not be allowed if it would interrupt

                                                                                                     20
 1          the connectivity of river systems, disrupt fish spawning and migration, and alter
 2          seasonal flood regimes.

24   SECTION 24. Criteria for the Allocation and Utilization of Lands within the Coastal Zones. The
25   allocation and utilization of lands within the coastal zones shall be subject to the following
26   guidelines:
27       (a) Areas vegetated with mangrove species shall be protected, preserved and managed
28           as mangrove forestland and shall not be converted to other uses;
29       (b) Areas that meet all accepted criteria on elevation, soil type, soil depth topography,
30           supply for successful fishpond development, and are not identified as mangrove
31           protected areas, shall be utilized for aquaculture purposes as long as a 4:1 ratio for
32           mangroves and fishponds is maintained to support ecological processes in mangrove
33           ecosystems;
34       (c) Areas sub-classified as mangrove and still suitable for use as such, or due to
35           environmental conditions need to be preserved as mangroves but are devoid of
36           mangrove stands, shall not be converted to other uses. The DENR shall ensure that
37           these lands shall be reforested within a given period of time;
38       (d) Areas accessible to the sea and identified for fisherfolk settlement and housing but
39           are not part of or are not within any protected land use areas shall be allocated to
40           traditional fisherfolk who are inhabitants of the coastal communities, members of
41           registered fisherfolk organizations, holders of stewardship lease contracts or titles to
42           ancestral domains or any form of property right arrangements who participate in
43           coastal resource management initiatives, subject to the usual census procedures of

45           That the government shall issue marine tenurial rights to organizations concerned
46           based on a criteria to be established by NLUPC;

                                                                                                  27
 1      (e) Areas that are neither sub-classified as mangrove, fisherfolk settlement nor fishpond
 2          may be devoted to recreational or tourism purposes: Provided, That such undertaking
 3          will not result in environmental degradation and displacement of small fisherfolks;

 5          such purpose. In line with this purpose, areas which are considered as traditional
 6          fishing grounds and within the 15 km municipal waters shall be used primarily for such

 1   SECTION 28. Protection of Waterways, Easements and Flood Plains. Structures of any kind

43   conversion shall not conflict with the prescriptions of watershed management plans and shall
44   not result in serious environmental and ecological problems.

22                          PHYSICAL FRAMEWORK AND LAND USE PLAN
23
24   SECTION 50. National Land Use Planning Process. The physical framework and land use
25   planning process shall be participatory, following a combined bottom-up and top-down
26   approach, with mandatory public hearings/consultations conducted at all levels, and shall
27   consider available and updated multidisciplinary scientific information of land uses. A set of
28   national policy guidelines and standards for physical planning shall be formulated by the
29   National Land Use Policy Committee (NLUPC). These shall guide the preparation and
30   formulation of the national, regional and provincial physical framework plans
31   (NPFP/RPFP/PPFP). These standards shall give due consideration to conflicting uses and
32   areas being used, declared or designated for agrarian reform, protected areas, coastal
33   resource management and/or ancestral domains. The National Physical Framework Plan
34   (NPFP) shall define the national strategy and objectives of the country’s urban, rural and
35   regional development. It shall guide the rational distribution of population, access to
36   economic opportunities and social services, sustainable utilization of resources, and
37   maintenance of environmental integrity.
38
39   Furthermore, it shall consider the existing Regional Physical Framework Plans (RPFPs) AND
40   Provincial Physical Framework Plans (PPFPs). The RPFPs, the PPFPs and the CLUPs, which
41   cover the physical development of their respective territories, shall be consistent with the
42   NPFP: Provided, That the integration and harmonization of physical framework plans at all
43   levels shall be iterative to ensure that the concerns of both top and bottom levels of
44   government are considered in the NPFP, RPFPs, PPFPs and CLUPs. The physical
45   FRAMEWORK and land use plans prepared at all levels shall be consistent with each other,
46   specifically on the linkages of the major land use categories to ensure their complementation
47   in the utilization, development and management of resources. The period of coverage of the
                                                                                                36
 1   national, regional and provincial framework plans shall be thirty (30) years with regular review
 2   and updating every ten (10) years.
 3
 4   SECTION 51. The NPFP shall guide the planning and management of the country's land and
 5   other physical resources at the national and sub-national, and indicate broad spatial directions
 6   and policy guidelines on protection land use, production land use, settlement development
 7   and infrastructure development. The NPFP shall be the basis for adopting the land use and
 8   physical planning-related guidelines, including zoning and other land use control standards
 9   that will guide the formulation of city/municipal zoning ordinances.
10
11   The NLUPC, in consultation with concerned sectors and communities shall update the NPFP
12   after such period that objectives and goals set by the NPFP have been substantially achieved.
13   It shall also formulate and issue the appropriate planning guidelines and standards through
14   which all physical planning, land use and management of resources shall be reviewed,
15   prepared, formulated and monitored. The resulting land use plan/physical framework shall be
16   the basis for the identification, formulation and development of national and local
17   development plans, programs, projects and activities of government at all levels.
18
19   SECTION 52. Regional Physical Framework Plan. The RPFP shall provide broad spatial
20   directions and policy guidelines on protection land use, production land use, settlement
21   development, and infrastructure development at the regional level and guide the formulation
22   of the PPFPs. The RPFP shall consider the existing PPFPs and the CLUPs of LGUs within the
23   territorial jurisdiction of the region. The Regional Land Use Policy Committee (RLUPC), created
24   under Section 59 of this Act, shall formulate and periodically update the RPFP based on the
25   guidelines issued by the NLUPC, in a manner consistent with and following the objective of
26   the NPFP. Likewise, the Medium-Term Regional Development Plan (MTRDP) and the Medium-
27   Term Regional Development Investment Program (MTRDIP) shall be guided by and made
28   consistent with the objectives identified in the RPFP.
29
30   SECTION 53. Provincial Physical Framework Plan. The PPFP shall determine the physical
31   development of the entire provincial territory, consolidate and harmonize the comprehensive
32   land use plans of component cities and municipalities, consistent with the RPFP. It shall
33   reflect the indicative land use management and physical development direction of the
34   province. Further, the PPFP shall serve as basis for other sectoral and development plans
35   related to land, natural resources, and infrastructure facilities, including the development plan
36   of the province; reconciliation and rationalization of land use proposals among component
37   cities and municipalities and with the higher level framework plan; guiding development
38   agencies and private developers, particularly those that undertake large-scale projects; and
39   providing a basis for resolving conflicts arising from the implementation of land use plans and
40   development projects involving two or more municipalities.
41   The PPFP shall serve as the basis for the preparation of the Provincial Development Plan
42   (PDP) and Provincial Development Investment Program (PDIP). The PPFP, PDP, PDIP and/or
43   Provincial Physical Framework and Development Plan (PPFDP) shall serve as the basis for
44   the formulation of sectoral action plans of national government agencies in the province and
45   all LGUs within its jurisdiction. The province may opt to prepare their PDP alongside the
46   preparation of PPFP and consolidate them into a PPFDP; Provided, That PPFDP shall remain

                                                                                                   37
 1   consistent with the PPFP; Provided further, That any review or changes in the PPFP shall
 2   coincide with the over-all review process of the NPFP.

 4   The Provincial Land Use Planning and Management Board (PLUPMB), created under this Act,
 5   shall ensure that the PPFP is consistent with the national and regional planning framework
 6   and guidelines issued by the NLUPC/RLUPC. The PPFP shall be presented to the Provincial
 7   Development Committee (PDC) for endorsement to the Sangguniang Panlalawigan, who shall
 8   formally adopt and approve the PPFP. The approved PPFP shall be submitted to the RLUPC

14   SECTION 54. City and Municipal Land Use Plans (CLUPs). All barangays shall provide their
15   sectoral, temporal, and spatial data for the CLUP, which shall serve as the foundation for the
16   formulation of the city or municipal CLUPs. The data shall be provided by the Sangguniang
17   Barangay through stakeholder consultations. The Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP) shall
18   determine the specific uses of land and other physical and natural resources, both private and
19   public, within their territorial jurisdiction, including areas co-managed with the national

21   watersheds, river basins, and protected areas.The CLUP shall delineate actual boundaries on

23   barangay, city, or municipality, translate and integrate sectoral plans, and provide appropriate
24   policies for each of the four land use planning categories. The spatial directions prescribed in
25   the CLUP shall serve as the basis for the preparation and formulation of the Comprehensive
26   Development Plan (CDP) and Local Development Investment Programs (LDIP) of the LGUs.
27   Consistent with the national standards and guidelines prescribed in Section 50, the cities and
28   municipalities shall, in consultation with the concerned sectors, prepare their respective
29   CLUPs. The City/Municipal Land Use Planning and Management Board (C/MLUPB), created
30   under this Act, shall be responsible for the preparation and formulation of the CLUP and shall
31   ensure its consistency with national and regional physical planning guidelines and standards.

33   Development Coordinator/Office (C/MPDC/PDO) shall provide technical, secretariat, and
34   administrative support in the preparation, consultation, integration, and formulation process

36

38   adoption and approval of the Sangguniang Bayan (SB). The approved CLUPs shall be
39   submitted to the province for integration into the Provincial Physical Framework Plan (PPFP).

20   SECTION 80. Fine for Failure to Commence or Complete the Development of Agricultural Lands
21   with Approved Conversion Order. For agricultural lands with approved conversion orders, the
22   provisions of Republic Act 6657 (CARL), as amended by Republic Act 9700, shall apply. A
23   landowner and/or the designated developer or duly authorized representative who fail to
24   commence and/or complete the development plan defined in the conversion order shall be
25   jointly and severally penalized. The following fines based on the zonal value or the fair market
26   value of the land, whichever is higher at the time the fine shall be imposed:
27
28      (a) Failure to commence within three (3) years from the date of the conversion order:
29              a. Three percent (3%) for the first three hectares;
30              b. Ten percent (10%) for the next three (3) hectares; and
31              c. Fifteen percent (15 %) for the remaining area.
32
33      In such case, the order of conversion shall be revoked by operation of law. The land shall
34      revert to its original use as agricultural land and may be covered by the DAR through
35      compulsory acquisition for distribution to qualified beneficiaries.
36
37      (b) Failure to complete sixty percent (60%) of the approved conversion plan within a
38          specified time frame shall result to the automatic revocation by the DAR of the
39          conversion plan on the undeveloped portion. The land shall be reverted to its original
40          use as agricultural land and may be covered under the CARP for land distribution.
41
42   SECTION 81. Persons Abetting Illegal Conversion. Any person initiating, causing, inducing, or
43   abetting illegal conversion with intent shall be punished with imprisonment or a fine in
44   accordance with Act No. 3815 or Republic Act 3019, or both at the discretion of the court.
45
46   If the offender is a public official or employee, whether elected or appointed, the penalty shall
47   also include dismissal from service, forfeiture of all benefits and entitlements accruing to the

 6   undertake the necessary researches and consultations.

 8   SECTION 90. Convening of the NLUPC. Within thirty (30) days from the effectivity of this Act,

16   (2) newspapers of national circulation.

19   Series of 1991 and EO No. 72 series or 1993, shall be immediately transferred to the NLUPC

22   provisions that pertain to or relate to HSAC non-planning functions.
