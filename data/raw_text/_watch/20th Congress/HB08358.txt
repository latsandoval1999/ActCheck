                            AN ACT
AMENDING SECTION 148 OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE
(NIRC), AS AMENDED, GRANTING THE PRESIDENT THE AUTHORITY TO
SUSPEND EXCISE TAX ON DIESEL, GASOLINE, LPG, KEROSENE, FUEL OIL,
AND BUNKER OIL, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE:

The Philippines remains heavily dependent on imported petroleum to sustain transportation,
agriculture, industry, electricity generation, and daily household activities. Crude oil—the
raw material from which fuels are refined—is processed into the petroleum products that
power the economy, including diesel, gasoline, liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), kerosene, fuel
oil, and bunker oil. These refined fuels are presently subject to excise taxes under Section 148
of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC), as amended by R.A. No. 10963, otherwise
known as the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) Law.

        Recent developments in the Middle East have dramatically heightened the risks to
global energy supply. Armed conflict involving Iran, Israel, and the United States has
escalated into direct military confrontations, including missile and air strikes across the
region. The conflict has resulted in the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, one of the world’s
most critical maritime oil chokepoints through which a substantial portion of global crude oil
shipments (around 20-30%) normally pass. The closure of this strategic route has
significantly disrupted global oil flows and triggered immediate volatility in international
energy markets.

       The Strait of Hormuz is of immense global importance. A large share of the world’s
seaborne crude oil exports from major producers such as Saudi Arabia, the United Arab
Emirates, Kuwait, Iraq, and Qatar normally transits through this narrow passage. Any
obstruction of this route sharply constrains global oil supply, triggering sudden increases in
crude oil prices. For countries like the Philippines that import the vast majority of their
petroleum requirements, such disruptions are quickly transmitted to domestic fuel prices.

       Crude oil is the primary feedstock used by refineries to produce the fuels that power
modern economies. Through refining and fractional distillation, crude oil is converted into
gasoline, diesel, jet fuel derived from kerosene, liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), and heavy
                                               1
fuel oils used for shipping and industrial purposes. As the international price of crude oil
rises, the cost of producing these refined petroleum products inevitably increases. The result
is higher pump prices for fuels such as diesel, gasoline, LPG, kerosene, fuel oil, and bunker
oil.

        The economic consequences of rising fuel prices are immediate and far-reaching.
Diesel powers the trucks that transport food, medicine, and basic goods throughout the
country. It fuels buses, jeepneys, and other public transport relied upon by millions of
Filipino commuters. Gasoline powers motorcycles and private vehicles used for daily
mobility. LPG is the primary cooking fuel used in millions of Filipino homes and small
businesses. Fuel oil and bunker oil are used in maritime transport and certain power
generation facilities. When the prices of these fuels rise sharply, transportation costs increase,
food prices climb due to higher logistics expenses, electricity costs escalate, and household
budgets are further strained.

        Ultimately, ordinary Filipinos—particularly low-income families—bear the brunt of
these economic shocks.

       This surge in fuel prices comes at a particularly delicate time for the Philippine
economy. Inflationary pressures remain a persistent concern, and rising energy costs threaten
to push the prices of basic goods and services even higher. At a time when many Filipino
families are already struggling to make ends meet, additional increases in fuel costs risk
aggravating inflation across the entire economy. Such conditions raise the specter of a
dangerous economic scenario in which inflation remains high while economic growth
stagnates.

       The Philippine economy suffered a significant slowdown in 2025, when actual gross
domestic product (GDP) growth fell substantially below initial projections. Policymakers
have identified 2026 as a critical year for economic recovery and expansion. However, the
prospects for economic rebound could be severely undermined if fuel prices continue to rise
sharply due to global geopolitical disruptions. High fuel costs cascade throughout the
economy, raising transportation costs, increasing production expenses for businesses, and
pushing up the prices of essential goods. If left unaddressed, this could erode purchasing
power, suppress consumer spending, and impede economic recovery.

        The TRAIN Law currently contains a safeguard provision allowing the President,
upon recommendation of the Development Budget Coordination Committee (DBCC), to
suspend scheduled increases in fuel excise taxes when international oil prices reach a certain
threshold. However, this mechanism was designed to address the phased tax increases
implemented during the early years of the TRAIN reform program. Those phased increases
have already been fully implemented as of 2020. As a result, there are no remaining
scheduled increases to suspend. In practical terms, the safeguard provision under the TRAIN
Law has become moot and academic, as all excise tax increases under the law are already in
effect.

        In light of current global realities, the Philippines requires a more responsive and
flexible policy mechanism that allows the government to cushion the impact of sudden global
energy shocks. The present bill addresses this gap by introducing new sections under Chapter
V of the NIRC that strengthen the government's ability to respond to energy crises.

       FIRST, the bill authorizes the President, upon the recommendation of the
       Development Budget Coordination Committee, to suspend or reduce excise taxes on
       key petroleum products when either of two conditions occurs: (1) when global crude
                                                2
       oil prices exceed a specified threshold; or (2) when significant global oil supply
       disruptions affecting Philippine imports arise, including the closure of major oil
       transit routes or armed conflict in major oil-producing regions. By recognizing supply
       disruptions as an independent trigger for government action, the measure allows the
       government to act proactively rather than merely reacting to price spikes after they
       have already inflicted economic harm.

       SECOND, the measure strengthens national energy security by requiring oil
       companies and bulk suppliers to maintain a minimum petroleum inventory equivalent
       to sixty (60) days of their average daily sales. It also authorizes the Department of
       Energy to establish a National Strategic Petroleum Reserve that may be deployed
       exclusively during a declared national energy emergency.

       THIRD, the bill provides for staggered price adjustments during a declared energy
       crisis in order to prevent sudden and destabilizing price shocks. By limiting the
       amount of price increase that may be imposed in a single week, the measure aims to
       moderate abrupt economic disruptions while allowing necessary market adjustments
       to occur over time.

       FOURTH, the measure establishes a Fuel Subsidy Contingency Fund to provide
       immediate assistance to vulnerable sectors whose livelihoods are directly affected by
       fuel price increases. Public utility vehicle drivers, small-scale farmers, and fisherfolk
       are particularly sensitive to fuel costs. The proposed fuel voucher system will ensure
       that these sectors receive targeted support during periods of energy crisis.

       Importantly, the authority granted under this measure is not open-ended. The
suspension or reduction of excise taxes is subject to clear safeguards. Any such suspension
shall automatically lapse after one (1) year, or earlier if the conditions that justified the
suspension no longer exist. These safeguards ensure that the authority is exercised only
during genuine emergencies while preserving the long-term fiscal stability of the government.

        This measure is firmly grounded in the principles of the 1987 Philippine Constitution,
Article II, Section 9, provides that:

       “The State shall promote a just and dynamic social order that will ensure the
       prosperity and independence of the nation and free the people from poverty
       through policies that provide adequate social services, promote full employment,
       a rising standard of living, and an improved quality of life for all.”

        In the face of global conflict, volatile energy markets, and rising inflationary
pressures, the government must remain vigilant and responsive in protecting the welfare of
the Filipino people. By providing tools for proactive fiscal intervention, strengthening energy
security, moderating price shocks, and protecting vulnerable sectors, this measure seeks to
ensure that the Philippine economy remains resilient even in the face of global energy
disruptions.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
