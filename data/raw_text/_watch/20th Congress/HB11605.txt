                             EXPLANATORY NOTE


In the Philippines, there is a recurring and problematic practice where drivers
involved in vehicular incidents—even those who are clearly not at fault—are
subjected to automatic arrest and detention. This often happens because law
enforcement perceives any involvement in an accident, particularly one
resulting in injury or death, as presumptive proof of criminal negligence.

This "automatic detention" culture places an unfair burden on individuals who
were merely reacting to sudden and unavoidable hazards. This bill seeks to
address this by:

   1. Institutionalizing "Defensive Driving": Recognizing that a driver’s
      attempt to avoid harm through reasonable maneuvers (such as braking,
      swerving, or stopping) is a crucial factual consideration.

   2. Protecting Due Process: Mandating that law enforcement and
      prosecutors evaluate the full circumstances of an incident—including
      dashcam footage, witness accounts, and environmental factors—before
      deciding to detain a driver or proceed with criminal charges.

   3. Ending Automatic Detention: Ensuring that if the evidence suggests
      the driver acted defensively and reasonably, the driver is not subject to
      unnecessary detention while the case is being investigated. It encourages
      the use of regular preliminary investigations for complex cases rather
      than immediate inquests that keep drivers in custody.

This measure does not provide immunity. If further investigation proves that
the driver was, in fact, negligent, they will remain liable under the law.
However, it ensures that the Philippine justice system distinguishes between
those who cause accidents through true recklessness and those who are
victims of unfortunate circumstances.

By requiring an objective, evidence-based approach to vehicular incidents, this
bill upholds the constitutional right to due process and prevents the waste of
public resources on the detention of innocent citizens.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

    AN ACT DEFINING DEFENSIVE DRIVING AND PROVIDING FOR THE
    PROTECTION OF MOTOR VEHICLE DRIVERS INVOLVED IN A ROAD
                             CRASH


Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Defensive Driving
Act."

SECTION 2. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following
terms shall be defined as follows:

      (a) Defensive Driving refers to a proactive approach to road safety and
      involves anticipating and responding to potential hazards, keeping an
      appropriate distance from other vehicles, and staying alert for possible
      risks on the road.

      (b) Dashboard Camera refers to a camera that is mounted in or around
      the dashboard of a motor vehicle. It faithfully records all it sees as the
      vehicle moves about its daily business.

      (c) Law Enforcement Officer refers to all officers of the law who exercise
      the powers of arrest or detention.

      (d) Road Crash refers to any collision involving vehicles on public roads
      within the territorial jurisdiction where the traffic accident occurred.

SECTION 3. Proof of Defensive Driving. - Any person driving a motor vehicle
who is involved in a Road crash shall not be detained by the law enforcement
officer if he or she can provide credible evidence of his or her defensive driving,
or upon any finding of the Officer of such defensive driving, such as, but not
limited to the following:

      (a) A dashcam video taken by the driver of the motor vehicle showing that
      he or she was not violating any traffic laws before and during the
      incident;

      (b) A closed-circuit television (CCTV) footage showing that the driver of
      the motor vehicle was not violating any traffic rules before and during the
      incident; or
      (c) Any other video footage or still image from any witness in the vicinity
      of the incident showing that the driver of the vehicle was not violating
      any traffic rules before and during the incident.

Provided, that the presentation of the foregoing evidence shall not exonerate
the driver of the motor vehicle from any liability under the law.

Provided, further, that any other person who suffered injury to himself or
herself or his or her property, or the heirs of the person who died due to the
road accident shall retain the right to pursue all other legal remedies available
to them under the law.

SECTION 4. Temporary Non-detention of persons involved in Defensive
Driving- All persons involved in traffic accidents shall be brought to the
nearest police station for traffic investigation. In case of submission of
sufficient proof of Defensive Driving, the law enforcement officer shall conduct
a thorough preliminary evaluation of the proof presented and the same shall be
concluded within a period not exceeding six (6) hours.

Upon a finding of apparent lack of fault, supported by the proofs under Section
3, the Law Enforcement Officer in charge may release the person without
detention, subject to the person’s written undertaking to appear when lawfully
required.

The temporary release is provisional in nature and shall not constitute a waiver
for the filing of criminal, civil, or administrative charges if subsequent evidence
establishes liability.

SECTION 5. Authority of the Law Enforcement Officer. - Notwithstanding
the presentation of evidence under Section 3 of this Act, the law enforcement
officer shall have the authority to acquire and process the personal information
of the driver of the vehicle involved in the incident, and impound the vehicle
involved in the incident, in accordance with pertinent laws, rules and
regulations.

SECTION 7. Separability Clause. - If any part, section or provision of this Act
is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by
such declaration shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and
regulations, or other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

SECTION 8. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
