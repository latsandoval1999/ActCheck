                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The safety and security of learners, teachers, and school personnel are
essential to the delivery of quality education. Schools must remain safe and
secure environments where children can learn, develop, and participate in
school activities free from the threat of firearms, weapons, violence, and
other preventable dangers.

      The increasing complexity of school security concerns calls for a
comprehensive and preventive approach. While existing laws, regulations,
and Department of Education policies provide important safeguards, there is
a need to institutionalize a coordinated framework that brings together
schools, families, local government units, barangays, law enforcement
agencies, and social welfare institutions.

       This measure, to be known as the “S.A.F.E. (Secure and Firearm-Free
Education) Schools Act,” seeks to establish such a framework. It recognizes
that school safety cannot depend solely on security personnel or law
enforcement after an incident has occurred. Prevention, early identification
of threats, responsible reporting, appropriate intervention, and secure school
environments must form part of a unified school safety system.

      The bill directs the Department of Education to establish national
standards for school access control, security risk assessments, an
Anonymous Digital Tip Line, behavioral threat assessment, conflict
resolution, peace education, and de-escalation training. It likewise requires
public and private basic education institutions to adopt appropriate access-
control procedures, reasonable security inspections, parental engagement,
incident reporting, and environmental safety measures.

      The measure further strengthens the participation of local government
units and barangays by providing for appropriate support for school security
infrastructure, community coordination, school-zone safety, and preventive
patrols. These measures are intended to complement, and not replace, the
responsibilities of national government agencies and school authorities.
      Recognizing that children require a distinct and protective approach,
the bill expressly provides that interventions involving minors shall be
undertaken in accordance with Republic Act No. 9344, otherwise known as
the “Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006,” and other applicable child-
protection laws and regulations. The objective is not merely to punish
misconduct, but to prevent violence while ensuring that children are
afforded appropriate intervention, rehabilitation, counseling, and family
support.

      The bill also promotes safe and responsible reporting of weapons and
threats through confidential or anonymous mechanisms and encourages
schools to establish controlled procedures for the voluntary surrender of
prohibited items. These measures are intended to provide students with a
safe avenue to prevent dangerous incidents before they occur, without
diminishing accountability under existing laws.

       At the same time, the proposed framework recognizes the importance
of privacy, dignity, due process, and the protection of personal information.
Security measures must therefore be implemented in a manner that is
reasonable, non-discriminatory, child-sensitive, and consistent with existing
laws on data privacy and the rights and welfare of children.

      Ultimately, the S.A.F.E. Schools Act seeks to institutionalize a whole-
of-community approach to school safety. By combining prevention, physical
security, early intervention, responsible reporting, community participation,
and child-sensitive measures, this Act aims to ensure that every school in
the country remains a secure and supportive environment conducive to
learning.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

                         “AN ACT
      ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE SCHOOL SAFETY
      AND WEAPON PREVENTION PROGRAM, MANDATING
      THE    COLLABORATIVE     IMPLEMENTATION   OF
      PREVENTIVE MEASURES BY THE DEPARTMENT OF
      EDUCATION,  LOCAL   GOVERNMENT    UNITS, AND
      BARANGAYS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND
      FOR OTHER PURPOSES”

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "S.A.F.E.
(Secure and Firearm-Free Education) Schools Act."

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels
and to ensure that educational institutions are safe, secure, and conducive
to learning. The State recognizes that preventing the entry of weapons into
schools requires a whole-of-community approach involving national
agencies, school administrations, local government units, and grassroots
barangay institutions.

       Security and safety measures under this Act shall be implemented in
a manner that is child-sensitive, non-discriminatory, proportionate, and
respectful of the dignity, privacy, and rights of learners, consistent with
existing laws on child protection, juvenile justice, and data privacy.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

      (a) Weapons shall refer to firearms, bladed or pointed instruments,
          explosives, improvised deadly devices, and other objects designed,
          intended, or used to cause physical injury, excluding ordinary tools
          and materials required for authorized academic, vocational, or
          extracurricular activities.

      (b) School Zones shall refer to the school campus and the immediate
          perimeter extending fifty (50) meter radius from the boundary of



                                       1
         the school or its principal entrances, as may be more particularly
         delineated by the Department of Education (DepEd) in coordination
         with the concerned local government unit.

      (c) Crime Prevention through Environmental Design (CPTED) shall
          refer to a multi-disciplinary approach to deterring criminal
          behavior through the physical design of the school environment.

      (d) Behavioral Threat Assessment shall refer to a proactive, evidence-
          based process used to identify, assess, and manage the risk of
          violence posed by a student, while observing applicable child
          protection, confidentiality, due process, and data privacy
          standards.

      (e) Anonymous Digital Tip Line shall refer to a secure, confidential,
          and accessible SMS, mobile application, web-based, or similar
          reporting mechanism established or authorized by the DepEd to
          allow students, school personnel, parents, and other concerned
          persons to report, without fear of retaliation, weapons, bomb
          threats, explosive devices, threats of violence, or other school safety
          concerns.

     SECTION 4. Mandates of the Department of Education (DepEd). -
The DepEd shall serve as the lead agency in implementing this Act. It shall:

      (a) Formulate and enforce a National School Access Control Policy,
          requiring uniform security risk assessments across all school
          divisions and allowing reasonable variations based on the physical
          layout and assessed risk of each school.

      (b) Establish a national framework for an Anonymous Digital Tip Line,
          providing a secure SMS or app-based channel for students, school
          personnel, parents, and other concerned persons to report
          weapons, bomb threats, explosive devices, threats of violence, or
          other school safety concerns without fear of retaliation. The system
          shall protect the confidentiality of reports and the personal
          information of users in accordance with applicable data privacy
          and child protection laws.

      (c) Mandate the creation of Behavioral Threat Assessment Teams
          (BTATs) in all school divisions, composed of guidance counselors,
          trained teachers, and administrators, to proactively identify and
          intervene with at-risk students exhibiting credible indicators of a
          potential for violence. Such assessment shall be conducted in
          accordance with child protection, confidentiality, due process, and
          data privacy standards, and shall prioritize appropriate
          intervention and support.




                                       2
         The DepEd shall likewise establish minimum standards for a
         School Safety Committee in every school to coordinate school
         safety, emergency preparedness, threat reporting, and community
         coordination.

      (d) Integrate conflict resolution, peace education, and the legal
          consequences of weapon possession into the primary and
          secondary education curricula.

      (e) Provide mandatory annual De-escalation Training for school
          security personnel and teaching staff to safely manage incidents
          involving armed students. Such training shall also include
          emergency response, evacuation and lockdown procedures, crisis
          communication, and child-sensitive response to incidents of
          violence, including bomb threats and explosive incidents.

      SECTION 5. Responsibilities of School Administrations. - All
public and private basic education institutions shall ensure the conduct and
implementation of the following:

      (a) Access Control: Designate specific, secured entry and exit points
          equipped with appropriate personnel and mechanisms. During
          class hours, enforce a Single Point of Entry policy by funneling all
          late arrivals and visitors through an appropriately staffed and
          monitored main gate, ensuring all other perimeters remain secure,
          where practicable and consistent with the school's physical layout
          and security risk assessment.

      (b) Bag Inspections and Transparency: Conduct random,
          reasonable, and non-intrusive inspections of bags prior to entry,
          strictly upholding the students' right to dignity and privacy.
          Additionally, schools may, through consultation with the Parents-
          Teachers Association (PTA), mandate a "Clear Backpack Policy" to
          mitigate concealed threats.

      (c) Parental Involvement: Require parents and guardians, through
          formal agreements during enrollment, to receive information and
          guidance on the safe storage of firearms and other dangerous
          items, responsible reporting of threats, and their role in promoting
          school safety.

      (d) Confiscation and Reporting: Immediately secure any discovered
          weapon and promptly turn it over to the appropriate law
          enforcement authorities, consistent with applicable safety and
          evidence-handling procedures, and report the incident to the local
          Philippine National Police (PNP) within twenty-four (24) hours.
          Where the incident involves a minor, the school shall likewise
          notify the Local Social Welfare and Development Office (LSWDO)
          within the same period.



                                      3
          Any person who, in good faith, reports a suspected weapon,
          threat, or act of violence pursuant to this Act shall not be subject
          to civil or administrative liability solely by reason of such report,
          absent bad faith or malicious intent.

      (e) Safe Surrender and Disposal: Establish secure and controlled
          surrender points outside the main gate, allowing students to safely
          surrender prohibited items before entering the campus. The
          surrender mechanism shall be subject to procedures established
          by the DepEd, in coordination with the PNP, for the safe handling,
          documentation, turnover, and disposition of surrendered items.
          Nothing herein shall be construed as granting immunity from
          liability for offenses committed in connection with the possession,
          use, or transfer of such items.

      (f) Environmental Security: Apply Crime Prevention Through
          Environmental Design (CPTED) principles by eliminating campus
          blind spots, trimming overgrown vegetation, ensuring adequate
          lighting, and removing abandoned structures where weapons may
          be hidden.

      (g) School Safety Officer: Designate a School Safety Officer who
          shall serve as the focal person for the coordination,
          implementation, documentation, and monitoring of school safety
          programs and measures, and shall coordinate with relevant
          government agencies and community partners during safety-
          related incidents.

      (h) Comprehensive School Safety Plan: Adopt and regularly update
          a comprehensive school safety and emergency response plan
          covering prevention, threat reporting, emergency communication,
          evacuation, lockdown, response to weapons-related incidents
          including bomb threats, explosive incidents, and other acts of
          mass violence, and coordination with emergency and law-
          enforcement authorities. The plan shall be reviewed periodically
          based on the school's security risk assessment.

      SECTION 6. Role of Local Government Units (LGUs). — City and
municipal LGUs shall actively support school safety by allocating, subject to
applicable budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws and regulations, a
portion of the Special Education Fund (SEF) for the procurement of modern
security infrastructure, including but not limited to:

      (a) Perimeter hardening and structural reinforcements.

      (b) Electronic or other appropriate access-control systems, including
          RFID and smart turnstiles, where appropriate, to verify student
          enrollment and regulate entry points.



                                      4
     (c) Artificial Intelligence (AI)-enhanced CCTV cameras and other
         appropriate security technologies capable of detecting or assisting
         in the identification of weapons or suspicious activity, subject to
         applicable data privacy, cybersecurity, procurement, and child
         protection standards.

     (d) Enacting or strengthening local ordinances, consistent with
         national law, that regulate the sale, display, or unauthorized
         possession of weapons within school zones.

     (e) Coordinating with the local PNP to ensure heightened police
         visibility and regular patrols in school zones during drop-off and
         dismissal hours.

       SECTION 7. Duties of the Barangay. - As the primary grassroots
unit, the barangay shall:

     (a) Deploy barangay tanods to conduct regular foot patrols within the
         immediate perimeter of schools located within their jurisdiction,
         subject to applicable laws, regulations, and coordination with the
         PNP.

     (b) Utilize the Barangay Council for the Protection of Children (BCPC)
         to identify and facilitate early interventions for at-risk youth
         showing credible indicators of violent behavior, consistent with
         applicable child protection and juvenile justice laws.

     (c) Establish a community watch system to promptly report to school
         authorities and appropriate law enforcement or social welfare
         authorities credible information concerning students suspected of
         carrying weapons before they enter school premises, without
         engaging in unauthorized physical intervention or confrontation.

      SECTION 8. Interventions for Minors. - Any student found in
possession of a weapon shall not be subjected to adult penal facilities.
Interventions shall strictly comply with Republic Act No. 9344, otherwise
known as the Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006, and other applicable
child protection laws.

      The Local Social Welfare and Development Office (LSWDO), in
coordination with the school and appropriate authorities, shall ensure the
immediate assessment and provision of appropriate intervention, diversion,
counseling, family support, and other necessary services, as may be
warranted under Republic Act No. 9344 and its implementing rules and
regulations, with appropriate notification and involvement of the child's
parent or legal guardian, consistent with applicable law.

      SECTION 9. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year's
appropriations of the DepEd. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for
the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

       SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). - Within
sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, in consultation
with the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), the PNP,
through its Firearms and Explosives Office (FEO), where appropriate, and
the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

      The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe minimum
standards for school security risk assessment, access control, threat
assessment, reporting mechanisms, safe surrender procedures, training,
emergency response, school safety planning, and data protection.

     The DepEd shall submit an annual report to Congress on the
implementation of this Act, including school safety assessments, reported
weapons-related incidents, training conducted, and compliance with the
minimum safety standards established under this Act.

      SECTION 11. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part hereof is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provision
not otherwise affected shall remain valid and subsisting.

      SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees,
executive orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof which are
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly: Provided, that nothing in this Act shall be construed to
diminish or impair the rights and protections accorded to children under
Republic Act No. 9344, as amended, Republic Act No. 7610, as amended,
and other applicable laws.

       SECTION 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.

      Approved.
