THEREFOR

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Trees and green spaces are essential for healthy and sustainable communities. They
help clean the air, reduce heat, prevent soil erosion, absorb loodwaters, and provide
safe spaces where families and children can relax and enjoy nature. However, many
municipalities in the Philippines still lack properly planned and maintained public
tree parks. As urban areas grow and natural spaces shrink, it becomes increasingly
important for local governments to protect and develop green areas for the bene it of
present and future generations.

National policy has long encouraged the establishment of tree parks and forests in
every locality. As early as 1969, Republic Act No. 5752 required municipalities and
cities to work toward creating permanent forests or tree parks within their areas.
Later, Letter of Instruction No. 1312 directed every barangay, municipality, and city to
establish and maintain at least one forest or tree park. Despite these policies, many
communities still lack accessible green spaces due to limited funding, land
constraints, and the absence of clear standards for planning and maintenance.

This bill seeks to strengthen local environmental governance by requiring every
municipality to establish, develop, and maintain at least one Municipal Tree Park.
The measure encourages local governments to pass local ordinances and create park

sustainably maintained. It also directs national agencies such as the Department of
Environment and Natural Resources and the Department of the Interior and Local
Government to provide technical guidance and support to local governments.

By ensuring that every municipality has a permanent and well-managed tree park,
this measure promotes environmental protection, strengthens climate resilience, and
creates healthier and greener communities for all Filipinos.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Municipal Tree Park Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology,
promote ecological balance and climate resilience, and strengthen local green spaces
through the establishment and maintenance of municipal tree parks.

Consistent with existing laws and policies on municipal forests, tree parks, and related
forest development projects, local government units (LGUs) shall be supported and
guided to ensure that every municipality maintains accessible and sustainably
managed tree parks for the bene it of present and future generations.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   (a) Tree Park refers to a designated public green area primarily planted with
       trees and compatible vegetation, established for ecological services,


                                                                            3|Page
       biodiversity support, environmental education, and passive recreation, and
       managed under a municipal ordinance and a park management plan.

   (b) Municipality refers to local government unit classified as a municipality under
       existing laws.

   (c) Park Management Plan refers to a document adopted by the municipality
       providing, at minimum, the site profile, planting design, species selection,
       maintenance and protection measures, community participation, and
       monitoring and reporting scheme.

SECTION 4. Mandatory Establishment of Municipal Tree Park. — Every
municipality shall establish, develop, and maintain at least one (1) Tree Park within
its territorial jurisdiction.

The Tree Park shall, as a minimum standard, have an area of not less than one (1)
hectare: Provided, That where a municipality can demonstrate, through a sangguniang
bayan resolution and supporting land-use or site assessment, that no single
contiguous one (1) hectare public site is reasonably available, the requirement may
be complied with through any of the following:

   (a) two (2) or more park sites whose aggregate area is not less than one (1)
       hectare; or

   (b) a cluster or shared Tree Park located in a suitable and accessible site serving
       two (2) or more adjacent municipalities, under an inter-local cooperation
       agreement, without prejudice to each municipality’s obligation to provide
       accessible green space.

       In all cases, the Tree Park shall be located on:
       (i)     public land owned by the municipality; or
       (ii)    land lawfully acquired or accepted by donation; or
       (iii) land otherwise made available to the municipality through lawful
               instruments consistent with existing laws.

SECTION 5. Site Selection and Minimum Standards. — The DENR, in coordination
with the DILG and representative LGUs, shall issue minimum standards on site
selection, design, and maintenance, taking into account, among others: accessibility,
safety, suitability for tree growth, climate and disaster risk, and compatibility with
local land-use plans.

In specifying areas for planting or reforestation, the municipality may prioritize, as
applicable, idle or vacant public lands, public parks, and other suitable public areas,
and may include privately owned lands only with the owner’s consent.

SECTION 6. Municipal Ordinance; Park Management Plan. — Within one (1) year
from the effectivity of this Act, every municipality shall enact an ordinance
establishing its Tree Park(s), appropriating local funds therefor, and adopting a Park
Management Plan.

SECTION 7. Roles of National Government Agencies. —

   (a) Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR). — The DENR
       shall provide technical assistance on species selection (including the use of
       endemic or indigenous species where appropriate), planting design,
       biodiversity management, and maintenance and protection measures, and
       shall harmonize assistance with existing reforestation and greening programs.

   (b) Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG). — The DILG shall
       assist in capacity building, governance guidance, and monitoring of LGU
       compliance.

   (c) Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — The DENR and DILG shall
       promulgate the IRR within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, in
       consultation with LGUs and relevant stakeholders.

SECTION 8. Primary Responsibility of LGUs; Maintenance. — The municipality
shall be primarily responsible for the establishment, development, protection, and
maintenance of the Tree Park(s), including the designation of a municipal of ice or
unit responsible for day-to-day administration.

Barangays, civil society organizations, and deputized partners may be mobilized for
maintenance and protection, consistent with applicable rules.

SECTION 9. Funding. — The amount necessary to implement this Act shall be
charged against the available funds of the municipality, subject to budgeting,
accounting, and auditing rules.

To support implementation, the DENR may, subject to existing laws and available
appropriations, extend assistance from applicable reforestation, greening, or forest

development programs and may prioritize municipalities with approved ordinances
and Park Management Plans.

LGUs are encouraged to enter into partnerships with the private sector,
nongovernmental organizations, people’s organizations, civic groups, and academic
institutions for technical, inancial, and community support, consistent with law.

SECTION 10. Incentives and Recognition. — Subject to existing laws, rules, and
available funds, municipalities that demonstrate effective establishment and
maintenance of Tree Parks based on the IRR standards shall be prioritized in relevant
DENR technical assistance, capacity-building, and program support.

SECTION 11. Monitoring and Reporting. — The municipality shall submit an annual
report to the DENR and DILG, in a form prescribed by the IRR, stating the location,
area, number of trees planted and surviving, maintenance activities, partnerships, and
budget utilization for the Tree Park(s).

SECTION 12. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. — All laws, rules, and regulations inconsistent with
this Act are hereby repealed or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 14. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
