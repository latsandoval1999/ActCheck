                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

          “Behold, children are a heritage from the Lord, the fruit of the womb a reward.”

                                                                                  -    Psalm 127:3


Society is evolving, and the traditional perception of the father’s role as solely the breadwinner
of the family is gradually shifting. This shift benefits not only the fathers themselves, but also
the mothers, their children, and society as a whole.

The influence of gendered norms and hegemonic masculinity perceptions are ingrained in the
current leave entitlement and workplace cultures. While fathers are entitled to take leave, the
leave is significantly insufficient to allow them to forge a meaningful bond with a newborn or
adapt to the change in routine brought about by the birth of their child. Furthermore, workplace
cultures fail to recognise the responsibilities that come with fatherhood and provide insufficient
support for fathers.1

With these considerations in mind, this bill proposes to expand our existing paternity leave law
by increasing the number of days for entitlement for paternity leave and removing the limit for
availment. The bill is rooted in supporting the diverse roles that fathers play in the lives of their
wife and children, the recognition of the importance of fatherhood, its positive impact on
families, and its contribution towards a more progressive society.

The proposed bill recognizes that fathers, both as a parent and a husband, no longer conform
to the traditional gender roles and expectations of traditional society and, at present, should
also be provided with a meaningful opportunity to bond with his newborn child, as well as to
take care of his wife.

The proposed extension will encourage and allow fathers to take a more active role in
parenting and will help cultivate a shared responsibility between the spouses. This will provide
the emotional support that mothers need during difficult and challenging times and will also
help distribute caregiving tasks more equitably as the mother regains her strength in her first
few weeks after giving birth or after a miscarriage.

By expanding the current paternity leave law, we embrace the enhanced role of fathers to their
families and we acknowledge the importance of nurturing family relationships by supporting

    AN ACT FURTHER PROMOTING AND VALUING THE ROLE OF LEGITIMATE
  HUSBANDS AND FATHERS DURING THE EARLY DAYS OF THEIR CHILD’S BIRTH,
    AMENDING, FOR THIS PURPOSE, SECTION 2 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 8187,
        OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE PATERNITY LEAVE ACT OF 1993

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Section 2 of Republic Act No. 8187, otherwise known as the Paternity Leave Act
of 1993, is hereby amended to read as follows:

       SEC. 2. Notwithstanding any law, rules and regulations to the contrary, every married male
       employee in the private and public sectors shall be entitled to a paternity leave of [seven (7)]
       TWENTY (20) days with full pay [for the first four (4)] IN EVERY INSTANCE OF deliveriesY of
       the legitimate spouse with whom he is cohabiting WITH AN OPTION TO EXTEND FOR AN
       ADDITIONAL TEN (10) DAYS WITHOUT PAY. The male employee applying for paternity
       leave shall notify his employer of the pregnancy of his legitimate spouse and the expected date
       of such delivery.

       For purposes of this Act, delivery shall include childbirth, [or any] miscarriage, OR
       EMERGENCY TERMINATION OF PREGNANCY: PROVIDED, THAT IN CASES OF
       MISCARRIAGE OR EMERGENCY TERMINATION OF PREGNANCY, A PATERNITY LEAVE
       OF TEN (10) DAYS WITH FULL PAY SHALL BE GRANTED.

SECTION 2. Rule-Making Authority.— The Secretary of Labor and Employment, the Chairman
of the Civil Service Commission, and the Secretary of Health shall, within thirty (30) days from
the effectivity of this Act, issue such rules and regulations necessary for the proper
implementation of the provisions hereof.

SECTION 3. Repealing Clause.— All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations
inconsistent with any provision of this Act are hereby repealed, modified, superseded, or
amended accordingly.

SECTION 4. Separability Clause.— If any provision of this Act or the application of such
provision to any person or circumstance is held invalid for any reason, the remainder of this
Act or the application of such provision to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected
thereby.

SECTION 5. Effectivity.—This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in at least two (2) national newspapers of general circulation, whichever
comes earlier.
