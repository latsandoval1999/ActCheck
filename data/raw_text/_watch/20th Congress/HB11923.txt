                        AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING CITIZEN-LED BUDGET MONITORING IN
THE INFRASTRUCTURE, HEALTH, AND EDUCATION SECTORS,
CREATING THE CITIZEN BUDGET MONITORING MECHANISM,
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Public financial management requires transparency, efficiency, and active
public participation to ensure that government expenditures directly improve
public welfare. Article II, Section 23 and Article XIII, Section 16 of the 1987
Constitution mandate the State to encourage non-governmental, community-
based, and sectoral organizations, and guarantee the right of the people and their
organizations to effective and reasonable participation at all levels of social,
political, and economic decision-making.

      While the Philippines has made significant strides under the Open
Government Partnership (OGP Commitment PH0056), through initiatives such
as the Citizen Participatory Audit (CPA) of the Commission on Audit (COA),
Budget Partnership Agreements (BPAs) of the Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), and local civic technology feedback tools, citizen
involvement in budget execution remains fragmented, ad-hoc, and unevenly
implemented across regions. In many municipalities, citizen engagement is


                                                                                  1
treated as a mere administrative box-ticking exercise without actionable feedback
loops or binding agency responses.

       The urgency of institutionalized citizen monitoring is heightened by the
implementation of the Mandanas-Garcia Supreme Court ruling, which
significantly expanded the share of national tax allocations downloaded to Local
Government Units (LGUs). As local governments assume broader
responsibilities in infrastructure development, health service delivery, and
educational support, local communities particularly in rural and countryside
areas must be equipped with statutory mechanisms to monitor budget
implementation in real-time.

      This bill institutionalizes Citizen-Led Budget Monitoring (CLBM)
across three vital social and economic sectors: infrastructure, health, and
education. Specifically, this measure:
   1. Establishes Citizen Budget Desks and Partnership Agreements: Mandates
      national agencies (DPWH, DOH, DepEd) and LGUs to partner with
      accredited local civil society organizations (CSOs) and community-based
      groups;
   2. Guarantees Machine-Readable Open Budget Data: Requires real-time,
      proactive public disclosure of project specifications, cost breakdowns,
      procurement records, and completion schedules in accessible digital
      formats;
   3. Institutionalizes an Actionable Feedback Protocol: Imposes a mandatory
      thirty (30) calendar-day period for implementing agencies and LGUs to
      issue written, reasoned responses or corrective action reports on
      community monitoring findings;
   4. Provides Dedicated Grassroots Capacity Building: Directs DBM and DILG
      to conduct regular training programs for grassroots, youth, women, and
      countryside organizations to bridge technical monitoring gaps; and
   5. Strengthens Joint Citizen Participatory Audits: Expands the COA Citizen
      Participatory Audit program to cover local infrastructure, school building
      programs, and health facility procurement.

       By empowering citizens to safeguard public funds at the community level,
this bill fosters responsive governance, prevents corruption, and ensures that
every peso allocated in the national budget reaches rural and regional

 communities.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
 sought.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.           It is the policy of the State to promote
transparency, public accountability, and fiscal openness in the utilization of
public funds. Grounded in Article II, Section 23 and Article XIII, Section 16 of
the 1987 Constitution, the State recognizes the vital role of non-governmental,
community-based, and sectoral organizations in governance.

To this end, the State shall institutionalize mechanisms for direct citizen
participation in monitoring budget execution across the infrastructure, health, and
education sectors, ensuring that public resources are spent efficiently, honestly,


                                                                                 1
and for the equitable development of all regions, particularly rural and
underserved communities.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms.       As used in this Act:

      (a) Citizen-Led Budget Monitoring (CLBM) means the process whereby
      accredited civil society organizations, community groups, and citizens
      independently track, inspect, verify, and evaluate the execution,
      procurement, physical progress, and service delivery of public budget
      allocations.

      (b) Civil Society Organization (CSO) means a non-governmental
                     lo        o               lo           -based association,
      cooperative, sectoral group, or academic institution organized for public
      welfare or local development.

      (c) Covered Sectors mean public infrastructure, public health, and basic
      and higher education.

      (d) Open Budget Data mean public financial and project data published in
      non-proprietary, machine-readable formats that are freely accessible,
      reusable, and downloadable by the public.

      (e) Citizen Budget Partnership Agreement (CBPA) means a formal
      memorandum of agreement between a government agency or local
      government unit and an accredited CSO defining mutual commitments,
      data-sharing protocols, and monitoring arrangements.

agencies, government-owned-and-controlled corporations, and local government
units implementing programs, activities, and projects in the following covered
sectors:

SEC. 5. Citizen-Led Budget Monitoring Framework.             Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, every implementing agency in the covered
sectors and every provincial, city, and municipal government shall establish a
Citizen Budget Desk under its planning or budget office.

The Citizen Budget Desk shall:

      (1) Receive applications and accredit local CSOs and community groups
      seeking to participate in budget monitoring;
      (2) Execute Citizen Budget Partnership Agreements (CBPAs) with
      accredited CSOs;
      (3) Provide accredited citizen monitoring teams with unhindered access to

      disbursement schedules; and

SEC. 6. Mandatory Disclosure of Open Budget and Project Data.
Implementing agencies and LGUs shall proactively publish updated project
information on their official websites, national open data portals, and bulletin
boards at project sites.

Published open budget data shall include:

      (a) Project title, exact location (with geolocated coordinates), and target
      beneficiaries;
      (b) Approved Budget for the Contract (ABC), winning bid amount, and
      contractor details;
      (c) Project timeline, program of work, technical specifications, and
      delivery schedules;
      (d) Itemized breakdown of allocated and disbursed funds; and
      (e) Name and contact information of the designated agency project
      engineer or responsible official.

Information shall be uploaded within fifteen (15) calendar days following
contract execution or issuance of the notice to proceed.

SEC. 7. Actionable Feedback Protocol and Mandatory Agency Response.
Accredited citizen monitoring teams may submit formal Citizen Monitoring
Reports (CMRs) detailing physical defects, delays, non-delivery of goods or
services, or discrepancies between technical specifications and actual execution.

Upon receipt of a CMR, the head of the implementing agency or local chief
executive concerned shall:


                                                                                  4
      (1) Issue an official written acknowledgment to the reporting CSO within
      five (5) working days;
      (2) Order an immediate technical inspection or investigation of the reported
      issue; and
   Ɣ (3) Submit a written Actionable Response Report to the reporting CSO
      and the DBM within thirty (30) calendar days, specifying corrective
      actions taken, rectification orders issued, or legal grounds if the report is
      contested.

Failure of an agency head or responsible official to issue the mandatory
Actionable Response Report within thirty (30) calendar days without valid legal
justification shall constitute administrative nonfeasance punishable under
existing civil service laws.

SEC. 8. Citizen Participatory Audit (CPA) Integration. The Commission on
Audit (COA) shall expand its Citizen Participatory Audit (CPA) program to
regularly conduct joint financial and value-for-money audits with accredited
community monitors for infrastructure, health, and education projects exceeding
Fifty million pesos (P50,000,000.00) in project cost.

COA shall publish the results of joint participatory audits on its official website
within thirty (30) days from audit completion.

SEC. 10. Protection and Non-Retaliation Clause.            No citizen monitor or
accredited CSO shall be subjected to harassment, intimidation, administrative
retaliation, or arbitrary cancellation of accreditation for submitting citizen
monitoring reports in good faith. Any public official or private contractor who
threatens, harasses, or retaliates against a citizen monitor shall be subject to
administrative and criminal liability under applicable laws.

SEC. 11. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current year's appropriations of the DBM,
DILG, DPWH, DOH, and DepEd. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for
the continued operation of Citizen Budget Desks, town hall mechanisms,
capacity-building programs, and civic technology platforms shall be included in
the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).

LGUs are hereby authorized to allocate not less than one percent (1%) of their
annual development funds to support local citizen budget monitoring activities,
CSO capacity building, and participatory town hall consultations.

SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DBM and DILG, in joint consultation with
COA, DPWH, DOH, DepEd, and representative accredited CSOs, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

SEC. 13. Separability Clause.      If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.
