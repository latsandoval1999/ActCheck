EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Filipino family is the foundation of Philippine society and a vital
institution in the development of a strong, productive, and resilient nation. The
Constitution recognizes the sanctity of family life and the family as the
foundation of the nation. Yet, while government programs address health,
education, employment, housing, social protection, child welfare, senior citizens,
disability, migration, and other concerns affecting families, these interventions
are often implemented separately, making it difficult to see the family as a
whole and to determine whether government action is actually improving family
welfare.

The National Committee on the Filipino Family (NCFF), created through
Executive Order No. 241, series of 1995, and strengthened and renamed under
Executive Order No. 238, series of 2003, was established to promote
coordination and a national approach to family welfare. After more than two
decades, however, the changing needs of Filipino families call for a stronger and
more permanent institutional mechanism capable of providing policy leadership,
coordinating existing programs, identifying gaps and duplication, generating
evidence, and holding government efforts to measurable outcomes.

This measure therefore transforms the existing NCFF into the National
Commission on Filipino Family (NCFF), a permanent statutory institution that will
serve as the country's principal policy and coordination body on family welfare
and development. It does not create a parallel bureaucracy or take over the
mandates of existing agencies. Instead, it brings together their efforts within a
coherent national framework while respecting their respective legal and technical
responsibilities.

The proposed Commission will establish a National Family Welfare and
Development Framework and Plan, develop measurable family welfare
indicators, conduct Family Impact Assessments of major government
interventions, monitor implementation, and submit an annual State of the

===== [page 2] =====
Filipino Family Report to the President and Congress. It will also promote
recognition of care work, strengthen family resilience, facilitate family
participation in policymaking, and identify gaps and duplication in existing
programs.

The measure recognizes that family welfare is not the responsibility of one
agency alone. It requires coordinated action across government and meaningful
participation by families and other stakeholders. By transforming the existing
NCFF into a permanent National Commission, this Act provides the institutional
structure needed to make family welfare a measurable, coordinated, and
sustained component of national development.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is

earnestly sought.

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “National
Commission on Filipino Family Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to recognize the
Filipino family as the foundation of the nation and to protect and strengthen
family solidarity, dignity, security, resilience, and total development.

The State shall pursue a coherent, coordinated, evidence-based, rights-
based, gender-responsive, child-sensitive, disability-inclusive, age-responsive,
and geographically responsive national family policy.

The State shall strengthen families' access to health, nutrition, education,
employment, livelihood, housing, social protection, childcare, eldercare,
disability support, social services, and other essential services while respecting
the rights, dignity, autonomy, and responsibilities of individual family members.

The State shall recognize that strong families contribute to human capital
development, poverty reduction, social cohesion, community resilience, and
national development.

The State shall, to the greatest extent practicable, strengthen and coordinate
existing programs and institutions rather than create duplicative programs,
databases, or administrative mechanisms.

SEC. 3. Objectives. — This Act shall pursue the following objectives:

(a) Institutionalize the National Commission on the Filipino Family (NCFF) as
the principal nationa! policy and coordination institution on family welfare and
development;

===== [page 4] =====
(b) establish a coherent and long-term national family policy;

(c) strengthen coordination among national government agencies, local
government units, civil society, private organizations, academic institutions, and
family associations;

(d) integrate family welfare considerations into national development
planning, policy formulation, budgeting, and program implementation;

(e) establish measurable family welfare indicators and a national system for
monitoring family conditions;

(f) identify gaps, overlaps, and emerging issues affecting Filipino families;
(g) strengthen evidence-based family policies and programs;
(h) recognize and address the economic and social dimensions of care work;

(i) strengthen family resilience to economic, health, environmental,
humanitarian, and other shocks;

(j) strengthen family participation in policies and programs affecting them;
and

(k) establish mechanisms for regular reporting to the President and Congress
on the condition of Filipino families.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

(a) Care Work refers to paid and unpaid activities necessary for the physical,
emotional, developmental, and social well-being of children, older persons,
persons with disabilities, persons with iliness, and other persons requiring care;

(b) Family refers to a group of persons related by blood, marriage, adoption,
or other legally recognized relationship, including such family arrangements as
may be recognized under applicable laws;

(c) Family Associations refers to duly organized and recognized organizations
whose principal purpose includes promoting the welfare, rights, development, or
interests of families;

(d) Family Development refers to measures that strengthen the capabilities,
resources, relationships, health, economic security, care capacity, and resilience
of families;

(e) Family Impact Assessment refers to a systematic assessment of the
potential effects of a proposed policy, program, regulation, or other government
intervention on families and their members;

(f) Family Policy refers to laws, policies, programs, services, regulations, and
public investments that directly or indirectly affect the welfare, development,
functioning, rights, responsibilities, and resilience of families;

===== [page 5] =====
(g) Family Welfare refers to the condition in which family members are able
to meet their basic needs, exercise their rights, access essential services,
maintain safe and supportive relationships, participate in economic and social
life, and withstand foreseeable risks and shocks; and

(h) Family Welfare Indicators refers to measurable indicators used to assess
the condition and deveiopment of Filipino families.

SEC. 5. Institutionalization of the National Commission on the Filipino Family.
— The National Committee on the Filipino Family (NCFF), originally established
pursuant to Executive Order No. 241, series of 1995, and expanded and
renamed pursuant to Executive Order No. 238, series of 2003, is hereby
transformed and institutionalized as a permanent national commission and the
principal national policy and coordination body on family welfare and
development to be called as the National Commission on Filipino Family.

The NCFF shall possess the powers and functions expressly granted under
this Act and such other powers as may be necessary and appropriate to
accomplish its mandate.

The NCFF shall operate in accordance with this Act, without prejudice to the
continued recognition of prior executive issuances relating to Family Week and
other family-related observances insofar as they are not inconsistent with this
Act.

SEC. 6. Mandate of the NCFF. — The NCFF shall formulate, coordinate,
monitor, evaluate, and recommend national policies and strategies concerning
the welfare and development of Filipino families.

The NCFF shall serve as the principal national mechanism for ensuring that
family welfare is considered in national development planning and in the
formulation and implementation of government policies and programs.

In the exercise of its mandate, the NCFF shall respect the constitutional and
statutory mandates, administrative authority, and technical jurisdiction of
national government agencies, local government units, and other public
institutions.

The NCFF shail exercise policy leadership, coordination, monitoring,
evaluation, research, and recommendation functions and shall not supplant the
powers and functions vested by law in other government agencies.

and functions:

Development Framework;

(b) Formulate the National Family Welfare and Development Plan;

(c) Develop national policy recommendations concerning family welfare and
development;

(e) Identify policy, program, institutional, geographic, and service-delivery
gaps affecting families;

(f) Identify duplication and fragmentation among government programs
affecting families and recommend appropriate harmonization measures;

(g) Establish common family welfare outcomes and indicators in coordination
with the Philippine Statistics Authority;

(h} Monitor and evaluate the implementation of national family policies and
programs;

(i) Conduct or commission research and policy studies on emerging family
issues;

(j) Establish a Family Impact Assessment mechanism;

(k) Review major government policies and programs that have substantial
and cross-cutting effects on families and issue policy recommendations;

(1) Recommend measures for integrating family welfare considerations into
national and local development plans;

(m) Recommend budgetary priorities and resource-allocation measures for
family welfare and development;

(n) Establish mechanisms for coordination with local government units;

(0) Establish mechanisms for meaningful participation of families and family

(q) Establish mechanisms for the exchange and responsible use of family-

SEC. 40. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred eighty

SEC. 41. Transition and Continuity. — Upon the effectivity of this Act, the

Order No. 241, series of 1995, and expanded and renamed under Executive
Order No. 238, series of 2003, shall be deemed transformed into the National

All existing personnel, records, programs, projects, assets, appropriations,

Within ninety (90) days from effectivity, the NCFF shall review and reorganize
its existing structure, policies, programs, and procedures to conform to this Act.

Nothing in this section shall be construed as requiring the creation of a
separate bureaucracy where existing personnel and resources can perform the
functions prescribed under this Act.

SEC. 42. Appropriations. -—- The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the DSWD and participating agencies, subject to applicable budgeting and

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 43. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain
in full force and effect.

SEC. 44. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
