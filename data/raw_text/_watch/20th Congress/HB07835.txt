                           AN ACT
STRENGTHENING THE PHILIPPINE CROP INSURANCE CORPORATION BY
EXTENDING ITS CORPORATE TERM AND EXPANDING ITS SERVICES,
AMENDING PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 1467, AS AMENDED


                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

Farmers and fisherfolk belong to among the most economically vulnerable sectors of Philippine
society. Their livelihoods are highly dependent on weather conditions, natural resources, and
biological cycles—factors that are increasingly unpredictable due to climate change,
environmental degradation, and recurring natural disasters. A single typhoon, drought, or
disease outbreak can wipe out an entire season’s income, push families into debt, and
perpetuate intergenerational poverty.

The Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation (PCIC), created under Presidential Decree No.
1467, was envisioned as a social protection mechanism to cushion farmers against production
losses. However, its original design—limited largely to palay and anchored on supervised
credit programs—no longer reflects the realities faced by today’s farmers and fisherfolk.
Agricultural production has diversified, risks have intensified, and many small producers
remain excluded from meaningful insurance protection.

This bill seeks to reframe and strengthen agricultural insurance as a core component of the
State’s social protection system. It expands the mandate of the PCIC to provide insurance
coverage not only for palay but also for corn, high-value crops, livestock, aquaculture and
fishery products, agroforestry crops, forest plantations, and essential non-crop agricultural
assets such as machineries, equipment, transport facilities, and related infrastructure. It further
recognizes the human cost of agricultural risks by authorizing life and accident term insurance
for farmers and fisherfolk.

In response to increasingly complex and climate-driven risks, the bill directs the PCIC to adopt
modern technologies and innovative insurance mechanisms, including parametric or index-

based insurance. These tools allow for faster and more transparent claims settlement, ensuring
that assistance reaches affected farmers and fisherfolk when they need it most—especially in
disaster-prone and geographically isolated communities.

The measure also reforms eligibility for insurance coverage by abandoning the outdated
supervised credit system and adopting a risk- and vulnerability-based approach. The PCIC
Board of Directors is empowered to prioritize coverage for farmers and fisherfolk who are most
exposed to natural, climatic, and biological risks. This shift ensures that public support is
targeted toward those who need protection the most and aligns agricultural insurance with the
government’s broader poverty reduction and food security goals.

To further expand access and sustainability, the bill encourages public-private partnerships
with agricultural cooperatives, farmers’ associations, and private insurance providers. Such
partnerships are intended to complement government support, extend insurance reach to
underserved areas, and strengthen the overall delivery of agricultural social protection services.

Recognizing the limits of public resources, the bill rationalizes premium subsidies by directing
government support primarily to subsistence farmers cultivating not more than three (3)
hectares. Government participation in premium sharing is confined to insurance coverage
against unforeseen and unavoidable risks such as typhoons, droughts, and pest and disease
outbreaks. This approach ensures that subsidies are both socially just and fiscally responsible.

Finally, by granting the Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation a perpetual corporate existence,
the bill reinforces the State’s long-term commitment to protecting farmers and fisherfolk from
livelihood shocks. A stable and permanent PCIC enables sustained investments in risk
management, innovation, and inclusive insurance coverage.

Ultimately, this measure affirms that agricultural insurance is not merely a financial tool, but a
vital social protection mechanism—one that safeguards livelihoods, prevents rural poverty, and
strengthens the resilience of Filipino farmers and fisherfolk in the face of growing
uncertainties. The passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

Section 1. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to provide
support to agriculture through adequate financial, production, marketing and other
support services. Towards this end, the State shall:

       a) Ensure food security, intensify food production, promote agricultural credit,
          and broaden the coverage of agricultural insurance amidst the recurrence
          of
          natural disasters and calamities, such as floods, typhoons, heavy rains, and
          drought and pests and diseases that destroy crops, especially palay and
          other grains and crops necessary for food security;

       b) Alleviate the financial burden of farmers and fisherfolk due to losses arising
          from destruction of their livelihood brought by disasters and calamities, keep
          them away from indebtedness, enhance their credit standing, and provide
          additional government support to farmers and fisherfolk to increase
          agricultural production;

       c) Assist banks and financial institutions by enhancing their capabilities to
          extend loans to farmers;

       d) Strengthen and expand the coverage of the Philippine Crop Insurance
          Corporation to provide insurance protection to farmers and fisherfolk against
          losses arising from natural calamities, fortuitous events, plant, livestock, and
          fish diseases, and pest infestations; and

      e) Encourage active private sector participation in the agricultural insurance
         industry through various public-private partnership arrangements that will
         unlock vast financial, technological and other resources for expanding
         agricultural insurance coverage in the country.
