                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Section 9, Article XIV of the 1987 Constitution mandates that the State
shall promote the growth and generalized spread of technology and ensure that
the benefits of science and technology are used to improve the productivity of the
country. This constitutional directive necessitates a legal environment that is not
only protective of digital infrastructure but also dynamic enough to respond to
the rapid evolution of information and communications technology.
Complementary thereto, Section 24, Article II recognizes the vital role of
communication and information in nation-building, while Section 11, Article II
mandates that the State value the dignity of every human person and guarantee
full respect for human rights, which, in the digital age, includes the right to be
secure in one’s electronic data and financial standing.

       The Philippine agricultural and financial sectors remain the dual backbones
of the national economy, yet they are increasingly vulnerable to a fragmented and
evolving digital threat landscape. As of 2025, the Philippines has cemented its
status as a global hotspot for cyber-attacks, ranking as the 3rd most targeted
country for web threats globally. While the Information Technology and Business
Process Management (IT-BPM) industry achieved historic success in 2025,
  generating $40.2 billion in export revenues and employing 1.9 million digital
  workers, this sector faces persistent and widespread computer-related fraud.
  These criminal activities resulted in estimated gross cyber losses of ₱5.82 billion
  in 2024 alone, threatening to erode investor confidence and hinder the industry's
  trajectory toward a projected $42 billion in revenue by 2026.

        Republic Act No. 10175, otherwise known as the "Cybercrime Prevention
  Act of 2012," was enacted to safeguard the integrity of computer systems.
  However, as detected cyber threats have surged—with a staggering 71.9%
  increase in complaints recorded in early 2025—the limitations of the fourteen-
  year-old law have become apparent. Modern threats such as Advanced Persistent
  Threats (APTs), AI-driven phishing, and sophisticated social engineering
  schemes (which accounted for 76% of total fraud losses in 2025) now bypass the
  narrow definitions currently found in Sections 4(a)(3) and 4(b) of RA 10175.

         This bill proposes to modernize our penal framework by widening the
  scope of computer-data-related offenses and allowing for the prosecution of key
  players, specifically targeting principals by inducement (the masterminds). By
  empowering parties who suffer direct or indirect damages to initiate prosecution,
  this measure ensures that technical loopholes do not become shields for criminals.
  These amendments are essential to ensuring the Philippines remains a
  competitive and secure hub for global business and a safer digital space for all
  Filipinos.

        Similar bills were also filed during the 19th Congress.

        In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
