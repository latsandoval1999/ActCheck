                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Persons with disabilities continue to face significant barriers to full and
effective participation in society. Beyond the challenges arising from their
impairments, many persons with disabilities incur additional costs for
transportation, personal assistance, medicines, rehabilitation, assistive devices
and technologies, education, communication, and other disability-related
needs. These additional costs can increase household expenses, limit
opportunities for education and employment, and contribute to poverty,
exclusion, and inequality.

       While existing laws and social protection programs provide important
benefits and assistance to persons with disabilities, these measures do not
always adequately address the recurring extra costs associated with disability.
There is therefore a need for a targeted and disability-responsive social
protection measure that directly assists persons with disabilities in meeting
such costs and supports their independent living, economic security, and full
participation in society.

      This measure seeks to establish a National Disability Support Allowance
Program (DSAP) that will provide a regular Disability Support Allowance of at
least Two Thousand Pesos (₱2,000.00) per month to eligible persons with
disabilities. The allowance shall be periodically reviewed and adjusted to
preserve its purchasing power and shall complement, rather than replace or
diminish, existing benefits and privileges granted under other laws.

      The proposed measure adopts a phased implementation to ensure that
the Program can be progressively expanded in accordance with available
resources and administrative capacity. The initial phase prioritizes children
with disabilities and adults with significant disabilities, followed by persons
with moderate disabilities and, ultimately, other eligible persons with
disabilities who incur disability-related extra costs.
       Eligibility shall be determined through a Disability and Needs
Assessment System (DNAS) that takes into account functional limitations,
support requirements, disability-related extra costs, and barriers to
participation. The measure further seeks to improve coordination and data
management among relevant government agencies through a Disability
Management Information System (DMIS), while ensuring compliance with
applicable data privacy and information security laws.

      The measure also strengthens accessibility in the application and
payment of benefits, establishes a clear appeal and grievance mechanism,
provides safeguards against fraudulent registration and misuse of benefits, and
requires regular monitoring and periodic evaluation of the Program.

       This bill is filed as the House counterpart measure to Senate Bill No.
2386, which seeks to establish a National Disability Support Allowance
Program for persons with disabilities. While maintaining the core objectives
and framework of the Senate measure, this proposed House version further
clarifies the coverage and phased implementation, strengthens eligibility and
assessment procedures, protects existing benefits, improves payment and
appeal mechanisms, reinforces data privacy safeguards, and promotes greater
accountability and transparency in implementation.

      The enactment of this measure will strengthen the country's social
protection framework for persons with disabilities and affirm the State's
commitment to ensuring that disability-related costs do not become a barrier to
dignity, independence, equal participation, and full inclusion in society.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            “AN ACT
      ESTABLISHING    A   NATIONAL  DISABILITY   SUPPORT
      ALLOWANCE PROGRAM AS A SOCIAL PROTECTION AND
      SUPPORT MEASURE TO PROMOTE THE INDEPENDENT
      LIVING, EMPOWERMENT, EQUAL PARTICIPATION, AND FULL
      INCLUSION OF PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES IN SOCIETY,
      PROVIDING   FOR   A  REGULAR   DISABILITY  SUPPORT
      ALLOWANCE, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR”

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "National
Disability Support Act."

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of
the State to uphold the dignity, autonomy, and rights of persons with
disabilities and to ensure their full and effective participation in society on an
equal basis with others.

      In accordance with Republic Act No. 7277, as amended, otherwise known
as the "Magna Carta for Persons with Disabilities," the State recognizes persons
with disabilities as part of human diversity and affirms its duty to promote
their rehabilitation, self-development, self-reliance, and integration into the
mainstream of society.

      The State further recognizes that persons with disabilities incur
additional economic costs arising from disability-related needs and barriers,
which, if left unaddressed, contribute to poverty, exclusion, and inequality. In
pursuit of social justice and equity, the State shall adopt appropriate, targeted,
and disability-responsive social protection measures to mitigate the impact of
such costs and to promote independent living, well-being, and economic
security.
      Towards this end, the State shall adopt an inclusive and disability-
responsive social protection framework that provides direct financial support to
address disability-related extra costs.

      The Disability Support Allowance established under this Act shall
complement, and shall not diminish, replace, or otherwise adversely affect, any
benefit, pension, subsidy, discount, privilege, service, or assistance to which
persons with disabilities are entitled under existing laws, rules, and
regulations.

      SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

  (a) Barriers refer to any factor that hinders or limits persons with
      disabilities from enjoying or exercising, on an equal basis with others,
      their human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic,
      social, cultural, civil, or any other field. These may include, but are not
      limited to, inaccessible physical environments, transportation, and
      infrastructure; the lack or unavailability of assistive devices and
      technologies; inaccessible information and communication in form and
      content; non-inclusive or non-adaptive means of communication; gaps in
      services, including the absence of personal assistance and sign language
      interpretation; discrimination, including the denial of reasonable
      accommodation; negative attitudes, prejudice, and stigma; and
      programs, policies, and laws that restrict or fail to uphold the rights of
      persons with disabilities, consistent with the United Nations Convention
      on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities;

  (b) Children with Disabilities refer to children below eighteen (18) years of
      age who have long-term physical, mental, intellectual or sensory
      impairments which, in interaction with various barriers, may hinder
      their full and effective participation in society on an equal basis with
      other children;

  (c) Disability refers to an umbrella term for impairments, activity
      limitations, and participation restrictions arising from the interaction
      between an individual and contextual factors;

  (d) Disability and Needs Assessment System refers to the standardized
      system and process for determining eligibility for support under this Act,
      based on an individual's functional limitations, support requirements,
      disability-related extra costs, and the barriers that restrict full and
      effective participation in society;

  (e) Disability-related extra costs refer to the additional direct and indirect
      costs borne by persons with disabilities, arising from disability-specific
      needs and from environmental, institutional, and attitudinal barriers,
      including the lack of accessibility and reasonable accommodation.
      Consistent with international frameworks, these costs include
      expenditures for assistive technologies, support services, personal
      assistance, disability-related healthcare, and higher costs of housing and
      transportation, as well as foregone income or productivity losses by
      persons with disabilities or their caregivers, contributing to heightened
      risks of poverty, exclusion, and restricted participation;

(f) Disability Support Allowance refers to the regular monetary amount or
    stipend received by qualified or eligible persons with disabilities under
    the Disability Support Allowance Program. This allowance is established
    to supplement and complement existing social protection measures,
    aiming to address economic hardships and disability-related extra costs
    regardless of the beneficiary's age or income level, thereby facilitating
    their greater independence and full inclusion in society;

(g) Diversity refers to the wide variety of identities and backgrounds
    manifested in differences in physical, mental, intellectual, and sensory
    conditions, age, sex, gender, language, ethnic, indigenous, or social
    origin, health status, and other personal or social characteristics. It
    recognizes disability as a natural and integral part of human diversity
    and affirms that all individuals, regardless of functioning, possess equal
    rights and inherent dignity;

(h) Learners with Disabilities refer to learners in the basic, alternative, and
    higher education system who require additional support and related
    services and adaptive pedagogic methods due to their long or short-term
    physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairments which, in
    interaction with various barriers, may hinder their full and effective
    participation in society on an equal basis with others to develop them to
    their maximum capability;

(i)   Multi-Stakeholder Committee (MSC) refers to the cross-sectoral body
      established under this Act to facilitate coordination among relevant
      government agencies, local government units, organizations of persons
      with disabilities, and other stakeholders, in the implementation and
      monitoring of this Act;

(j)   Neurodiversity refers to the natural variation in the human brain and
      mind, recognizing neurological conditions such as, but not limited to,
      autism and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) as part of the
      spectrum of human diversity rather than as pathologized impairments. It
      emphasizes neuro-affirming support and accommodations that highlight
      the strengths and rights of neurodivergent individuals;
  (k) Organization of Persons with Disabilities (OPDs) refers to an
      organization led, directed and governed by persons with disabilities with
      a clear majority of their membership being persons with disabilities
      themselves and promoting the rights and welfare of persons with
      disabilities. OPDs include, but are not limited to, community, local or
      national organizations of women with disabilities, impairment-specific
      organizations such as national organizations of persons with
      psychosocial disability, children and youth organizations of persons with
      disabilities, parents representing children with disabilities, cross-
      disability organizations, specific health conditions recognized under
      Philippine law such as patients and survivors of cancer, persons with
      rare diseases, and all other organizations of persons with disabilities as
      recognized under the United Nations Convention on the Rights of
      Persons with Disabilities;

  (l)   Persons with Disabilities refer to those who have long-term physical,
        mental, intellectual, or sensory impairments which, in interaction with
        various barriers, may hinder their full and effective participation in
        society on an equal basis with others;

  (m) Reasonable accommodations refer to the necessary and appropriate
      modification and adjustments so as not to impose disproportionate or
      undue burden, and to ensure that persons with disabilities enjoy or
      exercise on an equal basis all human rights and fundamental freedoms;

  (n) Users refer to persons with disabilities eligible for the Disability Support
      Allowance Program; and

  (o) Eligible Beneficiary refers to a person with disability who has been
      determined, through the Disability and Needs Assessment System and
      applicable eligibility criteria, to be entitled to receive the Disability
      Support Allowance under this Act.

      SECTION 4. Disability Support Allowance Program (DSAP). - The
DSAP shall provide individual support allowances to qualified persons with
disabilities, as determined by the Eligibility Determination Mechanism to be
developed by the Multi-Stakeholder Committee (MSC).

       The support allowance shall help cover disability-related expenses,
including transportation augmentation, food and nutrition, disability support
services requiring human assistance, maintenance medicines, mental and
psychosocial support, rehabilitation services, educational and learning needs,
assistive devices, technologies and support equipment, pre-employment and
employment-related services, and access to government assistance and
services.
      The DSAP shall provide a regular stipend to persons with disabilities. The
amount of Two Thousand Pesos (₱2,000.00) per month per eligible beneficiary
is hereby established as the minimum Disability Support Allowance and shall
be periodically adjusted based on the Consumer Price Index as published by
the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), without prejudice to any increase
authorized by law or appropriations.

      The allowance shall complement existing social protection programs and
statutory benefits to help address higher costs associated with transportation,
housing, nutrition, hygiene, and temporary human assistance requirements.
For working-age adults who are out of work, the allowance shall provide basic
income security and help cover disability-related extra costs associated with
seeking and retaining work or employment.

      The receipt of the Disability Support Allowance shall not, by itself, result
in the reduction, suspension, termination, or denial of any existing benefit,
pension, subsidy, discount, privilege, or social protection assistance to which
the beneficiary is otherwise entitled under existing law.

       The Program shall be implemented in three (3) phases starting with the
existing master list of local government units (LGUs) of qualified persons with
disabilities who already have the Persons with Disabilty (PWD) identification
cards:

        (a) Phase 1 – The initial phase of the DSAP, to be implemented in the
first three (3) years, shall prioritize provision of stipends to eligible persons with
disabilities based on the criteria to be formulated by the MSC:

         1. Children with disabilities; and
         2. Adults with significant disability.

      (b) Phase 2 – The second phase of the Program, to be implemented in the
next three (3) years, shall include all persons with moderate disabilities who
incur disability-related extra costs necessary for participation in social and
economic activities including participation in the labor market.

      (c) Phase 3 – All persons with disabilities who incur disability-related
extra costs not otherwise covered, and who are determined eligible under the
prescribed criteria, shall be entitled to the corresponding benefits.

      The phased implementation under this Section shall determine the order
and priority of enrollment and shall not be construed to permanently exclude
any person with disability otherwise covered by this Act. The DSWD may
accelerate the implementation of any phase when sufficient appropriations,
administrative capacity, and validated beneficiary data are available.
      SECTION 5. Coverage. - All persons with disabilities who incur
disability-related extra costs, based on a human rights approach, shall be
covered by the provisions of this Act.

      Priority shall initially be given to children with disabilities and adults
with significant disabilities who are facing significant barriers and disability-
related extra costs.

      SECTION 6. Appeal Mechanism. - The implementing agencies, together
with the MSC, shall formulate and incorporate a clear, accessible, and timely
appeal mechanism within the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of this
Act.

       This mechanism shall outline the explicit procedures, timelines, and
responsible desks for individuals seeking to contest or appeal any decision,
eligibility status, or assessment result arising from the Disability and Needs
Assessment System (DNAS) and the Disability Management Information
System (DMIS).

        The appeal mechanism shall provide the applicant or beneficiary with
written notice of the decision and the reasons therefor, accessible procedures
for filing an appeal, reasonable accommodation when necessary, and review by
an officer or body independent of the original determination, whenever
practicable.

      No application or appeal fee shall be charged to any person seeking
benefits under this Act.

      The filing or pendency of an appeal shall not, when warranted by the
circumstances, result in the automatic termination of an existing allowance
pending final resolution of the appeal, subject to rules and safeguards to be
provided in the IRR.

       SECTION 7. Eligibility. - In determining eligibility to avail of the DSAP,
the DNAS shall be developed and established, with a focus on functional
limitations, support requirements, and barriers to participation.

      In recognition of the multiple forms of poverty and the extent of
disability-related costs required for basic participation, eligibility of persons
with disabilities to avail of the DSAP shall be compatible with eligibility and
benefits under existing social protection and economic empowerment
programs, with the DSAP serving as a top-up to help subsidize disability-
related extra costs: Provided, That the MSC may, in its discretion, adopt
additional bases or mechanisms to achieve the objectives of this Act.
      Eligibility shall not be denied solely on the basis of age, employment
status, income, or receipt of other social protection benefits, unless otherwise
expressly provided by law.

      The DNAS shall take into account disability-related extra costs,
functional limitations, support requirements, and barriers to participation and
shall not rely solely on medical diagnosis or income-based criteria.

      Where valid and current government records sufficiently establish a
person's disability and relevant eligibility information, the person shall not be
required to undergo unnecessary or duplicative assessment.

      SECTION 8. Lead Agency. - The Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD) shall be the administrator of the Disability Support
Allowance Program.

       All applications for availing of the DSAP shall be lodged with the DSWD
through the Persons with Disabilities Affairs Office (PDAO) or the Focal Person
in the Local Social Welfare and Development Office (LSWDO) in Local
Government Units. The DSWD shall establish accessible application channels,
including, where practicable, in-person, online, mobile, assisted, and
community-based mechanisms, taking into account the needs of persons with
disabilities in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas. An MSC shall
be created to co-develop the DNAS to formally establish the Eligibility
Determination Mechanism including the eligibility criteria, implementation
strategy plan, monitoring and evaluation mechanism, enhancement of the
Disability Management Information System to improve access to programs and
services for equal participation, and any other necessary policy or guidelines to
effectively implement this Act. The MSC shall be chaired by the National
Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA).

      Members of MSC shall be representatives from the Department of Social
Welfare and Development (DSWD), National Council on Disability Affairs
(NCDA), Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), Department
of Health (DOH), Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), Department of
Education (DepEd), Civil Service Commission (CSC), Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA), LGUs, National Anti-Poverty Council (NAPC) - Persons with
Disabilities Sector, representatives from various organizations of persons with
disabilities reflecting a wide diversity of identity and backgrounds, and other
relevant stakeholders.

      The representation of organizations of persons with disabilities shall be
meaningful and shall include, to the extent practicable, representatives of
women with disabilities, children and youth with disabilities, persons with
physical disabilities, sensory disabilities, intellectual disabilities, psychosocial
disabilities, and other disability sectors.
       All policies concerning programs and services for persons with
disabilities shall be reviewed and enhanced for clarity of operationalization at
the local level by the MSC.

      The eligibility determination mechanism shall be made accessible,
affordable, reliable, timely, disability-responsive, and compliant with applicable
data privacy laws and the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons
with Disabilities.

       SECTION 9. Disability and Needs Assessment System (DNAS). - To
effectively implement social protection measures targeting persons with
disabilities, the DSWD, in coordination with the NCDA, DOH, DILG, PSA,
LGUs, and organizations of persons with disabilities, shall establish and
maintain a standardized Disability and Needs Assessment System (DNAS).

      The DNAS shall assess functional limitations, support requirements,
disability-related extra costs, and barriers to participation for purposes of
determining eligibility under this Act.

      The DNAS shall provide standardized, transparent, accessible, and non-
discriminatory assessment procedures and shall provide reasonable
accommodation to persons undergoing assessment.

      The DNAS shall, to the maximum extent practicable, recognize valid and
current government records and shall avoid unnecessary duplication of
medical examinations, certifications, or assessments.

      Any reassessment shall be conducted only when required to determine
continued eligibility or when there is a material change in the beneficiary's
circumstances.

      The DNAS shall be distinct from the Disability Management Information
System (DMIS), which shall serve primarily as the information-management
infrastructure supporting the administration of the Program.

      The DSWD shall establish, maintain, and manage the DMIS in
coordination with the NCDA, DICT, PSA, LGUs, and other relevant government
agencies. The DMIS shall have interlinkages between the DNAS, the Unified ID
System, the Community-Based Monitoring System, the National ID System, the
Philippine Persons with Disabilities Registry, the DOH Persons with Disability
Registry and the PhilHealth Database System through appropriate
interoperability mechanisms, subject to applicable data privacy, information
security, and cybersecurity laws, to provide disability data and facilitate access
to appropriate government programs and services.
      Only information necessary and proportionate to the administration,
monitoring, evaluation, and delivery of the DSAP shall be collected, processed,
retained, or shared.

      The DSWD shall coordinate with the Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT) for a secure and interoperable information
system that supports evidence-based and responsive policy-making.

      SECTION 10. Policy on Persons with Disability Identification Cards.
- The issuance of Persons with Disability Identification Cards pursuant to
Republic Act No. 7277, otherwise known as the "Magna Carta for Persons with
Disabilities," as amended, along with other relevant laws, administrative
issuances and local ordinances, shall include the information necessary to
determine eligibility for the DSAP.

       A standardized coding system shall likewise be adopted to ensure the
efficient and accurate identification of eligible persons with disabilities

      No person otherwise qualified to receive the Disability Support Allowance
shall be denied solely because of a delay in the issuance, replacement, renewal,
or updating of a PWD Identification Card, provided that the person's disability
and eligibility may be established through other valid government records or
the DNAS.

      The use of PWD Identification Card information for purposes of the DSAP
shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the "Data
Privacy Act of 2012," and other applicable data protection laws.

     SECTION 11. Direct Payment of the Disability Support Allowance. -
The DSAP User shall receive their allowance directly and securely through
Authorized Government Depository Banks (AGDBs).

        In localities where AGDB services are insufficient or unavailable, the
payment may be coursed directly, or through an AGDB, by engaging the
services of rural banks, thrift banks, cooperative banks, or money remittance
institutions duly accredited by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) to
facilitate the distribution of such payments.

      The DSWD shall provide alternative secure payment mechanisms for
beneficiaries who do not have access to an AGDB or bank account, particularly
beneficiaries in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

      No beneficiary shall be required to pay a fee, commission, or other charge
as a condition for receiving the Disability Support Allowance.
       Where a beneficiary is a minor or is unable, by reason of disability or
legal incapacity, to personally receive or manage the allowance, payment may
be made to a parent, legal guardian, or duly authorized representative, subject
to safeguards prescribed in the IRR.

      The DSWD shall establish safeguards to prevent unauthorized
withdrawal, diversion, exploitation, or misuse of allowances received on behalf
of beneficiaries.

       SECTION 12. Fraudulent Registration. - Any person who knowingly
and fraudulently submits false or falsified information or documents, makes a
material misrepresentation or causes, facilitates, or participates in the
registration of a person who is not eligible for inclusion in the DSAP shall be
penalized as follows:

      (a) Any person with disability, or their parent or relative of any degree of
          consanguinity or affinity, or any third person found to be deliberately
          committing any fraudulent act of enrolling a person with disability in
          the Philippine Registry for Persons with Disabilities when a person is
          not eligible shall suffer the following:

            1. For the first violation, a fine of not less than Fifty Thousand
               Pesos (₱50,000.00) but not exceeding One Hundred Thousand
               Pesos (₱100,000.00);

            2. For the second and subsequent violations, a fine of not less
               than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱100,000.00) but not
               exceeding Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱200,000.00);

       Provided, that no person shall be penalized under this Section for a mere
clerical error, inadvertent omission, or good-faith mistake in an application or
registration, in the absence of intent to defraud.

      (b) Where the offense is committed in connection with the offender's
          official functions, profession, business, or operations, the following
          additional penalties shall apply:

                  (1) Public officer or employee – In addition to the applicable
                      fine, the offender shall be subject to suspension or
                      dismissal from service and disqualification from holding
                      public office, without prejudice to other administrative
                      sanctions under applicable laws, rules, and regulations;

                  (2) Licensed professional – In addition to the applicable fine,
                      the offender shall be subject to suspension or revocation
                      of their professional license or accreditation by the
                     appropriate professional or regulatory body, after due
                     notice and hearing; and

                  (3) Business, organization, or other entity – The entity shall
                      be subject to a fine of not less than Two Hundred
                      Thousand Pesos (₱200,000.00) but not exceeding Five
                      Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00), and for repeated
                      violations, suspension or revocation of its business
                      permit, license, accreditation, or other authority to
                      operate, after due notice and hearing. Any responsible
                      officer, director, manager, or employee who knowingly
                      authorized, consented to, or participated in the
                      commission of the offense shall likewise be liable under
                      this Section.

      The penalties imposed under this Act shall be without prejudice to
criminal, civil, or administrative liability under other applicable laws.

      SECTION 13. Monitoring. - The DSWD and the NCDA shall convene the
MSC to make an annual assessment of the implementation of this Act for the
purpose of review and recommendation of additional measures necessary for
the attainment of the objectives of this Act.

      The annual assessment shall include, among others, the number of
applications received, approved, denied, and pending; geographic and
beneficiary coverage; payment timeliness; appeals and grievances;
implementation costs; reported cases of fraud or irregularity; and measures
taken to address implementation gaps.

      The DSWD shall publish an annual implementation report in an
accessible format, subject to Republic Act No. 10173 and other applicable laws
on privacy and confidentiality.

      SECTION 14. Periodic Review. - Within five (5) years from the effectivity
of this Act and every five (5) years thereafter, the NCDA shall conduct a
comprehensive performance evaluation of this Act and submit its findings and
recommendations to the appropriate committees of the Senate and the House
of Representatives.

       The periodic review shall specifically assess the adequacy of the Two
Thousand Pesos (₱2,000.00) minimum monthly allowance in relation to
inflation, poverty thresholds, disability-related extra costs, transportation and
other essential costs, and the purchasing power of beneficiaries.
      The review shall likewise assess the effectiveness of the phased
implementation and identify measures necessary to accelerate or expand
coverage where fiscal and administrative capacity permits.

      SECTION 15. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the
implementation of this Act shall be included in an annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA) of the DSWD in the year following its enactment into
law and thereafter.

      Appropriations for the DSAP shall include, as necessary, funding for:

            (a) Payment of the Disability Support Allowance;
            (b) Development, maintenance, and security of the DNAS and
                DMIS;
            (c) Beneficiary registration, validation, and assessment;
            (d) Accessible application, payment, and appeal mechanisms;
            (e) Monitoring and evaluation; and
            (f) Other reasonable administrative costs necessary for the effective
                implementation of this Act.

      The administrative costs of implementing the DSAP shall not be deducted
from or charged against the Disability Support Allowance of beneficiaries.

     The DSWD shall maintain a separate accounting of appropriations and
expenditures for the DSAP in accordance with applicable budgeting,
accounting, auditing, and reporting laws and regulations.

       SECTION 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within one
hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DSWD and the
DOH, in consultation with the DILG, PSA, NCDA, DepEd, NAPC- Persons with
Disabilities Sector, representatives of organizations of persons with disabilities
reflecting a wide diversity of disability sectors and lived experiences, and other
relevant stakeholders from government agencies and non-government
organizations, shall issue the necessary rules and regulations to implement the
provisions of this Act.

      The IRR shall ensure that no eligibility requirement, procedural
requirement, or administrative condition is imposed that is inconsistent with
the express provisions of this Act.

      The IRR shall provide clear standards for the DNAS, eligibility
determination,   application    and     registration,   payment,    authorized
representatives, reassessment, appeals, fraud prevention, data privacy,
monitoring, and coordination with existing social protection programs.
      The DSWD shall ensure meaningful consultation with organizations of
persons with disabilities in the formulation and periodic review of the IRR.

      SECTION 17. Suppletory Clause. - The provisions of Republic Act No.
7277, otherwise known as the "Magna Carta for Persons with Disabilities," as
amended by Republic Act No. 10754 and Republic Act No. 9442, have
suppletory effect on the provisions of this Act.

      SECTION 18. Separability Clause. - If any portion or provision of this
Act is held unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected
thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

      SECTION 19. Repealing Clause. - All laws, executive orders,
proclamations, rules, regulations and other issuances or parts thereof which
are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended
or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 20. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.

      Approved.
