                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Constitution recognizes the vital role of the Judiciary as an
independent and co- equal branch of government tasked with safeguarding the
rule of law and ensuring the fair and impartial administration of justice. To
preserve judicial independence and maintain the dignity of judicial office, it is
imperative that members of the Judiciary, including Judiciary officials granted
judicial rank, salary, and privileges, are afforded adequate retirement protection
and benefits commensurate to the nature and importance of their public service.

       Republic Act No. 910, as amended, grants retirement and survivorship
benefits to members of the Judiciary. Over the years, however, several Judiciary
officials who were granted judicial rank, salary, and privileges by law have
remained outside the full and uniform coverage of the said retirement law despite
performing functions that are closely related and indispensable to the
administration of justice. Moreover, disparities continue to exist in the
retirement treatment and survivorship benefits received by certain Judiciary
officials similarly situated with incumbent Justices and Judges.

      This bill seeks to standardize the retirement benefits of Justices, Judges,
and Judiciary officials with judicial rank, salary, and privileges by expressly
including them within the coverage of Republic Act No. 910, as amended. The
measure likewise seeks to ensure parity and equity in the grant of retirement
gratuities, survivorship benefits, disability retirement benefits, and pension
adjustments, including the automatic adjustment of pensions whenever there is
an increase in the salaries and allowances of corresponding positions in the
Judiciary.

      The measure further recognizes the invaluable contribution of members of
the Judiciary by extending educational benefits to their qualified children and
by strengthening survivorship protections for their legitimate surviving spouses.
These mechanisms serve not only as social protection measures but also as
means of promoting morale, institutional stability, and continued dedication to
public service within the Judiciary.
      This proposed legislation substantially reenacts a measure that was
approved on Third Reading by the House of Representatives during the
Nineteenth Congress, thereby reflecting the continuing recognition by Congress
of the necessity of strengthening and standardizing the retirement benefits of
members and officials of the Judiciary.

      Ultimately, this bill affirms the State’s commitment to uphold judicial
independence by ensuring that those entrusted with the administration of justice
are provided with adequate security and protection during retirement and after
long years of faithful and honorable public service.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

      SECTION 2. Section 2 of the same Act, as amended by, is further amended
to read as follows:

      “SEC. 2. In case a Justice of the Supreme Court or Court of Appeals, the
Sandiganbayan or of the Court of Tax Appeals, or a Judge of the regional trial
court, metropolitan trial court, municipal trial court in cities, municipal trial
court, municipal circuit trial court, shari'a district court, shari'a circuit court, or
any other court hereafter established, OR A JUDICIARY OFFICIAL
CONFERRED WITH THE SAME RANK AS A JUSTICE OR A JUDGE, dies while
in actual service, regardless of his/her age and length of service as required in
Section 1 hereof, his/her heirs shall receive a lump sum of five (5) years' gratuity
computed on the basis of the highest monthly salary plus the highest monthly
aggregate of transportation, representation and other allowances such as
personal economic relief allowance (PERA) and additional compensation
allowance received by him/her as such Justice, [or] Judge OR JUDICIARY
OFFICIAL: Provided, however, That where the deceased Justice, [or] Judge OR
JUDICIARY OFFICIAL has rendered at least fifteen (15) years OF SERVICE
either in the Judiciary or in any other branch of Government, or both, his/her
heirs shall instead be entitled to a lump sum of ten (10) years gratuity computed
on the same basis as indicated in this provision: Provided, further, That the lump
sum of ten (10) years gratuity shall be received by the heirs of the Justice, [or the]
Judge OR JUDICIARY OFFICIAL who was killed because of his/her work as
such: Provided, That the Justice, [or] Judge OR JUDICIARY OFFICIAL has
served in Government for at least five (5) years regardless of age at the time of
death. When a Justice, [or] Judge OR JUDICIARY OFFICIAL is killed
intentionally while in service, the presumption is that the death is work-related.”

      SECTION 3. Section 3 of the same Act, as amended, is further amended
to read as follows:
       “SEC. 3. Upon retirement, a Justice of the Supreme Court or of

thereafter upon survival after the expiration of five (5) years, to further
annuity payable monthly during the residue of his/her natural life

the retirement be any permanent disability contracted during his/her
incumbency in office and prior to the date of retirement, he/she shall
receive a gratuity equivalent to ten (10) years' salary and the allowances
aforementioned: Provided, further, That should the retirement under
Section 1(a) hereof be with the attendance of any partial permanent
disability contracted during his/her incumbency and prior to the date
of retirement, he/she shall receive an additional gratuity equivalent to
two (2) years lump sum that he/she is entitled to under this Act:
Provided, furthermore, That if he/she survives after ten (10) years or
seven (7) years, as the case may be, he/she shall continue to receive a
monthly annuity as computed under this Act during the residue of
his/her natural life pursuant to Section 1 hereof: Provided, finally, That
those who have retired with the attendance of any partial permanent
disability five (5) years prior to the effectivity of this Act shall be entitled
to the same benefits provided herein.
