                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Oil is the lifeblood of several essential sectors of the Philippine economy. For
millions of Filipino workers, fuel is not just another commodity; it is the basic input that
allows them to earn a living. Public transport drivers cannot operate without diesel.
Fisherfolk cannot venture into our waters without fuel for their boats. Farmers rely on
fuel-powered machinery to prepare land, irrigate crops, and transport produce to our
markets. Fertilizer production is also heavily dependent on fuel. Thus, when oil prices
surge, the effects are immediately felt by these sectors.

       In recent years, global supply disruptions and geopolitical tensions in Europe
and the Middle East have made oil prices more volatile. The Philippines, which imports
the vast majority of its petroleum supply, remains highly vulnerable to these
fluctuations. A significant portion of our country’s imported crude petroleum comes
from Saudi Arabia ($1.79B), United Arab Emirates ($1.22B), Iraq ($474M), Colombia
($65.5M), and Japan ($63.2M), making domestic fuel prices highly sensitive to
developments in global markets.1

       The effects of these fluctuations are immediately felt at the grassroots level.
Rising fuel prices have reportedly forced many small-scale fishers to reduce their

       The government has long recognized these challenges and has occasionally
responded with temporary fuel subsidies. Even as early as 2011, similar forms of
assistance had already been extended to transport workers during periods of
extraordinary price increases.3 In 2025, the national government allocated funding in
the 2025 General Appropriations Act to provide fuel assistance to more than one
million drivers and operators in the transport sector. However, these interventions
have largely been reactive, timebound, and the continuation of such programs often
depends on annual budget decisions and shifting administrative priorities.

       This bill seeks to address these concerns by institutionalizing a Fuel Subsidy
Program as a regular and continuing government initiative. Rather than responding to
crises on an ad hoc basis, the proposed measure establishes a stable mechanism
that supports vulnerable sectors when fuel price fluctuations threaten their livelihoods.
To ensure efficiency, the program focuses on sectors whose incomes are directly and
measurably affected by the surge of oil prices. Eligibility is also linked to existing
government registries such as the LTFRB databases, the Municipal Fisherfolk
Registration System, the BFAR boat registry, and the Registry System for Basic
Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA). This ensures that assistance reaches verified
beneficiaries while strengthening the government’s sectoral databases.

       Supporting fuel costs also has broader economic implications. Empirical
studies in agriculture and fisheries show that subsidies which reduce the cost of key
production inputs enable small producers to sustain operations during price shocks4
and maintain supply levels that help stabilize food and commodity markets.5 In the
context of the Philippines, sustaining the operations of farmers, fisherfolk, and drivers
protects essential supply chains, from food distribution to daily commuting.

       Several measures have previously been filed in Congress seeking to provide
fuel subsidies to specific sectors, such as fisherfolk and farmers, and separate
proposals focused solely on public transport drivers. This bill builds upon these efforts
by integrating these approaches into a single, comprehensive program that covers all

 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
 in Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Fuel Subsidy Program
Act.”

        Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the welfare of sectors whose livelihoods are directly affected by
fuel price fluctuations. The State recognizes the essential role of transport workers,
farmers, and fisherfold in maintaining economic productivity and food security. To this
end, the government shall institutionalize a sustainable and equitable fuel subsidy
program to mitigate the adverse effects of fuel price volatility in vulnerable sectors.

       Sec. 3. Establishment of the Fuel Subsidy Program. – There is hereby
established a Fuel Subsidy Program, to be implemented as a regular and continuing
government initiative to provide targeted financial assistance to qualified beneficiaries
adversely affected by abnormal increases in fuel prices.

       The Program shall provide cash assistance of not less than Seven Thousand
Pesos (Php7,000.00) per month to each eligible beneficiary under this Act. Provided
that the amount of the monthly subsidy shall be subject to annual review and
adjustment by the Advisory Council hereby created.
      The Fuel Subsidy Program shall be integrated into the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA) to ensure its sustainability and continuity.

      Sec. 4. Advisory Council. – An Advisory Council is hereby created to review
and adjust the amount of cash subsidy which shall be given to qualified beneficiaries
based on prevailing fuel prices, and taking into account inflation and fiscal capacity of
the government. The Advisory Council shall be composed of the following:
        a. Secretary of Transportation as Chairperson;
        b. Chairperson of the LTFRB as Co-chairperson;
        c.   Secretary of Energy;
        d. Secretary of Labor;
        e. Secretary of Budget and Management;
        f.   Secretary of Finance, and
        g. Secretary of Interior and Local Government; as members

      The Advisory Council shall meet at least twice a year for its regular meeting
and may call a special meeting in case of extraordinary increase in the price of fuel.
The secretariat of LTFRB shall act as the secretariat of the Advisory Council.

Sec. 5. Beneficiaries –This Fuel Subsidy Program shall primarily cover low-income
individuals and sectors whose livelihood are directly affected by fluctuations in fuel
prices, as determined by the implementing agencies under this Act.
       The following shall be considered eligible beneficiaries:

   (a) Public Transport Operators and Drivers – refer to low-income operators and
       drivers of public utility vehicles (PUVs), such as jeepneys, buses, taxis,
       tricycles, and transport network vehicle services (TNVS), duly registered with
       the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) or with
       their respective local government units (LGUs): Provided, that registration
       under the LTFRB's existing databases, or in coordination with the Department
       of Transportation (DOTr), shall be a requirement for eligibility under this Act;

   (b) Fisherfolk – refer to municipal fisherfolk engaged in fishing within municipal
       waters, whether coastal or inland, who operate motorized fishing vessels with
       maximum gross weight of three (3) gross, either individually or through a
       fisherfolk organization,    cooperative, or association: Provided, that such
       beneficiaries are duly listed in the registry system of the Department of
       Agriculture (DA), particularly in the Municipal Fisherfolk Registration System:
       Provided, further, that their fishing vessels are properly registered in the
       integrated boat registry system in the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic
       Resources (BFAR) Boat Registration, or the boat registry system of the
       concerned local government unit (LGU): Provided, finally, That municipal
       fisherfolk and fishing vessels that are not yet registered under aforementioned
       registry systems may still be beneficiaries provided they register in any of the
       aforementioned registry systems within the periods to be set by the BFAR or
       LGU;

   (c) Farmers and Agricultural Producers – refer to low-income farmers,
       farmworkers, and agricultural producers who utilize fuel-powered machinery
       or equipment for land preparation, irrigation, post-harvest processing, or the
       transport of agricultural products, as validated by the Department of Agriculture
       (DA): Provided, that registration in the Registry System for Basic Sectors in
       Agriculture (RSBSA) shall be a precondition for the receipt of benefits under
       this Act: Provided, further, that farmers who are not yet registered in the RSBSA
       may still be served under this Act, provided they enroll in the said registry; and

   (d) Other Low-Income Sectors – other similarly situated groups as may be
       identified by the Department of Energy (DOE) and the Department of Social
       Welfare and Development (DSWD), in coordination with the Department of
       Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), who are demonstrably
       affected by fuel price increases and meet the eligibility criteria to be prescribed
       in the implementing rules and regulations (IRR): Provided, that priority shall be
       given to individuals and households listed in the government's Community-
       Based Monitoring System (CBMS) or other verified registries of low-income
       individuals, to ensure that assistance is extended to those most in need.

       Sec. 6. Non-Diminution of Benefits. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
reduce, withdraw, or otherwise diminish any benefit, assistance, or privilege currently
enjoyed by transport workers, farmers, fisherfolk, and other eligible beneficiaries under
existing laws, ordinances, rules, regulations, or programs. These include, but are not
limited to, existing fuel subsidies, livelihood assistance, insurance coverage, training
opportunities, access to production resources, and other socio-economic support
programs being implemented by national government agencies or local government
units (LGUs): Provided, that no modification or discontinuance of such benefits shall
be made without due process and prior consultation with the affected sectors and
relevant stakeholders.

        Sec. 7. Appropriations. – The amounts as may be necessary for the
effective implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA) under the respective budgets of the Department of
Energy, Department of Transportation (DOTr), Department of Agriculture (DA),
and Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD).

       Sec. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Advisory Council, in consultation with stakeholders, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to implement this Act, including the
identification of eligible beneficiaries, distribution mechanisms, monitoring, and
reporting systems.

       Sec. 9. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is held invalid, the
other provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.


       Sec. 10. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, order, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions if this Act are hereby
repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

      Sec. 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after the
completion of its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of
general circulation.
