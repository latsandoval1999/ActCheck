PERSONNEL ON CERTAIN GOODS AND SERVICES, PROVIDING FOR A TAX
DEDUCTION MECHANISM THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Military and uniformed personnel (MUP) serve on the frontlines of national defense,
law enforcement, disaster response, and public safety. Every day, they face risks and
demanding duties in order to protect the nation and keep communities safe. Despite
their vital role, many members of the uniformed services continue to face the rising
costs of basic goods and services that affect their daily lives and the welfare of their
families.

The Philippines has long recognized that certain sectors deserve special assistance to
help meet essential needs. For example, the Expanded Senior Citizens Act of 2010
(Republic Act No. 9994) provides mandatory discounts on medicines, medical
services, transportation, and other basic goods and services for senior citizens. These
types of discount programs are part of the government’s social policy to support
sectors that require additional protection and recognition.

This bill seeks to extend a similar form of support to the country’s military and
uniformed personnel by granting them a ten percent (10%) discount on selected
essential goods and services, including medicines, health services, and
transportation. To ensure fair and practical implementation, the bill also allows
participating establishments to treat the discount as a tax deduction under the tax
laws, helping balance the welfare bene it with the interests of businesses.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Military and Uniformed
Personnel Discount Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to recognize the vital
role of military and uniformed personnel (MUP) in national defense, law enforcement,
and public safety, and to promote their welfare by adopting non-wage bene its that
help make essential goods and services more affordable, consistent with iscal
sustainability and the protection of property rights.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) Military and Uniformed Personnel (MUP) — refers to active-duty, in-service
       uniformed personnel of the following: (1) Armed Forces of the Philippines
       (AFP); (2) Philippine National Police (PNP); (3) Bureau of Fire Protection
       (BFP); (4) Bureau of Jail Management and Penology (BJMP); (5) Philippine
       Coast Guard (PCG); and (6) commissioned of icers of the Hydrography Branch
       of the National Mapping and Resource Information Authority (NAMRIA)


                                                                            3|Page
       transferred from the former Bureau of Coast and Geodetic Survey, as may be
       recognized under existing laws and issuances.

       For avoidance of doubt, “MUP” under this Act excludes:
       (i)    civilian employees;
       (ii)   uniformed personnel who are not in active service, unless otherwise
              provided by law; and
       (iii) cadets/trainees unless expressly covered by the IRR.

   (b) Establishment — refers to any person, natural or juridical, whether public or
       private, engaged in the sale of goods or services to the general public, including
       utilities and transport operators, subject to applicable regulation.

   (c) Exclusive use and enjoyment — means goods and services actually used,
       consumed, or availed of by the MUP as the end-user, and not for resale, transfer,
       or commercial use.

SECTION 4. Discount Privilege. — All covered MUP shall be entitled to a ten percent
(10%) discount on the sale of the following goods and services, for their exclusive use
and enjoyment:

   (a) Medicines, including essential vaccines and medical supplies, accessories, and
       equipment, subject to the guidelines to be issued by the Department of Health
       (DOH);

   (b) Professional fees of attending physician/s and other licensed health
       professionals, and medical and dental services, including diagnostic and
       laboratory fees, in private hospitals, medical facilities, outpatient clinics, and
       home health care services, subject to DOH guidelines to be issued in
       coordination with the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth);

   (c) Actual fares for land transportation and public railways, including public
       utility buses (PUBs), public utility jeepneys (PUJs), taxis, shuttle services, and
       trains, including LRT, MRT, and PNR;

   (d) Actual fares for domestic air and sea travel, based on the actual fare and
       subject to reasonable advance booking rules as may be provided in the IRR;

   (e) Services in hotels and similar lodging establishments, restaurants, and
       recreation centers;

   (f) Admission fees charged by theaters, cinema houses, concert halls, circuses,
       carnivals, and other similar places of culture, leisure, and amusement; and

   (g) Funeral and burial services for the death of a MUP, which may be claimed by
       the person who actually paid the funeral and burial expenses, upon
       presentation of the death certificate and such other requirements as may be
       provided in the IRR.

       In case of promotional discounts, the MUP may avail of either the promotional
       discount or the discount under this Act, whichever is higher, unless the
       promotional discount is expressly limited to specific non-stackable campaigns
       under the IRR.

SECTION 5. Limitations; Prohibition Against Abuse. — The discount privilege shall
apply only to the personal account of the MUP for goods and services actually used or
availed of by the MUP as end-user. It shall not extend to relatives, companions, or any
other person who is not a quali ied bene iciary under this Act.

The following acts shall constitute abuse:
   (a) using another person’s ID;
   (b) purchasing for resale;
   (c) misrepresentation of status; or
   (d) splitting transactions to circumvent reasonable quantity limitations under the
       IRR.

SECTION 6. Proof of Entitlement. — To avail of the discount, the MUP or an
authorized representative, where applicable under the IRR, shall present a valid
identification card issued by the MUP’s mother agency, or a digital/online verification
mechanism authorized by the IRR.

SECTION 7. Tax Treatment; Reimbursement Mechanism. — The discount granted
under this Act shall be treated as a deductible expense from gross income, based on
the net cost of the goods sold or services rendered, subject to proper documentation
and to the provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended, and the
implementing rules to be issued by the Department of Finance (DOF) through the
Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR).

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with the DOTr, DTI, DENR only as

   (a) reasonable quantity limits for medicines and essential goods to prevent abuse
       while ensuring access;
   (b) procedures for funeral and burial discount claims;
   (c) advance booking rules for air and sea travel; and
   (d) digital veri ication standards.

SECTION 9. Penalties. —

   (a) Refusal to Honor Discount. — Any person or establishment that refuses to
       honor the discount, after due veri ication, shall be punished by imprisonment
       of not less than two (2) years but not more than six (6) years, and a ine of not
       less than Fifty thousand pesos (₱50,000.00) but not more than One hundred
       thousand pesos (₱100,000.00), for the irst offense.

       For the second and subsequent offenses, the penalty shall be imprisonment of
       not less than two (2) years but not more than six (6) years and a ine of not
       less than One hundred thousand pesos (₱100,000.00) but not more than Two
       hundred thousand pesos (₱200,000.00).

   (b) Abuse of Privilege. — Any person who abuses the privileges granted herein
       shall be punished by imprisonment of not less than six (6) months but not
       more than two (2) years, and a ine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos
       (₱50,000.00) but not more than One hundred thousand pesos (₱100,000.00),
       without prejudice to administrative liability under applicable service rules.

   (c) Juridical Persons. — If the offender is a corporation, partnership, association,
       or similar entity, the president, general manager, managing partner, or other
       responsible of icer who participated in, authorized, or knowingly tolerated the
       violation shall be liable.
