                          AN ACT
PENALIZING ONLINE FRAUD, DECEPTIVE DIGITAL PRACTICES, AND

                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

            The Constitution affirms, under Article II, Section 5, the duty of the State to protect
life, liberty and property, and under Article XVI, Section 9, the obligation to safeguard
consumers against deceptive, unfair and fraudulent business practices. These mandates now
extend decisively into the digital sphere where the rapid expansion of e-commerce, social
media, mobile payments and online marketplaces has transformed how Filipinos conduct
business and interact with one another.

           However, this transformation has been accompanied by an alarming rise in online
fraud and malicious internet activities. The exponential growth of internet use has created
unprecedented opportunities for online transactions but also enabled a surge of schemes that
inflict financial loss, reputational damage, emotional distress and psychological harm on
unsuspecting individuals. Among the most prevalent are online selling scams, fake investment
solicitations, phishing and spoofing schemes, impersonation of private individuals or
institutions and harmful forms of so-called “online pranking” that are intentionally designed to
humiliate, deceive, coerce or defraud victims. These acts increasingly target minors, senior
citizens, persons with disabilities and other vulnerable sectors.

           Recent data makes the scale of this threat unmistakable. According to the
Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC), 10,004 cybercrime complaints
were recorded in 2024, more than triple the 3,317 cases reported in 2023. Of these, 3,242 cases
involved online fraud while 3,534 cases were classified as consumer fraud, including non-
delivery of purchased goods, fake advertisements and deceptive online transactions. Losses
attributed to online scams in the same year approached ₱198 million, representing only
reported cases and likely understating the true level of victimization.

           These risks are intensified by accelerating digital adoption. Based on the 2024
National ICT Household Survey conducted by the Philippine Statistics Authority and DICT,
48.8 percent of all households, or around 13.56 million homes, now have internet access, up
sharply from only 17.7 percent in 2019. In the National Capital Region, more than 90 percent
of individuals aged ten and above now own a smartphone. This widespread connectivity, while
beneficial for economic and social participation, has significantly increased exposure to online
deception. Studies by the Philippine Institute for Development Studies show that one in four
Filipinos has experienced a cyber incident ranging from phishing attempts to fraudulent
solicitation. Monitoring reports from the National Privacy Commission and the National
Cybersecurity Plan further highlight increasing incidents of identity theft, data breaches,
reputational attacks and emotional harm triggered by malicious online behavior.

           Despite the existence of laws such as the Cybercrime Prevention Act (RA 10175)
and the Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act (RA 11981), substantial gaps remain. Many
digital offenses, particularly those that involve fraudulent online selling, impersonation without
account intrusion, malicious “pranks” intended to cause harm, deceptive solicitations,
fabricated digital identities and manipulative online content, are not adequately covered by
existing statutes. These activities often escape prosecution because they do not neatly fall under
hacking, cybersex or the limited set of punishable acts enumerated in older legislation.

          This measure seeks to fill these gaps by defining, prohibiting and penalizing various
forms of online fraud and malicious internet activities that exploit digital platforms, social
media algorithms or electronic communications. It specifically targets online selling scams and
fraudulent commercial transactions, deceptive digital solicitations and fabricated
advertisements, impersonation of individuals or institutions for financial or malicious gain,
phishing, spoofing and similar techniques aimed at stealing personal or financial data and
malicious online pranking that inflicts psychological, reputational or financial harm.

           The bill likewise enhances inter-agency coordination among the Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT), the Department of Trade and Industry
(DTI), the Philippine National Police Anti-Cybercrime Group (PNP-ACG) and the National
Bureau of Investigation Cybercrime Division (NBI-CCD) to ensure robust enforcement, digital
forensics capability and efficient prosecution of offenders. Digital platforms operating in the
Philippines are also encouraged to establish accessible reporting mechanisms and to cooperate
lawfully with enforcement agencies.

          By modernizing the country’s legal framework against digital deception, protecting
citizens from emerging online harms and strengthening accountability in the digital
marketplace, this measure fulfills the State’s constitutional responsibility to uphold consumer
welfare, protect property and dignity and secure the well-being of Filipinos in an increasingly
interconnected society.

          In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                          AN ACT
PENALIZING ONLINE FRAUD, DECEPTIVE DIGITAL PRACTICES, AND
MALICIOUS INTERNET ACTIVITIES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Anti-Online Fraud and Malicious
Internet Activities Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. The State shall safeguard individuals and institutions from
online fraud, deceptive digital practices, and malicious internet activities. It shall promote a
secure and trustworthy digital ecosystem that upholds responsible online conduct, supports
legitimate e-commerce, and protects the public from psychological, reputational, and financial
harm.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act:
           a. Online Fraud — any act, scheme, or device carried out through digital or
              electronic means intended to deceive, misrepresent, or defraud individuals or
              entities for personal, financial, or material gain, whether completed or
              attempted.
           b. Online Selling Scam — any deceptive act involving the sale of goods or services
              online, including non-delivery, delivery of materially different or defective
              items, false representation of identity, or use of fabricated transaction proofs.
           c. Malicious Online Pranking — the intentional creation, transmission, or
              dissemination of false, harmful, humiliating, or distressing content through
              digital platforms for the purpose of causing emotional harm, reputational
              damage, public humiliation, financial loss, or disruption of daily activities.
           d. Digital Platform — any website, application, social media service, marketplace,
              communication service, or online system accessible through the internet.
              e. Phishing and Spoofing — fraudulent techniques used to deceive individuals
                 into divulging personal information, login credentials, financial data, or other
                 sensitive information through imitation, manipulation, or misrepresentation of
                 legitimate entities.

SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. The following acts shall be unlawful:
  a. Engaging in online selling scams or other fraudulent commercial transactions conducted
       through digital platforms.
  b. Creating or conducting malicious online pranks that cause or are reasonably expected to
       cause emotional distress, reputational damage, financial loss, or harassment.
  c. Impersonating or falsely representing an individual, organization, or institution to solicit
       money, property, services, or personal data.
  d. Disseminating fabricated, misleading, or deceptive online advertisements or promotional
       content.
  e. Conducting phishing, spoofing, false identity fabrication, or any other deceptive digital
       practices aimed at obtaining personal, financial, or sensitive information.
  f. Knowingly facilitating, aiding, or abetting the commission of any of the acts defined under
       this Act.

SEC. 5. Penalties.
  a. Any person found guilty of committing any prohibited act under this Act shall suffer
       the penalty of prisión correccional (six months and one day to six years) and/or a fine
       of ₱50,000 to ₱500,000, depending on the gravity and extent of damage.
  b. When the offender is a juridical person, the responsible officers, directors, managers,
       or agents who participated in, authorized, or tolerated the unlawful acts shall be held
       criminally liable.
  c. When the offense results in large-scale fraud, affects three (3) or more victims,
       involves government financial assistance or public funds, or compromises critical
       personal data, the penalty shall be imposed in its maximum period.
  d. When the victim is a minor, senior citizen, pregnant woman, or a person with
       disability, the penalty shall likewise be imposed in its maximum period.
  e. The court may further order restitution, return of unlawfully obtained property, and
       payment of actual damages.

SEC. 6. Jurisdiction. This Act shall apply to:
          a) Offenses committed within the Philippines; and
          b) Offenses committed outside the Philippines when any of the elements, effects, or
             damages are experienced within Philippine territory, or when the victim is a
             Filipino citizen.

SEC. 7. Reporting Mechanism. — Digital platforms operating in the Philippines shall establish
accessible reporting systems for suspected violations of this Act and shall cooperate with law
enforcement agencies upon lawful request.
SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). Within sixty (60) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Information and Communications Technology
(DICT), in consultation with the Department of Justice (DOJ), the Department of Trade and
Industry (DTI), the National Privacy Commission (NPC), the National Telecommunications
Commission (NTC), and relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the implementing rules and
regulations necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act.

SEC. 9. Appropriations. The funds necessary for the effective implementation of this Act shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SEC. 10. Separability Clause. If any part or provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
or invalid, the remaining portions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, issuances, or rules inconsistent with this Act
are hereby amended or repealed accordingly.

SEC. 12. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in the Official
Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
