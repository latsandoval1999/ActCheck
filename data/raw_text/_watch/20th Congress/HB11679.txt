                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

This measure seeks to strengthen the protection of OFW remittances by promoting affordable,
transparent, and secure money transfer services. It requires remittance service providers to clearly
disclose all applicable fees, charges, and exchange rates so that OFWs and their families know
exactly how much they are paying and how much will be received. It also seeks to prevent hidden
or unauthorized charges and strengthen government oversight to ensure that remittance providers
operate fairly and responsibly.

The measure further promotes the use of safe, reliable, and affordable digital remittance channels,
giving OFWs more convenient options for sending their hard-earned money home. At the same
time, financial literacy programs will help OFWs and their families better understand fees,
exchange rates, digital transactions, and common financial scams. Stronger coordination among
concerned government agencies will also ensure that complaints are addressed promptly and that
remittance service providers are held accountable for violations.

More importantly, this measure recognizes that OFW remittances are more than simple financial
transactions. Every peso sent home represents hours of hard work, personal sacrifice, and time
spent away from family. Ensuring that more of this money reaches their loved ones means helping
families afford education, healthcare, daily necessities, savings, and other opportunities for a better
future.

By making remittance services more affordable, transparent, and secure, the government can help
ensure that OFWs receive the full benefit of their hard earned income. It is also a way of honoring
their sacrifices and protecting the families who depend on them.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE PROTECTION OF OVERSEAS FILIPINO WORKERS
(OFW) REMITTANCES, MANDATING FEE CAPS AND TRANSPARENCY IN
TRANSFER COSTS, PENALIZING UNAUTHORIZED AND PREDATORY
REMITTANCE PRACTICES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "OFW Remittance Protection Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to afford full protection to labor,
local and overseas, and to recognize the vital role of Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) in national
development. The State recognizes that foreign remittances sent by OFWs represent the product
of their hard work and sacrifice. Towards this end, the State shall implement measures to protect
OFW remittances from unfair transaction fees, opaque exchange rate markups, predatory financial
practices, and illegal remittance schemes, while promoting accessible, secure, and affordable
financial channels.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall be defined
as follows:

   (a) Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) refers to a person who is to be engaged, is engaged, or
       has been engaged in a remunerated activity in a state of which he or she is not a citizen, or
       on board a vessel navigating foreign seas.
   (b) Remittance refers to foreign exchange earnings or money transfers sent by an OFW from
       abroad to a beneficiary in the Philippines through authorized financial channels.
   (c) Remittance Transfer Service Provider (RTSP) refers to any bank, non-bank financial
       institution, money transfer operator (MTO), electronic money issuer (EMI), microfinance
       institution, or authorized agent offering money transfer services to OFWs.
   (d) Exchange Rate Spread refers to the difference between the prevailing interbank exchange
       rate and the exchange rate offered by the RTSP to the sender or beneficiary.
SECTION 4. Cap on Remittance Fees. — No bank, non-bank financial institution, or licensed
RTSP operating in the Philippines shall charge remittance transfer fees exceeding fifty percent
(50%) of the prevailing standard market rate for money transfers, or a maximum ceiling of two
percent (2%) of the total amount transferred, whichever is lower. The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas
(BSP) shall periodically review and establish fee ceilings to ensure affordability.

SECTION 5. Full Disclosure and Exchange Rate Transparency. — All RTSPs operating or
partnering in the Philippines shall strictly adhere to transparency standards in all remittance
transactions. Prior to executing a transaction, the RTSP shall disclose to the OFW sender and the
recipient:

   a. The exact transfer fee charged;
   b. The prevailing interbank exchange rate and the actual exchange rate applied to the
      transaction;
   c. The total exchange rate margin or spread collected by the RTSP;
   d. The net amount in Philippine Pesos (PHP) to be received by the beneficiary; and
   e. The estimated date and time of availability of the funds.

No hidden charges, undisclosed processing fees, or dynamic currency conversion markups shall
be collected beyond what was explicitly disclosed prior to transaction confirmation.

SECTION 6. Tax Exemption on Remittance Services. — To reduce the cost burden on migrant
workers:

   a. All money transfers sent by OFWs to their legitimate family members in the Philippines
      shall be exempt from Documentary Stamp Tax (DST) and local government transaction
      taxes; and
   b. Service fees charged by domestic financial institutions and payment channels for
      processing OFW remittances shall be exempt from Value-Added Tax (VAT).

SECTION 7. Protection Against Fraud and Unlicensed Operators.

   a. Mandatory Registration — Only licensed RTSPs duly registered with the BSP and
      accredited by the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) shall be authorized to handle
      OFW remittance services.
   b. Anti-Fraud Protocols — All RTSPs shall implement robust cybersecurity measures, multi-
      factor authentication, and consumer dispute resolution mechanisms to protect transactions
      against fraud, phishing, and unauthorized deductions.
   c. Prohibition of Illegal Remittance Practices — The operation of unlicensed money transfer
      networks, informal hawala-style unregistered channels targeting OFWs, and predatory
      lending tied to remittance receipts is hereby declared illegal.

SECTION 8. Inter-Agency Oversight Council on OFW Remittance Protection. — An Inter-
Agency Coordinating Council is hereby established to monitor compliance, investigate abuses,
and streamline remittance services for OFWs. The Council shall be composed of:
   a. Secretary of the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) – Chairperson
   b. Governor of the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) – Co-Chairperson
   c. Secretary of the Department of Finance (DOF) – Member
   d. Secretary of the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) – Member
   e. Director-General of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) –
      Member
   f. Two (2) representatives from accredited OFW non-governmental organizations – Members

SECTION 9. Financial Literacy and Digital Inclusion. — The DMW, in coordination with the
BSP, OWWA, and accredited banks, shall integrate mandatory financial literacy training into the
Pre-Departure Orientation Seminars (PDOS) and Post-Arrival Orientation Seminars (PAOS) for
OFWs. The program shall cover savings, investment, financial planning, digital remittance tools,
and protection against financial scams.

SECTION 10. Penalties. — Any RTSP, financial institution, or individual found violating the
provisions of this Act shall be subject to the following administrative and criminal penalties:

   a. First Offense: A fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00) but not
      exceeding One Million Pesos (₱1,000,000.00), and a formal reprimand;
   b. Second Offense: A fine of not less than One Million Pesos (₱1,000,000.00) but not
      exceeding Two Million Pesos (₱2,000,000.00), and a temporary suspension of business
      operating license for six (6) months;
   c. Third and Subsequent Offenses: A fine of not less than Three Million Pesos
      (₱3,000,000.00) but not exceeding Five Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00), permanent
      revocation of the operating license, and imprisonment of responsible corporate officers for
      a period of not less than six (6) years nor more than twelve (12) years.

SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the BSP, in consultation with the DMW, DOF, DTI, OWWA, and relevant
private sector stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

SECTION 12. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, rules, and regulations, or parts
thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

SECTION 14. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
