                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

      This bill seeks to institutionalize a more comprehensive nursing education
framework that formally recognizes both basic and advanced nursing education
programs. It amends Republic Act (RA) No. 9173, otherwise known as the “Philippine
Nursing Act of 2002”, and integrates master’s and doctoral degree pathways into the
nursing education system. It enhances faculty qualification standards and aligns the
preparation of Filipino nurses with the evolving demands of healthcare delivery and
higher education.

       The Philippine healthcare system continues to face increasing demand for
highly trained nursing professionals capable of assuming advanced clinical,
research, academic, and leadership roles. According to the World Health
Organization1, nurses constitute the largest segment of the global health workforce.
Yet, many countries continue to experience shortages of highly skilled nursing
professionals capable of delivering specialized care and strengthening health
systems. Nursing students are presented with limited opportunities for structured
career progression and constrains the country’s capacity to expand nursing research,
improve evidence-based practice, and strengthen healthcare leadership.

       The proposed measure establishes a legal framework for advanced nursing
programs that cultivate expertise in clinical practice, research, health policy,
leadership, and nursing education, while raising qualification standards for faculty
engaged in graduate instruction. It likewise institutionalizes community-oriented
learning and research that respond to the healthcare needs of underserved and
vulnerable populations. These reforms require legislative action because they
redefine the structure of nursing education under Republic Act No. 9173 and
establish enduring national standards that cannot be achieved through
administrative issuances alone.

         Filipino nurses have long carried the country’s reputation for excellence in

challenges.

       More importantly, the measure creates pathways for highly skilled nurses to
further their careers within the country, allowing their expertise and experience to
directly benefit Filipino communities, particularly those who remain underserved.
Supporting advanced nursing education is therefore not only an investment in the
nursing profession but also a commitment to building a stronger, more resilient, and
more responsive healthcare system for future generations.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

KNOWN AS THE “PHILIPPINE NURSING ACT OF 2002”, AND FOR OTHER

Congress assembled:

     Section. 1. Section 25, Article V of Republic Act (RA) No. 9173, otherwise

      [program shall provide sound general professional foundation for the
      practice of nursing.] REFERS TO THE FORMAL LEARNING AND
      TRAINING IN THE SCIENCE OF NURSING PROVIDED BY
      COLLEGES,        UNIVERSITIES,       AND       OTHER      HIGHER
      EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS DULY RECOGNIZED AND
      ACCREDITED BY THE COMMISSION ON HIGHER EDUCATION
      (CHED). THE SCIENCE OF NURSING INCLUDES THE
      FUNCTIONS AND DUTIES IN THE PHYSICAL CARE OF
      PATIENTS,      AND      A   COMBINATION          OF    DIFFERENT
      DISCIPLINES THAT BOTH ACCELERATE THE PATIENT’S
      RETURN TO HEALTH AND WELLNESS, AND NORMALCY OF
      LIFE, AND HELP MAINTAIN IT.

             “[The learning experiences shall adhere strictly to specific
      requirements embodied in the prescribed curriculum as promulgated
      by the Commission on Higher Education’s policies and standards of
      nursing education.] THE NURSING EDUCATION IN THE
      PHILIPPINES SHALL CONSIST OF BASIC AND ADVANCED
      PROGRAMS WHEREBY THE FORMER IS COMPLETED
      THROUGH A FOUR-YEAR UNDERGRADUATE DEGREE AND
      THE LATTER, THROUGH A MASTER’S DEGREE AND A

      SEC. 2. A new Section 26 shall be inserted after the amended Section 25 of the
same Act to read as follows:

           “SEC. 26. BASIC NURSING EDUCATION. – THE BASIC
      NURSING EDUCATION PROGRAM SHALL CONSIST OF NO
      LESS THAN FOUR (4) YEARS OF UNDERGRADUATE STUDIES
      THAT WILL EQUIP STUDENTS WITH THE NECESSARY
      KNOWLEDGE, SKILLS, AND OVERALL COMPETENCY FOR
      ENTRY-LEVEL NURSING PRACTICE OF REPUTABLE QUALITY.

           “ADMISSION TO THE BASIC PROGRAM FOR NURSING
      SHALL REQUIRE A PASSING SCORE IN A NATIONAL
      NURSING ADMISSION TEST, AS SET BY THE COMMISSION
      ON HIGHER EDUCATION.

           “THE CURRICULUM FOR BASIC NURSING EDUCATION
      SHOULD INCLUDE COMMUNITY INTEGRATION AND
      IMMERSION FOCUSING ON THE MARGINALIZED SECTORS
      SUCH AS WOMEN, CHILDREN, THE ELDERLY, PERSONS WITH
      DISABILITIES, THE URBAN AND RURAL POOR, INDIGENOUS
      PEOPLE, AMONG OTHER VULNERABLE GROUPS OF
      PERSONS.”
