EXPLANATORY NOTE

The 1987 Constitution recognizes the family's role in nation-building. Article XV, Section
1 expressly provides that “The State recognizes the family as the foundation of the nation.” It
further declares that the State shall strengthen the family as a basic autonomous social institution
and shall equally protect the life of the mother and the life of the unborn from conception. The
Constitution likewise recognizes the vital role of the youth in nation-building and guarantees their
physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being.

Article II, Section 12 further mandates the State to protect the sanctity of family life and to
protect and strengthen the family as a basic autonomous social institution. Article XV, Sections 2
and 3 likewise recognize parents' rights and responsibilities in rearing children and direct the State
to defend children's right to assistance, including proper care and protection from neglect. abuse,
cruelty, exploitation, and other conditions prejudicial to their development. The Constitution also
recognizes, under Article XIII, Section 3, the right of workers to humane working conditions and
directs the State to afford full protection to labor.

These constitutional guarantees reveal that safeguarding the family, nurturing children, and
upholding the dignity of workers are not isolated duties. They are crafted together as shared
responsibilities of the State.

The latest available Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) Labor Force Survey as of
September 2026 is for June 2026. It reports 53.25 million Filipinos aged 15 and over in the labor
force, of whom 50.66 million were employed. The labor force participation rate was 65.1%, while
unemployment stood at 4.9%.

Current laws offer vital protections and leave benefits for workers and families, such as
maternity, paternity, solo-parent, and service incentive leaves. This measure does not seek to
replace or lessen these safeguards. Instead, it aims to complement them by addressing sitations
that existing leave options may not fully cover.

For many first-time parents, caring for a child can feel daunting, especially without
experience, guidance, or enough external support. Parents, guardians, and caregivers may algo face
tough situations but cannot easily seek help due to work demands. In these moments, even a short
period of protected leave can open the door to meeting a child's essential needs, reaching out for
help, talking with teachers or professionals, or simply being there when a child needs utmost care,
comfort, and guidance.

Even more urgently, some children suffer quietly, facing fear, neglect, confusion, or
distress without understanding what is happening or how to seek help. Their struggles often go
unnoticed when the adults meant to care for them cannot be present due to work commitments.

The State must therefore recognize that child care is more than attending to illness or
emergencies. It includes listening, guiding, observing, teaching, accompanying, protecting, and
understanding a child's behavioral, emotional, developmental, educational, and psychesocial
needs.

This measure is likewise consistent with the broader principle of responsible parenthood
recognized in existing Philippine legislation. Republic Act No. 10354, or the Responsible
Parenthood and Reproductive Health Act of 2012, recognizes responsible parenthood and provides
for access to information and services concerning responsible parenthood and reproductive health.
This proposed measure complements that framework by addressing the continuing responsibility
of parents and caregivers to provide care, guidance, protection, and support after a child is born.

The State must nurture the family from the home up. If the family forms the bedrock of
society, then safeguarding its protection and strength is ensuring the nation's very foundation. We
must guard that the basic unit of our society receives the care, dignity, protection, and support it
deserves.

This measure therefore seeks not merely to provide another workplace benefit, but to bring
the Constitution's recognition of the family and its promise to children and workers to life. It
acknowledges that presence is a powerful form of care, and that timely support from a pagent or
caregiver can keep a child's struggles from growing into greater challenges. Statutory and
contractual leave benefits. It therefore strengthens, rather than displaces, the protections already
established by law.

With this measure, the State affirms that whenever a child needs care, guidance, protection,
or simply a listening ear, the person responsible for that child's well-being should have a real
chance to be present.

Passing this measure will help fortify families, support first-time parents and caregivers,
aid those facing hardships, shield children who might otherwise suffer quietly, and fulfill the
Constitution's call to protect families, champion children's welfare, and promote humane working
conditions.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

RINA REIKO CHUA-TAI

HOUSE BILL NO. 11585

Introduced by REPRESENTATIVE KATRINA REIKO CHUA-TAI

AN ACT
GRANTING CHILD CARE LEAVE TO PARENTS, LEGAL GUARDIANS, AND
PERSONS LAWFULLY ENTRUSTED WITH THE CARE OF A CHILD, PROVIDING
FOR ITS IMPLEMENTATION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Child Care Leave Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to protect and strengthen the
family, promote the welfare and best interests of children, and recognize the importance of
caregiving responsibilities. The State shall ensure that workers are not compelled to choose
between their employment and their legitimate responsibilities toward the children under their

care.

This policy is consistent with the constitutional protection of family life, the rights and

welfare of children, and the protection of labor.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

a. “Child” refers to a person below eighteen (18) years of age, or a person over
eighteen (18) years of age who, by reason of disability or condition, is incapable of

fully caring for himself or herself;

b. “Parent” refers to a biological, adoptive, or legally recognized parent of a child;

c. “Legal guardian” refers to a person legally appointed or recognized to exercise

parental authority or guardianship over a child;

d. “Person lawfully entrusted with the care of a child” refers to a foster parent,
relative, or other person who has been legally or formally authorized, recognized,
or entrusted by a competent government agency, court, or the child's parent or legal

guardian to provide regular care and supervision to the child; and

e. “Child care leave” refers to the paid leave granted under this Act for purposes of

attending to the health, safety, education, welfare, or essential care needs of a child.
SEC. 4. Coverage. — This Act shall cover:

a. Employees in the private sector, regardless of employment status, subject to the

conditions provided herein; and

b. Employees in the national government, local government units, government-owned
or controlled corporations, state universities and colleges, and other government

offices, subject to applicable civil service and compensation laws and regulations.

SEC. 5. Entitlement to Child Care Leave. — A covered employee who is a parent, legal
guardian, adoptive or foster parent, or a person lawfully entrusted with the care of a child shall be

entitled to five (5) working days of child care leave with full pay for every year of service.

The leave may be availed of continuously or intermittently when the employee's presence
is reasonably necessary to attend to the child's health, safety, education, welfare, or other essential

care needs.

SEC. 6. Eligibility and Documentary Requirements. — Yo avail of the benefit, an
employee shall submit reasonable proof of parenthood, guardianship, adoption, foster care, or

lawful responsibility for the child, as may be prescribed by the implementing rules and regulations.

For private-sector employees, the employee must have rendered at least six (6) months of

service, whether continuous or cumulative, with the employer.

No employee shall be required to submit medical or other sensitive information beyond

what is reasonably necessary to establish entitlement to the leave.

SEC. 7. Non-Diminution of Existing Benefits. — The child care leave granted under this
Act shall be separate from and in addition to all existing statutory, contractual, or company-
provided leave benefits, including service incentive leave, maternity leave, paternity leave, solo-

parent leave, and other applicable benefits.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish or replace any leave or benefit more

favorable to the employee.

SEC. 8. Notice and Emergency Availment. — An employee intending to avail of child

care leave shall provide reasonable prior notice to the employer whenever practicable.

In cases involving an emergency affecting the child's health, safety, or welfare, prior notice
shall not be required; provided that the employee shall notify the employer at the earliest
reasonable opportunity.

SEC. 9. Protection Against Discrimination and Retaliation. — No employer shall
dismiss, discriminate against, harass, or otherwise prejudice an employee for applying for or

availing of child care leave in accordance with this Act.

Any adverse employment action taken primarily on account of the lawful exercise of this

right shall be subject to applicable labor and civil service remedies.

SEC. 10. Employer Records and Confidentiality. — Employers shall maintain appropriate
records of child care leave availment and shall ensure the confidentiality of documents and

information submitted by employees, in accordance with applicable data privacy and labor laws.

SEC. 11. Funding. — The implementation of this Act for government employees shall be
charged against the appropriations of the respective government agencies, subject to existing

budgeting, accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.

The payment of child care leave benefits for private-sector employees shall be borne by

the employer, subject to the applicable laws and regulations.

SEC. 12. Enforcement and Penalties. — The Department of Labor and Employment
(DOLE) shall enforce the provisions of this Act with respect to private-sector employees, while
the Civil Service Commission (CSC) shall oversee its implementation with respect to government

employees within its jurisdiction.

An employer who willfully refuses to grant the benefit or violates the non-discrimination
and non-retaliation provisions of this Act shall be subject to the administrative and labor safictions

provided under existing laws, without prejudice to civil or criminal liability when applicable.

SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DOLE, in coordination with the CSC and other appropriate government

agencies, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its effective implementation.

The implementing rules shall prescribe, among others, reasonable documentary
requirements, notice procedures, recordkeeping requirements, and mechanisms for resolving

disputes, without unduly restricting the exercise of the right granted under this Act.

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or

unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, regulations, and
other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or

modified accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in

the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
