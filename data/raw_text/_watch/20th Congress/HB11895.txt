                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Subsistence fisherfolk and farmers are those who utilize nearly all their
catches and harvest to feed their families. They are usually left with little to no
surplus for sale and trade. With hardly enough to make ends meet, subsistence
fisherfolk and farmers find it difficult to come up with capital to even increase
their outputs and generate income.

      Unfortunately, while these two classes of workers are some of the most
hardworking individuals, they remain among the poorest Filipinos. The
Philippine Statistics Authority placed poverty incidence in 2023 at 27.4 percent
among fisherfolk and 27.0 percent among farmers. Most of them have no regular
employer and could never afford regular contributions, so they reach old age with
no pension to lean on. The State can help lighten their predicament by
supporting them in retirement through the establishment of a retirement benefit
system in the interest of promoting social justice.

      Under Article XIII, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution, the legislature has
a duty to give highest priority to the enactment of measures that protect and
enhance the right of all the people to human dignity, reduce social, economic,
and political inequalities, and remove cultural inequities by equitably diffusing
wealth and political power for the common good.

     It is for the foregoing premises that the approval of this Bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
 PROVIDING RETIREMENT, DEATH, AND SURVIVORSHIP BENEFITS FOR
SUBSISTENCE FARMERS AND FISHERFOLK, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                         THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

     SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Subsistence
Farmer and Fisherfolk Pension Act”.

     SECTION 2. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms
and phrases shall mean as follows:

      1)    Farmer refer to a person whose occupation is to till, toil, graze, raise,
            plant, water, fertilize, manage, propagate, burn, recycle, prepare and
            accustom the land, pond or any territory for purposes of growing
            plants and animals, harvesting thereof, and earning from the said
            aforementioned acts or in relation thereof, and such earnings
            thereof is capable of pecuniary estimation. This encompasses fish
            farming (pond farming), ranch, growing of livestock for human
            consumption, hydroponic planting, among others, and excludes
            virtual and intangible accumulation of assets, and those matters in
            relation to shares of stocks or shares and other financial
            instruments whether or not trading to a registered exchange.
      2)    Fisherfolk refer a person who is directly and personally engaged in
            catching, gathering, culturing, or processing fish and other aquatic
            resources for a living.
      3)    Subsistence farmers refer to farmers that utilize nearly all of the
            crops or livestock raised to maintain the farmer and the farmer’s
            family, leaving little, if any, surplus for sale or trade.
      4)    Subsistence fisherfolk refer to fisherfolk that utilize nearly all catches
            of fish, sea products and related goods to maintain the fisherfolk
            and the fisherfolk’s family, leaving little, if any, surplus for sale or
            trade.

       SECTION 3. Coverage and Conditions. – This Act shall apply only to the
following subsistence farmers and fisherfolk with the following qualifications:

      a)    At least sixty (60) years old;
      b)    Duly registered with the Department of Agriculture (DA), through
            the Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture, the National
            Program for Municipal Fisherfolk Registration, or any successor
            registry, as a subsistence farmer or subsistence fisherfolk;
      c)    Farmers who own no farmlands, or who own or till an area not larger
            than what the DA may determine to be for subsistence;
      d)    Fisherfolk who own no more than one (1) banca of three (3) gross
            tons or less;
      e)    Practices no other profession;
      f)    Not gainfully employed with any employer;
      g)    Not an awardee of any agrarian reform lands;
      h)    Not a recipient of any pension from the Social Security System, the
            Government Service Insurance System, or any other contributory
            pension system at the time of claiming of the retirement, death or
            survivorship benefits;
      i)    Must submit at least three (3) sworn statements or affidavits of
            neighbors within the same barangay, or a certification from the
            barangay, the municipal or city agriculture office, or the Fisheries
            and Aquatic Resources Management Council, stating that the
            applicant for the retirement, death, or survivorship benefits is in fact
            a bona fide subsistence farmer or subsistence fisherfolk;
      j)    Must have been engaged in farming or fishing for at least fifteen (15)
            years, as shown by registry records or other competent proof,
            provided that owners or operators of farm lots who are not actually
            involved in the land preparation, planting, tilling or harvesting shall
            not be eligible; and
      k)    Not convicted by final judgment of terrorism or rebellion.

       SECTION 4. Year-Round Application Period. – Subsistence farmers and
subsistence fisherfolk meeting the above-mentioned qualifications shall file their
applications with the DA, which shall identify and set up application desks in
far-flung municipalities, in coordination with local government units, to increase
access. The DA shall also deploy personnel to fishing and farming communities
to ensure the dissemination of information and facilitate the application process.
Applications shall be received all year round, free of charge.
      SECTION 5. Retirement Benefits. – The qualified subsistence farmer or
subsistence fisherfolk shall be entitled to a monthly pension of not less than two
thousand pesos (Php 2,000.00) starting from the time of approval of the
application for retirement. This amount shall be adjusted every five (5) years
pursuant to the recommendation of the Department of Economy, Planning, and
Development (DEPDev) subject to approval by the President.

      SECTION 6. Death Benefits. – A qualified subsistence farmer or
subsistence fisherfolk shall be entitled to not less than five thousand pesos (Php
5,000.00) death benefits to be received by the heirs. This amount shall be
adjusted every five (5) years pursuant to the recommendation of the DEPDev and
must be approved by the President. This death benefit can be availed of by the
heirs of the registered subsistence farmer or subsistence fisherfolk even before
reaching the age of sixty (60) years old.

       SECTION 7. Gender Equality and Non-Discrimination. – This Act applies
equally to women and men who are subsistence farmers or subsistence
fisherfolk. Women who qualify shall be registered in their own right. Married
couples may both register in order to avail of the retirement, death, and
survivorship benefits. No qualified applicant shall be excluded by reason of
membership in an indigenous cultural community or indigenous people.

       SECTION 8. Survivorship Benefits. – The surviving spouse who was
married to the qualified subsistence farmer or subsistence fisherfolk shall be
entitled to fifty percent (50%) of the monthly retirement benefits until the death
thereof, and the children if any shall be entitled to the remaining fifty percent
(50%) to be divided among themselves, if the age is below eighteen (18) years, or
if they are incapable of self-support by reason of disability, otherwise no other
surviving children shall be entitled to the said benefit.

       SECTION 9. Additional Positions and Personnel for the DA. – For purposes
of this Act, the DA shall, in accordance with pertinent civil service rules, create
positions and enlist additional personnel to ensure the proper and appropriate
implementation of the program under this Act.

      SECTION 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the effective
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations
for the DA. Thereafter, such amounts as necessary for its continued
implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

       SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – The DA, in
coordination with the Department of Social Welfare and Development, the
Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources, the National Commission on
Indigenous Peoples, local government units, organizations of farmers and
fisherfolk, and other relevant government agencies, shall issue rules,
regulations, and guidelines necessary to carry out the implementation and
purposes of this Act within six (6) months from the date of effectivity.

       SECTION 12. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.

      SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 14. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
