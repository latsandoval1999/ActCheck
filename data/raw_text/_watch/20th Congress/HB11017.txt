                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is one of the Philippines’ most important natural
assets. Stretching from Cagayan in the north to Quezon in the south, it forms a vast
interconnected landscape of forests, watersheds, rivers, biodiversity areas, and
coastal ecosystems. Its ecological functions extend far beyond the mountain range
itself, providing essential services that sustain communities, agriculture, water
systems, infrastructure, livelihoods, and economic activities in both upland and
downstream areas.

The Sierra Madre likewise serves as a critical natural defense against typhoons,
flooding, landslides, storm surges, drought, and other climate-related hazards. Its
forests, watersheds, wetlands, riparian areas, ecological corridors, and coastal
ecosystems regulate water flows, store and sequester carbon, support biodiversity,
and contribute to the resilience of communities and critical infrastructure. The
protection and restoration of the Sierra Madre are therefore not merely matters of
environmental conservation, but matters of national development, public safety, water
security, climate resilience, and national security.

Despite its importance, the Sierra Madre remains vulnerable to continuing degradation
arising from illegal logging, destructive extraction, incompatible land conversion,
habitat fragmentation, biodiversity loss, and other activities that undermine ecological
integrity. Existing governmental mandates and management arrangements, while
important, operate through different agencies, jurisdictions, plans, and regulatory
systems. Such fragmentation makes it difficult to manage the Sierra Madre as one




                                                                                       1
interconnected ecological system and to consistently account for impacts that cross
administrative boundaries.

This proposed measure seeks to address this institutional and ecological gap by
establishing the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority (SMMRA) as a
government-owned or -controlled corporation with an original charter, attached to the
Office of the President for policy coordination and administrative supervision. The
Authority is envisioned as the lead policy, planning, coordinating, and regulatory body
for range-wide ecological governance, while preserving the lawful mandates of the
Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), local government units,
Protected Area Management Boards, the National Commission on Indigenous
Peoples, and other competent institutions.

At the center of the proposed framework is the Sierra Madre Ecological Integrity
and Sustainable Development Master Plan, a comprehensive, science-based,
geospatially explicit, 25-year plan that will establish ecological limits, management
zones, no-go areas, permitted and prohibited uses, restoration priorities, measurable
outcomes, investment requirements, and implementation responsibilities. The Master
Plan will provide a common framework for harmonizing national, regional, local,
ancestral-domain, and sectoral plans affecting the Sierra Madre.

The measure adopts a landscape-scale and ridge-to-reef approach to
conservation. Rather than treating forests, watersheds, rivers, biodiversity areas, and
coastal ecosystems as isolated units, the proposed framework recognizes their
ecological and hydrological interdependence. It provides for the identification and
protection of ecological corridors, riparian buffers, critical habitats, key biodiversity
areas, and other connectivity systems necessary to maintain biodiversity and
ecosystem resilience.

The bill further advances a Nature-Based Solutions-First policy. Government
interventions addressing flooding, landslides, erosion, watershed degradation, water
insecurity, coastal hazards, and climate risks will be required to consider the feasibility
of nature-based, green, or green-gray solutions before relying solely on conventional
infrastructure. The proposed approach recognizes natural ecosystems as functional
infrastructure capable of providing long-term disaster-risk reduction, water-security,
and climate-adaptation benefits.

Recognizing that conservation cannot be sustained without viable communities and
economic incentives, the proposed measure promotes sustainable livelihoods,
including agroforestry, ecotourism, and sustainable non-timber forest products. It also
provides avenues for Indigenous Peoples, upland communities, and lawful tenure
holders to participate in conservation and restoration activities and benefit from verified
ecological outcomes.

A significant innovation of the measure is the establishment of a Sierra Madre Nature
Credit System. The system is intended to create a transparent and independently
verified mechanism through which measurable protection, restoration, biodiversity,
watershed, water-security, and climate-resilience outcomes may be recognized and
financed. Covered enterprises that materially depend upon Sierra Madre ecosystem
services may be required to satisfy Nature Credit Purchase Obligations, thereby




                                                                                         2
helping align economic activity with the ecological services upon which such activity
depends. The system expressly does not authorize prohibited activities or substitute
for environmental, tenure, protected-area, or Indigenous Peoples safeguards.

To support the long-term implementation of the law, the bill establishes a Sierra Madre
Fund and provides for lawful and transparent financing mechanisms, including
national government appropriations, certain environmental charges, penalties and
restoration recoveries when legally assignable, grants and technical assistance, and
other lawful sources. The Fund is limited to the Authority’s statutory public-good and
market-governance functions and is subject to government accounting, procurement,
auditing, transparency, and disclosure requirements.

The measure also strengthens accountability by establishing ecological standards,
monitoring systems, restoration obligations, environmental liability mechanisms, and
stronger prohibitions against destructive activities. It prohibits, among others,
unauthorized land conversion, destructive extractive activities, introduction of invasive
species, unauthorized commencement of covered works, continuation of activities
despite lawful orders, and the use of false compliance documents.

Equally important, the proposed framework recognizes the constitutional and statutory
rights of Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples and local communities.
Their customary laws, ancestral domains, lawful tenure, participation, benefit-sharing,
and Free and Prior Informed Consent, where applicable, shall be respected and
protected. The measure likewise seeks to ensure that conservation does not become
a burden imposed upon communities, but an opportunity for them to become active
partners and beneficiaries in the protection and restoration of the Sierra Madre.

The proposed legislation therefore seeks to move Sierra Madre governance from
fragmented and predominantly reactive management toward an integrated, science-
based, preventive, accountable, and investment-oriented system. It recognizes that
protecting the Sierra Madre is an investment in the country’s water security, food
security, biodiversity, climate resilience, disaster-risk reduction, local economies, and
the safety and well-being of present and future generations.

The Sierra Madre is a national ecological foundation whose protection cannot be
postponed. By establishing a dedicated institution, a unified long-term Master Plan,
stronger ecological safeguards, modern monitoring and enforcement mechanisms,
sustainable livelihood opportunities, and innovative financing for conservation and
resilience, this measure seeks to secure the Sierra Madre not only for its own sake,
but for the millions of Filipinos whose lives and livelihoods depend upon the services
it provides.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

28   Sec. 5. Creation of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority. There is hereby
29   created the Sierra Madre Mountain Range Authority (SMMRA), hereinafter referred
30   to as the Authority, as a government-owned or -controlled corporation with an original
31   charter under Republic Act No. 10149, vested with the corporate and regulatory
32   powers expressly granted by this Act. It shall be attached to the Office of the President
33   for policy coordination and administrative supervision, and shall be subject to the
34   oversight of the Governance Commission for GOCCs and other applicable laws. For
35   policy and technical alignment, the Authority shall closely coordinate with the
36   Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) and other relevant
37   national agencies. The Authority shall be the lead policy, planning, coordinating, and
38   regulatory body, and the range-wide ecological governance and nature-market
39   authority, tasked with managing the Sierra Madre landscape as a whole.
40
41   Its principal mandate is to change the governmental and economic decision systems
42   affecting the mountain range by: (a) establishing and enforcing the Sierra Madre
43   Master Plan and binding ecological limits, and administering the Sierra Madre Master
44   Plan Conformity Certificate process; (b) compiling operational ecosystem, enterprise-

18                                  Sierra Madre Master Plan
19
20   Sec. 23. Formulation and Adoption. Within one (1) year from the effectivity of this
21   Act, the Authority shall formulate, adopt, and commence the implementation of a
22   Sierra Madre Master Plan covering a period of twenty-five (25) years. The Sierra
23   Madre Master Plan shall serve as the comprehensive, science-based, ecosystem-
24   oriented, and climate-resilient blueprint for the conservation, protection, restoration,
25   and sustainable development of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range.
26
27   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall integrate, among others, land use and
28   development planning; biodiversity conservation; watershed and ridge-to-reef
29   ecosystem management; climate change adaptation and mitigation; disaster risk
30   reduction and management; sustainable livelihoods; infrastructure compatibility; and
31   the traditional ecological knowledge, customary governance systems, and sustainable
32   resource management practices of ICCs/IPs, consistent with RA 8371, otherwise
33   known as the "Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997." The Authority shall likewise
34   recognize ICCAs and support indigenous co-management approaches consistent with
35   applicable laws.
36
37   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall harmonize and integrate national, regional, and
38   local development and land use plans affecting the Sierra Madre Mountain Range,
39   including Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs), Regional Development Plans
40   (RDPs), ADSDPPs, protected area management plans, and other relevant sectoral
41   plans, to ensure policy coherence and an integrated ridge-to-reef management
42   approach.
43




                                                                                              36
 1   In formulating the Sierra Madre Master Plan, the Authority shall build upon existing
 2   management plans prepared under the NIPAS, as amended, utilize the best available
 3   scientific information and ecological data, and conduct meaningful consultations with
 4   concerned national government agencies, local government units, FICCs/IPs, local
 5   communities, the academe, civil society organizations, and other stakeholders.
 6
 7   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall expressly identify binding ecological limits, no-go
 8   areas, permissible uses, restoration priorities, and decision rules. Harmonization shall
 9   be governed by ecological integrity, precaution, avoidance before mitigation, polluter
10   pays, beneficiary pays, connectivity, intergenerational equity, the more protective
11   applicable standard, and respect for ancestral domains, customary law, tenurial rights,
12   and FPIC.
13
14   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall be reviewed at least once every three (3) years
15   and may be updated earlier, whenever necessary, to incorporate advances in scientific
16   knowledge, respond to fortuitous events and environmental emergencies, address
17   emerging threats, or reflect significant changes in ecological, climatic, or socio-
18   economic conditions.

20   Sec. 24. Landscape Zoning System. The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall establish
21   a Landscape Zoning System that operationalizes the statutory classes under this Act
22   and shall be spatially organized into the following broad management groupings: (a)
23   Core Zone; (b) Sustainable Use Zone; (c) Buffer Zone; and (d) Special Management
24   Zone. The zoning system shall guide conservation, protection, restoration, sustainable
25   resource use, settlement, development, and disaster-risk management throughout the
26   Sierra Madre Mountain Range.
27
28   The Landscape Zoning System shall use the following statutory classes: (1) Strict
29   Protection and Irreplaceable Ecosystem Zones; (2) Ecological Corridor and
30   Connectivity Zones; (3) Watershed and Catchment Protection Zones; (4) Restoration
31   and Recovery Zones; (5) Sustainable Production and Community Stewardship Zones;
32   and (6) Existing Settlement and Compatible Development Zones. The Sierra Madre
33   Master Plan may create subzones only within these classes and for the purposes
34   stated in this Act. The Master Plan shall likewise identify the corresponding broad
35   management grouping and applicable subzone for each geographically delineated
36   area.
37
38        1. Core Zone. The Core Zone shall comprise areas requiring the highest level of
39           ecological protection and shall include the following statutory classes and
40           corresponding areas: (a) Strict Protection and Irreplaceable Ecosystem
41           Zones; (b) Ecological Corridor and Connectivity Zones; (c) Watershed and
42           Catchment Protection Zones; and (d) Restoration and Recovery Zones. It shall
43           include, where applicable, Protected Areas under the National Integrated
44           Protected Areas System (NIPAS), Local Conservation Areas (LCAs), Other




                                                                                          37
 1          Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (OECMs), Critical Habitats, and
 2          Indigenous Peoples’ and Community Conserved Areas (ICCAs).
 3
 4        2. Sustainable Use Zone. The Sustainable Use Zone shall comprise areas
 5           where environmentally sustainable and regulated economic, livelihood,
 6           community, cultural, educational, and compatible development activities may
 7           be undertaken. It shall include the following statutory classes and subzones:
 8           (a) Sustainable Production and Community Stewardship Zones; (b) Existing
 9           Settlement and Compatible Development Zones; (c) Cultural/Traditional and
10           Controlled/Special Use Zones; and (d) Recreational and Educational Use
11           Zones. Activities within these areas shall be subject to the ecological limits,
12           permitted and prohibited uses, and development standards established by the
13           Sierra Madre Master Plan and this Act.
14
15        Within ancestral domains, activities in the Sustainable Use Zone shall be
16          consistent with the applicable ADSDPPs, customary laws, and the rights of
17          ICCs/IPs under Republic Act No. 8371.
18
19        3. Buffer Zone. The Buffer Zone shall provide an additional layer of protection
20           to core conservation areas and other environmentally sensitive areas and
21           shall be managed to minimize adverse impacts from adjacent land uses and
22           enhance ecological connectivity. The Buffer Zone shall include: (a) a forty-
23           meter (40 m) easement on all bodies of water; and (b) a twenty-meter (20 m)
24           setback for areas classified as Sustainable Use Zones, subject to existing laws
25           and applicable rights and standards.
26
27        4. Special Management Zone. The Special Management Zone shall comprise
28           areas requiring specific management interventions due to unique ecological,
29           cultural, socio-economic, or disaster risk considerations. It may include
30           multiple-use areas, ecological restoration sites, hazard-prone areas, cultural
31           landscapes, and other areas requiring special management measures
32           identified through the Sierra Madre Master Plan.
33
34   The Sierra Madre Master Plan shall identify and map the boundaries, applicable
35   statutory class, broad management grouping, subzone, permitted and prohibited uses,
36   management objectives, and applicable development standards for each zone. Zoning
37   shall be based on the latest available geospatial data, sound science, hazard
38   assessments, and relevant Indigenous and local knowledge.
39
40   Any modification of the Core Zone or Special Management Zone shall be undertaken
41   only to improve the management response to the conservation and protection needs
42   of the Sierra Madre Mountain Range and shall be supported by updated geospatial
43   data, sound scientific and technical assessment, and appropriate stakeholder




                                                                                         38
 1   consultation. Any modification shall remain consistent with existing laws and the rights
 2   of ICCs/IPs, LGUs, PAMBs, and other lawful rights holders.
 3
 4   A no-go designation may be made only for primary or old-growth forest, critical habitat,
 5   an irreplaceable ecosystem, a legally protected strict-protection zone, a critical water-
 6   source area, an ecological corridor whose loss would materially sever connectivity, or
 7   an area where credible evidence shows that the proposed class of activity presents a
 8   serious risk of irreversible ecological harm that cannot be avoided through location or
 9   design. Each designation shall identify the legal and scientific basis, map the affected
10   parcels, address existing lawful rights and uses, and follow the notice, consultation,

31   The initial geographic coverage shall be shown in an official georeferenced map
32   attached to this Act as Annex A. It shall include the Sierra Madre Mountain system
33   and the watersheds, ecological corridors, and source ecosystems physically
34   connected to it. A beneficiary city, municipality, or business location outside the

 2   integrated enforcement system is hereby established for the Sierra Madre,

 8   Sec. 29. Structure of SMEES. The SMEES shall consist of:
 9
10        (a) National Coordinating Council. Chaired by DENR, this council provides
11            strategic command. Members include heads of PNP, AFP, National Bureau
12            of Investigation (NBI), Department of Justice (DOJ), and Bureau of Customs
13            (BOC). The council formulates national enforcement strategies, ensures
14            inter-agency coordination, allocates resources, and issues directives for
15            operations in the Sierra Madre;
16        (b) Regional Enforcement Task Forces. Established in key Sierra Madre
17            regions, each task force includes DENR field officers, PNP, including
18            maritime and environmental units, specialized AFP units, and LGU
19            representatives. These task forces plan and execute joint enforcement raids,
20            intelligence sharing, and rapid responses to violations within their
21            jurisdictions, guided by the council’s strategies; and
22        (c) Local Rapid Response Units (LRRUs). At the barangay or municipality
23            level, community-based teams are organized composed of forest rangers,
24            barangay officials, and trained representatives of Indigenous Peoples.
25            LRRUs act as frontline defenders: monitoring forests, reporting illegal
26            activities, and conducting initial patrols under the oversight of the regional
27            task forces.

30   Force (AILTF) is created under the SMEES. This unit focuses on timber syndicates
31   and trafficking networks. Key activities include: intelligence surveillance to identify
32   culprits; permanent and mobile checkpoints on logging routes; mobile ground patrols
33   and aerial reconnaissance of forests; coordination with courts for expedited search
34   warrants; and maintaining a shared database of offenders, seized logs, and
35   enforcement outcomes. Successful models like interagency aids have shown that
36   coordinated action is essential to stop chainsaw mills before they move stolen timber

26             Protection, Conservation, and Sustainable Development Programs

28   Sec. 37. Guiding Principles. All programs under this Article shall be guided by
29   constitutional and international principles: intergenerational equity, the precautionary
30   and public trust doctrines, and the polluter-pays principle. The Sierra Madre is
31   declared an inviolable ecological zone; its protection is as much a human rights issue
32   as an environmental one.

34   Sec. 38. Forest Protection and Management. The Authority shall enforce strict forest
35   governance:
36
37      (a) No-logging in Primary Forests. All logging, road-building for extraction, and
38          forest conversion in primary old-growth forests are absolutely prohibited. Only
39          scientifically justified removals like for research or severe pest control may be
40          allowed by special authority. This reflects findings that even small disturbances
41          in intact forest can undermine the Sierra Madre’s role as storm shield.




                                                                                          49
 1     (b) Criminalization of Illegal Logging. Illegal logging of any forest within the
 2         Sierra Madre like harvesting, transport, and possession or trade of timber

 4         six (6) to twelve (12) years; fines from ₱1,000,000 to ₱10,000,000; forfeiture of
 5         equipment and vehicles used; and permanent disqualification from public office
 6         if the offender is an official. These penalties are without prejudice to penalties
 7         under Republic Act No. 9175 or the Illegal Logging Act.
 8

10         Forest Protection Corps, trained and deputized to work with the PNP and AFP
11         in forest patrols and enforcement. This corps will carry out joint operations,
12         establish rapid response teams, and assist in disaster monitoring. Their
13         creation addresses gaps in manpower and ensures forest protection is
14         sustained.
15
16     (d) Technology-Assisted Monitoring. Mandatory use of satellite imagery, drone
17         surveillance, and artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted forest analytics shall be
18         integrated into enforcement. Real-time alerts shall trigger patrols. A public
19         website or dashboard may be established so communities and NGOs can also
20         monitor alerts, similar to citizen science platforms.
21
22     (e) Restoration Obligation. Any individual, company, or government entity found
23         responsible for forest destruction, through illegal logging or conversion, shall
24         be ordered to undertake full ecological restoration of the affected area at their
25         own expense. Restoration standards shall achieve ecological equivalence,
26         specifically similar forest structure and species diversity, and be supervised by
27         the Authority and DENR. Failure to restore shall be an additional offense, liable
28         to fines or injunctions.

32   Sec. 43. Ecosystem Connectivity and Fragmentation. The fragmentation of

37   resilience. The management regime for the Network shall promote the
38   interconnectivity of ecological corridors among protected areas and other key
39   biodiversity areas to ensure the long-term ecological integrity of the Sierra Madre
40   Mountain Range. Coordinated planning, zoning, monitoring, and enforcement shall be
41   undertaken to prevent conservation leakage and to enhance the effectiveness of

21   Sec. 72. Prohibited Acts. In addition to the acts prohibited under existing
22   environmental and natural resources laws, the following acts are likewise prohibited
23   within the Sierra Madre Mountain Range:
24
25       1. Unauthorized Land Conversion. The conversion of forest lands to
26          agricultural or other non-forest uses without the required authority under
27          existing laws and, where applicable, without first securing a Sierra Madre
28          Master Plan Conformity Certificate;
29
30       2. Destructive Extractive Activities. The conduct of mining or commercial or
31          non-commercial quarrying in a manner that causes significant or irreversible
32          environmental damage, including, but not limited to, the destruction of old-
33          growth forests, critical habitats, or watersheds, and the pollution or
34          contamination of rivers and other water bodies;
35
36       3. Introduction of Invasive Species. The deliberate introduction, release, or
37          propagation of non-native or invasive species of flora or fauna that harm native
38          species, biodiversity, or ecosystem integrity;
39




                                                                                         75
 1       4. Unauthorized Commencement of Covered Works. Beginning, financing,
 2          directing, or causing site clearing, tree cutting, grading, excavation, road
 3          opening, land development, construction, installation, expansion, or other
 4          physical works for a covered project before obtaining a valid Sierra Madre
 5          Master Plan Conformity Certificate or other Authority document expressly
 6          required by this Act, together with the ECC, PAMB approval, FPIC, tenure
 7          instrument, LGU permit, or sector authorization otherwise required by law. A
 8          permit issued by one institution shall not excuse the failure to obtain a separate
 9          certification, approval, or consent required by this Act or another law;
10
11       5. Continuation after Notice or Order. Continuing covered works after receipt
12          of a lawful stop-work, cease-and-desist, suspension, or restoration order
13          issued by the Authority, the DENR, a PAMB, an LGU, a court, or another
14          competent body, or removing an official seal or obstruction placed to enforce
15          that order; and
16
17       6. False Compliance Evidence. Knowingly submitting, using, altering, or
18          causing the submission or use of a false Sierra Madre Master Plan Conformity
19          Certificate, Nature Credit Compliance Clearance, registry record, electronic
20          confirmation record, or other Authority document to obtain a permit, financing,
21          procurement award, disbursement, or authority to proceed.
22
23   Emergency works strictly necessary to prevent an imminent threat to life or serious
24   damage to essential public infrastructure shall not constitute an offense if the
25   proponent gives notice to the Authority and the competent permitting agencies within
26   twenty-four (24) hours, limits the work to the emergency, and submits to post-action
27   review, restoration, and all other requirements of law.

31          maintain an accurate account, timely purchase and retire a final assessed
32          requirement, or comply with a procedural or Registry duty, without fraud or
33          material ecological harm, shall be subject only to the administrative sanctions
34          expressly provided in this Section after notice and hearing.

36          alters an Authority certificate, compliance clearance, Registry record,

41          Thousand Pesos (₱500,000.00) nor more than Ten Million Pesos
42          (₱10,000,000.00), or three (3) times the economic benefit obtained, whichever
43          is higher, without prejudice to prosecution under another law carrying a higher
44          penalty.
