                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Cooperatives remain among the country’s most effective people-
centered enterprises—mobilizing savings, expanding access to credit,
stabilizing livelihoods, and enabling ordinary Filipinos to pool resources and
share risks in pursuit of inclusive growth and social justice. As the economy
evolves and business models modernize, there is a continuing need to
strengthen the legal framework that governs cooperatives to ensure they
remain responsive, competitive, and faithful to universally accepted
cooperative principles.


      Despite their proven value, many cooperatives continue to face persistent
challenges such as uneven compliance, governance gaps, limited access to
capacity-building, and operational constraints that weaken sustainability and
member trust. Clearer rules on organization, registration, and internal
accountability can help professionalize cooperative management while
preserving democratic member control and autonomy.


       This bill seeks to provide for a Revised Cooperative Code of the
Philippines that updates key provisions on cooperative formation, governance,
registration, and operations, including mechanisms that support transparency,
auditability, and responsible stewardship of member resources. It likewise



                                                                               1
reinforces the role of the State—through the appropriate agencies—in
providing an enabling environment, technical assistance, and responsive
regulation that strengthens, rather than substitutes, the cooperative sector’s
own initiatives under the principle of subsidiarity.


      Further, by clarifying cooperative types and supporting legitimate
business expansion—while remaining consistent with competition policy—this
measure aims to help cooperatives scale responsibly, innovate, and participate
more meaningfully in value chains, public service delivery, and community
development. Ultimately, a stronger cooperative framework advances shared
prosperity by empowering communities to build enterprises that are both
economically viable and socially anchored.


     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

 7          SEC. 3. General Concepts. –
 8           (a) A cooperative is an autonomous and duly registered association of
 9              persons with a common bond of interest who have voluntarily joined
10              together to achieve their social, economic, and cultural needs of
11              aspirations by making equitable contributions to the capital required,
12              patronizing their products and services, and accepting a fair share of the
13              risks and benefits of the undertaking in accordance with universally
14              accepted cooperative principles;
15          (b) Cooperative principles – Every cooperative shall conduct its affairs in
16              accordance with Filipino culture, good values and experience, and the
17              following universally accepted principles of cooperation:
18                (1) Voluntary and Open Membership – Cooperatives are voluntary
19                    organizations, open to all persons able to use their services and
20                    willing to accept the responsibilities of membership, without
21                    gender, social, racial, cultural, political or religious discrimination.
22                (2) Democratic Member Control – Cooperatives are democratic
23                    organizations that are controlled by their members who actively
24                    participate in setting their policies and making decisions. Men and
25                    women serving as elected representatives, directors or officers are
26                    accountable to the membership. In primary cooperatives, members
27                    have equal voting rights of one member, one vote. Cooperatives at
28                    other levels are organized in the same democratic manner.




                                                                                            2
 1   (3) Member Economic Participation – Members contribute equitable
 2      to, and democratically control, the capital of their cooperative. At
 3      least part of that capital is the common property of the cooperative.
 4      They shall receive limited compensation or limited interest, if any,
 5      on capital subscribed and paid as a condition of membership.
 6      Members allocate surpluses for any or all of the following
 7      purposes: developing the cooperative by setting up reserves, part
 8      of which should at least be indivisible; benefitting members in
 9      proportion to their patronage of the cooperative’s business; and,
10      supporting other activities approved by the membership.
11   (4) Autonomy and Independence – Cooperatives are autonomous, self-
12      help organizations controlled by their members. If they enter into
13      agreements with other organizations, including government, or
14      raise capital from external sources, they shall do so on terms that
15      ensure democratic control of their members and maintain their
16      cooperative autonomy.
17   (5) Education, Training, and Information – Cooperatives shall provide
18      education and training for their members, elected and appointed
19      representatives, managers, and employees, so that they can
20      contribute effectively and efficiently to the development of their
21      cooperatives.
22   (6) Cooperation Among Cooperatives – Cooperatives serve their
23      members most effectively and strengthen the cooperative
24      movement by working together through local, national, regional
25      and international structures.
26   (7) Concern for Community – Cooperatives work for the sustainable
27      development of their communities through policies approved by
28      their members.

26             means towards sustainable socio-economic development;

10          SEC. 6. Cooperatives Not in Restraint of Trade. – No cooperative or
11   method or act thereof which complies with this Code shall be deemed a conspiracy
12   or combination in restraint of trade or an illegal monopoly, or an attempt to lessen
13   competition or fix prices arbitrarily in violation of any laws of the Philippines.
14          This notwithstanding, fair competition between cooperatives is paramount in
15   their collective growth. Cooperatives shall support and foster a level playing field in
16   their industries. Accordingly, Sections 6, 7, 21 and 111 of this Code shall be in
17   consonance          with          Republic        Act         (RA)          No.10667,
18   otherwise known as the “Philippine Competition Act.”

 9                          CHAPTER III. REGISTRATION

10          SEC. 14. Requirements for Registration. – The proposed Articles of
11   Cooperation, By-laws, and such other required documents shall be submitted in
12   accordance with the rules and regulations to be prescribed by the Authority.
13          Except for cooperative unions as described under Section 30 hereof, no
14   cooperative shall be registered unless the Articles of Cooperation is accompanied
15   with the bonds of the accountable officers and a sworn statement of the Treasurer
16   showing that at least twenty-five per centum (25%) of the authorized share capital
17   has been subscribed and at least twenty-five per centum (25%) of the total
18   subscription has been paid.
19          The Authority shall initially fix the minimum paid-up share capital in the
20   Implementing Rules and Regulations of this Code, periodically assess the required
21   paid-up share capital, and may increase or decrease it through subsequent regulation
22   whenever necessary upon consultation with the cooperative sector.
23          The Authority may prescribe such other registration requirements as it may
24   deem necessary.
25          SEC. 15. Registration. – A cooperative formed and organized under this
26   Code acquires juridical personality from the date the Authority issues a Certificate
27   of Registration under its official seal. All applications for registration shall be finally



                                                                                             15
 1   disposed of by the Authority within a period of fifteen (15) days from the filing of
 2   the complete documentary requirements, both in form and in substance, otherwise
 3   the application is deemed approved, unless the cause of the delay is justified as the
 4   case may be: Provided, That in case of a denial of the application for registration,
 5   the applicant may file a Motion for Reconsideration within five (5) days from the
 6   receipt of such denial. The Motion for Reconsideration must be resolved within
 7   fifteen (15) days from the filing thereof. An appeal to the Board of Directors of the
 8   Authority may be filed within fifteen (15) days from receipt of notice of denial of
 9   the Motion for Reconsideration. The decision of the Board of Directors shall be final
10   and executory but may be elevated to the Court of Appeals through a petition for
11   review.
12          The grounds for denial of the registration shall be prescribed in the
13   Implementing Rules and Regulations of this Code.
14          SEC. 16. Certificate of Registration. – A Certificate of Registration issued
15   by the Authority under its official seal shall be conclusive evidence that the
16   cooperative therein mentioned is duly registered unless it is proved that the
17   registration thereof has been cancelled.
18          SEC. 17. Amendment of Articles of Cooperation and By-laws. – Unless
19   otherwise prescribed by this Code and for legitimate purposes, any provision or

22   quorum, without prejudice to the right of the dissenting members to exercise their
23   right to withdraw their membership under Section 37 of this Code.
24          Subject to the guidelines to be issued by the Authority, a cooperative may opt
25   to amend its Articles of Cooperation and By-laws through a referendum by a vote
26   of two-thirds (⅔) of all the members entitled to vote.
27          Both the original and amended Articles of Cooperation By-laws shall contain
28   all the provisions required by law. Amendments shall be indicated by underscoring

20   issuance of its Certificate of Registration.
21          SEC. 20. Division of Cooperatives. – A registered cooperative, through a
22   resolution approved by a vote of two-thirds (⅔) of all the members entitled to vote,
23   present and constituting a quorum, may divide itself into two (2) or more
24   cooperatives. Such division of the cooperative shall be valid: Provided, That the
25   purpose of the division is not to defraud the creditors.
26          The dissenting members shall have the right to exercise their right to
27   withdraw their membership pursuant to Section 37 of this Code.

 1          The Authority shall prescribe the rules and regulations for the division of the
 2   cooperative: Provided, That all the requirements set forth in this Code have been
 3   complied with by the newly-formed cooperatives.

 4                CHAPTER IV. MERGER AND CONSOLIDATION
 5                                 OF COOPERATIVES

 6          SEC. 21. Merger or Consolidation of Cooperatives. –
 7          (a) Two (2) or more cooperatives may merge or consolidate into a single
 8             cooperative.
 9          (b) No merger or consolidation shall be valid unless approved by a two-thirds
10             (⅔) vote of all the members entitled to vote, present and constituting a
11             quorum of each of the constituent cooperatives at separate General
12             Assembly meetings. The dissenting members shall have the right to
13             exercise their right to withdraw their membership pursuant to Section 37
14             of this Code.
15          (c) The Authority shall issue the guidelines governing the procedure of
16             merger or consolidation of cooperatives. The merger or consolidation
17             shall be effective upon the issuance by the Authority of the Certificate of
18             Merger, or in the case of a consolidation of cooperatives, a new Certificate
19             of Registration.
20          SEC. 22. Effects of Merger and Consolidation. – The merger and
21   consolidation of cooperatives shall have the following effects:
22          (a) In case of merger of cooperatives, the constituent cooperatives shall
23             become a single cooperative or, in the case of consolidation of
24             cooperatives, the consolidated cooperative shall be the new cooperative;
25          (b) The single cooperative or the consolidated cooperative shall possess all
26             the assets, rights, privileges, immunities, and franchises and assume all
27             the liabilities and obligations of each of the constituent cooperatives;



                                                                                          18
 1   (c) Any claim, action, or proceeding instituted by or against the constituent
 2      cooperatives may be continued by the single or consolidated cooperative;
 3      and
 4   (d) The merger or consolidation shall not impair the rights of creditors nor
 5      any lien upon the property of the constituent cooperatives.

18              lending services;
19         (iii) Credit and Savings Cooperatives. – Credit and savings
20              cooperatives promote and undertake savings and lending services

22              order to provide financial assistance to their members for
23              productive and provident purposes;
24         (iv) Credit Surety Fund Cooperatives. – Credit surety fund

 6             their members;
 7      (vi) Savings and Deposits Cooperatives. – Savings and deposits
 8             cooperatives are engaged solely in promoting and encouraging
 9             savings among its members; and

11             this cluster in consultation with the cooperative sector.

14      products of its members, industrial production, and non-agricultural

16      goods or its derivative, such as:
17      (i)     Consumers Cooperatives. – Consumer cooperatives are engaged
18               primarily in procuring and distributing commodities to
19               members and non-members;
20      (ii)    Marketing Cooperatives. – Marketing cooperatives are engaged
21               in the supply of production inputs to members and markets

24               operations involving facilities and supplies whose business is

 9              CHAPTER VI. FEDERATION OF COOPERATIVES

10          SEC. 25. Area of Coverage. – A federation of cooperatives may be
11   organized at the municipal or city level, district or provincial level, regional, inter-
12   regional or national levels.

14   federation of cooperatives shall consist of cooperatives with common needs or
15   purposes: Provided, That a member cooperative may freely withdraw membership
16   from the federation.
17         The minimum number of members shall be at least ten (10) primary 9 or
18   secondary cooperatives for a federation of cooperatives.
19          SEC. 27. Authorized, Subscribed, and Paid-Up Capital of a Federation. –
20   The authorized share capital of a cooperative shall be provided for in its Articles of
21   Cooperation where at least twenty-five per centum (25%) of the authorized share
22   capital shall be subscribed to by the members and at least twenty-five per centum
23   (25%) of the subscribed share capital shall be paid up prior to its registration.
24         The minimum paid-up share capital for a federation of cooperatives shall be
25    One million pesos (P1,000,000.00).
26          SEC. 28. Registration of Federation of Cooperatives. – A federation of
27   cooperatives shall be registered with the Authority.

 4                     CHAPTER VII. COOPERATIVE UNIONS

 5         SEC. 30. Organization and Duties. – All registered cooperatives may
 6   organize or join cooperative unions to protect the interest and promote the welfare
 7   of all types of cooperatives: Provided, That a member cooperative may freely
 8   withdraw membership from the union. The cooperative unions shall represent the
 9   constituent cooperatives at the appropriate level-and exercise the following duties
10   and functions:
11         (a) To represent its member organizations;
12         (b) To acquire, analyze, and disseminate economic, statistical, and other
13             information relating to its members and to all types of cooperatives within
14             its area of operation;
15         (c) To sponsor studies in the economic, legal, financial, social and other
16             phases of cooperation, and publish the results thereof;
17         (d) To conduct trainings to member-cooperatives as an accredited training
18             provider;
19         (e) To promote the knowledge of cooperative principles and practices;
20         (f) To develop the cooperative movement;
21         (g) To assist the national and local government units in cooperative
22             development activities within their respective jurisdiction;
23         (h) To assist in the dissemination and monitoring of the Philippine
24             Cooperative Medium Term Plan;
25         (i) To assist the members in the submission of the required reports and other
26             compliances to the Authority and other government or regulatory bodies;




                                                                                       28
 1          (j) To assist the Authority, upon written request, in the performance of its

 3              information, such as research, reports, and financial records;
 4          (k) To advise the appropriate authorities on all questions relating to
 5              cooperatives at the municipal, city, district, provincial or regional levels;
 6          (l) To act as liquidators or rendering assistance in the liquidation of the
 7              remaining assets of dissolved primary cooperative members;
 8          (m) To do and perform such other non-business as may be necessary to attain
 9              the foregoing objectives.
10           SEC. 31. Sources of Funds for Cooperative Unions. – The funds of a
11   cooperative union and the Apex Cooperative shall be sourced from the membership
12   fees, dues and contributions, remitted cooperative education and training fund
13   (CETF) from member-cooperatives, training fees, fundraising activities, donations,
14   grants, or subsidies from local and foreign sources, whether private or public.

15                           CHAPTER VIII. MEMBERSHIP

16          SEC. 32. Membership. – A cooperative shall have regular members or
17   associate members.
18          A regular member is one who has complied with all the membership
19   requirements, including the Pre-Membership Seminar, and entitled to all the rights
20   and privileges of membership. An associate member is one who has no right to vote
21   nor be voted upon and shall be entitled only to such rights and privileges as the
22   bylaws may provide: Provided, That an associate member who meets all the
23   requirements of regular membership, including the Pre-Membership Education
24   Seminar, continues to patronize the cooperative for two (2) years, and signifies the
25   intention to remain a member shall be considered a regular member.
26          SEC. 33. Qualifications of Membership. – Members of registered primary
27   cooperatives must be natural persons, Filipino citizens or former natural-born



                                                                                           29
 1   Filipino citizens, of legal age, with capacity to enter into a contract, within the
 2   common bond of membership and area of operation, and must possess the
 3   qualifications and none of the disqualifications as provided for in the By-laws of the
 4   cooperative; Provided, that in case of former natural-born Filipino citizens, they
 5   must comply with Philippine taxation laws;
 6   Provided, further, that the age requirement shall not apply to laboratory cooperatives
 7   as defined in Section 8. A member must have completed the Pre- Membership
 8   Education Seminar.
 9          SEC. 34. Government Officers and Employees. –
10          (a) All elective officials of the Government shall be ineligible to become
11             officers of cooperatives: Provided, That the disqualification does not
12             extend to a party list representative who represents the cooperative sector:
13             Provided, further, That nothing precludes cooperatives from engaging in
14             activities in support of party lists representing the sector, such as, but not
15             limited to participating in campaigning or donating funds;
16          (b) Employees of the Authority are allowed to become a member of any
17             cooperative. However, they shall be disqualified to be elected or
18             appointed as an officer, except for cooperatives organized among
19             themselves; and
20          (c) Any government employee or official may, in the discharge of the duties
21             as a member in the cooperative, be allowed by the head of office
22             concerned to use official time for attendance at the General Assembly,
23             Board and committee meetings including cooperative seminars,
24             conferences, workshops, technical meetings, and training courses locally
25             or abroad.
26          SEC. 35. Processing of Applications for Membership. – An application for
27   membership shall be acted upon by the Board of Directors within the period fixed
28   in the By-laws but not later than thirty (30) days. Otherwise, the application for




                                                                                          30
 1   membership shall be deemed approved, subject to the condition that the applicant
 2   has submitted the duly accomplished application and has paid the membership fees
 3   to the cooperative.
 4          In case the application for membership is denied by the Board of Directors,
 5   an appeal may be made to the Election Committee, sitting as the appeals and
 6   grievance body, within fifteen (15) days after receipt of the notice of denial and the
 7   decision of the Election Committee shall be final.
 8          If the Election Committee fails to decide within the prescribed period, the
 9   appeal is deemed approved in favor of the applicant.
10          SEC. 36. Liability of Members. – A member shall be liable for the debts of
11   the cooperative to the extent of the member’s subscription to the share capital of the
12   cooperative.
13          SEC. 37. Termination of Membership. –
14          (a) A member of a cooperative may withdraw membership from the
15             cooperative by giving written notice to the Board of Directors which shall
16             be acted upon within thirty (30) days from receipt of said notice.
17          (b) The death or insanity of a member in a primary cooperative, and the
18             insolvency or dissolution, de-listing, or cancellation of the certificate of
19             registration of a member in a secondary cooperative shall be considered
20             valid grounds for termination of membership: Provided, That in the case
21             of death or insanity of an agrarian reform beneficiary-member of a
22             cooperative, the qualified next-of-kin may assume the duties and
23             responsibilities of the original member;
24          (c) Termination of membership in the cooperative shall be initiated through
25             the filing of a complaint before the ethics committee of the cooperative.
26             The committee shall recommend to the Board of Directors the termination
27             of membership for any of the following causes:




                                                                                        31
 1             (1) Failure to patronize any services of the cooperative for an
 2                unreasonable period of time as provided in the By-laws;
 3             (2) Failure to comply with the obligations of membership as provided in
 4                the By-laws or policies of the cooperative;
 5             (3) Violation of the By-laws of the cooperative;
 6             (4) Commission of any act or omission injurious or prejudicial to the
 7                interest or the welfare of the cooperative; and
 8             (5) Others, as may be provided in the By-laws of the cooperative.
 9         Such member shall be informed, in writing, and be given an opportunity to
10   be heard by the grievance committee. The result of the investigation shall be
11   referred to the Board of Directors for its consideration. Based on the
12   recommendation of the grievance committee, the Board may terminate membership
13   in the cooperative by a majority vote of all its members. The decision of the Board
14   shall be in writing, and communicated either personally, or by registered or
15   electronic mail. The decision may be appealed to the General Assembly within
16   thirty (30) days from receipt thereof.
17         The General Assembly shall decide on such appeal within thirty (30) days
18   after receipt thereof. Failure of the General Assembly to decide within the
19   prescribed period shall be deemed in favor of the member. The decision of the
20   General Assembly decision shall be final and executor. Pending the final decision,
21   the membership shall remain in force. This notwithstanding, the member may file
22   a petition for review of the decision of the General Assembly with the Authority
23   within fifteen (15) days from receipt thereof.
24         SEC. 38. Refund of Interests. – All sums computed in accordance with the
25   By-laws due from the cooperative to a former member shall be paid in accordance
26   with this Code and as prescribed in the Implementing Rules and Regulations:
27   Provided, That such refund shall not be made if upon such payment, the value of




                                                                                     32
 1   the assets of the cooperative would be less than the aggregate amount of its debts
 2   and liabilities exclusive of the members’ share capital contribution.
 3          In the computation of the interest due to the former member, the current
 4   balance of such member in the ledger of accounts of the cooperative shall be taken
 5   into account.

 6                        CHAPTER IX. ADMINISTRATION

 7          SEC. 39. Composition of the General Assembly and Representative
 8   Assembly. – The General Assembly shall be the full membership of the cooperative,
 9   exercising all the rights and performing all the obligations as provided in this Code,
10   and in its Articles of Cooperation and By-laws.
11          For cooperatives with numerous and dispersed membership, the General
12   Assembly shall delegate the exercise of its powers to a Representative Assembly,
13   the members of which are elected by each sector, chapter, or district of the
14   cooperative: Provided, That in cases provided under this Code for the call of a
15   regular or special General Assembly, or the approval or confirmation thereof, the
16   Representative Assembly shall also suffice, where applicable. Such delegation shall
17   be in accordance with the rules and regulations of the Authority.
18          SEC. 40. Powers of the General Assembly. – The General Assembly shall be
19   the highest policy-making body of the cooperative and shall exercise such powers
20   as are stated in this Code, and in the Articles of Cooperation and By-laws of the
21   cooperative. The General Assembly, through its members entitled to vote, shall have
22   the following exclusive powers which cannot be delegated:
23          (a) Determine and approve amendments to the Articles of Cooperation and
24             By-laws;
25          (b) Elect, appoint, or remove, the members of the Board of Directors.
26             However, in the case of the electric cooperatives registered under the




                                                                                        33
 1             Authority, the election of the members of the Board shall be in accordance
 2             with its By-laws or election guidelines;
 3         (c) Approve the annual strategic and developmental plans, and the budget of
 4             the cooperative;
 5         (d) Approve or disapprove proposals from the Board of Directors pertaining
 6             to external borrowings, investments, grants, donations, and other financial
 7             assistance; and
 8         (e) Decide membership issues within the cooperative.
 9         Subject to such other provisions of this Code and only for purposes of prompt
10   and intelligent decision-making, the General Assembly may by a three-fourths (3/4)
11   vote of all its members entitled to vote, present and constituting a quorum, delegate
12   some of its powers to the Board of Directors. These powers shall be enumerated
13   under the By-laws of the cooperative.
14         SEC. 41. Meetings. –
15         (a) The General Assembly shall conduct a regular annual meeting on a date
16            fixed in the By-laws, which shall be within ninety (90) days or one
17            hundred eighty (180) days after the close of each calendar year, for
18            primary or secondary cooperatives, respectively: Provided, That notice of
19            regular meetings shall be sent in writing, by posting or publication, or
20            through other electronic means to all members of record.
21         (b) Whenever necessary, a special meeting of the General Assembly may be
22            called at any time by a majority vote of the Board of Directors, or upon
23            petition of at least ten per centum (10%) of the total members who are
24            entitled to vote, or as provided for in the By-laws: Provided, That a notice
25            in writing shall be sent one (1) week prior to the meeting to all members
26            who are entitled to vote.
27                    If the Board fails to call a regular or special meeting within the
28            given period, the Authority, upon petition of ten per centum (10%) of




                                                                                       34
 1             members entitled to vote, or the quorum as stated in their By-laws, and
 2             for good cause shown, shall issue an order to the petitioners directing
 3             them to call a meeting of the General Assembly by giving proper notice
 4             as required in this Code or in the By-laws;
 5         (c) In the case of a newly registered cooperative, a special General Assembly
 6             meeting shall be called within sixty (60) days from such approval of its
 7             registration, for the following purposes:
 8             (1) Presentation of approved Articles of Cooperation and By-laws;
 9             (2) Approval of the annual strategic and development plans, and budget;
10                and
11             (3) Other matters, as may be determined by the cooperative.
12         (d) The Authority shall compel the cooperative to call a General or
13             Representative Assembly for the purpose of reporting to the members the
14             result of any examination or other investigation of the cooperative affairs;
15             and
16         (e) Notice of any meeting may be waived, expressly or impliedly, by any
17             member.
18         SEC. 42. Mode of Conducting Regular or Special Assembly Meetings. –
19   Regular and special assembly meetings may be conducted in any of the following
20   manner:

11   of the cooperative, a quorum shall consist of at least twenty-five per centum (25%)

15   Representative Assembly shall in no case be lower than twenty-five per centum
16   (25%) of the total membership entitled to vote.
17         In the case of federations and unions, the quorum requirement shall be fifty
18   per centum (50%) of the total number of members entitled to vote.
19         SEC. 44. Voting System. – Each member of a primary cooperative shall have
20   only one (1) vote regardless of the share capital of such member. In the case of
21   federations, they may opt to adopt in their By-laws incentive voting or share voting.
22         In the case of incentive voting, the member-cooperatives shall have one (1)
23   basic vote and up to five (5) incentive votes, as provided in the By-laws. In case of
24   share voting, the voting rights of the member-cooperatives shall be proportionate to
25   the number of their paid-up shares, subject to the limitation imposed under Section
26   84 of this Code: Provided, That at least one-third (1/3) of the seats in the Board
27   shall be reserved for small shareholders. The votes cast by the delegates shall be
28   deemed as votes cast by the members thereof.

 6         CHAPTER XI. INSOLVENCY, SUSPENSION, DISSOLUTION,
 7             LIQUIDATION, AND DELISTING OF COOPERATIVES

 8         SEC. 72. Proceeding Upon Insolvency. – In case a cooperative is unable to
 9   fulfill its obligations to creditors due to insolvency, such cooperative may apply for
10   such remedies as it may deem fit under the provisions of R.A. No. 10142, otherwise
11   known as the Financial Rehabilitation and Insolvency Act.
12         Nothing in this Article, however, precludes creditors from seeking protection
13   from said insolvency law.
14         SEC. 73. Effect of Declaration of Insolvency by a Competent Court. – Should
15   the cooperative be declared insolvent by the court, its Certificate of Registration
16   shall be cancelled, and it shall be delisted from the registry of cooperatives of the
17   Authority.
18         SEC. 74. Suspension of Certificate of Registration. – A penalty of
19   suspension, after due process, shall be imposed on a cooperative on any of the
20   following grounds:
21         a. Willful failure to submit annual progress reports with the necessary
22             attachments within the period mandated by this Code: Provided, That in
23             case of newly-registered cooperatives, this provision shall only apply
24             three (3) years after they have registered;
25         b. Willful failure or refusal to comply with an interlocutory order, a final
26             and executory decision, or award from the CDA Board of Directors, or
27             from any of its Regional Offices or adjudication officers, or by a voluntary



                                                                                        57
 1             arbitrator in relation to an investigation, voluntary arbitration, or
 2             adjudication.
 3         SEC. 75. Voluntary Dissolution Where no Creditors are Affected. – If the
 4   dissolution of a cooperative does not prejudice the rights of any creditor having a
 5   claim against it, the dissolution may be effected by a majority vote of the Board of
 6   Directors, and by a resolution duly adopted by the affirmative vote of at least three-
 7   fourths (3/4) of all the members entitled to vote, present and constituting a quorum
 8   in a special General Assembly called by the directors for such purpose: Provided,
 9   That the notice of time, place, and object of the meeting shall be posted for three
10   (3) consecutive weeks in the principal office of said cooperative, in a public place
11   in the municipality, city, or province where the cooperative operates, and in its
12   official website and social media accounts.
13         The notice of such meeting shall be sent to each member of record either by
14   personal delivery, registered mail, or through electronic means at least thirty (30)
15   days prior to said meeting. A copy of the resolution authorizing the dissolution shall
16   be certified to by a majority of the Board of Directors and countersigned by the
17   Cooperative Secretary.
18         Upon compliance with all the requisites for voluntary dissolution, the
19   Authority shall issue the Certificate of Dissolution.
20         SEC. 76. Voluntary Dissolution Where Creditors Are Affected. – In case
21   creditors of the cooperative are affected, the petition for dissolution, signed by a
22   majority of its Board of Directors or the Chief Executive Officer or General
23   Manager, as the case may be, and verified by the Chairperson of the Board or the
24   Cooperative Secretary, shall be filed with the Authority. The petition shall state that
25   the dissolution was affirmed by a vote of at least three-fourths (3/4) of all the
26   members entitled to vote, present and constituting a quorum in a special General
27   Assembly called for the purpose, and shall set forth all the claims and demands
28   against the cooperative.

22           SEC. 82. Capital and Funds. – The capitalization of cooperatives and

24   Implementing Rules and Regulations.
25           SEC. 83. Capital Sources. – Cooperatives registered under this Code may
26   derive their capital from any or all of the following sources:
27        a. Member’s share capital;



                                                                                           61
 1        b. Loans and borrowings;
 2        c. Deposits from members;
 3        d. Revolving capital which consists of the deferred payment of patronage
 4             refunds, or interest on share capital;
 5        e. Joint venture partners;
 6        f.   Subsidies, donations, legacies, grants, aids and such other assistance or
 7             investment from any local or foreign institution whether public or private:
 8             Provided, That capital coming from such subsidies, donations, legacies,
 9             grants, aids, investments, and other assistance shall not be divided into

11             of the donated capital or fund of the cooperative. Upon dissolution or
12             cancellation of the cooperative, such donated capital shall be subject to

14        g. Reserve funds utilized for investment and as capital for the operations of
15             the cooperative as approved by the Board of Directors or General
16             Assembly.
17         SEC. 84. Limitation on Share Capital Subscription. – No member of primary
18   cooperative other than the cooperative itself shall own or hold more than twenty per
19   centum (20%) of the subscribed share capital of the cooperative.
20         Where a member of a cooperative dies, his heir shall be entitled to the shares
21   of the decedent: Provided, That the total share holding of the heir does not exceed
22   twenty per centum (20%) of the share capital of the cooperative: Provided, further,
23   That the heir qualifies and is admitted as a member of the cooperative: Provided,
24   finally, That where the heir fails to qualify as a member or where his total share
25   holding exceeds twenty per centum (20%) of the share capital, the shares in excess
26   will revert to the cooperative upon payment to the heir of the value of such shares.




                                                                                       62
 1         For federations, the share capital holdings of any one member-cooperative
 2   shall not exceed forty per centum (40%) of the subscribed share capital of the
 3   federation.
 4         SEC. 85. Assignment of Share Capital Contribution or Interest. – Subject to
 5   the provisions of this Code and the approval of the majority of the Board of
 6   Directors, no member shall transfer his/her shares or interest in the cooperative or
 7   any part thereof unless:
 8        a. The member has held such share capital contribution or interest for more
 9            than one (1) year;
10        b. The assignment is made to the cooperative or to a member of the
11            cooperative or to a person who falls within the field of the membership of
12            the cooperative; and
13        c. The Board of Directors has approved such assignment.
14         SEC. 86. Capital Build-Up. – The By-laws of every cooperative, except for
15   cooperative unions, shall prescribe a reasonable and realistic member capital or
16   deposits build-up program to allow the continuing growth of the contribution of the
17   members to the cooperative as their economic conditions continue to improve.
18   Upon approval of the membership, the member shall execute a subscription
19   agreement on his equitable contribution to the cooperative. Upon full payment
20   thereof, the member may apply for an additional share capital subscription, and
21   execute a new subscription agreement.
22         SEC. 87. Shares. – The term “share” refers to a unit of capital in a
23   cooperative, except for cooperative unions, the par value of which may be fixed at
24   any figure not less than One hundred pesos (P100.00) but not more than One
25   thousand pesos (P1,000.00). The share capital of the cooperative is the money paid
26   or required to be paid by the members for the operations of the cooperative.

 1         Unless otherwise provided for in its policies, the cooperative shall issue Share

 7         A share can either be a common share issued to a regular member, or a

 9         SEC. 88. Fines. – The policies of a cooperative may prescribe a fine on

14          b. Securities issued or guaranteed by the Government;

18   Board of Directors to raise a revolving capital from the proceeds of products sold

27   independent from the cooperative or any of its subsidiaries, and must be a member

 3         The social performance audit may be conducted by the audit committee, by

 6         SEC. 92. Audit Report. – The complete audit reports shall be submitted to

 9   should it fail to present such reports to the General Assembly.
10         SEC. 93. Non-Liability for Defamations. – The auditor shall not be held liable
11   in any action for defamation for acts done or for any statement made by him/her in
12   good faith in connection with the conduct of audit of the cooperative.
13         SEC. 94. Right to Examine. – All members shall have the right to examine
14   the records of the cooperative under Section 60 of this Code at any time during
15   reasonable hours of the operating days of the cooperative: Provided, That the
16   member submits a letter request three (3) days prior to the date of his demand to
17   examine the records and stating therein the purpose of the request.
18         The Board of Directors shall consider the purpose and reason for the request
19   to examine the records to determine its propriety. The Board of Directors shall have
20   the right to refuse the request after proper consultation with the requesting member,
21   upon the following grounds:
22         a. The member has improperly used any information secured through any
23            prior examination of the records of such cooperative or of any other
24            cooperative; or
25         b. The member is not acting in good faith or for a legitimate purpose in
26            making the demand to examine records.
27         If the request to examine is denied based on the aforementioned grounds, the
28   member shall have the burden of proving to the Board that such request is made in




                                                                                       65
 1   good faith and for a legitimate purpose. If the denial is made in bad faith, the
 2   directors who voted for such refusal shall be penalized under Section 177 of this
 3   Code.
 4           SEC. 95. Safety of Records. – Every cooperative shall, at its principal office,
 5   keep and carefully preserve the records required by this Code to be prepared and
 6   maintained. The By-laws shall also provide the condition and the accountable
 7   persons for the safekeeping of the records in accordance with applicable laws on

 9   destruction, or falsification.

10           CHAPTER XIV. ALLOCATION AND DISTRIBUTION OF NET
11                                         SURPLUS

12           SEC. 96. Net Surplus. – Notwithstanding the provisions of existing laws, the
13   net surplus of cooperatives shall be determined in accordance with its By-laws.
14   Every cooperative shall determine its net surplus at the close of every calendar year.
15           Any provision of law to the contrary notwithstanding, the net surplus shall
16   not be construed as profit but as an excess of payments made by the members for
17   the loans borrowed, or the goods and services availed by them from the cooperative
18   or the difference of the rightful amount due to the members for their products sold
19   or services rendered to the cooperative including other inflows of assets resulting
20   from its other operating activities and which shall be deemed to have been returned
21   to them if the same is distributed as prescribed herein.
22           SEC. 97. Net Surplus Allocation and Statutory Funds. – The net surplus shall
23   be subject to the allocation of the following statutory funds, which shall be recorded
24   in the books of account, for the cooperative’s growth and development:
25           a. The reserve fund which shall be at least ten per centum (10%) of the net
26              surplus for the stability of the cooperative and to cover net losses in its
27              operations, including losses incurred during a pandemic, state of



                                                                                         66
 1     emergency, and other such force majeure, as may be approved or
 2     confirmed by the General Assembly, as the case may be.
 3     The fund shall be recorded in the books of account of the cooperative, and
 4     earmarked as such, and can be made available for investment to enhance
 5     the growth and development of the cooperative; Provided, That the
 6     utilization of the reserve fund for investment shall be at the option of the
 7     General Assembly; Provided, further, that the liquidity of the cooperative
 8     shall be taken into account when utilizing a portion of the reserve fund for
 9     investment. However, for cooperatives in their first five (5) years of
10     operation, the amount allocated shall not be less than thirty per centum
11     (30%) of the net surplus.
12     The general assembly may decrease the amount allocated to the reserve
13     fund when the reserve fund already exceeds the share capital.
14     Upon the dissolution of the cooperative, the reserve fund shall be
15     distributed in accordance with the rules and regulations on liquidation to
16     be issued by the Authority.
17   b. The Cooperative Education and Training Fund (CETF), which shall not
18     exceed ten per centum (10%) of the net surplus, shall be used for the
19     training, development and similar other cooperative activities geared
20     towards the growth of the cooperative movement:
21     1. Half of the CETF shall be used to provide for the education and training
22        of its officers, members, and employees; while the other half shall be
23        remitted to a federation and/or union that the cooperative opted to join,
24        which must redound to the benefit and development of the member-
25        cooperatives, such as, but not limited to trainings, seminars, and other
26        related activities.
27        Should a cooperative have multiple affiliations, it shall have the
28        discretion on how to allocate the CETF.




                                                                                67
 1     2. Upon the dissolution of the cooperative, the unexpended balance of the
 2        CETF shall be utilized within the succeeding calendar year following
 3        its allocation.
 4   c. The Community Development Fund (CDF), which shall not be less than
 5     three per centum (3%) of the net surplus, shall be used for projects or
 6     activities that will benefit the community where the cooperative operates.
 7     However, the cooperative may extend assistance to marginalized
 8     communities or affected areas outside its area of operation in times of
 9     calamities, disaster, or national emergency, at the discretion of the
10     General Assembly.
11     In the case of cooperatives whose common bond of membership is
12     institutional, the community refers to the institution where the cooperative
13     is operating.
14     The allocation for the CDF for the current year shall be fully utilized in
15     the following year unless the projects or activities as approved by a
16     majority of the members entitled to vote, present and constituting a
17     quorum in a General Assembly meeting, require multi-year utilization.
18     For multi-year projects or programs, the allocation shall be fully utilized
19     within the period as stated in the plan.
20   d. The optional fund, which shall not exceed seven per centum (7%) of the
21     net surplus, shall be used for the acquisition of land and construction of
22     building, and any other expenses anent to it, Provided, it is approved by
23     at least three-fourths (¾) of all members entitled to vote, present and
24     constituting a quorum, which shall be in accordance with the

10         SEC. 175. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the
