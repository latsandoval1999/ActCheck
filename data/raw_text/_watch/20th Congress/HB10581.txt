                   REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7305

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The country’s public health workers remain at the forefront of delivering
essential healthcare services, safeguarding public health, and responding to medical
emergencies. Their unwavering dedication, particularly during public health crisis,
underscores the indispensable role they play in ensuring the health and well-being
of every Filipino.

       Republic Act No. 7305, otherwise known as “The Magna Carta for Public
Health Workers” was enacted into law thirty-four (34) years ago to promote and
improve the social and economic well-being of health workers, their living and
working conditions and terms of employment. Hence, the law mandated among
other things the payment of benefits and allowances including night shift differential,
hazard, subsistence, laundry, to name a few. Despite the protections afforded under
the law, many of its statutory benefits and incentives have become inadequate due
to inflation, increasing demands of healthcare service, and the evolving challenges
faced by the country’s healthcare workforce.

     This measure seeks to ensure that the public health workers’ statutory benefits
and incentives are responsive to present-day realities. It aims to improve
compensation package, reinforce incentives for retention and professional

1|Page
development, and provide greater recognition and support to those who dedicate
their lives to public service in the healthcare sector.

      The measure likewise expands the definition of health workers to include
those deployed outside of traditional health facilities, such as those reporting in
health offices, State Universities and Colleges, and Universities, Government-
Owned and - Controlled Corporations, Local Government Units, among others.

       In our pursuit to address the gaps in our public health system and to strengthen
the delivery of healthcare services, it is paramount that we ensure that our health
workers are not only paid a living wage but that the benefits we provide are likewise
sufficient to sustain their living conditions.

      In view of the foregoing, we urge the swift passage of this measure.

SEC. 1. Section 3 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as follows:

        "Section 3. Definition. - For purposes of this Act, "health workers"
        shall mean all persons who are engaged in health and health-related
        work, and all persons employed in all hospitals, sanitaria, treatment
        and rehabilitation centers, health centers, rural health units, barangay
        health stations, clinics, health offices, agencies attached to the
        Department of Health and other health-related establishments owned
        and operated by the Government or its political subdivisions with
        original charters and shall include medical, allied health

       professionals, administrative and support personnel employed
       [regardless of their employment status] in national government
       agencies (NGAs), including state universities and colleges (SUCs),
       government-owned and controlled corporations (GOCCs),
       government financial institutions (GFIs) and local government units
       (LGUs) holding permanent or temporary status, casual or contractual,
       whose salaries are charged against personnel services (PS) lump sum
       appropriation, rendering services on full time or part time basis and
       whose positions are covered, or otherwise by R. A. 6758,
       "compensation and position classification act of 1989", as amended."

SEC. 2. Section 9 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as follows:

       "Section. 9. Discrimination Prohibited. - A Public Health Worker
       (PHW) shall not be discriminated against with regard to political or
       religious affiliations, sex, sexual orientation and gender identity, civil
       status, age, disability, or ethnicity in the exercise of his or her
       profession."

SEC. 3. Section 11 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as follows:

       "Section. 11. Administrative Charges. - Administrative charges
       against a national public health worker shall be heard by a committee
       or officer designated by the disciplining authority, or its delegated
       officer, or officers under its jurisdiction, in accordance with Executive
       Order 292, its implementing rules and regulations, and other pertinent
       civil service laws, rules and regulations.

       For local public health workers, administrative charges shall be heard
       by a committee or officer created or designated by the Provincial or
       City Health Board, in accordance with pertinent rules and regulations.

       The committee or hearing officer shall submit recommendation to the
       appropriate disciplining authority within thirty (30) days from the
       termination of the investigation and hearings.

       Where the provincial health officer or head of health facilities has
       been determined to be an interested party, the members of the
       committee or the designated officer shall be appointed by the
       Secretary of Health."

SEC. 4. Section 18 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as follows:

       "Section 18. Night Shift Differential. - (a) Every public health worker
       shall be paid a night shift differential of twenty percent (20%) of
       his/her regular wage for each hour of work performed during the
       night-shifts customarily adopted by hospitals, sanitaria, treatment and
       rehabilitation centers, birthing clinics and other health facilities
       operating in a 24/7 shift and during calamities, disasters and public
       health emergencies. (b) Every health worker required to work on the
       period covered after his/her regular schedule shall be entitled to
       his/her regular wage plus the regular overtime rate and an additional
       amount of twenty percent (20%) of such overtime rate for each hour
       of work performed between six (6) o'clock in the evening to six (6)
       o'clock in the morning: provided, that the foregoing rules on night
       shift differential shall be subject to the provisions of R.A. 11701 or an
       Act granting night shift differential pay to government employees
       including those in government-owned or -controlled corporations and
       its rules and regulations."

SEC. 5. Section 21 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as follows:

       "Section. 21. Hazard Allowance. - By the nature of their work, which
       subject them to health hazards and occupational risks, all public health
       workers regardless of the degree of their exposure to such hazards and
       risks shall be entitled to a monthly hazard allowance of Twenty Five
       Percent (25%) of their basic monthly salary across the board without
       prejudice to increase of this amount as may be warranted in the future
       by the Secretary of Health."

SEC. 6. Section 22 of Republic Act No. 7305 is hereby amended to read as follows:

       "Section 22. Subsistence Allowance. - All Public Health Workers
       shall be entitled to full subsistence allowance which shall not be less
       than Three Hundred Pesos (PHP 350.00) per day for rendering actual
       services. Provided, that after one (1) year from the effectivity of this
       Act, it shall be increased to four Hundred Pesos (PHP 400.00).
       Provided further, that after two (2) years from the effectivity of this
       Act, it shall be increased to Five Hundred Pesos (PHP 500.00).
       Provided finally, that it shall be reviewed every three (3) years and

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall
remain in force and in effect.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. - All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, and
their implementing rules, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette and in a newspaper of general circulation.
