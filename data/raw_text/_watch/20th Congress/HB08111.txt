PROVIDERS AND DELIVERY PLATFORM PROVIDERS, AND PROVIDING

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

Heavy traffic and weak public transport cost commuters time, money, and quality of
life every single day. Because of this, motorcycles-for-hire booked through mobile
apps have become a popular option, especially for short trips and “last-mile” travel.
They are often faster and more available, helping workers, students, and small
businesses get where they need to go. But without clear rules, these services also raise
serious concerns about road safety, rider training, fair fares, consumer protection,
and who is accountable when something goes wrong.

This bill recognizes that when motorcycles are offered to the public for a fee, whether
through an app or not, the service affects public safety and the public interest. That is
why it must be regulated. The measure makes clear who does what: the LTO handles
vehicle registration and driver licensing to ensure roadworthiness and competent
riders, while the LTFRB regulates motorcycles-for-hire as a transport service,
including permits, operating rules, and fares. Local governments help with planning
and enforcement, but national safety and licensing standards remain uniform across
the country.

The bill allows motorcycles-for-hire to operate legally and responsibly by setting
clear safety standards, requiring proper vehicle classification and professional

licensing, and holding digital platforms accountable for training, monitoring, and
responding to incidents. Its goal is not to replace mass transport or promote risky
driving, but to manage a reality that already exists, so passengers are protected, riders
are safer, and government can enforce the rules fairly and effectively. For these
reasons, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Motorcycles-For-Hire
Regulation Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State recognizes transportation as
essential to economic development and social mobility. Innovations in technology
have enabled motorcycles-for-hire to provide an additional mode of public
conveyance that is responsive to commuter needs. However, the business of holding
out transportation services to the public, including through online platforms, is
imbued with public interest and must be regulated to protect public safety, consumer
welfare, and fair competition.

It is hereby declared the policy of the State to allow the regulated operation of
motorcycles-for-hire, establish clear standards on safety, roadworthiness, rider
competence, platform accountability, fares, and consumer protection, and ensure
effective enforcement through coordinated action among the Department of
Transportation (DOTr), the Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board


                                                                              3 | Page
(LTFRB), the Land Transportation Office (LTO), and local government units (LGUs),
consistent with their respective mandates.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
defined:

   (a) “Digital platform” refers to an information and communications technology-
       enabled mechanism that facilitates the matching of passengers or goods
       consignors with riders and enables booking, dispatch, payment, and feedback
       functions;

   (b) “Motorcycle” refers to any two (2)-wheeled motor vehicle;

   (c) “Motorcycle-For-Hire” refers to a motorcycle duly registered and authorized
       under this Act to transport passengers and/or goods for compensation,
       offered to the public, whether dispatched through a digital platform or
       through authorized non-platform operations as may be allowed under this Act
       and its implementing rules;

   (d) “Motorcycle Taxi Platform Provider (MTPP)” refers to an entity that uses a
       digital platform to arrange passenger transport services by motorcycles-for-
       hire for compensation, and that is accredited by the LTFRB in accordance with
       this Act;

   (e) “Delivery Platform Provider (DPP)” refers to an entity that uses a digital
       platform to arrange the transport or delivery of goods by motorcycles-for-hire
       for compensation, and that is accredited by the LTFRB in accordance with this
       Act. For avoidance of doubt, this term includes platforms facilitating delivery
       connected to internet transactions;

   (f) “Multi-homing” refers to the accreditation of an operator and/or rider to
       more than one (1) platform provider, subject to the limitations and safety
       controls prescribed by the LTFRB;

   (g) “Operator” refers to the person in whose name the CPC and the authorized
       motorcycle-for-hire unit are issued;

   (h) “Rider” refers to the person who drives a motorcycle-for-hire and holds the
       appropriate professional driver’s license and endorsements required by the
       LTO and the LTFRB; and

                                                                             4 | Page
   (i) “Unbooked/Off-platform Operation” refers to a motorcycles-for-hire trip
       undertaken without the required booking or dispatch record in an area where
       platform operation is required under LTFRB rules.

SECTION 4. Scope and Application. — This Act shall apply to the franchising,
regulation, registration classification, and operation of motorcycles-for-hire used as
common carriers for passengers and/or goods for compensation, including those
operating through digital platforms.

This Act shall not apply to motorcycles directly owned by business establishments
and used exclusively for in-house delivery in the ordinary course of their business,
not offered to the public for hire, subject to applicable traffic and safety laws.

SECTION 5. Motorcycles-For-Hire; Public Interest; Standard of Diligence. —
The operation of motorcycles-for-hire as a business of transporting passengers or
goods to the public for compensation is imbued with public interest and shall be
subject to regulation by the State.

Platform providers accredited under this Act shall exercise a high standard of
diligence in accrediting, training, monitoring, and disciplining riders and operators
using their platforms, and in implementing safety and consumer protection
measures, as may be prescribed by the LTFRB and other competent agencies.

SECTION 6. Registration, Classification and Roadworthiness. — A motorcycle
intended to be used as a motorcycle-for-hire shall be registered with the LTO under
a classification and annotation prescribed under this Act and its IRR. The LTO shall
ensure roadworthiness as a condition for initial registration and renewal.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to transfer to LGUs the powers of registration
of motor vehicles and issuance of driver’s licenses, which remain with the LTO.

SECTION 7. Standards and Specifications; Allowable Accessories. — The
Department of Transportation (DOTr), through the LTO, in coordination with the DTI,
DENR, LTFRB, PNP-HPG, and other relevant agencies, shall prescribe minimum
standards for motorcycles-for-hire, including safety features, allowable accessories,
load/carrying specifications, and other requirements to protect passengers, riders,
and goods.

No modification shall be made except those specifically allowed under the standards,
including the installation of safety-compliant accessories (e.g., top box, luggage
carrier, bracket, crash guards, step boards/foot rests, speed limiter/monitoring
devices), subject to inspection and approval protocols.

SECTION 8. Authority to Grant CPC; Franchising and Regulation. — The authority
to grant a franchise or Certificate of Public Convenience (CPC) to motorcycles-for-hire
is vested in the LTFRB, consistent with applicable laws.

   (a) Platform-based Operation. — Where an accredited platform provider
       operates, motorcycles-for-hire services arranged through such platform shall
       be subject to LTFRB-issued CPCs, accreditation requirements, safety rules, and
       service standards.

   (b) Areas Without Accredited Platform Operations. — The LTFRB may authorize
       non-platform motorcycles-for-hire operations in areas without accredited
       platform operations, subject to route/area planning instruments and
       standards, and in consultation with the LGU concerned.

   (c) Goods/Delivery Operations. — Motorcycles-for-hire engaged in delivery or
       carriage of goods arranged by a DPP shall likewise be regulated by the LTFRB
       and must comply with CPC and safety requirements, without prejudice to the
       application of consumer and e-commerce-related laws.

SECTION 9. Requirements for CPC (Operators). — A CPC shall be issued only upon
compliance with requirements prescribed by the LTFRB, which shall include, at a
minimum:

   (a) Proof of Filipino citizenship for natural persons, and compliance with
       constitutional and statutory nationality requirements for juridical entities, as
       applicable;

   (b) Financial capacity , as determined by the LTFRB;

   (c) LTO registration and classification as motorcycle-for-hire;

   (d) Valid insurance coverage, including passenger personal accident and third-
       party liability, in amounts prescribed by the LTFRB; and

   (e) BIR registration/tax compliance documents as may be required.

    The LTFRB may set reasonable caps on the number of units per operator,
    consistent with road safety, demand management, and anti-dummy and
    consumer-protection objectives.

SECTION 10. Rider Qualifications; Licensing; Training. — Only riders holding a
professional driver’s license and such additional competency requirements as may be
prescribed by the LTO for motorcycles-for-hire shall be allowed to operate.

Platform providers shall conduct continuing safety training and periodic competency
validation. For authorized non-platform operations, the DOTr/LTO may require
equivalent training under standards prescribed in the IRR.

SECTION 11. Accreditation of Platform Providers. — Every MTPP and DPP must
be duly registered with the SEC and accredited by the LTFRB prior to operations
covered by this Act.

The DOTr, LTFRB, LTO, DTI, and DICT shall issue harmonized guidelines on
accreditation standards, consumer protection, data governance, complaint-handling,
and safety systems.

Minimum accreditation requirements shall include:

   (a) Financial capacity;
   (b) SEC registration and BIR Registration;
   (c) Verified safety and incident response system, including a Road Crash Quick
       Response System;
   (d) Capacity to train and evaluate riders, directly or through certified partners,
       under standards acceptable to the LTO and TESDA; and
   (e) Capability to maintain auditable trip records and to share required
       operational/safety data with regulators, subject to the Data Privacy Act.

SECTION 12. Operations; Booking Record; Multi-homing. — Motorcycles-for-hire
may operate through accredited platforms and/or through authorized non-platform
arrangements only when expressly allowed by LTFRB rules for the area.

Multi-homing may be allowed up to two (2) platform providers, subject to safety
controls, trip-record integrity, and anti-double booking safeguards prescribed by the
LTFRB. Platform providers shall not unreasonably refuse accreditation solely on the
ground that a rider/operator is accredited with another platform, subject to objective
safety and compliance criteria.

                                                                             7 | Page
Off-platform operations in areas where platform booking is required shall be deemed
unauthorized (colorum), without prejudice to due process.

SECTION 13. Fare Setting and Consumer Disclosure. — The LTFRB shall prescribe
the fare structure, surcharges, and other fees, including parameters for dynamic
pricing, after public consultation. The LTFRB shall also prescribe transparency and
disclosure rules to ensure that end users are informed of fare components prior to
trip confirmation.

Platform providers shall implement a mechanism that allows users to view and
compare fare estimates among available platforms, subject to feasible technical
standards.

SECTION 14. Processing of Personal Data — Any processing of personal and
sensitive personal information in connection with registration, accreditation,
booking, safety monitoring, and complaint handling under this Act shall comply with
the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (R.A. No. 10173) and its implementing rules.

SECTION 15. Liability; Insurance. — The operator and the platform provider at the
time of booking/dispatch shall be jointly and solidarily liable for death, injuries, or
property damage arising from the operation of the motorcycles-for-hire, subject to
rights of subrogation and applicable laws, and subject to a clear allocation of
responsibility in the IRR.

Platform providers shall ensure the existence and continuing validity of insurance
coverage for accredited units/riders, including passenger personal accident and
third-party liability coverages, and shall maintain a claims-assistance mechanism for
victims.

SECTION 16. Road Crash Quick Response System. — Each platform provider shall
establish and maintain a quick response system to address road crashes or
emergencies, including an in-app emergency function and coordination with
emergency services and nearest medical facilities, as may be prescribed by the
DOTr/LTFRB.

SECTION 17. Enforcement; Databases; Coordination with LGUs and MMDA. —
The LTO shall lead in enforcement of licensing and vehicle compliance (registration
classification, roadworthiness, and driver licensing), and may deputize LGUs and


                                                                              8 | Page
other agencies as allowed by law to assist in traffic enforcement and compliance
monitoring.

The LTFRB shall lead in enforcement of CPC/franchise terms and platform
accreditation conditions. Violations of CPC conditions observed by LTO/LGU/MMDA
enforcers shall be referred to the LTFRB for appropriate action.

The LTO shall maintain a centralized database of violations and road crashes
involving motorcycles-for-hire and publish periodic reports, subject to privacy and
security safeguards.

SECTION 18. Penalties; Due Process; Delegated Authority.—

   (a) Traffic, Licensing, and Vehicle Compliance Violations. — Riders and operators
       who violate traffic rules, licensing requirements, and vehicle compliance
       requirements shall be penalized under existing laws, rules, and ordinances,
       without prejudice to administrative sanctions by the LTO, including
       suspension or revocation of licenses, consistent with due process.

   (b) Franchise/Accreditation Violations. — Operators and platform providers who
       violate CPC/franchise conditions, accreditation rules, or safety and consumer
       protection standards under this Act shall be subject to administrative
       sanctions by the LTFRB, including suspension, cancellation, fines, and other
       remedies, after due notice and hearing.

   (c) Standards for IRR and Penalty Tables. — The IRR shall include a graduated
       penalty framework based on severity, frequency, number of affected users,
       and safety impact, to guide agency discretion and ensure penalties are
       reasonable and germane to public welfare.

   (d) Application of Other Consumer and Online Transactions Laws. — The penal
       and administrative provisions of the Consumer Act of the Philippines (R.A. No.
       7394), the Internet Transactions Act of 2023 (R.A. No. 11967), and the Data
       Privacy Act of 2012 (R.A. No. 10173), when applicable, shall apply to acts or
       omissions committed in the course of motorcycles-for-hire operations and
       platform-based transactions.

SECTION 19. Travel Demand and Operational Data. — Platform providers shall
submit to the DOTr annual travel demand and operational datasets necessary for
transportation planning, road safety interventions, and service regulation, in a form

                                                                            9 | Page
and level of aggregation prescribed by the DOTr, consistent with privacy and
cybersecurity safeguards.

SECTION 20. Transitory Provisions. — Existing accreditations, CPCs, and
provisional authorities for motorcycle taxi/delivery platform operations shall remain
effective until their expiry or until six (6) months from the effectivity of the IRR,
whichever is later, unless earlier suspended or cancelled for cause and after due
process.

SECTION 21. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOTr, in coordination with the LTFRB, LTO, DTI,
DICT, DILG, DOH, TESDA, MMDA, PNP-HPG, and other relevant agencies, and after
public consultation, shall promulgate the IRR.

SECTION 22. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 23. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

SECTION 24. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
