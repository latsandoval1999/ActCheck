 AN ACT INSTITUTING COMPREHENSIVE MEASURES TO ADDRESS CHILD
SAFETY AGAINST GUN VIOLENCE, STRENGTHENING SCHOOL SAFETY AND
SECURITY, REGULATING ACCESS AND USE OF FIREARMS, ESTABLISHING
ACCOUNTABILITY MECHANISMS AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR



                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        On 22 June 2026, two students, aged 14 and 15, opened fire inside San Jose
National High School in Tacloban City, Leyte, killing three of their classmates and
wounding several others. A .38 caliber revolver and a 9mm semi-automatic pistol were
used, one of which was registered to a suspect’s aunt, a non-commissioned police
officer. The two student-suspects had reportedly planned the attack in advance,
discussing the assault online before carrying it out.1

       The tragedy of the Tacloban shooting has exposed grave and longstanding
gaps in our legal framework governing school safety, firearms regulation, and the
welfare of minors.2 It was followed within days by two separate knife-related incidents
in schools in Cavite, and by the disruption of a planned attack on another school in
Tolosa, Leyte, all forming a pattern that the Department of Education (DepEd) has
described as a “high-alert situation” nationwide.3

      This Act is our nuanced response to this emerging threat. While laws on gun
ownership and possession exist, they are not tailored to specifically address the reality
of minors gaining access to and learning how to use firearms, especially in and out of
households where adults are licensed to own and operate the same.

        This Bill seeks to address: (1) the unauthorized possession of firearms by
minors, (2) the regulation of minors’ access to firearms training and shooting or firing
ranges; (3) the civil and administrative liability of parents, guardians, firearm
licensees, shooting range and gun club operators, as well as of school officials who
fail to act on credible threats of armed violence; (4) the strengthening of school
security and emergency preparedness, and (5) the reinforcement of anti-bullying
enforcement, guidance counseling, and intervention programs for at-risk youth.

      No single law can guarantee that what happened in Tacloban will never
happen again. But the State owes its children, their parents, and their teachers
nothing less than a society that does everything within its power to prevent it.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
sought.

 6                  ARTICLE II. UNAUTHORIZED POSSESSION OF FIREARMS
 7                   AND REGULATION OF ACCESS TO FIRING RANGES
 8
 9          SEC. 4. Prohibition on Possession of Firearms by Minors. – Without prejudice to
10   the Comprehensive Firearms and Ammunition Regulation Act or Republic Act No.
11   10591, no minor shall acquire, possess, carry, or have custody or control of any
12   firearm, major part thereof, or ammunition at all times. This prohibition shall apply
13   regardless of whether the firearm is registered in the name of a parent, guardian,
14   household member, or any other adult.
15
16          SEC. 5. Penalty for Unauthorized Possession by a Minor. – A minor found to be
17   in unauthorized possession of a firearm in violation of Section 4 shall be dealt with in
18   accordance with the Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006 or Republic Act No. 9344
19   as amended, and shall, regardless of age or exemption from criminal liability, be
20   referred for intervention to the Local Social Welfare and Development Office and,
21   where applicable, to a Bahay Pag-asa, without prejudice to the civil liability provided
22   under Article III of this Act and to whatever criminal liability may separately attach to
23   any adult who furnished, sold, or negligently allowed access to the firearm.
24
25          SEC. 6. Duty to Report. – Any person, including a parent, guardian, teacher,
26   school official, school staff, or fellow student, who has knowledge or reasonable
27   suspicion that a minor possesses, has access to, or has expressed an intent to bring
28   a firearm onto school premises, shall immediately report the same to the school
29   administration, the nearest Philippine National Police (PNP) station, or the barangay.
30   Reports made in good faith under this Section shall be free from any civil or
31   administrative liability.
32
33          SEC. 7. Gun-Free School Zones. – All schools and their immediate premises
34   within 100 meters thereof are hereby declared Gun-Free School Zones. No person,
35   regardless of license, permit, or authority to carry firearms under Republic Act No.
36   10591, shall bring, carry, or possess a firearm within a Gun-Free School Zone, except:
37
38          a) On-duty law enforcement officers and military personnel acting within the
39             scope of their official functions;
40
 1          b) Licensed security personnel assigned to the school pursuant to a duty detail
 2             order, subject to such storage and handling protocols as the school and the
 3             PNP may jointly prescribe; and
 4
 5          c) Such other exceptions as the Department of Education (DepEd), in
 6             coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local Government
 7             (DILG) and the PNP, may provide in the Implementing Rules and
 8             Regulations of this Act.
 9
10         Every school shall conspicuously post signages declaring itself a Gun-Free
11   School Zone at all entrances.
12
13           SEC. 8. Ban in Shooting Ranges and Firearms Training. – No shooting range,
14   firing range, or gun club accredited by the Firearms and Explosives Office (FEO) of the
15   PNP shall allow any person below 21 years old, especially minors, to enter its premises,
16   engage in live-fire shooting, handle a firearm, or otherwise receive hands-on firearms
17   training.
18
19          SEC. 9. Mandatory Safe Storage. – Every licensed firearm holder who resides
20   in the same household as a minor shall store all firearms and ammunition unloaded,
21   separately from ammunition, and inaccessible to the minor, in accordance with the
22   following requirements:
23
24          (a) Storage Standard. – The firearm shall be stored in a locked, tamper-
25   resistant gun safe equipped with a combination lock, biometric lock, or similar
26   mechanism designed to resist forced or unauthorized entry. A trigger lock, cable lock,
27   or ordinary lock box shall not be sufficient compliance under this Section for
28   households where a minor resides.
29
30          (b) Higher-Tier Licenses. – Where the licensee holds a Type 2 license or higher
31   under Republic Act No. 10591, or is licensed to possess more than one firearm, the
32   safe required under paragraph (a) shall, in addition, be anchored or bolted to a wall
33   or floor structure, or otherwise rendered resistant to removal from the premises, in
34   accordance with standards to be set in the Implementing Rules and Regulations of
35   this Act.
36
37           (c) Confidentiality of Access. – The combination, passcode, key, or biometric
38   credential used to access the safe shall be known only to the licensee and other adult,
39   legally authorized users of the firearm residing in the household. The licensee shall
40   not disclose, record, or store the combination, passcode, or key in any manner
41   accessible to a minor, including by writing it in a location visible or known to the minor,
42   or storing it in a device or application accessible to the minor.
 1         (d) Consistency with Republic Act No. 10591. – The requirements of this Section
 2   are additional to, and shall be observed together with, the safe storage requirements
 3   of Republic Act No. 10591 and its Implementing Rules and Regulations.
 4
 5          (e) Verification. – The Firearms and Explosive Office (FEO) shall, as part of its
 6   periodic inspection and license-renewal process under Republic Act No. 10591, verify
 7   compliance with this Section for any licensee who has disclosed, or is known to have,
 8   a minor residing in the same household.

10                  ARTICLE III. LIABILITY OF PARENTS, GUARDIANS, AND
11                     FIREARMS AND SHOOTING RANGE OPERATORS
12
13          SEC. 10. Civil Liability of Parents and Guardians. – Without prejudice to Article
14   2180 of the Civil Code and Article 221 of the Family Code, a parent or legal guardian
15   shall be presumed civilly liable, jointly and severally with the minor, for (a) the latter’s
16   unauthorized possession of the firearm, and (b)the death, injury, or damage caused
17   by the minor's use of the same, where:
18
19      1) The firearm used was owned by, registered to, or in the custody of the parent
20         or guardian, or any other person residing in the same household;
21
22      2) The parent or guardian failed to comply with the mandatory safe storage
23         requirements under Section 9 of this Act; or
24
25      3) The parent or guardian had actual knowledge, or should reasonably have
26         known, that the minor had access to or possession of a firearm, and failed to
27         report the same or take reasonable steps to remove such access.
28
29         This presumption may be overcome only upon proof that the parent or legal
30   guardian exercised extraordinary diligence in the safekeeping of the firearm and in
31   the supervision of the minor. This civil liability shall be independent of, and may
32   proceed regardless of any criminal or intervention proceeding involving the minor
33   under Republic Act No. 9344, as amended.
34
35            SEC. 11. Liability of Firearms Licensees and Other Persons Facilitating Illegal
36   Access. – A licensed firearm holder who negligently allows a minor to gain access to,
37   or who knowingly furnishes, lends, sells, or gives a firearm or ammunition to a minor,
38   or to any other person below twenty-one (21) years of age, shall, in addition to the
39   civil liability under Section 10 of this Act, be subject to suspension or revocation of his
40   or her firearm license and registration by the FEO, without prejudice to whatever
41   criminal liability may attach under Republic Act No. 10591 or other applicable laws.
42
 1          Any person who knowingly facilitates, assists, persuades, or induces a person
 2   below twenty-one (21) years of age to possess, carry, or use a firearm, or to commit
 3   any crime involving a firearm, shall be criminally liable therefor, without prejudice to
 4   prosecution under the Revised Penal Code, Republic Act No. 10591, or other applicable
 5   laws. Where the offender is a parent, legal guardian, or person exercising parental
 6   authority over the person so facilitated, the penalty shall be imposed in its maximum
 7   period.
 8
 9          SEC. 12. Liability of Shooting Range and Gun Club Operators. – A shooting
10   range, firing range, or gun club, and the officers thereof who knowingly and willingly
11   allow      or fail to prevent any of the following, shall be solidarily liable for resulting
12   death, injury, or damage, and shall be subject to suspension, non-renewal, or
13   revocation of accreditation by the FEO:
14
15          a) Allowing a minor to enter into the shooting range or gun club even if the
16             minor does not handle the firearms;
17
18          b) Allowing a minor to engage in live-fire shooting or firearms handling;
19
20          c) Releasing a firearm or ammunition owned or controlled by the range or club
21             into the custody of a minor.
22
23         This Section shall be without prejudice to the liability of a juridical entity under
24   Republic Act No. 10591, where applicable.
25
26          SEC. 13. Administrative Liability of School Officials. – A school head or other
27   school official who, having actual knowledge of a credible threat of armed violence or
28   of a learner's possession of a firearm on campus, fails to act in accordance with the
29   protocols required under Article IV of this Act, shall be subject to administrative liability
30   under the Civil Service rules, or, in the case of private schools, under DepEd rules
31   governing the suspension or revocation of a school's permit to operate, without
32   prejudice to whatever civil or criminal liability may separately attach.

12            SEC. 19. Minimum Physical Security Standards. – The Department of

11       ARTICLE V. ANTI-BULLYING, GUIDANCE COUNSELING, AND INTERVENTION
12                          PROGRAMS FOR AT-RISK YOUTH
13
14          SEC. 21. Strengthened Enforcement of the Anti-Bullying Act. – DepEd shall,
15   within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, review and strengthen the
16   implementation of Republic Act No. 10627 and its Implementing Rules and
17   Regulations, with particular attention to:
18
19          a) Ensuring that every school's Child Protection Committee is functional and
20             meets the frequency of review required by the Implementing Rules and
21             Regulations;
22
23          b) Ensuring that all bullying incidents, whether resolved through the school's
24             own disciplinary process or otherwise, are properly logged, reported to the
25             Schools Division Office, and analyzed for patterns that may signal a risk of
26             escalation to violence;
27
28          c) Ensuring that the victims, alleged perpetrators, and witnesses to bullying
29             incidents are all referred to the school's guidance counselor for appropriate
30             intervention, regardless of the outcome of any disciplinary proceeding.
31
32          SEC. 22. Guidance and Counseling Services. – Every public elementary and
33   secondary school shall be provided with access to a duly licensed guidance counselor
34   or guidance counseling associate, consistent with Republic Act No. 9258 (Guidance
35   and Counseling Act of 2004). DepEd shall prioritize the hiring and deployment of
36   additional guidance counselors to schools presently without one, and shall integrate
37   mental health screening, consistent with Republic Act No. 11036 (Mental Health Act),
38   into the regular services offered by school guidance offices.
39
40          SEC. 23. Intervention Program for Children at Risk. – Each school shall, in
41   coordination with the Local Social Welfare and Development Office, the Local Council
42   for the Protection of Children, and the PNP Women and Children Protection Desk,
 1   establish a referral and intervention program for Children at Risk, providing access to
 2   counseling, peer support, family-based intervention, and, where warranted, referral
 3   to the Local Social Welfare and Development Office for case management consistent
 4   with Republic Act No. 9344, as amended.
 5
 6           SEC. 24. Threat Assessment Teams. – Each school shall organize a multi-
 7   disciplinary Threat Assessment Team, composed of representatives from the school
 8   administration, guidance office, local social welfare office, and the PNP Women and
 9   Children Protection Desk, tasked with evaluating credible reports of a learner's
10   potential for violence and recommending appropriate, child-sensitive intervention,
11   which may range from counseling to referral for more intensive psychosocial or
12   psychiatric support, consistent with the best interest of the child.
13
14          SEC. 25. Digital Safety and Media Literacy. – DepEd, in coordination with the
15   National Council for Children's Television and the Department of Information and
16   Communications Technology (DICT), shall integrate digital citizenship, media literacy,
17   and online safety modules into the basic education curriculum, with particular
18   attention to the risks of exposure to violent online content and its potential influence
19   on youth behavior.
20
21         SEC. 26. Crisis and Trauma Response. – DepEd and the Department of Social
22   Welfare and Development (DSWD) shall jointly maintain a roster of trained crisis
23   counselors and psychosocial first responders who may be rapidly deployed to any
24   school that has experienced an incident of armed violence, to provide debriefing,
25   counseling, and continuing psychosocial support to affected learners, families, and
26   personnel.

12           SEC. 30. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – DepEd, in coordination

14   Implementing Rules and Regulations of this Act within ninety (90) days from its

17          SEC. 31. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of
18   this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act under the budget
19   of the DepEd and other concerned agencies. Such funds shall be sourced, in part,
20   from the Special Education Fund and the LGUs' Disaster Risk Reduction and
21   Management Fund, in accordance with applicable LGU budgeting rules. Thereafter,
22   such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be
23   included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
24
25          SEC. 32. Rule of Construction. – Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
26   amend, repeal, lower, or otherwise diminish the age requirement for the ownership,
27   possession, or licensing of firearms under Republic Act No. 10591, which requires a
28   person to be at least twenty-one (21) years of age. The term “minor” as used in this
29   Act refers exclusively to persons below eighteen (18) years of age for purposes of the
30   protective, custodial, and liability provisions herein, and shall not be construed to imply
31   that persons eighteen (18) years of age and above, but below twenty-one (21) years
32   of age, are authorized to own, possess, or carry a firearm.
33
34            SEC. 33. Separability Clause. – Should any provision of this Act be declared
35   unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain
36   in full force and effect.
37
38          SEC. 34. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, administrative
39   orders, rules and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
40   repealed, amended, or modified accordingly: Provided, That nothing in this Act shall
41   be construed to repeal, amend, or diminish the rights of children in conflict with the
42   law under Republic Act No. 9344, as amended, or the rights of children under Republic
1   Act No. 7610, as amended.
