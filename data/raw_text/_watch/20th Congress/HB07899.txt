                           EXPLANATORY NOTE


       Other than death, there is nothing more inevitable in life than taxes. This is
an old dictum often used or paraphrased in many ways to emphasize the burden
of taxes imposed upon the citizens of a nation. Although taxes, per se, are
necessary to sustain government operations, some taxes are burdensome and
unnecessary. One of these taxes is the estate tax, sometimes called the inheritance
tax.

       The TRAIN law simplified the computation of the estate tax and increased
the deduction to encourage compliance and increase government revenue. Years
have passed, yet compliance and revenue remain low. Unsettled estates remain
high. Many inherited properties in the Philippines are not transferred to legitimate
heirs. Instead of being potential investments, these untransferred/unsettled
properties become “dead capital” because their liquidity and economic use
become limited.

       In reality, the doctrinal justification for imposing an estate tax to reduce
wealth concentration no longer holds. Wealthy families have found ways to avoid
or reduce tax compliance. The burden is borne by the middle class, who cannot
afford legal fees and administrative costs in addition to the estate tax itself.
Oftentimes, they are forced to sell their inheritance because they cannot afford to
pay the tax and other expenses that come with the process. Aside from that, wealth
these days is no longer tied to lands or houses. Wealth comes in many forms, like
bank deposits, shares of stock, artworks, vehicles, investments, jewelry, and other
receivables. These kinds of wealth, including the traditional concept of wealth, are
already being taxed. It is no longer just to tax them again. The government should
adopt a progressive mindset that an efficient and immediate transfer will redound
to increased economic use, activity, and opportunities.
       The repeated clamor for tax amnesty and the recurring enactment of tax
amnesty extension legislation are clear indications that the underlying causes of
the problem remain unresolved. Abolishing the estate tax is the best solution to
solve this problem.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                                AN ACT
        ABOLISHING THE ESTATE TAX, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE THE
       NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED, AND FOR
                            OTHER PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

 1          SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Estate Tax
 2   Abolition Act of 2026 .”

 3          SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to ensure that
 4   any person shall be afforded equal protection under our laws. It shall endeavor to
 5   promote social justice in its pursuit of human and economic development. In
 6   pursuit of these policies, the state shall abolish the estate tax to ensure just,
 7   equitable, and efficient settlement and transfer of estates.

 8          SECTION 3. Abolition of Estate Tax. – The estate tax under the National
 9   Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, is hereby abolished.

10                  a.) Title III, Chapter I, Sections 84, 85, 86, 87, 88, 89, 90, 91, 92, 93,
11                      94, 95, 96, and 97 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997,
12                      as amended, are hereby repealed.
13                  b.) Sections 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, and 27 of Republic Act 10963,
14                      otherwise known as the Tax Reform for Acceleration and
15                      Inclusion (TRAIN), are hereby repealed.
16                  c.) All other references in relation to the estate tax in any law,
17                      decree, regulation or administrative issuance are hereby
18                      repealed.
 1          SECTION 4. Transitory Provision. – All obligations of heirs, executors,
 2   administrators, and transferees in relation to estate tax before the effectivity of
 3   this Act shall remain.

 4           SECTION 5. Implementing Rules and Regulations – The Department of
 5   Finance, in consultation with the Land Registration Authority and other relevant
 6   government agencies, shall formulate, within thirty (30) days from the effectivity
 7   of this Act, the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

 8          SECTION 6. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
 9   invalid or unconstitutional, the provisions not hereby affected shall remain in full
10   force and effect.

11          SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, orders, rules and
12   regulations or parts thereof, inconsistent with or contrary to the provisions of this
13   Act are repealed, amended or modified accordingly.
14
15          SECTION 8. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days from the
16   date of its publication in the Official Gazette, or a website provided for by law.
