                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to institutionalize a comprehensive strategy to reduce
and prevent child stunting by establishing the Anti-Stunting Action Plan
(ASAP) Council. It mandates the formulation of a national action plan and
centralized monitoring system, providing the necessary institutional
backbone to address chronic malnutrition, and safeguard the health of all
Filipino children.

       Child stunting remains a serious public health and development
concern in the Philippines. The Second Congressional Commission on
Education (EDCOM II) reported in 2024 that one in four Filipino children
under five years old are stunted. While prevalence declined from 31.9% in
2012 to 28.8% in 2022, progress has been slow and remains higher than the
global average of 22.3%1. The World Health Organization defines stunting as
impaired growth and development caused by poor nutrition, repeated
infection, and inadequate psychosocial stimulation. However, evidence from
countries such as Thailand and Singapore shows that sustained,
well-coordinated, and adequately financed early childhood nutrition
interventions can significantly reduce stunting rates2.

      This measure creates the ASAP Council as the lead coordinating body
to harmonize policies, align agency programs, and ensure accountability
across sectors. It mandates the formulation of a time-bound national
roadmap, the integration of nutrition-sensitive interventions, including those
under the First 1,000 Days and the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program,
and the establishment of a centralized database system under the Department

                             AN ACT
ESTABLISHING THE ANTI-STUNTING ACTION PLAN COUNCIL IN THE
PHILIPPINES, DEFINING ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS, APPROPRIATING
FUNDS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Anti-Stunting Action
Plan (ASAP) Council Act”.

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right to health of the people and adopt an integrated and
comprehensive approach to health development. The State shall make health a
critical component of sustainable development and fulfillment of human rights. It
shall prioritize the well-being of the children and promote their rights to proper care
and nutrition, in recognition of their vital role in nation-building and human capital
development.

       Towards this end, the State shall formulate, strengthen, and implement a
national framework for policies and programs targeted to reduce and prevent
stunting in the country, prioritizing nutritionally vulnerable groups and
communities in high-poverty areas. It shall ensure the integration and
operationalization of multi-sectoral anti-stunting programs to accelerate health and
nutrition progress through effective coordination mechanisms, intersectoral policy
coherence, high-impact interventions, capacity building, and centralized data and
monitoring systems.

       SEC. 3. Objectives. — The objectives of this Act are as follows:

          a. Institutionalize a strategic national action plan and roadmap in
       stunting reduction and prevention with a view of adopting a long-term vision
       and identifying specific and targeted health and nutrition interventions to
       address stunting in the country;



                                            1
          b.​ Establish a high-level and independent institution that shall have the
       mandate and the authority to integrate health and nutrition programs on
       reducing and preventing stunting; and

           c.​ Improve and strengthen health and nutrition governance using a
       whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach to translate high-level
       vision to multi-sectoral policy framework and operational plans, eliminate
       fragmentation of policies, and ensure accountability and transparency of
       health and nutrition programs addressed to reduce and prevent stunting in
       the country.
​
      SEC. 4. Establishment of the Anti-Stunting Action Plan Council of the Philippines.
— The Anti-Stunting Action Plan Council, hereinafter referred to as the ASAP
Council, is hereby established, to be organized upon the effectivity of this Act. The
ASAP Council shall be the lead agency to oversee the formulation and
implementation of the national stunting reduction and prevention plan.

       The ASAP Council shall be attached to the Office of the President.
​
       SEC. 5. Composition of the ASAP Council. — The ASAP Council shall be
composed of the Secretary of the Department of Health (DOH), as Chairperson; the
Secretary of the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), as
Co-Chairperson; and the following as members:

          a.​ Secretary of the Department of the Interior and Local Government
       (DILG);

          b.​ Secretary of the Department of Agriculture (DA);

          c.​ Secretary of the Department of Education (DepEd);

          d.​ Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology (DOST);

          e.​ Secretary of the Department of Economy, Planning and Development
       (DEPDev);

          f.​ Executive Director of the Early Childhood Care and Development
       (ECCD) Council;

          g.​ Executive Director of the Council for the Welfare of Children (CWC);
       and

           h.​ Two (2) representatives from non-governmental organizations (NGOs)
       engaged in maternal and child care and development and maternal and child
       health and nutrition, with a proven record of involvement in the prevention
       and reduction of stunting and malnutrition. These representatives shall be
       nominated by the government agency representatives of the ASAP Council,
       for appointment by the President for a term of three (3) years.

​      The members of the ASAP Council may designate their permanent
representatives who shall have a rank not lower than an assistant secretary or its
equivalent to meetings, and shall receive emoluments as may be determined by the
ASAP Council in accordance with existing budget and accounting rules and
regulations.

​      SEC. 6. ASAP Secretariat. — A Technical Secretariat shall be established under
the control and supervision of the ASAP Council. It shall be headed by an Executive
Director who shall be nominated by the ASAP Council and appointed by the
President of the Philippines. It shall provide the ASAP Council with technical and
administrative support.
​
       The Executive Director shall have at least ten (10) years of experience and
practice in the field of health and nutrition. He or she shall work full-time and shall
have the rank, privileges, and emoluments of an Executive Director IV (Salary Grade
29).
​
       SEC. 7. Powers and Functions. — The ASAP Council shall have the following
powers and functions:

          a.​ Develop and formulate a comprehensive and strategic national vision,
      action plan, and roadmap for stunting reduction and prevention, including
      but not limited to short-term and long-term goals, timelines, performance
      indicators, and specific and effective implementation strategies across all
      relevant sectors and agencies;

         b.​ Oversee, monitor, and conduct consultations with stakeholders and
      concerned government agencies on health and nutrition policies, programs,
      and interventions for stunting reduction and prevention;

          c.​ Recommend the improvement and harmonization of anti-stunting
      policies across various agencies and sectors, and facilitate the integration of
      multi-sectoral anti-stunting programs to ensure program and policy
      coherence and effective implementation of stunting reduction and prevention
      efforts, such as food security, maternal and child health, water and sanitation,
      and education;

          d.​ Establish and maintain a centralized, interoperable, and
      comprehensive database system on stunting prevalence and its causes and
      interventions. The database shall be accessible to relevant government
      agencies, local government units (LGUs), and other stakeholders to show
      indicators and important data on stunting, track progress, and monitor
      implementation with a view of arriving at and implementing evidence-based
      and targeted solutions;

          e.​ Communicate, whenever it deems necessary, the country’s national
      action plan and roadmap, including policies, programs, and interventions, for



                                           3
      stunting reduction and prevention to both local and international
      communities, including public reporting of the Anti-Stunting Action Plan;

          f.​ Conduct regular monitoring and evaluation of policies, programs, and
      interventions and recommend adjustments based on evidence and emerging
      trends and needs of the nutrition sector;

         g.​ Issue advisory opinions and guidelines on anti-stunting matters and
      submit annual and special progress reports, including plans and targets, and
      policy, financing, and legislative recommendations, to the President of the
      Philippines, the President of the Senate, and the Speaker of the House of
      Representatives;

         h.​ Formulate the necessary rules and guidelines for the effective
      implementation of this Act; and

         i.​ Perform such other duties and functions as may be assigned by the
      President of the Philippines to ensure the effective implementation of
      stunting reduction and prevention policies and programs.
​
       The ASAP Council shall serve as the primary government agency to oversee
the enforcement and administration of anti-stunting policies and programs. All
relevant government agencies and regulators shall comply with its policy directions
and recommendations, subject to prior consultation.
​
       SEC. 8. The Anti-Stunting Action Plan, Roadmap, and Programs. — The
Anti-Stunting Action Plan (ASAP) to be formulated shall establish the country’s
strategic vision for stunting reduction and prevention, and shall include long-term
and medium-term goals, plans, priorities, strategies for coordinated and coherent
implementation, and recommendations on corresponding programs and
interventions promulgated and implemented by relevant government agencies.

       The ASAP shall provide a roadmap for improving health and nutrition
governance through well-defined coordination and alignment of stunting
prevention policies and programs across all sectors and stakeholders. It shall also
identify areas of focus and strategies to strengthen and stimulate the role of
government agencies and LGUs in achieving its vision of reducing and preventing
stunting using a whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach.

       The ASAP shall be formulated by the ASAP Council, in consultation with
other relevant government agencies, LGUs, and other experts and stakeholders,
taking into consideration global best practices and evidence-based solutions. It shall
be completed within six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act.

        Upon completion of the ASAP, all concerned government agencies shall
strictly comply therewith and incorporate its pertinent policies in their respective
programs, activities, and projects.
​

       SEC. 9. Whole-of-Government and Whole-of-Society Approach; Monitoring and
Updating of Anti-Stunting Policies and Recommendations. — For the effective
implementation of this Act, a whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach
shall be adopted. The ASAP Council shall ensure inter-agency coordination and
integration of the policies and programs among relevant national government
agencies and LGUs as provided in their respective charters and mandates.

       For this purpose, the ASAP Council shall continuously monitor and regularly
update, as may be necessary, the following programs and provide recommendations
for their effective implementation:

          a.​ The National Nutrition Council’s (NNC) Feeding Program for the First
      1,000 Days (F1KD);

         b.​ The Department of Health’s (DOH) Disease Prevention and Control
      Bureau (DPCB) Interventions for F1KD;

         c.​ The Department of Social Welfare and Development’s (DSWD)
      Supplemental Feeding and Pantawid Programs;

         d.​ The Department of Education’s (DepEd) School-Based Feeding
      Program; and

         e.​ Such other anti-stunting-related programs, activities, and projects
      implemented by relevant government agencies.
​
       The ASAP Council shall likewise recommend minimum standards, financing
requirements, reporting and accountability structures, and guidelines to govern
programs and policies that are existing and may hereinafter be formulated by the
relevant government agencies and sectors, such as but not limited to:

          a.​ Proactive identification and monitoring of children facing severe
      stunting, extensive information dissemination through educational
      campaigns, establishment of local innovative measures to ensure access to
      nutritious food in the community, and improvement of the overall nutrition
      of pregnant mothers and children zero (0) to five (5) years old;

         b.​ Technical assistance and training for LGUs to promote food
      diversification and development of sustainable local food sources that meet
      family nutrition needs;

          c.​ Allocation of a minimum budget for a comprehensive anti-stunting
      intervention packages;

         d.​ Expansion of the coverage and increase of cash grants under Republic
      Act (RA) No. 11310, otherwise known as the “Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino
      Program (4Ps) Act”, and other similar programs, in line with RA No. 11310
      and other related laws, to address malnutrition and stunting;

          e.​ Mandatory health check-ups for 4Ps child beneficiaries aged zero (0) to
      five (5) years old, every six (6) months, as a conditionality aligned with the
      Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program, to allow for regular monitoring of
      nutritional status and prompt intervention to prevent or address stunting;

         f.​ Access to enhanced livelihood support and other appropriate
      programs offered by government agencies or accredited private institutions;
      and

          g.​ Integrated livelihood and anti-stunting strategies.
​
       SEC. 10. Private Sector Participation. — The private sector shall be highly
encouraged to partner with the government to finance and implement anti-stunting
programs and projects. Government agencies tasked with implementing these
programs shall be authorized to accredit development partners, which may accept
donations, aids, or grants, in cash or in kind, from duly accredited sources, to meet
the demands and uphold the basic rights of the poor to adequate and nutritious food
and the highest attainable standard of health. The acceptance and utilization of such
donations, aid, or grants shall be conducted with full transparency and comply with
applicable government regulations.

        SEC. 11. ASAP Centralized Database System. — The Department of Science and
Technology-Food and Nutrition Research Institute (DOST-FNRI), in coordination
with the Philippine Statistics Authority, is hereby mandated to develop and manage
a nationwide database on stunting in the country, to ensure that an evidence-based,
effective, and efficient formulation of ASAP is achieved. It shall provide a website
portal and digital tools to track and consolidate all relevant statistics and
information on stunting and program implementation of all concerned agencies. It
shall likewise coordinate with other government agencies whose mandates include
the conduct of community mapping programs relevant to health and nutrition, such
as the DILG, DOH, and the DSWD, to support stunting reduction and prevention
interventions.
​
        SEC. 12. Annual Report. — The ASAP Council shall submit annual progress
reports to the President and Congress on or before the last day of September to
provide an assessment of the ASAP Council’s implementation of the national action
plan and provide strategic recommendations to address implementation gaps. The
report shall also be made available to the public through the database system portal
to be created by the DOST-FNRI in accordance with the provisions of this Act.
​
        SEC. 13. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the continuous
implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations
Act (GAA).
​
        The funding for the anti-stunting programs and projects implemented under
this Act shall be sourced from the existing appropriations as authorized under the
GAA of the different departments and agencies implementing these programs.

       SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
approval of this Act, the ASAP Council, in consultation with relevant stakeholders,
shall formulate and promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
implementation: Provided, That, the Council may revise such implementing rules
and regulations as it deems necessary: Provided, however, That such revised
implementing rules and regulations shall only take effect fifteen (15) days following
its publication in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
​
       SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

       SEC. 16. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full
force and effect.

      SEC. 17. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
