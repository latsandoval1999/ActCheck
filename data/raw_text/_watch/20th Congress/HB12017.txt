EXPLANATORY NOTE

Para sa maraming pasyente sa probinsiya, ang pampublikong ospital ay lumilitaw
hindi lamang bilang gusali na may mga silid at kagamitan, kundi bilang lugar kung saan ang
isang pasyente ay umaasang may susunod na hakbang ang kanyang pagpapagamot. Sa harap
ng clinic counter, sa tunog ng numerong tinatawag at sa papel na referral na mahigpit na
hawak ng pasyente o ng kasama niyang naglaan ng pamasahe at araw ng trabaho, ang isang
espesyalistang konsultasyon ay hindi simpleng oras sa kalendaryo. Ito ay pagkakataong
maunawaan ang sakit, makakuha ng tamang direksiyon, at makabalik sa pamilya na may
malinaw na susunod na gagawin. Kapag may nakanselang tipanan ngunit walang mabilis,
patas, at maaasahang paraan upang maialok ito sa pasyenteng naghihintay, ang pagkakataong

iyon ay maaaring lumipas nang walang napakinabangan.

The central problem is not simply that specialist appointments are scarce. It is that
scarcity becomes more severe when the health system cannot promptly recover an
appointment that has been cancelled, declined, or left unclaimed, and cannot safely match
that recovered opportunity with the next appropriate patient in the referral pathway. A vacant
specialist slot is not an abstract administrative inefficiency. It is clinical time that has already
been scheduled, a consultation room already made available, and a chance at timely
assessment that may be lost while another patient remains uncertain about where to go, when
to return, or whether care will still be available. This measure therefore treats appointment
recovery as a patient-access function of the public health system, rather than as a mere

calendar-management exercise.

The constraint is real and national in character. According to the Commonwealth
Fund’s profile of the Philippine health system, as of 2024 the Philippines had 21.4 specialists
for every 100,000 people.' That figure reflects the indispensable service of the specialists who
continue to care for patients under demanding conditions; it also illuminates the cost of
allowing usable consultation time to go unrecovered. In a 2024 study of access to health
facilities, Leyso and Umezaki observed that private hospitals in the Philippines are generally
better equipped than public hospitals, while access to healthcare facilities depends on personal
preferences, geographical location, and financial capacity.2 They likewise describe the
Philippine health system as hierarchical, with varying capacities among facilities. In such a
system, a patient cannot fairly be expected to compete for a newly available consultation
merely because that patient has faster internet, more available data, greater freedom to leave

work, or the means to make another unplanned trip.

May mahalagang pundasyon nang naitayo ang pamahalaan. Ayon sa GMA News,
inilunsad ng Department of Health ang online Patient Appointment System sa
patientappointment.doh.gov.ph, kung saan ang proseso ay kinabibilangan ng pagsang-ayon
sa paggamit ng sistema, pagpili ng ospital at kinakailangang serbisyo, at pagpili ng
appointment option.> Nang ilunsad ito noong Mayo 2025, tatlong ospital ng DOH sa Maynila
ang unang gumamit nito, at maaaring magpa-book sa mismong ospital, sa tulong ng kawani,
ang pasyenteng walang internet.* Mahalaga ang hakbang na ito sapagkat binibigyan nito ang
pasyente ng daan upang simulan ang paghahanap ng serbisyong kailangan. Ang kulang ay
ang tulay mula sa unang pagpapa-appointment tungo sa sandaling may biglang mabakanteng
oras: isang malinaw na paraan upang makita, mabawi, maialok, at maipaabot ang slot sa
pasyenteng nasa wastong referral pathway, kabilang ang mga pasyenteng hindi laging

nakakonekta sa internet o hindi agad nakakagamit ng online platform.

The antagonist addressed by this bill is the missing system, not the patient who missed
an appointment, the family member who called late, the hospital worker at the counter, or the
specialist carrying a full clinic schedule. A late cancellation can leave the same empty slot as
a no-show, yet the Medical Group Management Association (MGMA) advises practices to
track cancellations and no-shows separately, because a cancellation gives notice and a chance
to refill the slot.> That distinction matters. Illness may worsen overnight, transport may fail,
a caregiver may become unavailable, and a referral or diagnostic requirement may not be
completed in time. At the same time, a public hospital must be able to use its finite specialist
capacity responsibly. MGMA also advises that attendance policies be fair, consistently
enforced, and clearly communicated to patients, although that guidance concerns no-show
fees, which this bill does not allow.® The answer is neither blanket punishment nor disorderly
discretion, but transparent, consistently applied rules that distinguish circumstances and

preserve appropriate clinical judgment.

Hindi dapat maging paligsahan sa bilis ng pag-click ang konsultasyon sa espesyalista.
Habang ang isang nabakanteng slot ay hindi naipapaalam, maaaring may pasyenteng
naghihintay sa barangay health center, may anak na nag-aantay ng resulta ng konsultasyon
ng magulang, o may manggagawang kailangang magpasya kung magpapaliban na naman ng
kita para sa isa pang biyahe. Ang oras ang tunay na dahilan ng pagkilos: bawat araw na hindi
nababawi ang isang maaaring magamit na appointment ay araw na hindi naibibigay ang
pagkakataong iyon sa susunod na pasyenteng handa at angkop na maipatingin. Ngunit ang
bilis ay dapat may kasamang pag-iingat. Ang abiso ay dapat maabot sa pamamagitan ng text,
tawag, at assisted offline channels, at ang alok na slot ay dapat may makatwirang panahon at
paraang tumugon upang hindi maisantabi ang mga pasyenteng may limitadong signal, pera

sa pamasahe, o kakayahang bumiyahe agad.

The governing principle of this bill is simple: every recoverable specialist
appointment in a public hospital should be offered promptly, transparently, and equitably to

the next clinically appropriate patient, whether that patient is online or offline. To give effect

to that principle, the measure establishes an equitable specialist appointment recovery,
waitlist, and patient notification system integrated with the Universal Health Care referral
system. It requires participating public hospitals to maintain current visibility over available,
cancelled, and unclaimed specialist slots; to operate transparent waitlists and rebooking
procedures; to notify patients through channels suited to their actual circumstances; and to
record attendance, strict no-shows, late cancellations, offers, responses, and rebookings in a
manner that permits consistent implementation and public accountability. It also keeps
clinical priority and referral appropriateness at the center, so that a recovered slot advances

continuity of care rather than becoming a first-come, first-served digital race.

Kapag gumana ang batas na ito, ang ordinaryong pasyente ay hindi lamang
makakakita ng mensaheng may bakanteng konsultasyon. Mararamdaman niyang may lugar
siya sa sistema: na ang referral niya ay may saysay, na ang paghihintay niya ay nakikita, at
na hindi siya nawala sa pila dahil lamang wala siyang smartphone, load, o kakayahang
sumagot sa isang sandali. Ang health worker naman ay magkakaroon ng malinaw na proseso
sa halip na umasa sa pira-pirasong tawag at listahang mano-mano; ang espesyalista ay
magkakaroon ng mas malaking pagkakataong magamit ang nakalaang oras para sa
pasyenteng nangangailangan; at ang ospital ay magkakaroon ng datos upang ayusin ang
serbisyo nang hindi ipinapasa ang bigat ng kakulangan sa mga taong humihingi ng lunas. The
measure complements ongoing efforts to improve access, navigation, referral coordination,
and digital health delivery by addressing the practical moment when a scarce appointment

unexpectedly becomes available and must be assigned with both speed and fairness.

Kapag may nabakanteng oras para sa espesyalista, ang susunod na pasyenteng may

tamang referral ay dapat makarinig ng pagkakataon, hindi ng katahimikan.

In view of the foregoing, immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT ESTABLISHING AN EQUITABLE SPECIALIST APPOINTMENT
RECOVERY, WAITLIST, AND PATIENT NOTIFICATION SYSTEM IN PUBLIC
HOSPITALS, LINKING THE SAME WITH THE HEALTH CARE PROVIDER
NETWORKS UNDER REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11223, AND APPROPRIATING
FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress

assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Tawag-Konsulta Act".

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — Pursuant to Article H, Section 15 and Article
XII, Section 11 of the Constitution, it is the policy of the State to make the limited
appointment capacity of public-hospital specialists available fairly, promptly, and in a manner
consistent with clinical need and an organized referral pathway. A patient who cannot use a
digital platform, who lives far from a facility, or who faces a documented clinical, caregiving,
work, transportation, or disaster-related barrier shall have a meaningful opportunity to

receive, confirm, cancel, and rebook a specialist appointment through accessible channels.

The State shall recover cancelled and unclaimed specialist consultation slots through
transparent waitlists and timely patient notification, without treating a late cancellation as a
strict no-show. Clinical urgency, continuity of care, the appropriateness of referral, and the

capacity of the receiving facility shall guide appointment allocation.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

(a) "Appointment recovery" refers to the process of identifying a cancelled, released,
or unclaimed specialist appointment slot and offering it to an eligible patient through

the waitlist procedures under this Act;

(b) "Assisted offline channel" refers to an in-person, telephone, community-based, or
other non-internet means by which a patient may register, receive notice, confirm an

appointment, cancel an appointment, or seek rebooking;

(c) "Eligible patient" refers to a patient with an appropriate referral, when a referral is
required under the applicable health-service pathway, or a patient otherwise eligible

for the specialist service under hospital rules;

(d) "Geographically isolated and disadvantaged area" or "GIDA” refers to an area
identified as such by the Department of Health or under applicable government
standards;

(e) "Late cancellation” refers to a patient-initiated cancellation made after the notice
period established by the hospital for the relevant service, but before the scheduled

appointment time;

(f) "Public hospital" refers to a hospital owned, operated, or controlled by the national
government, a local government unit, a state university or college, or another public

instrumentality;

(g) "Specialist appointment" refers to a scheduled outpatient consultation, diagnostic
assessment, follow-up consultation, or procedure-related consultation with a
physician or other health professional providing a specialty service, as determined by

the hospital;

(h) "Strict no-show" refers to a failure to appear for a scheduled appointment without

advance cancellation; and

SECTION 4. Establishment of the Specialist Appointment Recovery and Waitlist
System. — The Department of Health shall establish and administer a Specialist Appointment
Recovery and Waitlist System. The System shall operate in all hospitals of the Department
of Health and in the Philippine General Hospital. Other public hospitals, including those of
local government units and state universities and colleges, may join the System through
written agreements with the Department; together, these are the participating public hospitals
under this Act. The System shall be interoperable, where technically feasible and consistent
with Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012”, with the
Department's existing patient appointment platform and with the health care provider
networks and health information system under Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as

the “Universal Health Care Act.”

Each participating public hospital shall, subject to the availability of appropriations,

personnel, connectivity, and equipment, maintain procedures to:

(a) Record specialist appointment slots that become available through cancellation,

release, or non-confirmation;

(b) Offer available slots to eligible waitlisted patients through the prioritization
standards under this Act;

(c) Permit patient registration, confirmation, cancellation, and rebooking through text
message, voice call, in-person assistance, including at the hospital’s Malasakit Center
under Republic Act No. 11463, or another assisted offline channel, in addition to any

online channel made available by the hospital;

(d) Provide a clear notice of the appointment date, time, location, service,
requirements, and reasonable deadline for confirmation, without disclosing any

diagnosis or other sensitive personal information in a text or voice message;

SECTION 5. Waitlist Prioritization and Equitable Access. —- A participating public
hospital shall offer recovered specialist appointment slots according to the following order of

consideration:

(a) Clinical urgency and the risk of harm from delay, as determined by qualified health

personnel;

(b) Continuity of care, including patients requiring follow-up for an ongoing condition

or a time-sensitive diagnostic or treatment decision;
(c) Appropriateness and completeness of referral, where referral is required;

(d) Patients from GIDAs and patients facing documented or reasonably verified
barriers to specialist access, including distance, disability-related access needs,

transportation disruption, and limited access to digital communication; and

(e) The date and time of waitlist registration, among patients with comparable clinical

need and access circumstances.

A patient shall not lose waitlist standing solely because the patient lacks internet
access, a mobile device, digital literacy, prepaid communication credit, or the ability to
respond immediately through an online channel. Hospitals shall use reasonable attempts

through the patient's chosen contact method and shall provide an assisted offline option.

A hospital may establish service-specific confirmation windows that account for the
clinical urgency of the slot, the preparation required, the travel time reasonably needed by
patients, and the feasibility of notifying the next eligible patient. A slot may be offered to the
next eligible patient only after the applicable confirmation window has expired or the first-

offered patient declines or cannot reasonably be reached using the selected contact method.

SECTION 6. Patient Notice, Cancellation, and Rebooking. — Participating public
hospitals shall communicate attendance, cancellation, waitlist, and rebooking rules in plain
language at the time an appointment is made and through the hospital’s available patient-
contact channels. Such rules shall distinguish strict no-shows from late cancellations and shall

state the process by which a patient may provide an explanation or request review.

A patient who cancels late or fails to attend shall be offered a reasonable opportunity
to explain circumstances affecting attendance. An absence or late cancellation so explained
shall be recorded as excused and shall not count against the patient. A participating public
hospital shall not automatically remove, suspend, or deprioritize a patient from a waitlist or
future specialist appointment solely because of a late cancellation or strict no-show. Any
attendance-related action shall be proportionate, consistently applied to similarly situated
patients, documented, and subject to review, at the patient’s request, by the hospital’s
Malasakit Center or, where none exists, by the office designated by the head of the hospital,
which shall decide within ten (10) working days.

A patient shall not be held responsible for an appointment failure caused by a
hospital's cancellation, inaccurate contact record attributable to the hospital, system outage,
uncommunicated schedule change, unavailable specialist, facility closure, public emergency,
or another circumstance not attributable to the patient. This Act shall not authorize a no-show

fee, financial penalty, or denial of medically necessary care.

SECTION 7. Hospital Accountability, Reporting, and Improvement. —- The
Department of Health shall prescribe a uniform reporting template for participating public
hospitals. The template shall separately report strict no-shows, late cancellations, hospital-
initiated cancellations, recovered slots, successful waitlist offers, and appointments

completed through the System.

Performance assessment shall compare hospitals and specialist services with similarly
situated hospitals and services, taking account of patient volume, service type, staffing,
facility capacity, referral patterns, GIDA service coverage, connectivity, and other material
conditions. Assessment shall recognize both improvement from the hospital's own baseline
and achievement of applicable service standards. A hospital shall not be penalized for a
shortage, cancellation, or service interruption caused by factors outside its reasonable control,
including insufficient specialist deployment, unavailable equipment, funding delay, referral-

system interruption, or a public emergency.

The Department of Health shall publish an annual aggregate report on
implementation, patient access channels, appointment recovery, waitlist operation, and

operational barriers. No report shall disclose personally identifiable patient information.

SECTION 8. Implementation and Phased Rollout. — The Department of Health shall
implement this Act through a phased rollout. The first phase shall prioritize public hospitals
serving GIDAs, public hospitals with limited specialist appointment capacity, and facilities
demonstrating readiness to operate accessible online and assisted offline appointment

channels.

The Department of Health may provide participating public hospitals with technical
assistance, templates, training, connectivity support, and shared tools. Local government units
may participate voluntarily through agreements consistent with this Act, but shall not be
required to provide counterpart funding, personnel, equipment, or systems without their

written concurrence and an identified lawful funding source.

SECTION 9. Non-Diminution of Rights. — Nothing in this Act shall diminish any

protection, benefit, referral arrangement, or remedy available to patients under existing laws.

implementing rules and regulations necessary for its effective implementation, after
consultation with public hospitals, local government units, health workers, patient

representatives, and organizations representing persons with disabilities and communities in
