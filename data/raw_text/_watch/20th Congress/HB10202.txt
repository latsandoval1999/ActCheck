                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Traditional and complementary medicine continues to play an important
role in the healthcare practices of many Filipino communities, particularly in rural
and indigenous areas where culturally rooted healing systems and herbal
remedies remain widely used and trusted. Traditional healers, medicinal plants,
and indigenous wellness practices form part of the country’s cultural heritage and
community-based health traditions.

      This bill seeks to strengthen the promotion, regulation, and integration of
safe and evidence-informed traditional and complementary medicine into the
national healthcare system consistent with Republic Act No. 8423 or the
“Traditional and Alternative Medicine Act of 1997” and the Universal Health
Care Act.

       The measure establishes a national accreditation and registry system for
traditional healers, promotes research and validation of herbal remedies,
strengthens public health safeguards, and supports culturally sensitive and
community-based healthcare approaches. It likewise provides mechanisms for
training, monitoring, patient safety, and coordination between traditional
practitioners and licensed healthcare professionals.
         By promoting responsible and scientifically guided integration of
  traditional and complementary medicine, this measure aims to improve
  healthcare accessibility, preserve indigenous knowledge systems, support
  preventive wellness, and strengthen holistic public health services for Filipino
  communities.

        In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Traditional and
Complementary Medicine Promotion Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
recognize, preserve, and promote safe, evidence-informed, culturally sensitive,
and scientifically validated traditional and complementary healthcare practices as
part of a holistic approach to public health and wellness.


The State shall strengthen the integration of appropriate traditional and
complementary medicine into the national healthcare system consistent with
Republic Act No. 8423 or the “Traditional and Alternative Medicine Act
(TAMA) of 1997,” the Universal Health Care Act, patient safety standards, and
public health regulations.


The State shall likewise protect indigenous knowledge systems, promote
responsible use of medicinal plants and traditional healing practices, and ensure
that all complementary health services are subject to proper standards,
accountability, and ethical safeguards.


SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
hereby defined:
   a) “Traditional and Complementary Medicine (TCM)” refers to culturally
      rooted, indigenous, natural, alternative, complementary, and integrative
      healthcare practices recognized under Republic Act No. 8423 and related
      issuances.
   b) “Traditional   Healers” refers to individuals recognized by their
      communities as practitioners of culturally rooted healing systems,
      including but not limited to hilot, herbolaryo, albularyo, indigenous
      healers, and other traditional wellness practitioners.
   c) “Herbal Remedies”       refers to plant-based medicinal products or
      preparations derived from traditional knowledge, indigenous practices, or
      scientific research and approved or recognized by the Department of
      Health (DOH), Food and Drug Administration (FDA), or PITAHC.
   d) “Primary Health Care Facilities” refer to Barangay Health Stations (BHS),
      Rural Health Units (RHUs), health centers, and other government primary
      care facilities delivering first-contact healthcare services.
   e) “Evidence-Informed Practice” refers to traditional or complementary
      interventions supported by available scientific studies, documented

SEC. 4. National Registry and Accreditation System for Traditional Healers. –
The Department of Health (DOH), through the Philippine Institute of Traditional
and Alternative Health Care (PITAHC), shall establish and maintain a National
Registry and Accreditation System for Traditional Healers.


To qualify for registration, applicants must:
   a) Demonstrate recognized community-based healing practice or indigenous
      knowledge validated through appropriate local or cultural mechanisms;
   b) Complete orientation and training on public health standards, patient
      safety, sanitation, ethics, referrals, and prohibited medical claims;
   c) Comply with minimum standards and guidelines established by PITAHC
      and the DOH;
   d) Observe culturally respectful, ethical, and non-harmful healing practices.
   e) Accreditation shall not authorize traditional healers to perform acts
      reserved by law for licensed medical professionals.
   f) Certificates of accreditation shall be renewable every three (3) years
      subject to continuing education and compliance requirements.


SEC. 5. Integration into Primary Healthcare Services. – Primary healthcare
facilities may, where appropriate and subject to DOH and PITAHC guidelines:
   a) Coordinate with accredited traditional healers for culturally sensitive
      wellness promotion, non-invasive complementary care, and community
      health education;
   b) Provide public education on safe and evidence-informed use of approved
      herbal remedies and complementary health practices;

   c) Incorporate approved herbal products and validated complementary
         interventions into community health programs consistent with existing
         laws and regulations;
   d) Facilitate referrals between traditional healers and licensed healthcare
         professionals when medically necessary.


The DOH and PITAHC shall develop training programs for healthcare workers
and accredited traditional healers on integrative care, referral systems, public
health standards, and patient safety.


SEC. 6. Research, Validation, and Promotion of Herbal Remedies. – The DOH
and PITAHC, in coordination with the FDA, DOST, Commission on Higher
Education (CHED), and research institutions, shall:
   a. Maintain and regularly update a national compendium of scientifically
         validated medicinal plants, herbal remedies, and complementary therapies;
   b. Support research, clinical studies, conservation, and sustainable cultivation
         of indigenous medicinal plants and traditional health knowledge systems;
   c. Promote public awareness campaigns on the proper and safe use of
         approved herbal remedies;
   d. Strengthen quality assurance, labeling standards, and public safety
         monitoring for herbal products;
   e. Facilitate responsible access to approved herbal medicines through
         government health facilities, accredited community pharmacies, and
         lawful distribution channels.


SEC. 7. Protection Against Fraudulent and Harmful Practices. No person
shall:
   a) Falsely represent oneself as an accredited traditional healer;

                                                                                  4
   b) Claim to diagnose, cure, or treat serious medical conditions without lawful
         authority;
   c) Dispense unsafe, adulterated, unregistered, or prohibited herbal products;
   d) Discourage patients from seeking necessary medical treatment for serious
         or life-threatening conditions.


The DOH, FDA, and concerned agencies shall establish mechanisms for public
complaints, monitoring, and enforcement against fraudulent or dangerous
practices.


SEC. 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the implementation of this
Act shall be included in the annual appropriations of the Department of Health
and PITAHC under the General Appropriations Act.


Local government units may provide supplemental funding, logistical support,
training assistance, or facilities for local implementation.


The Program may also receive grants, donations, and research assistance from
lawful domestic or international sources subject to existing laws and regulations.


SEC. 9. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Accountability. – The DOH and PITAHC
shall:
   a) Develop standards and monitoring systems for accreditation, safety
         compliance, and public health integration;
   b) Submit annual reports to Congress on implementation, research progress,
         accredited   practitioners,   public health   outcomes,   and regulatory
         enforcement actions;

SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, PITAHC, and FDA, in consultation with
indigenous cultural communities, accredited traditional healers, medical
associations, academic institutions, and other stakeholders, shall promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations.


SEC. 11. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.


SEC. 13. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
