                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State recognizes that the right of qualified citizens to own and possess
firearms carries with it the corresponding responsibility to exercise utmost care in their
custody, storage, transportation, and use. Responsible firearm ownership is an
essential component of public safety and the protection of life, particularly the safety
and welfare of children.

       Republic Act No. 10591, otherwise known as the “Comprehensive Firearms and
Ammunition Regulation Act,” establishes the regulatory framework governing the
ownership, possession, carrying, manufacture, dealing in, and importation of firearms
and ammunition. While the law requires secure safekeeping and provides grounds for
the suspension or revocation of licenses, including the negligent loss of a firearm, it is
necessary to further specify the affirmative duties of firearm licensees to prevent
unauthorized access.

       Incidents in which minors or other unauthorized persons obtain firearms
underscore the need to strengthen the existing law. The responsibility of a firearm
licensee should not end upon the issuance of a license or registration. It must include
reasonable and enforceable measures to secure firearms and ammunition against
access by persons who are not legally authorized to possess or use them.

       This measure therefore proposes targeted amendments to Republic Act No.
10591. It strengthens the declaration of State policy and adds definitions relevant to
responsible firearm custody; establishes minimum safe-storage requirements, with
enhanced safeguards in households where minors reside; requires separate and
secure storage of ammunition in such households; prohibits leaving firearms
unattended and unsecured in motor vehicles; and requires prompt reporting of lost,
stolen, or missing firearms.
       The measure further provides for administrative consequences and a graduated
criminal offense for negligent or grossly negligent custody that results in unauthorized
access, with appropriate enhanced penalties where a minor obtains access or the
firearm is used to cause death or serious physical injury. Criminal liability shall not
arise solely because a firearm registered to a person was later used in an offense; the
prosecution must prove the elements of the particular offense beyond reasonable
doubt. Civil liability shall likewise be determined under applicable laws and principles
of causation.

       To ensure consistency with the existing statutory framework, the proposed
measure amends specific provisions of Republic Act No. 10591 and inserts new
sections where necessary. It also strengthens safe-storage education, dealer
advisories, institutional custody protocols, voluntary safekeeping, and the use of the
Firearms Information Management System, subject to due process, constitutional
protections, and data privacy laws.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 5   to read as follows:
 6
 7          SEC. 2. Declaration of State Policy. – It is the policy of the State to maintain
 8   peace and order and protect the people against violence. The State recognizes the
 9   right of its qualified citizens to self-defense through, when it is the reasonable means
10   to repel unlawful aggression under the circumstances, the use of firearms. Towards
11   this end, the State shall provide for a comprehensive law regulating the ownership,
12   possession, carrying, manufacture, dealing in and importation of firearms,
13   ammunition, or parts thereof, in order to provide legal support to law enforcement
14   agencies in their campaign against crime and stop the proliferation of illegal firearms
15   or weapons and the illegal manufacture of firearms or weapons, ammunition and parts
16   thereof. The State likewise recognizes that lawful firearm ownership entails a
17   continuing duty of responsible custody and shall promote safe storage, handling and
18   transportation of firearms; prevent unauthorized access, particularly by minors; and
19   protect children and the public from firearm-related violence, consistent with due
20   process and the rights guaranteed by the Constitution.

 1          (hh) Authorized user refers to a person who is legally authorized under this
 2   Act and its implementing rules and regulations to possess, carry, or use a particular
 3   firearm.
 4          (ii) Minor refers to a person below eighteen (18) years of age.
 5          (jj) Unauthorized access refers to the acquisition, possession, handling,
 6   carrying, or use of a firearm by a person who is not legally authorized to possess or
 7   use that firearm.
 8          (kk) Safe-storage device refers to a locked firearm safe, vault, or other
 9   tamper-resistant storage device approved or recognized by the PNP-FEO and designed
10   to prevent unauthorized access.

12   custodian to exercise the degree of care reasonably expected under the
13   circumstances, resulting in unauthorized access to a firearm. (mm) Grossly negligent
14   firearm custody refers to a conscious or reckless disregard of a substantial and
15   unjustifiable risk that a firearm may be obtained or used by an unauthorized person.
16          (nn) Immediate control refers to the actual physical possession or direct
17   control of a firearm by an authorized user who is in a position to prevent its

20          SECTION 3. The same Act is hereby amended by inserting new
21   Sections 4-A, 4-B, 4-C and 4-D immediately after Section 4 thereof, to read
22   as follows:
23          SEC. 4-A. General Duty of Responsible Firearm Custody. – Every licensed citizen
24   and licensed juridical entity shall exercise reasonable care in the custody, storage,
25   transportation, and use of every firearm registered or entrusted to it. No licensee or
26   person entrusted with custody of a firearm shall knowingly or negligently permit an
27   unauthorized person to obtain, possess, carry, or use the firearm. The duty under this
28   Section shall exist independently of whether the firearm is ultimately used in the
29   commission of a separate offense.
30
31           SEC. 4-B. Mandatory Safe Storage. – A firearm that is not under the immediate
32   control of an authorized user shall be unloaded and secured in a locked safe, vault, or
33   other tamper-resistant storage device approved or recognized by the PNP-FEO, and
34   shall be inaccessible to unauthorized persons. In a household where a minor resides
35   or is regularly present, every firearm shall be kept in enhanced safe storage whenever
36   it is not under the immediate control of an authorized user. Keys, combinations,
37   passwords, biometric credentials, and other means of access shall be secured and
38   kept inaccessible to minors. An ordinary drawer, cabinet, unlocked closet, bedside
39   table, bag, purse, or vehicle compartment shall not constitute a safe-storage device
40   for purposes of this Section.
41
42          SEC. 4-C. Separate Storage of Ammunition. – In a household where a minor
43   resides or is regularly present, ammunition shall be stored separately from the firearm
44   in a locked container or compartment inaccessible to minors. The licensee shall take
45   reasonable measures to prevent minors from obtaining access to the ammunition or
46   to the means of opening its storage container.
47
 1          SEC. 4-D. Firearms in Motor Vehicles. – No firearm shall be left unattended and
 2   unsecured inside a motor vehicle. A firearm transported in a motor vehicle shall be
 3   secured in accordance with regulations promulgated by the PNP-FEO. Where
 4   reasonably practicable and consistent with applicable law and permit conditions, the
 5   firearm shall be transported unloaded in a locked container.

10          SECTION 6. The same Act is hereby amended by inserting new
11   Sections 40-A, 40-B, 40-C, 40-D and 40-E immediately after Section 40
12   thereof, to read as follows:
13          SEC. 40-A. Negligent Firearm Custody Resulting in Unauthorized Access. – Any
14   licensee or person lawfully entrusted with custody of a firearm who, through
15   negligence, fails to comply with Sections 4-A, 4-B, 4-C or 4-D, and thereby allows an
16   unauthorized person to obtain access to or possession of the firearm, shall, upon
17   conviction, suffer imprisonment of one (1) month and one (1) day to six (6) months
18   and a fine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos (P50,000.00) but not more than Two
19   hundred fifty thousand pesos (P250,000.00). If the unauthorized person is a minor,
20   the penalty shall be imposed in its maximum period. In determining negligence, the
21   court shall consider the totality of the circumstances, including the manner of storage,
22   whether the firearm was loaded, the accessibility of ammunition and access
23   credentials, whether the licensee knew or should reasonably have known of the risk
24   of access, prior warnings, and other competent evidence.
25
26           SEC. 40-B. Grossly Negligent or Knowing Facilitation of Unauthorized Access. –
27   Any licensee or person lawfully entrusted with custody of a firearm who, through gross
28   negligence or knowingly, permits an unauthorized person to obtain or use the firearm
29   shall, upon conviction, suffer imprisonment of six (6) months and one (1) day to six
30   (6) years and a fine of not less than Two hundred thousand pesos (P200,000.00) but
31   not more than One million pesos (P1,000,000.00). If the unauthorized person is a
32   minor, the maximum period of the penalty shall be imposed. The firearm involved shall
33   be subject to confiscation or forfeiture in accordance with law, and the offender’s
34   license or permit shall be subject to revocation under Section 39.
35
36           SEC. 40-C. Negligent Firearm Custody Resulting in Death or Serious Physical
37   Injuries. – If, by reason of the offense under Section 40-A or 40-B, an unauthorized
38   person obtains a firearm and uses it to cause the death or serious physical injuries of
39   another person, the responsible licensee or custodian shall, without prejudice to
40   prosecution for any other offense under the Revised Penal Code or special laws, suffer:
41   (a) where the conduct constitutes simple negligence, imprisonment of one (1) year
42   and one (1) day to six (6) years and a fine of not less than Five hundred thousand
43   pesos (P500,000.00); or (b) where the conduct constitutes gross negligence or
44   knowing facilitation, imprisonment of three (3) years and one (1) day to twelve (12)
45   years and a fine of not less than One million pesos (P1,000,000.00). If the
46   unauthorized person is a minor, or the offense is committed within school premises or
47   against a learner, teacher, school employee or school official in connection with school
48   activities, the maximum period of the applicable penalty shall be imposed. The
 1   prosecution must establish beyond reasonable doubt the elements of the offense and
 2   the required causal connection between the accused’s conduct and the unauthorized
 3   access. Nothing in this Section shall preclude the application of the rules on complex
 4   crimes, civil liability, or other applicable laws.
 5
 6          SEC. 40-D. No Automatic Criminal Liability; Causation. – No licensee or
 7   custodian shall be criminally liable solely because a firearm registered to or entrusted
 8   to that person was subsequently used by another person to commit an offense.
 9   Criminal liability under Sections 40-A, 40-B or 40-C requires proof beyond reasonable
10   doubt of the specific negligent, grossly negligent, knowing, or willful act or omission
11   constituting the offense. In determining liability, the court shall consider whether the
12   act or omission was a substantial and proximate cause of the unauthorized access,
13   subject to the applicable rules on causation and intervening acts.
14
15          SEC. 40-E. Civil Liability. – A person found civilly liable for negligent or grossly
16   negligent firearm custody shall be liable for damages in accordance with the Civil Code
17   and other applicable laws. Recoverable damages may include medical and
18   hospitalization expenses, rehabilitation expenses, funeral and burial expenses, loss of
19   income or earning capacity, educational expenses arising from disability or death,
20   psychological or psychosocial treatment, and such other damages as may be awarded
21   by the court. Nothing in this Section shall create automatic or strict civil liability solely
22   by reason of firearm registration or ownership. Liability shall be determined in
23   accordance with applicable rules on negligence, causation, and damages.
