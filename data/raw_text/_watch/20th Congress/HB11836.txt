Mobility is not merely about moving vehicles. It is about enabling people
to reach their work, schools, hospitals, markets, and communities safely,
affordably, and efficiently.

For millions of Filipinos, however, daily mobility remains defined by long
travel times, traffic congestion, inadequate public transportation, unsafe or
disconnected sidewalks, limited bicycle facilities, and transport systems that too
often prioritize vehicles over people. The consequences go beyond
inconvenience: congestion consumes productive time, transport costs burden
household incomes, air pollution affects public health, and poor mobility limits
access to economic and social opportunities.

The country therefore needs to move beyond a transportation policy
centered primarily on expanding road capacity and toward a mobility system
that provides practical, safe, and accessible alternatives to private motor
vehicles.

This measure promotes walking, cycling, public transportation, water-
based transportation, bus rapid transit, intermodal connectivity, and other
sustainable modes as integral components of the country’s transportation
system. It provides for safer walkways and bicycle networks, improved public
transportation standards, travel demand management, bicycle parking,
integrated mobility facilities, and national and local sustainable transportation
plans.

The bill recognizes that sustainable transportation cannot be achieved
through isolated projects. Mobility must be planned as a connected system,

coordinated with land use, public services, economic activity, environmental
objectives, and the actual needs of communities. It therefore promotes
maintenance, climate resilience, local government capacity building, and the
responsible use of public and private financing.

Ultimately, this measure seeks to ensure that Filipinos have real, safe,
convenient, affordable, and accessible choices. When public transportation is
reliable, walking is safe, cycling is practical, and different modes are properly
connected, sustainable transportation becomes a viable choice rather than a
sacrifice.

A people-centered mobility system can help reduce congestion, improve
air quality, promote physical activity, ease transportation burdens, expand
access to jobs and essential services, and strengthen community resilience.

Transportation infrastructure should bring people closer to opportunity—
not keep them trapped in traffic. Every Filipino deserves to move safely,
efficiently, affordably, and with dignity.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

AN ACT
PROMOTING SUSTAINABLE AND INCLUSIVE TRANSPORTATION AND
MOBILITY, STRENGTHENING PUBLIC AND ACTIVE TRANSPORTATION
SYSTEMS, AND PROVIDING FOR INTEGRATED AND EFFICIENT MOBILITY
OPTIONS

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Sustainable
Transportation Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policies. - It is the policy of the State to promote the
general welfare and social justice in all phases of national development, to
protect and advance the right of the Filipino people to a balanced and healthful
ecology in accord with the rhythm and harmony of nature, and to conserve and
develop the patrimony of the nation. The State hereby adopts the following
policies relative to the transportation system in our country, whether public or
private:

(a) Promote a shift towards a safe, collective, efficient, non-congestive, non-
pollutive, and healthful locomotion and transportation system that includes non-
motorized transport;

(b) Increase mobility options of the genera! public and promote the use of
alternative modes of transportation such as walking, biking, and the use of
public transportation;

(c) Properly plan and establish facilities and infrastructures that will respond
to this paradigm shift in the transportation system; and

(d) Establish its plans and programs in conformity with the overall national
land use plan as well as the local comprehensive land use plans.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:
(a) Bicycle refers to any human-powered vehicle with two tandem wheels, at

least one of which has a diameter greater than 14 inches and is designed to be
ridden by a person;

===== [page 4] =====
(b) Motor vehicle refers to any self-propelled vehicle or any vehicle powered
by a source other than muscular power. This does not include an electric bicycle;

(c) Public transportation refers to shared passenger transportation services
that are available for use by the general public. These include, but is not limited
to, buses, trams, trains, rapid transit, and ferries;

(d) Walkways refer to a paved path for pedestrians alongside streets, these
may include, but is not limited to, sidewalks, footpaths, pedestrian overpasses,
or underpasses, and other similar facilities that ensure safe and convenient
movement on foot;

(e) Sustainable transport refers to any means of transport that is accessible,
safe, environment-friendly, and affordable. It includes, but is not limited to,
walking, cycling, transit-oriented development, green vehicles, carpooling, and
building or protecting urban transport systems that are fuel-efficient, space-
saving, and promote healthy lifestyles; and

(f) Travel demand management refers to a set of tools to offer people better
travel information and opportunities to help people choose to reduce their need
to travel, especially by car.

SEC. 4. Sustainable Transport Action Plan. - The Department of
Transportation (DOTr), in coordination with the Department of Economy,
Planning, and Development (DEPDev), shall establish a Sustainable Transport
Action Plan which aims to provide a roadmap for national and _ local
transportation system, to be adhered to at all Local Government Units (LGUs)
throughout the country.

The plan shall have a holistic approach, taking into consideration not only the
impact on infrastructures, but also its effect on health, environment, economy,
and livelihood of the people.

It shall also include strategies to improve parking, public transport, cycling,
walking, and transport infrastructure, among others. An action plan shall also be
drafted, with a timeline in achieving the target transition to alternative modes of
transportation, as identified by DOTr and DEPDev.

SEC. 5. Installation of Walkways. - Adequate sidewalk areas must be
maintained on all roads as walkways, to allow safe pedestrian passage, and for
the safe and convenient use of wheelchairs, strollers, and other similar mobility
devices. For primary and secondary roads, there shall be an allotted three (3)
meters for the sidewalks. If the allocation of space for such infrastructure is not
feasible and implementation proves impractical, an elevated footbridge shail be
installed in its place. The concerned local government unit (LGU) shall be
responsible for its construction within six (6) months from the effectivity of this
Act.

SEC. 6. Public Transportation. - The DOTr shail provide guidelines that will
promote a shift towards public transportation as the primary mobility option to
the general public, within twelve (12) months from the effectivity of this Act. The
government shall study and implement best practices in public transportation to

===== [page 5] =====
address the impacts of being a_ petroleum-based economy, including
environmental degradation, financial instability, high energy costs, pollution, and
urban poverty.

SEC. 7. Establishment of a Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) System. - The DOTr
within twelve (12) months from the effectivity of this Act, shall conduct a study
on the creation of a Bus Rapid Transit System which will organize buses into one
efficient long-distance transportation system with coordinated schedules, rates,
routes, and pick-up and drop-off points.

The LGU and the private sector, including but not limited to, mall owners and
bus companies, shall be engaged with to develop or utilize existing facilities such
as parks, parking lots, and bus depots, which will serve as large collective
transportation terminals where people can park their cars, bikes, and other
mobility devices to take the buses to their destinations.

SEC. 8. Establishment of a Water Ferry System. - As much as practicable, the
national agencies and water regulatory bodies with jurisdiction over a body of
water, led by the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), in
coordination with the DOTr, shall explore the establishment of a ferry system in
navigable bodies of water which may be operated by the said authority or bidded
out to a private contractor. The possibility of interconnecting said waterways
shall also be considered to enhance route efficiency and accessibility.

SEC. 9. Travel Demand Management Programs. - The DOTr, in coordination
with the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), Department of
Education (DepEd), Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and one
representative each from the civil society, transport sector and the youth, shall
come up with guidelines on travel demand management programs that will
reduce the volume of cars and motorized transport in the roads, as well as
enhance and encourage students and workers to use the public transport
system. These shall include, but are not limited to, the following:

(a) Carpoo!, vanpool, and other car-share projects;

(b) Congestion pricing measures;

(c} Programs to promote telecommunication;

(d) Flexible work schedules, or satellite work centers;
(e) Transport program for government employees; and

(f) Intelligent transportation systems or other operational improvements that
are certified by the DENR to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

This shall be completed within twelve (12) months from the effectivity of this
Act.

SEC. 10. Government and Institutional Travel Demand Management. -
National government agencies, government-owned or controlled corporations,
state universities and colleges, and, where appropriate, local government units

===== [page 6] =====
shall consider travel demand management measures within their respective
workplaces and institutions.

Such measures may include, as appropriate, flexible work arrangements,
staggered working hours, carpooling and vanpooling, employee transportation
programs, bicycle facilities, pedestrian access, and other measures consistent
with applicable laws and regulations.

SEC. 11. Designation of Bicycle Lanes. - A portion of the roadway or highway
shall be designated as "bicycle lanes" by the LGU having jurisdiction over such
roadway or highway, in coordination with the Department of Public Works and
Highways (DPWH), within six (6) months from the effectivity of this Act.

The designated bicycle lanes shall be for the preferential or exclusive use of
bicycles. The bicycle lanes shall be made identifiable by striping with signing or
striping with pavement markings. As much as practicable, permanent bicycle
lanes and walkways shall be physically separated from the main road through
physical barriers, or shall be elevated or otherwise constructed separately from
the main road.

These bicycle lanes shall not be obstructed by a parked or standing motor
vehicle or any other stationary object.

SEC. 12. Provision of Bicycle Parking Spaces and Bike Racks. - Owners of the
following establishments are hereby required to provide bicycle racks and
parking spaces:

(a) For non-residential use with gross floor area larger than seven thousand
five hundred (7,500) square feet;

(b) Multiple-family dwellings when twelve (12) or more are provided on a lot;
and

(c) Public parking lots.

The DOTr shail establish guidelines on the minimum supply of bicycle parking
spaces in buildings and other facilities, taking into consideration the population
of the short-term or long-term dwellers in the area, within six (6) months from
the effectivity of this Act.

SEC. 13. Protection and Maintenance of Bicycle Lanes. - The concerned LGU
or government agency having jurisdiction over a designated bicycle lane shall
establish appropriate measures to prevent its obstruction, encroachment,
unauthorized conversion, or use for purposes inconsistent with its designation.

The maintenance and enforcement of bicycle lanes shall be coordinated with
the appropriate traffic management, law enforcement, and road authorities.

SEC. 14. Bicycle Parking and End-of-Trip Facilities. - The guidelines on
bicycle parking spaces and bike racks issued pursuant to Section 12 shall, where
appropriate, provide for secure bicycle parking, adequate lighting, accessibility,

===== [page 7] =====
and reasonable proximity to building entrances, public transportation facilities,
and other destinations.

The guidelines may also provide for end-of-trip facilities, including changing
areas, lockers, and other appropriate facilities, taking into consideration the size,
nature, and location of the establishment.

SEC. 15. Maintenance of Sustainable Transportation Facilities. - Government
agencies and LGUs responsible for sustainable transportation facilities shall
ensure their regular inspection, maintenance, repair, and continued usability.

Sustainable transportation investments shall not be considered fully effective
solely upon completion of construction. Appropriate provisions for operation,
maintenance, rehabilitation, and replacement shall be incorporated into
applicable plans, programs, and budgets.

SEC. 16. Universal Accessibility and Inclusive Mobility. - Sustainable
transportation facilities and services established or improved pursuant to this Act
shall, to the extent practicable, conform to applicable accessibility standards and
shall provide safe and convenient mobility for persons with disabilities, older
persons, children, pregnant women, and persons with temporary or permanent
mobility limitations.

The planning and development of walkways, bicycle facilities, public
transportation facilities, terminals, and other mobility infrastructure shall take
into consideration universal design principles and applicable accessibility laws
and regulations.

SEC. 17. Continuity and Safety of Pedestrian and Bicycle Facilities. -
Walkways and bicycle lanes established pursuant to this Act shall, as practicable,
form continuous and connected networks and shall not be designed or
maintained as isolated segments that unreasonably terminate, obstruct, or
expose users to avoidable hazards.

The concerned implementing authorities shall establish measures for the
proper maintenance, lighting, drainage, signage, crossings, barriers, and other
safety features necessary to ensure the continued usability of pedestrian and
bicycle facilities.

SEC. 18. Public Information and Participation. - The DOTr and concerned
LGUs shall provide accessible and understandable information concerning
sustainable transportation programs, projects, routes, facilities, and travel
demand management measures.

In the formulation and periodic review of the Sustainable Transport Action
Plan and relevant local plans, the concerned agencies and LGUs shail provide
appropriate opportunities for consultation and participation by commuters,
pedestrians, cyclists, transport workers and operators, persons with disabilities,
older persons, women, youth, business groups, civil society organizations, and
other affected stakeholders.

===== [page 8] =====
SEC. 19. Public Transportation Service Standards. - The DOTr shall establish
appropriate standards and performance indicators for sustainable public
transportation services, taking into consideration, as applicable, reliability,
frequency, travel time, connectivity, affordability, accessibility, passenger safety,
environmental performance, and service coverage.

The standards shall serve as a basis for planning, evaluation, and
improvement of public transportation services.

SEC. 20. Facilities. - The DOTr, in coordination with the DPWH and the LGUs
concerned, shall carry out an investment program that shall encourage the
construction of different facilities to promote convenient and safe options to bike
and walk for routine travel and for other purposes, to promote a shift to
sustainable transport within selected communities.

This shall be completed within twelve (12) months from the effectivity of this
Act.

SEC. 21. Education and Research Programs. - The DOTr shail undertake
studies and research initiatives on emerging trends on sustainable development.
It shall disseminate relevant information to car owners, drivers, and commuters
to promote awareness and behavioral shift toward sustainable mobility.

The DOTr shall also provide technical assistance and other necessary
resources to government agencies and private sector stakeholders involved in
the development and operation of the transport system.

SEC. 22. Sustainable Transportation Investment Program. — The DOTr, in
coordination with the DEPDev, DBM, DPWH, DENR, and concerned LGUs, shall
identify priority sustainable transportation investments consistent with the
Sustainable Transport Action Plan and applicable national development and land
use plans.

Priority investments may include public transportation improvements,
pedestrian and cycling networks, intermodal facilities, © water-based
transportation, transport information systems, traffic © management
improvements, and other projects that directly advance the objectives of this
Act.

SEC. 23. Climate and Environmental Safeguards. — Sustainable transportation
programs and projects shall, as applicable, incorporate measures for reducing air
pollution and greenhouse gas emissions and for improving resilience to flooding,
extreme heat, severe weather events, and other climate-related risks.

SEC. 24. Capacity Building. - The DOTr, in coordination with concerned
national government agencies, LGUs, academic institutions, and other relevant
stakeholders, shall develop capacity-building programs for government
personnel responsible for sustainable transportation planning, project
development, traffic management, active mobility, data management, and
related functions.

===== [page 9] =====
Particular attention shall be given to LGUs with limited technical and
institutional capacity.

SEC. 25. Financing. - The DOTr, in coordination with the DENR, shall explore
and pursue opportunities to access international environmental financing
mechanisms and climate-related funding sources. These funds shall be utilized to
support the development of sustainable public transport systems and the
promotion of cleaner, less polluting energy sources. Public-private partnerships
shall also be encouraged in the development of transportation improvements.

SEC. 26. Separability Clause. If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in force and effect.

SEC. 27. Repealing Clause. - Any law, presidential decree or issuance,
executive order, letter of instruction, rule or regulation inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act is hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 28. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
following its complete publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of
general circulation.
