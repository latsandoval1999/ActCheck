                                        EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The State recognizes that quality basic education is not the exclusive
responsibility of the Department of Education. It is a shared undertaking of
families, communities, teachers, and government — one that requires
sustained, structured collaboration at every level of the education system.
      Parent-Teacher-Community Associations (PTCAs) have long served as
the principal mechanism for this collaboration. They are present in every
public basic education school and are anchored, however thinly, in existing
law. Their legal basis was written in 1974 as a narrow organizational
mandate at the school level.1 It has never been updated by statute to reflect
the governance architecture established by Republic Act No. 9155, the Local
Government Code, or the practical needs of a decentralized education
system.
       Studies show that sustained collaboration between parents and
teachers can contribute to improved academic performance. On distance
learning during the pandemic, researchers from the University of the
Philippines Center for Integrative and Development Studies observed that
teachers had come to treat parents as partners of their children to ensure
that learning happens at home.2 Likewise, the Philippine Institute for
Development Studies, found that parental support is consistently positively
correlated with test scores,3 with the relationship strongest for
low-performing students.
       The premise that this collaboration deserves stronger institutional
footing is not a new one. The Supreme Court has already recognized that
official PTA/PTCA recognition establishes the association as a pillar of the

       This Act amends Presidential Decree No. 603 to modernize the
school-level PTCA in line with contemporary child welfare and education
governance standards.
In response to recent incidents of violence involving learners and school
personnel, the Department of Education called for closer cooperation among
schools, parents, and communities to strengthen school safety and child
protection. As Education Secretary Sonny Angara observed, “Schools
cannot do this alone. Parents cannot do this alone.” He further
described the Parent-Teacher Association as “one of the most important
bridges linking the family, school, and community.”6 These statements show
that protecting learners and supporting their development require schools,
families, and communities to work together.
      In view of the foregoing, the passage of this Act is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE PARENT-TEACHER-COMMUNITY
        ASSOCIATION SYSTEM IN BASIC EDUCATION, AMENDING
     PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 603, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                                THEREFOR
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
      SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “PTCA
Institutionalization Act.”
       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to:
(a) Promote participatory governance in the basic education sector by
enabling parents, teachers, and community stakeholders to engage
meaningfully in school and local education decision-making;
(b) Strengthen the role of families and communities as active partners in
ensuring learner welfare and the quality of basic education;
(c) Institutionalize a multi-level PTCA structure that provides organizational
continuity, accountability, and sustained engagement across the school, city
or municipal, and provincial levels; and
(d) Integrate the PTCA system into existing local governance mechanisms,
particularly Local School Boards and Special Education Fund planning,
consistent with Republic Act No. 7160.
       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:
(a) PTCA or School PTCA refers to the Parent-Teacher-Community
Association organized at the school level pursuant to this Act and the
implementing guidelines of the Department of Education;
(b) City or Municipal PTCA Federation refers to the federation of all school
PTCAs within a city or municipality, recognized and organized pursuant to
this Act;
(c) Provincial PTCA Federation refers to the federation of all city or municipal
PTCA federations within a province, recognized and organized pursuant to
this Act;
(d) Local School Board or LSB refers to the body constituted under Section
98 of Republic Act No. 7160 at the city, municipal, or provincial level;
(e) Special Education Fund or SEF refers to the fund constituted under
Republic Act No. 7160 for the operational and capital requirements of public
schools; and
(f) Department refers to the Department of Education.​
​
​      SEC. 4. Amendment of Article 77 of Presidential Decree No. 603. —
Article 77, Chapter II of Presidential Decree No. 603, otherwise known as
“The Child and Youth Welfare Code,” is hereby amended to read as follows:
      “Art. 77. Parent-Teacher-Community Associations. — Every early
      childhood development center and basic education school shall
      organize a Parent-Teacher-Community Association, hereinafter
      referred to as PTCA.
      “The PTCA shall be composed of parents or parent-substitutes of
      enrolled learners, teachers, and a duly recognized community group
      or organization. They shall elect from among themselves their
      respective sets of officers.
      “For purposes of this Act, a parent-substitute refers to a person
      other than the biological parent who has legal custody of a child
      and is primarily responsible for the child’s care and development,
      including but not limited to legal guardians and foster care parents
      duly recognized by the Department of Social Welfare and
      Development.
      “All parents and parent-substitutes with children enrolled in a
      school shall be considered members of its PTCA and shall comply
      with the duties and responsibilities that membership entails.
      “PTCAs shall assist provincial, city, municipal, and other local
      authorities and school officials in the advocacy, protection, and
      promotion of the rights and welfare of children, including school
      achievement and related development initiatives.
      “PTCAs shall closely coordinate with school officials, student
      councils, local councils for the protection of children, and other
      concerned authorities in implementing holistic development
      programs for children in their respective localities.

​
​
​      SEC. 5. Insertion of Article 77-A — Principal Functions. — A new
Article 77-A is hereby inserted in Presidential Decree No. 603, to read
as follows:
      “Art. 77-A. Principal Functions of the PTCAs. — The principal
      functions of the PTCAs are as follows:
      “(1) Create and implement a development program for children in
      the school community to ensure that they become well-rounded,
      responsible, and civic-oriented citizens;
      “(2) Ensure the full cooperation of parents, parent-substitutes,
      teachers, and community stakeholders in the efficient and
      effective implementation of the development program;
      “(3) Consult children at all times, through their student councils,
      and in the case of younger children, through their parents,
      parent-substitutes, or caregivers, in the planning and
      implementation of programs, projects, and activities;
      “(4) Serve as a forum for local school stakeholders to design
      appropriate programs, discuss issues, identify solutions, and
      recommend actions that address children’s concerns;
      “(5) Raise awareness and support implementation of child
      protection policies in schools and communities;
      “(6) Assess the local implementation of the basic education
      program through collaboration and cooperation among parents,
      parent-substitutes, teachers, school officials, local government
      officials, and civil society;
      “(7) Partner with appropriate school officials in the design,
      implementation, follow-through, and monitoring of school
      programs that require community participation, including literacy,
      health and nutrition, and disaster risk reduction; and
      “(8) Strengthen accountability among school stakeholders by
      facilitating information dissemination regarding DepEd and local
      school programs and services, including participation in
      monitoring, assessing, and evaluating these services.”​
       SEC. 6. Establishment of City or Municipal PTCA Federations. —
There is hereby established in every city and municipality a City or
Municipal PTCA Federation, composed of all school PTCAs within the city or
municipality. The City or Municipal PTCA Federation shall be recognized as
a legal stakeholder body in city or municipal education governance.​
​
​     SEC. 7. Establishment of Provincial PTCA Federations. — There is
hereby established in every province a Provincial PTCA Federation,
composed of all City or Municipal PTCA Federations within the province.
The Provincial PTCA Federation shall be recognized as a legal stakeholder
body in provincial education governance.
      SEC. 8. Organization and Governance. — The City or Municipal PTCA
Federation and the Provincial PTCA Federation shall be organized, governed,
and managed in accordance with the implementing rules and regulations
issued by the Department pursuant to Section 16 of this Act.​
​
​      SEC. 9. Functions of Federated PTCA Bodies. — In addition to the
functions applicable to school-level PTCAs under Chapter II, City or
Municipal and Provincial PTCA Federations shall:
       (a) Coordinate programs and initiatives across school-level PTCAs
       within their jurisdiction;
       (b) Provide capacity-building, technical, and organizational support to
       school PTCAs;
       (c) Facilitate inter-school and inter-LGU coordination on education
       programs and concerns;
       (d) Represent the interests of parents, teachers, and community
       stakeholders before Local School Boards and other education
       governance bodies;
       (e) Conduct regular consultations with constituent school PTCAs on
       matters affecting learner welfare and local education planning; and
       (f) Submit periodic reports to the Department and relevant local
       government units on federation activities, programs, and financial
       operations.
       SEC. 10. Representation in Local School Boards. — Consistent with
​




​
Section 98 of Republic Act No. 7160, the duly elected presidents of City or
Municipal and Provincial PTCA Federations organized pursuant to this Act
shall serve as members of their respective Local School Boards. Their
participation shall include, as appropriate, engagement in the annual
preparation of the local education investment plan and deliberations on the
use of the Special Education Fund.​
​
​     SEC. 11. DepEd Supervision. — The PTCA system at all levels shall
operate under the regulatory supervision of the Department of Education.
Nothing in this Act shall be construed as diminishing the authority of the
Department to regulate, monitor, and provide technical guidance to PTCAs
and their federations.​
​
​      SEC. 12. Coordination with Local Government Units. — PTCAs and
their federations shall coordinate with their respective city, municipal, and
provincial governments in the planning and implementation of
education-related programs and activities. LGUs are authorized, but not
required, to appropriate funds in support of PTCA federation activities,
subject to their respective local appropriations ordinances and applicable
budget rules.​
​
​     SEC. 13. Special Education Fund Support. — Local School Boards
may, subject to the provisions of Republic Act No. 7160 and applicable joint
circulars on SEF use, authorize the use of a portion of the SEF to support
capacity-building activities of PTCA federations within their jurisdictions.​
​
​      SEC. 14. Financial Management and Reporting. — All school PTCAs
and their federations shall maintain financial records and comply with
financial management, transparency, and reporting requirements prescribed
by the Department, including periodic reporting on activities, programs, and
financial operations to the Department and, in the case of federations, to
their respective local government units.​
​
​      SEC. 15. Prohibited Activities. — PTCAs and their federations shall
not engage in partisan political activities. The Department shall prescribe,
through its implementing rules and regulations, such other prohibited
activities and corresponding sanctions as may be necessary to protect the
integrity and independence of the PTCA system.
       SEC. 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The Department of
Education, in coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local
Government and the Early Childhood Care and Development Council, shall
issue the implementing rules and regulations of this Act within ninety (90)
days from its effectivity.​
​
​     SEC. 17. Appropriations. — The amount necessary to implement this
Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act. ​
​
​     SEC. 18. Transitory Provisions. — All existing school PTCAs and
federated PTA bodies organized pursuant to DepEd Order No. 13, s. 2022 or
any predecessor order shall continue to function upon the effectivity of this
Act. Within one (1) year from the effectivity of the implementing rules, such
bodies shall complete any reorganization necessary to conform with the
requirements of this Act.​
​
​      SEC. 19. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not so
affected shall remain in full force and effect.​
​
​     SEC. 20. Repealing Clause. — All laws, orders, rules, and regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.​
​
​     SEC. 21. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
