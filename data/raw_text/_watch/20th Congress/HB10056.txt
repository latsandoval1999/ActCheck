                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill, to be known as the Magna Carta of Student Rights and Welfare
Act, affirms the State’s constitutional duty to prioritize education and to afford
special protection to the youth. It recognizes students not merely as recipients of
instruction but as essential partners in the educational process, contributors to
social development, and active participants in democratic life. Anchored on the
principles that education is a human right, a public good, and a public
responsibility, the measure institutionalizes a comprehensive framework for the
protection of student rights and welfare across senior high school, higher
education, and technical-vocational pathways. It seeks to ensure that student
welfare—covering access, participation, retention, completion, and lifelong
learning—is not treated as ancillary, but as integral to quality and inclusive
education.

       The proposed Act establishes clear guiding principles rooted in dignity,
non-discrimination, transparency, restorative discipline, and responsible use of
technology. It safeguards meaningful and non-tokenistic student participation in
governance, protects students from harassment, discrimination, red-tagging,
retaliation, and privacy-invasive practices, and mandates accessible and impartial
grievance redress mechanisms, including the creation of Student Ombud services.
In recognition of the growing digitalization of education, it also provides
protections against opaque or discriminatory uses of artificial intelligence and
technology-assisted decision-making. The measure promotes coordination and
policy coherence among the Commission on Higher Education, the Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority, and the Department of Education
to ensure integrated delivery of student welfare services and smooth transitions
across learning pathways.

       Ultimately, this bill establishes minimum, enforceable standards applicable
to all public and private educational institutions offering senior high school,
higher education, and TVET programs. It clarifies key definitions, ensures that
institutional rules and contracts are consistent with constitutionally protected
student rights, and declares void any policy inconsistent with the Act, without
precluding the adoption of more protective measures. By institutionalizing a
coherent and rights-based student welfare framework, this measure strengthens
democratic participation in education, enhances accountability and fairness in
institutional practices, and affirms the State’s commitment to nurturing
empowered, protected, and engaged learners.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

                                 CHAPTER I
                          GENERAL PROVISIONS


SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known and cited as the “Magna
Carta of Student Rights and Welfare Act” hereinafter referred to as the Act.
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the rights and welfare of students as essential partners in the
educational process, as contributors to social development, and as active
participants in democratic life, consistent with the Constitution which mandates
the priority of education, guarantees academic freedom, and affords special
protection to the youth.

The State recognizes that education is a human right, a public good, and a public
responsibility, and that student welfare is integral to access, participation,
retention, completion, and lifelong learning across formal, non-formal, and
technical-vocational pathways. Toward this end, the State shall:

      (a) Recognize education as a human right and a public good, and ensure
      that student welfare measures are integral to access, participation,
      retention, completion, and lifelong learning across formal, non-formal, and
      technical-vocational pathways;

      (b)   Uphold     academic    freedom,    institutional   autonomy,    public
      accountability, and academic integrity, provided that institutional policies,
      rules, and practices shall at all times be consistent with constitutional
      guarantees and shall not impair or diminish legally protected student
      rights;

      (c) Promote inclusive, learner-centered, and quality education responsive
      to contemporary and emerging challenges in education reform, including
      learning poverty, governance and quality assurance gaps, digital
      transformation, education-to-employment transitions, and the adequacy
      and sustainability of student support systems;

      (d) Ensure meaningful, effective, and non-tokenistic student participation
      in education governance, institutional policy-making, quality assurance


                                                                                  2
      mechanisms, and campus life, free from harassment, discrimination,
      intimidation, red-tagging, surveillance, or retaliation in any form;

      (e) Protect students in physical and digital learning environments,
      including through the responsible, transparent, and ethical use of
      educational technologies and artificial intelligence, and through the
      protection of privacy, personal data, and informational self-determination;

      (f) Institutionalize accessible, impartial, and timely grievance redress
      mechanisms, including student ombud services, and provide effective
      remedies, compliance measures, and administrative accountability for
      violations hereof; and

      (g) Promote convergence, coordination, and policy coherence among the
      Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Technical Education and
      Skills Development Authority (TESDA), the Department of Education
      (DepEd), and other concerned agencies to ensure integrated delivery of
      student welfare services and smooth transitions across learning pathways.

SEC. 3. Guiding Principles. – This Act shall be implemented, interpreted, and
enforced in accordance with the following guiding principles, which shall inform
all implementing rules, institutional policies, and administrative actions adopted
pursuant hereto:

      (a) Dignity and Non-Discrimination – Every student shall enjoy equal
      rights and freedoms without discrimination based on political conviction,
      religion, race, ethnicity, cultural origin, gender, sexual orientation, gender
      identity or expression, age, socio-economic status, language, disability,
      health condition, civil status, migration or refugee background, field or
      level of study, mode of delivery, or any analogous status;



                                                                                  3
      (b) Student Partnership – Students are equal stakeholders in education
      whose informed participation enhances relevance, quality, accountability,
      and responsiveness of educational institutions;

      (c) Social Dimension of Education – Student welfare, including housing,
      nutrition, health, mental health, learning resources, digital access, safety,
      inclusion, and support services, is integral to the realization of the right to
      education and academic success;

      (d) Transparency and Fairness – Students are entitled to clear,
      predictable, and accessible rules, fair assessment systems, transparent fees
      and charges, timely information, and impartial remedies;

      (e) Restorative Discipline and Due Process – Disciplinary measures shall
      be corrective, restorative, proportionate, and rights-respecting, anchored
      on due process and educational objectives rather than punishment alone;
      and

      (f) Digital and Technological Responsibility – Students shall be
      protected from opaque, unfair, discriminatory, excessive, or privacy-
      invasive uses of technology, data systems, and artificial intelligence in
      educational settings.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms shall be
understood as follows:

      (a) Student refers to any person enrolled in a public or private educational
      institution offering senior high school, higher education, or technical-
      vocational education and training (TVET), whether on a full-time, part-
      time, blended, or distance learning basis;


                                                                                   4
(b) Educational Institution refers to any public or private institution
authorized, chartered, or recognized by DepEd, CHED, or TESDA to offer
senior high school, higher education, or TVET programs, including
satellite campuses and learning centers;

(c) School Campus refers to all buildings, grounds, facilities, training
sites, dormitories managed by the school, and officially designated digital
platforms administered or required by the institution for academic,
administrative, or student welfare purposes;

(d) Student Organization refers to any student-initiated or student-led
association recognized pursuant hereto and to reasonable institutional rules
consistent herewith;

(e) Student Council or Student Government refers to the duly elected
representative body of students at the program, college, campus, or
institutional level, organized in accordance with student-approved rules;

(f) Student Union refers to an institutional, inter-campus, or sectoral
democratic body of students organized for representation, advocacy,
coordination, and the provision of student services;

(g) Student Ombud refers to an independent, impartial, and accessible
mechanism mandated to receive complaints, provide advice, facilitate
resolution, and recommend remedies for violations of this Act and related
policies;

(h) Student Welfare Services refer to programs and services supporting
student participation, retention, completion, and well-being, including
guidance, counseling, mental health services, health services, disability



                                                                            5
       support, financial aid assistance, career services, safety services, and
       emergency support systems;

       (i) Technology-Assisted Decision-Making refers to automated or semi-
       automated tools used in assessment, admissions, advising, monitoring,
       analytics, or discipline that materially affect academic standing or access
       to services;

       (j) Campus Safety Personnel refer to duly authorized and licensed

       or enemies of the State, which exposes them to harm, discrimination, or
       undue surveillance.

SEC. 5. Coverage and Construction. – This Act shall apply to all students
enrolled in public and private educational institutions offering senior high school,
higher education, and TVET programs, regardless of mode of delivery or
institutional classification.

All institutional policies, contracts, handbooks, circulars, and regulations shall be
construed consistently herewith. Any provision thereof inconsistent with this Act
shall be void to the extent of such inconsistency, provided finally that rights
recognized herein shall be deemed minimum standards and shall not preclude the
adoption of more protective measures.

                                  CHAPTER II
                   GENERAL RIGHTS AND FREEDOMS

SEC. 6. Freedom of Expression, Peaceful Assembly, and Petition. – Students
shall have the right to express views, publish materials, peacefully assemble,
organize forums, and petition school authorities on matters affecting their rights,
welfare, and academic life, subject only to limitations provided for by law and
narrowly tailored to protect the rights of others, campus safety, and the orderly
conduct of academic activities.

No school rule or administrative action shall impose prior restraint or penalize
student expression merely because it is critical, dissenting, or unpopular, provided
that restrictions may be imposed only where strictly necessary to prevent
unlawful content, true threats, targeted harassment, or direct incitement to
imminent lawless action, and provided further that such restrictions shall be
subject to due process and written justification.

SEC. 7. Protection of Lawful Online Expression. – Students retain their
constitutional and statutory rights when communicating through social media,
learning management systems, or other digital platforms.

A school may take action on online conduct only upon a clear, evidence-based,
and demonstrable showing that such conduct constitutes unlawful activity or
results in a substantial, material, and specific disruption of school operations,
provided that due process under this Act is strictly observed, and provided further
that lawful dissent, advocacy, whistleblowing, and criticism shall not be
suppressed under the guise of discipline.

SEC. 8. Academic Freedom of Students. – Students shall enjoy academic
freedom consistent with institutional academic freedom, including the right to

                                                                                  7
inquiry, research, discussion, expression of reasoned views, and participation in
academic discourse, provided that legitimate academic standards, curricular
requirements, and professional norms are respected.

No student shall be compelled to adopt political or ideological beliefs unrelated
to academic merit.

SEC. 9. Right to Privacy and Confidentiality. – Students shall have the right to
privacy and to the confidentiality of their personal, academic, health, counseling,
and disciplinary records.

Such records shall be accessed only by authorized personnel on a strict need-to-
know basis, and shall be protected through appropriate technical, organizational,
and administrative safeguards consistent with data privacy laws and ethical
standards.

SEC. 10. Student Records and Disclosures. – Educational institutions shall
provide students timely access to their academic records and allow reasonable
correction of inaccurate, misleading, or incomplete information through
transparent procedures.

Disclosure of student information shall require consent, provided that disclosures
authorized by law or necessary to address emergencies may be made, provided
further that such disclosures are limited in scope, documented thereafter, and
subject to audit and accountability.

                                  CHAPTER III
      SOCIAL DIMENSION, ACCESS, AND STUDENT WELFARE
                                    SERVICES


SEC. 11. Right to Access, Progress, and Completion Support. – Every student
shall have the right to meaningful access to education and to reasonable
opportunities to progress through and complete quality education within the
prescribed or appropriate period of study.

To this end, educational institutions, in coordination with concerned government
agencies where applicable, shall design, adopt, and implement student success
and retention systems intended to identify, prevent, and address barriers to access,
retention, progression, and completion.

SEC. 12. Student Success Systems and Support Mechanisms. – Such barriers
may include, but are not limited to, academic underpreparedness, learning
difficulties, financial hardship, health and mental health conditions, disability-
related needs, family responsibilities, digital access limitations, language barriers,
and psychosocial challenges.

Student success systems may include, but shall not be limited to, diagnostic
assessments, academic advising and mentoring, tutoring and learning support
services, bridging or remediation programs, flexible learning arrangements,
early-alert and referral mechanisms, and coordination with student welfare
services, provided that such systems shall be implemented in a supportive,
confidential, and non-stigmatizing manner.

SEC. 13. Non-Stigmatization, Standards, and Periodic Assessment of Student
Success Systems. – Participation in such support mechanisms shall not be
construed as evidence of academic deficiency or misconduct, nor shall it be used
                                                                                    9
as a basis for discrimination, exclusion, or adverse academic action, provided
further that reasonable academic standards and program requirements shall be
maintained.

Educational institutions shall periodically assess the effectiveness of such
systems and make reasonable adjustments thereto, taking into account student
feedback, equity considerations, and institutional capacity.

SEC. 14. Right to Non-Discriminatory Admission, Participation, and Access to
Services. – Admissions, enrollment, participation in academic, co-curricular, and
extracurricular activities, access to student welfare services, and eligibility for
institutional opportunities shall be conducted without discrimination, consistent
with the Constitution and applicable laws.

Educational institutions shall adopt clear, written policies prohibiting
discrimination and exclusion, and shall designate responsible officers or units
tasked with monitoring compliance, receiving complaints, and coordinating
appropriate responses.

SEC. 15. Grievance Accessibility and Interim Protective Measures in Non-
Discrimination Compliance. – Such officers or units shall ensure that grievance
procedures are accessible, confidential, timely, and impartial, provided that
students shall be informed of available remedies and procedures in clear and
understandable language, and provided further that interim protective measures
may be adopted where necessary to prevent further harm or prejudice.

Nothing in this section shall prevent educational institutions from implementing
reasonable admission standards or academic requirements, provided finally that
such standards are applied fairly, transparently, and without discriminatory intent
or effect.

                                                                                10
SEC. 16. Right to Adequate and Accessible Student Welfare Services. –
Educational institutions shall provide, or ensure access to, adequate, accessible,
and responsive student welfare services appropriate to their mandate, size,
geographic location, and student population.

      Such services shall include, at a minimum:

(a) guidance, career development services, and academic advising to assist
students in educational planning and transition to employment or further study;

(b) health services or formal referral systems, including access to preventive care
and health information consistent with law and policy;

(c) mental health and psychosocial support services, including counseling,
referral pathways, and crisis response mechanisms;

(d) disability support services, including assistive technologies, reasonable
accommodations, and accessible physical and digital infrastructure;

(e) safety and protection services addressing harassment, violence, exploitation,
abuse, and online harms; and

(f) information and referral services for scholarships, financial aid, internships,
and student opportunities.

SEC. 17. Training, Capacity-Building, and Resource Considerations for
Welfare Services. – Educational institutions shall ensure that personnel assigned
to student welfare services are adequately trained in non-discrimination,
safeguarding, trauma-informed response, data privacy, and mental health first aid,
provided that continuous capacity-building shall be undertaken where feasible.

Resource limitations shall not be used to justify the total absence or denial of
essential welfare services, provided further that institutions may adopt phased
                                                                                11
implementation, partnerships, or referral arrangements consistent with law and
available funding.

SEC. 18. Right to Mental Health Support, Crisis Intervention, and Suicide
Prevention. – Educational institutions shall establish, maintain, and periodically
review comprehensive mental health support systems that are accessible,
confidential, and responsive to student needs.

Such systems shall include:

      (a) clear protocols for the identification, referral, and response to students
      at risk of self-harm, suicide, or harm to others;

      (b) access to trained mental health professionals or formal referral
      partnerships with qualified external providers;

      (c) readily available information, in both physical and digital formats, on
      mental health services, support mechanisms, and emergency contacts; and

      (d) policies ensuring that students who seek mental health support are not
      subjected to punitive treatment, discrimination, or academic disadvantage.

SEC. 19. Standards for Crisis Response Measures and Mental Health
Promotion. – Crisis response measures shall prioritize safety, dignity,
confidentiality, and the least-restrictive intervention, provided that any temporary
academic or administrative adjustments shall be support-based, time-bound, and
subject to periodic review.

Educational institutions shall integrate mental health promotion, awareness, and
prevention strategies into student orientation and welfare programs, where
practicable.


                                                                                 12
SEC. 20. Right to Adequate Learning Necessities and Digital Access. – Students
shall have the right to reasonable and equitable access to learning necessities
essential to the completion of their programs of study, including textbooks,
instructional materials, laboratories, libraries, equipment, connectivity, and
required digital platforms.

Where educational institutions require the use of specific paid software,
platforms, devices, or digital tools as part of academic requirements, they shall
ensure the availability of affordable alternatives, institutional licenses, shared
access, or subsidy mechanisms, particularly for financially disadvantaged
students, provided that no student shall be academically penalized solely on
account of inability to procure such tools.

SEC. 21. Cost Minimization and Transparency in Required Learning Tools. –
Educational institutions shall take reasonable steps to minimize unnecessary costs
and shall provide timely notice of required materials and expenses, provided
further that transparency in the selection and use of required learning tools shall
be observed.

SEC. 22. Right to Safe, Accessible, and Inclusive Learning Spaces. –
Educational institutions shall ensure that physical, virtual, and hybrid learning
environments are safe, accessible, and inclusive, and conducive to learning and
student well-being.

This shall include compliance with applicable safety standards, reasonable
accommodations for students with disabilities, safeguards against discrimination
and harassment in digital platforms, and the adoption of inclusive and universal
design principles in facilities and learning systems.




                                                                                13
SEC. 23. Reporting, Response, and Anti-Retaliation for Safety and
Accessibility Concerns. – Institutions shall establish procedures for reporting
safety and accessibility concerns and shall act promptly thereon, provided that
students who raise such concerns shall be protected from retaliation.

SEC. 24. Right to Basic Needs Support and Referral Mechanisms. – In
coordination with relevant government agencies, local government units, and
accredited service providers, educational institutions—particularly public higher
education institutions and TVET institutions—shall establish mechanisms to
support students experiencing housing insecurity, food insecurity, or other unmet
basic needs.

Such mechanisms may include emergency assistance pathways, referral systems
to social services, dormitory access policies, meal or food assistance partnerships,
and information services on available government programs, provided that
assistance shall be provided in a dignified, non-stigmatizing manner.

SEC. 25. Availability, Capacity, and External Referral for Basic Needs
Support. – The provision of support under this section shall be subject to existing
laws, institutional capacity, and funding availability, provided finally that the
absence of immediate assistance shall not preclude referral to appropriate external
services.

                                  CHAPTER IV
   QUALITY EDUCATION, ACADEMIC RIGHTS, AND STUDENT-

SEC. 26. Right to Transparent Academic Programs, Requirements, and
Policies. – Every student shall have the right to clear, accurate, complete, and
timely information concerning academic programs, curricular requirements,
learning outcomes, course content, assessment methods, grading systems,
attendance policies, internship or practicum requirements, retention standards,
and all other academic rules governing their enrollment and progression.

Educational institutions shall ensure that such information is formally
documented, regularly updated, and made readily accessible to students through
official handbooks, program guides, course syllabi, institutional websites, and
official digital platforms administered by the institution.

SEC. 27. Publication, Notice, and Non-Retroactivity of Material Academic
Rules. – No material academic requirement, rule, or policy shall be enforced
against a student unless it has been duly published, communicated, or made
reasonably accessible prior to its application, provided that emergency or interim
academic measures may be adopted where necessary to protect academic
continuity or public safety, provided further that students shall be promptly
informed thereof in writing.

Educational institutions shall provide advance notice of substantial changes in
academic requirements, program structures, or assessment systems, and shall
adopt reasonable transition arrangements to avoid undue prejudice to currently
enrolled students, provided finally that such changes shall not be applied



                                                                               15
retroactively in a manner that impairs vested academic rights without compelling
justification.

SEC. 28. Right to Fair, Reasonable, and Pedagogically Sound Assessment and
Feedback. – Students shall have the right to be evaluated solely on the basis of
academic performance using assessment methods that are fair, reasonable,
transparent, pedagogically sound, and aligned with declared learning outcomes.

Assessment criteria, grading rubrics, performance standards, and evaluation
methods shall be clearly communicated at the commencement of each course or
learning activity, and shall be applied consistently and without arbitrariness or
discrimination.

SEC. 29. Feedback, Consultations, and Grade Review or Appeal. – Students
shall be entitled to timely, constructive, and meaningful feedback on their
academic performance, including access to grade consultations, review
procedures, and clarification of evaluation results, provided that such
consultations shall be conducted in a respectful and non-intimidating manner.

Educational institutions shall establish formal procedures for grade review or
appeal, which shall be impartial, time-bound, and accessible, provided further
that the exercise of such right shall not subject the student to retaliation, academic
disadvantage, or prejudicial treatment.

SEC. 30. Reasonable Individual Circumstances in Assessment and Attendance
Rules. – In the application of attendance rules, deadlines, and assessment
requirements, educational institutions shall take into account documented and
reasonable individual circumstances, including health conditions, mental health
concerns, disability-related needs, family emergencies, and force majeure events,
consistent with academic standards and program integrity.

                                                                                   16
SEC. 31. Right to Recognition of Prior Learning, Microcredentials, and
Flexible Learning Pathways. – Consistent with national education reform goals,
lifelong learning principles, and education-to-employment pathways, students
shall have the right to fair and transparent recognition of prior learning, including
competencies acquired through non-formal, informal, work-based, or experiential
learning, where program standards and regulatory frameworks allow.

Educational institutions shall adopt policies and procedures for the assessment
and recognition of prior learning that are objective, evidence-based, and aligned
with applicable frameworks issued by CHED, TESDA, or other competent
authorities.

SEC. 32. Quality Assurance and Information Standards for Microcredentials
and Flexible Units. – Institutions offering microcredentials, short courses,
stackable learning units, or modular programs shall ensure that such offerings
meet quality assurance standards, are transparently described, and are designed
to support mobility, credit transfer, and progression within or across institutions.

Students shall be provided clear information on the academic value, credit
equivalence, portability, and limitations of microcredentials and flexible learning
units, provided that such offerings shall not be misleading as to their recognition
or applicability, provided further that access thereto shall be non-discriminatory.

SEC. 33. Non-Compulsion and Written Explanation in Refusal of Recognition
of Prior Learning. – Nothing in this section shall be construed to compel an
educational institution to recognize prior learning inconsistent with essential
academic or professional standards, provided finally that refusal to recognize
prior learning shall be accompanied by written explanation.




                                                                                  17
SEC. 34. Right to Safe, Fair, and Educational Internships, Practicum, and
Work-Integrated Learning. – Students shall have the right to internships,
practicums, on-the-job training, and other work-integrated learning experiences
that are safe, educational, and oriented primarily toward learning outcomes rather
than labor substitution.

Educational institutions shall ensure that such learning experiences are governed
by written agreements or memoranda of understanding clearly specifying
learning objectives, duration, supervision arrangements, workload, assessment
methods, and grievance mechanisms.

SEC. 35. Due Diligence, Reporting, Compensation Transparency, and
Reasonable Adjustments in Work-Integrated Learning. – Institutions shall
exercise due diligence in the selection and monitoring of partner establishments
to ensure compliance with safety standards, labor and child protection laws, and
policies against harassment, abuse, and exploitation. Students shall be informed
of their rights and responsibilities prior to placement and shall have access to
clear and confidential mechanisms for reporting unsafe conditions, harassment,
coercion, or exploitative practices, provided that reports shall be acted upon
promptly and without retaliation.

Where internships or practicums involve compensation, allowances, or subsidies,
educational institutions shall ensure transparency in terms, conditions, and
deductions, provided further that unpaid placements shall not impose
unreasonable financial burden on students. Educational institutions shall provide
reasonable alternatives, adjustments, or accommodations when placements are
inaccessible due to disability, health concerns, safety risks, or other justified
hardship, provided finally that students shall not be penalized academically for
circumstances beyond their control.

                                    CHAPTER V
 DIGITAL RIGHTS, ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE, AND ACADEMIC
                                    INTEGRITY


SEC. 36. Right to Ethical, Transparent, and Accountable Use of Technology
and Artificial Intelligence. – Students shall have the right to the fair, ethical,
transparent, and accountable use of digital technologies, data-driven systems, and
artificial intelligence in educational settings, particularly where such technologies
materially affect academic standing, access to learning opportunities, retention,
discipline, or eligibility for institutional services.

Educational institutions shall ensure that students are clearly informed, in
advance and in accessible language, when technology-assisted decision-making
systems are used in admissions within the institution, assessment, grading,
monitoring of academic activity, learning analytics, advising, retention
interventions, or disciplinary processes.

SEC. 37. Human Review, Contestability, and Governance Standards for
Educational Technologies. – No student shall be subjected to an adverse
academic or disciplinary decision solely on the basis of automated or algorithmic
outputs, provided that meaningful human review shall be conducted by a
competent and impartial authority, and provided further that the student shall be
afforded the opportunity to understand, contest, and seek correction of the basis
of such decision.

Educational institutions shall adopt written and consultative policies governing
the procurement, deployment, and use of educational technologies and artificial
intelligence systems, which shall include, at a minimum: (a) clearly defined

                                                                                  19
purposes and permissible uses; (b) safeguards to prevent bias, discrimination, or
disparate impact; (c) data minimization, security, and retention standards
consistent with law; (d) accountability mechanisms for errors, misuse, or abuse;
and (e) procedures for student notice, feedback, and redress.

SEC. 38. Prohibition of Political Profiling, Surveillance, and Suppression of
Lawful Activity Through Technology. – The use of technology and artificial
intelligence shall not be employed for political profiling, surveillance of lawful
student activities, intimidation, coercive monitoring, or suppression of lawful
expression or association, provided finally that any monitoring tools used for
academic integrity or security purposes shall be proportionate, narrowly tailored,
and subject to oversight.

Educational institutions shall, where appropriate, integrate age- and level-
appropriate education on digital literacy, data rights, and responsible artificial
intelligence use into student orientation and support programs.

SEC. 39. Access to Academic Integrity Frameworks, Tools, and Due Process
in Enforcement. – Educational institutions shall maintain clear, written, and
publicly accessible academic integrity policies that define prohibited conduct,
standards of integrity, corresponding sanctions, and procedural safeguards,
consistent with principles of fairness, proportionality, and educational
development.

Such policies shall be framed primarily as educational and corrective
mechanisms, emphasizing learning, ethical formation, and academic honesty,
rather than as purely punitive instruments.




                                                                               20
SEC. 40. Student Support, Procedural Safeguards, and Proportionality in
Academic Integrity Enforcement. – Students shall be provided reasonable access
to academic integrity tools, guidance materials, and instructional support
designed to help them understand citation standards, avoid plagiarism,
responsibly use artificial intelligence tools where permitted, and comply with
integrity requirements.

Enforcement of academic integrity policies shall be subject to due process, which
shall include:

(a) timely and written notice of the alleged violation and factual basis thereof;

(b) reasonable access to relevant evidence and documentation;

(c) the opportunity to be heard and to present explanations or defenses;

(d) impartial evaluation by a designated authority or body; and

(e) a written decision stating the findings, applicable rules, and reasons.

SEC. 41. Sanction Determination and Prohibition Against Sole Reliance on
Automated Indicators. – In determining sanctions, educational institutions shall
consider the nature of the violation, the student’s intent, prior conduct, academic
level, and opportunities for remediation, provided that sanctions shall be
proportionate and educational in character, provided further that first-time or
minor violations may, where appropriate, be addressed through corrective or
learning-oriented measures.

No student shall be sanctioned for academic dishonesty on the sole basis of
similarity scores, automated flags, or algorithmic indicators without human
assessment and contextual evaluation.


                                                                                    21
SEC. 42. Standards for Device Use Policies, Monitoring Tools, and Digital
Wellbeing. – Educational institutions may adopt policies regulating the use of
digital devices, online platforms, and monitoring tools in order to protect learning
outcomes, mental health, safety, and academic integrity.

Such policies shall be clear, reasonable, consultative, and non-arbitrary, and shall
be formally adopted and communicated prior to enforcement.

SEC. 43. Minimum Standards for Monitoring and Digital Wellbeing
Measures; Dispute Coverage. – In the implementation of device use and
monitoring policies, educational institutions shall observe the following
minimum standards:

(a) respect for student dignity, privacy, and personal data;

(b) prohibition of humiliating practices, public shaming, or coercive access to
private accounts or personal devices;

(c) reasonable accommodations for students with disabilities, health conditions,
or emergency needs; and

(d) proportionality between the purpose of the policy and the degree of restriction
imposed.

      Monitoring technologies, including proctoring software and surveillance
tools, shall be used only where demonstrably necessary, and shall be configured
to collect the least amount of data required to achieve legitimate educational
objectives, provided that students shall be informed of the nature, scope, and
duration of such monitoring. Educational institutions shall adopt complementary
digital wellbeing measures, including guidance on responsible device use, screen
time management, online safety, and healthy engagement with digital platforms,

                                                                                 22
provided finally that such measures shall support, rather than penalize, student
wellbeing. Any dispute arising from the enforcement of device use or monitoring
policies shall be subject to the grievance and due process mechanisms provided
for under this Act.




                                                                             23
                                   CHAPTER VI
         STUDENT PARTICIPATION, REPRESENTATION, AND
                                 GOVERNANCE


SEC. 44. Right to Organize and Form Student Organizations. – Students shall
have the right to freely form, join, and participate in student organizations,
associations, formations, or alliances of their choosing for academic, cultural,
social, civic, religious, advocacy, or other lawful purposes, consistent with the
Constitution and applicable laws.

Educational institutions shall adopt reasonable, content-neutral, transparent, and
uniformly applicable rules governing the recognition, registration, operation, and
dissolution of student organizations.

SEC. 45. Standards for Recognition, Registration, and Protection Against
Ideological Discrimination. – Such rules shall be designed solely to ensure
accountability, safety, financial responsibility, and the orderly conduct of campus
life, and shall not be used, directly or indirectly, to suppress lawful expression,
advocacy, dissent, organizing, or issue-based engagement. Recognition and
registration procedures shall be clearly defined, time-bound, and appealable, and
shall specify objective requirements relating to membership thresholds, officers,
constitutions or by-laws, financial reporting, and compliance with lawful
institutional regulations, provided that such requirements shall be proportionate
to the nature and scale of the organization and shall not impose undue burdens on
student initiative or participation.

No student organization shall be denied recognition, suspended, deregistered, or
otherwise sanctioned on the basis of political belief, issue advocacy, ideology, or
lawful participation in public discourse, provided further that recognized

                                                                                24
organizations shall comply with reasonable rules on transparency, non-violence,
respect for the rights of others, and lawful use of institutional resources.

SEC. 46. Non-Discriminatory Access to Facilities, Platforms, and Institutional
Support. – Educational institutions shall ensure that duly recognized student
organizations are afforded reasonable and equitable access to campus facilities,
communication platforms, and institutional support consistent with available
resources, provided finally that such access shall be granted on a non-
discriminatory basis and subject to clear, published guidelines.

SEC. 47. Student Councils, Student Unions, Autonomy, and Independence
from Undue Institutional Interference. – Student councils and student unions,
whether at the program, college, campus, institutional, or inter-campus level,
shall be recognized as the legitimate representative bodies of the studentry, vested
with the authority to articulate student interests, engage institutional leadership,
and participate in governance processes affecting student welfare and academic
life.

Such bodies shall enjoy organizational autonomy and independence from undue
institutional interference, reasonable financial autonomy, and the freedom to
manage their internal affairs, including the conduct of elections, formulation of
platforms, adoption of internal rules, deliberative processes, and management of
funds, consistent with transparent and student-approved procedures.

SEC. 48. Prohibition on Institutional Installation of Student Leaders and
Retaliatory Conditioning of Support. – Educational institutions shall not install,
appoint, endorse, or impose student leaders, nor shall they directly or indirectly
interfere with student elections, electoral processes, platforms, campaigns,
internal deliberations, or lawful activities, provided that institutions may adopt


                                                                                 25
neutral and proportionate oversight measures solely to ensure electoral integrity,
financial accountability, and compliance with applicable laws and regulations.

Institutional recognition, access to resources, or administrative support shall not
be conditioned on alignment with institutional policies or administrative
positions, provided further that disagreement, criticism, opposition, or advocacy
expressed by student councils or student unions shall not constitute a ground for
sanction, withdrawal of recognition, diminution of privileges, or adverse
administrative action.

SEC. 49. Affiliation Freedom of Student Councils and Student Unions. –
Student councils and student unions shall have the freedom to determine their
affiliations with local, regional, national, or international student formations,
networks, or federations, provided finally that such affiliations are lawful,
transparent to their membership, and consistent with duly adopted internal rules.

SEC. 50. Right to Co-Governance and Representation in Institutional
Decision-Making Bodies. – Students shall have the right to meaningful, effective,
and non-tokenistic representation in institutional decision-making bodies that
substantially affect student rights, welfare, learning conditions, and campus life.

Such representation shall include, but shall not be limited to, participation in:

(a) student discipline boards or equivalent adjudicatory bodies;

(b) consultations, committees, or councils concerning tuition, fees, and other
student charges;

(c) quality assurance, accreditation, program evaluation, and curriculum review
processes;



                                                                                    26
(d) policy formulation, handbook review, and rule-making mechanisms affecting
students; and

(e) campus safety, security, student welfare, and crisis response committees.

SEC. 51. Selection, Access to Information, and Protection of Student
Representatives. – Student representatives shall be selected through transparent,
democratic, and student-determined processes and shall be afforded timely access
to relevant information, records, and materials necessary for informed and
meaningful participation, provided that confidentiality obligations applicable to
all members of such bodies are observed.

No student representative shall suffer academic, financial, disciplinary, or other
forms of retaliation, discrimination, intimidation, or disadvantage by reason of
lawful participation in governance bodies or by reason of positions taken in good
faith in the course of deliberations, provided further that good faith participation
shall be presumed in the absence of clear and convincing evidence to the contrary.

SEC.    52.     Capacity-Building    for    Student    Representatives     Without
Compromising Independence. – Educational institutions shall adopt reasonable
measures to capacitate student representatives, including orientation, procedural
guidance, and access to information, provided finally that such measures shall not
compromise the independence, representational mandate, or accountability of
student representatives to the student body.

SEC. 53. Right to Access Institutional Information Affecting Student Life. –
Students shall have the right to timely, meaningful, and reasonable access to
institutional regulations, policies, resolutions, decisions, minutes, circulars, and
other official records that directly affect student rights, welfare, obligations, or
campus life.

                                                                                 27
Educational institutions shall adopt clear procedures to ensure transparency and
accessibility of such information, including publication through official websites,
digital platforms, student handbooks, or other appropriate means, and shall ensure
that information is presented in clear, understandable, and accessible language.

SEC. 54. Limitations, Written Denials, and Protection Against Adverse
Treatment in Information Requests. – Access to institutional information may
be subject only to lawful limitations based on confidentiality, privacy, or security,
provided that such limitations are narrowly construed and justified, and provided
further that any denial or restriction of access shall be accompanied by a written
explanation and information on available remedies.

No student shall be disadvantaged, sanctioned, or subjected to adverse treatment
for requesting or accessing information in good faith under this section, provided
finally that the exercise of this right shall be without prejudice to other
transparency, disclosure, or freedom of information mechanisms provided by
law.




                                                                                  28
                                 CHAPTER VII
FEES, FINANCIAL RIGHTS, AND STUDENT ECONOMIC WELFARE


SEC. 55. Transparency, Accountability, and Consultation on Tuition and
Other Fees. – Educational institutions shall ensure full transparency,
accountability, and meaningful student consultation in the imposition,
adjustment, and utilization of tuition and other school fees.

To this end, educational institutions shall provide students and their duly
recognized representatives with a clear, itemized, and comprehensible breakdown
of tuition and all other mandatory and optional fees, including the purpose, legal
basis, and intended use thereof, prior to collection.

SEC. 56. Consultation Standards and Conditions for Fee Increases. – Any
proposal to increase tuition or other fees shall be subject to meaningful
consultation with duly recognized student councils or student unions, conducted
in good faith and in accordance with existing laws, rules, and regulations
governing tuition and fee adjustments, provided that consultation shall include
adequate notice, access to relevant financial information, and reasonable
opportunity for students to raise concerns and submit recommendations.

No tuition or fee increase shall be implemented unless supported by lawful
authority, justified by legitimate institutional needs, and accompanied by a
transparent explanation of how additional collections will be utilized to improve
educational quality, facilities, or student services, provided further that
consultation shall not be treated as a mere formality.

SEC. 57. Monitoring of Fee Utilization and Availability of Reports. –
Educational institutions shall establish internal mechanisms for monitoring the
use of collected fees and shall make available to students, upon request, periodic
                                                                               29
reports or summaries on the utilization of such fees, provided finally that
confidential or proprietary financial information may be protected in accordance
with law.

SEC. 58. Right to Take Examinations, Continue Studies, and Obtain Records
Despite Unpaid Fees. – No student shall be barred from taking periodic, midterm,
or final examinations, or from continuing participation in academic activities,
solely by reason of unpaid tuition or other school fees, subject to reasonable
institutional arrangements consistent with law.

Educational institutions may adopt policies allowing the withholding of certain
clearances or certifications in cases of unpaid obligations, provided that such
policies are reasonable, proportionate, and duly published, and provided further
that they do not result in undue prejudice to the student.

SEC. 59. Release of Records and Reasonable Settlement Arrangements for
Financial Difficulty. – In no case shall an educational institution unreasonably
withhold the release of a transcript of records, certificates of enrollment,
diplomas, or other documents required for transfer, employment, licensure,
scholarship application, or further study, where such withholding would
effectively deprive the student of educational or livelihood opportunities.

Educational institutions shall establish reasonable settlement arrangements,
installment plans, or referral mechanisms for students experiencing financial
difficulty, provided finally that the exercise of this right shall not extinguish
legitimate financial obligations lawfully incurred by the student.

SEC. 60. Scholarships, Grants, Subsidies, and Student Financial Support
Navigation. – Educational institutions shall establish and maintain student-facing
mechanisms to assist students in navigating scholarships, grants, subsidies,

                                                                               30
financial aid programs, and other forms of educational assistance available from
institutional, government, private sector, or international sources.

Such mechanisms may include designated financial aid offices or focal persons,
information desks, digital platforms, orientation programs, and referral systems,
and shall provide timely, accurate, and accessible information on eligibility
requirements, application procedures, deadlines, and documentary requirements.

SEC. 61. Equity Measures, Cooperation with Agencies, and Non-Entitlement
Clause. – Educational institutions shall take reasonable steps to ensure equitable
access to financial assistance opportunities, including outreach to financially
disadvantaged, marginalized, or at-risk students, provided that selection
processes for scholarships and grants shall be transparent, merit-based or need-
based as applicable, and free from favoritism or discrimination. Where
government-funded or institutionally administered financial assistance programs
are involved, educational institutions shall cooperate with concerned agencies in
facilitating student access thereto, provided further that delays, administrative
burdens, or documentation requirements shall be minimized to the extent
practicable.

Nothing in this section shall be construed as creating an automatic entitlement to
financial assistance, provided finally that the absence of available aid shall not
preclude referral to appropriate external programs or support services.




                                                                               31
                                CHAPTER VIII
       DUE PROCESS, DISCIPLINE, AND PROTECTION FROM
                       HARASSMENT AND VIOLENCE


SEC. 62. Restorative, Proportionate, and Educational Discipline. –
Disciplinary measures imposed upon students shall be corrective, restorative,
proportionate, and educational in character, and shall be designed primarily to
promote accountability, learning, rehabilitation, and the well-being of the school
community, rather than punishment for its own sake.

Educational institutions shall ensure that disciplinary policies and sanctions are
clearly defined, publicly accessible, and consistently applied, and that they take
into account the nature and gravity of the offense, the circumstances under which
it was committed, the student’s intent, academic level, prior conduct, and the
potential impact of the sanction on the student’s right to education.

SEC. 63. Restorative Approaches and Prohibited Punishments. – Discipline
shall, whenever appropriate and practicable, incorporate restorative approaches,
including mediation, dialogue, community service, reflection activities, or other
learning-oriented interventions that seek to repair harm, restore relationships, and
reintegrate the student into the academic community, provided that participation
in restorative processes shall be voluntary and shall not be used to compel
admissions of guilt.

Collective punishment, humiliating penalties, degrading treatment, public
shaming, penalties involving physical or psychological harm, and sanctions that
arbitrarily or disproportionately restrict constitutional or statutory rights are
hereby prohibited.



                                                                                 32
SEC. 64. Last Resort Standard for Expulsion and Severe Sanctions. – No
disciplinary measure shall be imposed where lesser, non-punitive interventions
would reasonably suffice to address the conduct, provided further that expulsion,
exclusion, or permanent separation shall be imposed only as a measure of last
resort and only for grave offenses, subject to the strict observance of due process.

SEC. 65. Right to Due Process in Disciplinary Proceedings. – No student shall
be subjected to disciplinary sanction without due process of law. Due process in
disciplinary proceedings shall, at a minimum, include the following:

(a) Written Notice – timely written notice of the charge or charges, stating the
specific acts complained of, the rules allegedly violated, and the possible
sanctions;

(b) Access to Information – reasonable access to relevant evidence, documents,
and records relied upon by the institution, subject to lawful confidentiality and
safety considerations;

(c) Opportunity to be Heard – the right to be heard, to submit written
explanations, and to present evidence or witnesses through fair and orderly
procedures;

(d) Assistance or Counsel – the right to be assisted by counsel or an adviser of
the student’s choice, including a representative from a student council, union, or
organization;

(e) Impartial Adjudication – determination of the case by an impartial and
properly constituted disciplinary body, which shall include student representation
where provided for under this Act or institutional rules;




                                                                                 33
(f) Reasoned Decision – a written decision stating the facts established, the rules
applied, the reasons for the decision, and the sanction imposed, if any; and

(g) Right to Appeal – the right to appeal the decision to a higher institutional
authority and, where applicable, to the appropriate government agency in
accordance with implementing rules.

SEC. 66. Timeliness, Confidentiality, and Preventive Suspension Standards. –
Disciplinary proceedings shall be conducted within reasonable periods and in a
manner that minimizes unnecessary delay, intimidation, or prejudice to the
student, provided that proceedings shall respect confidentiality and the dignity of
all parties.

Preventive suspension shall be imposed only as a measure of last resort, and only
when the continued presence of the student poses a clear and present danger to
persons or a specific and immediate risk of serious disruption. Such suspension
shall be time-bound, subject to periodic review, and accompanied by reasonable
academic support measures, provided further that preventive suspension shall not
be used as a substitute for prompt resolution of the case.

SEC. 67. Protection Against Discrimination, Harassment, Red-Tagging, and
Violence. – Educational institutions shall adopt, implement, and strictly enforce
comprehensive     policies   prohibiting   discrimination,   harassment,    sexual
harassment, bullying, hazing, violence, intimidation, abuse, and red-tagging,
whether committed online or offline, within campus premises or in connection
with school-related activities.

Such policies shall be consistent with existing laws and shall include, at a
minimum: (a) clear definitions of prohibited acts and behaviors; (b) accessible
and confidential reporting channels; (c) procedures for prompt, impartial, and

                                                                                34
victim-centered investigation; (d) interim protective and safety measures to
prevent further harm; (e) safeguards for confidentiality and data privacy; (f)
explicit anti-retaliation provisions; and (g) administrative accountability for
officials, employees, or agents who commit, tolerate, or fail to act upon
violations.

SEC. 68. Survivor Support, Express Prohibition of Red-Tagging, and Capacity-
Building. – Educational institutions shall ensure that victims and survivors of
prohibited acts are provided with appropriate support services, including
counseling, academic accommodations, referral to medical or legal assistance,
and other protective measures, provided that such support shall not be
conditioned on the filing of a formal complaint. Red-tagging, as defined under
this Act, is hereby expressly prohibited within educational institutions. Any act
of red-tagging by school officials, employees, security personnel, or persons
acting under institutional authority shall constitute a grave violation of this Act
and shall be subject to appropriate administrative sanctions, provided further that
such acts may give rise to civil or criminal liability under existing laws.

Educational institutions shall conduct regular information campaigns, training
programs, and capacity-building activities for administrators, faculty, staff,
security personnel, and students to promote awareness of rights, prevention of
abuse, and the creation of safe and inclusive learning environments, provided
finally that failure to adopt or enforce policies under this section shall itself
constitute a violation of this Act.




                                                                                35
                                 CHAPTER IX
                    CAMPUS SAFETY AND SECURITY


SEC. 69. Campus Safety Standards, Preparedness, and Visitors Management.
– Educational institutions shall establish, maintain, and periodically review
comprehensive campus safety and preparedness standards designed to protect
life, property, and the educational environment, while respecting academic
freedom, student rights, and the open character of educational institutions.

Such standards shall be embodied in a written campus safety plan, duly approved
by the appropriate institutional authority and communicated to the school
community, which shall include protocols for emergency preparedness, disaster
risk reduction, fire safety, health emergencies, violence prevention, and
coordination with local emergency responders.

SEC. 70. Visitor Management Protocols and Participatory Review of Safety
Standards. – Educational institutions shall adopt reasonable, non-discriminatory,
and proportionate visitor management protocols, which may include sign-in
procedures, identification requirements for campus personnel, access controls for
sensitive facilities, and reporting mechanisms for suspicious behavior, provided
that such protocols shall not encourage profiling, discrimination, or arbitrary
restriction of lawful access to campus spaces.

Campus safety standards shall be reviewed at reasonable intervals, and after
major incidents where applicable, with the participation of student representatives
and other stakeholders, provided further that feedback from the school
community shall be considered in improving safety measures.




                                                                                36
SEC. 71. Protocols for Engagement with Outside Agencies and Law
Enforcement. – Educational institutions shall adopt clear and rights-respecting
protocols governing engagement with law enforcement agencies, military units,
or other outside authorities within campus premises.

When outside agencies request access to campus or seek to interview a student
during school hours or within campus grounds, educational institutions shall, to
the extent permitted by law: (a) verify the identity, authority, and purpose of the
requesting personnel; (b) inform the student of the nature and voluntary or
compulsory character of the request, as applicable; (c) advise the student of their
rights, including the right to counsel or a support person where allowed by law;
(d) endeavor to notify the parent or legal guardian in the case of minor students,
or a designated support person for adult students, unless prohibited by law; and
(e) ensure that interviews are conducted in a manner and setting that minimizes
intimidation, coercion, or undue pressure.

SEC. 72. Documentation and Non-Obstruction Clause in Outside Agency
Engagements. – All requests and engagements under this section shall be
properly documented, subject to lawful confidentiality requirements, provided
that nothing herein shall be construed to obstruct lawful investigations or the
execution of valid court orders, provided further that compliance shall at all times
be consistent with constitutional rights and due process.

SEC. 73. Right Against Unreasonable Searches and Seizures; Standards and
Documentation. – Students shall enjoy the constitutional right against
unreasonable searches and seizures within campus premises and in connection
with school-related activities.




                                                                                 37
Educational institutions may conduct searches of school property or personal
effects only when there exists a reasonable, individualized, and articulable basis
related to safety or the enforcement of clearly defined institutional rules.

SEC. 74. Reasonableness, State Agent Rule, Exclusion, and Records of
Searches. – Any search conducted by school authorities shall be reasonable in
scope, manner, and intrusiveness, shall respect the dignity and privacy of the
student, and shall, whenever practicable, be conducted in the presence of the
student and a witness, provided that searches shall not be conducted as a form of
punishment or intimidation. Searches by state agents shall be governed by the
Constitution and applicable jurisprudence. Evidence obtained in violation of this
section shall not be used as the sole basis for disciplinary action under this Act,
provided further that such exclusion shall be without prejudice to lawful
proceedings conducted in accordance with due process.

All searches conducted by school authorities shall be properly documented,
indicating the basis, scope, time, and results thereof, provided finally that such
records shall be subject to review through grievance or oversight mechanisms.

SEC. 75. Crisis Response Teams, Emergency Referrals, and Use of Force
Standards. – Educational institutions shall establish trained crisis response teams
or designate responsible personnel to respond to emergencies involving threats to
safety, mental health crises, violence, or disasters.

Such teams or personnel shall be trained in de-escalation, trauma-informed
response, mental health first aid, and emergency coordination, and shall operate
under clear protocols emphasizing safety, proportionality, and respect for rights.




                                                                                38
SEC. 76. Police Assistance as Last Resort and Prohibition on Suppression of
Lawful Activity. – Requests for assistance from police or other state security
forces shall be made only as a measure of last resort, when there is an imminent
threat to life or serious harm that cannot be adequately addressed through internal
mechanisms, provided that such requests shall be guided by necessity and
proportionality.

Educational institutions shall ensure that any use of force by campus safety
personnel is strictly limited, lawful, proportionate, and subject to clear rules and
accountability mechanisms, provided further that the use of force shall never be
employed to suppress lawful student activities or expression.

SEC. 77. Rights-Based Standards on Security Presence, Non-Intimidation, and
Oversight. – Educational institutions shall ensure that campus security
arrangements preserve campuses as spaces of learning, inquiry, and democratic
engagement. Accordingly: (a) intimidation, harassment, coercion, surveillance,
or disruption of lawful student activities by campus security personnel or any
persons acting under institutional authority are hereby prohibited; (b) the
presence of armed state forces within campus premises shall be limited to
circumstances authorized by law and justified by necessity, such as emergencies
involving imminent threats to life or serious public safety concerns; (c) any
coordination with state security forces shall be governed by clear protocols that
respect institutional autonomy and student rights; and (d) safeguards shall be
adopted to prevent profiling, red-tagging, or abuse arising from security
operations.

Educational institutions shall establish oversight and accountability mechanisms
for campus security, including complaint procedures, investigation processes,
and appropriate sanctions for violations, provided that students shall have access

                                                                                 39
to such mechanisms without fear of retaliation. Regular orientation and training
programs on rights-based campus safety shall be conducted for administrators,
faculty, staff, and security personnel, provided finally that failure to adopt or
enforce standards under this Chapter shall constitute a violation of this Act.




                                                                                 40
                                  CHAPTER X
     IMPLEMENTATION, COMPLIANCE, AND ENFORCEMENT


SEC. 78. Student Rights and Welfare Office and Student Ombud. – Every
public and private educational institution covered by this Act shall establish a
Student Rights and Welfare Office, or designate an equivalent permanent and
adequately resourced unit, which shall serve as the primary institutional
mechanism for the implementation, coordination, and monitoring of compliance
with this Act and its Implementing Rules and Regulations.

The Student Rights and Welfare Office shall function as a focal point for student
rights and welfare concerns and shall, among others: (a) disseminate information
to students, faculty, and staff regarding the rights, obligations, and protections
provided for herein, including through orientation programs, student handbooks,
and official digital platforms; (b) coordinate with academic units, administrative
offices, student services, and campus security to ensure that institutional policies,
procedures, and practices are aligned with this Act; (c) receive referrals of student
concerns, grievances, or systemic issues and facilitate access to appropriate
institutional remedies or support services; (d) assist in the periodic review and
assessment of institutional rules, handbooks, contracts, and practices affecting
student life; and (e) maintain records, in a privacy-protective manner, of
complaints, resolutions, and compliance measures for monitoring and reporting
purposes.

SEC. 79. Student Ombud Mechanism: Independence, Mandate, and Reporting.
– In addition to the foregoing, every educational institution shall establish an
independent, impartial, and accessible Student Ombud mechanism, which shall
be functionally independent from disciplinary offices, academic departments, and


                                                                                  41
security units, and which shall operate in accordance with the principles of
neutrality, confidentiality, accessibility, and fairness.

The Student Ombud shall have the mandate to: (a) receive complaints, inquiries,
and requests for assistance from students or other concerned persons relating to
alleged violations of this Act or of institutional policies affecting student rights
and welfare; (b) provide confidential advice, information, and guidance on rights,
procedures, and available remedies; (c) facilitate informal or alternative
resolution of disputes where appropriate and with the consent of the parties,
without prejudice to formal proceedings; (d) recommend interim or protective
measures to prevent continuing harm, retaliation, or prejudice to any party; (e)
refer cases to the appropriate institutional offices or to competent government
agencies when jurisdiction so requires; and (f) identify recurring issues or
systemic concerns and recommend corrective or preventive measures.

The Student Ombud shall submit periodic reports, in aggregated and anonymized
form, to the head of the institution and to such oversight bodies as may be
provided for in the Implementing Rules and Regulations, provided that no
personally identifiable information shall be disclosed and that confidentiality
shall be strictly observed. The independence, security of tenure, and operational
autonomy of the Student Ombud shall be safeguarded by institutional rules,
provided further that the Ombud shall not be subject to instructions or
interference in the performance of its functions, provided finally that such
independence shall not exempt the Ombud from accountability under applicable
laws and ethical standards.

SEC. 80. Students’ Rights and Welfare Council. – There is hereby created a
Students’ Rights and Welfare Council, hereinafter referred to as the Council,
which shall serve as the primary national policy coordination, advisory, and
monitoring body for the effective and coherent implementation of this Act.
                                                                                 42
The Council shall be convened and chaired by the Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) as the lead agency, in coordination with the Technical
Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) and the Department of
Education (DepEd), insofar as senior high school students are concerned, as may
be further provided in the Implementing Rules and Regulations.

The Council shall be composed of representatives from CHED, TESDA, DepEd,
the Commission on Human Rights (CHR), the National Youth Commission
(NYC), and representatives of associations of public and private educational
institutions.

The Council shall include at least three (3) student representatives, consisting of
one (1) representative from higher education, one (1) representative from
technical-vocational education and training (TVET), and one (1) representative
from senior high school. Such student representatives shall be elected or
nominated by duly accredited national student formations corresponding to their
respective sectors.

No student representative shall be appointed solely by an educational institution
or government agency. The Implementing Rules and Regulations shall provide
for the accreditation of national student formations and the orderly nomination or
election process, consistent with the principles of independence, inclusivity, and
democratic representation.

SEC. 81. Functions and Limitations of the Council. – The Council shall have
the following functions:

(a) provide authoritative policy guidance on the interpretation and
implementation of this Act and its Implementing Rules and Regulations;



                                                                                43
(b) recommend minimum standards, indicators, and benchmarks on student
rights, welfare services, participation, and campus safety;

(c) coordinate national and sectoral capacity-building programs for educational
institutions, student leaders, and relevant stakeholders;

(d) monitor national compliance trends, identify systemic gaps or emerging
issues, and propose appropriate policy or regulatory responses;

(e) facilitate inter-agency coordination and information-sharing relevant to
student welfare; and

(f) submit periodic reports and policy recommendations to Congress and
concerned agencies for legislative, regulatory, or administrative action.

The Council shall not exercise adjudicatory, prosecutorial, or disciplinary
powers, provided that its findings and recommendations may inform oversight,
rule-making, program development, and legislative review, provided further that
its operations shall be guided by transparency, inclusivity, respect for institutional
autonomy, and meaningful student participation.

SEC. 82. Mandatory Student Handbook Harmonization and Consultation. –
All educational institutions covered by this Act shall conduct a comprehensive
review, revision, and harmonization of their student handbooks, codes of conduct,
enrollment contracts, and related policies to ensure full conformity with the
provisions hereof.

Such harmonization shall be undertaken through a meaningful, inclusive, and
documented consultative process with duly recognized student councils, student
unions, or student organizations, and with other relevant stakeholders, including
faculty and administrative representatives, as may be appropriate.

                                                                                   44
SEC. 83. Standards for Harmonized Handbooks; Effect of Inconsistencies;
Non-Compliance. – Educational institutions shall ensure that revised handbooks
and policies:

(a) clearly reflect the rights, obligations, procedures, and remedies provided for
under this Act;

(b) are written in clear, accessible, and non-technical language, without prejudice
to legal accuracy; and

(c) are made available to students in both physical and digital formats.

      Any handbook provision, rule, or policy inconsistent with this Act shall be
void to the extent of such inconsistency and shall not be enforced, provided that
rights recognized herein shall be deemed minimum standards and shall not
preclude the adoption of more protective institutional measures. Failure to
undertake timely harmonization as required under this section shall constitute
non-compliance with this Act and may be subject to appropriate administrative
action, provided finally that the timeline, procedures, and transitional
arrangements shall be specified in the Implementing Rules and Regulations.

SEC. 84. Complaints, Remedies, and Protection Against Retaliation. – Any
student, student organization, student council or union, parent or legal guardian
of a minor student, or any concerned person with direct knowledge of a violation
may file a complaint for violations of this Act or its Implementing Rules and

SEC. 85. Anti-Retaliation Rule and Protective Measures Pending Resolution.
– Retaliation against any person who files a complaint, participates in an
investigation, provides information, or asserts rights under this Act is hereby
strictly prohibited.

Any act of retaliation shall constitute a separate and independent violation of this
Act and shall be subject to administrative sanctions, provided that the filing of a
complaint in good faith shall not give rise to liability even if the complaint is
ultimately dismissed, provided finally that protective measures may be ordered
pending resolution of the case.

SEC. 86. Administrative Accountability, Institutional Liability, and Sanctions.
– Any violation of this Act, its Implementing Rules and Regulations, or lawful
orders issued pursuant hereto by school officials, employees, security personnel,
or persons acting under the authority of an educational institution shall subject
the responsible persons to administrative accountability under applicable civil
service, labor, education, or professional regulations.

Administrative sanctions may include, as appropriate: (a) reprimand, warning, or
censure; (b) suspension or removal from student-facing or supervisory functions;
(c) disqualification from holding positions involving student welfare, discipline,
or governance; (d) mandatory corrective action plans, training, or supervision;
and (e) such other penalties as may be authorized by law or implementing rules.




                                                                                 46
SEC. 87. Institutional Sanctions, Due Process, and Non-Prejudice Clause. –
For educational institutions found to have committed willful, repeated, systemic,
or egregious violations, the appropriate government agency may impose
graduated institutional sanctions, including compliance orders, monitoring
requirements, directives for corrective measures, or other regulatory actions
consistent with due process. Any recommendation affecting permits, recognition,
accreditation, or authority to operate shall be made only in accordance with
existing education laws and regulations, provided that institutional sanctions shall
be proportionate and corrective in nature, provided further that due process shall
be strictly observed.

The imposition of administrative sanctions under this section shall be without
prejudice to the filing of civil, criminal, or other administrative actions under
existing laws.

SEC. 88. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, CHED, TESDA, and DepEd, insofar as senior high
school students are concerned, in coordination with CHR, NYC, representatives
of public and private educational institutions, duly recognized national student
formations, and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the Implementing
Rules and Regulations (IRR) of this Act.

The IRR shall: (a) provide clear, detailed, and operational guidelines for the
implementation of the rights, duties, standards, mechanisms, and safeguards
provided for herein; (b) prescribe differentiated and context-sensitive standards
appropriate to senior high school, higher education, and TVET institutions; (c)
define institutional responsibilities, timelines, reporting requirements, and
compliance indicators; (d) establish coordination mechanisms among concerned
agencies and institutions; (e) specify procedures for complaints, remedies,

                                                                                 47
monitoring, and enforcement consistent with due process; and (f) adopt
measurable indicators aligned with national education reform priorities and
student welfare outcomes.

SEC. 89. Consultation Requirement and Non-Diminution Rule in the IRR. –
In the formulation of the IRR, concerned agencies shall ensure meaningful
consultation with students and stakeholders, provided that student participation
shall be substantive and documented, provided further that the IRR shall not
diminish, restrict, or dilute the rights guaranteed under this Act, provided finally
that any ambiguity in the IRR shall be resolved in favor of the protection and
promotion of student rights and welfare.

SEC. 90. Administrative Sanctions. – The CHED, DepEd, and TESDA are
hereby vested with powers necessary to investigate and impose administrative
penalties to ensure the enforcement of this Act, consistent with the complaint,
remedies, accountability, and due process mechanisms provided under this Act.

Any student, member of a student council or student government, or a national
organization of student councils or student governments, or youth organization
may file written complaints before the CHED, DepEd, and TESDA. The CHED,
DepEd, and TESDA may impose graduated sanctions on schools violating this
Act: (a) Warning and compliance order; (b) Fines from P200,000 to P500,000;
(c) Suspension of programs; (d) Revocation of permit or license for grave,
repeated violations.

SEC. 91. Penal Provisions. – Any person who willfully and maliciously
obstructs the exercise of rights under this Act shall be liable to imprisonment of
one (1) to five (5) years, or a fine of P50,000 to P100,000, or both.




                                                                                 48
If the offender is a juridical entity, responsible officers shall be held liable. Public
officials found guilty shall, in addition, be dismissed from service.

SEC. 92. Interpretation. – All doubts in the implementation and interpretation of
this Act, including its Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), shall be
resolved in favor of upholding students’ rights and welfare.

SEC. 93. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, invalid, or unenforceable, the remaining provisions not affected
thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect, it being the intent of Congress
that this Act be given the fullest possible effect consistent with the Constitution.

SEC. 94. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, issuances, and parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

Such repeal or modification shall be construed only to the extent of the
inconsistency, provided that existing laws and regulations affording equal or
greater protection to student rights and welfare shall remain in force, provided
further that nothing herein shall be construed to diminish rights already
recognized under the Constitution or other special laws.

SEC. 95. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least one (1) newspaper of general
circulation.
