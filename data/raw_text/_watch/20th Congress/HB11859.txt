                                         EXPLANATORY NOTE

In 2024, the Philippines recorded 426 rabies deaths, and approximately 260 cases in
20251. A bite or a scratch from an unvaccinated animal, most often a dog or cat, can
carry a deadly risk. Yet rabies-related deaths can be prevented when immediate
medical intervention such as post-exposure treatment is given promptly, before
symptoms appear.2

Although the Philippines has made great progress against rabies since the enactment
of Republic Act No. 9482, otherwise known as the Anti-Rabies Act of 2007 which
sought to control, prevent the spread of, and eventually eradicate human and animal
rabies, access and affordability of post-exposure treatment to animal bite victims as
well as pre-exposure prophylaxis, remain serious challenges in far-flung communities.
Because these treatments require multiple clinic visits, bite victims especially in
remote areas must shoulder transportation cost, turning free treatment into an expense
many families cannot sustain. Moreover, deeply rooted misconceptions push victims
toward dangerous delays in getting treated.

This measure seeks to ensure that communities have access to anti-rabies vaccines by
establishing Animal Bite Centers in Rural Health Units nationwide. These centers
shall provide free post-exposure prophylaxis and pre-exposure prophylaxis in
accordance with Department of Health (DOH) guidelines. Furthermore, this bill
mandates the DOH, in coordination with local government units (LGUs), to conduct
sustained public awareness and education campaigns aimed at dispelling common
misconceptions about rabies and the importance of immediate medical care.

Until anti-rabies vaccines reach communities and people understand the dangers of
rabies, the intersecting barriers of distance, cost, and false belief will continue to
claim lives.

For this reason, passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                          AN ACT
     STRENGTHENING THE NATIONAL ANTI-RABIES PROGRAM
   THROUGH THE ESTABLISHMENT OF ANIMAL BITE CENTERS IN
   RURAL HEALTH UNITS, THE PROVISION OF FREE ANTI-RABIES
     VACCINATIONS, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

       Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Free and Accessible
Anti-Rabies Vaccination in Rural Health Units Act of 2026.”

        Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the declared policy of the State in
accordance with the 1987 Philippine Constitution, to protect and promote the right to
health of the people and instill health consciousness among them.

       Towards this end, the State shall adopt measures to strengthen public health
services by ensuring accessibly and timely anti-rabies prevention and treatment
through the provision of free anti-rabies vaccinations and related medical
interventions in rural health units, and by promoting greater access to anti-rabies
services for all Filipinos.

        Section 3. Establishment of Animal Bite Centers in Rural Health Units.-
The Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with local government units
(LGUs), shall ensure the establishment of Animal Bite Centers (ABC) in Rural Health
Units (RHUs) nationwide for the prevention and management of rabies and animal
bite cases.

       Section 4. Free Anti-Rabies Vaccination Program.- All RHUs nationwide are
hereby mandated to provide free anti-rabies post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) , when
necessary, pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), in accordance with the guidelines set by
the DOH.

       Section 5. Program Implementation.- The DOH shall lead the
implementation of the national free anti-rabies vaccination program. It shall
coordinate with the Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG), LGUs,
and other concerned agencies to ensure the availability, timely distribution, and
administration of vaccines.

       Section 6. Public Awareness and Education Campaign.- The DOH, in
coordination with the LGUs, shall conduct a continuous public awareness and
education campaign on rabies prevention, first aid measures, the importance of
seeking immediate medical attention, and the availability of free vaccination services.

       Section 7. Funding.- The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this
Act shall be charged against the current budget of the DOH. Thereafter, such sums as
may be necessary for the continuous implementation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act (GAA).

        Section 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations.- Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with the DILG, LGUs, and
relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the
effective implementation of this Act.

         Section 9. Separability Clause.- If any part or provision of this Act is held
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain
in full force and effect.

       Section 10. Repealing Clause.- All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules and regulations
contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

        Section 11. Effectivity.- This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.


       Approved,
