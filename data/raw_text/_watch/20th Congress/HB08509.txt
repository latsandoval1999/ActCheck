                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Violence against women and children remains one of the most pressing
social and human rights concerns in the Philippines. Internationally, the United
Nations Declaration on the Elimination of Violence Against Women recognizes
violence against women as a manifestation of historically unequal power
relations between men and women, and as a mechanism through which women
are often forced into subordinate positions in society. This structural inequality
continues to manifest in various forms of abuse, including physical, sexual,
psychological, and economic violence.

      Similarly, the World Health Organization has identified violence against
children as a global public health and human rights problem affecting every
country. Children, by reason of their age and dependency, are particularly
vulnerable to abuse and exploitation in the home, in educational institutions, in
workplaces, and increasingly in digital spaces.

       Recognizing the vulnerability of these sectors, the Philippines has enacted
several landmark laws designed to protect women and children from violence
and abuse. Among these are Republic Act No. 9262, which penalizes acts of
violence committed against women and their children within intimate
relationships, and Republic Act No. 9710, which affirms the State’s commitment
to eliminating discrimination against women and ensuring substantive equality.
These measures are complemented by other protective statutes such as Republic
Act No. 7610 and Republic Act No. 11648.

      Despite the existence of these laws, violence against women and children
remains pervasive. Data from the Philippine Statistics Authority indicate that
approximately one in four Filipino women has experienced physical, sexual, or
emotional violence from a husband or intimate partner. Studies conducted by
the United Nations Children’s Fund further reveal alarming levels of violence
experienced by young people, with a significant proportion of Filipino children
and youth reporting exposure to physical, emotional, or sexual abuse in various
settings, including homes, schools, and communities.

        The Philippine Commission on Women has likewise consistently described
violence against women as among the country’s most pervasive social issues,
closely linked to persistent gender inequality and deeply rooted cultural norms
that tolerate or normalize abusive behavior. In recent years, emerging forms of
violence—including online harassment, digital exploitation, and technology-
facilitated abuse—have further complicated the landscape of gender-based and
child-related violence.

       These realities highlight the need not only to maintain but to strengthen
the country’s legal and institutional mechanisms for the protection of women
and children. While existing statutes provide a strong legal framework, gaps
remain in implementation, coordination among agencies, victim protection
mechanisms, and the adaptation of safeguards to emerging threats and evolving
social conditions.

      This proposed measure seeks to reinforce and strengthen existing
protections for women and children by enhancing the mechanisms for
prevention, protection, reporting, and accountability. The bill is designed to
complement—not supplant—the protections already provided under existing
laws such as Republic Act No. 9262, Republic Act No. 9710, and Republic Act
No. 7610. It does not create conflicting obligations but instead aims to close
operational gaps, improve coordination among relevant government agencies,
and ensure more effective enforcement of the State’s policies against violence
and abuse.

      The measure is likewise consistent with the State’s constitutional duty
under the 1987 Constitution of the Philippines to value the dignity of every
human person and guarantee full respect for human rights, particularly those
belonging to vulnerable sectors of society.

       Strengthening protections for women and children is not merely a matter
of criminal justice or social welfare; it is an investment in a safer, more just, and
more equitable society. Ensuring that women and children are free from violence
enables them to participate fully in national development and contributes to the
overall stability and well-being of communities.

      It is for these reasons that the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
MANDATING THE DEPARTMENT OF INTERIOR AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT
   AND THE PHILIPPINE NATIONAL POLICE TO UPDATE EXISTING
    PROTOCOLS OF THE CHILDREN AND WOMEN’S DESKS IN THE
                        PHILIPPINES

       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

    SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Children and
Women’s Desk Act.”

       SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to value the
dignity of every human person and guarantee full respect for human rights. It
also recognizes the role of the youth in nation-building and shall promote and
protect their physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being. In
addition to this, the State recognizes the role of women in nation-building, and
shall ensure the fundamental equality before the law of women and men.

      SECTION 3. Scope. – This Act shall apply to all children and women’s
desks in the country, whether currently existing or yet to be established.

       SECTION 4. Roles and Responsibilities. – The Department of Interior and
Local Government, with the assistance of the Philippine National Police, shall
identify, prepare, and provide policy guidance and technical resource assistance
with regard to the inclusion of updates in the existing protocols of the children
and women’s desks in the Philippines in order to ensure the prompt, efficient,
and effective response to reported cases of abuse and the necessary rescue
protocols therewith.
      SECTION 5. Immediate Rescue. – The Department of Interior and Local
Government and the Philippine National Police shall partner with the
Department of Social Welfare and Development and the Philippine Commission
on Women as regards protocols pertaining to response on the reported cases of
abuse and rescue of the victims thereof within twenty-four hours from the receipt
of report.

      SECTION 6. Mental Health Assistance. - There shall be stationed at every
children and women’s desk a mental health professional who is tasked with
providing assistance, including psychological first aid, to all victims referred to
such a desk.

       SECTION 6. Socio-civic organizations and the Private Sector. – This Act
shall also empower the private sector in engaging and partnering with the
government in promulgating these protocols. Non-governmental organizations,
civic organizations, the academe, people’s organizations, and the like shall be
tapped by the government in providing assistance in the promulgation and
enforcement of such guidelines.
      SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The Department of
Agriculture shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act
within ninety (90) days from its effectivity.
       SECTION 8. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is
held invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts unaffected
shall remain in full force and effect.
      SECTION 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential
decrees or issuances, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and
regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 10. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15)
days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general
circulation.
