                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        In the Philippines, resident citizens who are aged sixty (60) or older are considered senior
citizens. They are entitled to certain privileges such as twenty (20%) discount and VAT exemption
on essential goods and services, medicines, transportation, restaurants, and medical services,
among others.

        The downside, however, of being a senior citizen for some is that they have a slimmer
chance for employment after retirement. Republic Act No. 7641 or the Retirement Law provides
that compulsory retirement for private sector employees in the Philippines is at age sixty-five (65),
while optional retirement at age sixty (60) is allowed provided that they have served at least five
(5) years. The same is generally true for government employees.

        While some may enjoy their retirement and can well afford health services and other
essential goods and amenities, many senior citizens face the burden of financial limitations after
leaving the service. They either have to rely on their meager monthly pensions and/or insurance
benefits, or on their families’ financial aid for their daily needs.
       As of 2025, Filipinos aged sixty (60) and above comprise 9.6 percent of the country's 113.7
million population, just short of the ten percent (10%) threshold used by international agencies
such as the World Bank to define an aging society.1

      Philippine Statistics Authority data show that about 1.8 million senior citizens are
employed in agriculture, wholesale and retail trade, transportation and other service sectors.2

         For now, it is safe to say that we have a young society in the Philippines, but it is also
necessary that the government provides opportunities for our senior citizens who are still able and
willing to find a gainful employment.

        Republic Act 7432, as amended by Republic Act No. 9257 and Republic Act No. 9994,
provides assistance for gainful employment of senior citizens. It recognizes that senior citizens
who have the capacity and desire to work shall be provided information and matching services,
and a tax incentive for private entities employing qualified senior citizens, subject to conditions.

        Hence, this proposed measure seeks to mandate the Department of Labor and Employment,
through the Public Employment Service Offices nationwide, to assist senior citizens who are
willing to be employed or re-employed through job matching and employment facilitation.

        This bill likewise intends to encourage the private sector to provide employment
opportunities for senior citizens by increasing the allowable additional deduction from gross
income to twenty-five (25%) of the total amount paid as salaries and wages, and by expressly
including documented job-related training costs actually incurred for senior citizens, subject to the
rules on deductions under the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended. This incentive may
be availed by private entities, provided that the statutory conditions for employment incentive
continue to be observed.

        This bill further provides that the enhanced deduction shall be available only to employers
that maintain senior citizens at not less than twenty-five percent (25%) of their total workforce.
This requirement is intended to ensure that the increased incentive is granted to employers that
meaningfully and consistently invest in senior citizen employment. It also complements the
existing statutory safeguards that employment must continue for at least six (6) months and that
the annual income of the senior citizen must not exceed the latest poverty threshold published by
the Philippine Statistics Authority for that year.

           Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
     assembled:

 1   Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as “Equal Employment Opportunities for Senior
 2   Citizens Act”.
 3
 4   Section 2. Declaration of Policies. – It is the policy of the State to promote the welfare of senior
 5   citizens and to provide opportunities for beneficial employment to senior citizens who are willing
 6   and able to work, consistent with the Labor Code and applicable laws. Towards this end, the State
 7   shall strengthen employment facilitation for senior citizen jobseekers and provide incentives to
 8   private entities that employ senior citizens, subject to safeguards against abuse.
 9
10   Section 3. Further Amendment of Section 5 (a) (Employment) and (c) Health of Republic Act No.
11   7432, as amended. – Section 5(a) of RA 7432, as amended by Republic Act No. 9257, Republic
12   Act No. 9994, and Republic Act No. 11916, is hereby amended to read as follows:

 5       Senior citizens who have the capacity and desire to work, or be re-employed, shall be
 6       provided information and matching services to enable them to be productive members
 7       of society. Terms of employment shall conform with the provisions of the Labor Code,

10      The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), through the Public

23      annual income of the senior citizen does not exceed the latest poverty threshold as
