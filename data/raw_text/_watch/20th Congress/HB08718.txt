                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

       In the Philippines, the campus press has served as a bastion of democracy,
preserving freedom of speech and expression inside and outside our
nation’s campuses.

       Campus journalism is believed to have started in the country in 1890 when
the University of Santo Tomas (UST) published El Liliputiense during the Spanish
colonial period.1 However, other historical accounts credit the start of the formal
campus journalism to the publication of The College Folio, now The Philippine
Collegian, by the University of the Philippines (UP) in 1910 during the American
occupation. 2 In the Spanish, American, and Japanese occupations, campus
journalists stood bravely against repressive conditions to expose the grave realities
and abuses of the colonial governments.

       During Martial Law under the late dictator Ferdinand Marcos, Sr., the
State ordered the censorship and closure of major publications, television
networks, and radio stations. In response, campus publications emerged and
served as the alternative press – refuting State propaganda, exposing the
excesses and injustices of the oppressive Marcos government, and reporting on the
grim conditions to which Filipino people were subjected at the time. In fact, the first
female and student activist to die in detention during Martial Law was a campus
journalist named Liliosa Hilao.3

        Student publications serve as tangible pieces of evidence of students’ exercise
of their Constitutionally-guaranteed rights to freedom of speech and expression and
freedom of the press. Unlike the mainstream press, whose operations primarily
depend on ad placements, student publications are collectively managed and financed
by students.

        As guardians of the truth and democracy, and in service of the interest of the
students and the Filipino people, student publications cover a wide array of topics,
such as the incessant and unabated tuition and other fee increases, repressive student
policies, cases of abuse and sexual harassment within educational institutions, unfair
labor practices of school administrations, human rights violations, disregard for
national sovereignty, corruption in government, and various assaults to the rights of
the people, among others.

       Thus, student journalists often come into conflict with institutions who use their
authority to quell free speech and expression and censor the free press. Throughout
history, many student editors and writers have been persecuted. Incidents of
harassment were particularly high during the 1960s to the 1980s when nationalist
ideals were perceived as threats to the government.

       Therefore, on July 5, 1991, Republic Act No. 7079, otherwise known as the
Campus Journalism Act of 1991, was enacted with the aim to uphold and protect the
freedom of the press at the campus level and to promote the development and growth
of campus journalism. Unfortunately, despite the good intentions behind the crafting
and passage of the law, Republic Act No. 7079 has been proven to be seriously flawed
and riddled with deficiencies to fully maintain the existence of campus press, and to
protect the rights and welfare of student journalists.

       Among its glaring flaws are the following:

          1) It legalizes the non-mandatory collection of the publication fee, the
             lifeblood of most student publications. It only provides a provision that
             enumerates the sources where the student publication funds may be
             taken.
          2) It does not make it mandatory for all colleges and universities in the
             Philippines to establish student publications. Neither does the said law
             require that those student publications that remain closed until the
             present be reopened for the benefit of the students.
          3) It does not contain a penalty clause, leaving erring administrations
             unscathed from violations. School administrations are able to commit
             offense after offense without suffering any consequence.

      The aforementioned flaws and deficiencies in the law resulted in even more
campus press freedom violations that have become even wilier and devious in form.
Although information today is disseminated through more advanced and accessible

                                           2
platforms like social media, student journalists continue to face the same challenges
in harassment and censorship from the school administration and even red-tagging
and surveillance from state forces like the NTF-ELCAC.

        According to the College Editors Guild of the Philippines (CEGP), the oldest
intercollegiate alliance of student publications in the country, at least 206 cases of
campus press freedom violations have been recorded from 2023 to 2024, including
instances of administrative offices “meddling” with student papers’ coverage of
campus issues.4 Among the systemic assaults faced by student publications are the
following: (1) harassment of student writers and editors, (2) meddling with editorial
policies, (3) actual censorship of editorial content, (4) withholding of publication funds,
(5) non-collection of publication fee, (6) padlocking of the publication office, (7) closure
of the student publication, (8) suspension and expulsion of student editors and writers,
and (9) filing of libel charges against them.5

       This bill seeks to repeal Republic Act No. 7079 and to provide remedy by
undertaking various programs and projects aimed at empowering student journalists.
In particular, the bill seeks to afford the student press with a consistent and reliable
source of funds, provide them with in-depth training, and more importantly, bestow
them with the freedom and protections to determine the content of their publication.

      In the 17th and 18th Congresses, this Representation filed similar bills in the
Senate. In the 20th Congress, a similar bill was filed by Representatives Renee Co and
Antonio Tinio in the House of Representatives. This bill largely adopts this
Representation’s SBN 1868 (17th Congress) and SBN 1524 (18th Congress), and
Representatives Co and Tinio’s HBN 4172 (20th Congress).

      For the strengthening of democracy and the promotion of free speech and
expression, and the free press, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 1         SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Campus Press
 2   Freedom Act.”
 3
 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to promote
 5   the development and growth of campus journalism as a means of encouraging critical
 6   and creative thinking and as a beacon of nationalism and democracy inside and
 7   outside the nation’s campuses.
 8
 9          The State also firmly recognizes the historic role and contribution of the campus
10   press to press freedom in the country, in providing a free and safe venue for the
11   discussion of student concerns, and in promoting social consciousness and defending
12   the interests and welfare of the Filipino people by tackling both national and sectoral
13   issues in its pages. The State acknowledges that the campus press, as part of the
14   media, is an important institution in a democratic society that promotes this
15   constitutionally guaranteed right to press freedom.
16
17          The education of the students shall be of paramount concern. Towards this end,
18   the State shall ensure that the practice of campus journalism shall neither interfere
19   with nor affect the regular schooling of the students, especially at elementary and
20   secondary levels.

 3   to reopen all closed student publications, to establish student publications in schools

 7         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For the purposes of this Act, the following are
 8   hereby defined as follows:
 9
10             a) “Editor-in-Chief” – the publication’s primary editor chosen through a
11                competitive and fair selection process. The process of selecting the
12                editor-in-chief shall be determined by the student body;
13
14             b) “Editorial Board” – a body composed of student journalists who have
15                qualified in placement examinations and shall be primarily in charge of
16                setting the tone of the paper in accordance with the thrust of the
17                publication. The editorial board may include a publication adviser at the
18                option of its members;
19
20             c) “Editorial Policies” – a set of guidelines by which a student publication is
21                operated and managed, taking into account pertinent laws as well as the
22                school administration’s reasonable policies. The said guidelines, which
23                shall be created and determined by the editorial board, shall determine
24                the frequency of the publication, the manner of selecting articles and
25                features, and other similar matters;
26
27             d) “School” – an institution for learning in the elementary, secondary, or
28                tertiary level composed of the student body, administration, faculty, and
29                non-faculty personnel;
30
31             e) “Student” – any person enrolled in elementary, secondary, post-
32                secondary, tertiary, graduate, and post-graduate levels, including those
33                enrolled in vocational and technical education;
34
35             f) “Student Journalist” – any bona fide student enrolled for the current
36                semester or term, who has passed or met the qualifications and
37                standards of the editorial board;
38
39             g) “Student Publication” – the issue of any printed and/or online material,
40                such as, but not limited to, newspapers, wall news, literary folios,
41                newsletters, and other similar forms, independently published by, and
42                which meets the needs and interests of, the students.

 1          SEC. 4. Student Publication. – All educational institutions in the elementary,
 2   secondary, and tertiary levels, public or private, shall be mandated to establish a
 3   student publication. Every school shall have at least one (1) student publication without
 4   prejudice to other publications established within the campus. A student publication
 5   shall be published by the student body through an editorial board composed of
 6   students selected through fair and competitive examinations and selection process.
 7
 8         All student publications whose operations ceased upon the directives of the
 9   school administration shall be revived and allowed to operate again.
10
11         Once the publication is established or reopened, the editorial board shall freely
12   determine its editorial policies and manage the publication’s funds.
13
14          SEC. 5. Powers and Functions of the Editorial Board. – The editorial board shall
15   have the power to select staff members of the publication, granted that the student
16   journalists the board selected are able to pass a qualifying examination.
17
18          The editorial board shall be primarily accountable and responsible for the
19   operation of the student publication and the management of its funds. At the end of
20   each semester or term, as the case may be, the editorial board shall prepare a report
21   on the disbursement of funds, subject to accounting and auditing rules. Such report
22   shall be posted at the school’s bulletin board or published in the school publication.
23
24          SEC. 6. Operations of Student Publications. – The school administration shall
25   provide the student publication with an office, preferably located at the school’s student
26   center, and other materials and equipment necessary for its operations.
27
28          SEC. 7. Independence of Student Publications. – The school publication shall
29   be free from any form of intervention from the school owners or administration,
30   advertisers, or donors with regard to the content of the articles the editorial board
31   chooses to publish, and the selection of its publication staff and members of the
32   editorial board. However, for elementary and secondary institutions, the school
33   administration may prevent the publication of materials that are libelous, oppressive,
34   or otherwise oppressive to generally accepted ethical standards of journalism.
35
36         The editorial board for higher educational institutions shall have full autonomy
37   on the handling of its funds, subject to audit from the school administration. For
38   elementary and secondary institutions, the faculty adviser shall be responsible for
39   preparing the budget and handling the funds along with the editorial board, likewise
40   subject to audit from the school administration.
41
42         The operation of the student publications shall not be delayed, suspended, or
43   closed down in connection with the articles it has published, or on the basis of the
44   conduct or performance of its staff, without due process of law.

 2          SEC. 8. Funding for Student Publications. – Funding for the student publication
 3   shall be sourced primarily from student publication fees collected by the school
 4   administration. It shall be mandatory for the school administration to collect reasonable
 5   student publication/subscription fees during the enrollment period.
 6
 7           For public schools, a publication fund shall be included in the budget of the
 8   institutions.
 9
10         Secondary sources of publication funds shall include the savings of the
11   respective school’s appropriations and donations.
12
13           For this purpose, the number of bona fide students enrolled for the current
14   semester or term shall be used as basis for the amount of publication funds that shall
15   be released by the school administration who shall issue a list of the students enrolled
16   for the current semester or term to the editorial board.
17
18           SEC. 9. Transmittal of Funds. – The school administration shall effect the
19   automatic release of the student subscription fees to the student publication within
20   fifteen (15) days after the last day of enrollment, without need of request or demand
21   from the editorial board or any member of the student body. The publication funds
22   shall be deposited in a trust fund or in the account of the student publication in an
23   authorized depository bank.
24
25         In no instance shall the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), Technical
26   Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and the Department of
27   Education (DepEd), as the case may be, or the school administration concerned,
28   withhold the release of funds intended for the student publication.
29
30          SEC. 10. Publication Adviser. – The editorial board of student publications in
31   higher educational institutions shall have the discretion to appoint a faculty adviser. In
32   case the student publication in a higher educational institution decides to appoint a
33   faculty adviser, the editorial board shall have the power to choose its faculty adviser,
34   whose functions shall be limited merely to technical assistance. The technical
35   assistance of the faculty adviser shall be limited to the sound practice of journalism.
36
37         However, the faculty adviser for elementary and secondary institutions shall be
38   mandatory. They shall ensure that the editorial board maintains proper journalistic
39   standards and that the publication funds are properly spent and accounted for.
40
41           SEC. 11. Security of Tenure. – A member of the publication staff shall maintain
42   their status as a student in order to retain membership in the publication staff. The
43   student journalist shall not be suspended, expelled, or punished with administrative

 2   articles constitute a violation of the law.

 4          SEC. 12. Printing of the Student Publication. – The printing of the student
 5   publication shall be conducted by the editorial board and the student publication staff
 6   through canvass or public bidding. For public schools, the student publication shall be
 7   exempt from undergoing bidding process under Republic Act No. 12009, also known
 8   as the New Government Procurement Act, with regard to the selection of a printing
 9   press and the purchase of equipment and materials necessary for its operations. The
10   editorial board shall freely choose the printing press for the student publication.
11
12           SEC. 13. Press Conferences and Training Seminars. – The Commission on
13   Higher Education (CHED), the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority
14   (TESDA), and the Department of Education (DepEd), in partnership with the media
15   and campus press organizations and alliances, shall sponsor periodic competitions,
16   press conferences, and training seminars in which student editors/writers and teacher-
17   advisers of student publications in the elementary, secondary, and tertiary levels shall
18   participate. Such competitions, conferences, and seminars shall be held at the
19   institutional, divisional, and regional levels, culminating with the holding of the annual
20   National School Press Conference for elementary, secondary, and tertiary levels in
21   places of historical and/or cultural interest in the country.
22
23         SEC. 14. Power to Investigate. – The CHED, TESDA, and DepEd, as the case
24   may be, shall be granted the power to investigate any violation of this Act and its
25   implementing rules and regulations.
26
27          SEC. 15. Administrative Sanctions against Campus Press Freedom Violations.
28   – The CHED, TESDA, and DepEd, as the case may be, shall impose administrative
29   sanctions for campus press freedom violations, including, but not limited to:
30
31             a) interrogation and intimidation of editors and staff members, and other
32                forms of harassment by the school administration, faculty members, and
33                the military, such as, but not limited to, sending of death threats, filing of
34                libel cases against student journalists, and the issuance of derogatory
35                and libelous public statements against student journalists;
36             b) meddling of school administration with editorial policies;
37             c) censorship of editorial content, or any other form of prior restraint on
38                lawful content;
39             d) withholding and non-collection of publication fees;
40             e) expulsion and suspension of student editors and writers without due
41                process;
42             f) stopping or suspending the operations of the school publication, such
43                as, but not limited to, the outright closure of the publication, locking of

 2                 other utilities of the publication office;
 3              g) non-establishment of at least one (1) campus publication for every
 4                 school;
 5              h) refusal to reopen closed student publications; or
 6              i) any other manner that commits any act to defeat any provision of this
 7                 Act.

 9          SEC. 16. Penalties. – Any person who shall willfully interfere with, restrain, or
10   coerce any student publication/student journalist in the exercise of its/their functions
11   and rights guaranteed by this Act, or who shall in any other manner commit any act to
12   defeat any provision of this Act shall, upon conviction, be punished by a fine of not
13   less than One hundred fifty thousand pesos (P150,000.000) but not more than Two
14   hundred fifty thousand pesos (P250,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than one (1)
15   year but not more than five (5) years, or both at the discretion of the court.
16
17           If the offender is an educational institution or a juridical person, the penalty shall
18   be imposed upon the president, treasurer, secretary, or any other officer responsible
19   for the violation.
20
21         SEC. 17. Legal Assistance to Campus Journalists. – The CHED, TESDA, and
22   DepEd, as the case may be, shall provide legal assistance to student journalists in all
23   cases herein cited.
24
25         SEC. 18. Reportorial Requirements. – The CHED, TESDA, and DepEd shall
26   submit an annual report to both Houses of Congress on the status of the
27   implementation of the law, including violations thereof and the disposition of cases.
28
29          SEC. 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The CHED, TESDA, and
30   DepEd, in close consultation and coordination with student and campus press
31   organizations and all other concerned sectors, shall promulgate within sixty (60) days
32   from effectivity of this Act the rules and regulations necessary for its implementation.
33
34          SEC. 20. Separability Clause. – If any section or provision of this Act is declared

36   continue to be in full force and effect.

38           SEC. 21. Repealing Clause. – Republic Act No. 7079, entitled “An Act Providing
39   for the Development and Promotion of Campus Journalism,” is hereby repealed.
