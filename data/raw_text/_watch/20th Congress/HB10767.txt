                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Cancer remains one of the most serious public health challenges faced by the
Philippines.

       According to the 2022 GLOBOCAN estimates of the International
Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC)1, the Philippines recorded an
estimated 188,976 new cancer cases and 113,369 cancer deaths in 2022, with
approximately 466,012 five-year prevalent cancer cases. Breast cancer was the
most commonly diagnosed cancer among Filipino women, with an age-
standardized incidence rate (ASIR) of 60.34 per 100,000, the second-highest
rate in Southeast Asia after Singapore (72.61 per 100,000). Among Filipino
men, lung cancer was the most commonly diagnosed cancer, while lung
cancer was also the leading cause of cancer death when both sexes were
considered together. Among Filipino men, lung cancer had an age-
standardized mortality rate (ASMR) of 33.59 per 100,000— the highest male
lung-cancer mortality rate among the Southeast Asian countries included in
the comparison2.

       These figures underscore not only the scale of the disease burden but
also the urgency of ensuring that Filipinos, regardless of where they live, can
access timely diagnosis and treatment.

       In 2019, Republic Act (RA) No. 11215, otherwise known as the National
Integrated Cancer Control Act (NICCA), was a landmark law that established
the Philippine Cancer Center. This mandated the creation of regional and

specialty cancer centers outside Metro Manila, and created the Cancer
Assistance Fund (CAF) to help cover the cost of cancer medicines, treatment,
and care. However, more than five years since its enactment, access to cancer
care remains heavily concentrated in the National Capital Region (NCR). Of
the 11 facilities currently recognized under the Network of DOH-Designated
Advanced Comprehensive Cancer Centers, six are located in Metro Manila
alone, while only five are distributed across the rest of Luzon, the Visayas,
and Mindanao combined3. This means that patients from many regions must
still travel, often at great financial and emotional cost, to Metro Manila or to
another distant urban center simply to receive a diagnosis, undergo
chemotherapy or radiotherapy, or access follow-up care.

        This travel burden is not merely an inconvenience. Studies4 on the
economic impact of cancer in Southeast Asia have documented that a
significant share of patients and their families face catastrophic health
expenditure with costs exceeding a large portion of household income. This
is only within 12 months of a cancer diagnosis, a burden that is compounded
by transportation, lodging, and lost income when patients must seek
treatment far from home. For indigent and geographically isolated patients,
this often means delayed treatment, abandonment of care, or, in the worst
cases, forgoing treatment altogether.

       While the CAF under RA No. 11215 already provides financial
assistance for the direct cost of cancer treatment, the law as currently written
does not clearly authorize the use of the Fund for the construction, expansion,
upgrading, and equipping of the very regional cancer centers that RA 11215
itself mandated to be established. This has left the expansion of regional
cancer care infrastructure dependent on ad hoc appropriations, with no
dedicated, sustained, and properly classified funding stream for capital
investment in these facilities.

      This bill, to be known as the "Cancer Patients' Welfare Act," seeks to
bridge this gap by:

       1. Expanding the allowable use of the Cancer Assistance Fund to
          explicitly include the construction, expansion, upgrading, and
          equipping of regional cancer centers, by splitting the Fund into two
          distinct windows: a Treatment Assistance Window for direct
          patient care costs, and a new Infrastructure Development Window

          currently have no comprehensive cancer care facility;

       2. Establishing clear, transparent prioritization criteria for selecting
          sites for infrastructure development, based on objective factors
          such as cancer incidence and mortality data from the national
          cancer registry, population size, geographic accessibility, existing
          health system readiness, and local government support;

       3. Providing for proper budgetary classification of the Fund, treating
          the Infrastructure Development Window as Capital Outlay
          separate from Maintenance and Other Operating Expenses, while
          creating a distinct Treatment, Assistance, Medicines, and
          Procedures (TAMP) Sub-Window to ring-fence funds intended
          directly for patient treatment and medicines from administrative
          and operating costs; and allowing multi-year obligational authority
          so that infrastructure projects are not derailed by the annual
          character of government appropriations; and

       4. Formally recognizing regional cancer centers as a social protection
          mechanism under Section 22 of RA No. 11215, in the same manner
          as PhilHealth, the Malasakit Program, and other assistance
          mechanisms, in acknowledgment of the fact that a regional cancer
          center built close to where patients live directly reduces the travel,
          lodging, and lost-income burden that keeps many Filipinos from
          completing life-saving treatment.

   By ensuring that funding exists not only for treatment but also for building
the facilities where that treatment is delivered, this bill takes a concrete step
toward the equitable, nationwide access to cancer care envisioned under RA
No. 11215 and the Universal Health Care Act.

   In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT AMENDING SECTIONS 20 AND 22 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO.
 11215, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “NATIONAL INTEGRATED
CANCER CONTROL ACT (NICCA),” TO EXPAND THE ALLOWABLE
   USE OF THE CANCER ASSISTANCE FUND TO INCLUDE THE
CONSTRUCTION, EXPANSION, UPGRADING, AND EQUIPPING OF
REGIONAL CANCER CENTERS, PROVIDING FOR ITS BUDGETARY
  CLASSIFICATION, AND RECOGNIZING REGIONAL CANCER
 CENTERS AS A SOCIAL PROTECTION MECHANISM, AND FOR
                    OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title.- This Act shall be known as the “Cancer
Patients’ Welfare Act”

       SEC. 2. Section 20 of Republic Act No. 11215 is hereby amended to
read as follows:

      “SEC. 20. Cancer Assistance Fund. — There is hereby created a Cancer
      Assistance Fund (CAF) to support the cancer medicine and treatment
      assistance program, AND THE CONSTRUCTION, EXPANSION,
      UPGRADING, AND EQUIPPING OF REGIONAL CANCER
      CENTERS ESTABLISHED UNDER SECTION 12 OF THIS ACT.

      “The Cancer Assistance Fund and PhilHealth benefits shall be made
      available in public and private DOH-licensed cancer centers. The DOH
      and PhilHealth shall prescribe, in consultation with stakeholders, the
      coverage rates and applicable rules on options to charge co-payment
      for services rendered under the Treatment Assistance Window
      described below. Processes to avail of such funding shall be
      streamlined to ensure timely provision of cancer care.

      “THE FUND SHALL BE COMPOSED OF TWO (2) SEPARATE AND
      DISTINCT WINDOWS:
                                                                            4
      “(A) TREATMENT ASSISTANCE WINDOW. — THIS WINDOW
      SHALL BE UTILIZED EXCLUSIVELY TO FUND THE COST OF
      CANCER PREVENTION, SCREENING, DIAGNOSIS, TREATMENT,
      AND CARE-RELATED COMPONENTS FOR CANCER PATIENTS,
      PERSONS LIVING WITH CANCER, AND CANCER SURVIVORS,
      CONSISTENT WITH EXISTING RULES ISSUED BY THE DOH; AND

      “(B) INFRASTRUCTURE DEVELOPMENT WINDOW. — THIS
      WINDOW SHALL BE UTILIZED EXCLUSIVELY FOR THE
      CONSTRUCTION, EXPANSION, RENOVATION, UPGRADING,
      AND EQUIPPING OF REGIONAL CANCER CENTERS, CANCER
      CARE CENTERS, AND CANCER TREATMENT UNITS
      ESTABLISHED UNDER SECTIONS 10, 11, AND 12 OF THIS ACT,
      GIVING PRIORITY TO REGIONS WITHOUT AN EXISTING
      COMPREHENSIVE CANCER CARE FACILITY.

      “THE DEPARTMENT OF BUDGET AND MANAGEMENT, IN
      COORDINATION WITH THE DOH, SHALL ISSUE THE
      NECESSARY BUDGETARY CLASSIFICATION, RELEASE, AND
      UTILIZATION     GUIDELINES    GOVERNING       THE
      INFRASTRUCTURE DEVELOPMENT WINDOW, INCLUDING ITS
      TREATMENT AS CAPITAL OUTLAY, DISTINCT FROM THE
      MAINTENANCE    AND   OTHER   OPERATING   EXPENSES
      CLASSIFICATION  APPLICABLE  TO   THE   TREATMENT
      ASSISTANCE WINDOW.

      “UNLESS OTHERWISE PROVIDED IN THE GENERAL
      APPROPRIATIONS ACT, NOT MORE THAN FIFTY PERCENT (50%)
      OF THE TOTAL ANNUAL APPROPRIATION FOR THE CANCER
      ASSISTANCE FUND SHALL BE ALLOCATED TO THE
      INFRASTRUCTURE DEVELOPMENT WINDOW IN ANY GIVEN
      FISCAL YEAR.”

     SEC. 3. A new Section 20-A is hereby inserted after Section 20 of
Republic Act No. 11215, to read as follows:

      “SEC. 20-A. Regional Cancer Center Prioritization Criteria. — The
      DOH, through the Disease Prevention and Control Bureau (DPCB), in
      consultation with the Regional Development Councils (RDCs) and the
      National Integrated Cancer Control Council (NICCC), shall prescribe
      the criteria for prioritizing sites for infrastructure development under
      the Infrastructure Development Window of the Cancer Assistance
      Fund, which shall include, but not be limited to:

      “(a) The absence or inadequacy of an existing DOH-licensed
      comprehensive cancer care facility within the region;
      “(b) Cancer incidence and mortality data for the region, based on the
      national cancer registry;

                                                                            5
      “(c) Population size and geographic accessibility to existing cancer
      centers;
      “(d) Existing health system readiness, including available human
      resources for health and referral networks; and
      “(e) Land availability, site suitability, and local government unit
      counterpart support, where applicable.

     SEC. 4. A new Section 20-B is hereby inserted after Section 20-A of
Republic Act No. 11215, to read as follows:

      “SEC. 20-B. Budgetary Classification and Multi-Year Obligational
      Authority. —
      “(a) For purposes of budget preparation, execution, and accounting,
      amounts appropriated for the Treatment Assistance Window shall be
      classified as Maintenance and Other Operating Expenses (MOOE)
      under the Unified Accounts Code Structure (UACS), while amounts
      appropriated for the Infrastructure Development Window shall be
      classified as Capital Outlays (CO), consistent with the budgetary
      classification rules of the Department of Budget and Management
      (DBM): Provided, That within the MOOE classification for the
      Treatment Assistance Window, a separate and distinct sub-allotment
      class or expense window, to be known as the Treatment, Assistance,
      Medicines, and Procedures (TAMP) Sub-Window, shall be established
      exclusively for the payment of treatment costs, financial assistance to
      patients, purchase of medicines, and other costs of medical, surgical,
      or diagnostic procedures: Provided, further, That the TAMP Sub-
      Window shall be maintained as a separate budget line item, distinct
      from administrative, operating, and other MOOE expenditures of the
      Treatment Assistance Window, and shall be reflected as such in the
      General Appropriations Act (GAA), agency budget matrix, and other
      budget accountability reports: Provided, finally, That the DBM shall
      issue the necessary UACS sub-object codes or equivalent classification
      to enable separate tracking, monitoring, and reporting of TAMP Sub-
      Window disbursements apart from other MOOE items."

      “(b) Notwithstanding the annual character of appropriations under the
      General Appropriations Act (GAA), amounts released for the
      Infrastructure Development Window for the construction, expansion,
      or upgrading of Regional Cancer Centers shall be treated as continuing
      appropriations, obligatable for a period not exceeding two (2) years
      from the date such amounts were appropriated, consistent with the
      General Provisions of the applicable GAA governing capital outlays.

      “(c) For Regional Cancer Center projects requiring implementation
      beyond a single fiscal year, the DOH may, subject to the approval of
      the DBM, apply for Multi-Year Obligational Authority (MYOA)
      pursuant to prevailing DBM budget circulars, to allow phased
      implementation of infrastructure projects across fiscal years, without
      prejudice to annual reporting and audit requirements.

                                                                           6
      “(d) All procurement of civil works, goods, and consulting services for
      projects funded under the Infrastructure Development Window shall
      be subject to Republic Act No. 9184, otherwise known as the
      ‘Government Procurement Reform Act,’ and its Revised Implementing
      Rules and Regulations.

      “(e) The DOH and the DBM shall jointly issue, within ninety (90) days
      from the effectivity of this Act, a circular specifying the applicable
      UACS object codes, obligation and disbursement procedures, Program
      of Works and detailed engineering design requirements, and reporting
      requirements applicable to the Infrastructure Development Window.

      “(f) All infrastructure projects funded under the Infrastructure
      Development Window shall be subject to the standard rules of the
      Commission on Audit (COA) applicable to government infrastructure
      projects, including submission of as-built plans and completion
      reports.”

       SEC. 5. Section 22 of Republic Act No. 11215 is hereby amended to
read as follows:

      “SEC. 22. Social Protection Mechanisms. — The DOH, in collaboration
      with the Social Security System (SSS), Government Service Insurance
      System (GSIS), Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO),
      Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), Department of Social
      Welfare and Development (DSWD), PhilHealth and local government
      units (LGUs), shall develop appropriate and easily accessible social
      protection mechanisms for cancer patients, people living with cancer,
      cancer survivors, their families and carers. It shall aim to encourage the
      underprivileged and marginalized people living with cancer to
      undergo the necessary treatment and care.

      “TOWARD THIS END, THE ESTABLISHMENT OF REGIONAL
      CANCER CENTERS UNDER SECTION 12 OF THIS ACT SHALL BE
      RECOGNIZED AS A PRIMARY SOCIAL PROTECTION
      MECHANISM, AS IT REDUCES THE TRAVEL, LODGING, AND
      LOST-INCOME BURDEN BORNE BY CANCER PATIENTS AND
      THEIR FAMILIES WHO WOULD OTHERWISE NEED TO SEEK
      TREATMENT IN METRO MANILA OR OTHER DISTANT URBAN
      CENTERS. REGIONAL CANCER CENTERS FUNDED UNDER THE
      INFRASTRUCTURE DEVELOPMENT WINDOW OF THE CANCER
      ASSISTANCE FUND SHALL INTEGRATE PATIENT NAVIGATION
      SERVICES, MEDICAL SOCIAL WORK SUPPORT, AND REFERRAL
      LINKAGES WITH THE MALASAKIT PROGRAM, PHILHEALTH,
      AND LOCAL SOCIAL WELFARE AND DEVELOPMENT OFFICES,
      TO   ENSURE    THAT   INDIGENT   AND    FINANCIALLY-
      INCAPACITATED PATIENTS ARE ABLE TO AVAIL OF SERVICES
      AT THE NEAREST APPROPRIATE FACILITY WITHOUT NEEDING
      TO TRAVEL OUTSIDE THEIR REGION.”

       SEC. 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations.- Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in coordination with the Department
of Budget and Management (DBM) and the National Integrated Cancer
Control Council (NICCC), shall promulgate the rules and regulations
necessary to implement this Act.

      SEC. 7. Appropriations.- The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s
appropriation for the Cancer Assistance Fund under the General
Appropriations Act (GAA). Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its
continued implementation shall be included in the annual GAA.

      SEC. 8. Separability Clause.- If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 9. Repealing Clause.- All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, and other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the
provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

      SEC. 10. Effectivity.- This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
