EXPLANATORY NOTE

This bill seeks to declare as unlawful the making and dissemination of hoax
threats of mass violence, false active shooter reports, and related malicious pranks
in academic institutions and public spaces, and to provide penalties therefor.

The Constitution recognizes the vital role of youth in nation-building and
explicitly charges the State with protecting their physical, moral, spiritual,
intellectual, and social well-being. Crucial to this duty is ensuring that educational
institutions remain safe, secure, and conducive environments for learning.

In recent years, schools, universities, malls, transport terminals. and other
public spaces across the country have repeatedly been disrupted by hoax reports
of mass shootings, bombings, and other acts of violence. These false alarms -—
whether spread through social media, text messages, phone calls, or word of
mouth — force the evacuation of buildings, the deployment of law enforcement
and school personnel, and the diversion of scarce security resources, all in
response to a threat that does not exist.

While Presidential Decree No. 1727, otherwise known as the "Anti-Bomb
Joke Law.” already penalizes the malicious dissemination of false bomb threats,
existing law does not squarely address hoax threats involving firearms, active
shooter scenarios, and other forms of mass violence that do not involve
explosives. This gap has allowed perpetrators — often students or minors acting

===== [page 2] =====
as a "prank" — to escape appropriate liability, or has forced prosecutors to stretch
other penal provisions, such as unjust vexation or the alarms and scandals
provisions of the Revised Penal Code, that were never designed for threats of this
scale and impact.

This bill seeks to close that gap by specifically criminalizing hoax threats
of mass violence and false active shooter reports, whether made in academic
institutions or in other public spaces.

The bill defines the prohibited acts, fixes penalties calibrated to be
consistent with, and not in conflict with, the penalties already imposed under
other special penal laws of similar nature. It also provides for graduated, current,
and proportionate penalties depending on the gravity of the disruption caused and
the offender's age.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill 1s earnestly
sought.

AN ACT

DECLARING AS UNLAWFUL THE MAKING AND DISSEMINATION
OF HOAX THREATS OF MASS VIOLENCE, FALSE ACTIVE
SHOOTER REPORTS, AND RELATED MALICIOUS PRANKS IN
ACADEMIC INSTITUTIONS AND PUBLIC SPACES, AND PROVIDING
PENALTIES THEREFOR
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. --- This Act shall be known as the "Anti-Hoax
Threats of Mass Violence Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. --- \t is the policy of the State to protect and
promote the right of the people to a safe, secure, and orderly environment in
schools, universities, and other public spaces, and to ensure that the resources of

law enforcement, school administrators, and emergency responders are not

needlessly diverted by false reports of violence.

===== [page 4] =====
Toward this end, the State shall treat the making and dissemination of hoax
threats of mass violence, false active shooter reports, and related malicious pranks
as serious offenses against public order and safety, and provide penalties
consistent with and complementary to existing penal laws penalizing false bomb
threats and similar acts.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. --- For purposes of this Act:

(a) “Academic institution” refers to any public or private school, college,
or university, or other learning institution, including its buildings,
grounds, covered courts, dormitories, canteens, school-transport

vehicles, and immediate premises;

(b) "Disruption" refers to any consequence of a prohibited act under this
Act, including the evacuation, lockdown, or closure of a place; the
suspension of classes, examinations, work, or business operations, the
deployment or diversion of police, fire, medical, or rescue personnel
and equipment; the initiation of a bomb-search, evacuation, or tactical
response operation; the disruption of an ongoing public activity; or the
infliction of physical injury or death upon any person, whether resulting

from panic, a crush, a fall, a medical episode, or a response operation;

(c) "Emergency Response Agency" refers to the Philippine National Police

(PNP), including the Special Action Force (SAF) and the Anti-

===== [page 5] =====
Cybercrime Group, the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP), the Philippine
Coast Guard (PCG), the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), the
Office of Civil Defense (OCD), and the National Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) and its local

counterparts; and

(d) "False active shooter report" refers to any false statement, information,
in written, oral, electronic, or digital form, or by any other means,
reporting or announcing that a person is present in an academic
institution or public space, to commit an act of mass violence or is about
to shoot at persons in such public place or academic institution, whether
or not the shooter is identified or a weapon described, knowing the

same to be false;

(e) "High-density or sensitive area" refers to any academic institution,
hospital, airport, seaport, mass transit station, power or water facility,
government seat, or place where no fewer than three hundred (300)

persons are ordinarily present at the same time;

(f) "Hoax threat of mass violence" refers to any false statement,
information, report, or communication in written, oral, electronic, or
digital form, announcing, reporting, threatening, or insinuating an

intention or plan to commit an act of mass violence or mass casualty

===== [page 6] =====
attack, including the discharge of firearms or other deadly weapons;
arson; stabbing, poisoning, or chemical, biological, radiological, or
nuclear attack; or any other act likely to cause death or serious physical

injury to multiple persons, knowing the same to be false;

(g) "Information and Communications Technology" or "ICT" refers to ICT

as defined in Republic Act (RA) No. 10175, otherwise known as the

"Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012;"

(h) “Public place" refers to any place accessible to the general public, or a

(i)

substantial part thereof, including malls and commercial
establishments, hospitals and clinics, places of worship, airports and
seaports, land transportation terminals, railways and mass transit
systems and stations, government offices, hotels, parks, and other

venues of public assembly; and

"Related malicious prank" refers to any act or utterance not covered
under Presidential Decree (PD) No. 1727, entitled “Declaring as
Unlawful the Malicious Dissemination of False Information or the
Willful Making of Any Threat Concerning Bombs, Explosives or any
Similar Device or Means of Destruction and Imposing Penalties
Thereof,” done with intent to cause public alarm, panic, or disruption,

consisting of the dissemination of false information such as staged

videos, fabricated social media posts, or manufactured physical
evidence made with the intent to create the false impression that an act
of mass violence is imminent or ongoing, thereby causing public

alarm, panic, or disruption.
SEC. 4. Prohibited Acts. — It shall be unlawful for any person to:

(a) Willfully make, utter, transmit, disseminate, publish, post, or share, or
cause to be disseminated, any hoax threat of mass violence or false active shooter
report, by word of mouth or through the use of the mail, telephone, text message,
electronic mail, social media, messaging applications, online forums, printed
materials, or any other instrument, platform, or means of communication,

knowing, the same to be false;

(b) Willfully make, utter, transmit, or disseminate any information

constituting a related malicious prank, knowing the same to be false;

(c) Solicit, induce, conspire with, employ, or pay another to commit any
act penalized under this Act, or knowingly assist or materially contribute to its

commission, as principal, accomplice, or accessory:

(d) Make, prepare, plant, place, display, or fabricate any object, device,
drawing, writing, sign, or symbol, in a public place or academic institution, in

such manner and under such circumstances as would lead a reasonable person to

===== [page 8] =====
believe that a firearm or other deadly weapon is present or that an act of mass
violence is imminent, with specific, credible assertion of intent to cause public
alarm or disruption, other than any device, explosive, or incendiary matter

penalized under PD No. 1727 and existing explosives laws; and

(e) Report or relay to any person, emergency hotline, or emergency
response agency any information known to be false under paragraphs (a), (b), and

(d), whether or not the person was the original source thereof.

SEC. 5. Penalties. — Any person found guilty of any of the prohibited

acts under Section 4 shall be penalized as follows:

(a) The penalty of prision correccional in its maximum period, or
imprisonment of four (4) years, two (2) months, and one (1) day to six (6) years,
or a fine of not more than Two Hundred Thousand pesos (P200,000.00), or both
such imprisonment and fine at the discretion of the court, upon conviction where

the prohibited act caused no disruption;

(b) The penalty of prision mayor in its minimum period, or imprisonment
of six (6) years and one (1) day to eight (8) years, or a fine of not more than Five
Hundred Thousand pesos (P500,000.00), or both such imprisonment and fine at
the discretion of the court, upon conviction where the prohibited act caused any
form of disruption as defined in Section 3(b), or where it was directed at a high-

density or sensitive area;

===== [page 9] =====
(c) The penalty of prision mayor in its medium period, or imprisonment
of eight (8) years and one (1) day to ten (10) years, and a fine of not more than
One Million pesos (P1,000,000.00), upon conviction where the prohibited act

caused the death of, or serious physical injuries to, any person;

(d) Where the offender is a minor, the provisions of RA 9344, or the
"Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006," as amended, shall apply, without
prejudice to appropriate intervention, diversion, and rehabilitation programs, and
without prejudice to the civil and administrative liability of the minor's parents or

guardians under existing law.

In all cases involving a minor offender, the parents or legal guardians shall
be liable for the civil damages, including the costs of the emergency response,
evacuation, and restoration of services occasioned by the prohibited act, in
accordance with the governing provisions of Executive Order No. 209 or the

“Family Code of the Philippines,” as amended;

(e) The imposition of the penalties herein shall be without prejudice to the
filing of appropriate administrative, civil, or other criminal actions under the

Revised Penal Code, RA 10175, RA 9344, and other applicable law; and

(f) The penalty next higher in degree shall be imposed upon any person

who commits any act penalized under this Act while publicly identifying oneself

===== [page 10] =====
with, or purporting to act in the name of, any academic institution, government
office, emergency response agency, or news organization; who commits any act
penalized under this Act against any person, place, or institution with which the
offender has or had a relationship of employment, enrollment, teaching, coaching,
guardianship, or tenancy, where the act is done in retaliation for any actual or
perceived grievance arising from such relationship; or any person previously

convicted of an offense penalized under this Act or under PD No. 1727.

In no case shall the penalty imposed under this Act exceed prision mayor
in its medium period, and in no case shall the accused be denied bail as a matter
of right.

SEC. 6. Offenses Committed Through ICT. — Where any offense
penalized under this Act is committed by, through, or with the use of information
and communications technology, the penalty next higher in degree shall be
imposed, consistent with Section 6 of RA 10175: Provided, That in no case shall
the penalty imposed under this Act, whether in its original or increased degree,
exceed prision mayor in its medium period: Provided, further, That the same act
shall not be separately punished under both this Act and RA 10175, the offender

being liable only for the higher penalty.

SEC. 7. Rules of Construction; Exemptions. —- Nothing in this Act shall

be construed to penalize:

10

===== [page 11] =====
(a) The reporting in good faith of information which the reporter, in the
exercise of reasonable diligence, believed to be true, or which the reporter

received from another and relayed without knowledge of its falsity;

(b) Information disseminated in the course of bona fide instruction,
training, drill, exercise, or preparedness program conducted by or with the
authority of the school administration, the local government unit, the PNP, the
BFP, the OCD, or the NDRRMC: Provided, That the activity is properly

announced and participants are informed that the information used is fictitious;

(c) Information used in a bona fide artistic, literary, theatrical, film, or
academic work, or in the exercise of freedom of the press or academic freedom:
Provided, That the same is not accompanied by any specific, credible, and

apparently imminent threat directed at an identifiable person or place; and

(d) Any utterance made in the heat of anger in the nature of rhetorical or
hyperbolic expression, unaccompanied by any specific detail as to time, place,

means, or target, and not intended to be believed.

SEC. 8. Additional Penalties for Offenders Connected with Academic

Institutions. — In addition to the penalties imposed under Section 5:

(a) Any student found guilty under this Act shall be subjected to the

disciplinary proceedings of the institution concemed, which, in the case of basic

education learners, shall be conducted in coordination with the Department of
Education (DepEd) and the parents or guardians concerned, and which may, in
the case of tertiary students, result in suspension or expulsion in accordance with
the rules of the institution and the Commission on Higher Education (CHED),
and for technical-vocational students, with the rules of Technical Education and

Skills Development Authority (TESDA);

(b) Any teacher, administrator, or employee of an academic institution
found guilty under this Act shall constitute just cause for termination or dismissal,

without prejudice to the corresponding administrative case; and

(c) Any person found guilty under this Act shall be disqualified from
holding public office or employment, or any position of trust in an academic

institution, for a period of three (3) years after service of sentence.

SEC. 9. Civil Liability; Costs of Emergency Response. — In addition to
the penalties imposed under Section 5, the offender shall be civilly liable for all
actual damages suffered by the offended party and by the government, including
the documented costs of the police, fire, medical, and rescue response; overtime
pay and equipment used; the value of lost class hours, lost productivity, and lost
business revenue of the establishment concerned; and the medical and burial

expenses of any injured or deceased person. Such costs shall be proven by

SEC. 10. Investigation and Prosecution. —_ The PNP, through the Anti-
Cybercrime Group and in coordination with the Cybercrime Investigation and
Coordinating Center (CICC), and the NBI shall have primary responsibility for
the investigation of offenses under this Act. Cases shall be filed and prosecuted
in accordance with the Rules of Court and, where committed through ICT, in

accordance with RA 10175.

SEC. 11. Mandatory Referral and Coordination. — Upon the
commission or attempted commission of any offense under this Act involving an
academic institution, the school administration shall immediately report the
incident to the PNP, to the DepEd, the CHED, or the TESDA, as may be
appropriate, and to the parents or guardians of the learner concerned. Failure to

report shall be dealt with under existing civil service and administrative rules.

SEC.12. Information and Education Campaign. — The DILG, through
the PNP, in coordination with the DepEd, the CHED, the TESDA, and all local
government units, shall conduct a continuing nationwide information and
education campaign on the prohibition against, and the penalties for, hoax threats

of mass violence, false active shooter reports, and related malicious pranks. All

SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. --- Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of the Interior and Local
Government shall, in coordination with the Secretary of Justice, Secretarv of
Information and Communications Technology, Secretary of Education, and the
Chairperson of the Commission on Higher Education, formulate the necessary

rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. — {f any provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected shall remain

in full force and effect.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. --- All laws, decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof that are
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby amended, modified, or

repealed accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. --- This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
