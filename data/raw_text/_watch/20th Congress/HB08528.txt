                              AN ACT
 ESTABLISHING A PHILIPPINE STRATEGIC PETROLEUM RESERVE SYSTEM
  TO STRENGTHEN NATIONAL ENERGY SECURITY, PROVIDE EMERGENCY
PETROLEUM SUPPLY DURING GLOBAL OIL DISRUPTIONS, AND FOR OTHER
                            PURPOSES

                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Recent geopolitical developments have underscored the urgency of
strengthening the Philippines’ energy security framework. The ongoing conflict
involving the United States, Israel, and Iran has significantly heightened risks to global
oil supply chains. The Strait of Hormuz, located between Iran and Oman, remains one
of the most critical energy chokepoints in the world. According to the United States
Energy Information Administration, approximately 20 million barrels of oil per day
passed through the Strait of Hormuz in 2024, representing roughly twenty percent of
global petroleum liquids consumption. A substantial portion of global liquefied natural
gas trade also passes through this narrow waterway.

        The present conflict has resulted in severe disruptions to shipping and energy
infrastructure in the region, including reported attacks on vessels, threats to maritime
routes, and restrictions affecting tanker movements through the Strait of Hormuz.
These developments have triggered major volatility in global oil markets and raised
serious concerns regarding the continuity of petroleum supply worldwide. Global oil
prices have experienced sharp fluctuations as tensions escalate and as shipping
routes face security risks. These events demonstrate how geopolitical developments
occurring far from Philippine territory can immediately affect domestic fuel prices,
transportation costs, and the broader national economy.
       For an oil-importing country such as the Philippines, these developments
highlight a structural vulnerability. According to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas,
petroleum crude imports reached approximately US$4.3 billion in 2024. In the first
quarter of 2024 alone, petroleum crude imports amounted to US$892 million. These
figures demonstrate the scale of the country’s reliance on imported petroleum and the
extent to which the Philippine economy remains exposed to global supply disruptions
and oil price volatility.

        At present, the Philippines does not have a comprehensive statutory framework
establishing a government-owned and government-controlled strategic petroleum
reserve. The principal law governing the sector is Republic Act No. 8479, otherwise
known as the Downstream Oil Industry Deregulation Act of 1998. While this law
liberalized the oil industry and authorizes the Department of Energy to monitor
petroleum supply and industry inventories, the current system relies largely on
minimum inventory requirements maintained by private oil companies.

       The Department of Energy has reported that the minimum inventory
requirement currently imposed on industry participants is approximately thirty days for
refiners, fifteen days for bulk marketers, and seven days for liquefied petroleum gas
players. Although these requirements contribute to short-term supply stability, they are
not equivalent to a national strategic petroleum reserve designed specifically to
respond to severe global supply disruptions or geopolitical crises.

         Government itself has already recognized the importance of establishing a
strategic reserve. Memorandum Order No. 2019-11-0001 directed the Philippine
National Oil Company to conduct a comprehensive feasibility study and prepare an
implementation plan for a National Strategic Petroleum Reserve. Subsequently, on 16
September 2021, the Department of Energy issued Department Circular No. DC2021-
09-0028 establishing the Philippine Strategic Petroleum Reserve Program. These
initiatives confirm that the need for such a reserve has already been acknowledged at
the executive level. However, a clear legislative framework enacted by Congress
remains necessary to ensure permanence, institutional structure, and long-term
funding.

       Recent government disclosures also illustrate the limits of the current system.
Statements from the Department of Energy in March 2026 indicated that national
petroleum inventories ranged from approximately fifty to sixty days depending on the
product category. Reported inventory levels included about 50.5 days for diesel, 51.5
days for gasoline, 67.5 days for kerosene, 58 days for jet fuel, and approximately 29
days for liquefied petroleum gas. These figures indicate short-term supply stability but
also confirm that the country continues to rely on commercial industry inventories
rather than on a permanent national reserve dedicated to emergency response.
      Petroleum supply disruptions can have cascading effects throughout the
economy. Fuel price shocks affect transportation, agriculture, fisheries,
manufacturing, aviation, logistics, and electricity generation in off-grid areas that
depend on diesel-powered generation. In an archipelagic country where the
movement of goods, food, and people depends heavily on fuel-based transport
systems, supply disruptions can quickly translate into higher inflation and increased
economic hardship for Filipino households.

       The proposed measure seeks to address this structural vulnerability by
establishing a Philippine Strategic Petroleum Reserve that will function as a national
emergency buffer against severe supply disruptions. The bill provides for the creation
of a government-managed reserve composed of crude oil and refined petroleum
products, the development of geographically distributed storage facilities, the
implementation of stock rotation mechanisms to maintain fuel quality, and clearly
defined conditions for the emergency release of petroleum stocks.

       The measure also establishes a Strategic Petroleum Reserve Fund and
requires regular reporting to Congress to ensure transparency and accountability in
the management of the reserve. Importantly, the bill preserves the principles of a
deregulated downstream oil industry by providing that the reserve shall complement,
and not replace, existing private sector inventory requirements. It likewise provides
that the reserve shall not be used for routine price intervention but only for genuine
supply emergencies.

      Sa panahon ng tumitinding geopolitical tensions at panganib sa pandaigdigang
suplay ng enerhiya, mahalagang magkaroon ang Pilipinas ng malinaw at matibay na
mekanismo upang maprotektahan ang ekonomiya at kapakanan ng mamamayan.
Ang pagtatatag ng isang Philippine Strategic Petroleum Reserve ay magbibigay sa
pamahalaan ng kakayahang tumugon sa mga krisis sa suplay ng langis at mapanatili
ang katatagan ng transportasyon, suplay ng pagkain, at kabuuang ekonomiya ng
bansa.

      Para sa seguridad sa enerhiya, katatagan ng ekonomiya, at kapakanan ng
bawat Pilipino, ang agarang pagpasa ng panukalang batas na ito ay mariing hinihiling..

22        Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
23   Congress assembled:
24
25         Section 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Strategic
26   Petroleum Reserve Act.”
27
28          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
29   ensure the stability, security, and reliability of the national energy supply in order to
30   protect the economy and the welfare of the Filipino people from disruptions in the
31   global petroleum market. Recognizing that the Philippines relies heavily on imported
32   crude oil and refined petroleum products, the State shall establish and maintain a
33   strategic petroleum reserve system capable of providing emergency supply during
34   periods of severe supply disruption, natural disasters, geopolitical instability, or
35   extraordinary global oil price volatility.
36
37          The strategic petroleum reserve shall be designed and implemented in a
38   manner that promotes transparency, preserves the integrity of the deregulated
39   downstream oil industry, complements private sector inventory requirements, and
40   strengthens the country’s long-term energy security and disaster resilience.
41
42          Sec. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
43

 1         (a) Strategic Petroleum Reserve refers to government-controlled stockpiles of
 2   crude oil and petroleum products maintained for use during national emergencies or
 3   severe supply disruptions.
 4
 5          (b) Petroleum Products refer to gasoline, diesel, kerosene, jet fuel, liquefied
 6   petroleum gas, and other refined petroleum products determined by the Department
 7   of Energy.
 8
 9           (c) Supply Disruption refers to any sudden reduction in the availability of crude
10   oil or petroleum products resulting from geopolitical conflict, natural disaster, global
11   market shock, or other extraordinary circumstances affecting national energy supply.
12
13          (d) Stock Rotation refers to the systematic replacement or exchange of stored
14   petroleum products to maintain quality and operational readiness.
15
16          Sec. 4. Establishment of the Philippine Strategic Petroleum Reserve. – There
17   is hereby established a Philippine Strategic Petroleum Reserve (PSPR) which shall
18   consist of emergency stockpiles of crude oil and petroleum products maintained by
19   the national government for the purpose of safeguarding national energy security.
20
21         The strategic petroleum reserve shall be designed to complement existing
22   commercial inventories maintained by private oil industry participants and shall serve
23   as an emergency buffer against severe disruptions in petroleum supply.
24
25         Sec. 5. Strategic Reserve Levels. – The strategic petroleum reserve shall
26   maintain petroleum stocks equivalent to at least thirty (30) days of the country’s
27   average net petroleum imports during the initial phase of implementation. The
28   Department of Energy may gradually expand the reserve level based on national
29   energy security requirements and available resources.
30
31          Sec. 6. Strategic Storage Facilities. – Petroleum stocks under the Philippine
32   Strategic Petroleum Reserve may be stored through a combination of government-
33   owned storage terminals, leased commercial storage facilities, underground storage
34   structures where technically feasible, floating storage systems, and other secure
35   storage infrastructure.
36
37         The Department of Energy shall ensure that strategic petroleum reserves are
38   geographically distributed across key regions of the country to enhance resilience
39   against natural disasters, infrastructure failure, or security threats.

 1          Sec. 7. Acquisition of Strategic Petroleum Stocks. – Petroleum stocks for the
 2   strategic reserve may be acquired through direct government procurement,
 3   government-to-government agreements, long-term supply contracts, stock exchange
 4   or swap arrangements, or other mechanisms determined by the Department of
 5   Energy.
 6
 7          The Department of Energy may time the acquisition of petroleum stocks during
 8   favorable market conditions in order to minimize fiscal exposure and optimize the cost
 9   of reserve development.
10
11          Sec. 8. Stock Rotation and Quality Management. – The Department of Energy
12   shall implement a systematic stock rotation program to ensure the quality, usability,
13   and operational readiness of petroleum stocks maintained in the strategic reserve.
14
15          Petroleum stocks may be periodically sold, exchanged, or replaced in order to
16   prevent degradation of stored fuels and to reduce financial losses associated with
17   long-term storage.
18
19          Sec. 9. Conditions for Emergency Release. – Petroleum from the Philippine
20   Strategic Petroleum Reserve may be released only upon the authorization of the
21   President of the Philippines upon recommendation of the Department of Energy under
22   any of the following circumstances:
23
24          (a) severe disruption of global petroleum supply;
25
26          (b) declaration of a national emergency affecting fuel supply;
27
28           (c) major natural disasters affecting national transportation, food distribution, or
29   electricity generation; or
30
31          (d) extraordinary supply interruptions that threaten essential national services.
32
33        All releases shall be conducted in a transparent manner and reported to
34   Congress.
35
36          Sec. 10. Market Neutrality Safeguards. – The strategic petroleum reserve shall
37   not be used for routine market intervention or for the purpose of artificially controlling
38   petroleum prices. The reserve shall be utilized strictly as an emergency energy
39   security mechanism during supply disruptions.
40
41          Sec. 11. Integration with Private Sector Inventories. – The establishment of the
42   Philippine Strategic Petroleum Reserve shall not replace or reduce the minimum
43   inventory requirements imposed upon oil industry participants under existing laws and
44   regulations governing the downstream oil industry.

19        Such arrangements may be undertaken through public-private partnership

22          Sec. 14. Oversight and Reporting. – The Department of Energy shall submit an
23   annual report to Congress detailing the status of the Philippine Strategic Petroleum
24   Reserve, including stock levels, storage capacity, financial expenditures, acquisition
25   strategies, and any emergency releases conducted during the year.
26
27          Sec. 15. Lead Agency. – The Department of Energy shall be the lead agency
28   for the implementation of this Act in coordination with the Philippine National Oil
29   Company, the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council, the
30   Department of Finance, and other relevant government agencies.
31
32         Sec. 16. Appropriations. – The funds necessary to carry out the initial
33   implementation of this Act shall be charged against the respective budget of
34   concerned agencies for the current year. Thereafter, such amount as may be
35   necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual
36   General Appropriations Act.
37
38          Sec. 17. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of Energy, in
39   consultation with concerned agencies, shall promulgate the implementing rules and
40   regulations necessary to implement the provisions of this Act within ninety (90) days
41   from its effectivity.
