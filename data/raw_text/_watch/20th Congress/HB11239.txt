20                                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   When a building fails, the consequences are measured not only in pesos, but in lives.
23
24   In June 2026, a building collapse in Angeles City, Pampanga resulted in a government
25   investigation that found violations of occupational safety and health standards and led to more
26   than ₱11.7 million in liabilities and administrative fines. The incident is a painful reminder that
27   the safety of a building begins long before people enter it.
28
29   In short, the safety of a building begins with the knowledge, judgment, and professional
30   responsibility of those who design and build it.
31
32   Thus, a professional entrusted with the design and construction of a building must first be
33   equipped with the knowledge necessary to make that building safe.
34
35   Hence, this measure is as simple as it can be.
36
37   This measure now requires that the National Building Code should be taught in engineering and
38   architecture programs and included in their licensure examinations. This way, it ensures that
39   future professionals responsible for designing and building our structures know the rules that
40   keep people safe.
41
42   We cannot afford to learn the value of building safety only after a tragedy. This measure ensures
43   that safety begins where every profession begins: in education and in actual training.
44
45   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

21                                 AN ACT
22    MANDATING THE INTEGRATION OF THE NATIONAL BUILDING CODE OF THE
23   PHILIPPINES UNDER REPUBLIC ACT NO. 6541 ENTITLED “AN ACT TO ORDAIN
24     AND INSTITUTE A NATIONAL BUILDING CODE OF THE PHILIPPINES” AS A
25     MAJOR SUBJECT IN THE CURRICULUM OF THE BACHELOR OF SCIENCE
26    DEGREE PROGRAMS IN CIVIL ENGINEERING, MECHANICAL ENGINEERING,
27      ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING, SANITARY ENGINEERING, ELECTRONICS
28   ENGINEERING, AND ARCHITECTURE, AND ITS INCLUSION AS A TOPIC IN THE
29             LICENSURE EXAMINATIONS FOR THESE PROFESSIONS
30
31   Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
32   Congress assembled:
33
34        Section 1. This Act shall be known as the “Integration of the National Building
35   Code to the Engineering and Architecture Curriculum and Professional Licensure
36   Examination Act”.
37
38     SEC. 2. It is the policy of the State to promote the principles of sound environmental
39   management and control consistent with its Constitutional mandate to safeguard life,
40   health, property and public welfare. To this end, the State shall ensure that those who
41   are authorized to practice the engineering and architecture professions receive the
42   necessary formal instructions on building design, construction, occupancy and
43   maintenance, are sufficiently trained in ensuring the safety and integrity of building and
44   non-building structures, and given adequate instruction and training on the latest
45   regulations, know-how, and building technologies.

46      SEC. 3. Pursuant to its mandate under Republic Act No. 7722, otherwise known as
47   the “Higher Education Act of 1994”, the Commission on Higher Education is hereby
48   mandated to develop and include in the curriculum of the Bachelor of Science degree
 1   programs in Civil Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, Electrical Engineering,
 2   Sanitary Engineering, Electronics Engineering, and Architecture the study of the
 3   National Building Code of the Philippines as a major subject.

 4      SEC. 4. The Professional Regulation Commission is hereby mandated to include the
 5   National Building Code of the Philippines as a topic in the licensure examinations for
 6   the Civil Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Sanitary
 7   Engineering, Electronics Engineering, and Architecture professions.

 8       SEC. 5. Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Commission on
 9   Higher Education shall, in consultation with the Professional Regulation Commission
10   and other relevant stakeholders in higher education, promulgate the implementing rules
11   and regulations necessary to ensure the efficient and effective implementation of this
12   Act.

13       SEC. 6. All laws, decrees, provisions of charters, executive orders, ordinances,
14   rules and 15 regulations or other parts thereof contrary to or inconsistent with the
15   provision of this Act are repealed, 16 amended or modified accordingly.

16      SEC. 7. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
17   Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
