                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Agriculture and fisheries constitute the backbone of the Philippine rural
economy, providing food security and employment for millions of Filipino
farmers and fisherfolk. However, chronic deficits in post-harvest infrastructure
severely hamper agricultural productivity, income generation, and food price
stability across the nation.

       According to data from the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources
(BFAR) and agricultural industry assessments, national fish spoilage ranges
between twenty-five percent (25%) and forty percent (40%) annually due to the
absence of adequate cold storage, blast freezers, and ice-making facilities at
landing sites and coastal municipalities. Similarly, fruit, vegetable, and high-
value crop farmers suffer post-harvest losses exceeding thirty percent (30%) of
total yields, driven by inadequate drying technology, improper storage, and
fragmented cold chain transport. These losses reduce farm-gate incomes, inflate
market retail prices, and force reliance on imported food products.

      While recent government initiatives have allocated Three billion pesos
(P3,000,000,000.00) for ninety-nine (99) cold storage facilities and fish port
rehabilitations nationwide, these efforts require a statutory framework to ensure
long-term sustainability, equitable regional distribution, and institutional
protection against underutilization or abandonment.

       This bill institutionalizes a comprehensive, eco-friendly post-harvest
infrastructure framework. Specifically, this measure:
   1. Establishes the National Post-Harvest Infrastructure and Cold Chain
       Program: Directs the Department of Agriculture (DA), through the
       Agriculture and Fisheries Logistics Office (AFLO), Philippine Center for
       Postharvest Development and Mechanization (PHilMech), Bureau of
       Agricultural and Fisheries Engineering (BAFE), and Philippine Fisheries
       Development Authority (PFDA), to construct, maintain, and upgrade
       drying, processing, modular chiller, and mega-cold storage facilities
       nationwide;
   2. Mandates Renewable Energy Integration: Requires new cold storage
       and drying facilities to incorporate hybrid solar, wind, or clean energy
       sources to minimize operational electricity expenses and ensure continuous
       operations during grid interruptions;
   3. Prioritizes Small Farmers, Artisanal Fisherfolk, and High-Value
       Commodities: Directs priority deployment of facilities to rural coastal
       provinces, major vegetable-producing corridors, and underserved regional
       agricultural basins;
   4. Empowers Farmers and Fisherfolk Cooperatives: Institutionalizes co-
       management and operational models with accredited Farmers and
       Fisherfolk Cooperatives and Associations (FCAs) and Local Government
       Units (LGUs) to foster local economic growth and community ownership;
       and
   5. Creates an Integrated Logistics and Value-Chain Masterplan:
       Connects farm-gate drying and storage nodes to regional agri-ports, sea
       routes, and primary urban consumption centers.

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.          It is the policy of the State to achieve
food security, accelerate rural development, and protect the livelihoods of small
farmers and artisanal fisherfolk. Grounded in Article XIII, Section 5 of the
Constitution and Republic Act No. 8550, as amended by Republic Act No. 10654,
the State recognizes that reducing post-harvest losses is indispensable to
stabilizing food prices, increasing rural incomes, and building a competitive
agricultural economy.



                                                                                    3
      To this end, the State shall establish a modernized, energy-efficient, and
accessible network of post-harvest facilities, prioritizing high-value crops,
smallholder vegetables, and perishable marine products, while empowering local
communities and agricultural cooperatives in facility management.

      SEC. 3. Definition of Terms.    As used in this Act:

      (a) Cold Chain System means an uninterrupted logistical network of
      climate-controlled environments encompassing post-harvest chilling, cold
      storage, refrigerated transport, and processing facilities designed to
      preserve commodity quality from harvest to consumer.

      (b) High-Value Crops mean non-traditional crops including fruits,
      vegetables, spices, cut flowers, and other high-value commercial
      commodities defined under Republic Act No. 7900, otherwise known as
      the "High-Value Crops Development Act of 1995".

      (c) Modular Cold Storage Unit means a pre-fabricated, containerized, or
      scalable refrigerated storage unit equipped with chilling or freezing
      capabilities with a capacity ranging from seven (7) to fifteen (15) metric
      tons, capable of rapid deployment and operation.

      (d) Mega-Cold Storage Facility means a large-scale, multi-commodity
      regional warehouse facility with a minimum capacity of two thousand eight
      hundred (2,800) pallet positions equipped with multi-temperature chilling,
      freezing, and processing infrastructure.

      (e) Farmers and Fisherfolk Cooperatives and Associations (FCAs) mean
      organizations duly registered with the Cooperative Development Authority
      (CDA), Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), or Department of
      Labor and Employment (DOLE), and accredited by the Department of
      Agriculture.


                                                                               4
      (f) Post-Harvest Losses mean the quantitative and qualitative reduction in
      harvested agricultural or aquatic commodities occurring between
      harvesting or landing and final consumer delivery.

      SEC. 4. Establishment of the National Post-Harvest Infrastructure and
Cold Chain Network Program. There is hereby established the National Post-
Harvest Infrastructure and Cold Chain Network Program under the
Department of Agriculture (DA).

      The DA, through its Agriculture and Fisheries Logistics Office (AFLO), in
close coordination with the Bureau of Agricultural and Fisheries Engineering
(BAFE), Philippine Center for Postharvest Development and Mechanization
(PHilMech), Philippine Fisheries Development Authority (PFDA), Bureau of
Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR), and Local Government Units (LGUs),
shall formulate and implement a ten-year masterplan for the construction,
modernization, and operation of post-harvest facilities .

      SEC. 5. Scope and Prioritization of Facilities.         The Program shall
mandate the establishment of government-built post-harvest facilities categorized
as follows:

      (a) Modular Cold Storage Units: Deployed in major producing
      municipalities, barangays, and municipal fish landing sites for rapid
      preservation of vegetables, fruits, and small pelagic fish species;

      (b) Mega-Cold Storage Facilities: Constructed in key strategic logistics
      hubs, regional agri-ports, and central trading centers to serve regional
      supply aggregation;

      (c) Solar and Mechanical Drying Facilities: Built in major grain, high-
      value crop, and fish-processing communities to eliminate weather-
      dependent road drying; and


                                                                               5
      (d) Refrigerated Transport and Mobile Cold Units: Deployed to
      connect production nodes directly to municipal markets and urban
      distribution centers.

In the site selection and allocation of facilities, the DA shall prioritize rural and
coastal municipalities, high-poverty regions, major agricultural producing
corridors, and communities with documented fish or crop spoilage rates
exceeding fifteen percent (15%).

      SEC. 6. Clean Energy and Sustainability Mandate. To lower operating
overhead and ensure climate resiliency, all government-funded cold storage and
post-harvest facilities built under this Act shall incorporate hybrid renewable
energy systems, including solar photovoltaic panels, wind power, or thermal
insulation technologies.

Grid electricity shall serve as auxiliary power to guarantee continuous,
uninterrupted cooling operations.

      SEC. 7. Community-Based Co-Management and Operation.                        To
promote local ownership and prevent facility underutilization, the DA shall
execute Co-Management and Operation Agreements with qualified LGUs and
accredited Farmers and Fisherfolk Cooperatives and Associations (FCAs).

Under the agreement:

      (1) The DA shall retain ownership of the capital asset while providing
      technical support, equipment maintenance, and operator training through
      PHilMech and BAFE;

      (2) The partner LGU and accredited FCA shall oversee day-to-day
      operations, scheduling, and fee collection under transparent, cost-recovery
      rates approved by the DA; and


                                                                                   6
      (3) Priority access and subsidized storage rates shall be guaranteed to small
      farmers owning not more than three (3) hectares of land and municipal
      fisherfolk registered under the FishR database.

      SEC. 8. Value-Chain Integration and Processing Development.                The
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and Department of Science and
Technology (DOST), in coordination with the DA, shall establish localized value-
addition support programs adjacent to post-harvest facilities.

These programs shall assist FCAs in establishing community-level processing,
bottling, smoking, salting, vacuum-packaging, and cold-chain marketing to
maximize local economic retention and product shelf life.

      SEC. 9. Action Plan Against Fish and Produce Spoilage.            Within one
hundred twenty (120) calendar days from the effectivity of this Act, the BFAR
and PFDA, in consultation with coastal LGUs and fisherfolk organizations, shall
fully operationalize regional post-harvest intervention protocols for seasonal peak
fishing periods, particularly for high-volume pelagic species including sardines,
round scads, and mackerels, to eliminate mass dumping and spoilage.

      SEC. 10. Protection of Public Investments and Anti-Abandonment
Clause.       Facilities constructed under this Act shall not be converted to
commercial real estate, privatized, or diverted for non-agricultural purposes.

Any public official or administrator who abandons, intentionally neglects, or
willfully causes the prolonged non-operation of a completed post-harvest facility
for more than ninety (90) consecutive calendar days without justifiable cause
shall be subject to administrative discipline under existing civil service and anti-
graft laws.

      SEC. 11. Appropriations.          The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year's


                                                                                   7
appropriations of the Department of Agriculture. Thereafter, an amount of not
less than Five billion pesos (P5,000,000,000.00) shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act (GAA) for the continuous construction, expansion,
and upgrading of post-harvest infrastructure.

Local Government Units are hereby authorized to allocate a portion of their
twenty percent (20%) Development Fund and local agriculture budget to support
the maintenance, power costs, and operational logistics of local post-harvest
facilities.

       SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). Within sixty (60)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the DA, in joint consultation with the DTI,
DILG, PFDA, PHilMech, BAFE, representative LGUs, and accredited FCAs,
shall issue the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

       SEC. 13. Separability Clause. If any provision or part of this Act is held
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue in full force and effect.

       SEC. 14. Repealing Clause.      All laws, executive orders, administrative
rules, and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

       SEC. 15. Effectivity.   This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
