25                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
26
27         November 23 is the International Day to End Impunity for Crimes against
28   Journalists as proclaimed by the United Nations General Assembly in 2013 in
29   commemoration of the assassination of two French journalists in Mali on that same day
30   and year.
31
32          In the Philippines, November 23 is remembered as one of the grimmest incidents
33   where 58 men and women were massacred in Ampatuan town of Maguindanao province
34   in 2009. It is also the worst attack on press freedom in Philippine history because 32 of
35   the victims were media practitioners. Marking it as a National Consciousness Day to
36   End Impunity will remind us that as long as high crimes remain unpunished, the fight
37   against injustice in the country should continue to broaden and persist.
38
39          The Philippines has been tagged this year by international media groups as one
40   of the most dangerous places for journalists being ranked poorly or is placed among the
41   worst worldwide for unsolved journalist murders. Key witnesses end up dead or forever
42   fear for their lives like the ones in the Maguindanao case who were murdered, with one
43   of them even dismembered and mutilated.
44

 1          It took a decade for the Philippines to hand a verdict to the perpetrators of the
 2   Maguindanao massacre. In 19 December 2019, Quezon City Regional Trial Court
 3   Branch 221 convicted masterminds Andal Jr. and Zaldy Ampatuan and 26 accomplices,
 4   including police officials, of murder, and sentenced them to reclusion perpetua without
 5   parole. Meanwhile 15 were convicted as accessories and 56 other people, including
 6   Sajid Islam Ampatuan were acquitted.1
 7
 8          However, the case was still classified ongoing/unresolved by the United Nations
 9   Educational, Scientific, and Cultural Organization (UNESCO). While there are 58
10   victims, only 57 murder convictions were handed down in the landmark verdict,
11   excluding photojournalist Reynaldo "Bebot" Momay, who the court recognised as a
12   victim even if his body was not found.2 Moreover, the families of the victims are yet to
13   receive the damages as there are still no resolutions of the appeals on the case.3
14   Furthermore, nearly 80 other suspects are still at large.
15
16          Impunity exists whenever perpetrators of a crime are not prosecuted or are not
17   held accountable because of their power or influence in society. Families of the victims
18   of extrajudicial killings, enforced disappearances and other human rights violations
19   point to state security forces but to no avail.
20
21          The term “impunity” has attained prominence and common usage during the
22   previous administrations. Former President Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo’s nine-year term
23   suffered from corruption issues, electoral fraud and worsening poverty of its citizens,
24   thus the discontent of the populace over her leadership, and increasing calls for her
25   ouster. As a response to this growing dissent, the human rights situation under Arroyo
26   turned to worse. Determined to stay in power, the administration institutionalized a
27   campaign to repress forces critical of the administration through military and police
28   force. A systematized human rights violation then took place and the result: 1,206
29   victims of extrajudicial killings and 206 victims of enforced disappearance perpetrated
30   against activists and critics from among workers, peasants, women, indigenous people,
31   students, lawyers, church people, and media practitioners.
32
33          Until now, no commitments were fulfilled to hold accountable high officials for
34   the massive corruption and fraud under the previous administrations, and also for the
35   gross human rights violations that will in turn serve justice to the people especially the

 9          In fact, there is a clear lack of intention to investigate the extrajudicial killings
10   under Duterte’s bloody drug war. According to the National Bureau of Investigation
11   (NBI) in January 2025, only 52 cases were referred to them. Of this number, only 13
12   were recommended to be prosecuted, seven are still under investigation and 32 were
13   provisionally closed. The families of the victims have already resorted to the
14   International Criminal Court (ICC) in pursuit of justice. However instead of supporting
15   the aggrieved families, President Marcos Jr. refused to cooperate with the ICC
16   investigation “unless permitted by former President Duterte.”5 He also continually
17   ignores the call to rejoin the ICC.6
18
19         The police continue to refuse or stall investigations, the military persists in
20   making blanket denials and arrest warrants against alleged perpetrators have not been
21   executed. The climate of impunity persists.
22
23          The killings have continued⁠—and, have in fact worsened—under the present
24   administration. As of December 2025, Karapatan documented at least 135 victims of
25   extrajudicial killings and 16 victims of enforced disappearances since the incumbency
26   of President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr.7 Impunity also rears its ugly head in the policy and
27   practice of red-tagging against critics, the opposition, and ordinary citizens committed
28   by government officials and agents as well as the private individuals they enable.
29
30          Thus, there is a need to instill awareness and spur action on the part of the
31   government and for the people to stand up and fight for justice. The people’s clamor
32   and demand for justice should continue to compel the government and the authorities
33   to take action and prosecute those who are blanketed with impunity.

25         Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
26   in Congress assembled:
27
28   SECTION 1. This Act shall be known as the “National Consciousness Day to End
29   Impunity Act.”
30
31   SEC. 2. The State shall ensure that the perpetrators of extrajudicial killings and
32   enforced disappearances shall be punished so that justice shall be served to the victims
33   and their families.
34
35          The State shall intensify its efforts to curb impunity and bring it to an end. The
36   State recognizes that the participation of all sectors concerned shall ensure the success
37   of the fight against impunity. Towards this end, the State shall endeavor to raise
38   awareness of the people regarding this issue.
39
40   SEC. 3. In memory of the victims of the Maguindanao massacre and as a symbol of the
41   people’s struggle against impunity, the 23rd of November of every year is hereby
42   declared as National Consciousness Day to End Impunity.
43

                                                                                                           1
 1   SEC. 4. The implementation of this Act shall be under the Commission of Human
 2   Rights (CHR), with the help of the Department of Education and the Department of
 3   Interior and Local Government, which shall create programs directed towards educating
 4   the people about human rights issues and the need to uphold these rights. In this regard,
 5   civil society organizations shall be tapped to assist the CHR in this endeavor.
 6
 7   SEC. 5. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
 8   Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of national circulation.
