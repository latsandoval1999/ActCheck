                           EXPLANATORY NOTE
       Section 15 of Article II of the 1987 Philippine Constitution declares that
the State shall protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill
health consciousness among them. Complementing this constitutional mandate,
Section 11 of Article XIII provides that the State shall adopt an integrated and
comprehensive approach to health development and make essential health
services accessible and affordable to all, particularly the underprivileged. In
fulfillment of these constitutional mandates, this measure seeks to establish the
Philippine Health Commission (HEALTHCOM), an independent and multi-
sectoral congressional commission mandated to undertake a comprehensive,
evidence-based review of the Philippine health system and recommend
legislative, policy, regulatory, and institutional reforms. The proposed measure
recognizes that achieving Universal Health Care requires not only expanding
healthcare coverage but also strengthening governance, financing, service
delivery, health workforce development, infrastructure, digital health systems,
and institutional coordination to ensure that reforms remain equitable, efficient,
and sustainable.

      Despite the enactment of Republic Act No. 11223 or the Universal Health
Care Act, the Philippine healthcare system continues to face persistent structural
and institutional challenges that hinder the delivery of quality healthcare services.
Recent policy research by the Philippine Institute for Development Studies
(PIDS) found continuing implementation gaps in provider payment reforms,
service readiness, healthcare affordability, and the varying capacities of local
health systems, highlighting the need for stronger governance and continuous
policy evaluation.1 Likewise, a PIDS study on the transition toward Province-
wide and City-wide Health Systems emphasizes that stronger intergovernmental
coordination, integrated governance, and institutional reforms remain essential
for the effective implementation of Universal Health Care across local
government units.2
       International evidence likewise demonstrates that countries with robust
health system governance and institutionalized performance assessment
mechanisms achieve better health outcomes, greater efficiency, and improved
resilience. The World Health Organization emphasizes that continuous health
system assessment enables governments to strengthen accountability, improve
policy coordination, optimize resource allocation, and build resilient healthcare
systems capable of responding to future public health emergencies.3 Similarly,
the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)
concludes that systematic health system performance assessment supports
evidence-based policymaking, improves healthcare quality, and enhances equity
and efficiency in healthcare delivery.4

       Recognizing these policy and institutional gaps, this proposed legislation
establishes the Philippine Health Commission (HEALTHCOM) as a temporary,
independent, and multi-sectoral congressional commission modeled after the
governance framework adopted under the Second Congressional Commission on
Education (EDCOM II). HEALTHCOM shall undertake a comprehensive
national assessment of the Philippine health system, evaluate the implementation
and effectiveness of major health laws and policies, identify systemic governance
and institutional challenges, conduct nationwide stakeholder consultations, and
recommend legislative, policy, regulatory, and institutional reforms to strengthen

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. ± This Act shall be known as the "Philippine
Health Commission (HEALTHCOM) Act."

      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. ± It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health
consciousness among them. The State recognizes that an effective, equitable,
accessible, resilient, and responsive healthcare system is indispensable to national
development, social justice, and the attainment of universal health care.

      To this end, the State shall continually assess, strengthen, and reform the
Philippine health system through evidence-based policymaking and coordinated
governance. It likewise recognizes the necessity of harmonizing the policies and
programs of the Department of Health (DOH), the Philippine Health Insurance
Corporation (PhilHealth), local government units (LGUs), and other health-
related agencies and institutions to ensure efficient, integrated, and people-
centered healthcare service delivery.

      The State further recognizes the vital role of digital transformation, health
information systems, telemedicine, medical innovation, and emerging healthcare
technologies in improving the accessibility, quality, efficiency, and resilience of
healthcare services.

      SEC. 3. Objectives. ± This Act shall be guided by the following objectives:

      (a) Conduct a comprehensive, evidence-based, and sector-wide review of
the Philippine health system to identify systemic gaps in governance, financing,
human resources for health, infrastructure, regulation, service delivery, public
health, and health information systems;

      (b) Harmonize the policies, programs, and institutional arrangements of the
Department of Health (DOH), Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
(PhilHealth), local government units (LGUs), Food and Drug Administration
(FDA), Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), Department of Budget and
Management (DBM), Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG),
Commission on Higher Education (CHED), Technical Education and Skills
Development Authority (TESDA), and other agencies involved in healthcare
governance and workforce development;

      (c) Evaluate the implementation, effectiveness, and responsiveness of
Republic Act No. 11223, otherwise known as the Universal Health Care Act, and
other major health laws, policies, and programs;



                                                                                 2
      (d) Recommend legislative, policy, regulatory, administrative, and
institutional reforms necessary to address emerging healthcare challenges,
including digital health, telemedicine, artificial intelligence, interoperable health
information systems, pandemic preparedness, health security, and climate-
resilient healthcare systems;

      (e) Strengthen coordination among national government agencies, local
government units, private healthcare institutions, academic institutions, civil
society organizations, professional organizations, and development partners in
achieving a responsive and integrated health system; and

      (f) Formulate a long-term national roadmap for health sector reform
anchored on measurable performance indicators and internationally accepted
health standards.

      SEC. 4. Creation and Mandate. ± There is hereby created a Philippine
Health Commission (HEALTHCOM) which shall serve as an independent, multi-
sectoral congressional commission tasked to undertake a comprehensive national
review, assessment, and harmonization of the policies, programs, institutional
arrangements, and performance of the agencies and bodies responsible for the
Philippine health system.

      HEALTHCOM shall examine the implementation of existing health laws,
evaluate the effectiveness of current healthcare policies and programs, identify
institutional and systemic challenges affecting the health sector, and formulate
legislative, policy, and institutional reforms necessary to strengthen the country's
healthcare system.

      Within three (3) years from its organization, HEALTHCOM shall submit
to the President of the Philippines and both Houses of Congress a comprehensive

report containing its findings, recommendations, implementation roadmap,
measurable performance indicators, and proposed legislative measures.

      SEC. 5. Composition. ± HEALTHCOM shall be composed of five (5)
Members from the House of Representatives and five (5) Members from the
Senate to be designated by the Speaker of the House of Representatives and the
President of the Senate, respectively, with due consideration to proportional
representation of the major geographical regions of Luzon, Visayas, and
Mindanao.

      The Chairperson of the Senate Committee on Health and Demography and
the Chairperson of the House Committee on Health shall serve as Co-
Chairpersons of HEALTHCOM.

      The Secretariat of the said Committees shall serve as the Technical
Secretariat of HEALTHCOM.

      HEALTHCOM may engage technical experts, consultants, researchers,
and resource persons from government agencies, academic institutions,
professional organizations, international development partners, and the private
sector as may be necessary in the discharge of its functions.

      SEC. 6. Powers and Functions. ± To carry out its objectives,
HEALTHCOM shall have the following powers and functions:

      (a) Review, assess, and harmonize the policies, programs, institutional
arrangements, and performance of the DOH, PhilHealth, LGUs, FDA, PRC, and
other government agencies involved in health governance, regulation, financing,
education, manpower development, and healthcare service delivery;

For the foregoing purposes, HEALTHCOM shall:

      (d) Prescribe and adopt guidelines governing the conduct of its national
review, assessment, and consultations;

      (e) Approve and implement its work plan and research agenda;

      (f) Hold public hearings, receive testimonies, reports, technical advice, and
expert opinions from government agencies, healthcare professionals, academic
institutions, patients' organizations, civil society organizations, private healthcare
providers, and other stakeholders;

      (g) Summon by subpoena any public official or private citizen to testify
before it, or require by subpoena duces tecum the production of records, reports,
documents, or other evidence necessary in the performance of its mandate, in
accordance with the Constitution and applicable laws;

      (h) Require any department, bureau, office, government-owned or
controlled corporation, state university or college, local government unit, or other
government instrumentality to provide technical assistance, data, reports,
information, or personnel support necessary for the fulfillment of its mandate,
subject to existing laws on confidentiality and data privacy;

      (i) Collaborate with domestic and international institutions, development
partners, research organizations, and professional associations in conducting
studies, policy research, and technical assessments;

      (j) Conduct nationwide consultations and sectoral dialogues to ensure
meaningful participation of healthcare workers, patients, indigenous peoples,

persons with disabilities, senior citizens, youth, local governments, and other
stakeholders;

      (k) Submit periodic progress reports to Congress during the duration of its
mandate; and

      (l) Exercise such other powers as may be necessary and incidental to
accomplish the purposes of this Act.

      SEC. 7. Standing Committees. ± HEALTHCOM may establish standing
committees from among its members, co-chaired by representatives of relevant
government agencies, professional organizations, academic institutions, civil
society organizations, and other multi-sectoral stakeholders.

      The Commission may constitute Technical Working Groups and Expert
Advisory Panels as may be necessary.

      Members of the standing committees, Technical Working Groups, and
Expert Advisory Panels shall serve without compensation, except reimbursement
of reasonable and necessary expenses as may be authorized by law.

      SEC. 8. Sunset Clause. ± HEALTHCOM shall exist for a period of three
(3) years from the date of its organization. It shall organize itself immediately
upon the effectivity of this Act and complete its mandate within the prescribed
period.

      The Technical Secretariat shall continue to exist for an additional period of
two (2) years solely for the purpose of publishing reports, maintaining records,
monitoring the implementation of HEALTHCOM recommendations, and
completing administrative and archival functions.

      SEC. 9. Appropriations. ± The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
the Senate and the House of Representatives.

      Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the operation of
HEALTHCOM during the period of its existence shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

      SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. ± All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, rules, regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this
Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 11. Separability Clause. ± If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

      SEC. 12. Effectivity. ± This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least one (1) newspaper of general
circulation.

      Approved.
