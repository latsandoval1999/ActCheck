JUNIOR AND SENIOR HIGH SCHOOL CURRICULA

                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

This bill mandates the Department of Education (DepEd) to integrate voter education
in the Junior and Senior High School curricula in order to provide a fair and impartial
venue for discussion of the Philippine electoral process to young citizens as they
prepare to exercise their constitutional right to vote.

The electoral process being a catalyst for peaceful and democratic change in the
political, social, historical, and economic structures of the country, it is essential that
students in Junior and Senior High Schools are educated properly about the
importance of elections, plebiscites, or referendums outside the partisan advocacies
of competing political parties and candidates during election periods. By providing
the youth with suf icient knowledge about the nuances and importance of elections
to their future, it is hoped that the proposed High School Voter Education Act will
empower the youth to exercise their right to vote responsibly.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “High School Voter
Education Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to enlighten young
citizens on their right to vote and to inculcate in them the duty to participate
responsibly and preserve the integrity of the electoral process as a vital indicator of
the economic, social, and political development and maturity of a nation.

SECTION 3. De inition of Voter Education. — As used in this Act, “Voter Education”
means dissemination of information, materials, and programs designed to inform
citizens about the speci ics and mechanics of the voting process. It includes providing
information on the history and struggle of our forefathers to exercise the right to
suffrage; its importance in the growth and maturity of a nation; the proper exercise
thereof, as well as information on voter eligibility, quali ication and registration of
voters, veri ication of voters list; rationale and type of elections being held; where,
when, and how to vote; participation in elections as candidates; and how to seek
redress for grievances and to report violations of election laws.

SECTION 4. Integration of Voter Education in the Junior and Senior High School
Curricula. — The Department of Education (DepEd) is hereby mandated to integrate
Voter Education in the junior and senior high school curricula, to be formulated with
assistance from the Commission on Elections (COMELEC). Concerned teachers of the
DepEd shall undergo trainings, to be conducted by the COMELEC and other election
experts, on the legal and technical aspects of the electoral system and process.

SECTION 5. Voter Education Syllabus. —

   A. Voter Education. – The main syllabus shall include the following thematic
      areas:

       a. Suffrage. – This area introduces to students on the right to vote accorded
          by the Constitution and other rights enabling citizens to participate in the
          conduct of public affairs; and

       b. Electoral system and process. – This area deals with the general principles
          underlying the present electoral system, and legislation regulating all the
          phases of the election. It discusses the proper conduct of elections
          including registration, casting of ballots, and counting of votes.

   B. Educated Voting. – This area focuses on the attitude and behavior a voter must
      adopt to ensure fair, free, and honest elections. It emphasizes the importance
      of participating in elections in a manner that shall promote and strengthen
      democracy.

   C. Supplementary Topics. – This curriculum should have additional topics and
      studies on the political system of the country, the contextual background of
      elections, current political, social, and economic issues affecting the elections,
      and the possible solution to address them.

   D. Other Subject Matters. – Other areas of voter education maybe included in the
      syllabus.

SECTION 6. Educational Materials. — All schools offering secondary education are
hereby mandated to acquire an adequate number of copies of textbooks, primers, and
lea lets on voter education to be issued or approved by the DepEd. The DepEd shall
determine the adequacy of the number of books, depending upon the normal volume
of enrollment of the schools.

SECTION 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The DepEd, in consultation
with the COMELEC, shall, within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act,
promulgate the rules and regulations to carry out and enforce the provisions of this
Act.

SECTION 8. Non-impairment Clause. — Nothing in this Act shall be construed as to
diminish, impair, or repeal rights recognized, granted, or made available to
disadvantaged, marginalized, or speci ic sectors under existing laws.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. — If any part, section or provision of this Act is
declared invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected by such
declaration shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, issuances, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed, amended, or modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 11. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
