                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

The local hog industry plays an important role in the country’s food supply, agricultural economy,
and rural livelihood. Behind every farm are raisers who invest their savings, time, and labor in
raising livestock, often assuming substantial costs for feeds, medicines, veterinary services, and
farm maintenance. For many small-scale producers, hog raising is not merely a business venture
but a principal source of income for their families.

Despite their contribution, local hog raisers remain vulnerable to unfavorable market conditions,
particularly when they have limited access to reliable farmgate price information and are placed
in a weaker bargaining position against buyers. Unclear weighing practices, undocumented
deductions, delayed payments, and sudden changes in purchasing arrangements can further
diminish their earnings. Without adequate safeguards, the risks of production are borne by the
raisers while they have little control over the terms under which their products are sold.

The 1987 Constitution, particularly Article XII, Sections 1, 6, and 12, directs the State to promote
agricultural development, advance distributive justice, and encourage the preferential use of
locally produced goods. This proposed measure seeks to strengthen the position of local hog
raisers by establishing greater transparency and accountability in farmgate transactions. Through
a livestock transaction reporting system and the regular publication of reference farmgate prices,
raisers will have access to more reliable market information when negotiating the sale of their
animals. These reference prices are intended to guide commercial decisions, not to impose
government-fixed prices or interfere with legitimate market competition.

The measure likewise establishes basic safeguards in the buying process, including standardized
weighing, the issuance of transaction receipts, and protection against arbitrary deductions, delayed
payments, and other unfair trading practices. It also provides mechanisms for complaints,
mediation, and administrative enforcement to ensure that affected raisers have accessible means
of seeking redress.

Ultimately, protecting local hog raisers is also a matter of protecting the country’s agricultural
capacity. A sustainable livestock industry requires not only adequate production but also a fair,
transparent, and competitive market where producers can receive payment under clear and
reasonable terms.

Through this measure, the State takes a concrete step toward strengthening the local hog industry,
protecting small livestock raisers, and promoting a more transparent and accountable agricultural
trading system.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT ESTABLISHING MEASURES TO PROTECT LOCAL HOG RAISERS
 THROUGH FARMGATE TRANSACTION REPORTING, PRICE TRANSPARENCY,
    FAIR TRADING PRACTICES, AND PREFERENTIAL PROCUREMENT OF
LOCALLY PRODUCED MEAT, PROVIDING MECHANISMS FOR ENFORCEMENT,
               AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Hog Producers Market
Transparency and Protection Act.”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote a
strong, competitive, and sustainable domestic livestock industry, protect the livelihood of Filipino
hog raisers, and ensure a stable and adequate supply of safe and affordable meat for the people.

        Pursuant to the Constitution, the State shall promote the development of local industries,
provide support to agriculture, and ensure that farmers and other agricultural producers have
equitable access to economic opportunities. It shall likewise foster a business environment where
transactions are transparent, trading practices are fair, and producers are afforded reasonable
opportunities to participate in the market.

        Towards this end, the State shall establish mechanisms for monitoring farmgate
transactions and disseminating reliable price information, protect hog raisers from abusive and
deceptive buying practices, and strengthen their access to domestic markets.

         Nothing in this Act shall be construed as authorizing the arbitrary fixing of farmgate prices
or restricting legitimate market competition. Government intervention shall be directed towards
promoting transparency, preventing unfair trading practices, and addressing market conditions
that place local producers at an undue disadvantage.
        SEC. 3. Coverage and Definition of Terms. This Act shall apply to commercial
transactions involving the purchase of live hogs from local hog raisers by traders, assemblers,
consolidators, processors, meat establishments, and other commercial buyers.

        For purposes of this Act:

         (a) Hog Raiser refers to any natural or juridical person engaged in the breeding, raising,
or fattening of hogs for commercial or livelihood purposes;

        (b) Small Hog Raiser refers to a hog raiser whose production capacity, capitalization, or
annual gross sales fall within the thresholds to be established by the Department of Agriculture
(DA), taking into consideration the scale and nature of small-scale hog production;

       (c) Buyer refers to any person or entity engaged in the commercial purchase of live hogs
from local hog raisers, including traders, assemblers, consolidators, processors, and other
intermediaries;

        (d) Farmgate Transaction refers to the sale of live hogs by a hog raiser to a buyer,
whether conducted at the farm, a buying station, a livestock trading facility, or another agreed
place of delivery; and

       (e) Farmgate Price refers to the amount paid or payable to the hog raiser for live hogs,
expressed in pesos per kilogram of liveweight or through another mutually agreed and objectively
measurable pricing arrangement.

        The DA shall prescribe reasonable reporting requirements and thresholds for the
application of this Act, taking into account the need to avoid imposing disproportionate
administrative burdens on small and occasional buyers.

       SEC. 4. Farmgate Transaction Reporting System. The DA, through the Bureau of
Animal Industry (BAI), shall establish and maintain a Farmgate Transaction Reporting System to
monitor commercial purchases of live hogs throughout the country.

        Covered buyers shall submit reports containing the date and place of transaction, volume
and liveweight of hogs purchased, farmgate price, and payment terms, in the form and manner
prescribed by the DA.

        The BAI shall consolidate the information gathered and use it to monitor market
developments, identify significant regional price disparities, and provide relevant information for
the formulation of policies and programs for the domestic hog industry.

        Information identifying individual hog raisers or specific commercial transactions shall
be kept confidential and shall not be publicly disclosed except with the consent of the concerned
party or as otherwise authorized by law. All information collected shall be handled in accordance
with applicable data privacy laws.

        SEC. 5. Publication of Reference Farmgate Prices. The DA, in coordination with the
Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), shall determine and publish weekly national and regional
reference farmgate prices for live hogs based on reported transactions and other reliable market
information.

        The reference prices shall serve as an informational guide to assist hog raisers and buyers
in assessing prevailing market conditions and negotiating fair commercial terms. They shall not
constitute mandatory buying or selling prices.

         The DA shall ensure the timely and accessible dissemination of such information through
its regional and provincial offices, local government units, official websites, and other appropriate
communication channels. The reference prices under this Act shall be distinct from the official
statistical series of the PSA.

        SEC. 6. Standard Weighing and Transaction Documentation. All farmgate
transactions covered by this Act shall be based on the actual liveweight of the hogs, determined
through properly calibrated weighing instruments maintained in accordance with applicable laws
and standards.

       The buyer shall provide the seller with a written or electronic transaction receipt indicating
the number of hogs purchased, their total liveweight, the agreed price per kilogram, the total
purchase price, any authorized deductions, and the agreed date and manner of payment.

        The weighing process shall be conducted transparently and, whenever practicable, in the
presence of the seller or the seller’s authorized representative. No deduction or adjustment
affecting the agreed purchase price shall be imposed without a reasonable basis disclosed to and
acknowledged by the seller.

        SEC. 7. Prohibited Unfair Trading Practices. It shall be unlawful for any buyer covered
by this Act to engage in unfair or abusive trading practices against local hog raisers.

        The following acts are prohibited:

        (a) Failure or unreasonable delay in paying the agreed purchase price beyond fifteen (15)
days from delivery, unless a different payment period has been expressly agreed upon in writing
before delivery;

        (b) Imposition of undocumented, undisclosed, or unjustified deductions from the agreed
purchase price;

        (c) Refusal to issue the transaction receipt required under Section 6 or, upon reasonable
request, to enter into a written supply or purchase agreement;

        (d) Unjustified cancellation or refusal to accept hogs after confirming the purchase or
delivery arrangement, particularly when made on unreasonably short notice and resulting in
material loss to the raiser;

        (e) Manipulation or misrepresentation of the weight, quality, or other material
characteristics of the hogs for the purpose of reducing the amount payable to the seller;

         (f) Threats, intimidation, coercion, or retaliation against a hog raiser for asserting rights
or filing a complaint under this Act; and

        (g) Other deceptive or abusive acts intended to unfairly diminish the amount payable to a
hog raiser or deprive the latter of a fair opportunity to negotiate and complete a transaction.

        Nothing in this Section shall prohibit legitimate commercial negotiations or the reasonable
rejection of livestock that fails to comply with previously disclosed and mutually agreed
specifications.

        Agreements or practices involving price-fixing, market allocation, or other anti-
competitive conduct shall remain subject to Republic Act No. 10667, otherwise known as the
Philippine Competition Act, and the jurisdiction of the Philippine Competition Commission
(PCC).

        SEC. 8. Complaints and Administrative Proceedings. A hog raiser aggrieved by a
violation of this Act may file a complaint with the BAI or its designated regional office. A duly
recognized association or cooperative may likewise file a complaint on behalf of its affected
members with their authorization. The BAI may also initiate an investigation motu proprio.

       The BAI shall establish an accessible procedure for receiving, investigating, and resolving
complaints. The parties shall be afforded notice and a reasonable opportunity to be heard.
Mediation or conciliation may be undertaken when appropriate and with the consent of the parties.

         The BAI shall resolve the administrative complaint within sixty (60) days from the filing
of the respondent’s answer, unless an extension is warranted by the complexity of the case or other
justifiable circumstances, which shall be stated in writing.

        Any party aggrieved by the decision of the BAI may appeal to the Secretary of Agriculture
within fifteen (15) days from receipt thereof. The decision of the Secretary shall be final and
executory, without prejudice to judicial review in accordance with applicable laws and rules of
procedure.

        Matters falling within the jurisdiction of other government agencies shall be referred to
the appropriate authority.

        SEC. 9. Support for the Procurement of Locally Produced Meat. Government
agencies and instrumentalities implementing food distribution, feeding, relief, and similar public
assistance programs shall, whenever practicable, prioritize the procurement of locally produced
pork and meat products, particularly those supplied by qualified local hog raisers and their
cooperatives or associations.

        The DA shall assist qualified local hog raisers and producer organizations in participating
in government procurement opportunities, including through supply consolidation, accreditation
assistance, market information, and coordination with procuring entities.

        Procurement under this Section shall be undertaken in accordance with Republic Act No.
12009, otherwise known as the New Government Procurement Act, its implementing rules and
regulations, Republic Act No. 11981, otherwise known as the Tatak Pinoy (Proudly Filipino) Act,
and applicable international obligations of the Philippines.

       Nothing in this Section shall prevent the procurement of imported meat products when
domestic supply is insufficient or unavailable in the required quantity, quality, or delivery
schedule, or when otherwise permitted under applicable procurement laws and regulations.

        SEC. 10. Administrative Penalties. After notice and hearing, the BAI may impose the
following administrative penalties upon any buyer found to have violated this Act or its
implementing rules and regulations:

        (a) A fine of not less than Ten thousand pesos (₱10,000.00) but not more than One
hundred thousand pesos (₱100,000.00) for failure or unjustified delay in submitting the reports
required under Section 4;

        (b) A fine of not less than Fifty thousand pesos (₱50,000.00) but not more than Five
hundred thousand pesos (₱500,000.00) for knowingly submitting false or misleading reports or
violating the weighing and documentation requirements under Section 6; and

       (c) A fine of not less than One hundred thousand pesos (₱100,000.00) but not more than
One million pesos (₱1,000,000.00) for violations of Section 7, together with restitution of amounts
unlawfully withheld, when warranted.

        In determining the appropriate penalty, the BAI shall consider the gravity and frequency
of the violation, the amount involved, the extent of injury suffered by the hog raiser, the
respondent’s degree of bad faith, and other relevant circumstances.

        For repeated violations, the BAI may impose a higher fine within the applicable range
and, when warranted, suspend or revoke any accreditation or registration issued by the DA, subject
to due process.

        The administrative penalties under this Act shall be without prejudice to civil, criminal,
or other administrative liability under existing laws.

        SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DA, through the BAI and in consultation with the DTI, PSA, PCC,
relevant local government units, hog raiser associations, cooperatives, and other concerned
stakeholders, shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its effective
implementation.

        The implementing rules and regulations shall include the reporting requirements,
procedures for determining reference prices, standards for weighing and transaction
documentation, procedures for administrative complaints, and guidelines for the imposition of
penalties.

       SEC. 12. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this
Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DA and the BAI. Thereafter, such
sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

         SEC. 13. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or
invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

        SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
and other issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

        SEC. 15. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

        Approved.
