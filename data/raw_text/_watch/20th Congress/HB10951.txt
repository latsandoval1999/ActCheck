                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill seeks to grant our public school teachers funeral and memorial discount
privileges on account of work-related sickness and injuries.

        Teaching remains one of the noblest professions, entrusted with shaping the character
and capability of our people. The 1987 Constitution itself directs the State to give priority to
education in order to foster patriotism and nationalism, accelerate social progress, and promote
total human liberation and development. Yet our educators continue to receive compensation
that leaves little room to absorb life's heaviest expenses — among them, the cost of a decent
burial for a loved one lost in the course of their work. It is estimated that funeral services in the
Philippines cost at least P100,000 to cover registration of death, mortuary care, coffin or urns,
cremation, and columbarium or memorial lots.

        Having served the people of Marikina both as a legislator and, later, as Mayor, I have
seen the sacrifices our teachers make and the burdens their families carry when tragedy strikes
in the course of their duties. This measure, first filed in the 19th Congress, seeks to ease that
burden, if only in part.

          In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is, therefore, earnestly
sought.

                                           AN ACT
      GRANTING FUNERAL AND MEMORIAL DISCOUNT PRIVILEGES TO
      QUALIFIED PUBLIC SCHOOL TEACHERS, APPROPRIATING FUNDS
                        THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Funeral Privileges to Public School
Teachers Act."

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. It is the policy of the State to acknowledge the contribution of
educators in nation-building, as they are the drivers of our educational system and rear our
children for the development of the country, as well as to adopt a comprehensive approach to
other social services available to all the people at affordable cost. As times are changing, our
State recognizes the need to continuously improve the educational system through legislation
for its empowerment.

SEC. 3. Scope of Implementation. All qualified public school teachers in all levels shall be
granted funeral or memorial discount privileges in case of death or injuries sustained in the
course of their work:

       Provided, That the public school teacher has been employed for at least three (3) years
       in an accredited public educational institution.

SEC. 4. Funeral and Memorial Discount. Families of a public school teacher who died on
account of work-related sickness or injury will be granted a discount of twenty percent (20%)
on funeral services, which includes but is not limited to funeral documents, cremation,
embalming, and interment, and a discount of ten percent (10%) on a memorial lot or
columbarium.

SEC. 5. Requirements for the Funeral and Memorial Discount. The following requirements
must be submitted by the bereaved family to avail of the discount:

       a. Identification card of the deceased public school teacher issued by either the
       Department of Education (DepEd), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), or
       the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC);

       b. Death certificate.


SEC. 6. Deductions from Taxable Income. The discount granted to the families of a public
school teacher who died on account of work-related sickness or injury shall be considered as a
deduction in the computation of the taxable income of the funeral homes that grant the same:

       Provided, That the deduction shall not exceed ten percent (10%) of its taxable income;

       Provided further, That the deductions from taxable income must be supported by an
       identification card of the public school teacher issued by either DepEd or CHED.

SEC. 7. Penalty. Any person or entity proven to have violated this Act shall suffer the
following penalties:

       a. First offense: a fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00) but not
       exceeding One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00), or imprisonment of not less
       than six (6) months but not more than two (2) years, or both, at the discretion of the
       court;

       b. Second offense: a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00)
       but not exceeding Two Hundred Thousand Pesos (P200,000.00), or imprisonment of
       not less than two (2) years but not more than six (6) years, or both, at the discretion of
       the court;

       c. Subsequent offenses: forfeiture of business permit, which may no longer be granted
       for renewal.


SEC. 8. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall
be sourced from the budgets of DepEd and CHED. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary
for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in their annual General
Appropriations Act.

SEC. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. The Department of Education and the
Commission on Higher Education, in coordination with the Department of Trade and Industry
and the Bureau of Internal Revenue, and in consultation with the Department of Social Welfare
and Development, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations of this Act within
sixty (60) days from its effectivity.

SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. All provisions of existing laws, presidential decrees or issuances,
executive orders, letters of instruction, administrative orders, rules and regulations contrary to
or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

SEC. 11. Separability Clause. If any portion of this Act is declared unconstitutional, the same
shall not affect the validity and effectivity of the other provisions not affected thereby.

SEC. 12. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
