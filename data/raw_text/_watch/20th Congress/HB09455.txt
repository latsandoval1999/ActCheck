                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

         In 2003, the Optical Media Board (OMB) was created under Republic Act No. 9239 to regulate
the manufacturing, distribution, sale, and use of optical media such as compact discs (“CD”), and
digital video discs (“DVD”), and to combat piracy through administrative licensing and criminal
enforcement mechanisms.

          As years passed, the use of optical media has sharply declined as consumers and businesses
moved to digital downloads, streaming, cloud storage, and online platforms for accessing and
distributing copyrighted works. As the OMB’s statutory mandate is limited only to the regulation of
optical media, its core regulatory function no longer matches the realities of how copyrighted works
are created, distributed, and infringed in the digital age. As a result, the OMB’s original mandate has
lost its relevance, leading to institutional redundancy and an inefficient allocation of public funds.

        At the same time, the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL) serves as the
country’s primary agency for protecting intellectual property rights and already possesses the
specialized digital infrastructure, legal frameworks, and technical expertise required to dismantle
modern, web-based piracy operations effectively.

        Clearly, the OMB’s core functions, such as licensing optical media manufacturers and
replicators, monitoring the optical media industry, investigating piracy, and filing administrative and
criminal cases, are now subsumed by IPOPHL’s comprehensive copyright and IP enforcement
mandate. In other words, the OMB’s regulatory and enforcement activities are redundant because they
overlap with IPOPHL’s existing authority and capacity to address copyright infringement across all
media, including optical media, when they still exist

          In view of the foregoing, the bill provides the legal mechanism to abolish the OMB, transfer
all its functions to IPOPHL, and ensure a smooth transition that preserves enforcement continuity
while eliminating an obsolete and redundant agency. Furthermore, this bill ensures that affected OMB
personnel are equally protected through fair absorption mechanisms, separation packages, or
retirement benefits.

                                                   AN ACT

 ABOLISHING THE OPTICAL MEDIA BOARD CREATED UNDER REPUBLIC ACT
      NO. 9239, TRANSFERRING ITS POWERS AND FUNCTIONS TO THE
 INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY OFFICE OF THE PHILIPPINES, AND FOR OTHER
                              PURPOSES

        Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Abolition of the Optical Media Board. – The Optical Media Board (OMB) created
under the Republic Act No. 9239, otherwise known as the Optical Media Act of 2003, is hereby
abolished.


SECTION 2. Transfer of Powers and Functions to IPOPHL. – All powers, functions, and
responsibilities of the OMB under Section 10 of RA 9239 relating to:
   a) licensing, regulation, and inspection of establishments engaged in reproduction and
       distribution of copyrighted works in physical media or their digital equivalents, including
       visitorial powers;
   b) enforcement        of       administrative and         penal provisions           relating to
       unauthorized reproduction of copyrighted works; and
   c) database and registry maintenance on licensed establishments and reproduction equipment,
       are hereby transferred to the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL).
A separate division may be created under the IPOPHL’s Bureau of Copyright and Other Related
Rights may be created, as may be deemed necessary by the IPOPHL, in order to perform the absorbed
functions of the OMB.

                                                                                                             3
SECTION. 3. Absorption of Affected Personnel, Separation and Retirement Benefits. – The
Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL) shall, as needed, absorb the employees of
the Secretariat of the abolished OMB, without diminution of their salaries and other benefits.
Employees who are separated from the service as a result of the abolition of the OMB and its
Secretariat shall receive separation benefits under applicable laws and regulations: Provided, That
those who are qualified to retire under existing laws shall be allowed to retire and receive retirement
benefits to which they may be entitled under applicable laws, rules and regulations.

SECTION. 4. Transfer of Properties and Resources to IPOPHL. – The IPOPHL shall, by virtue of
this Act, be subrogated to all the rights and assume all the obligations and liabilities of the OMB, and
all records, property, assets, equipment, and funds of the OMB, including unexpended appropriations
or allocations, shall be transferred to the IPOPHL.

SECTION. 5. Appropriations. – Funds necessary to implement this Act shall be sourced from
current appropriations of the OMB. Thereafter, appropriations shall be included in IPOPHL’s annual
budget.

SECTION. 6. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION. 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees or issuances, letter
of instruction, administrative orders, rules, and regulations as well as local ordinances contrary to or
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION. 8. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in the Official
Gazette or in two (2) national newspapers of general circulation.
