                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to promote the rights and welfare of nurses by regulating and
limiting their consecutive hours of work, and by establishing clear safeguards
against excessive and uncompensated overtime.

       The 1987 Constitution mandates the State to protect and promote the right to
health and to ensure the availability of accessible and affordable health services to all
Filipinos. We cannot fulfill this constitutional duty without protecting the very
professionals who deliver care in our hospitals and health facilities. Nurses form the
backbone of our healthcare system, yet many continue to endure long working
hours, unsafe staffing ratios, and exhausting shifts that compromise both their
welfare and patient safety.

       In many facilities, nurse-to-patient ratios far exceed recommended standards,
forcing nurses to render twelve (12-) to sixteen (16-) hour shifts, sometimes without
corresponding overtime pay. Excessive consecutive hours not only undermine
nurses’ physical and mental well-being but also increase the risk of medical errors
and compromise the quality of patient care.

       This measure establishes reasonable limits on consecutive work hours,
prohibits mandatory overtime beyond regular schedules except in clearly defined
emergencies, and ensures that voluntary overtime remains protected. By setting clear
standards and accountability mechanisms, this bill strengthens labor protections for
nurses while maintaining flexibility during genuine health emergencies and
disasters. Supporting our nurses is a necessary step toward sustaining quality,
responsive, and humane healthcare services nationwide.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
PROMOTING THE RIGHTS AND WELFARE OF NURSES BY REGULATING
AND LIMITING THEIR CONSECUTIVE HOURS OF WORK

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Nurses Welfare Act”.

      SEC. 2. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

         a. Emergency refers to an unforeseen event that could not be prudently
      planned for and does not regularly occur in the facility;

          b. Health Care Employer refers to any individual, partnership,
      association, corporation, or any person or group of persons acting directly or
      indirectly on behalf of or in the interest of the employer, which provides
      Health care services in a facility licensed to operate as such including any
      facility operated by the State, a Political Subdivision or a Public Corporation;

         c. Nurse refers to a registered professional nurse as certified by the
      Professional Regulatory Board; and

         d. Regularly Scheduled Work Hours refer to those hours a nurse has
      agreed to work and is normally scheduled to work pursuant to the budgeted
      hours allocated to the nurse’s position by the health care employer; and if no
      such allocation system exists, some other measure generally used by the
      health care employer to determine when an employee is minimally supposed
      to work, consistent with the collective bargaining agreement, if any.

       SEC. 3. Prohibition of Excessive Work Hours. — Notwithstanding any other
provision of law:

         a. No health care employer shall require a nurse to remain an duty for a
      period longer than eight consecutive hours, or forty hours in a seven day

                                          1
       work week, except as consistent with the nurse’s regularly scheduled work
       hours; and

           b.​ No health care employer shall require a nurse to work more than that
       nurse’s regularly scheduled work hours, except pursuant to subdivision three
       of this Act.

       SEC 4. Exceptions to the Prohibition. — The Limitation provided for in this
section shall not apply in the case of:

          a.​ Health care Disaster such as a natural or other type of disaster that
       increases the need for health care personnel, unexpectedly affecting the
       country;

          b.​ A declaration of emergency in effect in the region in which the licensed
       health care employee is employed; or

           c.​ Where a health care employer determines there is an emergency
       necessary to provide safe patient care, in which case the health care provider
       shall, before requiring an on-duty employee to remain, make a good faith
       effort to be have overtime covered on a voluntary basis, including, but not
       limited to, calling per diems, agency nurses, assigning floats, or requesting an
       additional day off work from off-duty employees;

      SEC 5. No Prohibition Against Voluntary Overtime. — Nothing in this Act shall
prohibit a nurse from voluntarily working overtime.

       SEC 6. Penalty. — The Health Care Employer found guilty of violating this Act
shall be fined the amount of One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00) in addition
to other damages that shall be imposed by the imposed court.

      SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The Department of Health and
Department of Labor and Employment shall jointly issue the implementing rules
and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act, including the
supervision and monitoring of compliance among healthcare facilities.

       SEC. 8. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and effect.

       SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
