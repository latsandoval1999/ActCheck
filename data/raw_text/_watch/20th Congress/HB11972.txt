                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

       This bill seeks to provide equitable career pathways for public school teachers
and school leaders. Under this proposed measure, a Master Teacher I may choose to
advance as a Master Teacher in the classroom, become a School Principal, or pursue
instructional supervision as a Senior Education Program Specialist and, thereafter, as an
Education Supervisor.

        The current career progression framework leaves a critical function without a
defined pathway. Instructional supervision, the specialized function of observing,
coaching, and providing technical assistance to teachers, has no corresponding career
line. Educators with the aptitude and calling for supervision therefore have no clear path
to pursue it without leaving the classroom for school management. The gap is
compounded by severe staffing constraints in the field offices tasked with supervision.

        This measure requires entry-level positions in the supervision line, namely the
Senior Education Program Specialist, Education Supervisor I, and Education Supervisor
II, to be based in schools. This shall bring mentoring and coaching directly to where
teaching and learning take place. Establishing salary grades equivalent to those of
master teachers and principals ensures that educators who choose supervision are
neither disadvantaged in pay nor in stature. It gives teachers and school leaders the
flexibility to pursue the path best suited to their strengths at each stage of their careers.

       Supervisors serve as the vital link between education policy and classroom
practice, and the quality of their support directly shapes the quality of instruction that
millions of Filipino learners receive every day. A school system that seeks to raise
learning outcomes cannot leave instructional supervision without a clear structure,
adequate positions, and fair recognition.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
PROVIDING EQUITABLE CAREER PATHWAYS FOR PUBLIC SCHOOL
TEACHERS AND LEADERS BY ESTABLISHING A SUPERVISION CAREER
LINE AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Supervision Career Line
Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
uphold and enhance the right of all public school teachers and school leaders to
professional advancement, in recognition that a motivated and highly competent
teaching force is essential to attracting and retaining the best available talents
through competitive remuneration and job fulfillment.

       Towards this end, the State shall implement just and equitable measures to
provide more defined career pathways and advancement opportunities, as well as
quality professional development programs, not only for public school teachers and
school heads, but also for supervisors that shall advance the right of each Filipino to
quality education.

        SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:​

           a. Career Progression refers to the career advancement and professional
        development of teachers, school heads and supervisors in terms of, but not
        limited to, standards-based competencies and skills, position and stature,
        remuneration and incentives, and qualifications and development goals;​

           b. Career Progression System refers to a framework of professional
        development and career advancement along the established career pathways
        available to teachers and school leaders within the public basic education
        system;​
   c.​ Career Stages refer to the various phases of a teacher’s professional
journey, from beginning, proficient, highly proficient, to distinguished phases,
based on approved professional standards of the Department of Education
(DepEd);​

   d.​ Competencies refer to the knowledge, skills, attitudes and key
behaviors that are necessary for the efficient and effective performance of the
duties and responsibilities of a position;​

   e.​ Expanded Career Progression system or ECP system refers to the
career progression lines available to teachers and school leaders, which
specifically includes the supervision career line institutionalized under this
Act to ensure parallel growth with teaching and administrative tracks;​

   f.​ Instructional Supervision refers to the specialized professional function
of providing technical assistance, mentoring, coaching and objective
observation to teachers to improve instructional quality and learner
outcomes;​

    g.​ Master Teacher refers to a teacher who has reached the highly
proficient or distinguished career stage and is eligible to choose between
continuing in the classroom teaching line, moving into school administration,
or advancing into the supervision career line;​

   h.​ National Educators’ Academy of the Philippines (NEAP) refers to the
primary agency within the DepEd responsible for providing and overseeing
continuous professional development and training programs for teachers and
school leaders;​

   i.​ Professional development refers to continuous learning opportunities
aimed at improving teacher effectiveness, including workshops, training and
mentoring;​

   j.​ Professional standards refer to the professional practice expected of
quality professionals to achieve at every stage of their career;​

    k.​ Promotion refers to the advancement of teachers and school leaders
from one position to another with an increase in duties and responsibilities or
in the qualifications and competencies, and in the salaries of the incumbent as
authorized by law, through application for vacant permanent positions or
reclassification of positions, subject to existing laws, rules, and regulations;​

    l.​ Reclassification refers to a form of staffing modification or position
classification action that may be applied only when there is a warranted
substantial change in the regular duties and responsibilities of the incumbent
of the position, involving a change in position title, salary grade, or position
level;​
         m.​ School leaders refer to officials in the public school system who
      perform managerial and supervisory roles in their respective levels of
      governance, such as head teachers, department heads, principals and assistant
      principals, schools district supervisors, education program supervisors and
      chief education supervisors at the schools division and regional offices,
      schools division superintendents, assistant regional directors and regional
      directors;​

         n.​ Supervisors refer to incumbent education program supervisors, public
      schools district supervisors, and chief education program supervisors, as well
      as the education supervisor positions created under this Act, who perform
      managerial or instructional supervisory roles to ensure curriculum
      management and implementation;​

         o.​ Supervision career line refers to the career pathway of school leaders
      involved in supervisor functions at the district, division, and regional levels;​

          p.​ Switching refers to the non-hierarchical movement between the
      classroom teaching, school administration, and supervision career lines,
      provided the educator meets the qualification standards and competency
      requirements for the target position;​

          q.​ Teacher refers to a person qualified to practice teaching under the law
      and engaged in the teaching of any subject, including technical-vocational
      (Tech-Voc), at the basic education level in all private or public basic education
      institutions;​

         r.​ Teacher Education Council (TEC) refers to the body responsible for
      formulating policies on teacher education and ensuring that professional
      standards are harmonized and monitored across all career lines in the
      expanded system.

       SEC. 4. Institutionalizing a Supervision Career Line. – The expanded career
progression system for public school teachers and school leaders shall be
strengthened by institutionalizing a supervision career line, which shall further
promote their career advancement and professional development and define their
career lines within the public school system, specifically in the elementary and
secondary levels, and the teacher career progression framework of DepEd.

       The foundational positions of the teacher career progression system shall be
Teacher I to Master Teacher I . Entry to any foundational position shall be allowed;
Provided, That DepEd’s qualification standards, as approved by the Civil Service
Commission (CSC), have been met, and the Teacher Education Council (TEC) has
confirmed the achievement of the professional standards’ indicators relating to the
career stage of the position being applied for.

      Career advancement within these positions shall be non-hierarchical and
based on the principles of merit, fitness, and professional standards-based
competence, without prejudice to the three-salary grade limit and other CSC rules
and regulations.

       A holder of a Master Teacher I (MT I) position may choose a career line in
teaching, school administration, or supervision. Accordingly, an MT I may opt to
become a Master Teacher II, School Principal I, or Senior Education Program
Specialist (SEPS).

       SEC. 5. Advancing to SEPS. – Holders of the MT I position may opt to pursue
the supervision career line through promotion or reclassification to the SEPS
position, subject to the appropriate qualifications, competencies, and other
requirements prescribed herein or by existing laws, rules, regulations, and other
issuances.

       SEC. 6. Equivalence of Positions. – The positions within the Supervision Career
Line shall have the following equivalent ranks and salary grades across the three
career lines of the Expanded Career Progression System:

       SEC. 8. Switching Career Lines. – Switching to and from the Supervision Career
Line to and from other career lines shall be allowed; Provided, That the aspirant
possesses all the required qualifications and set competencies of the position being
applied for in another career line, subject to the necessary assessment and other
applicable staffing standards and deployment parameters for the classroom
teaching, school administration and supervisory positions.
       SEC. 9. Non-Diminution in the Salaries, Benefits and Other Privileges of Incumbent
Supervisors. – In no case shall incumbent supervisors’ ranks be demoted or their
salaries, benefits and other privileges, granted under existing laws, decrees and
others or stipulated in any policy, contract or agreement, between them and DepEd,
be diminished upon the effectivity of this Act.

        SEC. 10. Promotion of Supervisors. – Supervisors may be promoted through
natural vacancies or reclassification of positions. Applicants for promotion to any of
the supervisory positions shall be evaluated based on organizational and managerial
effectiveness, in terms of productivity, performance, utility, efficiency and other
requirements related to professional standards.

       DepEd shall formulate clear guidelines for assessing supervisors seeking
promotion. A standards-based assessment shall be implemented and the assessment
process, criteria and point system shall be made accessible to the public. DepEd may
opt to provide an impartial third party with clear guidelines on the
conduct of the standards-based assessment.

      SEC. 11. Promotion of Incumbents. – Qualified holders of current positions
under Section 7 hereof may be promoted through natural vacancies or reclassified to
appropriate positions in the ECP system, based on their qualifications, competencies,
and demonstrated proficiency.

       SEC. 12. Support Program for Supervisors. – Public school teachers and school
heads aspiring to become supervisors, as well as the supervisors aspiring to advance
in the supervision career line but who do not pass the professional standards-based
assessment for two (2) consecutive times, shall be required to undergo a professional
development program. This support program shall be developed by the NEAP and
shall be offered free of charge.

       TEC shall review, align and continuously monitor the roles of NEAP and the
Bureau of Human Resource and Organizational Development of DepEd to ensure
the continuous improvement of the career progression system and to enhance the
constitutional right of teachers and school leaders to professional advancement.

       SEC. 13. Role of Government Agencies. – In support of this Act, the CSC, DepEd,
TEC, NEAP and the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) shall harmonize the
qualification standards for all positions in the classroom teaching, school
administration and supervision career lines in the public school system.

       The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) shall create the new
position titles of Education Supervisor I, Education Supervisor II, Supervisor III and
Education Supervisor IV and include the same in the Index of Occupational Services,
Occupational Groups, Classes and Salary Grades.

        SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the CSC, DBM, DepEd, PRC and TEC, in consultation with the
teachers and school leaders’ organizations and other education stakeholders, shall
jointly issue the necessary rules and regulations for its effective implementation.

        SEC. 15. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation of either DepEd
or the Miscellaneous Personnel Benefits Fund, or both. Thereafter, the amount
necessary for its continuous implementation shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

        SEC. 16. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 18. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
