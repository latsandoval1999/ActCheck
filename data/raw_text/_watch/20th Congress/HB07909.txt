                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

Under current Philippine jurisprudence and the application of Article 365 of
the Revised Penal Code, drivers involved in road accidents are almost
automatically charged with "Reckless Imprudence Resulting in Homicide,
Serious Physical Injuries, or Damage to Property."

This is a long-standing grievance for many drivers—the "presumed guilt" that
often results in a driver being handcuffed while the person who jumped over
a highway barrier is rushed to the hospital, or a person who fell off a bridge
or overpass and was bumped or run over by a vehicle below and died.

In practice, this often leads to the immediate detention of the driver,
regardless of whether they were following traffic laws, speeding limits, and
safety protocols. This "detain first, investigate later" culture ignores the reality
of unavoidable accidents—situations where a pedestrian or another motorist
acts with such gross negligence (e.g., crossing a high-speed expressway,
ignoring "No Crossing" signs, or darting into traffic) that no amount of care by
the driver could have averted the collision.

This bill seeks to institutionalize the "Doctrine of Unavoidable Accident." It
aims to protect law-abiding drivers from the trauma of unwarranted detention
and prosecution when the evidence clearly shows that the accident was
caused by the victim’s own reckless behavior. It's time we stop punishing
people for failing to perform the impossible.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

AN ACT PROVIDING FOR THE EXEMPTION OF DRIVERS FROM
CRIMINAL AND CIVIL LIABILITY IN ACCIDENTS RESULTING FROM THE
GROSS NEGLIGENCE OF PEDESTRIANS OR OTHER PARTIES,
AMENDING FOR THIS PURPOSE ARTICLE 365 OF THE REVISED PENAL
CODE
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:


     SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Fair Liability
and Driver Protection Act."

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to ensure
justice for all parties in road safety incidents. While the State protects the life
and limb of pedestrians, it shall also protect drivers from unjust prosecution
and detention when they are not at fault, and the accident was unavoidable.

      SEC. 3. Exemption from Liability. – Any driver of a motor vehicle shall
be exempt from criminal and civil liability for any injury or death resulting
from a traffic accident, provided the following conditions are met:

   1. The driver was operating the vehicle in a lawful manner, adhering to
      speed limits and traffic regulations.

   2. The accident occurred due to the gross negligence of the pedestrian or
      another party (e.g., crossing in prohibited areas, ignoring traffic
      signals).

   3. The driver had no "Last Clear Chance" to avoid the accident due to the
      sudden or unpredictable nature of the other party's actions.

      SEC. 4. Presumption of Innocence Across All Vehicle Classes. – In any
investigation involving a collision between a four-wheeled (or larger) motor
vehicle and a motorcycle, bicycle, or any non-motorized transport, the size or
weight of the vehicle shall not be a basis for a presumption of fault.
      SEC. 5. Specific Negligence of Two-Wheeled Motorcycles and Bicycles -
A driver of a four-wheeled vehicle shall be non-liable if the accident was
caused by any of the following actions by the rider/cyclist:

             1. Lane Splitting or Filtering at High Speed: Moving between
                lanes of traffic in a manner that prevents the four-wheeled
                driver from perceiving them through standard mirrors.

             2. Counter-Flowing: Operating against the legal flow of traffic.

             3. Overtaking on the Right: Attempting to pass a vehicle on its
                right side while that vehicle is making a legal right-hand turn.

             4. Operating Without Lights: Using the road at night or during
                heavy rain without functional headlamps or high-visibility
                reflectors.

             5. Swerving into "No-Zones": Entering the immediate "blind spot"
                of a larger vehicle (trucks/SUVs) where the driver has no
                physical way of seeing the smaller craft.


      SEC. 6. Prohibition on Immediate Detention. – Law enforcement officers
are hereby prohibited from detaining a driver involved in an accident if:

   •   Dashcam footage, CCTV, or at least two (2) disinterested witnesses'
       testimonies clearly indicate the driver was not at fault.

   •   The accident occurred on a "limited access" facility (expressways) where
       pedestrian crossing is strictly prohibited.

   •   The driver remained at the scene to provide assistance (unless fleeing
       is necessary to avoid mob violence).


      SEC. 7. Protection from Mob Rule, Harassment, and Extrajudicial
Accusation. – If a driver feels threatened by a crowd at the scene, they may
proceed to the nearest police station to report the incident immediately. This
shall not be considered "Hit and Run" or "Abandonment of Victim," provided
the driver identifies themselves to authorities within one (1) hour.

      Law enforcement must secure the driver from physical or verbal assault
by bystanders. Any person who uses force or intimidation against a driver
before a formal investigation is concluded shall be liable for Grave Coercion
or Physical Injuries.


       SEC. 8. Evidentiary Weight of Technology. – Dashcam recordings and
verified CCTV footage shall be considered primary evidence in the immediate
scene investigation to determine if the "unavoidable accident" clause applies.
       SEC. 9. Penalties for Malicious Prosecution. – Any party or officer who
insists on the detention of a driver despite clear evidence of the driver’s
innocence under this Act shall be subject to administrative sanctions.

       SEC. 10. Right to Countersuit and Recovery of Damages. - A driver who
is cleared of liability under the provisions of this Act shall have the express
right to seek civil indemnity from the negligent party (pedestrian, rider, or
their legal guardians).

      SEC. 11. Compulsory Damages - Upon finding of gross negligence by
the other party, the court shall award the driver actual damages, moral
damages, attorney’s fees, and loss of income (compensation for workdays
missed due to court hearings, or vehicle unavailability).

       SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

      SEC. 13. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder of the Act or the provisions not
otherwise affected shall remain in full force and effect.

      SEC. 14. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
