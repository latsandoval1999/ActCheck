                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This bill seeks to amend Articles 87 and 116 of Presidential Decree No. 442,
otherwise known as the “Labor Code of the Philippines”, and penalize the act of wage
theft. It expands the prohibition against the unlawful withholding of wages and
employment benefits, and imposes criminal and administrative penalties on
employers who violate workers’ rights.

      Incidents of unpaid wages, underpayment, delayed compensation,
non-payment of overtime pay, and withholding of employment benefits continue to
undermine labor standards and workers’ right to just compensation. It affects
low-income workers who rely on timely payment of wages to meet their daily needs,
and that of the daily sustenance of their families.

        This bill expands existing prohibitions to cover unpaid wages, overtime pay,
bonuses, tips, reimbursements, and other legally due benefits. It likewise penalizes
discriminatory practices in the allocation of overtime work, imposes criminal
liability on responsible employers and corporate officers, and authorizes the
suspension or cancellation of business permits for repeated violations. These shall
strengthen workers’ rights while promoting compliance with labor standards.

      The payment of lawful wages is not merely a contractual obligation but a
matter of social justice. Legislation must uphold the dignity of labor and ensure that
every worker receives the compensation rightfully earned.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            AN ACT
PENALIZING THE ACT OF WAGE THEFT, AMENDING FOR THIS PURPOSE
ARTICLES 87 AND 116 OF PRESIDENTIAL DECREE NO. 442, OTHERWISE
KNOWN AS THE “LABOR CODE OF THE PHILIPPINES”, AS AMENDED

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Wage Theft Prevention
Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to not only ensure
workers’ right to a living wage and to a humane working condition, but full
protection to all the rights legally due to a worker.

     To promote and ensure full protection of a workers’ wage against unlawful acts
of wage theft, the State adopts and institutionalizes the legislation of penalizing the
act of wage theft that can possibly be committed against honest and hardworking
workers of both the public and private sectors, locally and abroad.

        SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

            a. Wage Theft refers to the act of depriving or withholding an employee
        of the wages, other earnings, and benefits to which the employee is legally
        entitled to as stated in the contract or agreement entered by both the employer
        and employee of the private sector, locally or abroad.

               The act may be in the form of, but is not limited to:

                   1. non-payment or incorrect payment of wage, including
               minimum wage, overtime premium, holiday/rest day pay, night shift
               differential, among others;

                  2. tip theft or failure to award the amount of tips honestly earned
               by a workers;

                                             1
                3.​ failure to pay bonuses granted to each employee as stated in
             their contract or agreement;

               4.​ off-the-clock work, wherein employees are required to perform
             work-related tasks before clocking in or after clocking out, essentially
             working for free and losing overtime pay; and

                5.​ discrimination on the classification of employees from other
             earnings and benefits.

       SEC. 4. Article 87 of the Labor Code of the Philippines is hereby amended to
read as follows:

             “ART. 87. Overtime Work. - Work may be performed beyond
      eight (8) hours a day provided that the employee is paid for the
      overtime work, an additional compensation equivalent to his regular
      wage plus at least twenty-five percent (25%) thereof. Work performed
      beyond eight hours on a holiday or rest day shall be paid an additional
      compensation equivalent to the rate of the first eight hours on a
      holiday or rest day plus at least thirty percent (30%) thereof.

           “IT SHALL BE UNLAWFUL FOR ANY EMPLOYER TO
      DISCRIMINATE ANY EMPLOYEE BY CLASSIFYING THOSE
      WHO CAN EXCLUSIVELY RENDER OVERTIME WORK ON THE
      BASIS OF SEXUAL ORIENTATION, ETHNIC ORIGIN,
      RELIGIOUS BELIEF, DISABILITY, EDUCATIONAL ATTAINMENT
      AND OTHER FORMS OF DISCRIMINATION.”

​      SEC. 5. Article 116 of the Labor Code of the Philippines is hereby amended to
read as follows:

            “ART. 116. Withholding of Wages and Kickbacks Prohibited. – It shall
      be unlawful for any person, directly or indirectly, to withhold any
      amount from the wages, OTHER EARNINGS AND BENEFITS
      SUCH AS OVERTIME COMPENSATION, TIPS, 13TH MONTH
      PAY, REIMBURSEMENTS DONE ON THE COURSE OF THEIR
      DUTY, HOLIDAY PAY, BONUSES, AMONG OTHERS ON THE
      AGREED RELEASE DATE OF SUCH WAGE, OTHER EARNINGS
      AND BENEFITS of a worker or induce him to give any part of his
      wages by force, stealth, intimidation, threat or by any other means
      whatsoever without the workers’ consent.”

​     SEC. 6. Penalties. – Employers who fail to comply with this Act shall be
criminally liable in accordance with paragraphs 1 to 6 of Article 309 of the Revised
Penal Code, as amended, and other existing laws, rules, and regulations.




                                          2
      In cases where the employee who is subject to wage theft is employed by a
corporation, the person criminally liable shall be the person directly responsible for
the release of wages or, in the absence thereof, the beneficial owner of the
corporation. In cases of partnerships, all partners shall be liable, and in cases of sole
proprietorship, the sole proprietor shall be liable.

      Furthermore, if proven that the violating entity has been withholding
workers' wages, other earnings and/or other benefits, suspension or cancellation of
business permits will be as follows:

          a.​ First Offense: suspension of three (3) months to six (6) months;

          b.​ Second Offense: suspension of six (6) months to 1 year;

           c.​ Third Offense: cancellation of business permit.
​       ​
        SEC. 7. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – In coordination with the
representatives of the public and private sectors, and other concerned government
agencies, the Secretary of Labor and Employment shall promulgate the necessary
rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.
​
        SEC. 8. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
invalid or unconstitutional, other provisions hereof shall remain in full force and
effect.

       SEC. 9. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
rules and regulations which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

      SEC. 10. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a national newspaper of general circulation.
