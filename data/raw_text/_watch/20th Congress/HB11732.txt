                                           EXPLANATORY NOTE
Peace is not simply the absence of armed violence. It requires institutions in which women
can shape the decisions that affect their safety, rights, livelihoods, and communities.
Filipino women have long served as mediators, negotiators, community organizers,
humanitarian responders, and architects of peace. Their contribution must be recognized
not only through policy commitments but through enforceable and sustained institutional
obligations.
The Philippines adopted its first National Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security
(NAP-WPS) in 2010 and is implementing its fourth-generation plan for 2023–2033. The
current plan is organized around Empowerment and Participation; Protection and
Prevention; Promotion and Mainstreaming; and Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability,
and Learning. It emphasizes cooperation with civil society, localization, emerging digital
risks, and measurable outcomes.
The Constitution recognizes the role of women in nation-building and guarantees equality
before the law. Republic Act No. 9710, the Magna Carta of Women, already requires
women's participation in peace processes and the protection of women and girls in
conflict-affected communities. Executive Order No. 865 (2010) created the National
Steering Committee on Women, Peace and Security. Yet much of the operational
framework continues to depend on executive arrangements and administrative planning.
House Bill No. 11423, filed in the Twentieth Congress, proposes to institutionalize this
agenda, including local action plans, civil society participation, protections for
peacebuilders, and legislative reporting. The United States Women, Peace, and Security
Act of 2017 offers a comparative model for government-wide strategies, agency-specific
implementation, training, and reporting to Congress. This measure draws on those
approaches while adapting them to Philippine law, the existing NAP-WPS, and
Bangsamoro autonomy. The United Kingdom’s fifth Women, Peace and Security National
Action Plan (2023–2027) provides a complementary policy-based model. Organized
around women’s decision-making, gender-based violence, humanitarian and crisis
response, security and justice, and transnational threats, it links the traditional WPS pillars
to emerging challenges such as climate insecurity and digital threats. It also identifies
departmental responsibilities, emphasizes partnerships with women-led organizations,
and provides for parliamentary reporting and independent assessment. Unlike the United
States statute, the UK plan is an executive policy framework; these comparative
experiences inform, but do not replace, the Philippine NAP-WPS and domestic legal
obligations.
The proposed Act establishes a permanent statutory coordination mechanism; requires
meaningful rather than token participation; mandates gender-responsive analysis in peace
processes; strengthens survivor-centered protection and safe civic participation;
integrates peace education, youth leadership, disaster and climate resilience; and
establishes measurable implementation plans, public reporting, and budget accountability.
It preserves the powers of the Bangsamoro Government and uses existing gender and
development and other lawful funding mechanisms without authorizing double counting.
This is a measure not only to protect women in conflict, but to recognize women as leaders
whose decisions are essential to a just and durable peace. In view of the foregoing,
immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE WOMEN, PEACE AND SECURITY AGENDA,
ENSURING THE MEANINGFUL PARTICIPATION AND LEADERSHIP OF WOMEN IN

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:
SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Women, Peace and
Security Act.”
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. The State recognizes that sustainable peace, human
security, and democratic governance require the full, equal, safe, and meaningful
participation and leadership of women in conflict prevention, peace negotiations,
peacekeeping, peacebuilding, humanitarian response, transitional justice, and recovery.
The State shall prevent discrimination and gender-based violence, protect the rights and
dignity of women and girls affected by conflict and crises, and uphold their agency as
leaders and rights-holders, consistent with the Constitution, Republic Act No. 9710,
applicable international obligations, and the autonomy of the Bangsamoro Government.
SEC. 3. Objectives. This Act shall: (a) institutionalize the Women, Peace and Security
(WPS) agenda and the National Action Plan on WPS (NAP-WPS); (b) ensure meaningful
participation and leadership of women in peace and security decisions; (c) prevent and
respond to conflict-related gender-based violence and threats to peacebuilders; (d)
integrate WPS into national, regional, and local planning, including disaster and climate
resilience; (e) strengthen partnerships with civil society and young women; and (f)
establish transparent financing, measurable results, and accountability.
SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act:
(a) WPS Agenda refers to the framework arising from United Nations Security Council
Resolution No. 1325 and subsequent WPS resolutions;
(b) NAP-WPS refers to the Philippine national government’s periodically updated strategy
for WPS;
(c) meaningful participation means timely, informed, safe, accessible, adequately
supported involvement with a genuine opportunity to influence decisions, rather than
ceremonial attendance;
(d) conflict-affected communities include areas experiencing or recovering from armed
violence, displacement, or related insecurity;
(e) women peacebuilders include women engaged in lawful mediation, reconciliation,
conflict prevention, humanitarian action, peace advocacy, and monitoring; and
(f) local action plan means an LGU-led WPS implementation framework integrated into
existing development and peace and order plans.
SEC. 5. National Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security. The NAP-WPS is hereby
institutionalized as the national policy and implementation framework. The 2023–2033
NAP-WPS shall remain the operative plan for its stated period, subject to lawful periodic
review and updating. Successor plans shall be prepared through transparent
consultations with women in conflict-affected communities, civil society, youth, Indigenous
Peoples, persons with disabilities, and relevant institutions. Each plan shall include
measurable goals, agency-specific responsibilities, indicators, timelines, resource
requirements, risk assessments, and a monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning
framework. No revision shall diminish rights or protections established by law. Consistent
with Philippine priorities, each plan shall also identify actions addressing gendered
transnational and emerging threats, including climate insecurity, technology-facilitated
violence, trafficking, and conflict-related displacement, and specify how affected women
will participate in developing and reviewing such actions.
SEC. 6. National Steering Committee. The National Steering Committee on Women,
Peace and Security (NSCWPS), established under Executive Order No. 865, series of
2010, is hereby institutionalized as the principal interagency policy-coordination and
monitoring body for this Act. It shall be chaired by the Office of the Presidential Adviser on
Peace, Reconciliation and Unity (OPAPRU), with the Philippine Commission on Women
(PCW) as Vice Chair. Its members shall include the departments and agencies currently
participating in the NAP-WPS, including the DND, DILG, DSWD, DOJ, DFA, NCIP, and
NCMF, and other relevant agencies designated in the implementing rules. The NSCWPS
shall maintain a technical working group and shall consult the Bangsamoro Women
Commission in matters affecting the Bangsamoro.
SEC. 7. Functions of the NSCWPS. The NSCWPS shall:
(a) coordinate implementation of the NAP-WPS;
(b) issue common implementation and reporting guidelines;
(c) review agency and local commitments and progress;
(d) coordinate gender-responsive early warning and prevention measures;
(e) facilitate capacity-building and assistance to local governments;
(f) convene regular consultations with civil society and affected women;
(g) oversee the national WPS results framework; and
(h) prepare the annual public report required under this Act. Its functions shall not impair
statutory mandates of other bodies.
SEC. 8. Meaningful Participation in Peace Processes. All national government bodies
responsible for formal peace negotiations, mediation, ceasefire arrangements,
normalization, and post-conflict reconstruction shall take affirmative measures to secure
women’s meaningful participation, including as negotiators, advisers, monitors, and
decision-makers. Before finalization of major peace agreements and implementation
frameworks, responsible bodies shall undertake gender analysis and consult affected
women, subject to legitimate confidentiality and security requirements. Agencies shall
document how women’s recommendations were considered and shall address practical
barriers, including accessibility, safety, caregiving, travel, and information access.
SEC. 9. Women’s Leadership in Community Peace and Security. The DILG, in
coordination with the PCW, OPAPRU, and concerned LGUs, shall support the substantive
participation of women in local peace and order councils, conflict-resolution bodies, early
warning networks, and community mediation. Participation shall comply with applicable
laws on membership, selection, and qualifications. Women’s organizations shall be given
reasonable opportunities to submit proposals and receive written feedback.
SEC. 10. Protection of Women and Girls in Conflict and Crisis. The DSWD, DOH,
DOJ, DILG, and relevant agencies shall strengthen coordinated, survivor-centered referral
pathways for conflict-related sexual violence, trafficking, exploitation, displacement-
related abuse, and other gender-based violence. Services shall include appropriate
medical and psychosocial care, legal assistance, safe shelter, livelihood and recovery
support, and accessible complaint channels, consistent with existing law and available
appropriations. Survivors’ confidentiality, informed consent, safety, and dignity shall be
protected.
SEC. 11. Protection of Women Peacebuilders. The State shall safeguard women
peacebuilders and human rights defenders from unlawful threats, harassment,
intimidation, retaliation, and violence. The CHR, DOJ, DILG, and other competent
authorities shall establish coordinated, accessible reporting and referral procedures, risk
assessment, and access to appropriate legal, psychosocial, and protective services. No
person shall be subjected to adverse action solely for lawful peacebuilding or human rights
activity. Nothing in this section shall limit lawful investigations, due process, freedom of
expression, or other constitutional rights. Acts constituting offenses shall be prosecuted
under applicable laws.
SEC. 12. Security Sector Integration and Training. The DND, AFP, PNP, Philippine
Coast Guard, and other relevant security agencies shall integrate WPS principles into
relevant doctrine, professional education, predeployment preparation, civilian protection
protocols, and leadership development. Training shall cover gender analysis, prevention
of sexual exploitation and abuse, trafficking, conflict-related sexual violence, international
humanitarian and human rights law, and women’s participation in conflict prevention and
resolution. Agencies shall assess training effectiveness and report results. Agencies shall
also incorporate survivor-centered handling of conflict-related sexual violence, appropriate
accountability and referral procedures, and safeguards against sexual exploitation and
abuse by security personnel, subject to applicable laws and mandates.
SEC. 13. Gender-Responsive Conflict Prevention and Disarmament. Relevant
government agencies shall incorporate women’s perspectives into conflict-risk
assessments, community early warning, prevention of violent extremism consistent with
human rights, small-arms harm reduction, disarmament, demobilization, reintegration, and
normalization programs. Such measures shall not stereotype women as informants or
impose disproportionate security risks on them.
SEC. 14. Localization. Provinces, cities, and municipalities in conflict-affected or conflict-
vulnerable areas, as identified through transparent and consultative guidelines, shall
integrate WPS priorities into existing local development, peace and order, disaster risk
reduction, and gender and development planning processes. Other LGUs are encouraged
to adopt appropriate WPS measures. DILG, PCW, and OPAPRU shall provide model
guidance, technical assistance, and proportionate reporting requirements to avoid
duplicative plans and administrative burdens. Barangays shall be engaged through
existing lawful mechanisms.
SEC. 15. Bangsamoro Autonomy. Implementation within the Bangsamoro Autonomous
Region in Muslim Mindanao shall respect Republic Act No. 11054, the Bangsamoro
Government’s powers, and its regional WPS policies and action plans. National agencies
shall coordinate with the Bangsamoro Government and the Bangsamoro Women
Commission, ensuring the meaningful participation of Bangsamoro, Moro, non-Moro
Indigenous, displaced, and grassroots women. National and regional plans shall be
mutually reinforcing, without derogating from autonomy.
SEC. 16. Civil Society Partnership. The NSCWPS shall establish a transparent
consultative platform for women’s rights organizations, grassroots peacebuilders,
Indigenous women, persons with disabilities, youth-led groups, academe, and other
relevant civil society actors. Representation shall be selected through published, inclusive
criteria and shall include women from Luzon, Visayas, Mindanao, and the Bangsamoro.
The platform may submit independent assessments and recommendations, which shall
receive a documented response.
SEC. 17. Young Women and the Youth, Peace and Security Agenda. The NSCWPS
shall coordinate with the National Youth Commission and appropriate youth institutions to
align, where relevant, the WPS and Youth, Peace and Security agendas, promote young
women’s leadership, and create safe and accessible opportunities for youth participation
without subsuming both agenda into the other.
SEC. 18. Humanitarian Action, Climate and Disaster Resilience. Relevant agencies
and LGUs shall integrate gender-responsive peace and conflict sensitivity into disaster
preparedness, climate adaptation, evacuation, humanitarian distribution, displacement
management, and recovery programs. Women and girls affected by overlapping conflict
and climate risks shall be consulted in program design and monitoring. Equal access to
relief, safety, sanitation, health services, and livelihood recovery shall be promoted.
Humanitarian response mechanisms shall facilitate safe and meaningful leadership by
women-led and community-based organizations, including in needs assessments,
distribution planning, feedback systems, and recovery monitoring.
SEC. 19. Peace Education and Digital Safety. DepEd, CHED, TESDA, and relevant
institutions shall, consistent with their mandates and academic freedom, promote age-
appropriate peace education, nonviolent conflict resolution, gender equality, media and
digital literacy, and women’s contributions to peacebuilding. Agencies shall address online
harassment and technology-facilitated threats affecting women peacebuilders through
existing legal and protective mechanisms. In coordination with competent authorities,
relevant agencies shall assess gendered risks associated with the misuse of digital
platforms and emerging technologies in conflict and crisis settings, consistent with privacy,
freedom of expression, due process, and other constitutional guarantees.
SEC. 20. Agency Implementation Plans. Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the
issuance of implementing rules, each NSCWPS member agency shall adopt or update an
agency WPS implementation plan stating its activities, accountable units, measurable
indicators, timetable, and financing sources. Such plans shall be integrated into regular
agency planning and performance systems.
SEC. 21. Funding. Amounts necessary for initial implementation shall be charged against
available appropriations of concerned agencies, subject to existing laws. Thereafter,
funding requirements shall be included in the agencies’ annual budget proposals and, as
authorized, the General Appropriations Act. Eligible WPS activities may be charged
against Gender and Development funds and other applicable programs only in
accordance with governing budgeting, accounting, auditing, and procurement rules. The
same expenditure shall not be counted against multiple funding requirements. Local
funding shall be subject to applicable LGU budgeting and fiscal rules.
SEC. 22. Data, Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning. The NSCWPS,
with PCW and appropriate agencies, shall implement a common results framework
consistent with the NAP-WPS, including quantitative and qualitative measures of actual
influence on decisions, access to services, prevention outcomes, institutional changes,
and budget utilization. Data shall be disaggregated where appropriate and lawful, with
safeguards for privacy, security, survivor confidentiality, and the safety of conflict-affected
communities. An independent participatory evaluation shall be undertaken at least once
every five (5) years and before adoption of a successor NAP-WPS. The framework shall
include baseline assessments, periodic public progress reviews, and independent
midterm and end-of-plan evaluations, where feasible, with findings used to adjust agency
implementation plans. The indicators shall assess the quality and influence of women’s
participation, not merely the number of women attending activities.
SEC. 23. Annual Report to Congress. Not later than March 31 of each year, the
NSCWPS shall submit to the Senate and the House of Representatives and publish a
report on implementation of this Act, including progress against indicators, agency and
local implementation, expenditures, training outcomes, consultations, protection and
referral mechanisms, and corrective measures. Sensitive personal and security
information shall be withheld or aggregated as required by law.
SEC. 24. Legislative Oversight. The appropriate committees of the Senate and House
of Representatives exercising jurisdiction over women, peace, national defense, and
appropriations may conduct periodic oversight hearings and require reports in accordance
with their respective rules. The NSCWPS shall furnish such committees with
implementation and evaluation reports.
SEC. 25. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within one hundred twenty (120) days
from effectivity, OPAPRU and PCW, through the NSCWPS and in consultation with DILG,
DBM, relevant agencies, the Bangsamoro Government, and women’s civil society
organizations, shall issue implementing rules and regulations.
SEC. 26. Non-Diminution and Harmonization. Nothing in this Act shall diminish rights,
remedies, or institutional guarantees under Republic Act No. 9710, Republic Act No.
11054, other applicable laws, or the Constitution. Existing WPS policies and administrative
mechanisms shall remain effective insofar as they are consistent with this Act.
SEC. 27. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared invalid, the
remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in force.
SEC. 28. Repealing Clause. All laws, executive issuances, rules, and regulations
inconsistent with this Act are hereby modified or repealed accordingly.
SEC. 29. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.


Approved.
