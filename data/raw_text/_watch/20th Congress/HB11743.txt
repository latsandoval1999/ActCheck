                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Traffic enforcement is important for keeping roads safe and orderly.
However, One of the growing concerns among motorists is the proliferation of
improper traffic apprehensions. Some traffic enforcers may be pressured by
ticket quotas or incentive systems to issue a certain number of citations. This
can lead to unfair or baseless apprehensions of drivers who have not broken
any law.

       Being wrongly apprehended can cause drivers financial loss, stress, and
inconvenience. Although drivers can challenge an improper ticket, many do not
because the process is often slow, complicated, and time-consuming. Daily
wage earners, public utility vehicle drivers, and employees may lose income or
miss work just to contest a ticket. Because of this, some drivers choose to pay
the fine even when they are innocent.

      This bill seeks to protect drivers by compensating those who are
wrongfully apprehended for the time, inconvenience, and expenses they incur.
It also imposes administrative penalties on traffic personnel who make
improper apprehensions.

     The bill further prohibits traffic ticket quotas and incentive systems. By
removing these pressures, traffic enforcers can focus on enforcing the law fairly
and providing penalty thereof.

      Overall, the bill aims to make traffic enforcement fairer, more
transparent, and more accountable. It seeks to protect drivers from unjust
penalties while ensuring that traffic personnel follow the law and prioritize
public safety.

      In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

   AN ACT STANDARDIZING TRAFFIC APPREHENSION PROCEDURES,
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE TRAFFIC ADJUDICATION UNIT, PRESCRIBING
    RULES FOR CITATION PURPOSES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “STANDARDIZED
TRAFFIC APPREHENSION AND TRAFFIC ADJUDICATION ACT.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to maintain
peace and order, promote discipline on the roads, and improve the traffic
management system through efficient and fair implementation of traffic laws,
rules and regulations. To this end, the State shall protect the rights of
motorists and their well-being, provide reasonable compensation to those who
are improperly apprehended and impose administrative sanctions against
erring traffic enforcement personnel.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

      a. Motorist refers to the driver or rider of a motor vehicle;

      b. Traffic adjudication unit refers to the office, division, bureau or service
         of traffic enforcement authorities whose function includes the
         adjudication of contested traffic apprehensions;

      c. Traffic apprehension refers to an act of flagging or stopping a vehicle
         by a traffic enforcement personnel on an alleged traffic violation;

      d. Traffic enforcement authorities refer to the Land Transportation Office
         (LTO), Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA) and local
         government units, as well as any public entity as may hereinafter be
         created and authorized to implement and enforce traffic laws, rules
         and regulations; and

      e. Traffic enforcement personnel refer to organic personnel of traffic
         enforcement authorities and deputized personnel duly authorized by
         law to enforce and implement traffic laws, rules and regulations.
SECTION 4. Compensation. – If a motorist proves that a traffic apprehension
was improper and that they did not violate any traffic law, rule, or regulation,
they shall be entitled to compensation. The concerned traffic adjudication unit
must include the compensation in its decision.

If the motorist disagrees with the amount awarded, they may go to court only
for the purpose of determining the proper amount. The court’s decision will be
final and enforceable.

For this Act, a traffic apprehension is considered improper if it violates or fails
to follow existing laws, rules, or regulations.

SECTION 5. Amount of Compensation. –                The compensation shall be
calculated as follows:

   a.   If the motorist is unemployed or self-employed, three (3) times the
        prevailing minimum daily wage in the area where the apprehension
        occurred.

   b.   If the motorist is employed, three (3) times the motorist’s daily wage or
        salary, supported by proof such as a certificate of compensation or
        payslip.

The compensation will be multiplied by the number of times the motorist
appears before the traffic adjudication unit to contest the improper
apprehension. The motorist will also be reimbursed for necessary expenses,
such as transportation and accommodation, provided these are supported by
receipts. The period covered begins when the protest is filed and ends when the
decision becomes final.

SECTION 6. Additional Compensation. – If an improper traffic apprehension
causes a driver’s license to be confiscated or a person’s driving privileges to be
suspended, the motorist may receive additional compensation.

   a. The amount is based on the applicable daily minimum wage or the
      motorist’s daily wage, multiplied by the number of days the motorist
      could not work. This applies only to people whose jobs require a driver’s
      license or who earn a living by driving.

   b. If a vehicle is improperly impounded, its registered owner is entitled to
      compensation equal to the applicable Motor Vehicle User’s Charge
      (MVUC), as provided under Republic Act no. 8794, multiplied by the
      number of days the vehicle was impounded. Further, the traffic authority
      must also pay all associated cost like the towing and storage fees, repair
      or replace any damaged or missing vehicle parts, and cover damage to
      the vehicle’s cargo.

   c. The traffic enforcement authority must provide the driver or rider of a
      towed vehicle with a Traffic Violation Receipt (TVR), Ordinance Violation
      Receipt (OVR), or Impounding Receipt of Motor Vehicle (IRMV). If no
      receipt is issued, the towing and impounding are presumed to be
      improper and illegal.

   d. The motorist may report the incident, and the authority that accredited
      the towing company must immediately arrange for the vehicle’s release
      and return and provide the required compensation.
SECTION 7. Source of Compensation. – The amount needed to compensate
for all improper traffic apprehension shall be sourced from the traffic
enforcement authority concerned from the collection of fines and penalties for
traffic violations.

The concerned traffic enforcement authority must pay the compensation within
five (5) days after the decision becomes final, provided that the motorist has
submitted all required documents.

If the authority or its representative unjustifiably delays payment, the
compensation will accrue legal interest until it is fully paid.

SECTION 8. Prescription. – A motorist apprehended by any traffic
enforcement personnel may contest the traffic apprehension before the traffic
adjudication unit to which the traffic enforcement personnel belong within
sixty (60) days from the date of apprehension or from the date a notice of
apprehension is served.

SECTION 9. Traffic Adjudication Unit and Common Procedure. – Within 90
days after the Implementing Rules and Regulations is issued, every traffic
enforcement authority must establish a functioning traffic adjudication unit to
handle disputed traffic violations within their jurisdiction.

Each Traffic Adjudication unit shall promulgate a uniform rule of procedure
consistent with the standard to be set in the Implementing Rules and
Regulations.

SECTION 10. Duties of the Traffic Adjudication Unit. – In addition to their
existing duties, the traffic adjudication unit shall:

      a. Provide advisory that the apprehended motorist may seek legal
         assistance;

      b. Ensure an impartial, transparent and orderly conduct of the
         proceedings;

      c. Submit a report or result of the investigation to the head of agency,
         bureau or office of the erring traffic enforcement personnel, for
         appropriate administrative action; and

      d. Award and Order the release of compensation should there be a
         favorable award to the motorist.

SECTION 11. Rights of the Motorist. – The motorist is entitled to:

      a. Know the basis of apprehension and the identity and authority of the
         apprehending traffic enforcer;

      b. Seek redress, in person or through representation, for contested
         apprehension through the traffic adjudication unit or regular courts;

      c. Seek legal assistance from any legal authorities, if necessary, subject
         to existing rules or regulations;

      d. Reasonable expectation of privacy;

      e. The Right to be given compensation as provided under Section 5 & 6,
         if applicable;
SECTION 12. Privacy of the Motorist. The privacy of the motorist is the
utmost priority. Any photo or video taken during an apprehension may be used
only as evidence or for handling the related case. It must not be posted, shared,
livestreamed, or uploaded online without the motorist’s consent or a lawful
order.

Only authorized persons involved in the case may access the photo or video.
Anyone who improperly uses or shares it may face administrative, civil, or
criminal liability under applicable laws.

SECTION 13. Data Privacy- Traffic authorities may collect, process, retain,
and disclose a motorist’s personal information, photographs, or videos only
when legally authorized and necessary, in accordance with the Data Privacy
Act and other applicable privacy laws. They are responsible for protecting this
information and keeping it confidential. Without the motorist’s consent or a
valid court order, they may not publicly post, livestream, or distribute such
materials. Any unauthorized use or disclosure may be subject to legal penalties.

SECTION 13. Administrative Sanctions. – Any traffic officer who knowingly
or negligently causes an improper apprehension may face administrative
charges. The traffic authority shall, motu proprio, file the appropriate case
against the officer.

If the officer acted intentionally or maliciously, the officer must personally pay
the compensation awarded to the motorist, face the applicable penalties, and
repay the traffic authority for any amount it paid because of the wrongful
apprehension. The responsible officer or agency head who fails to file the
required administrative case may also face administrative liability.

SECTION 14. Training and Equipment. – Traffic enforcement authorities
must regularly hold training seminars so their personnel understand traffic
laws, rules, and procedures, especially those involving traffic apprehensions.

They must also make reasonable efforts to provide officers with the equipment
needed to enforce these laws, such as body cameras, decibel meters, speed
radar guns, and breath analyzers.

SECTION 15. Prohibition on Quota and Incentive Systems for Traffic
Apprehensions. - Traffic authorities, such as the LTO, MMDA, and Local
Government Units, directly or indirectly, require or encourage traffic enforcers
to meet a quota or target number of apprehensions or citations.

Any official or employee who directly or indirectly, require or encourage such a
quota system may face administrative penalties under civil service rules. They
may also face separate civil, criminal, or other administrative liability under
existing laws.

SECTION 16. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Transportation, DILG, MMDA,
and LTO shall issue the Implementing Rules and Regulations, including the
guidelines for the formulation of a uniform rules of procedure, for the effective
implementation of this Act.

SECTION 17. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder of this Act or any provision
not affected thereby shall remain in force and effect.
SECTION 18. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, rules and regulations and other issuances, or any part thereof,
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or amended
accordingly.

SECTION 19. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of
general circulation.
