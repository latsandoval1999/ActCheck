     AN ACT PROHIBITING THE IMPOSITION OF SYSTEM LOSS CHARGES ON

                                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

Electricity is a basic necessity and an essential public service that every Filipino household
depends on. Yet, for decades, Filipino consumers have been burdened by persistently high
electricity rates. According to the Department of Energy (DOE), the Philippines now has the
highest electricity rates in Southeast Asia.1 This longstanding problem is rooted in the
neoliberal framework established by the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) of
2001, which privatized the power industry and transferred control to private corporations.

As a result, among the inequitable charges imposed on consumers is the system loss charge.
System loss refers to the difference between the electricity purchased by a distribution utility
and the electricity actually delivered and billed to the end-users. It consists of technical losses,
which arise from the inherent limitations of electrical equipment and transmission lines, and
non-technical losses, which result from illegal connections, electricity theft, meter tampering,
billing errors, and other unauthorized activities.

Under the current regulatory framework, these losses are passed on to consumers, provided
they remain within the allowable caps prescribed by the Energy Regulatory Commission
(ERC). Consequently, Filipino consumers are compelled to pay for electricity they neither
consumed nor benefited from. This effectively shifts to the public the costs of a distribution
utility’s operational inefficiencies and its failure to prevent pilferage and other avoidable
losses.

The DOE estimates that consumers could save approximately five to ten percent (5% – 10%)
on their electricity bills, depending on a utility’s system loss rate,2 if system loss charges
were removed.

This bill therefore seeks to prohibit the collection of system loss charges from electricity
consumers and instead require distribution utilities to absorb all costs arising from system
losses. This proposed measure will provide immediate relief to Filipino families, workers,
and small business owners who already reserve a significant portion of their income to
electricity expenses.

In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

  AN ACT PROHIBITING THE IMPOSITION OF SYSTEM LOSS CHARGES ON
   ELECTRICITY CONSUMERS, STRENGTHENING ACCOUNTABILITY OF
   ELECTRIC POWER INDUSTRY PARTICIPANTS IN REDUCING SYSTEM
                            LOSSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Scrap System Loss Charges
Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to protect the interests of
consumers and ensure the delivery of affordable and reliable electric power. The State
recognizes that electricity is a basic necessity and a vital service for the welfare of the
Filipino people.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act:

   (a) Electric Power Industry Participant refers to any person or entity engaged in the
       generation, transmission, distribution, or supply of electricity;
   (b) System Loss refers to the difference between the electricity purchased by an electric
       power industry participant and the electricity consumed by and sold to end-users,
       including both technical losses and non-technical losses;
   (c) Technical Losses refer to losses inherent in the physical delivery of electricity, such as
       those due to resistance in conductors, transformers, and other equipment;
   (d) Non-Technical Losses refer to losses caused by electricity theft, illegal connections,
       meter tampering, and billing errors;
   (e) End-User refers to any person or entity requiring the supply and delivery of electricity
       for its own use.
SECTION 4. Prohibition on the Imposition of System Loss Charges. - No electric power
industry participant shall charge, collect, or pass on to end-users any charges representing
system losses, whether technical or non-technical. All costs associated with system losses
shall be the sole responsibility of the electric power industry participant and shall not be
reflected in the electricity bills of end-users.

SECTION 5. Prohibition on Recovery of Past System Loss Costs. - No electric power
industry participant shall be allowed to recover, through rates or other charges, the cost of
system losses incurred prior to the effectivity of this Act. Any pending application before the
Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) for the recovery of such costs shall be deemed
dismissed.

SECTION 6. Subsidies to Cooperatives and Small Distribution Utilities. - The
government shall provide subsidies to electric cooperatives and small distribution utilities for
the upgrade of power lines, metering systems, and other facilities or measures necessary to
reduce technical and non-technical losses.

SECTION 7. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the immediate implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - The Energy Regulatory Commission,
in consultation with the Department of Energy and consumer groups, shall promulgate the
implementing rules and regulations of this Act within fifteen (15) days from its effectivity.

SECTION 9. Separability Clause. - If any provision or portion of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions hereof shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 10. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, issuances, rules and
regulations or parts thereof which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SECTION 11. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
