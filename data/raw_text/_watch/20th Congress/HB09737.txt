                            AN ACT
   STRENGTHENING THE KATARUNGANG PAMBARANGAY SYSTEM BY
  PROVIDING NATIONAL AND LOCAL SUPPORT MECHANISMS FOR THE
  LUPON NG TAGAPAMAYAPA, INSTITUTIONALIZING TRAINING-BASED
             INCENTIVES, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The Katarungang Pambarangay system under Republic Act No. 7160,
otherwise known as the Local Government Code of 1991, particularly under Title
I, book III (Sections 399-422), serves as the State’s primary mechanism for
community-based dispute resolution. It promotes the amicable settlement of
disputes at the barangay level, thereby decongesting court dockets, reducing
litigation costs, and preserving social harmony within communities.

      At the core of this system is the Lupon ng Tagapamayapa, constituted
pursuant to Section 399 of the Local Government Code, and vested with
mediation and conciliation functions under Sections 40 to 404 thereof. Lupon
members, who are appointed by the Punong Barangay, perform quasi-judicial
dispute resolution functions without compensation, motivated largely by civic
and community service.

       While the law recognizes the importance of the Lupon, existing provisions
on support mechanisms remain largely decentralized and discretionary,
resulting in uneven implementation across local government units. Although
Section 393 (d) of the Local Government Code authorizes the grant of allowances,
honoraria, and other emoluments to barangay officials and personnel subject to
budgetary limitations, there is no uniform national framework specifically
addressing the welfare, training, and incentives of Lupon members.

      Further, while the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG) has issued administrative guidelines to strengthen KP implementation,
including training programs and performance monitoring, these remain policy-
based and not statutorily institutionalized, resulting in gaps in capacity-
building, standardization, and sustainability.

       This measure seeks to address these gaps by institutionalizing a national
support framework for Lupon members, providing a carefully structured, non-
salaried honorarium that respects the fiscal autonomy of local government units
and avoids the creation of an employer-employee relationship, and linking
incentives to training, certification, and performance standards. It likewise
strengthens capacity-building mechanisms and ensures the professionalization
of Lupon service while preserving its community-based and voluntary character.

       In strengthening the Katarungang Pambarangay system, this measure
affirms the State’s commitment to accessible, efficient, and culturally responsive
justice at the barangay level.

      Despite their crucial role, Lupon members often receive uneven and
inadequate support, with incentives varying widely depending on local
government income classification. This disparity affects morale, capacity, and
the overall effectiveness of barangay-based dispute resolution, particularly in
low-income and geographically isolated communities.

       This measure seeks to strengthen the Katarungang Pambarangay system
by providing a nationally supported, fiscally responsible framework for Lupon
assistance. It establishes a nationally funded, minimum honorarium, subject to
training and certification, and authorizes tiered incentives tied to capacity-
building and performance. It preserves the voluntary and non-salaried nature of
Lupon service, avoids the creation of Plantilla positions, and respects fiscal
autonomy.

 Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Lupon ng
Tagapamayapa Support and Strengthening Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to strengthen
community-based dispute resolution, ensure the effective implementation of the
Katarungang Pambarangay system, and recognize the voluntary service of Lupon
ng Tagapamayapa members. The State also seeks to maintain fiscally
sustainable incentives in compliance with budgetary and audit laws, while
promoting capacity-building, accountability, and performance in barangay
justice.

SECTION 3. Nature of Lupon Membership. — Lupon ng Tagapamayapa
members shall remain non-salaried volunteers and shall not be deemed
government employees, elective officials, or holders of Plantilla positions.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed as placing Lupon members under the
Salary Standardization Law, the Position Classification and Compensation
System, or any other law governing standardized government salaries.
SECTION 4. National Minimum Honorarium Program. — There is hereby
established a National Lupon Honorarium Program, to be administered by the
Department of the Interior Local Government (DILG), subject to the availability
of funds and annual appropriations.

      1. Eligibility. Lupon ng Tagapamayapa members shall be eligible for the
         minimum honorarium under this program upon:
            a. Valid appointment by the Punong Barangay; and
            b. Completion of mandatory training and certification prescribed
                under Section 6 of this Act.

      2. Nature of Honorarium. The minimum honorarium shall be non-regular,
         non-fixed, and non-salaried, and shall not create an employer-
         employee relationship between the government and the Lupon
         members.


SECTION 5. Training-Based and Tiered Incentives –

   1. Advanced Training Incentives- Lupon members who complete advanced,
      specialized, or continuing education programs may receive incentives,
      subject to guidelines issued by the DILG.
   2. Performance-Based Incentives. Incentives may be granted based on
      objective performance indicators, including but not limited to :

           a. Case disposition rates;
           b. Timeliness of resolution;
           c. Compliance with Katarungan Pambarangay procedures; and
           d. Ethical and professional conduct.
  3. Form of Incentives. Incentives under the section may be in the form of
     honoraria, per diems, allowance, or non-monetary recognition, and shall
     not be treated as compensation or salary.

SECTION 6. Capacity-Building and Certification. -- The DILG, in
coordination with the Department of Justice (DOJ), shall develop and implement
standardized training and certification programs for Lupon ng Tagapamayapa
members.

   1. The training programs shall include, but not be limited to:
        a. Mediation and conciliation skills;
        b. Legal and procedural updates on Katarungan Pambarangay;
        c. Ethics, confidentiality, and due process; and
         d. Record-keeping and documentation.

   2. Completion of prescribed training shall be a prerequisite for eligibility
      under Sections 4 and 5 of this Act.

SECTION 7. Insurance and Logical Support. Subject to the availability of
funds, Lupon members may be provided with:

         a. Group life or accident insurance coverage while performing official
            function; and
         b. Logistical and training support, including meals, transportation,
            and materials during officially sanctioned activities.

Such support shall not be construed as compensation or employment benefits.

SECTION 8. Role of Local Government Units. Local government units may,
subject to the enactment of an ordinance and the availability of funds:

            a. Grant additional honoraria or incentives to Lupon members;
            b. Provide logistical support and facilities for Katarungang
               Pambarangay proceedings; and
            c. Adopt local performance recognition mechanisms.


SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DILG, in coordination with the DOJ, the
Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the Commission on Audit
(COA), shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations necessary to
carry out the provisions of this Act.

SECTION 10. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, orders, rules, and regulations, other
issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with any provision of this Act, are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SECTION 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
