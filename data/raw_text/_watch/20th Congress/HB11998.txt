                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Public infrastructure is built using the people's money. Every road, bridge, school building,
hospital, drainage system, and flood-control structure represents public resources entrusted to the
government to improve lives and protect communities. However, recurring reports of corruption,
questionable procurement practices, ghost projects, substandard construction, and irregularities in
government infrastructure spending have raised serious concerns about how these funds are being
utilized.

       The magnitude of these concerns cannot be ignored. According to the Commission on Audit
(COA), 747 infrastructure projects implemented by the Department of Public Works and Highways
(DPWH) in 2024 were found to be technically defective, unusable, or idle, with deficiencies
amounting to at least ₱6.459 billion. These findings demonstrate that weaknesses in project
supervision, inspection, and implementation can translate into billions of pesos in wasted public
resources.

        The problem is particularly alarming in flood-control infrastructure. From July 2022 to May
2025, the DPWH reportedly implemented approximately ₱548 billion worth of flood-control projects
nationwide. Subsequent COA fraud audits uncovered alleged ghost projects, unauthorized relocation
of project sites, missing structures, and questionable claims of project completion. In several cases,
projects were reportedly declared completed and paid for despite discrepancies between the approved
project locations and the actual structures on the ground.

         These are not merely accounting irregularities. Every peso lost to corruption is a classroom
that remains unfinished, a road that deteriorates prematurely, a community left vulnerable to flooding,
and a public service that Filipinos are deprived of. When infrastructure projects exist only on paper,
it is the ordinary Filipino who ultimately bears the cost.

        While existing auditing and inspection mechanisms are essential, periodic reports and
occasional physical inspections may not always provide timely and sufficient documentation of actual
construction activities. The public deserves more than project announcements, accomplishment
reports, and photographs submitted after the fact. There must be a reliable and continuous means of
determining whether a project is genuinely being implemented, where it is being constructed, and
whether its actual progress corresponds to the funds being released.

        This measure therefore seeks to institutionalize a technology-enabled Public Infrastructure
Monitoring System (PIMS) that will provide real-time and publicly accessible information on
government-funded infrastructure projects. It requires contractors to install and maintain timelapse
cameras at project sites and regularly upload time-stamped and geotagged video documentation. It
likewise requires implementing agencies to maintain updated online project dashboards containing
essential information on project costs, contractors, funding sources, implementation schedules,
physical accomplishments, and project status.

        By making infrastructure implementation more transparent and verifiable, this measure
strengthens public oversight, discourages fraudulent practices, facilitates the early detection of
irregularities, and promotes accountability among government agencies and private contractors. It
also empowers citizens, civil society organizations, and oversight institutions to participate in
safeguarding public funds.

        Public infrastructure must be built on solid foundations, not on corruption, deception, or paper
accomplishments. The Filipino people deserve projects that are completed according to approved
plans, specifications, costs, and timelines.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
      ESTABLISHING A TECHNOLOGY-ENABLED PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE
     MONITORING SYSTEM (PIMS) FOR PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE PROJECTS,
     REQUIRING THE SUBMISSION OF PROJECT IMPLEMENTATION DATA AND
          TIMELAPSE DOCUMENTATION, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Public Infrastructure Monitoring
System (PIMS) Act."

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – The State recognizes that real-time and continuous visual
documentation of construction activities is essential to ensuring that public funds are spent efficiently,
contractors fulfill their contractual obligations, and infrastructure projects are completed in accordance
with approved plans, specifications, and timelines. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote transparency, accountability, efficiency, and public participation in the implementation of
government-funded public infrastructure projects through the use of digital technologies for real-time
monitoring and reporting.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

(a) Contractor refers to any person, partnership, corporation, joint venture, cooperative, association,
or other juridical entity duly licensed under Republic Act No. 4566, or the Contractors' License Law,
as amended, that undertakes, contracts, or is engaged by a government agency to implement, construct,
repair, improve, rehabilitate, maintain, or complete a public infrastructure project;

(b) Implementing Agency refers to any national government agency, government-owned or controlled
corporation (GOCC), state university or college (SUC), local government unit (LGU), or other
government entity responsible for implementing a public infrastructure project funded wholly or
partially from public funds;

(c) Public Infrastructure Project refers to any construction, rehabilitation, improvement, repair, or
maintenance project involving roads, bridges, buildings, flood control systems, water supply systems,
seaports, airports, and other physical infrastructure funded wholly or partially from public funds;
(d) Public Funds refers to all moneys collected or received by the government, including national
funds, local government funds, Official Development Assistance (ODA) funds, and any other funds
managed by or on behalf of any government entity;

(e) Public Infrastructure Monitoring System (PIMS) refers to the government digital platform used for
monitoring and reporting the progress of public infrastructure projects;

(f) Timelapse Video refers to a video recording produced by capturing images at a set interval over a
period of time and then played back at a higher speed, resulting in a compressed visual record of
construction activities occurring at a project site;

(g) Project Site refers to the physical location where a public infrastructure project is being
implemented; and

(h) Geotagged Data refers to electronic data associated with a specific geographic location through
coordinates, location identifiers, or similar technologies capable of identifying the physical location of
a project site.

        SEC. 4. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all public infrastructure projects
funded wholly or partially from public funds and covered by contracts entered into after the effectivity
of this Act.

        SEC. 5. Establishment of the Public Infrastructure Monitoring System (PIMS). – The
Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), in coordination with the Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT) shall hereby establish and administer a Public
Infrastructure Monitoring System, or PIMS, which shall serve as a real-time digital platform for the
monitoring and reporting of government public infrastructure projects.

All public infrastructure projects covered by this Act shall be integrated into the PIMS. The contractor
for each project shall be required to upload timelapse videos under Section 6 of this Act, and to submit
timely and accurate implementation data, progress reports, and such other information as may be
required for the effective operation of the PIMS.

                 SEC. 6. Project Dashboard. – Each implementing agency shall maintain and
         regularly update a PIMS Project Dashboard for every covered public infrastructure project
         under its jurisdiction. The dashboard shall contain, at a minimum, the following information:

         (a) Project title and description;

         (b) Implementing agency;

         (c) Name of contractor and major subcontractors, where applicable;

         (d) Contract amount;

         (e) Funding source;

         (f) Contract duration and target completion date;

         (g) Notice to Proceed date;

         (h) Percentage of physical accomplishment;
         (i) Timelapse videos and related progress documentation;

         (j) Implementation status; and

         (k) Such other information as may be necessary to promote transparency and accountability.

       The PIMS Project Dashboard shall be publicly accessible through an online portal.

       SEC. 7. Mandatory Timelapse Video Upload Requirement. – All contractors covered under
Section 4 of this Act shall be required to:

(a) Install and maintain at least one (1) timelapse camera at each project site, positioned to capture a
comprehensive view of the ongoing construction activities. For large-scale projects involving multiple
construction fronts, the implementing agency may require the installation of additional cameras;

(b) Upload timelapse videos of construction progress to the PIMS at least once every two (2) weeks,
or at such intervals as may be prescribed in the project's Contract and applicable implementing rules
and regulations, from the commencement of construction until the issuance of the Certificate of
Completion and Final Acceptance by the government;

(c) Ensure that timelapse videos uploaded to the PIMS clearly capture construction activities, are time-
stamped, geotagged, and are of sufficient clarity and resolution to allow effective monitoring of project
progress and verification of project conditions;

(d) Maintain physical storage or cloud backup of all timelapse footage for a period of at least ten (10)
years from the date of final acceptance of the project, and make such footage available to authorized
government agencies and oversight bodies upon request; and

(e) Submit a written certification, signed by the contractor's authorized representative and the project
engineer, accompanying each timelapse video upload, attesting that the uploaded footage is authentic,
unaltered, and accurately represents the actual state of the project at the time of recording.

      SEC. 8. PIMS Standards. – The DPWH, in coordination with the DICT, shall ensure that the
PIMS is capable of:

(a) Receiving, storing, and organizing timelapse video uploads from contractors nationwide;

(b) Providing public access to all timelapse video uploads, subject to reasonable limitations on
bandwidth and data protection;

(c) Generating automated alerts to concerned government agencies when contractors fail to comply
with required upload schedules;

(d) Integrating timelapse video data with other project monitoring metrics such as financial
disbursements, inspection reports, and project milestones;

(e) Maintaining an audit trail of all uploads, modifications, and access to uploaded materials; and

(f) Generating dashboard summaries and project status reports for public viewing.
The DPWH and the DICT shall ensure that the PIMS is integrated and interoperable with existing and
future government digital platforms to promote transparency, accountability, and effective monitoring
of public infrastructure projects and the utilization of public funds.

        SEC. 9. Independent Verification, Data Integrity, and Financial Transparency. – To
ensure the accuracy, authenticity, and reliability of information submitted to the Public Infrastructure
Monitoring System (PIMS), the following measures shall be implemented:

(a) Independent Verification. – All covered public infrastructure projects shall be subject to
independent technical verification of their reported physical accomplishments, project locations, and
geotagged timelapse documentation. The implementing agency shall engage qualified and independent
technical personnel or third-party verifiers who have no actual or potential conflict of interest with the
contractor or project implementer. Such verification shall include, when necessary, actual site
inspections and comparison of submitted documentation against approved plans, specifications, and
project implementation schedules;

(b) Protection Against Manipulation of Records. – The DPWH, in coordination with the DICT, shall
establish appropriate technical and security measures to prevent the unauthorized alteration, deletion,
fabrication, or manipulation of timelapse videos, geotagged data, project reports, and other records
uploaded to the PIMS. These measures shall include secure digital storage, time-stamped and
geotagged records, tamper-evident audit trails, and appropriate authentication and verification
mechanisms to preserve the integrity and traceability of all submitted information. Original video
recordings and associated metadata shall be preserved and made available to authorized oversight and
investigating authorities upon request;

(c) Public Disclosure of Contract Variations. – All approved variation orders, change orders,
contract amendments, revised plans and specifications, extensions of contract duration, and
adjustments in project costs shall be disclosed through the PIMS Project Dashboard. Such disclosures
shall include the justification, corresponding amount, date of approval, and approving authority,
together with the relevant supporting documents, subject to applicable laws on confidentiality and data
protection;

(d) Transparency in Actual Disbursements. – The implementing agency shall regularly disclose the
actual financial disbursements for each covered public infrastructure project, including the amount
released, date of payment, recipient or payee, and corresponding progress billing or payment
milestone. Such information shall be reconciled with the reported physical accomplishments of the
project and made publicly accessible through the PIMS Project Dashboard, subject to applicable laws;
and

(e) Reporting and Accountability. – The results of independent verification, including any
discrepancies between reported and actual project accomplishments, suspected irregularities, or
inconsistencies in financial and physical progress, shall be documented and reported to the
implementing agency and made accessible to appropriate government oversight bodies. Suspected
fraud, falsification, or other violations of existing laws shall be referred to the appropriate investigative
or prosecutorial authorities, without prejudice to the conduct of independent audits by the Commission
on Audit (COA).

        SEC. 10. Responsibilities of the Implementing Agency. – The implementing agency shall:

(a) Include the timelapse video upload requirement as a standard provision in all contracts for covered
public infrastructure projects;
(b) Ensure the timely and accurate updating of the PIMS Project Dashboard for projects under its
jurisdiction;

(c) Monitor contractor compliance with the timelapse upload requirement and incorporate such
compliance into project monitoring reports;

(d) Include compliance with the timelapse upload requirement as a condition for the processing and
release of progress billings; and

(e) Ensure that no progress billing shall be processed unless the contractor is compliant with all
applicable timelapse upload requirements.

        SEC. 11. Prohibited Acts. – The following acts are prohibited under this Act:

(a) Failure or refusal to submit or upload the implementation data, progress reports, timelapse videos,
or other information required under this Act;

(b) Uploading of altered, edited, fabricated, or misrepresented timelapse videos;

(c) Deliberate obstruction, tampering, or destruction of timelapse recording equipment;

(d) Submission of false certification;

(e) Unauthorized access to, deletion of, or tampering with timelapse videos stored in the PIMS.

       SEC. 12. Penalties. – Any person who willfully commits any of the prohibited acts under
Section 10 involving fraud, falsification, deliberate tampering, or other intentional misconduct shall,
upon conviction, be punished with a fine of up to Three Million Pesos (P3,000,000.00), without
prejudice to suspension or blacklisting under existing procurement laws and any applicable civil or
criminal liability under existing laws.

If the offender is a juridical entity, the responsible officers who authorized or knowingly participated
in the commission of the violation shall be held liable.

Public officials found liable for willfully violating this Act shall, in addition to the applicable penalties
under this Act, be subject to the appropriate administrative, civil, and criminal sanctions under existing
laws.

        SEC. 13. Evidentiary Value. – Timelapse videos, raw footage, certifications, upload logs,
metadata, audit trails, and other electronic records generated, maintained, or stored within the PIMS
shall be admissible as electronic evidence in accordance with the Rules on Electronic Evidence and
other applicable laws.

        SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Rules and regulations necessary for the
effective enforcement of the provisions of this Act shall be formulated by the DPWH, in coordination
with the DICT and the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), and shall be
implemented within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act.

        SEC. 15. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement the provisions of this Act
shall be charged against the current year's appropriations of the DPWH. Thereafter, such amounts as
may be necessary for the continued implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act (GAA).
        SEC. 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid or unconstitutional,
the other provisions not so declared shall remain in force and effect.

       SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders and rules and regulations contrary to
or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.

        SEC. 18. Effectivity. – Notwithstanding the non-issuance of the implementing rules and
regulations, this Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in
a newspaper of general circulation.

        Approved.
