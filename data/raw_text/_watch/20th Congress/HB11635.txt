                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The alarming rise of violence in schools involving minors calls for
strengthened measures to protect children and promote safe learning
environments. Recent incidents involving violence in schools, including
shootings, knife attacks, and the confiscation of weapons from minors,
underscore the need to address children's access to dangerous products and
realistic toy weapons that may create safety risks in schools and communities.

       The 1987 Constitution provides the foundation for this measure. Article II,
Section 13 declares that the State recognizes the vital role of the youth in nation-
building and shall promote and protect their physical, moral, spiritual,
intellectual, and social well-being. Article XV, Section 3(2) further mandates the
State to defend the right of children to assistance, including proper care and
protection from all forms of neglect, abuse, cruelty, exploitation, and other
conditions prejudicial to their development.

      This bill builds upon existing laws and policies, including:

      1) Republic Act No. 7610, otherwise known as the Special Protection of
         Children Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act, which
         provides special protection to children from abuse, exploitation, and
         discrimination;

      2) Republic Act No. 10627, otherwise known as the Anti-Bullying Act of
         2013, which provides measures to prevent and address bullying and
         violence in schools;

      3) Republic Act No. 11934, otherwise known as the SIM Card Registration
         Act, insofar as applicable to measures promoting accountability in
         digital transactions; and
      4) Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the Local Government Code
         of 1991, which provides the framework for local government action and
         community-based programs.

       Research on children's exposure to weapon-related toys and violent stimuli
has identified potential short-term associations with aggressive cognition or
behavior, although such exposure alone has not been established as a predictor
of later criminality. These findings underscore the importance of age-appropriate
safeguards, responsible supervision, and preventive education in school and
community environments.

      This bill therefore seeks to:

      a) Prohibit the sale, distribution, or provision of specified harmful
         products and realistic toy weapons to minors;

      b) Establish reasonable age-verification requirements for transactions
         involving restricted products, including online transactions, while
         respecting applicable data privacy laws;

      c) Mandate school awareness and prevention programs to promote peace,
         conflict resolution, responsible behavior, and child safety;

      d) Strengthen coordination among national government agencies, local
         government units, schools, parents, and community organizations; and

      e) Provide  appropriate mechanisms for monitoring, reporting, and
         enforcement while ensuring that children who may come into
         possession of prohibited products are treated primarily as children
         requiring protection and appropriate intervention.

      This measure is not merely about restricting toy guns, knives, or other
products. It seeks to strengthen the State's preventive approach to child
protection by addressing children's access to products that may endanger their
safety, while promoting education, responsible behavior, and a culture of peace
in schools and communities.

      The immediate passage of this bill is, therefore, earnestly sought.

                            “AN ACT
      PROVIDING MEASURES TO PREVENT MINORS FROM
      PURCHASING PRODUCTS THAT ARE HARMFUL OR MAY INCITE
      VIOLENCE,   INCLUDING   REALISTIC TOY    WEAPONS,
      MANDATING SCHOOL AWARENESS PROGRAMS, AND FOR
      OTHER PURPOSES”

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

     Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Child Protection
Against Harmful Products and School Awareness Act of 2026.

      Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to:

      1. Protect children from harmful products that may endanger their safety,
         health, and well-being;

      2. Promote awareness and education in schools to prevent violence and
         foster a culture of peace, respect, responsibility, and responsible
         citizenship;

      3. Uphold the rights of children under Article XV of the 1987 Constitution,
         Republic Act No. 7610, otherwise known as the “Special Protection of
         Children Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act,” and
         Republic Act No. 10627, otherwise known as the “Anti-Bullying Act of
         2013”; and

      4. Strengthen the regulation of sales and online transactions involving
         minors, consistent with Republic Act No. 11934, otherwise known as
         the “SIM Card Registration Act,” Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise
         known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012,” and other applicable laws.
       Section 3. Prohibited Acts. - No person, establishment, or entity shall
sell, offer for sale, distribute, deliver, or otherwise provide to a minor, whether
directly or through an online transaction, any of the following:

      1. Firearms, ammunition, and explosives;

      2. Bladed weapons, such as knives, daggers, and similar instruments;

      3. Toy guns, replicas, and other imitation weapons that closely resemble
         real firearms; and

      4. Other harmful products as may be identified and classified by the
         Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), in consultation with the
         Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) and other
         concerned agencies, based on clear, reasonable, and objective criteria
         established under the implementing rules and regulations.

      No seller, distributor, provider, or other person or entity covered by this
Act shall knowingly facilitate, assist in, or circumvent the prohibition under this
Section, including through online transactions or through the use of another
person to purchase or obtain a product on behalf of a minor.

      Nothing in this Section shall be construed to prohibit products or activities
otherwise authorized by law, subject to applicable age restrictions, licensing
requirements, and safety regulations.

       Section 4. Duties of Sellers and Retailers. - Sellers, retailers,
distributors, and online platforms covered by this Act shall:

         1. Require valid proof of age before selling, offering for sale,
            distributing, or otherwise providing restricted products to a minor;

         2. Adopt reasonable and reliable age-verification mechanisms for
            online transactions involving restricted products, which may utilize
            government-issued identification or other lawful means of
            verification, consistent with Republic Act No. 11934, Republic Act
            No. 10173, and other applicable laws;

         3. Ensure that personal information collected for age verification is
            processed only for legitimate purposes, is adequately protected, and
            is not retained longer than necessary, in accordance with applicable
            data privacy laws and regulations; and

         4. Display conspicuous notices in physical stores and, where
            applicable, on online platforms prohibiting the sale, distribution, or
            provision of restricted products to minors.
      Section 5. Role of Government Agencies. - The following government
agencies shall have the following responsibilities:

     1. Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD): Conduct
        information, education, and advocacy campaigns on child protection,
        violence prevention, and the responsible use and handling of products
        covered by this Act;

     2. Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT):
        Coordinate, within its mandate and in consultation with the National
        Privacy Commission, with online platforms and other relevant
        stakeholders in promoting appropriate and privacy-compliant age-
        verification mechanisms;

     3. Philippine National Police (PNP): Enforce the applicable prohibitions
        under this Act, conduct lawful inspections and investigations, and
        coordinate with concerned government agencies and local government
        units (LGUs);

     4. Department of Education (DepEd): Integrate appropriate awareness
        and prevention programs in schools and issue relevant guidelines for
        their implementation; and

     5. Department of Trade and Industry (DTI): Issue the necessary
        implementing rules and regulations, establish and maintain the
        appropriate list or classification of products covered by this Act, and
        monitor compliance by manufacturers, importers, distributors, sellers,
        retailers, and online platforms, consistent with its mandate.

     Section 6. School Awareness Programs. - To strengthen prevention, the
DepEd shall:

     1. Develop and implement age-appropriate school-based awareness
        programs on the dangers associated with harmful products, realistic
        toy weapons, and other products covered by this Act;

     2. Integrate appropriate lessons or modules on peace education, conflict
        resolution, responsible behavior, child safety, and violence prevention
        into existing relevant subjects and programs;

     3. Conduct, at least annually, seminars, orientations, or workshops for
        students, parents, teachers, and other school personnel on child safety
        and violence prevention;

     4. Partner with LGUs, nongovernment organizations (NGOs), parent-
        teacher associations (PTAs), and other relevant stakeholders in
         sustaining community-based awareness and prevention campaigns;
         and

      5. Strengthen existing school-based guidance, counseling, and referral
         mechanisms to provide appropriate support to children exhibiting
         behavioral or other concerns associated with exposure to violence or
         harmful products.

      Section 7. LGU Partnership Provisions. - LGUs, through their existing
   Local Councils for the Protection of Children (LCPCs) and, at the barangay
   level, the Barangay Council for the Protection of Children (BCPC), shall
   support the implementation of this Act by:

      1. Conducting community awareness drives in coordination with schools,
         PTAs, NGOs, and other relevant stakeholders;

      2. Enacting, where necessary and consistent with national law, local
         ordinances reinforcing the prohibitions and preventive measures
         provided under this Act;

      3. Providing, subject to applicable budgeting, accounting, and auditing
         laws, rules, and regulations, resources for training, seminars,
         information campaigns, and other child-protection activities related to
         this Act;

      4. Collaborating with the PNP, DepEd, DSWD, DTI, and other concerned
         agencies in reporting violations and implementing preventive and
         community-based programs; and

      5. Maintaining or strengthening appropriate community-based reporting
         and referral mechanisms for incidents involving minors' access to
         products covered by this Act.

      SECTION 8. Penalties. - Any person, establishment, or entity who violates
the prohibitions and requirements under this Act shall be subject to appropriate
administrative sanctions, including fines, suspension, or revocation of applicable
business permits or licenses, in accordance with this Act, existing laws, and its
implementing rules and regulations, without prejudice to civil or criminal liability
under existing laws.

      Where the acts constituting a violation also constitute an offense under
Republic Act No. 7610, otherwise known as the “Special Protection of Children
Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act,” or other applicable penal
laws, the offender shall be prosecuted and penalized in accordance with such
laws.
      No minor shall be subject to criminal or administrative penalty solely by
reason of the minor's purchase or possession of a product covered by this Act.
Instead, the minor shall, when appropriate, be referred to the parent or guardian,
school guidance personnel, social worker, or other appropriate child-protection
service for counseling, education, intervention, or other measures consistent
with the best interests of the child.

   Section 9. Exemptions. - This Act shall not apply to the legitimate
educational, cultural, historical, artistic, or theatrical use of replicas or imitation
weapons, provided that such use is authorized by the appropriate institution or
authority and undertaken in accordance with applicable safety requirements
and other relevant laws and regulations.

   The exemption provided in this Section shall not apply to the unauthorized
sale, distribution, or provision of such products to minors.

   Section 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), as
the lead implementing agency, in coordination with the DSWD, DICT, DepEd,
PNP, National Privacy Commission, and other concerned government agencies,
shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

    The implementing rules and regulations shall, among others, establish the
criteria for identifying and classifying harmful products covered by this Act,
prescribe appropriate age-verification procedures, provide guidelines for online
transactions, and establish mechanisms for inter-agency coordination,
reporting, monitoring, enforcement, and periodic review of the classification of
covered products.

   Section 11. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

    Section 12. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, or other issuances, or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions
of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

  Section 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
