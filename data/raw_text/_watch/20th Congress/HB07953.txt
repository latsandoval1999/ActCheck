                               AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING A NATIONAL FRAMEWORK FOR SUICIDE PREVENTION AND
CRISIS RESPONSE, CREATING THE SUICIDE PREVENTION AND CRISIS RESPONSE
CENTER, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

         The 2021 data of the World Health Organization show that the Philippines has a suicide
mortality rate of 5.2 males and 1.8 females per 100,000 population. While more men die by suicide,
women are more likely to experience suicidal ideation or attempt suicide. The higher completion rate
among men is partially due to the fact that men tend to choose more violent and highly lethal methods
for suicide, such as firearms, hanging, or jumping from heights, which have lower chances of survival.
In contrast, women are more likely to use methods like drug overdoses, which, are less immediately
fatal and allow for a greater chance of medical intervention. Alarmingly, it is also observed that suicide
rate among the 15-24 age group has been steadily increasing in recent years.

         This bill seeks to establish the Suicide Prevention and Crisis Response Center (SPCRC) under
the Department of Health. It also helps aims to provide tele-counseling, psychological first aid, and
referrals to mental health professionals for affected individuals. The proposed law also seeks to
establish, operate and maintain a 24/7 toll-free National Crisis Hotline which can be easily accessed by
affected individuals at the time of their weakness. Likewise, the SPCRC will provide reading, media,
and information materials on suicide prevention, crisis response, and stigma reduction.

         A range of factors - at the individual, relationship, community, and societal levels – contributes
to suicide risk. However, it has been found that the implementation of collaborative evidence-based
interventions is most effective in helping people who are at risk of suicide. Thus, this proposed measure
focuses on the conduct of coordinative efforts between the SPCRC, relevant national government
agencies and local government units (LGUs) in the implementation of community-based interventions,
safe spaces, and emergency response, with the end in view of minimizing, if not totally eliminating,
suicide among Filipinos.

        In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known and referred to as the “Suicide Prevention and
Crisis Response Act”.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is a declared policy of the State that the maintenance of peace
and order, the protection of life, liberty and property, and promotion of the general welfare are
essential for the enjoyment by all the people of the blessings of democracy.

The State recognizes suicide as a serious public health concern requiring a comprehensive,
compassionate, and community-driven response. The government shall develop accessible,
inclusive and science-based crisis intervention and suicide prevention programs to protect the
mental health and promote the emotional resilience of its citizens, especially the youth.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

   a) Suicide refers to the act of intentionally causing one’s own death.
   b) Crisis Response refers to timely intervention strategies aimed at persons in acute
      psychological, emotional, or mental distress.
   c) First Responders refer to persons designated or trained to respond to a suicide emergency,
      including police officers, emergency medical service workers, barangay health workers,
      social workers, disaster response personnel, security personnel and firefighters.

SEC. 4. Creation of the Suicide Prevention and Crisis Response Center (SPCRC). – There is
hereby created the Suicide Prevention and Crisis Response Center under the Department of Health
(DOH) tasked with:
    a) Providing tele-counseling, psychological first aid, and referrals to mental health
       professionals;
    b) Operating and maintaining a 24/7 toll-free National Crisis Hotline;
    c) Developing digital and SMS-based mental health support systems;
   d) Coordinating with local government units (LGUs) for community-based interventions, safe
      spaces, and emergency response;
   e) Providing and distributing reading, media, and information materials on suicide prevention,
      crisis response, and stigma reduction.

SEC. 5. Multi-Sectoral Suicide Prevention Framework. – The SPCRC shall formulate a multi-
sectoral suicide prevention framework in coordination with the following:
    a) Department of Education (DepEd), Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and
        Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) for learning-based
        mental health programs and suicide risk assessments;
    b) Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) and Civil Service Commission (CSC) for
        workplace wellness and burnout mitigation strategies;
    c) Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) for wellness and mental resilience of overseas
        Filipino workers;
    d) Philippine National Police (PNP), Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP), Philippine Coast
        Guard (PCG) and Bureau of Jail Management and Penology (BJMP) to train personnel on
        suicide risk detection and compassionate crises response;
    e) Media agencies to ensure responsible and ethical reporting of suicide cases.

SEC. 6. Hiring and Deployment of Psychologists in City Health Offices and Municipal Health
Offices. – The DOH shall hire and deploy at least one (1) licensed behavioral psychologist in every
city or municipality charged with the responsibility of coordinating and implementing suicide
prevention programs, including the creation of customized safety plan or crisis response plan for
affected citizens.

SEC. 7. Responsibilities of City Health Offices and Municipal Health Offices. – The
implementation of the programs under this Act shall be the responsibility of the City Health Office
and Municipal Health Office within their respective territorial jurisdictions.

SEC. 8. Mandatory Training. – All first responders, teachers, guidance counselors, CHO and
MHO psychologists, and human resource focal persons, shall undergo annual training in suicide
risk recognition and crisis de-escalation, to be administered by DOH and accredited providers.

SEC. 9. Suicide Surveillance and Research. – The SPCRC shall establish a Suicide Data and
Monitoring System, in coordination with the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) and LGUs, to
track suicide cases, risk patterns, and intervention outcomes. Suicide-related reports shall be
submitted annually to Congress and made accessible to stakeholders.

SEC. 10. Public Awareness Campaign. – The SPCRC, in coordination with the Philippine
Information Agency (PIA) and private partners, shall implement a nationwide education campaign
on suicide prevention, mental health literacy, and stigma reduction.

SEC. 11. Protection from Liability. – No trained first responder, volunteer, or accredited mental
health provider shall be held civilly or criminally liable for actions performed in good faith under
this Act.

SEC. 12. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall
be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the DOH. Thereafter, such sums as may be
necessary for the continuous implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.
SEC. 13. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The DOH shall, in coordination with other
relevant agencies, promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the effective implementation
of this Act within ninety (90) days after its approval.

SEC. 14. Separability Clause. – If any part or section of this Act is declared unconstitutional, such
declaration shall not affect the other parts or sections of this Act.

SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, and issuances inconsistent with any provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.

SEC. 16. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
