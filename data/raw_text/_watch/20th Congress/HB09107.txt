                               EXPLANATORY NOTE
Executive Order No. 18 (s. 1986) established the Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) to
oversee the regulation and orderly development of the sugar industry. Thereafter, Republic Act
No. 10659, otherwise known as the Sugarcane Industry Development Act of 2015, instituted
programs to enhance productivity, modernize, support farmers, and improve industry
competitiveness, thereby laying the foundation for sustained sectoral growth.
Despite these legislative milestones, the sugar industry now faces market volatility, rising
production costs, climate-related risks, infrastructure constraints, and outdated technical
standards. Collectively, these challenges underscore the need to strengthen the operational
capacity of existing institutions and programs to ensure the effective delivery of benefits
intended under existing law.
In response to these continuing challenges, this bill seeks to strengthen and operationalize the
mandates of the SRA under Executive Order No. 18 (s. 1986) and Republic Act No. 10659.
Specifically, it aims to:
   ●   Improve access for qualified producer organizations to programs, incentives, and
       support mechanisms;
   ●   Advance modernization in productivity, mechanization, logistics, research, and climate
       resilience;
   ●   Enhance market transparency and evidence-based planning through reliable industry
       data.
   ●   Establish standards for the licensing, review, and evaluation of mills, refineries, and
       covered entities under existing authority;
   ●   Strengthen the implementation capability of the SRA; and
   ●   Improve the utilization of the Sugarcane Industry Development Fund for programs that
       directly benefit the industry.
The proposed amendments are carefully designed to avoid unnecessary regulatory burdens or
unwarranted capital replacement requirements. They provide practical tools and balanced

appropriate safeguards.
Ultimately, by reinforcing institutional capacity, improving transparency, and promoting
modernization, this measure equips the sugar industry to address both longstanding and
emerging challenges in a coordinated and sustainable manner.
In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
     AMENDING CERTAIN PROVISIONS OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10659,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “SUGARCANE INDUSTRY DEVELOPMENT ACT
OF 2015,” TO MODERNIZE AND TO FACILITATE COMPETITIVENESS OF THE
                         SUGAR INDUSTRY

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:


Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as "A Stronger Sugar Industry Act".

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the declared policy that the State institute programs to
enhance productivity, modernize, support farmers, and improve sugar industry
competitiveness. The State shall also continue to strengthen the operational capacity of existing
institutions and programs to ensure the effective delivery of benefits, improve transparency,
and promote modernization to equip the sugar industry to address both longstanding and
emerging challenges in a coordinated and sustainable manner.

Section 3. Section 3 of Republic Act No. 10659, otherwise known as the Sugarcane Industry
Development Act of 2015, is hereby amended by adding a new paragraph at the end of the
section to read as follows:
      “Accredited sugarcane planters’ associations and cooperatives shall be entitled
      to equal participation in, and access to, all programs, incentives, support
      mechanisms, and benefits under this Act similar to those accorded to block
      farms, subject to accreditation and compliance with guidelines prescribed by
      the Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA)
      Such participation shall include eligibility for mechanization programs,
      farm-equipment grants, training and technical assistance, fertilizer and input
      support, financing and credit programs, and other modernization and
      productivity initiatives: Provided, That such associations and cooperatives

                                                3
     shall be duly accredited and compliant with SRA regulatory
     standards: Provided, further, That no program benefit under this Act shall be
     denied solely on the basis of organizational structure.”


Section 4. Section 6 of the same Act is hereby amended by adding a new paragraph (f) to read
as follows:
     “(f) The Sugar Regulatory Administration shall strengthen its institutional
     capacity to effectively carry out its audit, technical, engineering, data
     management, and compliance functions through human resource
     development, the filling of authorized positions, and the lawful engagement of
     qualified personnel, subject to existing laws. Beneficiaries of scholarship,
     training, or technical assistance programs funded under this Act may be
     required to render return service in accordance with guidelines issued by the
     Sugar Regulatory Administration, in coordination with the Department of
     Agriculture and other relevant agencies, where appropriate.”


Section 5. A new Section 7-A is hereby inserted after Section 7 of Republic Act No. 10659 to
read as follows:
     “Section 7-A. Sugarcane Industry Modernization Program. — The Sugar
     Regulatory Administration, in coordination with the Department of
     Agriculture, local government units, and relevant agencies, shall support and
     strengthen modernization initiatives to improve farm productivity,
     operational efficiency, climate resilience, and the competitiveness of the
     sugarcane industry.
     Such programs shall include, but not be limited to, irrigation development
     and management, farm mechanization and technology transfer, soil
     rejuvenation and fertility management, fertilizer and input support, technical
     assistance, extension services, capacity-building programs, and other lawful
     measures as may be provided in the implementing rules and regulations.
     Priority shall be given to small farmers, block farms, and accredited planter
     associations and cooperatives.
     Funding for this Section shall be charged to the Sugarcane Industry
     Development Fund (SIDF), subject to existing budgeting, appropriation,
     accounting, and auditing laws.”
Section 6. A new section denominated as Section 7-B is hereby inserted after Section 7-
A of the same Act to read as follows :
     “Section 7-B. Sugar Market Transparency and Monitoring. — Consistent
     with Executive Order No. 18 (s. 1986), this Act, and other applicable laws, the
     Sugar Regulatory Administration, in coordination with the Department of


                                              4
     Agriculture and other relevant agencies, shall maintain an integrated system
     for monitoring production, inventory, utilization, importation, exportation,
     and other market indicators relating to sugar, its by-products, and other
     sweeteners for evidence-based policy making, market transparency, and
     supply planning, consistent with the Philippines’ international trade
     obligations.
     Proprietary or commercially sensitive information submitted pursuant to this
     Section shall be protected, and only aggregated or non-confidential data shall
     be publicly disclosed, unless otherwise required by law.
     The Sugar Regulatory Administration shall establish reasonable and
     proportionate operational and performance standards for sugar mills,
     refineries, and other regulated entities for licensing, permit renewal, and
     industry oversight.
     The Sugar Regulatory Administration shall conduct inspections, reviews, and
     assessments on a periodic, risk-based, or for-case basis, including pre-season
     readiness assessments and post-season performance reviews relating to
     recovery, efficiency, and compliance.
     Findings may be considered in permit and licensing actions, and may give rise
     to appropriate corrective measures or sanctions as authorized under this Act
     and other applicable laws, subject to notice, hearing, and due process
     requirements under existing laws.
     Such standards shall promote efficiency, reliability, safety, fair treatment of
     industry participants, and lawful performance, and shall not, by reason of age
     alone, require the replacement of otherwise serviceable, safe, and efficient
     equipment.
     The Sugar Regulatory Administration shall publish quarterly market
     summaries and an annual industry performance report, and shall furnish
     copies thereof to the appropriate committees of Congress through the
     Department of Agriculture, in such form as may be prescribed in the
     implementing rules and regulations.”
Section 7. Section 9. of Republic Act No. 10659 is hereby amended to read as follows:

      “Section 9. Classification and Regulation of Supply of Sugar The Sugar
     Regulatory Administration (SRA), in the exercise of its regulatory authority,
     shall classify imported sugar in accordance with its appropriate classification
     at the time of importation, taking into account prevailing domestic supply
     conditions. The Bureau of Customs (BOC) shall require importers or
     consignees to secure from the SRA the appropriate classification of imported
     sugar prior to its release.



                                             5
The SRA shall exercise regulatory authority and supervision, consistent with
existing laws, over the production, processing, distribution, and monitoring of
sugar and its by-products, including molasses, bagasse, and other derivatives.

For purposes of ensuring market transparency, data integrity, informed
policy-making, and fair competition, the SRA shall establish and maintain a
system for the monitoring, data collection, and analysis of the supply,
importation, exportation, and use of other sweeteners that may serve as
substitutes for sugar.

In furtherance of this mandate, the SRA may require importers,
manufacturers, processors, distributors, and other relevant entities to register
for monitoring and reporting purposes and to submit periodic reports, data,
and such information as may be necessary for market analysis and policy
development, subject to existing laws and applicable trade obligations.

The Bureau of Customs (BOC) shall, consistent with applicable tariff and
trade laws, cooperate with and provide relevant importation data to the SRA
for purposes of data verification and monitoring.

The SRA shall coordinate with the Department of Health (DOH) and the Food
and Drug Administration (FDA) on matters concerning public health, food
safety, and nutrition.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to grant the SRA authority to regulate
product standards, registration, labeling, health claims, or to impose
restrictions on the lawful importation of products, which remain within the
mandates of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the Department of
Health (DOH), and other competent agencies.

For purposes of this Act, “other sweeteners” refers to caloric or non-caloric
substances, whether natural or synthetic, that impart a sweet taste or function
as sugar substitutes in food, beverage, or industrial applications, including but
not limited to sugar syrups, glucose, dextrose, fructose, high-fructose corn
syrup (HFCS), and high-intensity sweeteners such as aspartame, sucralose,
steviol glycosides, and similar substances recognized or approved by the Food
and Drug Administration (FDA) or the Codex Alimentarius Commission.

The SRA shall issue the necessary rules and regulations to implement this
Section in a transparent, non-discriminatory, and evidence-based manner
consistent with the World Trade Organization (WTO) Agreement on
Technical Barriers to Trade (TBT) and other applicable laws. Nothing herein
shall be construed to diminish or supersede the mandates of the Bureau of

      Customs (BOC), the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the Department
      of Health (DOH), or other competent agencies under existing laws.”

Section 8. Section 11 of Republic Act No. 10659 is hereby amended by adding a new paragraph
at the end of the section to read as follows:
    “Administrative fines, penalties, and other monetary sanctions collected
    pursuant to this Act shall accrue to the Sugarcane Industry Development Fund
    (SIDF), subject to existing budgeting, appropriation, accounting, and auditing
    laws. Amounts appropriated therefrom shall be used solely for programs and
    projects authorized under this Act.”
Section 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity
of this Act, the Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) in coordination with the Department of
Agriculture (DA), and representatives from the Luzon Federation of Sugarcane Growers Association, Inc.
(LUNZONFED), United Sugar Producers Federation (UNIFED) and Mindanao Federation of Sugarcane
Planters, Inc. (MINFED), shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations.

Section 10. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the other provisions and parts hereof not otherwise affected shall remain in
full force and effect.

Section 11. Repealing Clause. -All laws, decrees, orders, proclamations, rules and regulations,
or parts thereof which are inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or modified
accordingly.

Section 12. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
