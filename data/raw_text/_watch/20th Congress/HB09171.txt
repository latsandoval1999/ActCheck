                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Ticket scalping refers to the practice of purchasing admission tickets to
entertainment events and reselling them at prices far exceeding their original face
value, often exploiting high demand for concerts, sports matches, and festivals.

       In recent years, the rise of online resale platforms and the use of automated
purchasing tools or “bots” have made this practice more prevalent, allowing certain
individuals or groups to hoard large quantities of tickets and artificially limit supply.
While some argue that scalping reflects free market principles, it is widely regarded as
an abusive and unfair practice that inflates prices, restricts access for ordinary
consumers, and undermines the integrity of the ticketing system.1

       This issue has been underscored by recent events in the Philippines. During
the concert of a Filipino American singer, tickets originally priced at ₱1,500.00 were
resold online for as much as ₱15,000.00, sparking widespread public outrage.2
Similarly, in a concert of the K-pop girl group in the country, numerous complaints were
raised by fans regarding excessive resale prices. VIP tickets with an original price of
₱19,500.00 were reportedly resold by scalpers for up to ₱60,000.00 each.3 These

       This bill is therefore a necessary response to protect consumers and ensure
fair access to entertainment events. It seeks to curb exploitative practices by
prohibiting the unauthorized buying and selling of tickets, particularly those resold at
prices exceeding 10% above face value without proper authorization. It also imposes
penalties on related acts such as ticket hoarding, selling through unauthorized
channels, and failure to disclose accurate pricing information.
      Furthermore, the measure requires event organizers and ticketing entities to
adopt clear anti-scalping policies and provide public advisories, thereby promoting
transparency, accountability, and a more equitable ticketing system.

      Ultimately, the bill aims to restore public trust and ensure that access to live
events is determined by fairness rather than the ability of intermediaries to exploit
demand for profit.

      In the 20th Congress, similar bills were filed by Senator Francis N. Pangilinan,
Mark A. Villar, Erwin T. Tulfo, Joel Villanueva, and Representative Karen Hope F.
Garcia. This bill fully adopts the Senate Substitute Bill No. 1989.

       Early approval of this measure is requested.

 1          SECTION 1. Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Anti-Ticket Scalping Act."
 2
 3          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
 4   protect the interests of consumers, promote general welfare, and establish standards
 5   of conduct for business and industry.
 6
 7         Towards this end, the State shall implement measures to protect the consumers
 8   against deceptive, unfair, and unconscionable sales acts and practices.
 9
10         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - As used in this Act, the following terms are
11   defined as follows:
12
13      a) Admission Ticket refers to any document, voucher, token, item, barcode, quick
14         response (QR) code, or other form of access credentials, whether in physical,
15         printed, or digital format, that grants the holder the right to enter a venue or
16         participate in an Entertainment Event;
17
18      b) Automated Bot refers to a software, program, script, medium, or any other type
19         of technology or means that is designed to automatically purchase multiple
20         tickets online, and/or bypass security measures, access control systems,
21         Purchase Limit, or other technological control on a ticketing website,
22         application, or service, in a manner that disrupts fair access for the general
23         public;
24
25      c) Entertainment Event refers to any event, performance, exhibition, gathering,
26         occasion, occurrence or activity that provides amusement or recreation

 2          distribution of admission tickets such as sports shows, concerts, stage
 3          performances, beauty pageants, festivals, road shows, fan meets, movie
 4          screenings or any similar undertakings;
 5
 6      d) Event Organizer refers to a natural or juridical person who produces,
 7         coordinates, plans, manages, and executes an Entertainment Event;
 8
 9      e) Face Value refers to the purchase price of an Admission Ticket as determined
10         by the Event Organizer or its authorized ticketing agent, and printed on the
11         ticket itself, but shall exclude all applicable charges and fees set by Event
12         Organizer or its authorized ticketing agent;
13
14      f) Official Ticketing Platform refers to the: (i) online or electronic platform, website,
15         mobile application, device, or other similar online or electronic service; or (ii)
16         physical outlet, booth, store, or any other media or platform, that is/are
17         designated or authorized by the Event Organizer or its authorized ticketing
18         agent to handle the legitimate sale of Admission Tickets;
19
20      g) Purchase Limit refers to the maximum number of Admission Tickets that a
21         single consumer purchaser may legally purchase for a particular Entertainment
22         Event, that is determined by the Event Organizer and/or its authorized ticketing
23         agent, for specific Entertainment Events;
24
25      h) Resale or Reselling refers to the act of selling or, offering for sale, advertising,
26         listing, or otherwise conveying or transferring for consideration, any Admission
27         Ticket that has already been purchased or acquired by another; and
28
29      i) Unauthorized Seller refers to any person or entity selling or distributing
30         Admission Tickets without the written authorization, designation, or consent of
31         the Event Organizer or its authorized ticketing agent.
32
33          SEC. 4. Prohibition of Ticket Scalping. - The following acts shall constitute ticket
34   scalping, whether committed online or in person:
35
36      a) Offering, hoarding, selling, distributing, buying, dealing in, disposing of or
37         otherwise obtaining admission tickets for entertainment events, without the
38         written permission from the authorized event producer, organizer and
39         distributor, for the purpose of reselling for more than ten percent (10%) higher
40         than the face value price of the ticket. Provided, that the ten percent (10%)
41         increase or markup shall be subject to the periodic review and update by the
42         implementing agencies;
43
44      b) Financing, managing, or operating ticket scalping activities; and
45
46      c) Using Automated Bot, fake accounts, false identities, multiple accounts, or
47         other similar deceptive, misleading, fraudulent means, media, or technologies,
48         to gain unauthorized access to ticket inventories, bypass security controls and
49         measures, or circumvent the Purchase Limits, for the purpose of acquiring
50         Admission Tickets.

 2          SEC. 5. Other Offenses. - The following acts are likewise penalized under this
 3   Act:
 4
 5      a) Offering or selling admission tickets for entertainment events without providing
 6         the face value price on each ticket;
 7
 8      b) Selling admission tickets on any platform other than the authorized reselling

10
11      c) Willfully aiding or abetting in the commission of any of the offenses enumerated
12         under Section 4; and
13
14      d) Willfully attempting to commit any of the offenses enumerated under Section 4.
15
16           SEC. 6. Duties and Responsibilities of Entities Engaged in the Business of
17   Event Ticketing, Event Production, and Event Services. - Within ninety (90) days from
18   the issuance of the Implementing Rules and Regulations of this Act, all entities
19   engaged in the business of event ticketing, event production, and event services that
20   organizes, sells, distributes or resells admission tickets shall post, publish or display
21   digital and/or physical signages on their websites and on conspicuous places within
22   their premises, warning against ticket scalping. In addition, these entities are also
23   mandated to adopt, implement and enforce respective internal policy guidelines,
24   prevention and reporting mechanisms on ticket scalping.
25
26         SEC. 7. Institution of Complaint. - The Department of Justice (DOJ), the
27   Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) through its relevant bureaus or attached
28   agencies, and the Department of Interior Local Government (DILG), including all law
29   enforcement agencies, may institute a complaint for violation of this Act before the
30   appropriate court.
31
32          SEC. 8. Penalties. –
33
34      a) The following penalties shall be imposed upon any person found guilty of any
35         act prohibited in Section 4 hereof:

37                100,000.00) or imprisonment of six (6) months, or both, at the discretion
38                of the court;
39             2) For the second offense, a fine of Two hundred fifty thousand pesos (Php
40                250,000.00) or imprisonment of one (1) year, or both, at the discretion of
41                the court; and
42             3) For the third and subsequent offenses, a fine of Five hundred thousand
43                pesos (Php 500,000.00) or imprisonment of three (3) years, or both, at
44                the discretion of the court.
45
46      b) The following penalties shall be imposed upon any person found guilty of any
47         act prohibited in Sections 5 and 6 hereof:
48             1) For the first offense, a fine of Fifty thousand pesos (Php 50,000.00);
