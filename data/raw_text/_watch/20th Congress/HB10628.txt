                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Access to safe, effective, quality, and affordable medicines is a
fundamental component of the constitutional right to health. However,
recurring reports of medicine shortages, stock outs, expired medicines,
procurement inefficiencies, counterfeit products, fragmented inventory
systems, and wastage of publicly funded health commodities continue to
undermine the delivery of healthcare services throughout the country.

       Despite significant government expenditures for medicines, vaccines,
medical devices, diagnostics, and other health commodities, the absence of a
unified national system for forecasting, procurement planning, inventory
visibility, redistribution, traceability, and accountability often results in
avoidable losses, uneven distribution, supply disruptions, and reduced access
to essential medicines, particularly among indigent and vulnerable
populations.

      Recent audit findings and health sector assessments have highlighted
persistent challenges involving expired and unused medicines, delayed
procurement, inadequate forecasting, and limited visibility over inventories
across government health facilities. These weaknesses not only waste scarce
public resources but also compromise patient care and public health
outcomes.

      This proposed measure seeks to establish a National Medicine Security
System (NMSS), a comprehensive and integrated framework that will
strengthen the management of medicines and other health commodities
procured using public funds. The measure institutionalizes modern
forecasting systems, national inventory visibility, medicine shortage
monitoring, redistribution mechanisms, anti-counterfeit safeguards, track-
and-trace technologies, procurement transparency measures, and
accountability mechanisms throughout the government health supply chain.

      The bill further promotes efficient procurement through pooled
purchasing arrangements, framework contracting, strategic reserves for
essential medicines, public price transparency, beneficial ownership
disclosure, and data-driven monitoring systems capable of detecting
overpricing, procurement anomalies, and excessive wastage. By ensuring that
medicines reach the patients who need them, when they need them, the
measure supports the effective implementation of Universal Health Care and
strengthens public trust in government health programs.
      Ultimately, this measure aims to protect public funds, reduce wastage,
prevent shortages, strengthen supply chain resilience, combat counterfeit and
substandard products, and guarantee the continuous availability of essential
health commodities for all Filipinos.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                            “AN ACT
      ESTABLISHING A NATIONAL MEDICINE SECURITY SYSTEM
      FOR THE PROCUREMENT, FORECASTING, INVENTORY
      MANAGEMENT, TRACEABILITY, REDISTRIBUTION, ANTI-
      COUNTERFEIT PROTECTION, AND ACCOUNTABILITY OF
      MEDICINES, VACCINES, MEDICAL DEVICES, DIAGNOSTICS,
      AND OTHER HEALTH COMMODITIES PROCURED USING
      PUBLIC FUNDS, STRENGTHENING TRANSPARENCY AND
      INTEGRITY IN GOVERNMENT PROCUREMENT, PREVENTING
      WASTAGE, SHORTAGES, COUNTERFEIT INFILTRATION, AND
      PROCUREMENT FRAUD, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
      THEREFOR”

      SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Medicine
Security and Anti-Wastage Act of 2026."

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of
the State to ensure the continuous availability, accessibility, affordability,
quality, safety, efficacy, and integrity of medicines and other health
commodities procured using public funds. The State shall establish a unified
national system that prevents medicine shortages, eliminates avoidable
wastage, strengthens procurement transparency, prevents counterfeit and
substandard products from entering government supply chains, and
safeguards public funds from corruption and inefficiency.

      SECTION 3. Objectives. This Act shall pursue the following objectives:

(a) Eliminate avoidable expiry and wastage of medicines;
(b) Prevent stock shortages in government health facilities;
(c) Strengthen medicine security and supply chain resilience;
(d) Prevent procurement of counterfeit, falsified, smuggled, adulterated, and
unregistered products;
(e) Improve forecasting and procurement planning;
(f) Promote transparency and accountability;
(g) Reduce opportunities for corruption and overpricing;
(h) Strengthen support for Universal Health Care;
(i) Improve access of indigent patients to essential medicines.
       SECTION 4. Coverage. This Act shall apply to all government-funded
procurement, storage, inventory management, warehousing, transportation,
distribution, utilization, transfer, and disposal of:

(a) Medicines;
(b) Vaccines;
(c) Biologics;
(d) Medical devices;
(e) In-vitro diagnostic devices;
(f) Laboratory reagents;
(g) Emergency medical supplies;
(h) Public health commodities;
(i) Other health products as may be determined by the Department of Health.

      SECTION 5. Covered Government Entities. This Act shall apply to:

(a) Department of Health;
(b) Government hospitals;
(c) Specialty hospitals;
(d) Provincial governments;
(e) City governments;
(f) Municipal governments;
(g) Barangay health stations;
(h) Rural health units;
(i) Local government hospitals;
(j) Devolved hospitals;
(k) State universities and colleges operating health facilities;
(l) Government-owned and controlled corporations operating health facilities.

      SECTION 6. National Medicine Security System. A National Medicine
Security System (NMSS) is hereby established under the Department of
Health (DOH). The NMSS shall integrate forecasting, procurement planning,
inventory     management,     redistribution,  anti-counterfeit verification,
traceability, monitoring, and accountability mechanisms nationwide.

     SECTION 7. National Health Commodities Management Platform.
The DOH shall establish a centralized electronic platform that shall contain:

(a) Inventory balances;
(b) Batch and lot numbers;
(c) Expiry dates;
(d) Procurement information;
(e) Supplier information;
(f) Utilization rates;
(g) Distribution records;
(h) Traceability information;
(i) Stock movement records.


      SECTION 8. National Forecasting and Quantification Center. The
DOH shall establish a National Forecasting and Quantification Center
responsible for national demand forecasting using:

(a) Epidemiological data;
(b) Disease burden information;
(c) Utilization trends;
(d) PhilHealth claims;
(e) Health facility reports;
(f) Population health statistics.

      SECTION 9. National Medicine Shortage Early Warning System. The
DOH shall establish a National Medicine Shortage Early Warning System
capable of identifying potential shortages, supply disruptions, abnormal
demand surges, manufacturing interruptions, importation delays, and other
risks affecting the availability of medicines and health commodities. The
System shall generate alerts and recommend mitigation measures to
concerned agencies and health facilities.

      SECTION 10. Specialized Procurement Modalities for Health
Commodities. Consistent with the Government Procurement Reform Act, the
New Government Procurement Act, and other applicable procurement laws,
health commodities may be procured through:

(a) Framework Agreements;
(b) Call-Off Contracts;
(c) Multi-Year Contracts;
(d) Pooled Procurement;
(e) Strategic Procurement Agreements;
(f) Vendor-Managed Inventory Systems;
(g) Other procurement modalities authorized by law and recognized
international best practices for health commodities.

     SECTION 11. National and Regional Pooled Procurement. The DOH
may establish national, regional, provincial, and inter-local procurement
mechanisms to maximize economies of scale and reduce procurement costs.

      SECTION 12. Mandatory Framework Contracting. Whenever
practicable, procurement of medicines and health commodities shall utilize
framework agreements and staggered deliveries based on actual demand and
consumption.

      SECTION 13. National Inventory Visibility. All covered entities shall
report inventory information through the National Health Commodities
Management Platform.

      SECTION 14. First Expiry First Out Policy. All government facilities
shall implement a First Expiry, First Out (FEFO) inventory management
system.
      SECTION 15. Near-Expiry Alert System. Automatic alerts shall be
generated twelve (12), six (6), and three (3) months before product expiry.

       SECTION 16. National Medicine Redistribution Program. The DOH
shall establish a National Medicine Redistribution Program. Redistribution of
government-owned health commodities between facilities shall be authorized
without the need for separate procurement proceedings.

       SECTION 17. National Medicine Exchange Network. Government
hospitals, local government units, specialty hospitals, state universities and
colleges operating health facilities, and other covered entities shall participate
in the National Medicine Exchange Network subject to DOH guidelines.

       SECTION 18. Minimum Shelf-Life Requirement. No government
entity shall accept delivery of medicines with inadequate remaining shelf life
except in emergencies or under conditions prescribed by the DOH.

     SECTION 19. National Medicine Security Reserve. The DOH shall
maintain strategic reserves of essential and life-saving medicines for
emergencies and public health crises.

      SECTION 20. Anti-Counterfeit Procurement Safeguards. No
government entity shall procure: (a) Counterfeit products; (b) Falsified
products; (c) Smuggled products; (d) Diverted products; (e) Unregistered
products; (f) Unauthorized products.

       SECTION 21. Mandatory Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
Verification. Prior to award of any procurement contract, procuring entities
shall electronically verify the validity of:

(a) FDA Certificates of Product Registration;
(b) Licenses to Operate;
(c) Manufacturing authorizations;
(d) Importation authorizations.

      SECTION 22. Phased Serialization and Traceability. The DOH and
the FDA shall implement a phased, risk-based serialization, track-and-trace,
and electronic verification system for medicines and health commodities
procured using public funds.

Priority shall be given to:

(a) Essential medicines;
(b) Vaccines;
(c) Controlled substances;
(d) High-value medicines;
(e) Products identified by the FDA as vulnerable to counterfeiting.


      SECTION 23. Government Health Products Verification System.
The FDA shall establish a Government Health Products Verification System
to authenticate all products procured using public funds.

      SECTION 24. Beneficial Ownership Disclosure. All bidders and
suppliers shall disclose their beneficial ownership structures and ultimate
controlling interests. Such disclosures shall be submitted under oath and
updated annually.

      SECTION 25. Cybersecurity and System Protection. The National
Health Commodities Management Platform and all related systems shall
comply with national cybersecurity standards and shall be subject to periodic
vulnerability assessments, penetration testing, security audits, and other
safeguards necessary to protect critical government health supply chain
information.

       SECTION 26. Conflict of Interest Prohibition. No procurement
official shall participate in procurement activities involving entities in which
the official or relatives within the fourth civil degree have direct or indirect
interests.

      SECTION 27. National Price Reference Dashboard. The DOH shall
establish a public medicine procurement price reference dashboard.

      SECTION 28. Procurement Transparency Portal. All procurement
contracts, notices of award, delivery records, inspection reports, and payment
records shall be publicly accessible.

     SECTION 29. Red Flag Analytics System. The DOH shall establish
automated systems capable of detecting:

(a) Overpricing;
(b) Bid rotation;
(c) Cartel behavior;
(d) Repeated supplier dominance;
(e) Abnormal procurement patterns;
(f) Excessive wastage.

       SECTION 30 Prevention of Fictitious, Undelivered, or Fraudulent
Deliveries. All deliveries of medicines and health commodities procured using
public funds shall be supported by verifiable documentation, including
electronic receipts, geotagged proof of delivery, digital acceptance records,
inventory uploads, and such other verification mechanisms as may be
prescribed by the DOH.

       SECTION 31. Citizen Monitoring. Accredited civil society
organizations and patient groups may observe procurement and monitoring
activities.

      SECTION 32. Local Government Responsibilities. Local government
units shall:

(a) Participate in forecasting;
(b) Maintain inventory accuracy;
(c) Submit utilization reports;
(d) Participate in redistribution programs;
(e) Establish accountability mechanisms.

      SECTION 33. National Medicine Security Council. A National
Medicine Security Council is hereby created. The Council shall be chaired by
the Secretary of Health. Members shall include representatives from:

(a) Department of Health;
(b) Food and Drug Administration;
(c) Department of Budget and Management;
(d) Department of the Interior and Local Government;
(e) PhilHealth;
(f) Government Procurement Policy Board;
(g) Bureau of Customs;
(h) Department of Information and Communications Technology.

      The Commission on Audit (COA) may designate a non-voting observer.
The Council may invite representatives from the Philippine National Police,
National Bureau of Investigation, Office of the Ombudsman, civil society
organizations, patient groups, and other relevant stakeholders whenever
necessary.

      SECTION 34. Annual Performance Scorecard. The Department of
Health shall publish annual performance indicators covering:

(a) Expiry rates;
(b) Stock out rates;
(c) Forecast accuracy;
(d) Availability rates;
(e) Redistribution compliance;
(f) Procurement efficiency.

     SECTION 35. Annual Audit. The COA shall conduct annual audits of
medicine procurement, inventory management, and wastage.

      SECTION 36. Prohibited Acts. The following acts are prohibited:

(a) Willful overstocking;
(b) Deliberate concealment of expiry losses;
(c) Procurement of counterfeit products;
(d) Falsification of inventory records;
(e) Fictitious or fraudulent deliveries, including the submission or acceptance
of false delivery receipts, fabricated proof of delivery, deliveries that did not
actually occur, or deliveries that materially differ from the quantity, quality,
or specifications contracted for.
(f) Deliberate non-reporting of inventory;
(g) Manipulation of procurement processes;
(h) Circumvention of verification requirements.

      SECTION 37. Penalties. Any person who violates this Act shall be
subject to administrative, civil, and criminal liability without prejudice to
prosecution under the Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act, the Revised Penal
Code, the FDA Act, the Government Procurement Law, and other applicable
laws.

      In cases involving fictitious or fraudulent deliveries, procurement fraud,
counterfeit products, deliberate destruction of records, or concealment of
inventory losses, the penalties under existing laws shall be imposed in
addition to penalties under this Act.

       SECTION 38. Whistleblower Protection. Any person reporting
violations under this Act in good faith shall be protected from retaliation and
shall be entitled to protection under applicable laws and regulations.

     SECTION 39. Appropriations. The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against existing appropriations of
the DOH and thereafter included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

      SECTION 40. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within one
hundred twenty (120) days from effectivity of this Act, the DOH shall
promulgate the implementing rules and regulations in consultation with
concerned agencies and stakeholders.

        SECTION 41. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is
declared unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in force and
effect.

      SECTION 42. Repealing Clause. All laws, rules, regulations, and
issuances inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

       SECTION 43. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of
general circulation.

      Approved.
