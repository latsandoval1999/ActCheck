                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Enshrined in the 1987 Constitution is the people's right to health, a right
grounded in the State's commitment to upholding the dignity of every person and
guaranteeing full respect for human rights.1 The primacy of such right is further
articulated in Article XIII, Section 11, which commands the State to adopt an integrated
and comprehensive approach to health development, ensuring that affordable health
care is available to all people at all levels of society.

        Pursuant to this Constitutional mandate, Congress has passed several
legislations that endeavor to curtail hospital practices that are prejudicial in nature.
One such law is Republic Act No. 9439, otherwise known as "An Act Prohibiting the
Detention of Patients in Hospitals and Medical Clinics on Grounds of Nonpayment of
Hospital Bills or Medical Expenses". R.A. 9439 declares as unlawful the detention of
patients in hospitals when they are unable to pay the full value of their medical bills.

       However, to this day, complaints against hospitals on allegations of hospital
detention continue to persist.2 Reports indicate that some health care institutions

        More stringent measures are thus necessary. This amendatory bill seeks to
strengthen the safeguards of RA 9439 by expressly prohibiting the detention not only
of patients, but also of their relatives, representatives, carers, and newborn children.
It also expands the prohibition by stating that even outstanding professional fees shall
not constitute a lawful ground for detention. Lastly, the measure exempts indigent
senior citizens from the requirement of executing a promissory note upon proper
endorsement by the hospital's social service office.

       Nevertheless, this bill, in recognizing the legitimate interests of health care
providers in securing payment, broadens the acceptable forms of security for unpaid
obligations. In addition to promissory notes, it authorizes the use of deeds of
assignment covering benefits from the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS)
or the Social Security System (SSS), as well as guarantee letters issued by the
Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO), the Department of Social Welfare and
Development (DSWD), or the Department of Health (DOH).

        Ultimately, by providing more safeguards and imposing more effective
sanctions for violations, this bill emphasizes what seems to be forgotten by many: the
right to health is not a privilege reserved only to those who can afford it, but it is a right
of every Filipino irrespective of status and capacity.

       In the 20th Congress, a similar bill was filed by Senators Risa Hontiveros,
Jinggoy Estrada, and Erwin Tulfo as Senate Bill No. 1511, Representatives Dadah
Kiram Ismula, Jose Manuel Diokno, Percival Cendana, and Kaka Bag-ao as House
Bill No. 6832, and Representative Arlyn Ayon as House Bill No. 7476.

        The immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.
