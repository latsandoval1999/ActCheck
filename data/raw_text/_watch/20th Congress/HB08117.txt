                               AN ACT
      ESTABLISHING PROVINCIAL SOIL LABORATORY FACILITIES IN ALL
      PROVINCES OF THE PHILIPPINES, STRENGTHENING SOIL HEALTH
          MANAGEMENT AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

Agricultural productivity and long-term food security in the Philippines are highly
dependent on the proper management and conservation of soil resources. However,
many Filipino farmers continue to rely on generalized fertilizer recommendations and
limited field-based Soil Test Kits (STK), which provide only basic soil information. The
absence of accessible and comprehensive soil laboratory services at the provincial level
results in inefficient fertilizer use, declining soil fertility, increased production costs, and
environmental degradation.

While soil testing initiatives exist, their availability remains uneven and often
inaccessible to farmers, particularly those in rural, island, and geographically isolated
areas. Without accurate and localized soil data, farmers are unable to adopt science-based
nutrient management practices necessary to improve productivity while protecting soil
health and natural resources.

This bill seeks to establish Provincial Soil Laboratory Facilities in all provinces of the
Philippines under the Department of Agriculture, through the Bureau of Soils and Water
Management, to provide accessible, reliable, and comprehensive soil testing and advisory
services. Unlike conventional soil test kits, these laboratories shall be capable of
conducting advanced soil analysis, including macro- and micronutrient evaluation, soil
organic matter assessment, soil structure analysis, and other diagnostic procedures
necessary for precision agriculture.

The measure further institutionalizes a Site-Specific Nutrient Management framework to
ensure that fertilizer recommendations are based on laboratory-generated data and
localized field conditions rather than uniform application practices. By promoting
science-based fertilizer optimization, the proposed measure aims to reduce input waste,
lower production costs, improve crop yields, and minimize adverse environmental
impacts.

Through strengthened soil health monitoring and data-driven agricultural planning, the
establishment of provincial soil laboratories will enhance climate resilience, support
sustainable farming systems, and empower farmers with the knowledge needed to
increase productivity while preserving soil resources for future generations.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
                                       assembled:

Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Provincial Soil Laboratory
Development Act."

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
accelerate agricultural development by optimizing the use of resources such as soils with
the goal of attaining food security through science-based soil management. The state shall
ensure that farmers have accessible soil testing services to guide efficient fertilizer use,
improve soil fertility, and enhance climate-resilient agricultural practices.

Section 3. Establishment of Provincial Soil Laboratory Facilities. – There shall be
established at least one (1) Provincial Soil Laboratory Facility in every province of the
Philippines to provide soil testing, analysis, and technical advisory services to farmers
and local government units.

Section 4. Implementing Agency – The Department of Agriculture (DA), through the
Bureau of Soils and Water Management (BSWM), shall serve as the lead implementing
agency and shall:

   (a) Establish, operate, and supervise provincial soil laboratories;
   (b) Develop uniform soil testing standards and protocols;
   (c) Provide technical assistance to provincial governments;
   (d) Maintain quality assurance and laboratory accreditation systems;
   (e) Establish partnerships with state universities and colleges and research
       institutions; and
   (f) Develop a nationwide soil health monitoring program.

Section 5. Functions f Provincial Soil Laboratories. – Each provincial soil laboratory
shall:
   (a) Conduct soil fertility and nutrient analysis;
   (b) Provide crop-speci ic fertilizer and amendment recommendations;
   (c) Support sustainable and climate-resilient farming practices;
   (d) Assist in soil mapping and land suitability assessment
   (e) Provide training and extension services to farmers and agricultural technicians;
       and
   (f) Generate soil data for agricultural planning and policy development.

Section 6. Comprehensive Soil Testing and Advanced Analysis. – In addition to
standard soil testing services, Provincial Soil Laboratory Facilities established under this
Act shall be equipped and authorized to conduct comprehensive and advanced soil
analysis beyond the capabilities of conventional Soil Test Kits (STK).

Such testing shall include, but shall not be limited to:
   (a) Macro- and micronutrient analysis, including nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium,
       calcium, magnesium, sulfur, zinc, iron, manganese, copper, and boron;
   (b) Soil organic matter and carbon content determination;
   (c) Soil texture, structure, and water-holding capacity analysis;
   (d) Salinity, sodicity, and cation exchange capacity assessment;
   (e) Contaminant and heavy metal testing when necessary;
   (f) Soil biological and microbial activity assessment, where applicable; and
   (g) Crop- and site-speci ic nutrient management recommendations based on
       laboratory results.

The Department of Agriculture, through the Bureau of Soils and Water Management, shall
ensure that provincial laboratories are provided with appropriate equipment, technical
standards, and trained personnel to enable more exhaustive and science-based soil
evaluation compared to field-based Soil Test Kits.

Results generated from comprehensive testing shall serve as the basis for precision
agriculture programs, fertilizer optimization initiatives, and sustainable land
management policies.

Section 7. Site-Specific Nutrient Management and Fertilizer Optimization. – The
Department of Agriculture (DA), through the Bureau of Soils and Water Management
(BSWM), shall adopt and implement a Site-Specific Nutrient Management (SSNM)
Framework utilizing data generated from Provincial Soil Laboratory Facilities
established under this Act.
Accordingly:
   (a) Fertilizer recommendations issued to farmers shall, as far as practicable, be based
       on laboratory-generated soil analysis and localized ield conditions rather than
       uniform or blanket fertilizer application rates;
   (b) Provincial Soil Laboratories shall generate crop-speci ic and location-speci ic
       nutrient management advisories to guide farmers in the ef icient and sustainable
       use of fertilizers and soil amendments;
   (c) The DA shall progressively integrate soil laboratory results into existing
       agricultural support programs, including fertilizer assistance, crop production
       programs, and extension services;
   (d) Agricultural extension workers and local government unit personnel shall be
       trained to interpret laboratory results and assist farmers in adopting precision and
       climate-resilient nutrient management practices; and
   (e) The DA shall promote reduced input waste, improved soil health, increased farm
       productivity, and minimized environmental impact through science-based
       fertilizer optimization.

Section 8. Accessibility and Farmer Support. – Soil testing services shall be made
affordable and accessible, with subsidized rates prioritized for smallholder farmers,
agrarian reform beneficiaries, agricultural cooperatives, and farmers in geographically
isolated and disadvantaged areas.

Mobile soil testing services may be deployed to remote municipalities and island
provinces.

Section 9. Personnel and Staffing. – The Department of Agriculture, in coordination
with provincial governments, shall ensure the hiring, training, and continuous capacity
development of soil scientists, laboratory technicians, and support personnel necessary
for effective operations.

Section 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of
this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Department of
Agriculture. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

Section 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act, the DICT, in consultation with concerned agencies and stakeholders,
shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations.

Section 12. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision of this Act
shall be declared as unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining parts or provisions not
affected shall remain in full force and effect.

Section 13. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders and rules
and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
Section 14. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
