                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

        In the Philippines, both private employees and government offices essentially
follow the same weekday work schedule. This presents a fundamental difficulty for
working Filipinos who must process official transactions during business hours such
as license applications, loan and benefits facilitation, or payment of taxes and fees.
They are often left with an impossible choice: postpone essential transactions, use
their vacation leaves or absent themselves from work at the cost of their daily wage.
This burden falls heaviest upon our daily wage earners and frontline workers.

        Through the years, technology has been integrated into government services to
assist citizens who cannot personally visit government offices. Various agencies such
as the Social Security Service (SSS), Philippine Health Insurance Corporation
(PhilHealth), and Home Development Mutual Fund (PAG-IBIG) have adopted digital
solutions, including mobile applications, chat support services, and online portals.
However, these advancements remain insufficient. Certain transactions inherently
demand face-to-face processing and personal review, particularly those involving
frontline services that require physical presence, identity verification, or the
submission of original documents.

        This bill seeks to institutionalize accessible government service. Frontline
services shall operate on extended working hours, including Saturdays or even
Sundays if deemed necessary, with 24/7 availability for digital platforms, hotline,
inquiries, appointments, and document submissions. By mandating extended
operating hours across all covered agencies, we ensure that Filipinos can complete
essential matters including urgent and emergency transactions without sacrificing
their time and wages.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                            AN ACT
     EXPANDING FRONTLINE SERVICE HOURS INCLUDING WEEKEND
    COVERAGE FOR VOICE AND NON-VOICE INQUIRIES AND IN-PERSON
                        TRANSACTIONS


       Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

       SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “The 24/7 Access
to Government Services Act of 2026”

        SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the declared policy of the State to ensure
seamless, accessible, and convenient frontline services to the people, including 24/7
availability, extended operating hours, and an efficient response team for inquiries, in
order to uplift national life, development, and programs.

       It is likewise the policy of the State, in accordance with the 1987 Philippine
Constitution, that science and technology are essential for national development and
progress. The State shall support indigenous, appropriate, and self-reliant scientific
and technological capabilities and their application to the country's productive system
and national life.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act, the following terms are
defined as follows:

             a) Frontline Services refers to the process or transaction between clients
                and government offices or agencies involving applications for any
                privilege, right, permit, reward, license, concession, or for any
                modification, renewal or extension of the enumerated applications and/or
                requests which are acted upon in the ordinary course of business of the
                agency or office concerned;1

             b) Voice Response Team refers to civil servants from the office who
                answers queries from the constituents through their landline or hotline
                for their available frontline services;

       SEC. 4. Coverage. – The government offices with respective frontline
services that shall provide extended hours of operations from seven (7) o’clock in the
morning to seven (7) o’ clock in the evening and extended days of operations
including Saturdays, are:

        1) Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA)
        2) Department of Migrant Workers (DMW)
        3) National Bureau of Investigation (NBI)
        4) Public Attorney’s Office (PAO)
        5) Land Transportation Office (LTO)
        6) Professional Regulation Commission (PRC)
        7) Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR)
        8) Social Security System (SSS)
        9) Government Service Insurance System (GSIS)
        10) Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth)
        11) Home Development Mutual Fund (PAGIBIG)
        12) Philippine Postal Corporation (PhilPost)
        13) Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA)
        14) Philippine National Police (PNP)
        15) Such other government offices, agencies, government-owned or
            controlled corporations (GOCCs), and local government units as may be
            determined under the implementing rules and regulations (IRR).

        All covered agencies shall ensure that their Essential Frontline Services are
available on a twenty-four/seven (24/7) basis through digital, voice, non-voice, or
other accessible service channels. Face-to-face frontline transactions of the covered
agencies shall be made available from seven o’clock in the morning to seven o’clock
in the evening including Saturday subject to the guidelines issued pursuant to this Act.

        Government offices, including Local Government Units (LGU) that offer Zero
Contact Services are tasked to provide a Frontline Service System. Other offices that
require face-to-face transaction in all of their frontline services are not required to
create FSS.

        SEC. 5. Availability of Frontline Services and Response Team – The
frontline services and the response team of covered agencies shall be available to the
public twenty-four/seven (24/7). Such availability may be provided through digital
platforms, online portals, hotline, voice response teams, non-voice response teams, or
other service channels that allow continuous public access. For services that cannot be

fully completed outside extended office hours, the covered agency shall at least
provide 24/7 access to inquiry response, appointment setting, status verification,
submission or uploading of requirements where legally allowed, complaints receipt,
and referral mechanisms. Face-to- face frontline transactions shall be available from
7:00am to 7:00pm, including Saturdays. This shall not prevent any agency from
providing longer hours, Sunday operations, or additional service days when necessary
and feasible.

        SEC. 6. Working Arrangements. – Covered agencies of this Act shall adopt a
working arrangement that ensures sufficient personnel, systems support, and
operational capacity to implement the twenty-four/seven (24/7) availability of
Frontline Services and extended hours for face-to face frontline transactions.

       Covered agencies are hereby authorized to adopt shifting schedules, flexible
work arrangements, skeletal staffing, digital support systems, automated response
system, and other appropriate mechanisms to ensure continuous service delivery
without unduly impairing employee welfare. Where additional personnel are
necessary, the covered agencies may submit staffing plans and funding requirements
to the Department of Budget and Management for evaluation and inclusion in the
appropriate budgetary process, subject to existing civil service, budgeting, and
auditing laws, rules and regulations.

        SEC. 7. Zero Contact Services. – Government offices shall provide a list of
their frontline services that can be delivered without face-to-face interaction. Services
requiring the submission of written documents shall not be hindered from
implementation, provided that submission of electronic documents will suffice. The
rules under Section 7 of Republic Act No. 8792, otherwise known as the Electronic
Commerce Act of 2000, shall be observed in determining whether electronic
documents shall be accepted. Additionally, processing of information from submitted
documents shall comply with Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of
2012.

        SEC. 8. Non- exclusivity of Frontline Service System – Constituents who
choose to personally go to the office to avail themselves of the services shall not be
rejected due to the institution of the Frontline Services System. No person shall be
deprived of the services due to failure to use the FSS. Covered agencies shall ensure
that the digitalization and 24/7 service availability shall supplement, and not replace,
reasonable access to face-to-face frontline transactions.

       SEC. 9. Information Dissemination. – The government offices shall
disseminate information about their available Zero Contact Services through a
comprehensive outreach campaign utilizing physical postings in office facilities and
public spaces, traditional media channels, and official social media pages and
websites.

        SEC. 10. Monitoring and Reporting System. – Government offices shall
include in their annual report a comprehensive assessment of the Frontline Service
System's effectiveness, incorporating disaggregated data by age, gender, geographic
location, number of individuals served, and type of service rendered.

       SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. – All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees
