                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE

The rapid evolution of global financial systems, coupled with sophisticated methods
employed by criminal and terrorist organizations, necessitates a robust and adaptive
legal framework to combat money laundering, terrorism financing, and proliferation
financing. The Philippines, as an active member of the international community, has a
solemn duty to protect the integrity of its financial system and to fulfill its commitments
under United Nations Security Council Resolutions and the standards set by the
Financial Action Task Force (FATF).

Recent assessments, including the FATF’s ongoing monitoring, have highlighted areas
where the Philippine Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA) of 2001, as amended,
requires strengthening to align with international best practices. The continued
presence of the Philippines on the FATF "grey list" underscores the urgency of
enacting decisive reforms to address strategic deficiencies, particularly in the areas of
supervision, investigation, and prosecution of money laundering cases. Data from the
Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC) itself reveals a concerning landscape: in 2023
alone, the AMLC received over millions of covered and suspicious transaction reports,
a figure that underscores both the vigilance of covered institutions and the scale of the
challenge.

This bill seeks to comprehensively amend Republic Act No. 9160 to address these
gaps, enhance the country's defenses, and ensure our laws are equipped to tackle
modern threats. The proposed amendments are designed to transform the AMLC into
a more powerful, independent, and proactive central authority.

The key features of this bill include:

1. Enhanced Mandate and Independence of the AMLC. The bill declares a clear
policy to establish an operationally independent central authority. It redefines the
AMLC as the Philippines' sole Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU), specialized
investigation agency, and integrated regulator/supervisor for anti-money laundering
and counter-terrorism financing (AML/CTF), consolidating powers and eliminating
regulatory fragmentation;

2. Expansion of Covered Persons. The scope of entities obligated to comply with
the AMLA is broadened significantly. Key additions include:
                                            1
                                                                                           2

      (a) Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs). - Bringing cryptocurrency
    exchanges and wallet providers under regulation, closing a critical loophole;

      (b) Online Gambling Operators. - Explicitly covering all online gambling,
    regardless of license type, and their service providers; and

      (c) Specific Professional Services. - Clarifying the coverage of lawyers and
    accountants when they perform certain financial or business activities (e.g.,
    acting as a trustee, managing client money, buying/selling real estate for
    clients), while safeguarding legal professional privilege.

3. Strengthened Investigative and Supervisory Powers. The AMLC is granted
direct authority to:
       (a) Issue subpoena duces tecum and subpoena ad testificandum;
       (b) Conduct inspections and examinations of all covered persons;
       (c) Issue ex parte Administrative Freeze Orders on suspected assets for up to
       20 days, and apply for court-ordered freeze extensions;
       (d) Issue Transaction Suspension Orders to temporarily halt suspicious
       transactions; and
       (e) Inquire into bank deposits based on probable cause, without the need for a
       prior court order in the initial investigative stage, while maintaining judicial
       oversight for related accounts;

4. Streamlined Procedures and Forfeiture. The bill simplifies the process for civil
forfeiture, allows the AMLC to file petitions directly with competent courts, and
introduces the doctrine of substitute assets to ensure crime does not pay. It also
clarifies that laws on prescription cannot be invoked to protect unlawfully acquired
assets;

5. Stiffer Penalties and Administrative Sanctions. Monetary penalties for violations
are increased substantially (up to PHP 2,000,000 per transactional violation). The
AMLC is empowered to impose a wider range of administrative sanctions, including
restitution, disqualification of directors/officers, and publication of violators. Its quasi-
judicial authority is solidified, making its decisions final and executory, appealable only
via certiorari to the Court of Appeals;

6. Robust Safe Harbor and Confidentiality Provisions. The bill provides strong
protections for covered persons and AMLC officials acting in good faith. It expands
confidentiality obligations to all entities handling AMLC information and explicitly
shields compliance with AMLC orders from liability;

7. Financial Sustainability. The bill establishes a clear funding mechanism for the
AMLC, including appropriations from the General Appropriations Act, the authority to
retain a portion of forfeited assets (capped at PHP 150,000,000 annually) for
operational expenses, and the power to levy supervisory fees;

This bill represents a critical and necessary step to fortify the Philippines' financial
system against abuse by criminals and terrorists. By empowering the AMLC with the
tools, independence, and resources it needs, we enhance national security, uphold
the rule of law, and fulfill our international obligations. The passage of this measure

will significantly contribute to the country's efforts to exit the FATF grey list and promote
a secure economic environment conducive to growth and investment.

It is for these compelling reasons that the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

Section 1. Section 2 of Republic Act No. 9160, otherwise known as “The Anti-Money
Laundering Act of 2001”, as amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:

     SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
     protect and preserve the integrity OF THE FINANCIAL SYSTEM [and
     confidentiality of bank accounts] and to ensure that the Philippines shall not be
     used as a money laundering site for the proceeds of any unlawful activity.
     Consistent with its foreign policy, the State shall extend cooperation in
     transnational investigations and prosecutions of persons involved in money
     laundering activities wherever committed, as well as in the implementation of
     targeted financial sanctions related to the financing of the proliferation of
     weapons of mass destruction terrorism, and financing of terrorism, pursuant to
     the resolutions of the United Nations Council.

     LIKEWISE, THE STATE SHALL MAINTAIN AN OPERATIONALLY
     INDEPENDENT CENTRAL ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING AND COUNTER-
     TERRORISM FINANCING AUTHORITY THAT SHALL FUNCTION AND
     OPERATE AS THE FINANCIAL INTELLIGENCE UNIT, SPECIALIZED MONEY
     LAUNDERING AND TERRORISM FINANCING INVESTIGATION AGENCY,
     AND ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING AND COUNTER-TERRORISM FINANCING
     REGULATOR AND SUPERVISOR OF THE PHILIPPINES. IN LINE WITH THIS
     POLICY, AND CONSIDERING ITS UNIQUE FUNCTIONS AND
     RESPONSIBILITIES THAT AFFECT THE ECONOMY, PEACE AND ORDER,
     AND NATIONAL SECURITY, AMONG OTHERS, THE ANTI-MONEY
     LAUNDERING COUNCIL (AMLC) SHALL BE ENHANCED WITH EXPANDED
     AUTHORITY, CAPABILITY, AND RESOURCES.
