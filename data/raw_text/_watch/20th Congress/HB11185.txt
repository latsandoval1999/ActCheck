THEREFOR

                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE


        The quality and competitiveness of Philippine higher education are closely tied to the
qualifications, research capabilities, and continuous professional development of its faculty.
However, available data indicate that these areas remain significantly underdeveloped. In Academic
Year (AY) 2018–2019, only 54.2% of faculty members in Philippine higher education institutions
(HEIs) possessed advanced degrees, an improvement from 32.8% in AY 1997–1998. Of this figure,
only 16.6% held doctoral degrees. These levels remain below international standards for tertiary
education and demonstrate that progress in faculty development has been gradual rather than
transformative.

        The implications go beyond the quality of instruction. Research productivity among
Philippine HEIs continues to lag behind other ASEAN countries. Based on Scopus-indexed
publications, the Philippines ranks below Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam
and remains considerably below the ASEAN average. This disparity is projected to persist and
potentially widen. Moreover, the country has only 172.01 researchers per million people, compared
with the World Bank benchmark of 322.51 researchers per million for lower-middle-income
countries in 2021. This limited research capacity weakens the ability of higher education institutions
to contribute meaningfully to the country's science, technology, and innovation ecosystem.

         Existing government mechanisms have not generated the level of improvement necessary to
address these persistent gaps. The Higher Education Development Fund, established under Republic
Act No. 7722 to support the development of higher education, saw the share of its resources allocated
for developmental functions decline from 86.63% in 2016 to only 11.43% in 2022. Increasingly,
resources have been directed toward regulatory functions instead of long-term capacity-building.
Faculty development initiatives have likewise lacked a unified, adequately funded, and
institutionally sustained national framework. Consequently, support remains fragmented, unevenly
distributed across regions, and insufficient to establish a continuous pipeline for faculty scholarship
and research advancement.

        The Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM II) National Education Plan
2026–2035 recognizes the need for substantial investment in faculty development. It sets a target of
increasing the proportion of HEI faculty with at least a graduate degree from 58.2% in AY 2023–
2024 to 88.1% by 2035. Meeting this target requires sustained and systematic investment in
professional development and research capacity. Legislative intervention is therefore necessary, as
administrative initiatives alone have not been sufficient to ensure the funding, coordination, and
accountability required for nationwide and long-term reform.
         This proposed measure seeks to address these gaps by establishing a permanent and
comprehensive national framework for faculty development and research across all HEIs. It provides
for the creation of a Faculty Development and Research Fund supported by multiple funding
sources; institutionalizes competitive scholarships and research grants responsive to national
priorities; and establishes equity-based funding mechanisms for underserved and geographically
isolated institutions. It further mandates the establishment of a national research repository and
creates within the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) a Faculty Development and Research
Service to serve as the principal coordinating and oversight body. The bill also introduces a
Professors of Practice program to bring industry and professional expertise into higher education
classrooms and strengthen the relevance of academic instruction.

        Taken together, these measures seek to improve faculty qualifications, expand the country's
research capacity, promote greater equity among higher education institutions, and strengthen the
role of Philippine higher education in advancing national development.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                                                    AN ACT
     ESTABLISHING A COMPREHENSIVE AND SUSTAINABLE FRAMEWORK FOR FACULTY
     DEVELOPMENT AND RESEARCH IN PHILIPPINE HIGHER EDUCATION, CREATING THE
     FACULTY DEVELOPMENT AND RESEARCH FUND, AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS
     THEREFOR



     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

 1          Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the "Philippine Higher Education
 2   Faculty Development and Research Act".

 3          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the State to strengthen
 4   Philippine higher education as a strategic driver of inclusive growth, innovation, and national
 5   competitiveness by institutionalizing a permanent, adequately funded, and outcomes-oriented
 6   framework for faculty development and research.

 7           The State recognizes that faculty members are the intellectual backbone of the higher
 8   education system, and that the quality, relevance, and credibility of higher education institutions are
 9   inextricably linked to the qualifications, competencies, research capacity, and ethical standards of
10   their faculty.

11           To this end, the State shall advance the strategic role of higher education research and faculty
12   development in strengthening the national science, technology, and innovation ecosystem, and in
13   positioning the Philippines as a knowledge economy capable of addressing national and global
14   challenges, while enabling Philippine higher education institutions to participate competitively in
15   global knowledge networks.

16          Sec. 3. Scope and Coverage. - This Act shall apply to all public and private
17   higher education institutions (HEIS) recognized by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED),
18   including state universities and colleges (SUCS), local universities and colleges (LUCs), and private
19   HEIS.

20          Faculty members engaged in instruction, research, extension, innovation, academic
21   governance, or institutional service functions, whether full-time or part-time, permanent or
22   contractual, shall be eligible for programs and support under this Act, subject to such conditions as
23   CHED may prescribe.

24          Sec. 4. National Faculty Development and Research Framework. -
25   CHED shall formulate, adopt, and periodically review a National Faculty Development and
26   Research Framework, hereinafter referred to as the Framework, which shall serve as the primary
27   policy instrument governing the design, implementation, coordination, and evaluation of faculty
28   development and research initiatives under this Act.

29          The Framework shall:
30      (a) Establish a coherent national direction for faculty development and research;

31      (b) Ensure alignment with national development priorities and the National Research and
32          Development Agenda;

33      (c) Recognize differentiated institutional missions, typologies, and development stages; and

34      (d) Provide guidance to HEls in the formulation and implementation of institutional faculty
35          development and research plans, which shall be integrated into institutional development
36          plans, outcomes-based education frameworks, accreditation and quality assurance systems,
37          and faculty career progression frameworks.

38          In formulating and updating the Framework, CHED shall consult with public and private
39   HEIs, faculty organizations, professional bodies, research institutions, industry groups, relevant
40   government agencies, and civil society. The Framework shall be regularly updated to respond to
41   emerging trends, monitoring and evaluation results, and evolving national and global contexts.

42          Sec. 5. Alignment with the National Research and Development Agenda. —

43          (A) Primary Purpose. Faculty development and research initiatives
44   implemented under this Act shall support and contribute to the priorities of the National Research
45   and Development Agenda, consistent with academic freedom and institutional autonomy. For this
46   purpose, CHED shall:

47      (a) Encourage and support interdisciplinary, mission-oriented, and problem-driven research,
48          including international collaborative research initiatives, responsive to national and
49          regional development needs;

50      (b) Promote collaboration among HEls, government agencies, research institutes, industry,
51          civil society organizations, and local communities; and

52      (c) Establish coordination mechanisms and joint funding arrangements with relevant
53          government agencies to strengthen such alignment.

54          Alignment with the National Research and Development Agenda shall be integrated into
55   funding eligibility criteria, program evaluation standards, and performance monitoring under this
56   Act.

57          (B) Inter-Agency Coordination.

58          CHED shall coordinate with the Department of Science and Technology (DOST), the
59   Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev), and other agencies responsible for
60   science, technology, innovation, and digital development to ensure that the higher education
61   research agenda contributes coherently to the national science, technology, and innovation
62   ecosystem.

63            Sec. 6. Faculty Development and Research Programs. - CHED shall support faculty
64   development and research programs that enhance faculty competence, research capacity,
65   professional growth, and institutional performance.
66   Such programs shall be aligned with approved institutional development plans and national
67   priorities, and shall include the following categories:

68      (a) Graduate and post-graduate degree programs, whether undertaken locally or internationally,
69          including master's, doctoral, post-doctoral, sandwich, joint, dual-degree, and transnational
70          arrangements, international academic exchanges, visiting scholar programs, and
71          collaborative research mobility arrangements to deepen disciplinary expertise, strengthen
72          research capacity, and improve teaching effectiveness;
 73      (b) Teaching and learning innovation initiatives, including outcomes-based education, digital
 74          and blended pedagogy, the responsible integration of emerging technologies in education,
 75          flexible learning modalities, inclusive education, and curriculum modernization;

 76      (c) Research capability-building programs, including training and mentoring in research design,
 77          scholarly writing and publication, supervision of graduate research, research ethics and
 78          compliance, data governance, intellectual property protection, and research utilization;

 79      (d) Industry, professional, government, and community immersion programs to strengthen
 80          applied instruction, practice-oriented research, and alignment of academic work with labor
 81          market and societal needs; and

 82      (e) Academic leadership, research management, and institutional governance programs to
 83          enhance strategic planning, research administration, quality assurance, and institutional
 84          performance.

 85           Provided, That access to programs under this section shall be equitable, transparent, and
 86   merit-based, and shall be supportive of differentiated institutional missions and development stages.
 87           Sec. 7. Research Capacity Development System. - CHED shall institutionalize a higher
 88   education research capacity development system to : strengthen the ability of HEls to generate,
 89   manage, disseminate, and utilize research in support of academic excellence, innovation, and
 90   national development. The system shall be developmental, inclusive, and responsive to
 91   differentiated institutional mandates, and shall include the following components:

 92      (a) Support for institutional research agendas aligned with national, regional, and sectoral
 93          priorities, including assistance in agenda-setting, thematic clustering, and interdisciplinary
 94          collaboration;

 95      (b) Competitive research grants, seed funding, and early-career researcher support, including
 96          postdoctoral fellowships and emerging researcher development programs, to encourage
 97          research initiation, sustain productivity, and develop the next generation of researchers;

 98      (c) Research ethics review mechanisms, including the establishment, accreditation, or
 99          enhancement of institutional ethics review boards, to ensure adherence to recognized ethical
100          standards;

101      (d) Research dissemination, publication, and utilization support, including assistance for
102          publication, open-access platforms, technology transfer, and integration of research findings
103          into public policy and professional practice;

104      (e) Graduate research supervision capacity, including training, mentoring, and quality assurance
105          mechanisms to strengthen adviser capability and support timely completion of graduate
106          research; and

107      (f) Research infrastructure development and shared use, including laboratories, digital libraries,
108          databases, high-performance computing systems, advanced research data platforms, research
109          data management systems, repositories, and inter-institutional facilities to maximize
110          resource utilization and promote collaboration.

111                   CHED shall adopt policies and monitoring mechanisms to ensure that the system
112          contributes to sustained improvements in research quality, productivity, utilization, and
113          institutional performance.

114           Sec. 8. Professors of Practice. - CHED shall develop and administer a Professors of Practice
115   program to engage qualified practitioners with demonstrated professional, industry, or creative
116   expertise to teach or co-teach in HEIs, with the aim of improving the relevance and responsiveness
117   of higher education. Appointments under this program shall be non-tenure, fixed-term, or part-time,
118   and shall complement and not replace regular faculty positions.
119          CHED shall prescribe the qualifications, appointment process, compensation guidelines, and
120   accountability mechanisms, consistent with existing civil service laws and the higher education
121   regulatory framework.

122           Sec. 9. Faculty Scholarships. - CHED shall institutionalize and administer a system of
123   faculty scholarships to strengthen faculty qualifications, research readiness, and teaching
124   effectiveness across the higher education system. Scholarships and financial assistance shall be
125   provided to qualified faculty members of public and private HEls and may cover tuition and other
126   school fees, stipends, research and thesis or dissertation support, travel and mobility expenses,
127   insurance, and other allowable costs.

128           Provided, That scholarship awards shall be merit-based and need-sensitive, aligned with
129   approved institutional faculty development plans and national development priorities, and shall take
130   into account equitable access across regions, disciplines, institutional typologies, and development
131   stages: Provided, further, That the specific categories of eligible scholarship programs, coverage,
132   and conditions shall be prescribed by CHED in the implementing rules and regulations.

133           Sec. 10. Research Grants. -CHED shall provide competitive, transparent, and performance-
134   informed research grants to support faculty-led and institution-based research initiatives that
135   contribute to knowledge creation, innovation, policy development, and societal problem-solving.
136   Research grants shall support basic, applied, policy-oriented, interdisciplinary, and creative
137   research, including international collaborative research and emerging interdisciplinary fields; early-
138   career and emerging researcher development; graduate research supervision; collaborative and
139   multi-institutional research; and research utilization, commercialization, technology transfer,
140   innovation incubation, and policy translation.

141          Research grants may cover personnel services, research-related expenses, equipment and
142   supplies, fieldwork and data collection, dissemination and publication costs, and other necessary
143   expenditures, subject to applicable government accounting, auditing, and procurement rules.

144            Sec. 11. Eligibility, Selection, and Return Service. - Eligibility for scholarships and research
145   grants under this Act shall be merit-based, need-sensitive, and institutionally endorsed. CHED shall
146   prescribe specific eligibility and selection criteria, which shall give priority to alignment with
147   institutional and national priorities, equitable access for underserved and geographically isolated
148   institutions, and the applicant's potential contribution to teaching quality, research productivity, and
149   institutional capacity.

150           Recipients of scholarships and research grants shall be subject to return service obligations
151   proportionate to the level, duration, and nature of support received. Return service may be rendered
152   through teaching, research, graduate supervision, curriculum development, institutional capacity-
153   building, community engagement, or other forms of academic or public service as determined by
154   CHED, and may be fulfilled through inter-institutional or national arrangements that serve the
155   objectives of this Act.

156            Provided, That return service obligations shall be developmental and implemented in a
157   manner that respects academic freedom, institutional autonomy, and due process: Provided, further,
158   That CHED may grant a waiver or deferment of return service, in whole or in part, in cases of serious
159   illness, permanent disability, fortuitous events, further approved studies, substantial achievement of
160   program objectives, or other analogous circumstances, subject to transparent criteria and due
161   process: Provided, finally, That nothing in this section shall limit the authority of the government to
162   impose sanctions or recover public funds in cases of fraud, misrepresentation, or willful non-
163   compliance.

164           Sec. 12. Creation of the Faculty Development and Research Fund. -There is hereby created
165   the Faculty Development and Research Fund (FDRF), which shall consolidate and strengthen
166   existing faculty development and research initiatives of CHED, and serve as a dedicated fund to
167   finance faculty development and research programs, projects, and activities under this Act. The
168   FDRF shall be administered by CHED in a transparent, accountable, performance-informed, and
169   equity-sensitive manner, and shall complement existing funding mechanisms for higher education,
170   research, and innovation.

171          The FDRF shall be sourced from:

172      (a) Annual appropriations under the General Appropriations Act, which shall constitute the
173          primary and recurring source of funding;

174      (b) A portion equivalent to ten percent (10%) of the Higher Education Fund created under
175          Republic Act No. 7722, otherwise known as the Higher Education Act of 1994, which shall
176          be earmarked and automatically allocated to the FDRF;

177      (c) Grants, donations, endowments, bequests, and contributions from domestic or foreign
178          sources, whether public or private, subject to existing laws governing the acceptance and
179          utilization of such funds;

180      (d) Assistance received from donor parties through memoranda of agreement entered into
181          pursuant to Republic Act No. 8525, otherwise known as the Adopt-A-School Act of 1998;

182      (e) Income derived from research commercialization, technology transfer, royalties, and
183          licensing fees arising from activities supported under this Act, subject to CHED-prescribed
184          revenue sharing policies;

185      (f) Contributions from partnerships with government agencies, industry, civil society
186          organizations, and development partners; and

187      (g) Other sources as may be authorized by law, including special purpose funds, trust funds, or
188          future financing mechanisms consistent with the objectives of this Act.

189          Funds accruing to the FDRF shall be used exclusively for the purposes authorized under this
190          Act, shall not revert to the general fund of the National Treasury at the end of the fiscal year,
191          and shall be subject to applicable government accounting, auditing, and reporting rules.

192            Sec. 13. Fund Allocation. - The FDRF shall be allocated through a performance-based and
193   differentiated funding framework that promotes continuous improvement, equity, and system-wide
194   development, not to impose punitive or exclusionary measures. Performance-based funding
195   mechanisms shall be developmental in character and shall take into account institutional mandates,
196   development stages, and improvement trajectories, and shall not disadvantage institutions facing
197   structural or geographic constraints. The allocation framework shall recognize the diversity of
198   institutional mandates, typologies, development stages, and regional contexts within the higher
199   education system.

200          CHED shall establish multiple funding windows, which shall include, but not limited to:

201      (a) A Baseline Institutional Support Window, providing minimum funding to ensure equitable
202          participation by all eligible HEIS;

203      (b) A Performance-Based Incentive Window, allocating additional resources based on
204          demonstrated improvements in faculty qualifications, research productivity, graduate
205          education capacity, and institutional impact;

206      (c) A Strategic National Priority Window, supporting initiatives aligned with nationally
207          identified priority disciplines and development challenges; and

208      (d) A Capacity-Building and Catch-Up Window, providing targeted support to emerging,
209          underserved, geographically isolated, or resource-constrained institutions. In the
210          implementation of programs under this Act, due consideration shall be given to emerging,
211          geographically isolated, and resource-constrained higher education institutions to promote
212          balanced regional development.
213           CHED shall adopt performance indicators to assess outputs, outcomes, improvement
214   trajectories, and impact of faculty development and research investments, covering: faculty
215   qualification outcomes; research and creative outputs; graduate education and research supervision
216   capacity; institutional and societal impact; and regional and sectoral contributions. The formulation,
217   collection, and use of performance data shall be consistent with the budgeting and expenditure
218   accountability frameworks of the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and the
219   accounting and auditing standards of the Commission on Audit.

220           Provided, That no HEI shall be denied access to baseline institutional support solely on the
221   basis of performance classification: Provided, further, That performance-based allocations shall
222   account for improvement trajectories and contextual factors, and shall not penalize institutions for
223   structural disadvantages beyond their control.

224           Sec. 14. Performance Assessment Safeguards. - CHED shall establish and enforce the
225   following safeguards to ensure fairness, transparency, integrity, and due process in the application
226   of performance indicators and performance-based funding mechanisms under this Act:

227      (a) All performance indicators shall be clearly defined, publicly disclosed, and uniformly
228          applied across similarly situated HEls, taking into account differentiated institutional
229          mandates, typologies, and development stages;

230      (b) Performance data shall be sourced from standardized, verifiable, and auditable sources, and
231          shall be subject to validation, verification, and reconciliation processes consistent with
232          applicable government data governance standards and COA audit requirements;

233      (c) CHED shall provide structured and accessible mechanisms for institutional feedback,
234          clarification, and appeal of performance assessments, including reasonable timelines for
235          submission, review, and resolution, and the issuance of written determinations explaining
236          the basis of funding or performance-related decisions; and

237      (d) CHED shall periodically review and refine performance indicators and methodologies,
238          informed by monitoring and evaluation results, stakeholder consultations, and audit findings,
239          to ensure continued relevance, accuracy, and policy coherence.

240           Sec. 15. Faculty Development and Research Service. - There is hereby created within
241   CHED the Faculty Development and Research Service (FDRS), which shall serve as the primary
242   policy, programmatic, technical, and coordinating unit responsible for the formulation, governance,
243   implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of faculty development and research initiatives under
244   this Act.
245   The FDRS shall have the following powers and functions:

246      (a) Formulate, harmonize, and coordinate national policies, standards, guidelines, and programs
247          on faculty development and research, consistent with this Act, national education reform
248          priorities, and the National Research and Development Agenda;

249      (b) Administer, manage, and oversee the FDRF, including the development of funding
250          guidelines, evaluation and approval of proposals, release and utilization of funds, financial
251          reporting, and enforcement of fiscal accountability, in accordance with applicable budgeting,
252          accounting, and auditing rules;

253      (c) Design, implement , ana maintain a monitoring and evaluation system to assess program
254          implementation, outcomes, impact, and value for money, and to generate evidence for
255          planning, performance-based funding, and policy refinement;

256      (d) Develop and sustain integrated data systems, including centralized databases, registries, and
257          reporting            platforms,           to            support             evidence-based
258           decision-making, transparency, and compliance with government data governance policies;
259      (e) Coordinate and collaborate with relevant government agencies, HEIS, research institutions,
260          industry partners, professional associations, and civil society organizations to promote policy
261          coherence and resource mobilization;

262      (f) Provide technical assistance and advisory support to HEls in the formulation and
263          implementation of faculty development and research plans; and

264      (g) Perform such other functions as may be necessary or complementary to the effective
265          implementation of this Act, as authorized by CHED.

266           Sec. 16. Responsibilities of Higher Education Institutions. - HEIs shall bear primary
267   responsibility for the effective implementation of faculty development and research initiatives under
268   this Act, consistent with their academic mission, institutional autonomy, and accountability for
269   public resources. Higher education institutions shall undertake the responsibilities under this section
270   consistent with their institutional mandate, capacity, and stage of development. To this end, HEIS
271   shall:

272      (a) Develop, adopt, and implement a faculty development and research plan aligned with the
273          National Framework and national policies, articulating institutional priorities, strategies,
274          resource requirements, and performance targets;

275      (b) Establish and maintain internal research governance mechanisms, including research
276          management offices or units, ethics review boards, responsible conduct of research policies,
277          and data governance systems;

278      (c) Adopt and periodically update an Intellectual Property Manual governing the ownership,
279          protection, utilization, commercialization, and revenue-sharing of intellectual property
280          arising from faculty research, creative work, and innovation, consistent with existing laws
281          and CHED policies;

282      (d) Ensure transparent, equitable, and merit-based selection of faculty beneficiaries for
283          scholarships, research grants, and other opportunities, and provide appropriate institutional
284          support and enabling conditions for faculty participants, including workload adjustments and
285          reintegration mechanisms;

286      (e) Submit accurate, timely, and complete performance, financial, and utilization reports to
287          CHED in accordance with prescribed standards; and

288      (f) Cooperate with monitoring, evaluation, audit, and verification processes conducted by
289          CHED and other authorized government entities, and take corrective or remedial action in
290          response to findings and recommendations.

291           Sec. 17. Equity and Regional Development. - CHED shall adopt and implement equity-
292   sensitive measures to promote inclusive growth and balanced regional development within the
293   higher education system, recognizing disparities in institutional capacity, geographic access,
294   resource availability, and faculty qualifications across regions and sectors.

295   CHED shall provide targeted and sustained support to emerging, geographically isolated, and
296   resource-constrained HEls, including SUCs, LUCs, and private institutions serving underserved
297   communities, through preferential access to the Baseline Institutional Support Window and the
298   Capacity-Building and Catch-Up Window, targeted scholarships and research grants, technical
299   assistance, shared research infrastructure, and mentoring and partnership programs. Equity-oriented
300   interventions shall address structural constraints and progressively strengthen the faculty
301   qualifications, research capacity, and academic standards of institutions in less developed regions.
302   CHED shall coordinate with relevant government agencies, local government units, and
303   development partners to maximize impact and avoid duplication, and shall subject equity measures
304   to regular monitoring and evaluation to inform program refinement and funding decisions.

305          Sec. 18. National Higher Education Research Repository. -
306       (a) Establishment and Purpose.

307           CHED shall establish, maintain, and administer a National Higher Education Research
308           Repository, which shall serve as a centralized, publicly accessible, and secure online
309           platform for the collection, archiving, dissemination, and preservation of research outputs
310           produced by HEIS, particularly those funded in whole or in part by public funds or
311           supported under this Act.

312       (b) Mandatory Submission To Repository

313           Submission of eligible research outputs, including research papers, theses, dissertations,
314           technical and policy papers, terminal reports, and other scholarly or creative works, shall be
315           a mandatory condition for: the completion and close-out of funded research projects; the
316           release of final tranches of research grants or institutional research funding; compliance with
317           return service requirements; and institutional reporting and performance evaluation by
318           CHED.

319       (c) Guidelines on Storage and Maintenance of Outputs.

320            CHED shall prescribe policies, standards, and technical guidelines governing submission,
321   metadata requirements, access levels, interoperability, and long-term digital preservation, consistent
322   with existing laws on intellectual property, data privacy, and open access, and with internationally
323   recognized open science and research data standards. Submission requirements shall be
324   implemented consistent with intellectual property laws, publication agreements, confidentiality
325   obligations, and reasonable embargo periods as may be prescribed by CHED. CHED may allow
326   restricted access levels or deferred publication for justifiable grounds, subject to clear criteria in the
327   implementing rules and regulations.

328           Provided, That failure to comply with mandatory submission requirements, without
329   justifiable cause, shall be grounds for the withholding, suspension, or recovery of funding, or the
330   imposition of other appropriate administrative measures, subject to due process and applicable laws.

331           Sec. 19. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Annual Report. - CHED shall establish and maintain
332   a results-oriented monitoring and evaluation system, supported by integrated digital data systems
333   and analytics, to assess the implementation, effectiveness, efficiency, and impact of faculty
334   development and research programs under this Act, covering program outcomes, fund utilization,
335   compliance, regional distribution of benefits, and contribution to national development objectives.

336           CHED shall submit to the Senate and House of Representatives an Annual Faculty
337   Development and Research Report presenting outcomes achieved, including improvements in
338   faculty qualifications, research productivity, and graduate education; fund utilization and financial
339   performance; and geographic and institutional distribution of resources. The Report shall be made
340   publicly accessible through CHED's official website.

341            Sec. 20. Non-Impairment Clause. - Nothing in this Act shall be interpreted to impair or
342   restrict academic freedom, institutional autonomy, or the right to due process, as guaranteed under
343   the Constitution and existing laws, nor to authorize any action that would unduly interfere with the
344   lawful governance, academic judgment, or internal management of higher education institutions.

345           Sec. 21. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act
346   shall be charged against the current appropriations of CHED. Thereafter, such sums as may be
347   necessary for the full and sustained implementation of this Act shall be included in the annual
348   General Appropriations Act.

349          Sec. 22. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity
350   of this Act, CHED shall promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations, in
351   consultation with relevant government agencies, higher education institutions, faculty organizations,
352   research institutions, and other stakeholders.
353           Sec. 23. Separability Clause. - If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
354   unconstitutional, the remainder of this Actor any provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
355   force and effect.

356            Sec. 24. Repealing Clause. - Any law, presidential decree, or issuance of an executive order,
357   letter of instruction, rule, or regulation inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed
358   or modified accordingly.

359          Sec. 25. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its complete
360   publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.
