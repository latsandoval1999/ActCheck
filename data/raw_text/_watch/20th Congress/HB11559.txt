EXPLANATORY NOTE

This bill institutionalizes a national drug education and life skills program across
public and private basic education institutions, as well as the Alternative Learning System,
covering learners from Grade 4 through Grade 12. Despite the existing statutory mandates
under Republic Act No. 9165 and administrative initiatives like the Department of
Education's Preventive Drug Education Program, drug prevention instruction in schools
remains fragmented and vulnerable to administrative transition. By establishing a clear
legislative framework to strengthen current laws and national programs, this measure ensures
sustained, age-appropriate prevention education that equips young learners with critical life
skills and capabilities to curb drug use and addiction at an early age. Despite the decline in
drug prevalence observed by the Dangerous Drugs Board (DDB), purely punitive measures
have proven insufficient in protecting the youth, necessitating a proactive, school-based
approach anchored on health, education, and social development. This legislation adopts a
comprehensive structure encompassing curriculum integration, student-led co-curricular
initiatives, teacher capacity-building, parental engagement, and continuous program
evaluation. Through this measure, the State fulfills its constitutional obligation to safeguard
the well-being of the youth, empowering them to make informed, healthy choices for their
future.

AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING A COMPREHENSIVE NATIONAL DRUG
EDUCATION AND LIFE SKILLS PROGRAM IN BASIC EDUCATION,
MANDATING CAPACITY-BUILDING FOR EDUCATIONAL PERSONNEL,
ENHANCING COMMUNITY AND PARENTAL ENGAGEMENT, AND
APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "National Drug
Education Program Act."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy — It is the policy of the State, as enshrined in the
1987 Constitution, to protect and promote the physical, mental, and social well-being of the
youth, recognizing their vital role in nation-building. The State acknowledges that substance
abuse remains a critical threat to youth development and that purely punitive approaches are
insufficient to address the drug problem. Pursuant to the mandates of Republic Act No. 9165
and existing administrative frameworks such as the Department of Education's Preventive
Drug Education Program, the State hereby establishes a statutory, standardized, and
age-appropriate National Drug Education Program across basic education institutions.

SECTION 3. Target Scope and Age Range — To intervene prior to peak risk ages for
substance experimentation, the Drug Education Program shall cover learners from Grade 4
through Grade 12 across all public and private basic education institutions, as well as the
Alternative Learning System.

SECTION 4. Curricular Integration Framework — The Department of Education, in
coordination with the Dangerous Drugs Board and the Department of Health, shall integrate
age-appropriate drug prevention education into the basic education curriculum.

For the Intermediate Level covering Grades 4 to 6, instruction shall be incorporated
into Health Education and Values Education, focusing on fundamental understanding of
medicines versus harmful substances, basic health effects of tobacco, alcohol, and illicit
drugs, refusal skills, building self-esteem, and identifying trusted adults for assistance.

For Junior High School covering Grades 7 to 10, instruction shall be embedded in
Health Education under MAPEH, focusing on the biological, psychological, and social
impacts of substance abuse, managing peer pressure, conflict resolution, emotional
regulation, decision-making models, and legal awareness.

For Senior High School covering Grades 11 and 12, instruction shall be integrated
into Physical Education and Health as well as Personal Development, focusing on advanced
risk assessment, situational awareness, stress management, community leadership, youth
mental health, and active resistance strategies.

SECTION 6. Capacity-Building and Accreditation for Educators. — The Department
of Education, in coordination with the Dangerous Drugs Board, the Department of Health,
and the Department of Interior and Local Government shall likewise develop a basic course
on drug abuse prevention for the Local School Board that shall serve as a partner in the
implementation of the program for the learners. The content of the course shall include basic
principles on the types of illegal drugs, their harmful effects, and how the partners can teach
and guide minors and the youth on matters related to drug abuse. The basic course shall be
constantly evaluated by the DepEd, in consultation with the DDB, the DOH, and the DILG to
ensure its sustainability.

SECTION 7. Inter-Agency Coordination and Community Partners. — To ensure a
holistic support ecosystem, the Department of Education shall collaborate with the
Department of the Interior and Local Government and Local Government Units via Local
School Boards to fund local school-level seminars and community outreach. The Department
of Education shall likewise partner with the Department of Social Welfare and Development
and local health centers to establish clear referral pathways for learners requiring guidance
counseling or medical! intervention.

SECTION 8. Annual Assessment and Congressional Reporting. — The Department
of Education shall conduct an annual monitoring and evaluation of the program's
implementation across all schools division offices. The Department of Education shall submit
a consolidated report to the Senate Committee on Basic Education and the House Committee
on Basic Education and Culture on or before May 31 of every year.

SECTION 9. Appropriations — The amount necessary for the implementation of this
Act shall be charged against those authorized in the current appropriations of the Department
of Education (DepEd). Thereafter, the amount necessary for the continued implementation of
this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty days from the
effectivity of this Act, the Department of Education, in consultation with the Dangerous

Government, and relevant non-governmental organizations, shall promulgate the necessary

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision or part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions or parts not affected thereby shall
remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
