                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE
      The barangay is the basic political unit and the primary planning and
implementing unit of government programs, projects, and activities in the
community.1 Under the 1987 Constitution and the Local Government Code
of 1991 (R.A. 7160), the State is committed to decentralization, local
autonomy, and the empowerment of local government units to deliver
essential services to the Filipino people.
       In April 2026, the Executive Branch launched the “Bawat Barangay
Makikinabang” Program, allocating PhP 200,000 to each of the country’s
approximately 42,000 barangays through the Socio-Civic Projects Fund
(SCPF) of the Office of the President.2 The SCPF traces its statutory origin to
Presidential Decree No. 1869, s. 1983 (PAGCOR Charter), Section 7(a) of
which empowers the allocation and distribution of PAGCOR earnings
earmarked for infrastructure and socio-civic projects, with the approval of
the Office of the President.3 Section 12 of the same decree mandates that the
government’s share of PAGCOR’s aggregate gross earnings be set aside for
infrastructure and socio-civic projects, with the Office of the President
authorized to direct such allocations throughout the Philippines.4 The
current administration of the SCPF is governed by Memorandum Order No.
11, s. 2022, which reverted SCPF management to the Office of the Executive
Secretary, citing Section 6 of Executive Order No. 1, s. 2022 and Section 7(a)
of P.D. No. 1869.5
      The program has supported community development projects and
educational assistance for graduating college students. However, the SCPF
has a statutory origin in P.D. No. 18696 and is supported by an executive
issuance chain (E.O. No. 17 and M.O. No. 118), the actual per-barangay

   AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE BARANGAY ECONOMIC RELIEF AND
   COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT FUND, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:
     SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the
“Barangay Economic Relief and Community Development Fund Act.”
      SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — The State recognizes barangays as
the basic political unit and the primary planning and implementing unit of
government programs, projects, and activities in the community. Consistent
with the policy of decentralization and local autonomy enshrined in the
Constitution and the Local Government Code of 1991, the State shall ensure
a regular, equitable, and non-partisan allocation of funds to every barangay
to support community development, educational access, disaster
preparedness, and grassroots-level public service delivery.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For the purposes of this Act, the
following terms are defined:
      (a) “Barangay” refers to the basic political unit as defined in Republic
      Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the Local Government Code of
      1991;
      (b) “BERCD Fund” refers to the Barangay Economic Relief and
      Community Development Fund established under this Act;
      (c) “Community Development Projects” refers to projects for public
      safety, disaster preparedness, health, infrastructure, and other
      priorities identified through a participatory barangay assembly
      process;
      (d) “Educational Assistance” refers to the financial grants provided to
      qualified students under this Act;
      (e) “Implementing Agency” refers to the Department of the Interior and
      Local Government (DILG).
        SEC. 4. Establishment of the Fund. — There is hereby established a
permanent fund, to be known as the Barangay Economic Relief and
Community Development Fund (BERCD Fund), which shall provide annual
financial assistance to every barangay in the Philippines.
        SEC. 5. Amount and Allocation Formula. —
(a) Each barangay shall receive a minimum annual allocation of Two
Hundred Thousand Pesos (P200,000.00), subject to adjustment every three
(3) years based on the Consumer Price Index (CPI) as published by the
Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), upon joint recommendation of the
DILG and the Department of Budget and Management (DBM).
(b) The allocation for each barangay shall be divided as follows:
    (i) Not less than fifty percent (50%) for Community Development Projects,
    including but not limited to: public safety infrastructure such as
    streetlights, closed-circuit television systems, and patrol equipment;
    disaster risk reduction and management equipment and facilities; power
    generators for health centers and evacuation facilities; and other projects
    identified through a participatory barangay assembly process; and
    (ii) Not more than fifty percent (50%) for Educational Assistance,
    prioritizing students in the final two (2) years of their college degree
    program who are at risk of dropping out due to financial hardship.
(c) An additional or differentiated allocation formula may be adopted by the
Implementing Agency, in consultation with the DBM, for barangays
classified as: (i) geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs); (ii)
disaster-affected areas as declared by the National Disaster Risk Reduction
and Management Council (NDRRMC); or (iii) areas with higher-than-average
poverty incidence as determined by the PSA.
        SEC. 6. Funding Source. —
(a) The BERCD Fund shall be sourced from an annual appropriation in the
General Appropriations Act (GAA), which shall not be lower than the amount
appropriated in the immediately preceding fiscal year.
(b) The appropriation for the BERCD Fund shall be treated as an automatic
appropriation to ensure continuity of the program regardless of changes in
administration: Provided, That the DBM may recommend a temporary
reduction, but not elimination, of the allocation during a fiscal emergency as
declared by the President with the concurrence of Congress.
(c) Such other funds as may be authorized by law, including donations,
grants, and aid from domestic or international sources, may supplement the
BERCD Fund.
        SEC. 7. Implementing Agency. — The Department of the Interior
and Local Government (DILG), in coordination with the Department of
Budget and Management (DBM) and the Liga ng mga Barangay, shall serve
as the lead implementing agency for the BERCD Fund. The DILG shall be
responsible for the release, monitoring, and evaluation of fund utilization.
         SEC. 8. Eligibility and Selection Criteria for Educational
Assistance. —
(a) Student beneficiaries shall be selected by the Barangay Council through
a duly enacted resolution, subject to the following minimum criteria:
    (i) The student must be a bona fide resident of the barangay;
    (ii) Enrolled in an accredited higher education institution, whether public
    or private;
    (iii) In the final two (2) years of their degree program;
    (iv) Belonging to a household classified as poor or near-poor based on
    PSA data, or as certified by the local social welfare and development
    office; and
    (v) Not a recipient of any other full national government scholarship.
(b) Selection shall be non-partisan and shall not consider political affiliation,
family connections to barangay officials, or other partisan criteria.
(c) The Barangay Council shall post the list of applicants and selected
beneficiaries in at least three (3) conspicuous places within the barangay,
and on any available digital platform, for at least fifteen (15) days.
         SEC. 9. Prohibited Acts and Anti-Patronage Safeguards. —
(a) No elected or appointed official of the national government, including but
not limited to members of Congress, governors, mayors, vice mayors, and
councilors, shall participate in the identification of beneficiaries, selection of
projects, or distribution of funds under this Act.
(b) No barangay shall be excluded from receiving its allocation, in whole or
in part, on the basis of the political affiliation or party alignment of its
officials or residents.
(c) Any person who withholds, diverts, or misuses funds allocated under this
Act, or who uses the same for partisan political purposes, shall be liable
under Republic Act No. 3019, otherwise known as the “Anti-Graft and
Corrupt Practices Act,” Republic Act No. 6713, otherwise known as the
“Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public Officials and
Employees,” and other applicable laws, without prejudice to criminal
prosecution under the Revised Penal Code.
(d) The distribution or release of funds under this Act shall be suspended
forty-five (45) days before and thirty (30) days after any national or local
election, consistent with the Omnibus Election Code (Batas Pambansa Blg.
881): Provided, That funds already released to barangays prior to the
election period may continue to be utilized.
         SEC. 10. Transparency and Accountability. —
(a) Each barangay shall post in at least three (3) conspicuous places within
the barangay, and on any available digital platform, the following
information: the amount received; the list of projects funded and their
status of implementation; the names and qualifications of educational
assistance beneficiaries; and the total amount utilized and remaining
balance.
(b) The DILG shall establish and maintain a publicly accessible online portal
containing the complete list of barangay allocations, disbursements, project
reports, and utilization records.
(c) The Commission on Audit (COA) shall conduct annual audits of barangay
utilization of BERCD funds and shall submit a consolidated report to
Congress within six (6) months after the close of each fiscal year.
       SEC. 11. Reporting Requirements. —
(a) Each barangay shall submit to the DILG, through its regional or
provincial office, a utilization report within ninety (90) days after the end of
each fiscal year, in a form prescribed by the Implementing Rules and
Regulations.
(b) The DILG shall submit to the President of the Philippines, the Senate
President, and the Speaker of the House of Representatives a consolidated
annual report on the implementation of this Act within one hundred twenty
(120) days after the close of each fiscal year.
       SEC. 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The DILG, in
consultation with the DBM, COA, Liga ng mga Barangay, and
representatives of civil society organizations, shall promulgate the
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) within ninety (90) days from the
effectivity of this Act. The IRR shall prescribe the forms, procedures,
timelines, and guidelines for the release, utilization, reporting, and auditing
of funds under this Act.
       SEC. 13. Inflation Adjustment. — The base allocation per barangay
under Section 5(a) shall be reviewed and adjusted every three (3) years by
the DILG and DBM, based on the cumulative increase in the Consumer
Price Index as published by the PSA, subject to the availability of funds and
approval of the DBM.
       SEC. 14. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is held
invalid or unconstitutional, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.
       SEC. 15. Repealing Clause. — All laws, presidential decrees,
executive orders, letters of instruction, rules and regulations, or parts
thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modified accordingly.
       SEC. 16 Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2)
newspapers of general circulation.
