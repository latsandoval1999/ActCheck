                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution promises full protection to workers and equal access to jobs for all.
Yet many Filipinos aged forty (40) and above still struggle to ind work, return to
work, or learn new skills. Rapid changes in technology, shifting workplace demands,
and long breaks due to job loss or family responsibilities make it harder for mature
and older workers to compete, despite their experience, reliability, and willingness to
work.

Our laws already say that age should not be a barrier to employment. Existing statutes
direct the government, especially the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE),
to promote skills training, job matching, and support for older persons who want to
work. However, these efforts remain scattered. There is no single, nationwide
program focused on helping mature and older workers upgrade skills, connect to
employers, and successfully return to decent work.

This bill ills that gap. It brings together training, job matching, and support services
into one clear, DOLE-led program for workers aged forty and above, especially those
who are unemployed or displaced. By using existing training institutions and local
employment of ices, the bill strengthens what already exists and makes it work better
for older workers. This measure is timely, practical, and fair. It helps experienced

behind simply because of age.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Mature and Older Workers
Job Training Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
afford full protection to labor, promote full employment, and ensure equality of
employment opportunities for all, including mature and older workers, by improving
access to market-relevant skills training, employment facilitation, and reemployment
support.

In furtherance of this policy, the State shall adopt measures that complement existing
laws that prohibit age-based discrimination in employment and promote productive
employment of senior citizens. The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE)
shall lead implementation in coordination with relevant agencies, local government
units (LGUs), and the private sector.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act, the following terms are
de ined:

   (a) “Mature or Older Worker” refers to any person forty (40) years old or
       above seeking employment, reemployment, or skills upgrading;

   (b) “Participant” refers to a Mature or Older Worker determined by DOLE or its
       accredited implementing partner as eligible and enrolled under the Program;

   (c) “Case Management” refers to a client-centered approach that includes:

       (i)     pro iling and assessment;
       (ii)    preparation of an individualized employment and training plan;
       (iii)   referral to training, employment facilitation, and support services; and
       (iv)    monitoring and post-placement assistance;

   (d) “Basic Skills Training” refers to training intended to improve functional
       literacy, numeracy, digital skills, and workplace communication, as assessed by
       DOLE or a designated provider using standards it shall prescribe under the
       IRR;

   (e) “Occupational Skills Training” refers to competency-based training aligned
       with in-demand occupations, including enterprise-based training registered
       or recognized by TESDA where applicable;

   (f) “Support Services” refers to services necessary to enable participation in
       training or work, which may include transportation allowance, meals,
       childcare or dependent care support, and other analogous assistance as may
       be allowed in the IRR, subject to funding availability; and

   (g) “Public Employment Service Of ice (PESO)” refers to the of ice established
       and maintained by LGUs to provide employment facilitation services and
       coordination with DOLE.

SECTION 4. Establishment of the Program. — A Mature and Older Workers Job
Training and Reemployment Program (the Program) is hereby established under
DOLE to provide accessible, market-driven skills training and employment facilitation
for Mature or Older Workers, particularly those who are vulnerable to displacement,
long-term unemployment, or underemployment.

SECTION 5. Eligibility and Priority Bene iciaries. —

   (A) GENERAL ELIGIBILITY. Any Mature or Older Worker may apply for the
       Program, subject to screening and available slots.

   (B) PRIORITY BENEFICIARIES – Subject to implementing guidelines, priority shall
       be given to applicants who are any of the following:

      1.) Below or near-poverty income as determined under the IRR;
      2.) Unemployed or long-term unemployed as de ined under the IRR;
      3.) Underemployed, including those working reduced hours or in precarious
          work;
      4.) Displaced workers, including those noti ied of imminent separation;
      5.) Returning workers seeking reentry to the labor market after caregiving
          or similar circumstances;
      6.) Homeless or in unstable housing situations, as may be de ined under
          the IRR;
      7.) Those with incomplete basic education or assessed need for Basic Skills
          Training; and
      8.) Senior citizens who have the capacity and desire to work or be re-
          employed, consistent with existing law.

SECTION 6. Program Components and Services. — The Program shall provide one
or more of the following services to each Participant, as appropriate to the
Participant’s individualized plan:

   (a) Pro iling, intake, and objective assessment of skills, work history,
       employability barriers, and support service needs through Case Management;

   (b) Individualized employment and training plan, including an employment
       goal and sequence of services, with periodic review and updating;

   (c) Job search assistance and career coaching, including resume preparation,
       interview coaching, and labor market information;

   (d) Training interventions, which may include:

      1. Basic Skills Training;
      2. Occupational Skills Training, including TESDA-aligned competency-
         based programs; and

                                                                       5|Page
      3. Enterprise-based training consistent with the national enterprise-based
         education and training framework, where applicable;

   (e) Work exposure and transition-to-work measures, such as on-the-job
       training, supervised work experience, or internships/industry immersion,
       subject to applicable rules and safeguards;

   (f) Job matching and placement assistance through PESOs and DOLE channels,
       including referrals to employers who commit to age-fair hiring;

   (g) Support Services necessary to enable participation, subject to funding
       availability and implementing rules; and

   (h) Post-placement support for a reasonable period to improve retention and
       address workplace adjustment issues.

SECTION 7. Coordination, Implementing Partners, and Age-Fair Hiring. —

   (A) Lead Agency. DOLE shall be the lead implementing agency.

   (B) Coordination. DOLE shall coordinate with TESDA, DepEd, CHED, DTI,
       LGUs/PESOs, and other relevant agencies to align training with labor market
       demand and facilitate placement.

   (C) Implementing Partners. DOLE may engage and/or accredit quali ied training
       institutions, NGOs, employers’ organizations, labor groups, and other service
       providers with demonstrated competence in implementing programs for
       Mature or Older Workers.

   (D) Non-Discrimination. In implementing this Act, DOLE and its partners shall
       ensure that recruitment, screening, training admission, and placement
       assistance are consistent with the policy against age discrimination in
       employment.

SECTION 8. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reportorial Requirement. — DOLE shall
establish a monitoring and evaluation system, including sex- and age-disaggregated
data, to measure enrollments, completion rates, placement rates, and retention
outcomes.

The Secretary of Labor and Employment shall submit to the President of the
Philippines and to Congress an annual report on Program implementation, including
regional/provincial performance and recommendations for improvements.

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90)
days from the effectivity of this Act, DOLE, in consultation with TESDA, DepEd, CHED,
DTI, LGUs, and relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary IRR.

SECTION 10. Appropriations. — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of
DOLE. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for the continued implementation
of the Program shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected shall remain in full force
and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in at least two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
