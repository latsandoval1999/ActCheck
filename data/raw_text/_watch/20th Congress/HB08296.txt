                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

        This legislative measure seeks to institutionalize a skills training program for
Persons Deprived of Liberty (PDLs) through TESDA Training Programs. It seeks to
institutionalize training programs to ensure that all PDLs are afforded meaningful
opportunities for rehabilitation, capacity-building and productive reintegration upon
completion of their time in custody.

       In 2023, TESDA formalized its partnership with the Bureau of Jail Management
and Penology (BJMP) through a Memorandum of Agreement entitled “Rehabilitation
and Re-Integration through Skills Development.” The agreement clarifies the
respective roles and responsibilities of both agencies, establishes coordination and
implementation mechanisms and serves as the policy framework for the systematic
delivery of skills training, competency assessment, and certification for persons
deprived of liberty (PDLs), consistent with existing rules and guidelines. As of 2025, a
total of 15,838 persons deprived of liberty (PDLs) have graduated from TESDA
programs implemented in partnership with the Bureau of Jail Management and
Penology (BJMP).

       The latest data from the Bureau of Jail Management and Penology (BJMP) has
reported that as of July 31, 2025, there is a total detainee population of 115,065
persons, comprising both detainees and sentenced individuals. Given the current state
of the world and the Philippines, securing employment is crucial. One of the challenges
of PDLs after their release is the difficulty in securing employment opportunities. The
existence of their criminal record serves as a barrier hindering their effective
reintegration into society. PDLs who have fully served their sentence should not be
subjected to continued punishment beyond imprisonment. It would be inhumane and
contrary to the principles of rehabilitation and reintegration if they remain socially
excluded.
        This legislative measure seeks to institutionalize and expand TESDA skills
training programs in all jails and prisons nationwide, in coordination with the Bureau
of Corrections (BuCor), Bureau of Jail Management (BJMP) and local government
units. The proposed bill also aims to ensure adequate funding, facilities and
certification mechanisms to uplift the lives of PDLs and improve public safety.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                                AN ACT
     INSTITUTIONALIZING A SKILLS TRAINING PROGRAM AND PROVIDING
     JOB OPPORTUNITIES TO PERSONS DEPRIVED OF LIBERTY (PDLs) IN
     DETENTION FACILITIES, APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR AND FOR
     OTHER PURPOSES

           Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
     in Congress assembled:

 1           SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known and cited as the
 2   “Institutionalization of Skills Training for Persons Deprived of Liberty (IS-PDL).”
 3
 4          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to uphold the
 5   principles of the Constitution, ensuring that no one, regardless of their social status,
 6   is denied their right to education and gainful employment.
 7
 8          The State, by exercising its authority, shall employ appropriate actions in
 9   ensuring that education is made accessible to all. All persons deprived of liberty, whose
10   fundamental rights are not deemed extinguished, shall be afforded access to
11   education, wherein, the period of service of their sentence shall not be regarded as a
12   punishment but rather, a time for rehabilitation and reintegration into society.
13
14          Sec. 3. Institutionalization of Skills Training Program for PDLs. (IS-PDL) – The
15   Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), in coordination with
16   the Bureau of Jail Management and Penology (BJMP), the Bureau of Corrections
17   (BuCor) and Local Government Units (LGUs) shall establish and maintain regular,
18   accessible and accredited technical-vocation education and skills training programs
19   within jails and detention centers nationwide.
20
21           TESDA, in coordination with the DOLE, shall institutionalize the provision of
22   technical-vocational education and training (TVET) and livelihood programs designed
23   specifically for rehabilitated drug dependents to further enhance their ability to find
24   gainful employment and entrepreneurial livelihood opportunities.
25
                                                                                            1
 1          Sec. 4. Scope and Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all national, regional,
 2   provincial, city and municipal detention and correctional facilities under the jurisdiction
 3   of the BuCor and BJMP.
 4
 5            Sec. 5. Livelihood Manufacturing Hubs. – The Department of Trade and
 6   Industry (DTI), in partnership with the BuCor and BJMP, shall facilitate the
 7   establishment of livelihood manufacturing hubs within the vicinity of detention
 8   facilities. These hubs shall allow PDLs to produce goods or provide services for
 9   government agencies or private partners, with the income earned being placed in a
10   trust account for the PDL’s family or for use upon their release.
11
12          Sec. 6. Reintegration and Job Placement Program. – The Department of Labor
13   and Employment (DOLE) shall create a “Public Employment Service Office (PESO) PDL
14   Desk” to assist PDLs who are nearing their release date. The desk shall provide career
15   counseling and job matching, referrals to former PDL-friendly employers and
16   assistance in securing necessary pre-employment documents.
17
18          Sec. 7. Incentives for Private Sector Participation. – Private entities that employ
19   former PDLs for at least one (1) year shall be entitled to an additional deduction from
20   their gross income, equivalent to fifteen percent (15%) of the total amount paid as
21   salaries and wages to such employees, subject to the rules and regulations of the
22   Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR).
23
24          Sec. 8. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
25   of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s appropriations of the BuCor,
26   BJMP, TESDA, and DOLE. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued
27   implementation shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).
28
29           Sec. 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the
30   effectivity of this Act, the TESDA shall, in coordination with the DOLE, formulate the
31   rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.
32
33          SEC. 10. Separability Clause. – If for any reason, any part or provision of this
34   Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not
35   affected shall remain in full force and effect.
36
37          SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,
38   rules and regulations contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
39   hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
40
41          SEC. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
42   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of national circulation.
