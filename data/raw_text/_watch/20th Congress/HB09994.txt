                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Philippine agricultural sector, the backbone of our nation’s food security,
remains shackled by a persistent and debilitating cycle of indebtedness. While
the New Agrarian Emancipation Act (RA 11953) provided landmark relief by
condoning debts related to agrarian reform land awards, it addressed only one
facet of the crisis. It left a significant portion of the farming
population—specifically smallholder farmers with private-sector and
commercial bank obligations—without a lifeline.

Currently, distressed agricultural loans prevent farmers from accessing formal
credit markets, rendering them "un-bankable." This financial exclusion forces
producers into the hands of informal lenders and predatory interests, often
leading to "pawning" or the outright loss of agricultural lands. Without a
dedicated mechanism to resolve these non-performing loans, the sector cannot
modernize or achieve the productivity levels required for national food security.

Through the establishment of the Agricultural Debt Management Corporation
(ADMC), this measure introduces a strategic, long-term solution to debt
restructuring, ensuring that our agricultural producers can transition from
financial distress toward sustainable productivity. The ADMC aims to restore the
creditworthiness of small farmers through proactive loan negotiations and
rehabilitation, allowing their reentry into the formal banking system under
sustainable terms. Likewise, the ADMC seeks to safeguard national food security
through the management of non-performing assets to prevent the loss of
productive lands to premature conversion or land grabbing.

Lastly, this measure secures the future of Philippine agriculture by keeping land
in the hands of those who till it. It empowers our farmers and creates the stable
financial foundation required to build a truly resilient agricultural economy for
the benefit of all Filipinos.

In light of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT ESTABLISHING THE AGRICULTURAL DEBT MANAGEMENT
CORPORATION (ADMC) TO PROVIDE FOR THE DEBT REHABILITATION
  AND ASSET PROTECTION OF SMALL FARMERS AND FISHERFOLK,
            AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "The Agricultural
Financial Recovery Act of 2026."

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to ensure national
food security by protecting the financial viability of small farmers and fisherfolk.
The State shall provide a mechanism to rehabilitate distressed agricultural credit,
prevent the alienation of productive lands, and transition rural producers from
informal, predatory lending toward the formal financial system.

SECTION 3. Creation of the Agricultural Debt Management Corporation
(ADMC). – The Agricultural Debt Management Corporation (ADMC) is hereby
established as an attached agency of the Department of Finance (DOF). The
ADMC shall possess the powers of a corporation and is specifically mandated to
perform the following functions:

   a. To purchase non-performing loans (NPLs) from government financial
      institutions (GFIs) and private banks. Acquisitions shall be valued based
      on the Net Present Value (NPV) of the collateral, ensuring no windfall
      profits for selling institutions.
   b. To offer eligible borrowers a Rehabilitation Plan with a grace period of up
      to ten (10) years and a fixed interest rate not exceeding 1.5 % per annum.
   c. To provide refinancing for farmers with proven informal debts (usury),
      provided such debts are verified by the Barangay Agrarian Reform
      Committee (BARC).
   d. To automatically trigger a two-year foreclosure moratorium in provinces
      declared under a State of Calamity.

SECTION 4. Eligibility. –
   a.​ Coverage. – The benefits of this Act shall apply to small farmers owning
       or cultivating not more than five (5) hectares of agricultural land, and
       fisherfolk utilizing fishing vessels of three (3) gross tons or less.

   b.​ Anti-Moral Hazard and Distressed Status. – Eligibility for debt
       rehabilitation shall be strictly limited to "distressed" borrowers whose
       delinquency is directly attributable to force majeure, significant market
       fluctuations, or documented crop failure. Any "strategic default,"
       characterized by a borrower’s intentional or willful refusal to pay despite
       having the financial capacity to do so, shall result in immediate
       disqualification and the acceleration of the original debt.

   c.​ Productivity and Insurance Linkage. – To maintain their restructured
       status and the 2% preferential interest rate, borrowers are required to:

         1.​ Enroll in the Department of Agriculture’s (DA) relevant technical
             assistance and extension programs; and

         2.​ Register with the Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation (PCIC) to
             mitigate future credit risks.​
             Failure to comply with these linkages shall be grounds for the
             cancellation of the Rehabilitation Plan.

   d.​ Financial Graduation and Credit Incentivization. – Borrowers who have
       successfully settled at least fifty percent (50%) of the principal amount of
       their restructured debt under the ADMC Rehabilitation Plan, and have
       maintained a consistent repayment record for at least three (3) consecutive
       years, shall be issued a "Certificate of Credit Eligibility."

         1.​ The Land Bank of the Philippines (LBP) and the Development Bank
             of the Philippines (DBP) shall establish a dedicated credit window
             for certified borrowers to provide immediate access to low-interest
             production loans.

         2.​ Any new credit extended by banking institutions to a holder of a
             Certificate of Credit Eligibility shall be entitled to a two-hundred
             percent (200%) credit valuation for the purpose of compliance with
             the mandatory twenty-five percent (25%) agriculture, fisheries, and
             rural development financing requirement under Republic Act No.
             11901.

         3.​ The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) shall, within sixty (60) days
             from the effectivity of this Act, issue guidelines to lower the
             risk-weighting of loans extended to certified "Credit-Rehabilitated"
             borrowers to further encourage private sector participation.

SECTION 5. Funding.– The ADMC shall have an authorized capital stock
subscribed by the National Government, the structure and payment of which
shall be determined by the DOF and DBM. The sum necessary for its initial and
continued operations is hereby authorized to be appropriated and included in
the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).

b) Regulatory Incentives. – The BSP, in coordination with the DOF, shall prescribe
the rules for ADMC instruments, including their eligibility for statutory
compliance, tax treatment, and other incentives to encourage private sector
participation.

SECTION 6. Sunset Clause. – The ADMC shall have a corporate term of
twenty-five (25) years, after which its assets and remaining functions shall be
absorbed by the Land Bank of the Philippines.

SECTION 7. Separability Clause. – If any provision is declared unconstitutional,
the remaining sections shall remain valid.

SECTION 8. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or two (2) newspapers of general circulation.

Approved.
