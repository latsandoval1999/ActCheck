                                EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Global oil markets have become increasingly volatile due to geopolitical
tensions, supply disruptions, and fluctuations in international currency
markets. As a net importer of petroleum products, the Philippines remains highly
vulnerable to sudden increases in global oil prices. These external shocks are
quickly transmitted to domestic fuel prices, resulting in rising transportation
costs, higher food prices, and inflationary pressures that disproportionately
affect Filipino households, workers, and small businesses.

       Fuel price volatility also creates broader macroeconomic risks. Sharp
increases in petroleum prices can trigger inflation beyond government targets,
increase production and logistics costs, weaken purchasing power, and slow
economic growth. In a country where transportation and energy costs
significantly influence the prices of basic goods and services, sustained oil price
spikes can produce widespread economic hardship.

      While existing tax policy provides a stable framework for revenue
generation and public spending, extraordinary oil price shocks may require
timely calibrated and temporary interventions to protect consumers and the
national economy. At present, existing law provides only limited flexibility in
responding to such situations.

      This proposed measure seeks to establish a Fuel Price Stabilization
Mechanism that allows the government to respond swiftly and responsibly to
extraordinary oil price shocks. The bill authorizes the President of the
Philippines, upon the recommendation of the Secretary of Finance and in
consultation with the Department of Energy and the Department of Economy,
Planning, and Development, to temporarily suspend or reduce excise taxes on
petroleum products when clearly defined economic conditions are present.
      Importantly, the measure does not permanently alter the excise tax
structure established under existing law. Instead, it provides a temporary and
conditional fiscal tool designed to stabilize domestic fuel prices during periods of
exceptional global volatility. The bill includes safeguards to ensure responsible
implementation, including:

      clearly defined economic trigger conditions;

      a limited duration for any tax adjustment;

      mandatory pass-through of tax reductions to consumers;

      reporting requirements to Congress; and

      a sunset clause ensuring that the authority expires unless renewed by
       Congress.

      By providing a structured and rule-based mechanism for temporary tax
adjustments, this measure balances the need for consumer protection with the
equally important goal of maintaining fiscal stability and responsible tax policy.

      The measure is designed as a rule-based economic stabilization
mechanism rather than a permanent tax reduction. By establishing objective
economic triggers and fiscal safeguards, the bill ensures that temporary
adjustments to fuel excise taxes may be implemented only when extraordinary
economic conditions threaten national economic stability.

      The proposed legislation therefore serves as an economic stabilization tool
that equips the government with the flexibility necessary to respond to
extraordinary global oil price shocks while preserving the integrity of the
country’s fiscal framework.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           “AN ACT
   AUTHORIZING THE PRESIDENT TO TEMPORARILY SUSPEND OR
   REDUCE EXCISE TAXES ON PETROLEUM PRODUCTS DURING
   EXTRAORDINARY GLOBAL OIL PRICE SHOCKS, PROVIDING A FUEL
   PRICE STABILIZATION MECHANISM, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES”

      SECTION 1. Short Title.- This Act shall be known as the “Fuel Price
Stabilization and Excise Tax Flexibility Act.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy.- It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to protect the Filipino people and the national economy from severe
economic disruption caused by extraordinary increases in global oil prices and
related macroeconomic shocks.

       The State recognizes that geopolitical tensions, supply disruptions, and
currency volatility may cause sharp increases in domestic petroleum prices,
resulting in inflationary pressures, higher transportation costs, and economic
hardship for Filipino households and enterprises.

      Accordingly, the State shall establish a temporary and conditional fiscal
mechanism allowing the suspension of the imposition of excise taxes on
petroleum products in order to mitigate the impact of extraordinary oil price
shocks while maintaining fiscal responsibility and the stability of government
revenues.

       Nothing in this Act shall be construed as permanently modifying the excise
tax structure established under existing law.

      SECTION 3. Initial Emergency Authority. - Within six (6) months from
the effectivity of this Act, the President of the Philippines may, upon the
recommendation of the Secretary of Finance and after consultation with the
Secretary of Energy, and the Secretary of the Department of Economy, Planning,
and Development, temporarily suspend or reduce excise taxes on petroleum
products subject to the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended by
Republic Act No. 10963, otherwise known as the “Tax Reform for Acceleration
and Inclusion (TRAIN)” law.

   SECTION 4. Fuel Price Stabilization Mechanism. - After the lapse of the
emergency period provided under Section 3, the authority granted under this Act
may thereafter be exercised only upon the determination that extraordinary
global oil price shocks and related macroeconomic pressures exist, as evidenced
by the presence of two (2) or more of the following conditions:

   a. The average price of Dubai crude oil reaches or exceeds Ninety United
      States Dollars (US$90) per barrel for three (3) consecutive months, based
      on internationally recognized benchmarks;

   b. the national inflation rate exceeds the government’s target range or shows
      significant upward pressure attributable in part to increases in domestic
      fuel and transportation costs, as determined by the appropriate economic
      authorities; and

   c. the Philippine peso has experienced significant depreciation against the
      United States dollar over the same period, resulting in higher import costs
      for petroleum products.

       The existence of the foregoing conditions shall be certified by the Secretary
of Energy, in coordination with the Secretary of Finance and the Secretary of the
Department of Economy, Planning, and Development, who shall thereafter
recommend to the President the appropriate suspension or reduction of excise
taxes.

       SECTION 5. Coverage.- The authority granted under this Act shall apply
to petroleum products subject to excise tax under the National Internal Revenue
Code, including but not limited to the following:

   a) diesel fuel oil;
   b) gasoline;
   c) kerosene;
   d) liquefied petroleum gas (LPG);
   e) aviation fuel; and
   f) other petroleum products subject to excise tax under the National Internal
      Revenue Code of 1997, as amended by Republic Act No. 10963.

      SECTION 6. Mandatory Pass-Through of Excise Tax Reduction to
Consumers.- Any suspension or reduction of excise taxes on petroleum products
authorized under this Act shall be fully and promptly reflected in the retail pump
prices of petroleum products.
      All refiners, importers, distributors, and retailers of petroleum products
shall adjust their pricing to ensure that the corresponding reduction in excise
taxes is passed on to consumers.

      Such reduction shall be reflected in the retail pump prices within a
reasonable period to be determined by the Department of Energy.

      The Department of Energy, in coordination with the Department of Trade
and Industry and other relevant agencies, shall:

   1. Monitor the retail prices of petroleum products to ensure compliance with
      this provision;

   2. Publish regular reports on fuel price movements and the estimated impact
      of excise tax adjustments; and

   3. Recommend appropriate administrative sanctions or enforcement actions
      against entities found to be engaging in unjustified price increases or
      failing to pass on the tax reductions to consumers.

     Failure to comply with this provision shall constitute a violation subject to
appropriate administrative sanctions pursuant to existing laws, including the Oil
Deregulation Law and other applicable regulations.

       SECTION 7. Duration of Tax Adjustment. - Any suspension or reduction
of excise taxes authorized under this Act shall be temporary and shall not exceed
six (6) months for each activation, unless extended by Congress through a joint
resolution.

       SECTION 8. Revenue Neutrality Safeguard. - The exercise of authority
under this Act shall be implemented in a manner that minimizes adverse fiscal
impact and preserves the overall revenue objectives of national tax policy and
fiscal sustainability.

      For this purpose, the Department of Finance and the Department of
Budget and Management shall recommend appropriate fiscal or expenditure
adjustments necessary to maintain the sustainability of government programs
and public finances.

      SECTION 9. Report to Congress. - Within thirty (30) days from the
exercise of authority under this Act, the President shall submit a report to
Congress containing:

   1. The economic basis for the suspension or reduction of excise taxes;
   2. The estimated fiscal impact on government revenues;
   3. The expected effects on domestic fuel prices, inflation, and transportation
      costs.

      SECTION 10. Sunset Clause. - The authority granted under this Act shall
expire five (5) years after its effectivity unless sooner terminated or extended by
Congress through a joint resolution and after review of its economic and fiscal
impact.

      Nothing in this Act shall be construed as permanently modifying the excise
tax rates established under existing law.

      SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within fifteen (15)
days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance, in coordination
with the Department of Energy, the Bureau of Internal Revenue, and the
Department of Economy, Planning, and Development, shall promulgate the rules
and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

       SECTION 12. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

      SECTION 13. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
rules, and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.

      SECTION 14. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after
its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
