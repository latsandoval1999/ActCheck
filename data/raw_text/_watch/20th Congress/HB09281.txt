19                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

20           The Philippines faces a persistent solid waste management crisis, with plastic packaging
21   constituting a significant portion of improperly disposed waste. Despite the enactment of
22   Republic Act No. 9003, or the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000, which
23   established the framework for waste reduction, segregation, recycling, and disposal,
24   implementation gaps remain evident, particularly in waste segregation at source and material
25   recovery efficiency.

26            To address these challenges, Republic Act No. 11898, otherwise known as the Extended
27   Producer Responsibility (EPR) Act of 2022, mandated producers to take responsibility for the
28   recovery and proper management of plastic packaging waste. While this law has created
29   mechanisms for accountability, consumer participation in proper waste segregation remains a
30   critical factor in ensuring the success of EPR schemes and in advancing the country’s transition
31   toward circular economy practices.

32          This bill introduces the Philippine Recycling Labeling Act of 2025, which mandates the
33   standardized labeling of consumer product packaging to provide clear, consistent, and accessible
34   information on recyclability, material composition, disposal instructions, and recycled content.

35          This Act seeks to strengthen waste segregation at source by empowering consumers with
36   accurate disposal guidance, enhance material recovery by aligning consumer practices with the
37   capacity of local solid waste management systems, improve compliance with extended producer
38   responsibility obligations through transparency and traceability of packaging materials, and
39   promote environmental awareness and sustainable consumption through consumer education.

40           Countries with similar labeling systems, such as the European Union, Japan, and South
41   Korea, have demonstrated that standardized recycling information significantly improves
42   recycling rates and consumer compliance. Adopting a similar framework in the Philippines will
43   help harmonize waste management practices, support the goals of RA 9003 and RA 11898, and
44   reduce the volume of residual and mismanaged waste that pollutes the environment.
45   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought

75   SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Recycling Labeling Act of
76   2026.”

77   SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - Pursuant to its commitment to environmental sustainability and
78   responsible resource management, the State hereby declares the following policies:

79   To ensure the environmentally sound management of solid waste in accordance with the provisions
80   of Republic Act No. 9003, otherwise known as the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000,
81   by promoting waste segregation, reduction, recycling, and proper disposal practices across all sectors
82   of society.

83   To strengthen the recovery, recycling, and circular utilization of packaging materials in alignment
84   with Republic Act No. 11898, or the Extended Producer Responsibility Act of 2022, thereby fostering
85   producer accountability and advancing plastic neutrality through systematic and measurable recovery
86   programs.

87   To institutionalize consumer education and active participation by mandating the provision of clear,
88   accurate, and accessible recycling information on product packaging, empowering consumers to
89   make informed choices and contribute meaningfully to national waste reduction efforts.

90   SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. - For purposes of this Act, the following terms are defined:

91   Composite/Multilayer Flexible Packaging (MLP)
92   Consumer Packaging
93   Industrial Packaging
94   E-Commerce Packaging
95   “Packaging” – shall refer to any material used to contain, protect, handle, deliver, or present goods,
96   consistent with RA 11898.
97   Post-Consumer Recycled Content (PCR)
 98   Recycling Label” – refers to a standardized mark or printed instruction indicating the material type,
 99   recyclability, and appropriate disposal pathway, in accordance with local solid waste management
100   systems.
101   “Recyclability” – shall refer to the ability of a material to be collected, segregated, and reprocessed
102   in existing material recovery facilities, accredited recyclers, or other authorized recovery schemes.
103   Resin Identification Code

104   SEC. 4. Mandatory Recycling Labels. - All consumer product packaging, whether locally
105   manufactured or imported, shall bear a standardized recycling label, hereinafter referred to as the
106   Philippine Recycling Label (PRL), which shall contain the following information:

107   Material Type – specifying the material composition of the packaging, including but not limited to
108   polyethylene terephthalate (PET), high-density polyethylene (HDPE), polypropylene (PP), paper,
109   aluminum, and glass;

110   Recyclability Indicator – explicitly stating whether the packaging is recyclable in the Philippines, to
111   be indicated as “Recyclable in PH” or “Not Recyclable in PH,” as may be applicable;

112           Recyclable Nationwide;

113           Recyclable in Special Facilities; and

114           Not Recyclable in the Philippines.

115   Disposal Instruction – providing clear instructions for proper disposal, such as “Segregate under
116   Recyclables” or “Dispose as Residual Waste”;

117   Recycled Content Declaration – declaring the percentage of recycled material used in the packaging,
118   if any, e.g., “Made with 30% recycled plastic”; and

119   Language Requirement – all required information shall be printed in the English language;

120   SEC. 5. Label Specifications. – The recycling label shall be legible, occupying not less than two
121   percent (2%) of the total surface area of the packaging, and in no case smaller than two square
122   centimeters (2 cm²) for every one hundred square centimeters (100 cm²) of packaging surface.

123   The label shall be printed in high contrast, placed in a conspicuous location on the packaging, and
124   shall not be obscured by decorative elements, seals, or any other markings. For flexible packaging,
125   PRL may be printed, sleeved, banded, or stickered using high-adhesion labels. Labels may
126   include QR codes linking to recyclability databases.

127   SEC. 6. Scope and Coverage. – The provisions of this Act shall apply to all primary and secondary
128   packaging of consumer products, including but not limited to food, beverages, household goods,
129   personal care items, and electronic products.

130   SEC. 7. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act may be
131   charged against the current appropriations of the PNAC. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary
132   for the continued implementation of this Act must be included in the annual General Appropriations
133   Act.

134   SEC. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), in
135   coordination with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) and the National
136   Solid Waste Management Commission (NSWMC), shall promulgate and issue the rules and
137   regulations within one (1) year from the effectivity of this Act.

138   SEC. 9. Separability Clause. – If any portion or provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional,
139   the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall remain in force and effect.
140   SEC. 10. Repealing Clause. – All decrees, executive orders, proclamations, and administrative
141   regulations or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended
142   or modified accordingly.

143   SEC. 11. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official
144   Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.
