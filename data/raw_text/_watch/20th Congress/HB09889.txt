                               EXPLANATORY NOTE


       Article II, Section 16 of the 1987 Constitution mandates the State to protect
and advance the right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology in accord with
the rhythm and harmony of nature. Pursuant to this constitutional duty, Republic Act
No. 11038, otherwise known as the “Expanded National Integrated Protected Areas
System Act of 2018” or the E-NIPAS Act, was enacted to strengthen the protection,
conservation, and sustainable management of the country’s ecologically significant
areas.

       Among the protected areas declared under the E-NIPAS Act is the Ticao-Burias
Pass Protected Seascape, or TBPPS, a marine ecosystem of national importance
spanning portions of Masbate, Albay, Sorsogon, and Camarines Sur. As part of the
country’s protected-area system, the TBPPS represents the State’s commitment to
preserve biodiversity, protect critical habitats, and ensure the responsible use of
natural resources for present and future generations.

        The Province of Sorsogon fully recognizes the importance of the TBPPS. For
many years, Sorsogon has been at the forefront of marine conservation, coastal
protection, and nature-based tourism. The municipalities of Pilar, Donsol, Magallanes,
and Castilla, among others, are directly linked to the life, livelihood, and ecological
integrity of the Ticao-Burias Pass. These coastal communities depend on the Ticao-
Burias Pass for fishing, tourism, transportation, small enterprises, and local economic
activity. However, implementation of the E-NIPAS Act has raised serious concerns
where long-established barangay sites, town centers, residential communities, private
properties, alienable and disposable lands, and areas devoted to public use appear to
have been included within the protected area. These areas are not pristine or

                                          1
uninhabited conservation zones, but existing communities where families have long
lived, built homes, sustained livelihoods, and established lawful possession or
ownership prior to the passage of the E-NIPAS Act. The strict application of protected-
area restrictions and coastal easement rules may therefore cause uncertainty, delay
essential public infrastructure, burden small enterprises, and expose residents to
hardship, displacement, and avoidable disputes. This concern is particularly felt in
Sorsogon’s coastal municipalities, where marine conservation must be balanced with
access to livelihood, transport, tourism, and basic public services.

        This bill, therefore, seeks to amend the territorial coverage of the Ticao-Burias
Pass Protected Seascape under Republic Act No. 11038, in order to correct the
inclusion of alienable and disposable lands, titled or privately owned properties,
existing barangay settlements, town sites, and areas already devoted to lawful public
use. The measure does not seek to diminish the State’s commitment to environmental
protection. Rather, the proposed amendment is a practical and balanced response to
the realities of Sorsogon’s coastal municipalities. It preserves the ecological integrity
of the Ticao-Burias Pass, while ensuring that residents, fisherfolk, small businesses,
and local governments are not unnecessarily burdened by an overly broad or imprecise
protected-area boundary. In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill
is earnestly sought.

       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is earnestly sought.
