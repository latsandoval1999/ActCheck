                            EXPLANATORY NOTE


Digital social media platforms have evolved far beyond basic communication
networks; they are now the primary infrastructure for public discourse,
commerce, news dissemination, and social interaction in the Philippines. As of
recent estimates, over 90 million Filipinos—roughly 78 percent of the national
population—utilize social media networks such as Facebook, TikTok,
YouTube, and X daily.

Despite generating billions of pesos in advertising revenues from local
businesses and content creators, major foreign technology conglomerates
operate within Philippine digital borders without a physical corporate
footprint, local regulatory oversight, or proper operational accountability. This
lack of local jurisdiction creates significant public policy challenges.

Platform operators frequently fail to act swiftly on takedown requests involving
dangerous content, online scams, cyberbullying, hate speech, deepfakes, and
illegal acts targeting Filipino minors. Further, these operators generate
massive revenue streams locally while evading equivalent domestic corporate
taxes, regulatory fees, and compliance burdens imposed on traditional
broadcast and telecommunication entities.

Under Commonwealth Act No. 146 (the Public Service Act), as amended,
entities operating services of significant public consequence must be subject
to state regulation to safeguard the public welfare. Just as traditional
television, radio, and telecommunications networks require legislative
franchises to access public airwaves and infrastructure, social media
platforms operating at scale utilize public digital space and national
telecommunications networks to reach citizens.

This bill mandates that major social media platforms and digital content
networks reaching a defined active user threshold must secure a
Congressional Franchise to operate legally within the Republic of the
Philippines. This requirement ensures that multinational technology
companies establish a registered physical office, appoint local corporate
officers subject to Philippine jurisdiction, comply with strict content safety
protocols, and contribute fairly to the nation's tax base.


In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

 AN ACT REQUIRING MAJOR SOCIAL MEDIA PLATFORMS AND DIGITAL
 CONTENT NETWORKS TO SECURE A CONGRESSIONAL FRANCHISE TO
      OPERATE IN THE PHILIPPINES, PROVIDING FOR REGULATORY
     COMPLIANCE, AND PRESCRIBING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS
                                  THEREOF
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Social Media
Franchise and Digital Accountability Act."
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to recognize the
vital role of communication and information technology in nation-building.
While the State respects free expression and digital innovation, it maintains
the fundamental duty to protect public safety, consumer rights, national
security, and fiscal integrity against unregulated digital public services
operating within its jurisdiction.

SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Act:

   1. Social Media Platform (SMP) — Refers to any web- or application-based
      service that enables user-generated content creation, sharing,
      networking, interactive media distribution, or digital public aggregation.

   2. Covered Platform — Any Social Media Platform or digital content
      network operating in the Philippines that exceeds a monthly active user
      (MAU) threshold of five million (5,000,000) users within the national
      territory.

   3. Franchise — A privilege granted by an Act of Congress authorizing a
      Covered Platform to operate digital services, offer targeted digital
      advertising, and process user data within the domestic jurisdiction.
SEC. 4. Franchise Requirement. — No Covered Platform shall directly or
indirectly provide social networking services, digital media distribution, or
monetization features to users within the territory of the Philippines without
first securing a valid Congressional Franchise.



SEC. 5. Mandatory Local Presence and Registration. — As a prerequisite to the
issuance and retention of a Legislative Franchise, a Covered Platform must:

   1. Register as a domestic corporation or establish a legal branch office
      registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and
      the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR).

   2. Maintain a physical head office within Metro Manila and appoint a
      resident Corporate Manager accountable to Philippine administrative
      and judicial authorities.



SEC. 6. Regulatory Framework and Obligations. — All Covered Platforms
holding a franchise under this Act shall strictly comply with the following
operational obligations:

   1. Content Safety and Takedown Compliance: Establish automated and
      human-led moderation protocols to review and remove illegal content—
      including child sexual abuse material (CSAM), non-consensual imagery,
      online fraud, and malicious deepfakes—within twenty-four (24) hours of
      receiving a formal notice from Philippine law enforcement or regulatory
      agencies.

   2. Grievance Redress Mechanism: Maintain a direct, localized user support
      center to address account compromise, identity theft, and content
      appeals for Philippine-based users.

   3. Tax and Fiscal Compliance: Submit to local corporate taxation, value-
      added tax (VAT) on digital service providers, and local auditing
      standards for all ad revenues generated from Philippine users and
      businesses.



SEC. 7. Regulatory Authority. — The Department of Information and
Communications Technology (DICT), in coordination with the National
Telecommunications Commission (NTC) and the National Privacy Commission
(NPC), shall act as the primary administrative regulatory bodies supervising
franchised platforms.
SEC. 8. Penalties. —

   1. Administrative Sanctions: Failure to comply with content safety
      directives or tax duties shall subject the franchise holder to
      administrative fines ranging from Five Million Pesos (₱5,000,000.00) to
      One Hundred Million Pesos (₱100,000,000.00) per violation.

   2. Access Restrictions: Operating without a valid Congressional Franchise
      after the designated compliance period shall empower the NTC to order
      local Internet Service Providers (ISPs) to block public access to the non-
      compliant platform.

   3. Franchise Revocation: Congress reserves the statutory right to suspend
      or revoke a franchise for repeated breaches of national security, child
      safety mandates, or persistent non-compliance with Philippine judicial
      orders.

SEC. 9. Transitory Provisions. — Social Media Platforms actively operating in
the Philippines prior to the effectivity of this Act shall be granted a grace period
of twelve (12) months from the promulgation of the Implementing Rules and
Regulations (IRR) to submit their franchise application and establish a local
corporate entity.

SEC. 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). — Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the DICT, in consultation with the NTC, SEC,
BIR, and relevant civil society stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary
rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SEC. 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision or section of this Act is held
invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall continue in full
force and effect.

SEC. 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed,
amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
circulation.
