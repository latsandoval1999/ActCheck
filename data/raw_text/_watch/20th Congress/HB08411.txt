                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Constitution unequivocally mandates that public funds be safeguarded
and expended strictly in accordance with law. Article IX-D of the 1987
Constitution vests the Commission on Audit (COA) with the authority,
responsibility, and duty to examine, audit, and settle all accounts pertaining to
government revenues and expenditures. This constitutional grant affirms the State
policy that accountability and transparency in the use of public funds are
indispensable pillars of democratic governance.

       While the COA traditionally exercises post-audit functions, the Constitution
likewise empowers it to promulgate accounting and auditing rules and regulations
designed to prevent illegal, irregular, unnecessary, excessive, extravagant, or
unconscionable expenditures. Preventive mechanisms, such as pre-audit, are
therefore well within the constitutional mandate of the COA and serve as crucial
first line of defense in protecting the public treasury.

      Prior to July 2011, pre-audit functions were regularly performed by COA,
and large-scale irregularities such as “ghost” or substandard flood control projects
receiving full payment did not happen or were far less prevalent. On July 22,
2011, COA issued Circular No. 2011-002, which lifted the selective pre-audit of
government transactions and removed pre-audit as a requisite for payment. This
policy shift was anchored on the premise that government agencies have already
instituted and are maintaining adequate control systems capable of safeguarding
public funds against abuse and misuse. Under this framework greater reliance
was placed on post- audit procedures, with the expectation that agency-level
controls sufficiently ensure compliance with applicable laws, rules, and
regulations.

1
       What has been happening, however, since the pre-audit requirement was
removed shows that serious irregularities have been occurring, to the extent that
even ghost projects have been paid using public funds. This exposed serious
weaknesses in the internal controls of government agencies and demonstrated the
limitations of relying solely on post-audit mechanisms, where irregularities are
often discovered only after public funds have been disbursed, thereby reducing
accountability measures to remedial rather than preventive actions. It is likened
to a situation where thieves can steal before the government can run after them.
Furthermore, experience has shown that the recovery of misused or lost public
funds through post audit processes is extremely limited, as the percentage of
recovered funds remains very minimal compared to the total amount of
government expenditures and losses incurred.

      This measure therefore seeks to address these serious problems and
systemic gaps by institutionalizing pre-audit coupled by geo-mapping and geo-
tagging as mandatory requirements for payment of all government funded
projects.

        Moreover, this measure likewise provides for the corresponding
augmentation of personnel and resources since the institutionalization of
mandatory pre-audit and geo-mapping requirements necessarily expand the
operational responsibilities of the COA; hence, it requires adequate manpower,
technical capacity, and digital infrastructure support to ensure timely review of
transactions without unduly delaying legitimate government operations. The
inclusion therefore of necessary funding in the annual General Appropriations
Act is necessary. This ensures that the constitutional mandate of the COA under
Article IX-D is supported not only in principle but also through sufficient
institutional capacity.

      Ultimately, this bill gives full effect to the constitutional mandate of the
Commission on Audit and advances the fundamental principle that public office
is a public trust. It recognizes that the protection of public funds demands not
only accountability after the fact, but more importantly, robust preventive
safeguards to ensure that government resources are used solely for their intended
purposes and for the benefit of the Filipino people.

    In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

    AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING MANDATORY PRE-AUDIT AND
    GEO-MAPPING REQUIREMENTS FOR GOVERNMENT-FUNDED
        PROJECTS, STRENGTHENING PREVENTIVE AUDIT
            MECHANISMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:
SECTION 1: Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Mandatory Pre-
Audit and Geo-Mapping Accountability Act”.
Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to ensure
accountability, transparency, and integrity in the management and utilization of
public funds. The government shall adopt measures that promote prudent fiscal
management, prevent corruption, and ensure that appropriated funds are used in
accordance with the law and for their intended purposes
Sec. 3. Mandatory Pre-Audit Requirement. - All government-funded projects
shall be subject to mandatory pre-audit clearance by the Commission on Audit
(COA) prior to the release of funds or processing of payments.
      No disbursement shall be made from government funds unless the COA
pre-audit clearance is attached to the payment request or voucher, except where
law expressly provides otherwise.
Sec. 4. Mandatory Geo-Mapping and Geo-Tagging in Project
Implementation. – All government-funded projects, particularly infrastructure
and capital outlay projects, shall be subject to mandatory geo-mapping and geo-
tagging throughout all stages of project implementation, including:


3
    1. Project planning and site validation;
    2. Project commencement.
    3. Periodic progress monitoring; and
    4. Project completion and turnover.
      Geo-mapping documentation shall include geo-tagged photographs,
geographic coordinates, and other location-based information sufficient to verify
existence, location, and physical accomplishment.
Sec. 5. Geo-Mapping as Supporting Documentation for Pre-Audit. – Geo-
mapping and geo-tagging documentation shall form part of the required
supporting documents for COA pre-audit clearance and payment processing.
       Failure to comply with geo-mapping requirements shall constitute
sufficient ground for withholding pre-audit clearance and suspension of payment.
Sec. 6. Duties of the Commission on Audit. – The COA shall:
    1. Establish procedures for mandatory pre-audit implementation;
    2. Issue standards on geo-mapping and geo-tagging documentation;
    3. Coordinate with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM),
       Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) and
       implementing agencies on digital reporting systems; and
    4. Submit annual implementation reports to Congress.
Sec. 7. Funding and Personnel Augmentation. To ensure the effective
implementation of the mandatory pre-audit and geo-mapping requirements under
this Act, the personnel complement of the COA shall be augmented upon its
determination of the necessary additional staffing requirements, including
auditors, technical personnel, and information support staff
      The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Act shall be
charged against the current appropriations of the COA. Thereafter, such sums as
may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act (GAA).
       The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) shall give priority
consideration to the funding requirements necessary to strengthen the preventive
audit and digital monitoring capabilities of the COA pursuant to this Act.
Sec. 8. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the COA, in consultation with the DBM and other
concerned agencies, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for
effective implementation of this Act.
