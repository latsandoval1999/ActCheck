                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Correlative to the State's constitutional mandate to "protect and advance the
right of the people to a balanced and healthful ecology,"1 is the duty to refrain from
impairing the environment.2 While such duties are applicable in all circumstances, they
become even more urgent in areas where environmental degradation can lead to great
ecological and socio-economic losses.

       One such area is the Verde Island Passage (VIP), a 1.3-million-hectare marine
corridor spanning five provinces, and is scientifically known as the "center of the center
of marine shore fish biodiversity" and globally recognized as the "Amazon of the
Oceans".3 The VIP is not only home to approximately 1,736 fish species and over 300
coral species, but it is also a fundamental source of food security and livelihoods of
over 2 million people, with its fisheries producing approximately P1 billion annually, not

        The importance of the VIP is uncontested, yet it continues to lack significant
legislative protection that would preclude environmental degradation in the face of
increasing industrial developments. As of current, five of the six fossil gas plants in the
Philippines are located in the passage, and proposals are underway for eight (8) more
power plants and seven LNG terminals.6

       The catastrophe brought about by the 2023 MT Princess Empress oil spill,
which led to extensive damages amounting to over 40 billion,7 lends credence to the
grave risk the VIP is continuously subjected to. Such industrial expansion
exponentially increases the risk of air, water, and thermal pollution, potentially leading
to irreversible damage to VIP’s marine and terrestrial ecosystems.8 It is thus
unsurprising to find that the passage is characterized by extremely low water quality,
an excessive concentration of toxic substances, and a subsequent decrease in daily
fish catches for our country's fisherfolk.9

       Moreover, intensified commercial fishing and land reclamation activities
continue to deplete fish stocks and destroy mangroves and coastal ecosystems,
disproportionately affecting small-scale fishers10.

       Immediate legislative intervention is thus crucial. The Verde Island Passage
Protected Seascape (VIPPS) Bill seeks to address the glaring inadequacy of
environmental safeguards in existing legal and institutional mechanisms for the
protection and sustainable management of the VIP by legally declaring the passage a
protected area with the category of Protected Seascape. Such classification will allow
for more comprehensive regulations necessary to ensure both the rehabilitation and
conservation of the passage.

      This measure aligns with the Philippines’ commitment to protect 30% of its
marine and terrestrial areas by 2030 and responds to international calls, including from
the United Nations (UN), to strengthen protection of the VIP11.

       This bill shall also institutionalize the creation of the VIPPS Management Board
(VIPPS-MB) as the sole policy-making body, consisting of regional directors from
various government agencies, local authorities, and at least 20 representatives from
NGOs or POs. It shall further ensure representation from fisherfolk, indigenous
peoples, academic institutions, the private sector, and the youth to promote inclusive
and science-based governance. The bill also mandates the creation of a specialized
Fisheries Monitoring Center (FMC) to identify Strict Protection Zones and No-Take
Zones based on regular scientific assessments and surveys. This ensures that
management decisions are guided by scientific data rather than purely political
considerations.

       Lastly, the measure strictly regulates industrial and extractive activities while
safeguarding the preferential rights of small-scale fishers, exempting them from
licensing requirements and mandating their representation in the VIPPS-MB and in
quarterly public consultations. It likewise prohibits destructive activities such as large-
scale mining, use of explosives or toxic substances in fishing, and operation of
commercial fishing vessels within designated coastal limits, while allowing sustainable
fishing practices outside no-take zones.

       Ultimately, this bill seeks to protect the Verde Island Passage from ecological
ruin and ensure the livelihoods and food security of our coastal communities. It affirms
that environmental protection and community welfare are complementary objectives
that must be pursued in tandem for long-term sustainability.

      In the 20th Congress, a similar bill was filed by Senator Loren Legarda as
Senate Bill No. 831 and Representative Arnan C. Panaligan as House Bill No. 132.

            Early approval of this measure is requested.

 4          SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.- In view of the global recognition of the area as
 5   the "center of the center of marine shore fish biodiversity" and cognizant of the
 6   profound impact of human activities on all components of the natural environment, the
 7   Verde Island Passage in the Provinces of Batangas, Occidental Mindoro, Oriental

 9   category of Protected Seascape and shall hereinafter be referred to as the Verde
10   Island Passage Protected Seascape (VIPPS). As such, the State shall ensure the

12   government shall also undertake the rehabilitation of a target ecosystem when

 8   with Section 14 of the R.A 7586, as amended, the exploration for energy resources

32          SEC. 18. Integrated Protected Area Fund (IPAF). - Pursuant to the ENIPAS Act
33   of 2018, income generated from the operation and management of the VIPPS shall
34   accrue to the Integrated Protected Area Fund (IPAF). The income shall be derived
35   from fees and charges from the use of resources and facilities of the VIPPS;
36   contributions from industries and facilities directly benefiting from the protected area;
37   and such other fees and income derived from the operation of the VIPPS.
38
39           The VIPPS-MB shall retain seventy-five percent (75%) of all revenues raised
40   10 through the above means, which shall be deposited in the Protected Area-Retained
41   Income Account (PA-RIA) in any authorized government depository bank within the
42   locality: Provided, That disbursements out of such deposits shall be used solely for the
43   protection, maintenance, administration, and management of the protected area and
44   implementation of duly approved projects of the VIPPS-MB.

                                                20
 1
 2          Grants, donations, and endowments from various sources, domestic or foreign,
 3   shall be deposited in full in a special account in the National Treasury to be used for
 4   the purpose specified in the deeds and instruments covering them.
 5
 6          Voluntary or legislated payments for ecosystem goods and services, including
 7   fines, penalties, and compensation for damages from protected area offenses, shall
 8   accrue fully to the PA-RIA and shall be managed by the VIPPS-MB.
 9
10          The remaining twenty-five percent (25%) of revenues shall be deposited as a
11   special account in the General Fund in the National Treasury for purposes of financing
12   the projects of the System.
13
14          The use of the IPAF shall be in accordance with existing accounting, budgeting,
15   and auditing rules and regulations: Provided, further, That the IPAF shall not be used
16   to cover personal services expenditures.

 5   shall appropriate such funds as may be necessary for its continued implementation in
 6   the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA).
 7
 8           SEC. 23. Interpretation and Suppletory Application of Existing Laws. - The
 9   provisions of this Act shall be construed liberally in favor of achieving biodiversity
10   conservation, protection and sustainable development. Provisions of the ENIPAS Act
11   of 2018, Philippine Fisheries Code as amended, the Wildlife Resources Conservation
12   and Protection Act, and existing environmental laws and their corresponding rules and
13   regulations not inconsistent hereto shall have suppletory effect in the implementation
14   of this Act.
15
16          SEC. 24. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
17   unconstitutional or invalid, other parts or provisions hereof not affected thereby shall
18   continue to be in full force and effect.
19
20          SEC. 25. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules, and
21   regulations or parts thereof which are contrary to or inconsistent with this Act are
22   hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.
23
24          SEC. 26. Effectivity Clause. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
25   complete publication in the Official Gazette or in two (2) newspapers of general
26   circulation.
