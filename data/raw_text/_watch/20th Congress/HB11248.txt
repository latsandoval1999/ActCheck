                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

Mental health is an integral component of overall health and well-being and has become an
increasingly pressing concern among young people, particularly students in higher education. College
and university students face academic, financial, social, and personal pressures during a stage of life
when mental health conditions may first emerge or become more pronounced.

The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that one in seven persons aged ten (10) to nineteen
(19) years experiences a mental disorder, with depression and anxiety among the leading causes of
illness in this age group. Suicide is likewise the third leading cause of death globally among persons
aged fifteen (15) to twenty-nine (29) years, an age group that includes a significant portion of the
college and university population.

In the Philippines, the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported a suicide mortality rate of 2.7
deaths per 100,000 population in 2024. At the same time, access to mental health services remains
limited. The Department of Health’s National Objectives for Health 2023–2028 reports only seventy-
five (75) mental health facilities nationwide and three hundred sixty-two (362) Mental Health
Management and Assistance Program (MH-MAP) access sites, which the government seeks to expand
to five hundred fifteen (515).

The WHO Mental Health Atlas 2024 Country Profile for the Philippines further reflects gaps in
available human resources, reporting only 3.9 mental health workers per 100,000 population,
including 0.2 psychiatrists and 0.3 psychologists per 100,000 population. The same profile also
reported no functioning school-based mental health program under WHO reporting criteria,
underscoring the need to strengthen accessible mental health services in educational settings.

This need is particularly relevant to State Universities and Colleges (SUCs). Data from the
Commission on Higher Education (CHED) show that, as of June 26, 2025, there were one hundred
thirteen (113) SUC main campuses and four hundred forty-two (442) satellite campuses nationwide.
Their broad reach places SUCs in a strategic position to provide mental health promotion, early
intervention, counseling, crisis response, and referral services to students, faculty, and personnel.

Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise known as the “Mental Health Act,” established the national policy
for the promotion and protection of mental health and the delivery of integrated mental health services.
Consistent with this policy and the Philippine Council for Mental Health Strategic Framework 2024–
2028, there is a need to strengthen institutional mechanisms for mental health services within SUCs.

Thus, this bill seeks to institutionalize Mental Health Offices in SUC campuses and ensure the hiring,
training, deployment, and professional development of qualified mental health personnel. Through
accessible campus-based services, appropriate referral mechanisms, suicide prevention programs, and
timely crisis intervention, SUCs can better support the mental health and well-being of their academic
communities.

During the 19th Congress, a similar measure, which this representation co-authored, was approved on
Third Reading by the House of Representatives.

In light of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

                             AN ACT
  STRENGTHENING THE MENTAL HEALTH SERVICES OF STATE UNIVERSITIES
         AND COLLEGES AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “State Universities and Colleges
(SUCs) Mental Health Service Act”.

     SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to uphold the basic
right of every Filipino to sound mental health and ensure access to appropriate interventions, therapy,
and treatment as needed. Pursuant to this mandate, the State shall institute policies to promote the
mental health and wellness of students, faculty, teaching and non-teaching staff, and other personnel
in the campuses of SUCs through, among others, the establishment of a Mental Health Office in all
SUCs, and the hiring, deployment, and training of additional SUCs-based mental health service
personnel.

    All SUCs shall ensure that proper and adequate mental health services are provided to their
students, faculty, non-teaching staff and other personnel. In this regard, all SUCs, through their Mental
Health Offices, shall design programs and provide services that shall assist students in resolving
emotional, behavioral, and psychosocial issues that are barriers to learning and enhance the
preparedness of the faculty, teaching, non-teaching staff, and other personnel to foster an enabling
environment for students to acquire knowledge and to develop intellectually.

    Mental health services under this Act shall respect the dignity, privacy, confidentiality, and rights
of persons availing of such services, consistent with other applicable laws.

    SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

       a) Guidance counselor refers to an individual who possesses a license or certification granted
    by the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) and holds at the minimum a master’s degree
    in Guidance and Counseling, Guidance, or Counseling, without prejudice to those registered under
    the grandfather’s clause of Republic Act No. 9258, otherwise known as the “Guidance and
    Counseling Act of 2004”, and is competent to perform guidance and counseling, career guidance
    and implement mental health programs;

       b) Mental health refers to a state of well-being in which the individual realizes one’s own
    abilities and potentials, copes adequately with the normal stresses of life, displays resilience in the
    face of extreme life events, works productively and fruitfully, and is able to make a positive
    contribution to the community;

       c) Mental health professional refers to a medical doctor, nurse, guidance counselor,
    psychologist, or any other appropriately-trained and qualified person with specific skills relevant
    to, and necessary for, the provision of mental health services;

       d) Mental health services refer to the psychosocial, psychiatric, or neurologic activities and
    programs along the whole range of the mental health support services including promotion,
    prevention, treatment, and aftercare, which are provided by mental health facilities and mental
    health professionals;

       e) Psychologist refers to a natural person who has professional training and clinical skills in
    psychotherapeutic intervention and assessment for mental health problems and possesses a license
    or certification granted by the PRC and holds at the minimum a master’s degree in Psychology
    without prejudice to those granted licenses under the grandfather’s clause of Republic Act No.
    10029, otherwise known as the “Philippine Psychology Act of 2009”;

       f) SUCs-based mental health service personnel refer to any mental health professional who is
    hired or deployed by, or has a contract with an SUC to render mental health services in its campus.

    SEC. 4. Establishment of Mental Health Offices in All SUCs Campuses. – The Commission on
Higher Education (CHED) shall, subject to the rules and regulations of the Civil Service Commission
(CSC) and the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and the policies, standards, and
guidelines of the CHED, require all SUCs to establish a Mental Health Office in all their campuses.

The Mental Health Offices shall set up campus hotlines with dedicated and trained guidance
counselors to provide assistance to the whole SUC Community, especially the students. Special
attention shall be given to those identified to have mental health problems or conditions, especially
those at risk of committing suicide.

The CHED and the SUCs, through their Mental Health Offices, shall initiate and sustain a heightened
campaign to raise the collective consciousness about mental health. The awareness campaign shall
give special emphasis to the issues of suicide prevention, stress handling, mental health and nutrition,
and guidance and counseling. The campaign shall ensure that the whole SUC community, especially
the students, is aware of in-campus mental health services.

The SUCs, through the Mental Health Offices, shall implement mental health programs and projects
designed to promote the mental health and well-being of faculty, students, and personnel alike.

Each Mental Health Office shall be manned by trained mental health professionals who shall serve as
the mental health service personnel of the SUCs.

The SUCs-based mental health service personnel of the Mental Health Offices, including those who
shall be hired upon the effectivity of this Act, shall create a mechanism for timely crisis intervention
and suicide prevention and the appropriate response strategies for the whole SUCs community,
especially the students.

The CHED shall, in coordination with the SUCs, establish mechanisms to maximize existing services
offered by other executive departments such as the attached agencies to the Department of Health
(DOH) dealing with mental health and the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD).

    SEC. 5. Hiring, Training and Deployment of Mental Health Professionals. – Subject to the
guidelines set by the CHED and the SUCs, CSC and DBM, the SUCs shall hire, train and deploy
mental health professionals, including a psychologist, to the Mental Health Office to ensure that each
SUC shall have mental health professionals or mental health service providers within five (5) years
from the effectivity of this Act.

The SUCs shall, in coordination with the CHED, prepare the appropriate plantilla positions to be
submitted to the DBM for evaluation and approval for the additional SUC-based mental health service
personnel to be hired under this Act, subject to the existing organizational, staffing, position
classification and compensation policies, rules, and regulations.

     Notwithstanding the provisions of Republic Act No. 9258, otherwise known as the “Guidance
and Counseling Act of 2004”, SUCs may hire, as may be necessary, college graduates who do not yet
qualify as mental health professionals but who possess a background in psychology or similar fields
to provide mental health services: Provided, That the CHED shall provide for the appropriate training
and instruction of such college graduates in order for them to gain and develop the necessary skills to
render counselling services to faculty, students and school personnel, and assist them in managing
mental health issues: Provided, further, That such services shall be rendered under the supervision of
qualified mental health professionals within the schools division: Provided, finally, That a college
graduate hired to work in a Mental Health Office shall, within three (3) years from the date of
employment in the SUC, secure the appropriate license to practice as a mental health professional and
meet the relevant qualification requirements of their position.

The CHED and the SUCs, through the Mental Health Offices, shall formulate a scheme for the
continuous training of mental health service personnel, taking into consideration the latest scientific
studies, psycho-social approaches and know-how on mental health and mental health services, in
accordance with the provisions of Republic Act No. 10912, otherwise known as the “Continuing
Professional Development Act of 2016”.

     SEC. 6. Professional and Career Development of Mental Health Professionals. – The CHED
shall, in coordination with the DBM, CSC, DOH and the PRC:

      a) Prepare a uniform career and personnel development plan that shall include provisions on
    personal wellness, merit promotion, performance evaluation, in-service training, graduate
    scholarships, research grants, and incentive award system;

      b) Match the salary grades of mental health professionals with related plantilla items under the
    provisions of Republic Act No. 6758, otherwise known as the “Compensation and Position
    Classification Act of 1989,” as amended;

      c) Align the qualifications and standards for the qualification outcomes of Mental Health
    Professionals to the provisions of Republic Act No. 10968, otherwise known as the “Philippine
    Qualifications Framework Act”;

      d) Revisit the organizational structure of the SUCs to provide competent and professional
    personnel who shall provide supervision and technical assistance to the guidance counselors;

      e) Review, revise and develop the duties and responsibilities of mental health professionals in
    accordance with laws governing their professions;

       f) Prepare a performance evaluation plan that considers the improvement of the efficiency and
    organizational effectiveness of each counselor: Provided, That each employee shall be informed
    regularly by their immediate supervisor of the evaluation of their performance; and
      g) Provide periodic training, aimed at retooling and upgrading the skills of mental health
    professionals.

     SEC. 7. Salary Scale. – The career and salary scales of mental health professionals in SUCs shall
provide for a gradual progression from a minimum to a maximum salary by means of regular
increments, to be given regularly based on merit and competencies.

The entry level of mental health professionals shall not be lower than Salary Grade 16. Mental health
professionals hired by SUCs as Psychometricians shall be hired at Salary Grade 11 at the entry level.

    SEC. 8. Contractual Hiring of Mental Health Personnel. – In case of a scarcity of mental health
professionals who are available to work on a full-time basis in a Mental Health Office, the concerned
SUC may hire qualified professionals on a contractual or part-time basis, subject to the evaluation and
approval of the DBM.

    SEC. 9. Benefits. – All mental health professionals employed in the various Mental Health
Offices shall enjoy the rights, privileges, and other benefits provided in Republic Act No. 7305,
otherwise known as the “Magna Carta of Public Health Workers”, Republic Act No. 11036, otherwise
known as the “Mental Health Act”, and other pertinent laws.

     SEC. 10. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act shall be
included in the Annual General Appropriations Act.

     SEC. 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of
this Act, the CHED shall, in coordination with the DOH, DBM, CSC, PRC, the Philippine Association
of State Universities and Colleges, and other concerned stakeholders, issue rules and regulations to
govern the hiring, training and deployment of guidance counselors, psychologists and other mental
health service personnel in SUCs, and for the implementation of this Act.

     SEC. 12. Suppletory Application. – The applicable provisions of Republic Act No. 11036,
otherwise known as the “Mental Health Act”, insofar as they are not inconsistent with the provisions
of this Act, shall apply suppletorily.

    SEC. 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional, the
remainder of this Act or any provision not affected, shall remain in full force and effect.

     SEC. 14. Repealing Clause. – Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive order, letter of
instruction, administrative order, rule or regulation contrary to or inconsistent with the provisions of
this Act is repealed, modified or amended accordingly.

    SEC. 15. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
