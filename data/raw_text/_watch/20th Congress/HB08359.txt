16                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE
17
18           Recent escalation of armed conflict in the Middle East has created uncertainty in
19   global energy markets and caused increases in international crude oil prices. As a country
20   that relies heavily on imported petroleum, the Philippines is particularly vulnerable to these
21   external shocks. Rising fuel prices quickly translate into higher transportation costs,
22   increased electricity generation expenses, and higher prices of basic goods, placing a
23   significant burden on Filipino consumers and businesses.
24
25           Under the current framework established by Republic Act No. 10963 (Tax Reform
26   for Acceleration and Inclusion Law), excise taxes on petroleum products are fixed under
27   Section 148 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997. While these taxes are an
28   important source of government revenue, the law does not provide sufficient flexibility for
29   the government to respond quickly to extraordinary global events that sharply drive up fuel
30   prices.
31
32           This bill seeks to grant the President limited and temporary authority to suspend or
33   reduce excise taxes on petroleum products during periods of extraordinary increases in oil
34   prices caused by global emergencies, such as the ongoing conflict in the Middle East. The
35   authority may only be exercised under clearly defined conditions, including when
36   international crude oil prices exceed a specified threshold or when global events cause
37   significant and sustained increases in domestic fuel prices.
38
39          To ensure accountability and fiscal responsibility, the proposed measure includes
40   safeguards such as prior certification from the Department of Finance, a strict six-month
41   limit on the suspension or reduction, and mandatory reporting to Congress. Excise tax rates
42   will automatically revert to their original levels once the authorized period expires.
43
44          By providing a flexible but carefully regulated policy tool, this measure aims to
45   cushion Filipino consumers and the national economy from the immediate impact of sharp
46   petroleum price increases during global crises.
47
48         In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.

101       AN ACT GRANTING THE PRESIDENT LIMITED AUTHORITY TO
102    TEMPORARILY SUSPEND OR REDUCE EXCISE TAXES ON PETROLEUM
103   PRODUCTS DURING NATIONAL OR GLOBAL EMERGENCIES, AMENDING
104   FOR THE PURPOSE SECTION 148 OF THE NATIONAL INTERNAL REVENUE
105    CODE OF 1997, AS AMENDED BY REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10963 (TRAIN LAW)
106
107   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
108   assembled:
109
110   SECTION 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the “Emergency Petroleum Excise
111   Tax Adjustment Act.”
112
113   SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State to protect
114   consumers and ensure economic stability during periods of extraordinary increases in
115   petroleum prices while preserving the fiscal integrity of the government.
116   Towards this end, Congress may authorize limited and conditional adjustments to excise
117   taxes on petroleum products during extraordinary circumstances, subject to clear standards,
118   safeguards, and congressional oversight.
119
120   SECTION 3. Amendment to Section 148 under Chapter V of the National Internal Revenue
121   Code, as amended. Section 148 of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended
122   by Republic Act No. 10963, is hereby further amended by inserting a new subsection to
123   read as follows:
124
125   “SECTION 148-B. Temporary Adjustment of Excise Taxes on Petroleum Products During
126   Emergencies.
127   (a) Authority. — Consistent with the policy of protecting consumers and maintaining
128   economic stability during extraordinary increases in petroleum prices, and
129   notwithstanding the excise tax rates imposed under Section 148 of this Code, the President
130   of the Philippines, upon the recommendation of the Secretary of Finance and in
131   consultation with the Secretaries of Energy and Trade and Industry, may temporarily
132   suspend or reduce the excise taxes on petroleum products, subject to the limitations
133   provided in this Section.
134   (b) Standards. — The authority under this Section may be exercised only when any of the
135   following conditions exists:
136       1. The average international price of crude oil based on the Dubai crude benchmark
137           exceeds Eighty United States Dollars (USD 80) per barrel for three (3) consecutive
138           months;
139       2. A state of national emergency or calamity affecting energy supply or economic
140           stability has been declared; or
141       3. Extraordinary global events, including armed conflict or major disruptions in
142           petroleum supply, result in significant and sustained increases in domestic fuel
143           prices.
144   (c) Scope and Duration. — The President may reduce the excise tax imposed under Section
145   148 to any level, including zero (0), for the petroleum products directly affected, for a
146   period not exceeding six (6) months, unless extended by Congress.
147   (d) Fiscal Safeguard. — Prior to the issuance of an executive order under this Section, the
148   Department of Finance shall certify the fiscal impact of the proposed adjustment and
149   ensure consistency with the national government’s fiscal program, unless otherwise
150   authorized by Congress.
151   (e) Congressional Oversight. — Within fifteen (15) days from the issuance of the executive
152   order, the President shall submit a report to Congress stating the basis for the adjustment,
153   the petroleum products covered, the revised tax rates, and the estimated revenue impact.
154   Congress may terminate or modify the adjustment through a joint resolution.
155   (f) Reversion of Rates. — Upon expiration of the authorized period, the excise tax rates
156   under Section 148 of this Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 10963, shall automatically
157   revert to their original levels.”
158
159   SECTION 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations. Within thirty (30) days from the
160   effectivity of this Act, the Department of Finance, the Bureau of Internal Revenue, and the
161   Department of Energy shall promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for the
162   implementation of this Act.
163
164   SECTION 5. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional
165   or invalid, the remaining provisions shall not be affected thereby.
166
167   SECTION 6. Repealing Clause. All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and regulations,
168   or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
169
170   SECTION 7. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in the
171   Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
