                                 EXPLANATORY NOTE


The Constitution affirms the State’s duty to afford full protection to labor and to ensure just and
humane conditions of work, including the right of workers to receive a fair and living wage.
Central to this guarantee is the ability of workers to know, verify, and, when necessary, contest
how their wages are computed and paid. Without clear and accessible documentation of
earnings and deductions, these rights are rendered difficult to enforce in practice.

Under the Labor Code of the Philippines and related issuances of the Department of Labor and
Employment, employers are required to pay wages properly and ensure transparency in
deductions. However, the obligation to issue itemized pay statements or payslips is not
explicitly provided in the statute itself, but instead inferred from implementing rules and
reinforced through administrative advisories. This gap in statutory clarity has resulted in
uneven compliance across industries and employment arrangements, particularly in
subcontracted, small and medium enterprise, and non-standard or emerging forms of work.

In practice, many workers do not regularly receive payslips. This creates a serious enforcement
gap. Without documentary proof of wages received, workers are placed at a significant
disadvantage in asserting claims for underpayment, non-payment of statutory benefits, or
illegal deductions. Existing enforcement mechanisms, which are largely complaint-driven and
inspection-dependent, often require workers to substantiate claims without access to basic

payroll records. As a result, wage-related violations, including wage theft and disguised
deductions, may persist with limited accountability.

This bill addresses these gaps by making the issuance of itemized payslips an explicit statutory
obligation and, more importantly, by positioning payslips as a core enforcement and
transparency mechanism in labor standards compliance.

The measure strengthens enforcement by integrating payslip compliance into the visitorial and
enforcement powers of the Department of Labor and Employment under Article 128 of the
Labor Code. It ensures that compliance is subject to routine and complaint-based inspections
and treated as a labor standards violation when not observed.

To address evidentiary barriers faced by workers, the bill establishes a rebuttable presumption
in favor of the employee in cases where the employer fails to issue payslips, thereby aligning
legal procedure with the realities of workplace documentation and strengthening workers’
ability to assert their claims.

Recognizing the recurring nature of wage opacity, the bill imposes graduated administrative
penalties per affected employee and per pay period, ensuring that non-compliance is not treated
as a one-time infraction but as a continuing violation warranting proportionate sanctions. This
deterrence framework is designed to promote sustained compliance rather than episodic
correction.

The measure likewise addresses enforcement gaps in contracting and subcontracting
arrangements by imposing joint and solidary liability, ensuring that wage transparency
obligations cannot be evaded through layered employment structures. It also clarifies coverage
of non-standard work arrangements, without altering existing legal tests for determining
employer-employee relationships.

At the same time, the bill adopts a practical and modern compliance framework by allowing the
issuance of electronic payslips, subject to accessibility, verifiability, and record-keeping
requirements. This ensures that the measure remains feasible for employers, including small
enterprises, while safeguarding workers’ access to their wage records.

In sum, this measure does not merely restate existing policy. It closes a clear statutory gap,
strengthens enforcement through integration with existing powers, introduces evidentiary tools
that make labor rights actionable, and establishes a proportionate deterrence system against
wage opacity and abuse.

                                            AN ACT

 STRENGTHENING WAGE TRANSPARENCY AND ENFORCEMENT THROUGH
    THE MANDATORY ISSUANCE OF ITEMIZED PAYSLIPS, PROVIDING FOR
           ENFORCEMENT MECHANISMS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES




 Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title.

This Act shall be known as the “Wage Transparency and Payslip Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy.

It is hereby declared the policy of the State to afford full protection to labor, ensure just and
humane conditions of work, and guarantee the right of workers to receive fair, timely, and
transparent compensation.

Toward this end, the State shall promote wage transparency and accountability by requiring
clear documentation of wage payments, strengthening enforcement mechanisms against
wage-related violations, and ensuring that workers are able to effectively verify and assert their
rights.



                                                                                           Page 4 of 8
SEC. 3. Coverage.

This Act shall apply to all employers in the private sector, regardless of size, industry, or
employment arrangement, including but not limited to:

(a) Regular, probationary, casual, project-based, seasonal, and fixed-term employees;​
(b) Workers engaged through contracting or subcontracting arrangements;​
 (c) Workers in non-standard or emerging work arrangements, including platform-based or
digital work arrangements where an employer-employee relationship exists as determined
under existing laws and jurisprudence; and​
(d) Such other categories of workers as may be determined by the Department of Labor and
Employment consistent with existing laws.

SEC. 4. Mandatory Issuance of Payslips.

All employers shall issue to every employee an accurate, itemized payslip for every pay period,
at the time of wage payment or not later than three (3) days thereafter.

The payslip may be issued in printed or electronic form, provided that it is accessible,
downloadable, and capable of being stored or reproduced by the employee.

SEC. 5. Minimum Contents of Payslip.

The payslip shall, at a minimum, contain the following information:

(a) Name of the employer and registered business name;​
 (b) Employer identification details, including address and registration number, where
applicable;​
(c) Name of the employee and employee identification number, if any;​
(d) Covered pay period and date of payment;​
(e) Basic wage or salary rate;​
(f) Total number of hours or days worked, including overtime, night shift differential, holiday,
and rest day work, where applicable;​
(g) Gross pay for the period;​
(h) Itemized deductions, including but not limited to taxes and mandatory contributions such
as SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG;​
(i) Other authorized deductions;​
(j) Net pay received; and​
(k) Such other information as may be prescribed by the Secretary of Labor and Employment.



                                                                                         Page 5 of 8
SEC. 6. Applicability to Micro and Small Enterprises
Specific to micro and small enterprises (MSEs), the Department of Labor and Employment shall
prescribe core compliance standards for the implementation of this Act, taking into account
enterprise size, operational capacity, and available resources, including simplified formats or
systems for payslip issuance.​
​
Such standards shall ensure that the essential requirements of wage transparency, particularly
the accurate and timely disclosure of earnings and deductions, are maintained.​
​
Provided, That compliance with such standards shall be mandatory and shall not be construed
as exempting MSEs from any obligations or liability under this Act: Provided further, That
nothing in this section shall be interpreted to permit the non-issuance of payslips.


SEC. 7 Record-Keeping and Accessibility.

Employers shall maintain copies of all payslips, whether in physical or electronic form, for a
period of at least three (3) years from issuance.

Employees shall have the right to request and be provided copies of their payslips at no cost.

The Department of Labor and Employment shall ensure that systems for electronic payslip
issuance comply with data privacy, accessibility, and verification standards.

SEC. 8. Evidentiary Presumption.

In any administrative or judicial proceeding involving wage-related claims, the failure of the
employer to issue payslips as required under this Act shall give rise to a rebuttable presumption
that the employee’s claims as to wages, hours worked, and deductions are correct.

Such presumption may be overturned only upon presentation of substantial evidence consisting
of contemporaneous payroll records or other credible documentation.

SEC. 9. Joint and Solidary Liability.

In cases of contracting or subcontracting arrangements, the principal and the contractor or
subcontractor shall be jointly and solidarily liable for compliance with the provisions of this Act,
without prejudice to existing laws on labor-only contracting and employer liability.

SEC. 10. Prohibited Acts.


                                                                                           Page 6 of 8
It shall be unlawful for any employer to:

(a) Fail or refuse to issue payslips as required under this Act;​
(b) Issue payslips containing false, misleading, or incomplete information;​
(c) Deny employees access to their payslips;​
(d) Retaliate, dismiss, suspend, or otherwise prejudice an employee for asserting rights under
this Act or for reporting violations; or​
 (e) Commit any analogous act that undermines wage transparency and the rights protected
under this Act.

Acts under paragraph (d) shall be treated as unfair labor practice or illegal dismissal, as
applicable, under existing laws.

SEC. 11. Enforcement and Visitorial Powers.

The Secretary of Labor and Employment or duly authorized representatives shall enforce the
provisions of this Act pursuant to the visitorial and enforcement powers under Article 128 of
the Labor Code of the Philippines.

Compliance with this Act shall form part of routine labor inspections, including the Labor Law
Compliance System (LLCS), and shall be subject to both routine and complaint-based
inspections.

SEC. 12. Administrative Penalties.

Any employer who violates the provisions of this Act shall be subject to administrative fines to
be imposed by the Secretary of Labor and Employment, as follows:

(a) First offense – Not less than Five thousand pesos (₱5,000.00) but not more than Twenty
thousand pesos (₱20,000.00) per affected employee;

(b) Second offense – Not less than Twenty thousand pesos (₱20,000.00) but not more than Fifty
thousand pesos (₱50,000.00) per affected employee;

(c) Third and subsequent offenses – Not less than Fifty thousand pesos (₱50,000.00) but not
more than One hundred thousand pesos (₱100,000.00) per affected employee, and may include
the issuance of compliance orders, work stoppage orders in extreme cases, or other sanctions
authorized under existing labor laws.

Each pay period in which a violation occurs shall constitute a separate offense.

The imposition of penalties under this Act shall be without prejudice to the exercise of powers
under Article 128 of the Labor Code of the Philippines and other applicable laws.

SEC. 13. Non-Diminution of Benefits.

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish or reduce existing rights and benefits granted
under current laws, rules, regulations, or collective bargaining agreements.

SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations.

Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Labor and
Employment, in consultation with relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary rules
and regulations for its effective implementation.

Such rules shall ensure integration with existing labor inspection systems, including the Labor
Law Compliance System (LLCS), and alignment with data privacy, accessibility, and
verification standards.

SEC. 15. Separability Clause.

If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not
affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 16. Repealing Clause.

All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed
or modified accordingly.

SEC. 17. Effectivity.

This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a
newspaper of general circulation.
