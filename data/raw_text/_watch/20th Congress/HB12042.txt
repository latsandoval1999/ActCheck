AN ACT MANDATING A GUARANTEED BUDGET ALLOCATION FOR THE
LOCAL YOUTH DEVELOPMENT OFFICE, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
SECTIONS 26 AND 27 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10742, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
THE SANGGUNIANG KABATAAN REFORM ACT OF 2015, AS AMENDED BY
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11768, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

EXPLANATORY NOTE

The youth represent not just the future of the Republic, but its most dynamic
present demographic. Republic Act No. 8044 or the Youth in Nation-Building Act,
defined the age-bracket of the Filipino youth from 15 to 30 years old.' In the 2024
Census of Population (POPCEN) showed that the household population of the
Philippines has already reached 112.33 million. The youth hold the largest share of
the populace with Filipinos aged 15-19 at 10.6 million, ages 20-24 at 10.3 million, and
ages 25-29 at 9.5 million.2 In Quezon Province alone, 59 percent of its 1.94 million
residents belong to this critical age bracket. Translating this demographic dividend into
genuine national progress requires robust, properly funded institutional mechanisms
at the grassroots level.

While Republic Act No. 10742, or the Sangguniang Kabataan Reform Act of
2015, as amended established the Local Youth Development Office (LYDO) to serve
as the administrative nerve center for local youth advancement, its implementation
has been severely hampered by fiscal ambiguities. Under the current legal framework,
the allocation of funds for the LYDO is largely discretionary. Consequently, numerous
provinces, cities, and municipalities operate their respective LYDOs at a highly limited
capacity, relegating youth development to a secondary priority subject to the changing
political will of local administrations.

Unlike the Sangguniang Kabataan at the barangay level—which enjoys a direct
10% appropriation from the barangay general fund—the SK Federations and LYDOs
at the municipal, city, and provincial levels lack a guaranteed statutory funding stream
to faithfully execute their Local Youth Development Plans (LYDP).

This bill, Known as the Youth Empowerment Support (YES) Act, cures this
institutional problem by mandating a guaranteed budget allocation of at least one
percent (1%) but not more than two percent (2%) of the Local Government Units
Annual Budget for the LYDO. To balance this mandate with local fiscal autonomy and
the limitations set by the Local Government Code of 1991, the bill applies a tiered
approach: ensuring that only 1st, 2nd, and 3rd class provinces and municipalities, and
highly urbanized cities and component cities are required to create a separate LYDO
department, while permitting 4th, 5th, and 6th class provinces and municipalities to
designate existing personnel to avoid breaching their Personal Services (PS) caps.

By ensuring that youth empowerment programs are backed by mandatory
statutory funding, the State transforms its constitutional duty to the youth from mere
rhetoric into actionable, sustained governance.

In view of the foregoing, the approval of this bill is most ardently sought.

AN ACT MANDATING A GUARANTEED BUDGET ALLOCATION FOR THE
LOCAL YOUTH DEVELOPMENT OFFICE, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
SECTIONS 26 AND 27 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10742, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS
THE SANGGUNIANG KABATAAN REFORM ACT OF 2015, AS AMENDED BY
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11768, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the
Philippines in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "Youth
Empowerment Support (YES) Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — \t is the declared policy of the State to
ensure that youth empowerment and local youth development programs are backed
by guaranteed and mandatory statutory funding. The State shall mandate
proportionate resource allocation by local government units to fully professionalize and
sustain the Local Youth Development Offices (LYDO), without compromising local
fiscal autonomy.

SECTION 3. Section 26 of Republic Act No. 10742, as amended by Republic
Act No. 11768, is hereby further amended to read as follows:

“SECTION 26. Creation. — There shall be in every province, city,
and municipality a Youth Development Office which shall be headed by
a youth development officer with the rank of at least division chief. Such
may be put under the Office of the Local Chief Executive, the Office of
the Planning and Development, the Office of the Social Welfare, or in
any other office deemed appropriate by the local government unit. If the
funds of the local government unit are sufficient, it can be a separate

department with divisions and units for policy and _ planning,
administration and finance, and programs and operations: PROVIDED,
THAT IN FIRST-, SECOND-, AND THIRD-CLASS PROVINCES AND
MUNICIPALITIES, AND HIGHLY URBANIZED AND COMPONENT
CITIES, THE LOCAL YOUTH DEVELOPMENT OFFICE SHALL BE

personnel who she or he deems fit to serve this purpose until such time
that the local government unit can already create this office UNTIL
SUCH TIME THAT THE LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNIT CAN ALREADY
CREATE THIS OFFICE: Provided, That a local youth development
officer shall be appointed within one (1) year from the effectivity of this
Act.”

SECTION 4. Section 27 of Republic Act No. 10742, as amended by Republic
Act No. 11768, is hereby further amended to read as follows:

“SECTION 27. Funding. - The local government unit shall
appropriate AT LEAST ONE PERCENT (1%) BUT NOT MORE THAN
TWO PERCENT (2%) OF ITS ANNUAL BUDGET FOR THE
OPERATIONS AND EFFECTIVE FUNCTIONING OF THE Local Youth
Development Office. THIS MANDATORY APPROPRIATION SHALL
BE CHARGED DIRECTLY AGAINST THE GENERAL FUND OF THE
LOCAL GOVERNMENT UNIT.”

SECTION 5. Compliance and Penal Provisions. Any local chief executive or
responsible local government official who willfully fails or refuses to appropriate the
mandated funding under Section 4 of this Act shall be held liable for Dereliction of Duty
under Title Seven, Chapter Two of the Revised Penal Code, and shall be subject to
administrative sanctions under Republic Act no. 7160, without prejudice to other
liabilities under existing laws.

SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG)
and the National Youth Commission (NYC), in direct consultation with the Department
of Budget and Management (DBM), shall promulgate the necessary rules and
regulations for the effective implementation of this Act.

SECTION 7. Separability Clause. — \f any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue
in full force and effect.

SECTION 8. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, administrative
orders, rules, and regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
repealed or modified accordingly.
