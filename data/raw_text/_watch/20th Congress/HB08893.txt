                                       EXPLANATORY NOTE

           The 1987 Philippine Constitution mandates the State to protect and promote
   the right to health of the people (Article II, Section 15) and to undertake appropriate
   health human resource development responsive to the country’s needs and problems
   (Article XIII, Section 12).

          Respiratory therapists play a critical role in the health care system, particularly
   in the management of patients with acute and chronic cardiopulmonary conditions.
   They are responsible for operating life-support equipment such as mechanical
   ventilators, and for performing high-risk procedures, including airway management
   and arterial blood gas analysis. Their specialized expertise is indispensable in intensive
   care units, emergency settings, and neonatal care, where timely and precise
   intervention is often lifesaving.

          Despite the essential nature of their work, respiratory therapists in government
   service remain comparatively disadvantaged in terms of compensation, relative to the
   technical demands, risks, and responsibilities of their profession. Many are deployed
   in high-pressure clinical environments, yet their current salary grade does not
   adequately reflect the critical services they provide.

          At the same time, the increasing global demand for respiratory therapists has
   contributed to the continued outmigration of skilled Filipino health care professionals
   seeking better compensation and working conditions abroad. This trend poses a
   significant challenge to the country’s ability to maintain a competent and sufficient
   healthcare workforce, particularly in public health institutions.

          This measure seeks to upgrade the minimum entry-level salary grade of
   government respiratory therapists from Salary Grade 10 to Salary Grade 15. The
   proposed adjustment aims to promote equitable compensation, enhance workforce
   retention, and align their remuneration with similarly situated healthcare
   professionals.

          Ultimately, this measure affirms the State’s commitment to uphold decent work
   standards in the public health sector and to strengthen the delivery of accessible,
   timely, and quality healthcare services for all Filipinos.

         In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly
   sought.

                                “AN ACT
            UPGRADING THE MINIMUM SALARY GRADE OF
            GOVERNMENT    RESPIRATORY   THERAPISTS FROM
            SALARY GRADE 10 TO SALARY GRADE 15”

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      SECTION 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Government
Respiratory Therapists’ Salary Upgrading Act of 2026.”

      SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is hereby declared the policy of the
State to ensure adequate and just compensation for government respiratory
therapists in order to attract and retain competent professionals in the public health
sector, consistent with the State’s mandate to provide accessible and quality
healthcare services to all Filipinos.

      SECTION 3. Upgrading the Government Respiratory Therapists’
Minimum Salary Grade Level. - The minimum salary grade of government
respiratory therapists occupying entry-level positions (Respiratory Therapist I) is
hereby upgraded from Salary Grade 10 to Salary Grade 15.

       The salary grades of respiratory therapists occupying higher positions shall
be correspondingly adjusted in accordance with existing compensation and position
classification systems.

      SECTION 4. Coverage. - This Act shall cover all respiratory therapists
employed in public health facilities and other health-related institutions owned and
operated by the national government, local government units, and other government
instrumentalities.

     SECTION 5. Appropriations. - The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current year’s available
appropriations of the Department of Health (DOH) and other concerned agencies.

       Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary for its continued implementation
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
      SECTION 6. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within ninety (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Health (DOH), in coordination with
the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and the Civil Service Commission
(CSC), shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for its effective
implementation.

     SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules
and regulations, or parts thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are
hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

       SECTION 8. Separability Clause. - If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
full force and effect.

      SECTION 9. Effectivity. - This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
