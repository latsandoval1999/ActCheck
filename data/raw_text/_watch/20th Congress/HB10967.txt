                                     Explanatory Note

        The Philippine energy sector is principally governed by Republic Act No. 9136,
otherwise known as the Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001 (EPIRA), and Republic
Act No. 9513, otherwise known as the Renewable Energy Act of 2008 (RE Act). EPIRA seeks
to ensure reliable, secure, and affordable electricity while promoting private sector
participation and the utilization of indigenous and renewable energy resources. Likewise, the
RE Act establishes a framework for accelerating the development and use of renewable energy
sources to achieve greater energy self-reliance and sustainability. Over the past years, the
Department of Energy (DOE), the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC), and other
governmental agencies have implemented various programs to encourage renewable energy
development intended to expand the country's renewable energy capacity. These policies reflect
the State's commitment to enhancing energy security, improving electricity affordability, and
increasing the share of renewable energy in the national energy mix.

        Despite these advancements and programs, Qualified End-users remain predominantly
dependent on centralized generation and distribution systems. While existing laws recognize
the value of renewable energy, they do not provide a comprehensive framework specifically
designed to empower ordinary Filipino households and local communities to become active
participants in electricity generation for their own consumption through distributed renewable
energy systems. Ordinary Filipinos continue to face fragmented permitting requirements,
inconsistent local government processes, limited access to affordable financing, insufficient
technical support, lengthy certification procedures, administrative delays, and other
environmental attributes. As a result, many Qualified End-users remain unable to fully realize

                                              1
the benefits of generating electricity for their own use despite the availability of increasingly
accessible renewable energy technologies.

       This Act seeks to strengthen and complement the policy objectives of EPIRA and the
RE Act by establishing a comprehensive framework that empowers Qualified End-users to
generate, consume, store, and share electricity from distributed renewable energy systems
primarily for self-consumption. Drawing strength from the core principles of our national
energy statutes, this bill introduces critical structural enhancements:

       1. Whole-of-Government Facilitation and Regulatory Streamlining: It mandates the
          Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), the Bureau of Customs (BOC), local
          government units (DILG) and other concerned agencies to establish streamlined
          procedures, Green Lane mechanisms, and standardized permitting systems with
          clear timelines and deemed-approved provisions to reduce unnecessary
          administrative burdens.

       2. True Consumer Empowerment: It scales up hosting and renewable energy (RE)
          absorption capacity targets, ensuring that distribution networks can seamlessly
          accommodate distributed generation.

       3. Improved Net-Metering Framework: It streamlines net-metering mechanisms on a
          direct kilowatt-hour (kWh) to kilowatt-hour equivalence offset, ensuring that
          Qualified End-users receive fair value for clean energy exported to the grid.

       4. Expanded Access to Financing: It activates accessible financing windows via the
          RE Trust Fund for LGUs, dedicated green loans through the SSS and GSIS, and
          specialized credit facilities from the Land Bank of the Philippines (LBP) and the
          Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP) to support the acquisition and
          installation of distributed renewable energy systems.

        By facilitating the widespread adoption of distributed renewable energy systems, this
measure is expected to expand Qualified End-user participation in the energy sector and
strengthen the country's transition toward a more resilient, decentralized, and sustainable
energy system. The proposed reforms will enable households, communities, small businesses,
and LGUs to reduce their exposure to fluctuating electricity costs, improve energy reliability,
and increase access to clean and locally generated electricity.

        Ultimately, this measure recognizes that the energy security is no longer solely the
responsibility of large power producers and utilities. It is likewise a shared responsibility and
opportunity for every Filipino household, community, and local government. By granting
Filipinos greater freedom and capacity to generate their own clean and affordable electricity,
the State promotes energy independence, consumer empowerment, economic sustainability,
and national resilience in the face of an increasingly uncertain global energy environment.

       By decentralizing power generation and placing clean energy directly into the hands of,
Qualified End-users, this bill empowers citizens and communities to lower their utility
expenses, insulates our domestic economy from external geopolitical shocks and achieves true
national energy self-sufficiency and security.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                           AN ACT
PROMOTING "SARILING KURYENTE" BY ADVOCATING THE RIGHTS OF
HOUSEHOLDS, COMMUNITIES, AND OTHER QUALIFIED END-USERS TO
GENERATE, STORE, CONSUME, AND SHARE ELECTRICITY FROM
DISTRIBUTED RENEWABLE ENERGY SOURCES PRIMARILY FOR SELF-
CONSUMPTION, PROVIDING ACCESSIBLE FINANCING MECHANISMS,
INCENTIVES, AND REGULATORY REFORMS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

      Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the "Sariling Kuryente Act of
2026".

        SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
protect and promote the right of the people to an affordable, reliable, sustainable, and secure
supply of electricity. Recognizing that exorbitant electricity rates place an undue burden on
Filipino households and industries and acknowledging the vulnerability of the national energy
supply to volatile global markets, geopolitical conflicts, and external disruptions, the State shall
actively foster energy self-reliance and security at the grassroots level.

       Towards this end, the State shall promote Qualified End-user empowerment by
encouraging the development and utilization of distributed renewable energy resources for self-
consumption, consistent with the principles of energy security, affordability, sustainability,

                                                 4
technology neutrality, competition, public safety, engineering standards, environmental
protection, and reliable operation of the electric power system. The State shall remove
unnecessary regulatory and administrative barriers and promote policies that enable Qualified
End-users to generate, store, consume, and share electricity from distributed renewable energy
resources in accordance with this Act.

        SECTION 3. Guiding Principles. — The implementation of this Act shall be guided
by the principles of: (a) Qualified End-user empowerment; (b) technology neutrality; (c) least-
cost energy transition; (d) energy security and resilience; (e) grid reliability and power quality;
(f) public safety; (g) regulatory transparency and accountability; (h) fair and non-
discriminatory access to distributed renewable energy; and (i) environmental sustainability.

       SECTION 4. Objectives. — The primary objectives of this Act are:

        (a) To Mitigate High Electricity Costs: To reduce electricity costs and improve
            Qualified End-user welfare through self-generation from renewable energy sources
            as well as encourage greater participation in the energy sector, foster competition,
            reduce pressure on the power grid, and improve the overall efficiency of electricity
            delivery and consumption.

        (b) To Achieve Consumer Energy Self-Sufficiency: To empower Qualified End-users
            to generate, store, consume and share electricity from distributed renewable energy
            resources primarily for self-consumption.

        (c) To Accelerate Renewable Energy Adoption: To accelerate the deployment of
            distributed renewable energy systems, energy storage systems and other emerging
            clean energy technologies through simplified regulatory processes, appropriate
            incentives, and financing mechanisms.

        (d) To Insulate Against External Disruptions: To strengthen national energy security
            and resilience against fuel price volatility, supply disruptions, natural disasters, and
            other emergencies by expanding distributed renewable energy resources.

        (e) To Integrate Distributed Energy Resources and Smart Grid Technologies: Promote
            the modernization of the electric power system through the integration of
            distributed energy resources, smart grid technologies, energy storage systems, and
            other enabling technologies.

        (f) To Promote Active Energy Participation: Promote active Qualified End-user
            participation in the energy transition through fair access to distributed renewable
            energy, net metering, renewable energy markets, and other lawful mechanisms.

       SECTION 5. Scope and Application. – This Act shall apply to all Qualified End-users
within the Philippines who generate or intend to generate electricity primarily for their own

                                                 5
consumption using distributed renewable energy systems, as well as other similarly situated
persons or entities as may be determined by the Department of Energy (DOE) through the
Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of this Act.

       This Act shall govern the planning, installation, ownership, operation, maintenance,
upgrading, and use of decentralized renewable energy systems primarily intended for self-
consumption, subject to the following parameters:

   (a) Eligible Technologies:
       This Act shall apply to all forms of distributed renewable energy systems, including but
       not limited to solar photovoltaic (PV), micro-wind turbines, run-of-river micro-hydro
       systems, micro-biomass, and domestic battery energy storage systems (BESS), fuel
       cells, renewable hydrogen, vehicle-to-home (V2H), vehicle-to-grid (V2G), thermal
       storage and other future RE technologies capable of producing and/or storing electricity
       for own use and consumption.

   (b) Application to the Grid:
       This Act shall apply to both off-grid (standalone) self-generation systems and grid-tied
       systems. Grid-tied systems covered under this Act shall remain eligible to participate
       in net-metering schemes and other compensation mechanisms authorized by law or
       ERC rules or any subsequent peer-to-peer energy trading, virtual net metering and
       community energy programs when authorized by law.

        Nothing herein shall be construed to authorize the generation, transmission,
distribution, supply, or sale of electricity as a public utility or regulated electricity business
without the licenses, permits, franchises, or other authorizations required under existing laws.

        SECTION 6. Rights of Qualified End-users. – Qualified End-users shall have the
right to install, own, operate, maintain, upgrade, interconnect, generate, store, consume, and,
where authorized by law, export or share electricity generated from distributed renewable
energy systems, subject only to reasonable technical, safety, reliability, and environmental
requirements established by law or regulation.

       SECTION 7. Technical Compliance. — All systems installed under this Act shall
comply with the Philippine Electrical Code, Fire Code of the Philippines, applicable DOE,
ERC, DTI and other mandatory technical standards. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to
exempt any person from compliance with applicable technical, safety, environmental, and
interconnection requirements.

         SECTION 8. Non-Application / Exclusions. – This Act shall not apply to large-scale
commercial power generation facilities, independent power producers (IPPs), or any generation
facility developed, financed and constructed primarily to engage in the business of generating
electricity for sale to third parties.


                                                6
    SECTION 9. Definition of Terms –
(a) “Battery Energy Storage System” (BESS) refers to a system with all related equipment
    essential to its functioning as a single entity which is capable of storing electrical energy
    through reactions from which it is able to charge or discharge electrical energy to the
    power system.

(b) “Community Renewable Energy System” (CRES) refers to a renewable-energy-based
    Distributed Energy Resource System established for collective ownership,
    participation, or benefit of a community.

(c) “Distributed Energy Resources” (DER) refers to power sources connected to the
    Distribution System or electrical system of the End-Users that could be aggregated to
    meet a demand.

(d) “Distributed Renewable Energy System” (DRES) under this Act refers to a micro-to-
    small scale renewable energy generation facility—including, but not limited to, solar
    photovoltaic, wind, biomass, or hybrid systems—owned or operated by private
    residential, commercial, or industrial Qualified End-users, installed within or adjacent
    to their premises, connected either on-grid or off-grid, primarily aimed at meeting the
    energy consumption of the Qualified End-user and/or exporting or sharing excess
    generation in accordance with national distribution and interconnection standards.

(e) “Energy Storage System” (ESS) refers to a facility acting as a load and a generator,
    which is designed to receive, store and convert such energy to electricity.

(f) “Grid Interconnection” refers to the connection of a Community Renewable Energy
    System, renewable energy facility, energy storage system, or distributed energy
    resource to the transmission system or distribution system for purposes of delivering,
    receiving, sharing, wheeling, importing, or exporting electricity, subject to applicable
    technical, safety, and reliability requirements.

(g) “Hosting Capacity” refers to the maximum amount of electric generation, energy
    storage, demand-side resource, electric load, or other distributed energy resource that
    can be interconnected to a transmission or distribution system, or a specific portion
    thereof, without adversely affecting safety, reliability, power quality, or operational
    performance and without requiring material system upgrades.

(h) “Net Metering” refers to a system, appropriate for distributed generation, in which a
    distribution grid user has a two-way connection to the grid and is only charged for his
    net electricity consumption and is credited for any overall contribution to the electricity
    grid.




                                             7
   (i) “Off-Grid System” refers to a local electricity supply system independent of the
       national transmission and distribution grid, utilized by Qualified End-users who reside
       in remote areas.

   (j) “Renewable Energy Resources” refer to energy resources that do not have an upper
       limit on a national scale and that continuously replenish themselves through natural
       ecological cycles, including, but not limited to, solar energy, wind, biomass,
       geothermal, ocean energy, and hydropower.

   (k) “Qualified End-user” under this Act refers to any person or entity that consumes
       electricity for its own domestic, residential, commercial, institutional, agricultural,
       industrial, or governmental use and is authorized under this Act to install, own, operate,
       and maintain distributed renewable energy systems for self-generation and self-
       consumption.

       The term includes without limitation to, households, condominium corporations,
       homeowners' associations (HOAs), micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs),
       cooperatives, farmers' organizations, indigenous peoples' communities (IPCs), local
       government units (LGUs), government agencies, government-owned or -controlled
       corporations (GOCCs), educational institutions, religious institutions, non-stock and
       non-profit organizations, and other similarly situated entities.

        SECTION 10. Recognition of Qualified End-user Self-Generation Rights. – The
State recognizes the inherent right of Qualified End-Users to participate in energy generation
through clean and renewable technologies. Qualified End-users shall be recognized as active
energy participants ("prosumers") with full legal authorization to construct, install, own, and
operate distributed renewable energy systems within their premises, without being classified
as commercial generation companies under the EPIRA, provided that the electricity generated
is intended primarily for self-consumption.

        The DOE, ERC, and all local distribution utilities and electric cooperatives shall ensure
that administrative policies, technical standards, and institutional frameworks foster rather than
hinder this right.

       Every Qualified End-user shall have the uninfringed right to install, own, finance,
operate, or participate in end-user self-generation facilities from renewable energy sources
primarily intended to meet their electricity requirements. In exercising this right, Qualified
End-users shall be entitled to:

   (a) Generate electricity from renewable energy and other qualified energy resources;

   (b) Utilize safely, within end-user premises, generated renewable energy directly to supply
       their internal electrical loads without arbitrary disconnections, unwarranted technical
       restrictions, or punitive standby charges from distribution utilities or electric

                                                8
       cooperatives, provided the installation complies with the Philippine Electrical Code
       (PEC) and national safety standards;

   (c) Store excess electricity through appropriate energy storage technologies;

   (d) Manage electricity consumption through energy efficiency and demand-side
       management measures;

   (e) Connect their distributed renewable energy systems safely to the distribution grid
       through a streamlined, transparent, and non-discriminatory net-metering or equivalent
       interconnection framework;

   (f) Adopt innovative renewable energy technologies that promote energy independence;
       and

   (g) Share electricity generated from distributed renewable energy systems with other end-
       users through peer-to-peer energy transactions.

   No Qualified End-user shall be prohibited from developing a lawful distributed renewable
energy facility solely on the basis that the end-user is connected to an existing electricity
supplier or distribution network.

         SECTION 12. Sources of Funds and Financing Mechanisms. — To effectively carry
out the objectives of this Act, the funding and financial assistance required for the deployment
of distributed renewable energy systems intended primarily for self-consumption for Qualified
End-users shall be sourced and institutionalized through the following mechanisms:

       (a) Renewable Energy Trust Fund (RE Trust Fund) for Local Government Units:
           Subject to Republic Act No. 9513 and existing budgetary laws, the Department of
           Energy (DOE) is hereby mandated to allocate and utilize a portion of the RE Trust
           Fund, established under Republic Act No. 9513, specifically to extend grants and
           financial support exclusively to LGUs.

               This fund shall be utilized to install distributed renewable energy systems in
           public buildings and structures, including but not limited to,
           provincial/city/municipal capitol buildings, public schools, public markets,
           transport terminals, health centers, public hospitals, recreational and community
           facilities, public water systems, evacuation centers and disaster response facilities,
           among eligible projects to reduce public operational costs and model energy self-
           sufficiency.

       (b) Dedicated Qualified End-user Distributed Renewable Energy Technologies
           Financing Programs through SSS and GSIS: The Social Security System (SSS) and
           the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS) are hereby encouraged and

                                               9
           authorized, consistent with their respective charters, to establish and maintain
           dedicated, low-interest, and non-burdensome RE technology financing and green
           loan programs, loan guarantees, and credit enhancements. These programs shall
           provide Qualified End-users accessible credit options with highly competitive
           interest rates and extended amortization terms, exclusively designated for the
           procurement, installation, and integration of micro-generation and small-scale
           renewable energy systems for own-use, including the purchase of battery energy
           storage systems and the replacement of major components.

                   The SSS and GSIS shall formulate simplified procedures and reasonable
           repayment terms to encourage the participation of members in self-generated
           electricity initiatives, ensuring that loan processing is streamlined and integrated
           with existing member-benefit platforms.

       (c) Public Financing and Specialized Credit Facilities via LBP and DBP: The Land
           Bank of the Philippines (LBP) and the Development Bank of the Philippines (DBP)
           are hereby directed to establish and maintain dedicated financing windows, loan
           facilities, guarantee programs, credit enhancements, and other financial assistance
           mechanisms to support the acquisition, installation, expansion, and maintenance of
           micro-generation and small-scale renewable energy systems, including the
           purchase of battery energy storage systems and the replacement of major
           components, for own-use by Qualified End-users, subject to applicable banking
           laws, credit evaluation and risk management policies.

       (d) Additional Sources of Financing: The programs established under this Act may
           likewise be supported through grants, subsidies, concessional financing, official
           development assistance, climate finance facilities, ESG funds, blended finance
           mechanisms, green bonds, public-private partnerships, carbon finance mechanisms,
           and such other lawful sources as may be authorized by existing laws and
           regulations.

        The DOE is hereby authorized to develop, access, mobilize, and facilitate the utilization
of international climate finance and other funding mechanisms from bilateral, multilateral, and
international development partners, for the implementation of programs and projects under this
Act.

        SECTION 13. Incentives and Benefits for Qualified End-users. — Qualified End-
users who install, own, and operate on-site distributed renewable energy systems under this Act
shall be entitled to the following direct economic, technical, and market-based incentives:

   (a) Kilowatt-Hour to Kilowatt-Hour (kWh-to-kWh) Equivalent Net-Metering Scheme:
       Pursuant to the objectives of Republic Act No. 9513 and to maximize financial relief
       from volatile power utility pricing, Qualified End-users participating in the Net-


                                               10
    Metering Program shall be credited based on a direct kilowatt-hour (kWh) to kilowatt-
    hour equivalence offset for excess electricity exported back to the distribution grid.

           Every kilowatt-hour exported by a Qualified End-user to the distribution system
   shall directly offset an equivalent kilowatt-hour consumed from the grid during the
   corresponding billing cycle. The ERC shall establish a fair, transparent and Qualified
   End-user-responsive compensation mechanism for electricity exported to the grid.

(b) Ownership and Monetization of Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs): Qualified
    End-users owning or operating eligible distributed renewable energy systems and
    technologies shall retain full legal ownership of the Renewable Energy Certificates
    (RECs) and/or similar instruments arising from the generation of renewable electricity
    by such systems. The RECs and/or and similar instruments corresponding to the
    renewable electricity generated, whether consumed on-site or exported to the grid
    pursuant to applicable laws, rules, and regulations, shall accrue exclusively to the
    Qualified End-user unless expressly transferred, assigned, sold, or otherwise disposed
    of through a written agreement.

           The Department of Energy (DOE), the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC),
   and the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines (IEMOP) shall
   establish streamlined and accessible administrative frameworks enabling Qualified
   End-users to aggregate, hold, and trade their RECs through the Renewable Energy
   Market (REM), either directly or through accredited aggregators recognized under
   applicable laws, rules, and regulations. This mechanism shall convert private renewable
   energy generation into a viable, direct secondary revenue stream, empowering ordinary
   citizens to actively participate in and benefit from national environmental compliance
   markets.

           The DOE or ERC may prescribe reasonable minimum aggregation thresholds,
   registration requirements, and participation rules for the aggregation, trading, transfer,
   or monetization of Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs) and other environmental
   attributes by Qualified End-users, taking into consideration market efficiency,
   consumer protection, and the promotion of distributed renewable energy systems.

(c) Priority Access to Future Incentive Programs: Qualified End-users shall be eligible to
    participate in future government programs involving renewable energy credits, carbon
    markets, distributed energy resource markets, green financing, incentive schemes,
    demand-side management programs, demand response programs, flexibility services,
    ancillary services, energy storage programs, and other market-based or regulatory
    mechanisms established to promote renewable energy development, grid reliability,
    energy security, and consumer participation in the electric power sector.

          The DOE or ERC shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations to enable
   and facilitate the participation of Qualified End-users, whether individually or through

                                          11
       aggregation, in such programs and markets, including those that may be established in
       the future.

   (d) Protection Against Unjust Disqualification: No Qualified End-user shall be denied,
       restricted, or otherwise discriminated against in the availment of incentives, credits,
       certificates, financing programs, interconnection services, permitting processes, or
       other benefits solely by reason of generating electricity primarily for personal
       consumption rather than commercial sale.

               No government agency, local government unit, distribution utility, electric
       cooperative, financial institution, market participant, or other entity shall impose
       discriminatory requirements, conditions, charges, procedures, timelines, or technical
       standards on Qualified End-users that are not uniformly applied to similarly situated
       persons or entities. Technical, engineering, safety, interconnection, inspection, and
       operational requirements shall be fair, reasonable, transparent, non-discriminatory, and
       consistently applied.

        SECTION 14. Consumer Right to Grid Interconnection. — Qualified End-users
utilizing distributed renewable energy systems capable of exporting power shall have the right
to connect to the distribution grid. Distribution utilities and electric cooperatives must act on
complete interconnection applications within the period prescribed by laws, rules, or
regulations.

       SECTION 15. Consumer Right to Non-Discriminatory Treatment. — Every
Qualified End-user under this Act shall have the right to fair and equal treatment in permitting,
financing, interconnection, net-metering, incentives, and access to government support
programs. No Qualified End-user shall be denied, delayed, or subjected to unreasonable
requirements solely because it generates electricity primarily for its own consumption.

        SECTION 16. Interconnection Dispute Resolution. — Any unjustified and
unreasonable delay, discrimination, unauthorized technical demand, arbitrary fees, or refusal
by distribution utilities or electric cooperatives regarding distributed renewable energy systems
shall be subject to immediate summary action and penalties by the ERC to protect prosumer
rights. The ERC shall establish an expedited dispute resolution mechanism to protect the rights
of Qualified End-Users and facilitate the timely connection of renewable energy systems to the
grid.

        SECTION 17. Battery Energy Storage Incentives. — Qualified End-users who install
and integrate battery energy storage systems together with their renewable energy systems shall
be eligible for financing assistance, incentives, and other support programs under this Act to
improve energy reliability, resilience, and self-sufficiency.

       SECTION 18. Community Renewable Energy Projects and Association Freedom. —
Qualified End-Users may voluntarily join, organize, or participate in community renewable

                                               12
energy projects, homeowners' associations, cooperatives, or similar groups for the shared
development, ownership, operation, and use of renewable energy systems, subject to applicable
laws and regulations.

      SECTION 19. Government Agency Mechanisms and Inter-Agency Responsibilities.
— The following agencies shall perform the duties prescribed under this Act:

   (a) Department of Energy (DOE): The DOE shall act as the primary lead agency tasked
       with formulating and promulgating the comprehensive Implementing Rules and
       Regulations (IRR) of this Act.

                The DOE shall exercise overall policy coordination and implementation
       monitoring functions under this Act and shall work closely with the Office of the
       President, ERC, the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), the Bureau of Customs
       (BOC), the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), the
       Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), government financial
       institutions, and other concerned agencies to ensure the effective and coordinated
       implementation of this Act. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish or
       interfere with the statutory independence, powers, or functions of the ERC or any other
       constitutional or independent regulatory body.

              The DOE shall establish a monitoring and evaluation system to assess
       compliance with this Act and publish on its official website, an annual implementation
       report containing information on program accomplishments, agency compliance,
       deployment of distributed renewable energy systems, financing availments, challenges
       encountered, and policy recommendations.

               The DOE shall conduct a comprehensive review of the implementation and
       effectiveness of this Act every three (3) years and, as necessary, recommend legislative,
       regulatory, or policy measures to further strengthen citizen-generated electricity and
       distributed renewable energy development in the country.

               The DOE shall likewise publish annually on its official website a report on the
       status, collections, allocations, disbursements, beneficiaries, and utilization of the
       Renewable Energy Trust Fund and other financing mechanisms established or utilized
       under this Act, subject to applicable transparency, auditing, and accountability
       requirements.

   (b) Department of Trade and Industry (DTI): The DTI, through the Bureau of Philippine
       Standards (BPS), is mandated to streamline, standardize, and accelerate the certification
       and registration requirements applicable to distributed renewable energy systems, such
       as solar energy systems and components and other renewable energy technologies,
       including photovoltaic panels, inverters, batteries, charge controllers, mounting
       systems, wind energy equipment, biomass energy systems, micro-hydropower

                                              13
   components, energy storage systems, electric vehicle charging infrastructure, and other
   related equipment.

           The DTI shall adopt and implement internationally recognized product
   standards, including applicable International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC),
   International Organization for Standardization (ISO), or equivalent standards, to ensure
   product quality, safety, reliability, and interoperability.

           The DTI shall establish Green Lanes and designate dedicated personnel or
   offices to process applications involving renewable energy equipment, products,
   suppliers, installers, and service providers. Administrative processing of complete
   applications shall be subject to prescribed timelines. Failure to act within such timelines
   shall result only in the administrative processing stage being deemed approved, without
   prejudice to the conduct and completion of the required technical, safety, quality,
   testing, or compliance evaluations.

           The DTI shall ensure that certification and trade facilitation processes are
   administered in a manner that facilitates, rather than hinders, the deployment of
   distributed renewable energy systems.

           The DTI shall maintain and publish on its official website a regularly updated
   registry of accredited renewable energy equipment, certified products, registered
   suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, installers, service providers, testing laboratories,
   and certification bodies authorized to operate under applicable laws and regulations.

(c) Bureau of Customs (BOC): The Bureau of Customs shall establish dedicated Green
    Lanes and expedited customs processing procedures for the importation of distributed
    renewable energy equipment, components, spare parts, energy storage systems, and
    other renewable energy technologies covered by this Act. Declarations and
    consignments complying with standard safety certifications shall be processed on a
    priority basis with minimum documentary delays and "deemed approved" release
    parameters upon lapse of statutory working limits.

            The BOC shall designate dedicated personnel or office to process, facilitate, and
   prioritize applications, declarations, clearances, and shipments involving distributed
   renewable energy equipment. The BOC shall likewise maximize the use of electronic
   processing systems, digital documentation, risk-based assessment mechanisms, and
   pre-arrival clearance procedures to expedite customs processing and reduce
   administrative delays.

          Shipments of distributed renewable energy equipment that comply with
   applicable customs, safety, security, and other regulatory requirements shall be
   processed on a priority basis and within prescribed processing timelines. Nothing in
   this Act shall be construed to exempt any importation from the requirements of the

                                           14
   Customs Modernization and Tariff Act, customs laws, public safety regulations,
   national security measures, or other applicable laws and regulations.

          The BOC shall ensure that customs procedures facilitate, rather than
   unnecessarily hinder, the timely importation and deployment of renewable energy
   technologies intended for use by qualified end-users under this Act.

(d) Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA): TESDA shall
    formulate, expand, and implement nationwide technical-vocational education, training,
    assessment, certification, and workforce development programs to build the technical
    capacity of renewable energy professionals, including solar designers, photovoltaic

           TESDA shall coordinate with DOE, the Professional Regulation Commission
   (PRC), the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), industry associations, and other

           TESDA shall establish continuing competency assessment, upskilling,

   installer accreditation and competency certification program for renewable energy

           To ensure equitable access nationwide, TESDA shall promote the
   decentralization of renewable energy training, assessment, and certification services
   through regional, provincial, and local training centers, accredited institutions, mobile
   training programs, and digital learning platforms, with priority given to underserved,
   off-grid, geographically isolated, and disadvantaged areas. TESDA shall ensure the
   availability of a skilled, competent, and regionally accessible renewable energy
   workforce capable of supporting the safe and efficient deployment, operation, and
   maintenance of distributed renewable energy systems throughout the country.

        SECTION 20. Expansion of Renewable Energy (RE) Absorption Capacity and Grid
Integration Mandate. — The Department of Energy (DOE), the Energy Regulatory
Commission (ERC), the National Grid Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP), the National
Transmission Corporation (TransCo), and all distribution utilities (DUs) shall, within their
respective mandates, adopt measures to progressively increase grid hosting and system
absorption capacity for renewable energy, particularly variable renewable energy (VRE),
distributed generation, rooftop solar photovoltaic systems, and other Distributed Renewable
Energy Systems (DRES).

        In formulating policies, plans, standards, and investment programs, the DOE and ERC
shall encourage the development of transmission and distribution infrastructure, operational
protocols, and interconnection standards capable of accommodating substantially higher levels
of renewable energy penetration, with the objective of enabling the power system to support
increased variable renewable energy integration.

        The DOE and ERC shall likewise review and update existing distribution code
provisions, interconnection requirements, and connection standards to promote transparent,
objective, and non-discriminatory assessment of renewable energy interconnection
applications, and to ensure that claims of grid constraints, hosting capacity limitations, or
system saturation are supported by verifiable technical evidence.

       SECTION 21. Prohibited Acts. — The following acts are hereby prohibited:

   (a) Willful Refusal. Willfully refusing, obstructing, or imposing undue conditions on the
       installation, operation, maintenance, upgrading, or interconnection of a qualified
       distributed renewable energy systems intended primarily for self-consumption, except
       on legitimate legal, technical, safety, reliability, or security grounds established by law
       or regulation.

   (b) Discriminatory Treatment. Subjecting owners or operators of qualified distributed
       renewable energy systems to discriminatory charges, fees, technical requirements,
       permitting requirements, interconnection conditions, or other regulatory burdens not
       imposed on similarly situated consumers as well as discrimination in billing, metering,
       inspections, and processing times.

   (c) Imposition of Unlawful Charges. Imposing fees, taxes, penalties, standby charges,
       minimum consumption requirements, unauthorized interconnection fees and
       unreasonable upgrade charges, or other financial obligations that have the purpose or
       effect of discouraging or impairing the exercise of the right to generate, store, consume,
       or share renewable energy, unless expressly authorized by law or ERC regulation.


                                               17
   (d) Anti-Competitive Conduct. Engaging in any act or practice that restricts, prevents, or
       unfairly limits participation in renewable energy self-generation, community energy
       projects, energy sharing arrangements, abuse of dominant position by regulated
       utilities, or the sale of excess renewable energy as allowed under this Act.

   (e) Unauthorized Interference. Tampering with, disconnecting, damaging, disabling, or
       otherwise interfering with any authorized distributed renewable energy facilities and
       systems, smart meter, distributed energy resource, energy storage system, battery
       energy storage systems, smart inverters, or associated equipment without lawful
       authority, and unauthorized remote access or disabling of systems.

   (f) False or Misleading Representations. Knowingly making false, fraudulent, deceptive,
       or misleading statements, financing, warranties and performance guarantees regarding
       the performance, safety, certification, capacity, eligibility, environmental attributes, or
       economic benefits of renewable energy systems covered by this Act. This shall cover
       installers, suppliers, financiers and marketers.

   (g) Unauthorized Access or Energy Theft. Unauthorized access to, diversion of
       manipulation of, or theft of electricity produced by authorized distributed renewable
       energy facilities and systems or energy storage facility.

   (h) Falsification of Records. Falsifying applications, permits, certifications, metering data,
       energy production records, billing records, or other documents required under this Act.

   (i) Misappropriation of Incentives. Obtaining or attempting to obtain any subsidy, grant,
       tax incentive, credit, or benefit under this Act through fraud, misrepresentation, or
       concealment of material facts.

   (j) Any Other Act. Any act that defeats, circumvents, or frustrates the rights, objectives,
       and policies established under this Act, as may be determined by the appropriate
       regulatory authority after due notice and hearing.

        SECTION 22. Prohibition Against Retaliatory Acts. — No distribution utility, electric
cooperative, retail electricity supplier, grid operator, government agency, or any of their
officers, employees, or agents shall engage in any retaliatory, discriminatory, or punitive act
against an End-user on account of the installation, operation, ownership, or use of a distributed
renewable energy system, participation in net-metering or similar programs, or the exercise of
any right granted under this Act.

        Any person aggrieved by a violation of this Section may file a complaint with the
Energy Regulatory Commission, which shall have the authority to order corrective measures
and impose appropriate administrative sanctions in accordance with existing laws and
regulations.


                                               18
        SECTION 23. Protection of End-User Energy Data. — Metering, consumption,
generation, export, and other energy usage data of an End-user shall be treated as confidential
and shall not be disclosed, sold, shared, transferred, processed, or used for commercial,
marketing, or any other purpose without the prior written consent of the end-user, except where
disclosure is required by law, authorized by the Energy Regulatory Commission, or necessary
for billing, system operations, grid reliability, cybersecurity, or other legitimate regulatory
purposes.

       Distribution utilities, electric cooperatives, retail electricity suppliers, aggregators,
service providers, and other entities with access to such data shall implement appropriate
measures to ensure its security, confidentiality, and protection against unauthorized access, use,
disclosure, alteration, or destruction.

         SECTION 24. Protection Against Unauthorized Disconnection. — No distribution
utility, electric cooperative, retail electricity supplier, or other electricity service provider shall
disconnect, refuse electric service to, or otherwise impair the provision of electric service to an
End-user solely on the ground that the end-user has installed, owns, operates, or uses a
distributed renewable energy system, energy storage system, or other self-generation facility
authorized under this Act.

       SECTION 25. Non-Compliance with Lawful Orders. — Willful failure or refusal to
comply with any lawful order, directive, rule, regulation, or decision issued pursuant to this
Act shall constitute a violation of this Act. The Department of Energy and the Energy
Regulatory Commission shall have the authority to impose administrative fines and other
appropriate sanctions, including the suspension or revocation of permits, licenses,
accreditations, or operating authority, without prejudice to the filing of civil or criminal actions
under applicable laws.

        SECTION 26. Obstruction of Inspections and Investigations. — No person or entity
covered by this Act shall obstruct, impede, delay, refuse, or otherwise interfere with any lawful
inspection, audit, monitoring activity, inquiry, or investigation conducted by the Department of
Energy, the Energy Regulatory Commission, or any duly authorized government authority in
the exercise of its powers under this Act.

       Any person or entity found to have violated this Section shall be subject to the
administrative, civil, or criminal penalties provided under this Act and other applicable laws.

        SECTION 27. False Claims of Accreditation or Certification. — It shall be unlawful
for any person or entity to forge, falsify, alter, use, present, or misrepresent any accreditation,
certification, license, permit, qualification, or similar credential required under this Act, or to
falsely claim eligibility to perform activities regulated herein.




                                                  19
        Violations of this Section shall constitute a ground for the denial, suspension, or
revocation of any accreditation, certification, license, permit, or authorization issued under this
Act, in addition to any applicable administrative, civil, or criminal penalties.

       SECTION 28. Penalties. —
   (a) Any person who commits any prohibited act under this Act shall, upon conviction, be
       punished with a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (PHP100,000.00)
       but not more than Five Million Pesos (PHP5,000,000.00) or imprisonment of not less
       than six (6) months but not more than six (6) years, or both, at the discretion of the
       court.

   (b) Where the violation consists of a willful refusal, delay, obstruction, discriminatory
       treatment, or imposition of unlawful charges by a distribution utility, electric
       cooperative, retail electricity supplier, homeowners' association, condominium
       corporation, local government unit, or other juridical entity, the responsible entity shall
       be liable for a fine of not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (PHP500,000.00) but
       not more than Ten Million Pesos (PHP10,000,000.00) for each violation, without
       prejudice to the issuance of corrective orders and administrative sanctions by the
       appropriate regulatory authority.

   (c) Any person who knowingly falsifies records, submits fraudulent documents,
       misrepresents technical or financial information, tampers with metering equipment,
       steals electricity, or unlawfully obtains incentives under this Act shall suffer a fine of
       not less than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (PHP500,000.00) but not more than Ten
       Million Pesos (PHP10,000,000.00), or imprisonment of not less than two (2) years but
       not more than eight (8) years, or both, at the discretion of the court.

   (d) If the offender is a corporation, partnership, association, cooperative, distribution
       utility, electric cooperative, or other juridical entity, the president, general manager,
       chief executive officer, members of the governing board, or other officers directly
       responsible for the violation shall be held liable, without prejudice to the imposition of
       administrative penalties upon the entity itself.

   (e) If the offender is a public officer or employee, the maximum penalty shall be imposed,
       without prejudice to administrative liability and disqualification from holding public
       office in accordance with applicable laws.

   (f) Each day that a continuing violation persists after receipt of a lawful order to comply
       shall constitute a separate offense.

   (g) The penalties provided under this Act shall be without prejudice to the application of
       other civil, criminal, or administrative remedies and penalties provided under existing
       laws.

       This is without prejudice to the penalties provided for under existing environmental

        SECTION 29. Administrative Enforcement. — The Department of Energy, the Energy
Regulatory Commission, and other competent government agencies shall have the authority to
investigate violations of this Act, conduct administrative proceedings, issue compliance orders,
and impose such administrative sanctions as may be authorized under this Act and other
applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

        SECTION 30. Administrative Complaint Mechanism. — Any person aggrieved by a
violation of this Act may first file a complaint with the Department of Energy, the Energy
Regulatory Commission, or the appropriate government agency exercising jurisdiction over
the matter.

       The filing of an administrative complaint shall not prejudice the right of the
complainant to seek judicial relief after exhaustion of administrative remedies or upon
unreasonable delay by the concerned agency in resolving the complaint, as may be provided
by law or regulation.

        SECTION 31. Promotion of Mediation and Alternative Dispute Resolution. — It is
the policy of the State to encourage the amicable settlement of technical, interconnection,
metering, billing, and other disputes arising under this Act through mediation and alternative
dispute resolution mechanisms. Resort to such mechanisms shall not prejudice the right of any
party to pursue the appropriate administrative or judicial remedies available under law.

        SECTION 32. Whistleblower Protection. — Any person who, in good faith, reports a
suspected violation of this Act shall be protected from retaliation. It shall be unlawful for any
person or entity to take or threaten any adverse action against a whistleblower on account of
such report or participation in an investigation, inspection, audit, administrative proceeding, or
judicial action arising under this Act.

        The identity of a whistleblower shall be kept confidential to the fullest extent permitted
by law, unless disclosure is required by due process, court order, or with the whistleblower's
written consent.

        Any retaliatory act committed in violation of this Section shall constitute a separate and
distinct offense and shall be subject to the penalties provided under this Act, without prejudice
to any other remedies available under existing laws.

      SECTION 33. Mitigation for Voluntary Compliance. — The voluntary disclosure and
prompt correction of a violation before the initiation of a formal investigation or enforcement

action may be considered a mitigating circumstance for purposes of determining administrative
penalties under this Act. Such disclosure or correction shall not extinguish liability but may
warrant a reduction of administrative sanctions, subject to rules to be prescribed by the

        SECTION 34. Education, Capacity Building, and Protection of Renewable Energy
Prosumers. — The State shall promote public awareness, technical literacy, consumer
protection, and capacity building to enable qualified persons and entities to exercise their rights
under this Act and to participate effectively in renewable energy self-generation, self-
consumption, energy storage, energy sharing, and related activities.

       To this end, the Department of Energy (DOE), in coordination with the Energy
Regulatory Commission (ERC), the National Electrification Commission (NEA), the
Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG), Cooperative Development Authority (CDA), National Commission on Indigenous
Peoples (NCIP), Department of Agriculture (DA), Department of Science and Technology
(DOST), distribution utilities, electric cooperatives, academic institutions, and other relevant
government agencies, shall develop and implement programs specifically for:

   (a) Private citizens and households, to increase awareness of renewable energy
       technologies, energy efficiency practices, consumer rights, available incentives,
       financing options, system safety, and opportunities for self-generation and self-
       consumption;

   (b) The Department of Human Settlements and Urban Developments (DHSUD) shall
       support the development and management of shared, community-based, and common-
       area renewable energy systems, including guidance on governance, financing,
       operation, maintenance, and energy sharing arrangements of Homeowners'
       Associations (HOAs);

   (c) The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) shall facilitate the adoption by Micro,
       Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) of distributed renewable energy systems
       through technical assistance, business advisory services, financing information,
       workforce development, and access to government support programs;

   (d) The Department of Agriculture (DA) shall encourage and promote the use of renewable
       energy for irrigation, post-harvest facilities, cold storage, processing operations, rural
       electrification, and other productive agricultural activities of Agricultural Cooperatives
       and Farmers' Organizations;

   (e) The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) shall strengthen local
       planning, permitting, investment promotion, climate resilience initiatives, and the
       integration of distributed renewable energy systems into local development programs
       of the various Local Government Units;

   (f) The National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) shall encourage, assist and
       strengthen the adoption, implementation and maintenance of Indigenous Peoples'
       Communities of community-owned distributed renewable energy systems and
       sustainable energy access, in a manner consistent with their rights, customs, traditions,
       and self-determined development priorities, including capacity-building programs;

   (g) Other similarly situated groups and organizations that may benefit from renewable
       energy self-generation, self-consumption, and community energy projects.

   (h) The Department of Energy (DOE) shall establish consumer education materials,
       technical assistance platforms, and public information campaigns to provide accessible,
       accurate, and up-to-date information regarding renewable energy systems, applicable
       standards, interconnection procedures, available incentives, consumer rights, and

        No person or entity engaged in the sale, installation, financing, leasing, or operation of
distributed renewable energy systems shall engage in deceptive, fraudulent, or misleading
marketing, advertising, pricing, or contractual practices. The Department of Energy (DOE), in
coordination with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and other appropriate agencies,
shall adopt measures to protect consumers from unfair practices and ensure access to effective
dispute resolution mechanisms.

       SECTION 35. Periodic Review. — The DOE and the ERC shall jointly undertake a
comprehensive review of the implementation of this Act every three (3) years and submit to
Congress a report on their findings, including recommendations for legislative, regulatory, or
policy measures necessary to improve the effectiveness of this Act and to address emerging
technical, economic, and market developments affecting distributed renewable energy.

        SECTION 36. Non-Impairment and Grandfathering of Existing Systems. —
Existing distributed renewable energy installations and net-metering participants lawfully
operating prior to the adoption of any subsequent regulatory change shall be grandfathered and
allowed to continue operating under the rules, rates, benefits, and interconnection arrangements
in effect at the time of their approval, unless the owner voluntarily elects to migrate to a new
regulatory regime.

       No regulatory change shall be applied retroactively so as to impair vested rights or
materially diminish benefits previously granted under this Act.

       SECTION 37. Appropriations. – Such sums as may be necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be taken from the current appropriations of the DOE.
Thereafter, the fund necessary to carry out the provisions of this Act shall be included in the
annual General Appropriations Act.

        SECTION 40. Separability Clause. — If any provision, section, or part of this Act is
