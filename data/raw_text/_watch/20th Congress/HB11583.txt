                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Professional competence is not static. Physicians and nurses must remain
abreast of developments affecting patient care. Engineers, architects, accountants,
teachers, and other regulated professionals must continuously update their knowledge
and skills. Filipino seafarers undergo recurrent training and certification to maintain
competencies demanded by domestic regulation and international maritime standards.
Many other occupations likewise require periodic training as a condition for continued
employment or professional practice.

       These requirements serve legitimate and important public purposes. The State
is justified in requiring persons engaged in professions and occupations affecting
public health, safety, welfare, and national development to maintain appropriate
standards of competence.

      Yet mandatory training carries a corresponding economic consequence.

      When the State makes completion of a particular course, training program,
continuing professional development activity, competency assessment, or certification
a prerequisite for the continued exercise of one's profession or livelihood, the
professional or worker effectively has no option but to incur the corresponding
expense.

      For many Filipino workers and professionals, the burden is cumulative.
Training fees may be accompanied by transportation, accommodation, meals, lost
working days, certification expenses, examination fees, renewal fees, and other
incidental costs. The financial burden is particularly significant for entry-level
professionals, government employees, healthcare workers, seafarers between
contracts, and workers supporting their families.

       Republic Act No. 10912, or the Continuing Professional Development Act of
2016, recognizes the importance of continuously improving the competence of
Filipino professionals. The objective of continuing professional development is
unquestionably important. The accessibility and affordability of compliance must,
however, form an equally important component of the State's professional
development policy.

       Congress has already recognized this principle in the maritime sector. Republic
Act No. 12021, or the Magna Carta of Filipino Seafarers, expressly declares that
seafarers shall have access to educational advancement and training at reasonable and
affordable costs. The law further authorizes the regulation of fees charged by
accredited maritime training institutions for mandatory training required under the
STCW Convention.

       There is compelling reason to extend the underlying principle to other similarly
situated Filipino professionals and workers.

       The Constitution provides ample basis for such legislative intervention. Article
II, Section 9 directs the State to promote a just and dynamic social order and free the
people from poverty through policies providing adequate social services, promoting
full employment, a rising standard of living, and improved quality of life. Article II,
Section 18 affirms labor as a primary social economic force and directs the State to
protect workers and promote their welfare.

       Article XII, Section 6 further declares that the use of property bears a social
function, that all economic agents shall contribute to the common good, and that
private economic enterprise remains subject to the duty of the State to promote
distributive justice and intervene when the common good so demands.

      Article XIII, Section 1 commands Congress to give the highest priority to
measures that protect and enhance human dignity and reduce social and economic
inequalities. Article XIII, Section 3 likewise commands the State to afford full
protection to labor, local and overseas.

       The proposed measure is also consistent with established principles concerning
the police power of the State.

     In Carlos Superdrug Corporation v. Department of Social Welfare and
Development, the Supreme Court sustained legislation requiring private
establishments to provide statutory discounts as a legitimate exercise of police power.
The Court recognized that the State, in advancing the welfare of a class requiring
legislative assistance, may validly require private establishments to participate in
carrying out a public welfare program.

       This doctrine was subsequently reiterated in Manila Memorial Park, Inc. v.
Secretary of Social Welfare and Development and subsequent cases involving
statutory discounts. These decisions recognize the broad authority of Congress to
regulate economic activity in furtherance of the general welfare, while preserving the
constitutional limitation that regulation must not become arbitrary, oppressive, or
confiscatory.

       This bill takes these constitutional considerations into account. It does not
simply compel training institutions to absorb the entire cost of a public program. It
provides a tax mechanism for discounts granted, authorizes subsidies and fiscal
assistance, directs regulators to establish reasonable fee frameworks, and requires
government to progressively expand affordable public and online training alternatives.

       The classification of lower-salaried government employees for additional
assistance is likewise founded upon substantial distinctions directly related to the
purpose of the measure.

       The Supreme Court has consistently held that equal protection does not prohibit
reasonable classification. A classification is constitutionally permissible when it: (1)
rests upon substantial distinctions; (2) is germane to the purpose of the law; (3) is not
confined to existing conditions; and (4) applies equally to all members of the class.

       Lower-salaried government employees required to personally finance
continuing professional requirements occupy a materially different economic position
from employees possessing greater capacity to absorb recurrent professional expenses.
Providing additional assistance to employees within designated Salary Grades is
directly germane to the legislative objective of preventing mandatory professional
requirements from imposing disproportionate financial hardship.

      This bill therefore proposes a balanced framework.

       It grants covered professionals and workers a fifty percent (50%) discount on
fees for training that government itself makes mandatory; establishes financial
assistance for qualified lower-salaried government employees; permits tax deductions
and other mechanisms to mitigate the burden upon private training providers; requires
transparency and regulation of mandatory training fees; encourages government-
provided and online training; prevents artificial increases and hidden charges; and
directs regulatory agencies periodically to determine whether existing training
requirements remain necessary and proportionate.

      Most importantly, the measure recognizes a simple principle of fairness:

       When compliance with training is made compulsory by the State as a condition
for a Filipino to continue practicing a profession or earning a livelihood, the cost of
that compulsory compliance should not fall disproportionately upon the individual
worker or professional.

     The State must simultaneously protect professional standards and make
compliance with those standards reasonably accessible.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

     AN ACT REDUCING THE FINANCIAL BURDEN OF MANDATORY
     CONTINUING      PROFESSIONAL     EDUCATION,    TRAINING,
     CERTIFICATION, AND PERIODIC COMPETENCY REQUIREMENTS
     UPON FILIPINO PROFESSIONALS AND WORKERS, PROVIDING FOR
     A FIFTY PERCENT (50%) DISCOUNT ON QUALIFIED MANDATORY
     TRAINING FEES, ESTABLISHING SUBSIDIES AND OTHER FORMS OF
     FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE, PROVIDING ADDITIONAL PROTECTION
     FOR QUALIFIED GOVERNMENT EMPLOYEES, AND FOR OTHER
     PURPOSES

     Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
     Congress assembled:

1          SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Mandatory
2    Professional Training Affordability Act.”
3
4           SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
5    to promote the continuous development, competence, safety, professionalism, and
6    international competitiveness of Filipino professionals and workers while ensuring
7    that compliance with training, continuing education, certification, revalidation, and
8    competency requirements imposed by law or regulation does not constitute an
9    unreasonable financial barrier to the continued exercise of a profession, occupation,
10   trade, or calling.
1
2           The State recognizes that continuing and periodic training requirements serve
3    legitimate public purposes, including the protection of public health and safety, the
4    maintenance of professional standards, compliance with international obligations,
5    and the development of a competent national workforce. However, where the State
6    requires such training as a condition for the issuance, renewal, maintenance, or
7    revalidation of a professional license, certificate, accreditation, competency,
8    qualification, or authority to work, the financial burden of compliance should, as far
9    as practicable, be equitably shared among the professional or worker, the State,
10   employers when otherwise required by law, and accredited training providers.
11
12          Towards this end, the State shall adopt measures to make mandatory
13   professional and occupational training accessible and affordable, particularly for
14   lower- and middle-income professionals and workers, government employees,
15   overseas Filipino workers, seafarers, health professionals, teachers, engineers,
16   skilled workers, and other similarly situated persons.
17
18         SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:
19

20      (a) Mandatory Training refers to a training course, seminar, workshop,
21          continuing professional development activity, refresher course, competency
22          assessment, revalidation program, certification program, or substantially
23          similar activity expressly required by law, regulation, or lawful issuance of a
24          competent government regulatory agency as a condition for the continued
25          practice of a profession or occupation or the maintenance or renewal of a
26          license, certificate, competency, or qualification.
27      (b) Training Provider refers to any public or private person, corporation,
28          institution, association, school, academy, training center, professional
29          organization, or other entity duly accredited or recognized by the competent
30          government agency to conduct Mandatory Training.
31      (c) Training Fee refers to the tuition, registration, assessment, laboratory,
32          simulator, instructional, or other fee necessarily and directly imposed for
33          participation in Mandatory Training.
1       (d) Qualified Government Employee refers to a civilian government employee
2           occupying a position allocated to Salary Grade 20 or below, or its equivalent
3           under a compensation and position classification system applicable to
4           government-owned or -controlled corporations, local government units, or
5           other government entities, whose Mandatory Training is required for the
6           continued performance of the functions of the employee's position or the
7           maintenance of a professional license, certification, accreditation, or
8           competency required for such position.
9       (e) Regulatory Agency refers to the PRC, MARINA, Department of Health
10          (DOH), Department of Migrant Workers (DMW), Civil Aviation Authority
11          of the Philippines (CAAP), Technical Education and Skills Development
12          Authority (TESDA), Civil Service Commission (CSC), or any other
13          government department, bureau, commission, authority, or instrumentality
14          vested by law with authority to prescribe professional or occupational training
15          requirements.
16
17         SEC. 4. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to any Filipino professional or
18   worker who is required by law, rule, regulation, international convention
19   implemented by the Philippines, or lawful issuance of a competent government
20   regulatory agency to undergo periodic or continuing education, training, competency
21   assessment, revalidation, refresher training, or certification as a condition for:
22

23      (a) the issuance, renewal, or maintenance of a professional license;
24      (b) the issuance, renewal, maintenance, or revalidation of a certificate of
25          competency, proficiency, qualification, accreditation, or similar authority;
26      (c) continued lawful practice of a regulated profession, occupation, trade, or
27          calling;
28      (d) continued eligibility for deployment or employment in an occupation subject
29          to mandatory government training or certification requirements; or
30      (e) compliance with mandatory continuing professional development or
31          equivalent regulatory requirements.
32
1          Covered persons shall include, but shall not be limited to:

4       (2) medical, nursing, allied health, and other healthcare professionals;
5       (3) Filipino seafarers subject to mandatory training or certification requirements
6           administered by the Maritime Industry Authority (MARINA);
7       (4) teachers and other regulated education professionals;
8       (5) engineers, architects, accountants, lawyers, and other regulated professionals;

17          SEC. 5. Fifty Percent (50%) Mandatory Training Discount. – All covered
18   professionals and workers under this Act shall be entitled to a discount equivalent to
19   fifty percent (50%) of the regular and published Training Fee charged by a private
20   Training Provider for Mandatory Training.
21
22         The discount shall apply only to training that is mandatory within the meaning
23   of this Act and shall not apply to elective courses, voluntary professional
24   development programs, optional certifications, social activities, accommodation,
25   transportation, meals not forming an inseparable part of the training fee, or other
26   incidental expenses.
27
28          No Training Provider shall require a covered professional or worker to waive
29   the discount as a condition for enrollment, admission, certification, assessment, or
30   completion of Mandatory Training.
31
1          SEC. 6. Protection Against Artificial Increase of Training Fees. – No
2    Training Provider shall increase, restructure, rename, transfer, or otherwise
3    manipulate its tuition, registration, miscellaneous, assessment, equipment,
4    simulator, administrative, or other charges for the purpose of recovering,
5    circumventing, or defeating the discount granted under this Act.
6
7          Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, every
8    concerned Regulatory Agency shall establish or approve a reasonable fee
9    framework for Mandatory Training under its jurisdiction, taking into consideration:
10      (a) the actual and reasonable cost of providing quality training;
11      (b) necessary investments in equipment, simulators, laboratories, facilities,
12          instructors, and technology;
13      (c) reasonable administrative and operating expenses;
14      (d) reasonable return necessary to ensure the continued availability and quality of
15          training services;
16      (e) prevailing industry and international standards;
17      (f) the financial capacity of covered professionals and workers; and
18      (g) the public interest in maintaining an adequate number of competent and
19          accredited Training Providers.
20
21          Any increase in fees for Mandatory Training beyond such threshold as may
22   be prescribed by the appropriate Regulatory Agency shall require prior approval or
23   justification in accordance with implementing regulations.
24
25         SEC. 7. Tax Treatment of Mandatory Training Discounts. – A private
26   Training Provider granting the discount required under Section 5 may claim the
27   amount of the discount granted as a deduction from gross income, based on the net
28   cost of the training services actually rendered, subject to the provisions of the
29   National Internal Revenue Code, as amended, and the rules and regulations to be
30   issued by the Department of Finance (DOF) and Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR).
31   Congress may, upon recommendation of the DOF, DBM, PRC, DMW, MARINA,
32   and other concerned agencies, establish additional fiscal incentives, reimbursement
1    mechanisms, or targeted subsidies where necessary to preserve the financial viability
2    and availability of essential Mandatory Training.
3
4          Nothing in this section shall be interpreted to authorize a Training Provider to
5    recover from the trainee the amount granted as a discount.
6
7           SEC. 8. Mandatory Training Assistance for Qualified Government
8    Employees. – A Qualified Government Employee under Salary Grade 20 or below
9    who is required to undertake Mandatory Training directly related to the functions,
10   qualifications, professional license, certification, or competency required by the
11   employee's government position shall be entitled to financial assistance from the
12   employing government agency for the portion of the Training Fee remaining after
13   application of the discount under Section 5.
14
15          Subject to the availability of appropriations and applicable budgeting,
16   accounting, and auditing rules, the employing agency shall pay or reimburse up to
17   one hundred percent (100%) of the remaining fifty percent (50%) of the Training
18   Fee, thereby allowing qualified lower-salaried government employees to undertake
19   required training at no or minimal personal expense.
20
21          The DBM and CSC may periodically review and adjust the Salary Grade
22   threshold, taking into account inflation, changes in the government compensation
23   structure, prevailing training costs, fiscal capacity, and the objectives of this Act:
24   Provided, That no adjustment shall diminish benefits already vested or due.
25
26          Government agencies are encouraged to enter into institutional agreements
27   with public universities, government training institutions, professional
28   organizations, and accredited private Training Providers for discounted group
29   training rates.
30
31         SEC. 9. Government Training Facilities. – Where a government
32   department, agency, state university or college, government training institution, or
33   other public entity possesses the facilities and competence to provide Mandatory
34   Training, it shall, as far as practicable, make such training available to covered
35   professionals and workers at cost or at substantially subsidized rates.

1          Where existing law, regulation, agreement, or company policy provides a
2    benefit more favorable to the professional or worker, the more favorable benefit shall

5          SEC. 12. Recognition of Employer-Sponsored Training. – Regulatory
6    Agencies shall, whenever consistent with professional standards, public safety, and
7    applicable international obligations, establish mechanisms for the accreditation or
8    recognition of qualified employer-sponsored, government-sponsored, institutional,

13   substantially duplicative training where equivalent competencies have already been

18          SEC. 13. Transparency of Mandatory Training Requirements. – Every
19   Regulatory Agency shall maintain and regularly publish on its official website:
20   (a) a complete list of Mandatory Training under its jurisdiction;
21   (b) the legal or regulatory basis for each requirement;
22   (c) the frequency with which the training must be undertaken;
23   (d) the accredited Training Providers authorized to conduct the training;
24   (e) the approved or prevailing range of Training Fees;
25   (f) available government-sponsored or subsidized alternatives;
26   (g) available online or blended-learning alternatives; and
27   (h) the applicable discounts, subsidies, and financial assistance under this Act.
28
29         No training program shall be considered mandatory for purposes of this Act
30   unless the legal or regulatory basis therefor is publicly available.
31
32         SEC. 14. Periodic Review of Mandatory Training Requirements. – Every
33   Regulatory Agency shall, at least once every three (3) years, review all Mandatory
34   Training requirements under its jurisdiction to determine whether:
35
1    (a) the requirement remains necessary;
2    (b) its frequency remains reasonable;
3    (c) substantially similar or duplicative requirements can be consolidated;
4    (d) competencies may be demonstrated through assessment, recognition of prior
5    learning, professional experience, or alternative modes;
6    (e) training fees remain reasonable and proportionate to the cost of delivery; and
7    (f) less costly means can accomplish the same regulatory purpose without
8    compromising public safety, professional competence, or international obligations.
9
10          No Mandatory Training requirement shall be maintained merely for revenue
11   generation by a Regulatory Agency, professional organization, accredited training
12   institution, or any other entity.

3          SEC. 17. Prohibited Acts. – It shall be unlawful for any Training Provider or
4    responsible officer thereof to:
5
6    (a) refuse without lawful cause to grant the discount provided under this Act;
7    (b) falsely represent an elective or optional course as Mandatory Training;
8    (c) impose hidden or additional charges for the purpose of recovering the statutory
9    discount;
10   (d) discriminate against a person availing of the discount;
11   (e) issue false receipts, certificates, training records, or statements concerning the
12   discount;
13   (f) artificially increase Training Fees to defeat the purposes of this Act; or
14   (g) enter into any arrangement with another provider, association, or person to fix
15   prices or otherwise circumvent this Act.

17         SEC. 18. Penalties. – Any Training Provider found to have violated Section
18   17 shall, after notice and hearing, be subject to:
19
20   (a) for the first offense, a fine of not less than Fifty Thousand Pesos (P50,000.00)
21   but not more than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00);
22   (b) for the second offense, a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos
23   (P100,000.00) but not more than Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00), and
24   suspension of accreditation for a period not exceeding six (6) months; and
25   (c) for the third and subsequent offenses, a fine of not less than Five Hundred
26   Thousand Pesos (P500,000.00) but not more than One Million Pesos
27   (P1,000,000.00), and suspension or revocation of accreditation.
28   Where the offender is a corporation, partnership, association, or other juridical
29   entity, liability shall attach to the responsible officers who knowingly authorized,
30   participated in, or permitted the violation.
