                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Sudden cardiac arrest (SCA) can happen to anyone at any time,
anywhere, without warning. One effective way to counter SCA is to perform
CPR, or cardiopulmonary resuscitation. However, if CPR is performed late or
incorrectly on an SCA patient, it can decrease the patient’s chance of survival
and bring about severe mental impairment.

      Students are equally susceptible to the danger of experiencing sudden
cardiac arrest due to their high level of physical and athletic activity in
schools. Thus, it is critical that there are always CPR- and basic first aid-
trained personnel in school.

       This bill seeks to require every school to have at least one person
certified to perform CPR. Through this initiative, not only do we guarantee
protection for our students, but also safety and protection for teachers, school
administrators, and other staff.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “CPR and First Aid
in Schools Act.”

SECTION 2. Medically-Qualified Personnel Required in All Schools. –
Each school shall have at least one (1) person certified in cardiopulmonary
resuscitation and first aid. Any employee of a school may be designated as
medically qualified personnel.

SECTION 3. Definition of Medically Qualified Personnel – For the
purposes of this Act, a person is considered certified in cardiopulmonary
resuscitation and first aid upon the completion and yearly renewal of a course
which meets the requirements established by the Secretary of Health or his
representative.

SECTION 4. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – The Secretary of
Education is hereby authorized and directed to adopt a system to implement
and carry out the provisions of this Act. The Secretary of Education shall,
within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Act, promulgate the rules and
regulations, including those of a disciplinary nature, to carry out and enforce
the provisions of this Act.

SECTION 5. Non-Impairment Clause. – Nothing in this Act shall be
construed as to diminish, impair, or repeal rights recognized, granted, or
made available to disadvantaged, marginalized, or specific sectors under
existing laws.
SECTION 6. Separability Clause. – If any part of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the other provisions not affected thereby shall
continue to be in full force and effect.

SECTION 7. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive
orders, administrative orders, rules and regulations inconsistent with or
contrary to the provisions of this Act are deemed amended, modified or
repealed accordingly.

SECTION 8. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect after fifteen (15) days
following its complete publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2)
newspapers of general circulation.
