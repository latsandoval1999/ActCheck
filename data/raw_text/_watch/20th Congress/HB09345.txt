                                   EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Constitution guarantees every Filipino the right to adequate food and mandates the State
to uplift the living standards of the poor. Yet despite existing poverty alleviation and social
protection programs, many Filipino families continue to experience involuntary hunger brought
about by food insecurity, unemployment, inflation, and economic vulnerability. Addressing
hunger remains essential not only to national development, but also to the country’s
commitment to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly SDG
2 or “Zero Hunger,” which aims to end hunger, achieve food security, improve nutrition, and
promote sustainable agriculture.

Millions of households struggle daily to provide sufficient and nutritious food for their families,
particularly children, senior citizens, persons with disabilities, and pregnant or lactating
women. Hunger and malnutrition not only threaten physical health and development, but also
impair educational outcomes, productivity, and overall quality of life.

The Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) has demonstrated that targeted
food assistance programs can help address hunger among the country’s most vulnerable
sectors. By providing food credits that may only be used to purchase nutritious food items
through accredited merchants, the program promotes healthier food consumption while
ensuring accountability and accessibility in the delivery of assistance.

Therefore, this bill seeks to institutionalize the Walang Gutom Program as a permanent
national food assistance initiative that will strengthen the State’s efforts to combat involuntary
hunger and improve food security among low-income households. Through a more responsive
and sustainable system of food assistance, the program aims to protect vulnerable families
from the long-term effects of hunger and undernutrition.

Ultimately, this bill affirms that access to adequate food is a basic human right and that no
Filipino family should be left to suffer from hunger in a country capable of providing for its
people.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

AN ACT INSTITUTIONALIZING THE WALANG GUTOM PROGRAM AS A FOOD
STAMP INITIATIVE TO COMBAT INVOLUNTARY HUNGER, PROVIDING FUNDS
               THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in the
Congress assembled:


SEC. 1. Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Walang Gutom Act”.
SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to promote social justice,
reduce poverty, and protect the family as a basic autonomous social institution. The
State shall guarantee the right of every Filipino to freedom from hunger and shall
ensure adequate food intake, especially for the most vulnerable sectors.
The State affirms its obligations under the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and
the International Covenant on Economic, Social, and Cultural Rights to guarantee the
right to adequate food.
SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act, the following terms shall mean:
   a. Walang Gutom Program – A national food stamp program aimed at addressing
      involuntary hunger by providing eligible beneficiaries with electronic food
      credits for the purchase of nutritious food items;
   b. Electronic Benefit Transfer (EBT) – A system that allows the transfer of food
      credits through reloadable cards to be used at accredited partner-merchants;
   c. Food Poverty Line – The minimum monthly income required by a family to meet
      basic food requirements, computed and regularly updated by the Philippine
      Statistics Authority (PSA), and serving as a basis for benefit levels and
      eligibility;
   d. Eligible Beneficiaries – Households whose income falls below the food poverty
      line, with prioritization for families with pregnant or lactating women, children
      under five, senior citizens, or persons with disabilities; and
   e. Listahan ng mga Pamilyang Nangangailangan (Listahanan) – Also known as
      the National Household Targeting System for Poverty Reduction (NHTS-PR),
      an information management system of the Department of Social Welfare and
      Development (DSWD) that identifies who and where the poor are in the
      Philippines for purposes of establishing a basis for targeting beneficiaries of
      social protection programs. Inclusion in the Listahanan is determined through
      nationwide household assessments conducted in cycles, the results of which
      are periodically updated to reflect changes in household conditions.
SEC. 4. Establishment of the Walang Gutom Program. – There is hereby established
a Walang Gutom Program under the Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD), which shall implement and oversee the food stamp initiative nationwide.
SEC. 5. Program Components. – The program shall include the following components:
   a. Identification of Beneficiaries;
   b. Distribution of Electronic Benefit Transfer Cards preloaded with monthly food
      credits equivalent to at least Five Thousand Pesos (Php 5,000) per household;
   c. Accreditation of Partner-Merchants such as grocery stores, wet markets, and
      cooperatives that will accept food stamps as payment;
   d. Nutrition Education and Counseling to ensure proper food choices and prevent
      malnutrition;
   e. Regular Monitoring and Evaluation to assess program impact and make
      evidence-based improvements;
   f. Limitation of food credit use to eligible food items, as defined by the DSWD in
      consultation with the Department of Health (DOH), which shall not be converted
      to cash nor used to purchase non-food items; and
   g. Establishment of alternative benefit delivery mechanisms in geographically
      isolated or disadvantaged areas, with support from LGUs and accredited
      community-based organizations, ensuring offline accessibility and inclusivity.
SEC. 6. Beneficiary Identification. – For purposes of determining eligibility under this
Act, the DSWD shall utilize the most recent official results of the Listahanan household
assessment cycle and Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) poverty data. The DSWD
shall ensure that beneficiary identification is based on updated and validated data to
reflect current household conditions. In the event that a new cycle is ongoing or
pending validation, the DSWD may provisionally use the latest available official results,
subject to immediate updating upon completion of the subsequent cycle.
SEC. 7. Phased Implementation. – The Walang Gutom Program shall be implemented
in phases, beginning with the bottom one (1) million households nationwide based on
the most recent Listahanan and Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) poverty data.
Priority shall be given to households with pregnant or lactating women, children under
five (5) years old, persons with disabilities, and senior citizens. Expansion to additional
households shall be subject to evaluation and availability of funds.
LGUs shall assist in program dissemination, field validation, grievance handling, and
beneficiary profiling, in coordination with the DSWD.
SEC. 8. Local Government Counterpart and Participation. – Local Government Units
(LGUs) shall be encouraged to provide a counterpart fund for the implementation of
the Walang Gutom Program within their jurisdictions. The national government may
provide matching grants or incentives to LGUs that commit resources or implement
complementary hunger mitigation programs with adjustments for household
composition and nutritional vulnerability, including higher allocations for families with
pregnant or lactating women, based on Department of Health nutritional standards.
SEC. 9. Integration with Existing Social Protection Programs. – The DSWD shall
ensure that the Walang Gutom Program complements and integrates with other
national programs such as the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps), the
Department of Education's school-based feeding programs, and the Department of
Agriculture’s food distribution initiatives to ensure coherence, avoid duplication, and
maximize impact.
SEC. 10. Lead Implementing Agency. – The DSWD shall be the primary implementing
agency and may coordinate with the following:
   a. Department of Health (DOH);
   b. Department of Agriculture (DA);
   c. Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG);
   d. National Nutrition Council (NNC);
   e. Local Government Units (LGUs); and
   f. Non-Government Organizations and Civil Society Partners.
SEC. 11. Funding. – The amount necessary to implement the program shall be
charged against the annual appropriations of the DSWD. Thereafter, such sums as
may be necessary for its continued implementation shall be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act (GAA).
The DSWD shall be authorized to receive grants, donations, and other forms of
assistance from public or private, domestic or international sources.
SEC. 12. Private Sector Engagement and Alternative Financing. – The Department of
Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) is authorized to enter into partnerships with
private sector entities for the co-financing, support, or implementation of the Walang
Gutom Program. Contributions by private partners may be recognized as part of their
Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) programs and may be eligible for tax incentives
under existing laws.
Partner merchants shall offer eligible food items that meet the nutritional standards set
by the Department of Health (DOH) and the National Nutrition Council (NNC).
SEC. 13. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Independent Assessment. – An independent
evaluation of the program’s effectiveness, efficiency, and impact shall be conducted
annually in partnership with recognized institutions such as the Philippine Institute for
Development Studies (PIDS), academic organizations, or civil society networks.
Results shall guide subsequent phases and improvements of the program.
SEC. 14. Penal Clause. – Any person who commits fraud or misrepresentation to
receive or misuse program benefits shall be penalized in accordance with relevant
laws, including but not limited to the Revised Penal Code, the Anti-Graft and Corrupt
Practices Act, and RA 10175 or the Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012.
SEC. 15. Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DSWD, in consultation with stakeholders, shall
promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of this
Act.
SEC. 16. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional,
the remainder shall not be affected and shall continue to be in full force and effect.
SEC. 17. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules and
regulations inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or amended accordingly.
SEC. 18. Effectivity Clause. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
