                          EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The education system in the Philippines has undergone numerous
reforms throughout its long history, reflecting continuing efforts to improve
access, governance, and outcomes. Among the most significant of these was
the 1991 Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM), which
recommended the restructuring of the system to enhance efficiency and
accountability. This led to the tri-focalization of education in 1994,
establishing separate agencies for basic education, higher education, and
technical-vocational training.

      While this structure enabled each sub-sector to focus on its respective
mandate, it also resulted in fragmentation, coordination gaps, and
inconsistencies in policies and programs. Recognizing these challenges,
subsequent reforms sought to establish mechanisms for alignment, including
the creation of the National Coordinating Council for Education (NCCE) in
2000. However, these efforts were not sustained, leaving the system without a
permanent and institutionalized body to ensure coherence across all levels of
education.

      As a result, the Philippine education system continues to face issues in
coordination, efficiency, and overall performance. The absence of a central
coordinating mechanism has limited the alignment of programs with national
development goals and constrained long-term, strategic planning.




                                                                                 1
       This bill seeks to address these gaps by establishing a National
Education Council that will serve as the central body for coordination,
planning, monitoring, and evaluation across the three education sub-sectors.
It aims to align policies and programs, promote long-term strategic direction,
and strengthen collaboration among government agencies, local government
units, the private sector, and other key stakeholders.

      By institutionalizing a unified and permanent coordinating mechanism,
this measure seeks to reduce fragmentation, improve governance, and
enhance the overall effectiveness of the Philippine education system.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is
earnestly sought.

 7      promote the right of all citizens to quality education at all levels, take appropriate
 8      steps to make such education accessible to all, and establish, maintain, and support
 9      a complete, adequate, and integrated system of education relevant to the needs of
10      the people and society, the State hereby adopts the following policies:
11             (a)    The State shall place education at the center of its development
12                    policies, guided by a clear and long-term set of goals in the form of a

 8          SEC. 6. The National Education Agenda. – The national education
 9   agenda shall be anchored on national development plans and establish the
10   country’s strategic vision for education, long-term and medium-term goals, plans
11   and priorities, strategies for a coordinated and coherent implementation, and
12   recommendations on the corresponding investments and appropriations in the form
13   of a national education agenda. It shall provide a roadmap for improving education
14   governance through clear-cut delineation and complementation of education
15   policies and programs across the three sub-sectors and other stakeholders. It shall
16   also identify areas and strategies to stimulate the role of other government agencies
17   and LGUs in the attainment of its vision.
18          The national education agenda shall have a minimum of five (5)-year
19   horizon, to be updated annually and reviewed periodically by the NEDCO. The
20   three sub-sectors, concerned government agencies, and concerned stakeholders
21   shall strictly comply with the national education agenda and shall incorporate its
22   pertinent policies in their respective programs.
23          The national education agenda shall be developed by the NEDCO, in
24   consultation with other relevant government agencies, LGUs, and other
25   stakeholders. It shall be completed within six (6) months from the effectivity of
26   this Act.
