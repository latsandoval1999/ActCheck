                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

       According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), cancer consistently
ranks among the top three leading causes of mortality in the country. A major factor
contributing to this alarming reality is the late diagnosis of cancer cases, which not
only reduces survival rates but also imposes severe financial hardship on affected
families. Studies reveal that treatment of late-stage cancer can consume more than
30 percent of a household’s income, often pushing families into financial
catastrophe.

      In contrast, high-income countries report higher cancer incidence but lower
mortality, owing to robust early detection and prevention programs. Evidence shows
that early detection significantly improves survival rates while reducing overall
treatment costs, making it both a life-saving and cost-effective public health strategy.

        This bill seeks to institutionalize annual community-based cancer early
 detection programs through risk profiling, barangay-level screening, and awareness
 campaigns across municipalities. In addition, it aims to strengthen the role of
 Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) by providing capacity development and training,
 enabling them to effectively assist in cancer prevention, screening promotion, health
 navigation, and patient support.

       By integrating grassroots health workers into systematic cancer risk detection
 and awareness efforts, the State fulfills its mandate under the Constitution to protect
 and promote the right to health, while complementing the objectives of Republic Act
 No. 11215, or the National Integrated Cancer Control Act.

       In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines
in Congress assembled:


SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Cancer Risk Screening
and Awareness Act.”


SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill health


                                                                                1
consciousness among them. Recognizing cancer as one of the leading causes of
mortality and morbidity in the Philippines, the State shall:

  i.      Strengthen early detection and prevention through systematic cancer risk
          profiling and evidence-based screening programs;
 ii.      Ensure equitable access to free or subsidized cancer screening, particularly for
          indigent and high-risk populations;
iii.      Promote awareness and culturally sensitive health education campaigns on
          cancer prevention and early detection;
iv.       Support and capacitate community health workers to effectively assist in
          cancer risk navigation and patient support; and
 v.       Complement and reinforce the objectives of Republic Act No. 11215, or the
          National Integrated Cancer Control Act.



SECTION 3. Coverage. – The initial coverage of this Act shall focus on cancers
with high prevalence and potential for early detection and prevention, namely:


       A. Breast cancer;
       B. Cervical cancer;
       C. Colorectal (colon) cancer; and
       D. Prostate cancer.

This coverage shall be subject to periodic review every three (3) years by the
Department of Health (DOH), upon recommendation of the National Integrated
Cancer Control Council, to expand the list based on emerging evidence and national
burden of disease.



                                                                                        2
SECTION 4. Cancer Risk Profiling and Registry. The DOH, in coordination with
local government units (LGUs), provincial, city, and municipal health offices,
barangay health centers, academic institutions, and other concerned agencies, shall
establish a comprehensive cancer risk profiling system nationwide.

Subsequently, a Municipality- or City-Wide Cancer Risk Registry shall be created
to record prevalence trends, screening results, and follow-up care: Provided, That
patient confidentiality shall be strictly observed pursuant to Republic Act No. 10173,
otherwise known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012. Anonymized and aggregated data
may be authorized for national research, monitoring, and policy development.



SECTION 5. Cancer Screening. – Through the implementing agencies lead by
DOH, in coordination with Local Government Units (LGU), municipal or city health
centers, Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) and Rural Health Units (RHUs), shall
conduct regular and periodic barangay-based cancer screening, free of charge or
subsidized; Provided, That priority shall be granted to the indigent and high-risk
population.

Health facilities conducting screening shall establish a referral mechanism to higher-
level facilities for confirmatory diagnosis and treatment. Referral shall include
linkage to PhilHealth benefits and other financial or logistical support services.




SECTION 6. Cancer Awareness and Education. –The DOH, in collaboration with
local government units (LGUs), civil society, and professional medical associations



                                                                                     3
shall accordingly develop and integrate a culturally sensitive health education
campaign addressing:


   a) Cancer risk factors and lifestyle prevention;
   b) Early signs and symptoms of cancer; and
   c) Importance of early detection and treatment adherence.


Campaigns shall be evaluated periodically to assess effectiveness and reach,
especially among vulnerable and rural communities.


SECTION 7. Capacity Enhancement for Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) –
This Act shall promulgate capacity development among BHWs, including training
on:
   A. Cancer prevention and health promotion;
   B. Screening assistance and navigation;
   C. Psychosocial support; and
   D. Referral pathways and linkage to financial assistance programs.


SECTION 8. Appropriations — The amount necessary for the initial
implementation of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the
DOH. Thereafter, the necessary sums for its continued operation and maintenance
shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act (GAA) under the budget
of the DOH.


SECTION 9: Monitoring and Evaluation – The DOH shall establish a monitoring
and evaluation (M&E) framework to assess the effectiveness, coverage, and


                                                                                   4
outcomes of cancer risk profiling, screening, and awareness programs. Results shall
be consolidated into an annual report submitted to Congress, the Department of the
Interior and Local Government (DILG), and the National Integrated Cancer Control
Council.


SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulation (IRR) – Within one hundred
eighty (180) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DOH, in consultation with the
DILG, the Philippine Cancer Center, the National Integrated Cancer Control
Council, LGUs, and other relevant stakeholders, shall promulgate the necessary
rules and regulations to carry out the provisions of this Act.


SECTION 11. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision of
this Act shall be held unconstitutional or invalid, other parts of provisions thereof
not affected shall thereby continue to be in full force and effect.


SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. - All laws, decrees, executive orders,
proclamations, rules and regulations, and other issuances or parts thereof contrary to
or inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.


SECTION 13. Effectivity. - This Act shall take fifteen (15) days after its publication
in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
