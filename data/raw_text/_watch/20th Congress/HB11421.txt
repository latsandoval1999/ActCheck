20                                                EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   In 2024, only 48.8%, of Filipino households had access to the Internet at home. While
23   access was highest in the National Capital Region and Central Luzon at 68.7% and
24   61.3%, respectively, only 21.2% of households in the Zamboanga Peninsula and 27.7%
25   in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao had access to the Internet
26   at home. This disparity is reflected in public education, where 39,972, or 69.3%, of
27   schools were reported to have below ideal student to device ratios.
28
29   These figures highlight the continuing need to bridge the digital divide and ensure that
30   Filipino learners, regardless of where they live or their economic circumstances, have
31   meaningful access to technology in education.
32
33   Hence, this proposed measure seeks to institutionalize the Public Schools of the Future
34   in Technology Program by providing public schools with reliable Internet connectivity,
35   appropriate ICT devices and equipment, digital learning resources, and adequately
36   trained teachers. It likewise promotes digital literacy, online safety, and the responsible
37   use of technology in teaching and learning.
38
39   Technology should serve as a tool to improve education, not become another source
40   of inequality. By strengthening digital infrastructure and ensuring that teachers and
41   learners are equipped to use technology effectively, this measure seeks to give every
42   Filipino student a fairer opportunity to learn and prepare for the future.
43
44   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

21                                 AN ACT
22      ESTABLISHING THE PUBLIC SCHOOLS OF THE FUTURE IN TECHNOLOGY
23                   AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR
24
25   Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
26   Congress assembled:
27
28         Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Public Schools of the
29   Future in Technology (PSOFT) Act”
30                                        ARTICLE I
31                               POLICIES AND OBJECTIVES
32
33          Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. — Cognizant of the necessity of adopting
34   innovative technologies to attain development in more positive and transformative
35   ways, the State shall:

36   (a) Uphold the constitutional mandate to accord the highest priority to quality education
37   by providing appropriate mechanisms and resources to ensure the enjoyment of quality
38   education and services by all Filipino students, regardless of social and economic
39   status;

40   (b) Harness technological innovations, approaches, and instruments in the teaching
41   and learning processes that would enhance the performance of students and teachers;
42   .

43   (c) Provide all learners with the opportunity to enhance individual capabilities and
44   knowledge through information and communications technology (ICT);
 1   (d) Create a system that shall promote and regulate the use of digital technology in
 2   public education;

 3   (e) Enhance the learning experience of students by providing them access to digitized
 4   copies of necessary reference materials and digital and online content through the use
 5   of ICT and other related technologies for the advancement of education in the country;

 6   (f) Provide an accessible complementary mode of learning to allow students to continue
 7   their education during national health emergencies;

 8   (g) Encourage the wider participation of private groups, local government units (LGUs),
 9   and community-based organizations in content generation and the utilization of the
10   broader spheres of science and technology; and

11   (h) Promote public and private partnership in improving access to quality education and
12   lifelong learning.

13          Sec. 3. Objectives. — This Act shall pursue the following objectives:

14   (a) Establish an Interagency Taskforce to craft and implement the road map for the
15   PSOFT;

16   (b) Institutionalize a program for the integration of digital technology and innovation in
17   public basic education through the PSOFT Road Map;

18   (c) Identify key components of the PSOFT Road Map; and

19   (d) Provide a public-private partnership (PPP) mechanism to facilitate the attainment of
20   the objectives of the PSOFT Road Map.

21                                       ARTICLE II
22                                  DEFINITION OF TERMS

23          Sec. 4. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

24   (a) Fourth Industrial Revolution — refers to the ongoing automationof traditional
25   manufacturing and industrial practices using modern smart technology by integrating
26   advances in large-scale machine-to-machine communication, artificial intelligence,
27   genetic engineering, the Internet of Things, and other technologies for increased
28   automation, improved communication and self-monitoring, and production of smart
29   machines that can analyze and diagnose issues without the need for human
30   intervention;

31   (b) High Touch, High Tech Framework — refers to a policy framework supported by the
32   United Nations Education Commission that espouses the need for educators to find a
33   balance between the more modern electronically-delivered and digitally-supported
34   method of teaching and the traditional face-to-face mode, the latter being teaching
 1   methods that are founded on a teacher’s capability to identify, develop, and improve an
 2   individual student’s academic weakness;

 3   (c) Internet safety policy — refers to an age-appropriate, research-based policy that
 4   regulates the safe, ethical, and responsible use of the Internet in public education,
 5   promotes an informed and critical understanding of the Internet, and the education of
 6   children and communities about how to prevent or respond to problems or dangers
 7   related to the Internet and new media; and

 8   (d) New media — refers to emerging digital, computerized, or network information and
 9   communication technologies that often have interactive capacities, which includes e-
10   mail, instant messaging, text messaging, web sites, blogs, interactive gaming, social
11   media, cellphones, and mobile devices.

12                                 ARTICLE III
13                 PUBLIC SCHOOLS OF THE FUTURE IN TECHNOLOGY

14           Sec. 5. Public Schools of the Future in Technology (PSOFT) Program. — There
15   is hereby instituted a PSOFT Program in all public elementary and secondary schools.

16          The PSOFT Program shall adopt the following principles upon administering
17   basic education in the public school system:

18         (a) Establish a system of upgrading and replacing outdated technology;

19         (b) Broaden and maximize the use of ICT to aid learning;

20         (c) Provide technical support to maintain ICT and other technology;

21         (d) Train and support teachers and students on ICT and other technology;

22         (e) Develop educators to use ICT effectively in the classroom; and

23         (f) Ensure that the balance between modern and traditional methods of teaching
24         are equally utilized to obtain optimum learning outcomes.

25          Sec. 6. Creation of the PSOFT Inter-Agency Task Force (PSOFT-IATF). —
26   There is hereby created the PSOFT-IATF.

27            The PSOFT-IATF shall, in consultation with concerned non-government entities,
28   including private companies, non-profit organizations, and private education
29   institutions, develop the PSOFT Road Map, a consolidation of development plans,
30   programs, and projects to be implemented by various departments and agencies of the
31   national government for the purpose of advancing digital technology and innovation in
32   public basic education.

33           The PSOFT-IATF shall be composed of the Secretary of the Department of
34   Education (DepEd) as Chairperson and the following as members: (1) Secretary of the
 1   Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT); (2) Secretary of
 2   the Department of Science and Technology (DOST); (3) Secretary of the National
 3   Economic and Development Authority (NEDA); (4) Secretary of the Department of
 4   Budget and Management (DBM); and (5) Director General of the Philippine Space
 5   Agency (PhilSA).

 6          The PSOFT-IATF shall finalize the PSOFT Road Map within twelve (12) months
 7   from the effectivity of this Act, in accordance with national development goals. The
 8   PSOFT Road Map shall be incorporated in the Philippine Development Plan.

 9              The Secretaries of the DepEd, DICT and the Director General of the PhilSA
10   may recommend for the creation of positions as they deem necessary for the effective
11   implementation of this Act, subject to the approval of the DBM, in accordance with civil
12   service laws, rules and regulations.

13           The Department of Social Welfare and Development shall formulate a program
14   extending assistance to marginalized and disadvantaged students to ensure that they
15   may fully participate in the PSOFT Road Map.

16            Sec. 7. PSOFT Road Map. — The PSOFT Road Map shall serve as the basis
17   for programs and projects aimed at improving the quality of education through the use
18   of digital technology.

19           The PSOFT Road Map shall be subject to review and amendment, if necessary,
20   every three (3) years.

21            Sec. 8. Components of the Roadmap. — The PSOFT Road Map shall include
22   the following:

23   (a) Physical and Technological Infrastructure

24         (1) Universal Access to Internet for Public Schools

25         (2) Annual Investment Program for ICT Devices, Tools, and Equipment

26   (b) Enhanced Digital Education Program

27         (1) National Human Resource Development

28         (2) Training and Development of DepEd personnel

29   (c) Digital Reforms in Public School Administration

30         (1) Internet Safety Policy

31         (2) Strengthening the Enhanced Basic Education Information System (EBEIS)

32         (3) Digital Alternative Learning System (ALS)
 1   (d) Development of Online Learning Portals

 2         (1) DepEd E-Learning Resources

 3         (2) Public Online Library

 4                                  ARTICLE IV
 5                 PHYSICAL AND TECHNOLOGICAL INFRASTRUCTURE

 6           Sec. 9. Universal Access to the Internet in Public Schools. — All public basic
 7   education schools shall be provided access to the Internet for the purpose of reducing
 8   the disparity in computing and information technology knowledge among public school
 9   learners and raising the standards of Philippine education.

10          In addition to traditional means of accessing the Internet, such as commercially
11   available connections or existing land-based initiatives of government programs to
12   expand access to the Internet, the PSOFT Road Map shall exhaust all options in
13   ensuring internet connectivity for all public schools.

14          In order to provide the remote areas with digital connectivity, the PSOFT Road
15   Map shall provide cost-effective options for establishing a telecommunication satellite
16   or installing fiber optic technology infrastructure as part of the National Government
17   Program under the DICT, DOST, and PhilSA.

18          The necessary funding for the conduct of studies to determine the feasibility of
19   implementing a Telecommunication Satellite Program, through building, purchasing, or
20   leasing of a satellite, shall be appropriated in accordance with the PSOFT Road Map.

21           Sec. 10. ICT Devices, Tools, and Equipment. — The DepEd shall provide the
22   following equipment and services in all public elementary and secondary schools:
23   personal computers (PCs), laptops, tablets, learning devices, digital classroom
24   equipment, and internet and hybrid intranet connectivity.

25           The PSOFT Road Map shall include a plan for the procurement of PCs, laptops
26   or tablets for the educational needs of all elementary and secondary public schools. All
27   learners shall be provided with the necessary learning devices as stipulated in the
28   PSOFT Road Map. Should budgetary constraints prevent the attainment of such target,
29   the national government shall endeavor to meet a ratio of one (1) device for every two
30   (2) learners from Grades 4-6 to ensure that learners can make use of productivity tools
31   in the conduct of regular classes. The national government shall also maintain one (1)
32   device for every fifteen (15) learners from Grades 7-12 to ensure that learners can avail
33   of weekly classes on ICT topics that require higher computing power.

34          The PSOFT Road Map shall provide for the establishment of functional digital
35   classrooms in all public schools. Digital classrooms shall be equipped with digital
36   boards, computers, wireless router, multimedia projector, printer, and other necessary
37   tools and devices.
 1           The PSOFT Road Map shall likewise provide for stable and reliable internet and
 2   hybrid intranet connectivity. Educators in public schools shall make use of ICT and
 3   hybrid intranet connectivity as presentation tools to complement traditional teaching
 4   methods. The DepEd shall, with the technical assistance of DICT, develop an
 5   administrative system in all public schools utilizing hybrid intranet connectivity in order
 6   to enhance organizational management and administration, and streamline front office
 7   procedures.

 8         Sec. 11. PSOFT Pilot Schools. — The national government shall establish
 9   PSOFT pilot schools in the academic year following the effectivity of this Act. The pilot
10   schools shall be located in Metro Manila, Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao and shall fully
11   comply with the requirements of Article IV in this Act, as follows:

12          (a) The pilot school shall have full, unrestricted access to the internet and for this
13          purpose shall be equipped with wireless technology or such other modes of
14          connectivity that may be developed: Provided, That school administrators shall
15          closely supervise the access of learners to content in order to protect them from
16          inappropriate, violent, or immoral material;

17          (b) Teachers shall be adequately trained in all technologies that the pilot schools
18          shall adopt for the use of learners;

19          (c) All pilot schools shall employ trained information technology specialists to
20          assist faculty and learners; and

21          (d) All pilot schools shall provide each teacher and learner with the appropriate
22          ICT devices, tools, and equipment to ensure optimum satisfaction in both the
23          teaching and learning experiences.

24           The DepEd shall, in coordination with NEDA, conduct an impact study of the
25   pilot schools to determine its potential in improving the quality of education. Additional
26   pilot schools may be established in the following years, subject to the availability of
27   funds and through cooperative arrangements with LGUs and the public sector.

28                                     ARTICLE V
29                             ENHANCED DIGITAL EDUCATION

30           Sec. 12. National Human Resource Development. — Students in public
31   elementary and secondary schools must be prepared for the disruptive technologies
32   that come with the Fourth Industrial Revolution and have a significant impact on skills
33   requirements and on creating a range of opportunities for them in the future.
34           To better equip students to overcome the challenges enabled by ever-
35   increasing technological change and to improve student engagement and achievement,
36   they shall be provided with introductory courses on foundational technology that will
37   enhance their future competencies. The DepEd shall determine the content and mode
38   of delivery of such foundational technology courses and shall introduce them in the
39   curriculum in the school year following the effectivity of this Act.
 1           To allow students to acquire the appropriate aptitudes and competencies on
 2   these courses, the PSOFT Road Map shall provide for the establishment of innovation
 3   hubs in every school to enable students to hone their skills and techniques using a wide
 4   variety of physical and digital tools and equipment. The innovation hubs shall allow
 5   teachers and learners to carry out technology-based projects that address local
 6   problems in the community.
 7           In addition to the standard ICT curriculum taught for basic education in the public
 8   schools, the DepEd shall endeavor to include the following subject matters in its
 9   curriculum:
10          (a) Respect for and protection of personal information on the Internet;
11          (b) Understanding computer security systems;
12          (c) Online gender sensitivity;
13          (d) Proper use of social media and social networking;
14          (e) Responsible and ethical use of the Internet, photographic equipment, smart
15          phones, and other equipment of similar nature; and
16          (f) Insights of Republic Act No. 10175, or the “Cybercrime Prevention Act of
17          2012” and other related legislation.
18           The DepEd shall, in coordination with the DICT, enjoin the use of the latter’s ICT
19   Literacy Competency and Development Bureau and its relevant specialized ICT
20   subjects to be utilized as elective courses in Grades 11-12.
21           Sec. 13. Training and Development of DepEd Personnel. — The PSOFT Road
22   Map shall determine the training and development needs of school administrators,
23   teachers, and personnel with respect to ICT.
24           To ensure that teachers are technologically knowledgeable and well-equipped
25   with the right aptitudes and competencies, the DepEd shall establish an Education
26   Technology Division that shall implement teaching programs and utilize technology-
27   based instruction aids for the effective and efficient performance of teachers under the
28   PSOFT Road Map. The DICT shall extend its support in the implementation of training
29   for teachers on ICT, through its ICT Literacy Competency and Development Bureau.
30           The National Educators Academy of the Philippines shall carry out training
31   programs for educators in public schools and integrate ICT in classroom learning and
32   instruction to highlight the professional and educational benefits that may be derived
33   from personally investing in ICT.
34                                          ARTICLE VI
35                     DEVELOPMENT OF ONLINE LEARNING PORTALS
36
37            Sec. 14. DepEd E-Learning Resources. — The DepEd shall develop an online
38   portal that provides access or links to Internet learning resources. The online portal shall
39   include e-books and videos, learning materials and teachers guides, materials for senior
40   high school subjects ranging from math, disaster readiness, and science, materials on
41   science, agriculture, livelihood and information technology, interactive exercises on
42   math, science, history, and economics.
43            The DepEd shall develop its own library to ensure the quality and safety of
44   knowledge and information made available to learners.
 1            Sec. 15. Public Online Library. — The PSOFT Road Map shall include the
 2   establishment of the Public Online Library.
 3              The DepEd, DICT, DOST, the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), and
 4   the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) shall, in partnership
 5   with the National Library of the Philippines (NLP), the National Book Development
 6   Board (NBDB), and other concerned government agencies, develop and maintain the
 7   Public Online Library, a repository of educational materials and digitized copies of books
 8   and publications suitable for Filipino students and researchers that shall enrich the
 9   learning experience by complementing the textbooks and reference materials
10   prescribed for the elementary and secondary levels. The Public Online Library shall be
11   directly connected to the website of the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA). The DepEd
12   and the NLP shall have joint custody over the digitized copies of textbooks and
13   references in the Public Online Library which shall be jointly managed by the DepEd
14   and the DICT. In this regard, the DICT shall ensure the security of the online repository
15   system to guarantee its integrity.
16            The DepEd may solicit additional reference materials and publications from the
17   NLP, other agencies of the government, and the private sector to augment its
18   resources.
19            The authors of the digitized books and publications shall be covered by the
20   protection provided for in R.A 8293, as amended, otherwise known as the “Intellectual
21   Property Code of the Philippines.” The DepEd and all concerned agencies shall ensure
22   that the authors of the digitized books and publications shall be properly remunerated
23   in accordance with law.
24                                          ARTICLE VII
25                  DIGITAL REFORM IN PUBLIC SCHOOL ADMINISTRATION
26            Sec. 16. Internet Safety Policy. — To advance the objective of providing a safe
27   and secured education program under the PSOFT Road Map, the DepEd shall, within
28   six (6) months after the effectivity of this Act, formulate and enforce an internet safety
29   policy in order to regulate students” access to content especially against visual
30   depictions that are obscene, pornographic, and harmful to minors. In this regard, the
31   internet safety policy shall be implemented to accomplish the following objectives:
32           (a) Development and implementation of Internet safety education, including safe
33           access to educational technology, multimedia and interactive applications, and
34           online resources;
35           (b) Development of online risk interventions for learners and children;
36           (c) Training and support to stakeholders on peer-driven Internet safety education
37           initiatives;
38           (d) Undertaking of researches on online risks to form the bases for Internet safety
39           education syllabi and instructional material development;
40           (e) Development and implementation of a public education campaign to promote
41           awareness of online risks to learners and children; and
42           (f) Education of parents to augment school initiatives on safe Internet use.
43            Sec. 17. Strengthening the Enhanced Basic Education Information System
44   (EBEIS). — The EBEIS shall be the central school database of the DepEd and it shall
 1   contain pertinent personal data and school records of students. It shall be continually
 2   improved to ensure the efficient management of school records.
 3            School administrators and personnel shall be trained on the operation and
 4   access of the EBEIS and be given access to student records and other related data in
 5   the EBEIS such as a student’s school attendance record, records pertaining to the
 6   character of a student, the level of the student’s digital literacy and proficiency, grade
 7   level, examination scores, school performance, immunization and health records,
 8   among others, for purposes of recording student biographical data, handling
 9   admissions and discharges, and transferring to other schools.
10            Any person who is caught illegally altering, falsifying, or misusing student
11   information in the EBEIS shall be sanctioned in accordance with R.A. 10173, otherwise
12   known as the “Data Privacy Act of 2012.”
13            Sec. 18. Digital Alternative Learning System. — The DepEd shall institute a
14   digital format of the ALS in the PSOFT Road Map. To attain this objective, the DepEd
15   shall provide professional training to teachers and non-teaching personnel involved in
16   this program and formulate the necessary guidelines for its implementation.

17                                ARTICLE VIII
18                PARTNERSHIP MECHANISM AND ROLES OF PARTNERS

19            Sec. 19. Partnership Mechanism and Roles of Partners. —In the furtherance of
20   the PSOFT Road Map, the DepEd may coordinate and enter into an agreement or
21   contract with other government agencies, LGUS, private and non-profit agencies,
22   institutions or organizations duly accredited or recognized by the government, civil
23   society, business and industrial sector, and other concerned sectors.
24            The PSOFT Road Map shall define and delineate the areas of responsibility of
25   government agencies in conformity with their respective charters and mandates. For
26   purposes of this Act, these agencies shall have the following roles:
27            (a) The DepEd shall be the lead government agency responsible for the proper
28   and effective implementation of the various provisions of this Act and ensure
29   coordination and collaboration among agencies of the government.
30            (b) The DICT shall provide advice on matters relating to ICT and closely
31   coordinate and collaborate in the implementation of this Act. It shall undertake the
32   installation, maintenance, and operation of hybrid intranet connectivity in all public basic
33   education schools, provide an efficient and reliable broadband connection, assist in the
34   implementation of training and development for teachers concerning ICT and
35   ICTassisted instructions, and coordinate and collaborate in the provision of digital
36   connectivity in public schools.
37           (c) The PhilSA shall advise and collaborate with the DepEd on matters
38   concerning the use of space technology and relevant space-enabled services to
39   implement the provisions of this Act.
40              (d) The DOST, CHED, TESDA, DILG, NLP, NBDB, PSA, the LGUs, and other
41   concerned government agencies mentioned in this Act shall provide support and
42   assistance for the attainment of the purposes of the PSOFT.
 1           Sec. 20. Public-Private Partnerships (PPP) for PSOFT Program. — The DepEd
 2   may pursue PPPs from any of the modalities under R.A. 6957, as amended by R.A
 3   7718, entitled, “An Act Authorizing the Financing, Construction, Operation and
 4   Maintenance of Infrastructure Projects by the Private Sector, and For the Other
 5   Purposes.”
 6           To this end, the DepEd shall, in consultation with the PPP Center of the
 7   Philippines created under Executive Order No. 8, series of 2010, entitled, “Reorganizing
 8   and Renaming the Build-Operate and Transfer (BOT) Center to the Public-Private
 9   Partnership (PPP) Center of the Philippines and Transferring its Attachment from the
10   Department of Trade and Industry to the National Economic and Development Authority
11   and For Other Purposes,” establish a PPP Unit to coordinate all matters relating to PPP,
12   in accordance with the PSOFT Road Map. It shall likewise coordinate with the Project
13   Development and Monitoring Facility of the PPP Center in the conduct of feasibility
14   studies and the preparation of tender documents for the PPP projects of the DepEd.

15                                    ARTICLE IX
16                         AUTHORITY TO RECEIVE DONATIONS

17           Sec. 21. Authority to Receive Donations. — The DepEd is authorized to accept
18   grants, contributions, donations, endowments, or bequests in cash or in kind from local
19   and foreign sources in support of the development and implementation of this Act,
20   subject to the regular auditing guidelines and procedures: Provided, That in case of
21   donations from foreign sources, acceptance thereof shall be subject to existing
22   government rules and regulations. The proceeds of such grants, contributions,
23   donations, endowments, or bequests shall be used to finance the following:
24          (a) Establishment of pilot schools as provided in Section 11 of this Act;
25          (b) Research, development, and utilization of emerging digital technologies;
26          (c) Provisions of computer laptop units and improvement of ICT infrastructure,
27          including hardware and Internet connectivity;
28          (d) Training and capacity building; and
29          (e) Conduct of other activities necessary to carry out the objectives of this Act.
30
31                              ARTICLE X
32    THE JOINT CONGRESSIONAL OVERSIGHT COMMITTEE AND REPORTORIAL
33                          RESPONSIBILITIES
34
35           Sec. 22. Joint Congressional Oversight. — Upon the effectivity of this Act, a
36   joint congressional oversight committee to be known as the Joint Oversight
37   Committee on the PSOFT Program or JOC-PSOFT, is hereby constituted. The JOC-
38   PSOFT shall be composed of ten (10) members with the chairpersons of the
39   Committee on Basic Education, Arts and Culture and the Committee on Finance of
40   the Senate, and the Committee on Basic Education and Culture and the Committee
41   on Appropriations of the House of Representatives as members and three (3)
42   additional members from each House to be designated by the Senate President and
43   the Speaker of the House of Representatives, respectively. The members of the
 1   Committee from each House shall have at least one (1) member from the minority
 2   party or coalition.

 3           Sec. 23. Annual Report. — The DepEd shall submit a report to both Houses of
 4   Congress, or upon the request by the JOC-PSOFT, providing a detailed account of
 5   the status of the implementation of this Act.

 6                                      ARTICLE XI
 7                                   FINAL PROVISIONS

 8           Sec. 24. Appropriations. — The Secretaries of Education and Information and
 9   Communications Technology and the Director General of PHilSA shall immediately
10   include in their respective programs the implementation of this Act, the funding of
11   which shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
12           The funding for the implementation of PSOFT Pilot Schools under Section 11
13   hereof shall be charged against the current year’s appropriation of the DepEd.
14           Sec. 25. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within one hundred-twenty
15   (120) days from the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd and DICT shall, in coordination
16   with other concerned agencies of the government and the private stakeholders,
17   promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective implementation of
18   this Act.
19           Sec. 26. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
20   unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provision not affected thereby shall
21   remain in full force and effect.
22           Sec. 27. Repealing Clause. — All laws, executive orders, presidential decrees,
23   proclamations, rules, regulations, issuances and enactments or parts thereof
24   inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
25           Sec. 28. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
26   publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
