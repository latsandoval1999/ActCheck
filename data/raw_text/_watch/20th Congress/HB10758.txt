  AN ACT EXTENDING THE SIX PERCENT REAL PROPERTY TAX CAPPING
    PERIOD AND THE TAX AMNESTY PERIOD TO FOUR YEARS UNDER
 REPUBLIC ACT NO. 12001, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "REAL PROPERTY
   VALUATION AND ASSESSMENT REFORM ACT", AMENDING FOR THE
               PURPOSE SECTIONS 29 AND 30 THEREOF



                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Republic Act No. 12001, also known as the Real Property Valuation and Assessment
Reform Act (RPVARA), was borne out of the need to optimize the government’s real property
tax collection.

        Aside from promoting a nationally consistent real property valuation based on
international standards and the market value as single base for tax assessment, it also mandates
the development and maintenance of a comprehensive, up-to-date electronic database of real
property transactions.

        Notably, it improves revenue generation at the local level, further enhancing local
government units’ capacity to pursue projects and programs which are responsive to the needs
of their constituents.

       The transition period, however, provided in the RPVARA may be too short. Concerns
have been raised regarding the need to further study its impacts beyond property valuation, such
as its possible effects on housing affordability, economic competitiveness, and property
ownership, to name a few.

       With the transition period having already lapsed, the policy status quo could do more
harm than good.

        Under the directive of President Marcos Jr. in his fourth Legislative-Executive
Development Advisory Council (LEDAC) to ease the financial burden of the public, including
private property owners and businesses, this measure proposes to extend the six percent real
property tax capping period and the tax amnesty period to four years, to avoid the abrupt
additional property costs and allow for smoother and gradual transition to the new valuation
system.


       In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled

SECTION 1. Section 29 of Republic Act No. 12001 is hereby amended to read as follows:

    “SEC. 29. Transitory Guidelines. x x x

    For the [first year] FIRST FOUR (4) YEARS of effectivity of the approved SMV
    in accordance to this Act, any increase in real property taxes shall be limited to a
    maximum of six percent (6%) of the real property taxes assessed on such properties
    prior to the effectivity of this Act: Provided, That the cap shall be applicable to each
    type of real property tax, including Special Education Fund, idle land tax, and other
    special levies in real property: Provided, further, That the LGU may enact an
    ordinance to impose a cap on the increase in real property taxes for the succeeding
    years.

    x x x”

SEC. 2. Section 30 of the same Act is hereby amended to read as follows:

    “SEC. 30. Grant of Tax Amnesty on Real Property Taxes and Special Levies on Real
    Property. x x x

    There is hereby granted a real property tax amnesty which shall cover penalties,
    surcharges, and interests from all unpaid real property taxes, including Special
    Education Fund, idle land tax, and other special levy taxes, prior to the effectivity of
    this Act: Provided, That the real property tax amnesty hereby authorized and granted
    can only be availed within a period of [two (2)]FOUR (4) years after the effectivity
    of this Act.
      x x x”

SEC. 3. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional, the
remainder not affected thereby shall remain in full force and effect.

SEC. 4. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, orders, rules, and regulations or parts thereof
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

SEC. 5. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in the
Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
