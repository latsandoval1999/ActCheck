                           AN ACT
 MANDATING THE FULL ACCOUNTING OF ALL DISASTER-RELIEF AID OR
DONATIONS RECEIVED BY THE PHILIPPINE GOVERNMENT FOR NATURAL
               AND HUMAN-INDUCED CALAMITIES

                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

      The Philippines is among the countries with the highest number of
calamities annually due to its geographic location along the Pacific Ring of Fire
and the typhoon belt. The country is highly vulnerable to earthquakes, volcanic
eruptions, typhoons, and severe flooding, which result in significant economic
and humanitarian challenges each year.

      The Department of Science and Technology (DOST) reports that an average
of 20 storms and typhoons enter the Philippine Area of Responsibility annually,
while the country also experiences between 100 and 150 earthquakes of varying
magnitudes. These recurring disasters necessitate robust mechanisms,
including immediate relief efforts and long-term recovery programs.

       Over the years, the resilience of the Filipinos in facing these disasters has
been strengthened by both local and international humanitarian aid. Donations
in the form of cash and in-kind assistance from foreign governments, multilateral
organizations, private institutions, and individuals have played a crucial role in
the disaster relief and recovery efforts. However, concerns regarding the
transparency, accountability, and proper utilization of these donations have
persisted, raising questions about their efficient deployment to affected
communities.
      The proposed Donation Transparency Act seeks to establish a legal
framework for the full disclosure and accounting of all disaster-related aid
received by the Philippine government. The measure aims to ensure that every
donation- whether from foreign entities or domestic sources-is properly
documented, tracked, and utilized strictly for its intended purposes.

      The bill is designed to enhance public trust in disaster-relief efforts by
eliminating opportunities for mismanagement and corruption in the handling of
donations. By ensuring that all aid is appropriately accounted for and disclosed,
the measure promotes good governance, transparency, and efficient disaster
response and recovery.

     In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly
sought.

                           AN ACT
 MANDATING THE FULL ACCOUNTING OF ALL DISASTER-RELIEF AID OR
DONATIONS RECEIVED BY THE PHILIPPINE GOVERNMENT FOR NATURAL
               AND HUMAN-INDUCED CALAMITIES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Donation
Transparency Act”.

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. It is hereby declared the policy of the State
to promote transparency and accountability in the receipt and utilization of
foreign and local donations for natural and human-induced calamities that
cause havoc and destruction in the country.

SECTION 3. Coverage. This Act shall cover all foreign and local donations
directed to the national government, government-owned or controlled
corporations (GOCCs), and local government units (LGUs) from all sources, such
as foreign governments, bilateral or multilateral organizations and institutions,
and private individuals or groups.

SECTION 4. Definition of Terms. For purposes of this Act, the following shall
refer to:

   (a) “Calamity” – a natural or human-induced disaster causing widespread
       human, material, economic or environmental losses to a community or
       society.
   (b) “Donations” – cash or non-cash aid or grants received by the government
       for disaster relief, recovery, or rehabilitation.

   (c) “Donor” – refers to foreign governments, bilateral or multilateral
       organizations and institutions, private individuals or groups who donate
       cash or non-cash aid or grants to the Philippine government for disaster
       relief or rehabilitation.

   (d) “Donee” – refers to departments, bureaus, and offices of the national
       government including constitutional offices, state universities and
       colleges, government-owned or -controlled corporations, and local
       government units that receive donations from various sources.

   (e) “Donation in Cash” – refers to cash assistance from a donor
       entity/individual to the Philippine government through any of its agencies
       or instrumentalities.

   (f) “Donations in Kind” – refers to assistance in kind such as, but not limited
       to, food, clothing, medicine, and equipment coming from the donor
       entity/individual to a specific national government agency or local
       government units for the purpose of disaster relief, recovery, and
       rehabilitation efforts.

SECTION 5. Donations. Departments, bureaus, and offices of the National
Government, including constitutional offices enjoying fiscal autonomy, and state
universities and colleges may accept donations in cash or in kind from various
sources, domestic or foreign, for purposes relevant to their respective functions,
and which shall be used to cover aid, relief and rehabilitation, repair and
reconstruction of permanent structures affected by natural and man-made
calamities. Such donations, whether in cash or in kind, shall be deemed
automatically appropriated.

SECTION 6. Utilization and Treatment of Donations. Donations shall be
utilized in accordance with the purpose identified by the donor. Donations for a
specific purpose, as identified by the donor, shall be treated as trust receipts:
Provided, in case the donor does not identify a specific project or activity to be
funded, such donation shall be considered as calamity aid for the use of the
Department of Social Welfare and Development: Provided, further, that
donations intended for program support for calamities in general shall be booked
as income of the National Government or local government units, as the case
may be.

SECTION 7. Cash Donations. All cash donations shall be deposited under the
account of the Bureau of Treasury (BTr), except when the donation is made
directly to the agencies, in which case the done-agency shall inform the BTr of
cash donation upon receipt thereof: Provided, that if donation is made directly
to an LGU, the Bureau of Local Government Finance (BLGF) shall be informed
upon its receipt of the cash donation for the purposes of consolidation of the
quarterly reports to be submitted to the Office of Civil Defense (OCD), the
secretariat of the National Disaster Rick Reductions and Management Council
(NDRRMC). An official receipt shall be issued by the concerned donee agency or
LGU for cash donations received.

Donations in foreign currency shall be converted to the Philippine Peso at the
prevailing rate at the time of the receipt of the donation. Donations shall be taken
up in the books of the BTr as income or trust receipts.

The amounts received by the done-agency shall thereafter be deposited to the
account of the Treasurer of the Philippines, and shall be made available to the
implementing agency concerned through a Special Budget pursuant to Section
35, Chapter 5, Book VI of E. O. No. 292: Provided, that of the done-agency is an
LGU, the same shall be deposited to the account of the Local Treasury as a
separate special account maintained in every provincial, city or municipal
treasury and recorded as a Trust Fund under the Special Funds pursuant to
Section 309 (b) Article I, Chapter II, Title V, Book II of the Local Government
code.

In no case shall the cash donation be used for payment of Personal Services of
any government unit or any other expenses not related to disaster relief, recovery
and rehabilitation efforts.

SECTION 8. Donations in Kind. For donations in kind, an acknowledgement
receipt shall be issued to the donor by the donee agency. The value of donated
Property, Plant and Equipment (PPE) shall be based on the declared value in the
Deed of Donation or the bill of landing/airway bill/parcel notice and other related
documents. The fair market value/appraised value shall be used in the absence
of declared value. The donee-agency shall submit either in printed form or by
way of electronic document to the OCD quarterly reports on the receipt of
donations in kind: Provided, that the LGU shall submit to OCD through BLGF
all donations in kind that they have received: Provided, further, that the Bureau
of Customs (BOC), which processes all foreign donations pursuant to Section 18
of RA 10121, otherwise known as the “Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Act”, shall submit a report on conditionally-free importation under
this section to the OCD.
The head of the donee agency or LGU shall be primarily responsible for the
accounting and safeguarding of all donated supplies, materials, equipment, and
relief goods against loss and wastage.

SECTION 9. Foreign Donations. Foreign donations, whether in cash or in kind,
from foreign governments shall be subject to the prior clearance and approval of
the President upon the recommendation of the Department of Foreign Affairs
(DFA). The DFA shall also submit to OCD a report on foreign donations that it
has processed.

SECTION 10. Consolidated Reports. The head of the donee-agency and the
agency’s web administrator or his/her equivalent shall be responsible for
ensuring that the quarterly report of all donations received, whether in cash or
in kind, is posted on the agency’s official website.

The BTr shall submit to OCD a quarterly report on all amounts deposited under
the special account it maintains for all donations received, including the amount
retained by the donee-agency under Section 7 of this Act.

The Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) shall likewise submit a report on all
pledges made by foreign governments, bilateral or multilateral organizations and
institutions, private foreign individuals or groups to the Philippine government
for disaster relief, aid, or rehabilitation to OCD.

The OCD shall submit, either in printed form or by way of electronic document,
to the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), the House Committee on
Appropriations and the Senate Committee on Finance, a consolidated quarterly
report of all submissions made to OCD under this Act. The Executive Director of
the OCD and the NDRRMC’s web administrator or his/her equivalent shall be
responsible for ensuring that said quarterly reports are likewise posted on the
official website of the NDRRMC.

SECTION 11. Implementing Rules and Regulations. The DBM, OCD, DOF,
DFA, Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG), and the Commission
on Audit (COA) shall promulgate the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR)
for the implementation of this Act within thirty (30) days from its approval.

The said IRR shall take effect five (5) days after its publication in a newspaper of
general circulation.
SECTION 12. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
complete publication in the Official Gazette or at least two (2) newspapers of
general circulation.
