                        AN ACT
STRENGTHENING AND REGULATING EXPERIENTIAL LEARNING
     AND OFF-CAMPUS ACTIVITIES IN BASIC EDUCATION
 INSTITUTIONS, ESTABLISHING PROPORTIONATE STANDARDS
FOR THEIR APPROVAL, PREPARATION, IMPLEMENTATION AND
   ASSESSMENT, REGULATING THE COLLECTION OF FEES,
 PRESCRIBING STANDARDS FOR EDUCATIONAL DESTINATIONS
    AND SERVICE PROVIDERS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

                           EXPLANATORY NOTE


   Education does not take place only inside the classroom. Learners understand
history differently when they encounter the places where it happened. Science
becomes more concrete when they see it applied. Communities, workplaces,
museums, cultural institutions, government offices, farms, laboratories, and other
learning environments can help connect classroom instruction with real life.

   Experiential learning, however, takes many forms. A nearby museum visit is
different from a multi-day educational tour. A community immersion is different
from participation in a national competition. Senior High School work immersion
has purposes and requirements distinct from a commercial tour package.
Regulation should recognize these differences.
    This measure therefore adopts a proportionate approach. Educational field
trips and tours involving substantial travel, organized itineraries, tour operators,
significant fees, or overnight stays are subject to stricter permit, financial,
destination, and safety requirements. Site-based experiential learning activities
are subject to a simplified approval process, unless their cost, distance, duration,
or risk warrants higher review. Competitions and work exposure or immersion
remain principally governed by their respective program rules, while being
subject to the minimum learner-protection standards of this Act.

    At the heart of every activity must be a genuine educational purpose. An
activity should begin with clear learning objectives, be implemented with proper
supervision and safeguards, and end with reflection and assessment. An
educational tour should not simply be a recreational trip given an educational
label.

    The measure likewise allows schools to collect reasonable fees for approved
activities, while requiring transparency, affordability, proper accounting, and
protection against commissions, markups, and other personal benefit. For public
schools, approved expenses may also be supported from the Special Education
Fund, subject to the approval of the Local School Board and applicable budgeting
and auditing rules.

    Finally, the bill establishes clear standards for destinations and service
providers. Commercial tourism enterprises must possess the appropriate
government accreditation, while historical, cultural, scientific, environmental,
workplace, and similar learning sites must be duly recognized, licensed, or
authorized by the competent government agency. Theme parks, amusement
parks, water parks, resorts primarily intended for recreation, gaming
establishments, and similar entertainment venues are expressly excluded as
educational destinations.
                                                                                  2
   Experiential learning should open the world to the learner. But every activity
undertaken in the name of education must remain, first and foremost, educational,
safe, accessible, and worthy of the trust placed in our schools.


      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

in Congress assembled:
SECTION. 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Experiential
Learning and Off-Campus Activities Act.”

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
promote experiential learning as an integral means of connecting classroom
instruction with authentic situations, communities, institutions, culture, history,
science, technology, the environment, and the world of work.

   The State shall ensure that experiential learning and off-campus activities in
basic education serve clear educational purposes and are developmentally
appropriate, inclusive, affordable, safe, and subject to effective supervision and
accountability.

   Regulation under this Act shall be proportionate to the nature, duration, cost,
distance, and level of risk of the activity, recognizing that educational tours and
field trips require different safeguards from site-based learning activities,
competitions, community immersion, and work exposure or immersion.

   At all times, the best interests, safety, dignity, and well-being of learners shall
prevail over convenience, recreation, commercial interest, or profit.

SEC. 3. Coverage. – This Act shall apply to all public and private basic education
institutions offering kindergarten, elementary, junior high school, and senior high
school programs under the supervision or regulation of the Department of
Education (DepEd).

   It shall cover school-authorized activities involving learners conducted
wholly or partly outside school premises.

SEC. 4. Definition of Terms. – For purposes of this Act:

(a) Experiential Learning refers to a structured educational process through which
   learners acquire, apply, deepen, or reflect upon knowledge, skills, values, and
   competencies through direct engagement with authentic situations or
   environments;

(b) Educational Field Trip or Educational Tour refers to an organized off-campus
   activity involving travel to one or more educational destinations under a
   planned itinerary and ordinarily involving hired transportation, a tour or travel
   service provider, substantial travel, the collection of fees, an overnight stay,
   or other arrangements beyond an ordinary site-based learning activity;

                                                                                    2
(c) Site-Based Experiential Learning Activity refers to a short-duration,
   community-based or destination-specific learning activity, such as a museum,
   library, historical, cultural, scientific, environmental, civic, government,
   farm, industry, or community visit, community immersion, service learning,
   or site observation, which is not conducted as a packaged educational tour
   and ordinarily does not involve an overnight stay;

(d) Competition or Official Representation refers to learner participation, upon
   school or DepEd authorization, in academic, athletic, cultural, artistic,
   scientific, technical, leadership, or similar competitions, conferences,
   exhibitions, and official events outside school premises;

(e) Work Exposure or Immersion refers to curriculum-based workplace learning,
   observation, simulation, apprenticeship exposure, work immersion, or similar
   activities undertaken with a duly qualified partner institution or workplace
   under applicable DepEd and other government regulations;

(f) Educational Destination refers to a site, facility, institution, community,
   enterprise, or environment used for an experiential learning activity because
   of its demonstrable relevance to the intended learning outcomes;

(g) Activity Head refers to the school official or personnel formally designated to
   assume primary responsibility for an activity; and

(h) Service Provider refers to a tour operator, transportation operator,
   accommodation provider, activity facilitator, facility operator, or other
   external entity engaged in the conduct of an activity.

SEC. 5. Classification and Proportionate Regulation of Activities. – Off-
campus activities shall be regulated according to the following classifications:


                                                                                   3
(a) Educational Field Trips and Tours. - These shall be subject to the full permit,
   planning, destination, provider, safety, fee, and post-activity requirements
   prescribed under this Act.

(b) Site-Based Experiential Learning Activities. - These shall require approval by
   the school head and compliance with the minimum standards under this Act.
   A separate DepEd permit shall be required only when the activity:

   (1) involves an overnight stay;

   (2) is conducted outside the Schools Division or such geographic limit as
       DepEd may prescribe;

   (3) involves significant safety or health risks;

   (4) involves fees exceeding a threshold prescribed by DepEd;

   (5) engages a commercial tour operator; or

   (6) otherwise meets criteria for higher review prescribed by DepEd.

(c) Competitions and Official Representation. - These shall be governed
   principally by applicable DepEd rules or the rules of the sponsoring
   government agency or recognized organization. A separate educational field
   trip permit shall not be required unless the school adds an educational tour,
   recreational itinerary, or other activity independent of the competition or
   official event.
(d) Work Exposure or Immersion. - These shall be governed principally by
   applicable laws and DepEd, Department of Labor and Employment,
   Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, and other relevant
   government regulations. They shall not be treated as educational field trips or
   tours solely because learners perform the activity outside school premises.

                                                                                 4
    Regardless of classification, all activities shall comply with the applicable
minimum requirements on educational relevance, learner safety, child protection,
parental consent, financial accountability, and appropriate supervision under this
Act.

SEC. 6. Governing Standards. – All experiential learning and off-campus
activities shall observe the following:

(a) Educational Relevance. The activity shall have a direct relationship with
    identified curriculum competencies or legitimate learner-development
    objectives;

(b) Learner Welfare and Safety. Appropriate safeguards shall be adopted
    considering the age, needs, circumstances, and number of learners and the
    nature of the activity;

(c) Equity and Inclusion. No learner shall suffer academic or disciplinary
    disadvantage because of inability to participate in a voluntary activity;

(d) Affordability. Costs shall be reasonable and shall not constitute an
    unnecessary barrier to participation;

(e) Transparency and Accountability. Material information concerning the
    activity, including its purpose, itinerary, costs, risks, and responsible persons,
    shall be disclosed to learners and parents or guardians; and

(f) Learning Assessment. The educational experience shall be appropriately
    processed, reflected upon, or assessed after its completion.

SEC. 7. Preparation, Implementation, and Assessment. – Every activity shall
be supported by a program appropriate to its classification and complexity.

                                                                                    5
    For educational field trips and tours, the school shall prepare a comprehensive
Experiential Learning Activity Plan specifying:

(a) the learning objectives and curriculum alignment;

(b) date, duration, destinations, and complete itinerary;

(c) expected participants and supervision arrangements;

(d) learning activities to be undertaken;

(e) transportation, accommodation, and service providers, when applicable;

(f) risk assessment, health, safety, child protection, and emergency measures;

(g) required licenses, accreditations, or government authorizations;

(h) itemized costs, funding sources, and proposed fees, if any;

(i) insurance coverage, when required; and

(j) procedures for post-activity learning assessment, evaluation, and financial
    liquidation.

    For site-based activities, the DepEd shall prescribe a simplified plan
proportionate to the nature and risk of the activity.

    Competitions, official representation, and work exposure or immersion may
use the planning and reporting documents prescribed under their respective
governing programs in lieu of the Activity Plan.

SEC. 8. Permit for Educational Field Trips and Tours. – No educational field
trip or tour shall be conducted without prior authorization from the appropriate
Schools Division Office.

                                                                                 6
    The application shall include the Activity Plan and documents necessary to
establish the educational relevance of the activity, qualifications of destinations
and providers, reasonableness of costs, transportation arrangements, and
adequacy of health, safety, and emergency measures.

    Applications shall be submitted within the period prescribed by DepEd,
which shall provide a simplified and electronic process whenever practicable.

    In determining whether to approve an application, the Schools Division
Office shall consider:

(a) educational relevance;

(b) suitability and regulatory standing of destinations and providers;

(c) reasonableness and affordability of costs;

(d) learner protection and supervision;

(e) transportation and travel safety; and

(f) overall risk of the activity.

    Failure of the approving authority to act shall not constitute approval.

    The DepEd may prescribe higher approval or notification requirements for
interregional, international, extended-duration, or particularly high-risk activities.

SEC. 9. Approval of Site-Based Experiential Learning Activities. – Site-based
experiential learning activities not requiring a permit under Section 5(b) shall be
approved by the school head upon determination that:

(a) the activity has a clear educational purpose;

                                                                                    7
(b) the destination is suitable and duly authorized to operate;

(c) reasonable safety and supervision arrangements are in place;

(d) any fee to be collected complies with this Act; and

(e) the parent or guardian has been properly informed and, when required, has
    consented to the participation of the learner.

    The DepEd may establish a simplified notice or reporting system for such
activities but shall avoid unnecessary documentary requirements that do not
materially contribute to learner safety or accountability.

SEC. 10. Competitions, Official Representation, and Work Exposure or
Immersion. – Learners participating in competitions or official representation
shall be governed by applicable authorization, travel, financial, safety, and
supervision rules for the concerned program or event.

    Work exposure or immersion shall be conducted only with qualified partner
institutions or workplaces and under an appropriate agreement specifying
learning objectives, supervision, safety, learner protection, and responsibilities of
the school and partner institution.

    Neither a competition nor work exposure or immersion shall be used as a
means to conduct an otherwise prohibited recreational or commercial tour.

SEC. 11. Voluntary Participation and Parental Consent. – Except for curricular
activities required under applicable education standards, participation in
educational tours, field trips, and other voluntary off-campus activities shall not
be compulsory.



                                                                                   8
    No learner shall be academically penalized, denied school services or
privileges, or otherwise disadvantaged for inability or decision not to participate
in a voluntary activity. Where appropriate, the school shall provide a reasonable
alternative learning activity.

    A minor learner shall not participate without the informed consent of a parent
or legal guardian. The parent or guardian shall be provided, prior to consent, with
material information concerning the educational purpose, itinerary or destination,
cost, transportation, supervision, major risks, and emergency arrangements.

    No waiver shall exempt any person or entity from liability for gross
negligence, willful misconduct, or violation of law.

SEC. 12. Standards for Educational Destinations. – Every educational
destination shall:

(a) be directly relevant to the intended learning objectives;

(b) possess the licenses, permits, accreditation, recognition, or authorization
    required by law;

(c) have facilities and operating conditions reasonably appropriate for the age,
    number, and needs of participating learners; and

(d) comply with applicable health, safety, security, accessibility, and emergency
    requirements.

    Commercial       tourism     enterprises   used   as   educational   destinations,
accommodation facilities, or tourism service providers shall possess valid
accreditation from the Department of Tourism (DOT), where such accreditation
is required under applicable law or regulation.

                                                                                    9
    Historical sites, shrines, landmarks, heritage structures, museums, and
cultural destinations shall be administered, registered, recognized, accredited, or
otherwise authorized, as applicable, by the National Historical Commission of
the Philippines, the National Museum of the Philippines, or other competent
cultural agency.

    Environmental, scientific, agricultural, industrial, workplace, and similar
destinations shall possess the appropriate authorization or regulatory compliance
required by the competent government agency.

    Government-operated        educational,    cultural,    scientific,   historical,
environmental, civic, and community facilities may be used without separate
accreditation when they are lawfully established and operated by the competent
government authority.

    Accreditation or regulatory recognition shall establish the eligibility of a
facility for consideration but shall not, by itself, establish its educational
relevance to a particular activity.

SEC. 13. Prohibited Destinations. – The following shall not be approved or
included as educational destinations in a school-authorized educational field trip
or tour:

(a) theme parks and amusement parks;

(b) water parks and similar recreational facilities;

(c) resorts whose primary purpose is leisure or recreation;

(d) casinos, gaming, betting, and similar establishments;



                                                                                  10
(e) shopping malls and commercial shopping centers when the principal purpose
   of the visit is shopping, recreation, or entertainment;

(f) commercial entertainment establishments primarily intended for amusement
   rather than learning;

(g) establishments inappropriate for minors; and

(h) destinations closed, suspended, or subject to an official safety or travel
   restriction.

   A separately identifiable museum, gallery, cultural institution, science
facility, or other legitimate educational facility located within a commercial
complex shall not be prohibited solely by reason of its location.

   DOT accreditation or any other regulatory accreditation shall not convert a
theme park, amusement park, resort, or other prohibited recreational facility into
an allowable educational destination.

   No authorized educational itinerary shall include a prohibited recreational
destination merely as a side trip, incentive, or component of a commercial
package.




SEC. 14. Service Providers and Transportation. – Tour operators engaged for
educational field trips or tours shall possess valid DOT accreditation and all
licenses required by law.




                                                                               11
    Transportation providers and vehicles shall possess the appropriate licenses,
franchises, registrations, safety certifications, and insurance required by the
Department of Transportation and its attached agencies.

    Schools shall exercise due diligence in selecting service providers based on
regulatory compliance, safety record, capability, cost, and suitability for learners.

    Public schools shall comply with applicable procurement, accounting, and
auditing laws. Private schools shall adopt transparent procedures for selecting
providers.

SEC. 15. Regulation of Fees and Charges. – Basic education institutions may
collect reasonable fees or charges from participating learners for approved
educational field trips, tours, and other experiential learning activities, subject to
the following:

(a) the fee shall relate only to an activity duly authorized under this Act;

(b) the amount shall be limited to actual, reasonable, and necessary costs,
    including transportation, admission, accommodation when necessary, meals,
    insurance, learning materials, and other expenses directly attributable to the
    activity;

(c) the school shall consider affordability and shall keep costs at the lowest
    reasonable level consistent with safety and educational quality;

(d) the complete amount and its material components shall be disclosed to parents
    or guardians before collection;

(e) no school, school personnel, parent organization, or intermediary shall receive
    any undisclosed markup, commission, rebate, referral fee, free travel
    privilege, personal benefit, or profit arising from the activity;
                                                                                   12
(f) no fee shall be collected from a learner who does not participate in a voluntary
    activity;

(g) collections and expenditures shall be properly receipted, recorded, and
    liquidated, and any refundable or unused amount shall be returned or credited
    to the learners concerned; and

(h) cancellation, withdrawal, and refund conditions shall be disclosed before
    payment.

    Schools     shall,   whenever    practicable,   provide   reasonable   payment
arrangements, subsidies, sponsorships, or other assistance to prevent financial
incapacity from unnecessarily excluding a learner from an educationally valuable
activity.

    Fees for competitions, official representation, and work exposure or
immersion shall be governed by their respective program rules. No educational
tour fee shall be imposed under the guise of such activities.

    The DepEd may prescribe reasonable fee ceilings, disclosure requirements,
and additional safeguards according to the type, duration, and location of the
activity.

SEC. 16. Special Education Fund Support. – In addition to the purposes
authorized under Section 272 of Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the
“Local Government Code of 1991,” the Special Education Fund may, upon
approval of the Local School Board, be used to support approved experiential
learning and off-campus activities of public basic education institutions under this
Act.



                                                                                 13
    Allowable expenditures may include transportation, entrance or instructional
fees, insurance, learning materials, and other reasonable and necessary expenses
directly related to the approved activity.

    In allocating such support, priority may be given to activities of clear
educational value and to learners who may otherwise be unable to participate
because of financial incapacity.

    No expense already paid from the Special Education Fund or another public
source shall be charged again to learners.

    All expenditures shall remain subject to applicable budgeting, procurement,
accounting, and auditing laws, rules, and regulations.

SEC. 17. Health, Safety, Supervision, and Child Protection. – The school shall
ensure that every activity has supervision, health, safety, and emergency
arrangements appropriate to the age and number of learners, destination, duration,
mode of travel, and degree of risk.

    The DepEd shall prescribe minimum standards including, where appropriate:

(a) adult-to-learner supervision ratios;

(b) learner accounting and communication procedures;

(c) first aid and access to emergency medical services;

(d) emergency evacuation and parent-notification procedures;

(e) accommodations for learners with disabilities or medical needs;

(f) insurance requirements;

(g) pre-activity safety orientation; and
                                                                               14
(h) continued application of child protection, anti-bullying, and safeguarding
    policies.

SEC. 18. Changes, Suspension, or Cancellation of Activities. – A material
change in the approved destination, itinerary, provider, cost, or risk profile of an
educational field trip or tour shall require approval in the manner prescribed by
DepEd.

    Immediate changes necessary to protect learners during an emergency may
be made by the Activity Head and shall be reported thereafter.

    An activity shall be postponed, suspended, modified, or cancelled when
severe weather, disaster, disease outbreak, armed conflict, transportation
conditions, official advisories, or other circumstances create an unreasonable risk
to participants.

    Financial loss, advance payment, or a nonrefundable booking shall not justify
proceeding with an unsafe activity.

SEC. 19. Post-Activity Assessment and Reporting. – Educational field trips and
tours shall include an appropriate post-activity assessment connecting the
experience with the intended learning outcomes.

    The Activity Head shall thereafter submit, within the period prescribed by
DepEd, a concise report covering:

(a) attainment of learning objectives;

(b) significant learner and teacher feedback;

(c) material safety incidents or deviations;

(d) performance of major destinations and service providers; and
                                                                                 15
(e) financial liquidation, where fees were collected.

    Site-based experiential learning activities may be subject to a simplified
assessment and reporting process.

    Competitions, official representation, and work exposure or immersion may
satisfy this requirement through reports already required under their respective
governing programs.

    Serious injury, hospitalization, missing learners, major transportation
accidents, serious child protection incidents, or other emergencies shall be
reported immediately to the parent or guardian and the appropriate government
authorities.

SEC. 20. Prohibited Acts. – It shall be unlawful to:

(a) conduct an educational field trip or tour without the permit required under this
    Act;

(b) falsely represent an activity primarily intended for tourism, entertainment,
    recreation, or fundraising as an educational activity;

(c) include a prohibited destination or materially deviate from an approved
    itinerary without lawful justification;

(d) compel participation in, or penalize nonparticipation in, a voluntary activity;

(e) collect an unauthorized, excessive, undisclosed, or unliquidated fee;

(f) solicit, demand, accept, or receive an undisclosed commission, rebate,
    kickback, referral fee, free travel privilege, gift, or personal financial benefit
    from a service provider;

                                                                                   16
(g) knowingly engage a provider that lacks a license or accreditation required by
    law, or that has been suspended or disqualified from school activities;

(h) knowingly submit false or materially misleading information to secure
    approval of an activity;

(i) conceal a serious safety incident, financial irregularity, or material violation
    of this Act; or

(j) retaliate against any person who reports a violation in good faith.

SEC. 21. Accountability and Sanctions. – Without prejudice to civil or criminal
liability under existing laws, a violation of this Act may result in:

(a) appropriate administrative sanctions against responsible public school
    officials or employees;

(b) administrative sanctions against responsible officers or personnel of private
    basic education institutions;

(c) suspension or revocation of the school’s authority to conduct educational tours
    or field trips;

(d) disqualification or blacklisting of a service provider from school-related
    activities;

(e) restitution or refund of unauthorized or improperly collected amounts; and

(f) referral to the appropriate regulatory, licensing, law enforcement, or
    prosecutorial agency.

    Administrative proceedings shall observe due process.


                                                                                 17
   A permit or approval issued by DepEd shall not relieve a school, school
personnel, or private service provider of responsibility for negligence,
misconduct, breach of statutory duty, or violation of law.

SEC. 22. Responsibilities of the Department of Education. – The DepEd shall
be the principal implementing agency of this Act and shall:

(a) prescribe differentiated standards and approval requirements for the classes of
   activities established under this Act;

(b) establish standardized but proportionate planning, permit, consent, financial
   disclosure, and reporting requirements;

(c) maintain or provide access to information necessary to verify qualified and
   disqualified service providers and destinations;

(d) monitor compliance and maintain information on serious incidents involving
   school-authorized activities;

(e) provide guidance and capacity building for schools; and

(f) coordinate with appropriate government agencies in the implementation of this
   Act.

   The DepEd shall avoid requirements that unnecessarily duplicate valid
licenses, certifications, accreditation, reports, or records already maintained by
another government agency.

SEC. 23. Interagency Coordination. – The DepEd shall coordinate, as necessary,
with the DOT, Department of Transportation, National Historical Commission of
the Philippines, National Museum of the Philippines, Department of Environment
and Natural Resources, Department of Science and Technology, Department of
                                                                                18
Agriculture, Department of Health, Department of Labor and Employment,
Technical Education and Skills Development Authority, Department of the
Interior and Local Government, local government units, and other concerned
agencies for the verification of qualifications, safety standards, advisories, and
regulatory compliance.

SEC. 24. Local and Accessible Experiential Learning. – Schools are encouraged
to prioritize accessible, nearby, and cost-effective educational opportunities when
these can substantially achieve the intended learning objectives.

      DepEd and local government units may facilitate partnerships with
museums, cultural and historical sites, universities, government institutions,
farms, industries, scientific facilities, environmental sites, civic organizations,
and communities to expand meaningful experiential learning opportunities for
learners.

SEC. 25. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DepEd, in consultation with appropriate
government agencies and representatives of public and private basic education
institutions, teachers, parents, learners, and relevant service providers, shall
promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its implementation.

The implementing rules shall particularly provide for:

(a) objective criteria distinguishing educational field trips and tours from site-
    based experiential learning activities;

(b) simplified and risk-based approval and permit procedures;

(c) appropriate supervision, transportation, insurance, and safety standards;


                                                                                19
(d) standards and procedures governing fees, financial assistance, liquidation, and
    refunds;

(e) qualifications and verification of destinations and service providers;

(f) procedures for reporting serious incidents and violations; and

(g) transition from existing DepEd rules to the framework established under this
    Act.

SEC. 26. Appropriations. – The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the DepEd.
Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual General
Appropriations Act.

SEC. 27. Transitory Provision. – Existing DepEd orders and regulations
governing off-campus activities, competitions, work immersion, and related
experiential learning programs shall remain effective insofar as they are not
inconsistent with this Act until amended, replaced, or superseded pursuant hereto.

      Within one hundred eighty (180) days from the issuance of the
implementing rules and regulations, public and private basic education
institutions shall align their policies and practices with this Act.

SEC. 28. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall
continue in full force and effect.

SEC. 29. Repealing Clause. – All laws, decrees, executive orders, rules,
regulations, and issuances or parts thereof inconsistent with this Act are hereby
repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

SEC. 30. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
