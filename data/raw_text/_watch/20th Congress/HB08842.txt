              THE "ANTI-DRUNK AND DRUGGED DRIVING ACT OF 2013"

                                            EXPLANATORY NOTE

Globally, the World Health Organization (WHO) cites road traffic crashes as the leading
cause of death among people aged 15-29 years. In the Philippines, in the years 2015-
2019, out of 18, 735 deaths due to road crashes, 5,213 are attributable to alcohol use.1
An average of three out of ten deaths from vehicular crashes involved an alcohol-impaired
driver.

While Republic Act No. (RA) 10586, also known as the "Anti-Drunk and Drugged Driving
Act of 2013," is a laudable legislation promoting safe transportation, the Global Status
Report on Road Safety 2015, gave a rating of 1 out of a maximum score of 10 in its
enforcement and implementation.

This proposed legislation aims to strengthen existing rules and regulations on road safety
by instituting the following measures:
    • Providing funds in order to procure breathalyzers to fully implement the "Anti-
       Drunk and Drugged Driving Act of 2013";
    • Sobriety checkpoints and random breath-testing;
    • Administrative suspension of licenses
    • Other proactive measures such as alternative transportation, designated drivers,
       and mass media campaigns.

Deaths caused by vehicular crashes are completely preventable. Road safety measures
should be implemented proactively to reduce the risk of alcohol-related harms. Safe
transportation should be an important component of any country's roadmap towards
development and progress.

In light of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.
