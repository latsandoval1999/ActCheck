25                                      EXPLANATORY NOTE
26
27           November 1, 2013. Super Typhoon Yolanda was one of the worst natural
28   disasters to strike the country in recent memory. The death toll was apocalyptic in
29   magnitude and the damage to property was biblical in proportion. Conservative reports
30   from the National Risk Reduction Management Council (NDRRMC) tallied 6,300 dead,
31   28,688 injured, and 1,062 still missing.1 However, survivors’ group People’s Surge
32   placed the number of casualties up to 19,000 people, citing the uncounted dead in the
33   far-flung areas of Eastern Visayas. The total cost of damages reached some ₱95.5
34   billion.
35
36          Days prior to Yolanda’s landfall, the Philippine Atmospheric Geophysical and
37   Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA) already gave warnings that a
38   supertyphoon was about to enter the Philippine Area of Responsibility (PAR) that could
39   generate storm surges of up to 17 feet high. President Benigno Aquino III addressed
40   the country, confidently saying that all preparations are already in place and that the

 4          But the aftermath of the disaster manifested the lack of comprehension of the
 5   warnings released by PAGASA, from the local government units to the highest office
 6   in the Executive Department.
 7
 8           The response was dismal, there was a clear lack of body bags, and the dead were
 9   left lying on the streets for days. Authorities had a difficult time identifying bodies.
10   Worst, the people were left almost entirely to their own devices on fleeing, taking
11   shelter, and surviving, even for days afterward.
12
13          Additionally, reconstruction efforts have been abysmal. According to the
14   National Economic Development Authority (NEDA), the government allocation for
15   Yolanda relief, reconstruction and rehabilitation amounted to approximately ₱144.4
16   billion as of June 2016. While 71% of this has been released, only 53.4% were
17   disbursed according to the submissions of the government agencies. The overall
18   weighted accomplishment or physical completion of projects funded by these releases
19   was only 40%, while 27.7% are ongoing and 33% have not yet been started.3
20
21           Independent think-tank IBON Foundation noted that agencies tasked to facilitate
22   survivors’ fundamental needs, such as housing, food, livelihood, and water received
23   rehabilitation funds, had very low overall weighted accomplishment. The National
24   Housing Authority (NHA) received the largest allocation amounting to ₱39.2 billion
25   but the Department of Budget Management (DBM) reported that its overall weighted
26   accomplishment was only 12.3%, with 45% ongoing and 42.6% not yet started, three
27   years after the disaster struck. The overall weighted accomplishment of the Department
28   of Agriculture (DA), which received ₱1.5 billion, was only 14.1%, with 11.6% ongoing
29   and 74.3% not yet started. The overall weighted accomplishment of the Local Water
30   Utilities Administration (LWUA), which received ₱737 million, was only 2.9%, with
31   2.5% ongoing and 94.6% not yet started.4
32
33         Additionally, IBON Foundation noted, on the fourth-year anniversary
34   of Yolanda, that only 25% percent or 50,891 of the 205,128 houses for the victims have
35   been built. Of the completed units, only 27 percent are occupied.5
36

24
25   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
26   assembled:
27
28   SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Yolanda Memorial Act of
29   2026.”
30
31   SEC. 2. Construction of the Memorial. – A memorial for the victims of Supertyphoon
32   Yolanda shall be constructed in Tacloban City under the supervision of the Department
33   of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) in coordination with the Local Government
34   Units (LGUs) and the National Historical Commission of the Philippines.
35
36   SEC. 3. Inscription. – A message shall be inscribed unto the base of the memorial and
37   shall read as:
38
39               “In memory of the thousands of victims of Supertyphoon Yolanda
40
41                                    May this remind all:
42     That calamities have been transformed into monstrosities due to climate change;
43   That the leaders of this country must employ their utmost knowledge and care for the
44                                          people,
45                         And to work for a disaster-resilient nation;
46        That appropriate, decisive, and climate-adaptive policies must be enforced

                                                                                                           1
 1       To help protect the lives and livelihood of the people and mitigate the effects of
 2                                            disasters,
 3        To prevent losses should another calamity as Yolanda come our way anew.
 4
 5                                May this never happen again.”
 6
 7          The inscription shall have Filipino and Waray versions.
 8
 9   SEC. 4. Implementing Agency. – The DPWH shall be the lead implementing agency
10   of this project in coordination with the concerned LGUs.
11
12   SEC. 5. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to implement the provisions of this
13   Act shall be charged against the allocations in the General Appropriations Act (GAA)
14   of the Department of Public Works and Highways.
15
16   SEC. 6. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15 days) after its complete
17   publication in at least two (2) newspapers of general circulation.
