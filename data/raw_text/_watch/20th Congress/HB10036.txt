                                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

        Noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) such as cancer, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and
chronic respiratory diseases account for 68% of the total deaths in the Philippines.1 Obesity has
been identified as a key risk factor for these NCDs.2 In the Philippines, the prevalence of
overweight and obesity among children 5-10 years old has increased from 5.7% in 2003 to 12.9%
in 2023.3Among adolescents, the prevalence increased from 4.9% in 2003 to 12.5% in 2023.4 With
these 2023 levels, the country is not on track to achieve the targets of the Philippine Plan of Action
for Nutrition 2023-2028 for overweight and obesity. Without interventions, more than 30% of
Filipino adolescents are estimated to fall into this category by 2030.5

       Filipinos have become more vulnerable to NCDs because of the increased availability and
consumption of prepackaged food products that contain unhealthy types of fat or are high in
sodium or sugar6, together with marketing strategies that disproportionately target children7, who
are especially vulnerable, to the influence of advertising and nutritional claims.8This is
compounded by the fact that the country does not have an effective labelling system. Nutrition
information on food labels is often difficult to access as they are printed on the back of product
packaging, and even more difficult to understand—especially among consumers from lower-

        It is the State’s duty to “protect and promote the right to health of the people and instill
health consciousness among them.”11 The State must protect and promote the right of every
Filipino to the enjoyment of the “highest attainable standard of health,”12 and in fulfillment of the
best interests of the child, defend the right of children to proper care and nutrition, and “special
protection from conditions that are prejudicial to their development.”13In meeting these duties, the
State must strive to create a healthy environment that enables Filipinos, especially children, to
make healthy food choices.

      This proposed measure aims to address the foregoing issues, and fulfil the above-
mentioned duties of the State, by:
      (1) Introducing mandatory front-of-package warning labels (FOPWL) to help Filipinos
          avoid food that exceed prescribed thresholds for energy, fat, sodium, and sugar; and
      (2) Regulating all forms of marketing including those in locations frequented by children,
          to minimize the power of, and reduce the exposure of children to, marketing of food
          that exceed prescribed thresholds for energy, fat, sodium, and sugar.

       Similar policies in other countries have shown promising results, with FOPWL having been
proven to reduce purchases of food and beverages with excessive amounts of nutrients of concern14
and marketing restrictions leading to a significant decrease in children's exposure to advertising of
the same.15

       The proposed measure aligns with our shared vision of creating a healthier Philippines
where Filipinos can lead healthy lives. Thus, approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

28                ARTICLE II.          FRONT-OF-PACKAGE WARNING LABELS
29
30           SECTION 6. Mandatory front-of-package warning labels (FOPWL). No person shall
31   sell, manufacture, import, export, or distribute prepackaged food without a FOPWL as prescribed
32   in this Act, when the final composition of the product contains nutrients of concern that exceed
33   the maximum values prescribed under Section 8 of this Act.

34           SECTION 7. Form, size, placement, and characteristics of the warning label. When
35   the final composition of the prepackaged food product contains critical nutrients or calories in
36   excess of the maximum values under Section 8 of this Act, the packaging shall display a warning
37   label as prescribed by the FDA, in accordance with the following guidelines:

38          a. The packaging must include one warning label for each nutrient of concern in excess
39             of prescribed values, namely:
40                             i. “HIGH IN SUGAR”;
41                            ii. “HIGH IN SODIUM”;
42                           iii. “HIGH IN SATURATED FAT”;
43                           iv. “HIGH IN TOTAL FAT”;
44
45          b. If the product’s calories is in excess of the prescribed values the package must include
46             the following warning label:
47                             i. “HIGH IN CALORIES”.
48
49          c. If the product contains sweeteners, the package must include the following warning
50             label:
51                             i. “CONTAINS SWEETENERS, NOT RECOMMENDED FOR
52                                CHILDREN”.
53          d. The warning label for a and b of this section shall have the form of black octagons with
54             a white edge and capital letters;

                                                                                                      3
 1          e. The warning label for c of this section shall have the form of a black rectangle with a
 2             white edge and with capital letters;
 3          f. The size of each warning label shall never be less than five percent (5%) of the total
 4             area of the main face of the packaging. For products in which the main face of the
 5             packaging is equal to or less than ten (10) square centimeters, it will contain a single
 6             octagon that will show inside of it how many nutrients of concern above the thresholds
 7             the product has. The warning label shall be fifteen (15%) of the total area of the front
 8             of the package.;
 9          g. The warning labels shall form part of the packaging and their full visibility and integrity
10             must not be susceptible to being damaged, concealed, obstructed or changed by any
11             package design, feature, or mechanism, or covered by any other markings;
12          h. The warning label must be located on the top right edge of the product’s main face. If
13             the shape of the product is round or cylindrical, it must be on the top center of the main
14             face.
15          i. If the product has more than one warning label, they must be placed adjacent to each
16             other on the product’s main face.
17          j. For packages with multiple products, the outer packaging, as well as each individual
18             package inside must also include the warning label.

19   The provisions established in this Section are applied in addition to the existing rules and
20   regulations governing the labeling of prepackaged food distributed in the Philippines.

21           SECTION 8. Maximum values of nutrients of concern for prepackaged food. The
22   Department of Health (DOH) shall develop or adopt a nutrient profile model (NPM) to determine
23   the maximum values of energy, sugar, sodium, saturated fat, total fat, and other nutrients for
24   prepackaged food. Pending the development of a local NPM, a conflict-free, evidence-based NPM
25   recommended by the World Health Organization shall be used as the basis for such maximum
26   values.
27

31

33          b. Logotypes or phrases stating the sponsorship or endorsements of scientific societies or
34             civil associations or other relevant actors;
35          c. Any other health and nutrition claim; and
36          d. Any advertisement or promotional feature that qualifies as child-directed marketing as
37             defined in this Act.
38

46          b. The adulteration or misbranding of any food.
