                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

      Every November, our nation observes Filipino Values Month, pursuant to
Proclamation No. 479 (s. 1994). This serves as a reminder of the principles that have
long defined who we are, such as love for family and community (pagmamahal sa
pamilya at pamayanan); faith (pananampalataya); attaining education (pagkamit ng
edukasyon); health and wellness (kalusugan at kagalingan); resilience (katatagan);
honesty and integrity (katapatan at karangalan); and shared responsibility for the
common good (pakikipagkapwa). These values have carried Filipino families and
communities through crises, disasters, and national challenges, and continue to
guide us as we navigate an increasingly complex and fast-changing world.

        Through Proclamation No. 62 (s. 1992), the government launched the Moral
Recovery Program to spark a “moral awakening” and strengthen the country’s
commitment to values that are uniquely and positively Filipino. Three decades later,
this call remains urgent. In an era defined by rapid digitalization, social media
conflicts, weakening of morals, and global pressures, the country’s cultural and
moral foundation must not be left to chance.

      A stronger effort to preserve and transmit Filipino values is more necessary
than ever.

                                         1
     This Bill, the Filipino Values Act, establishes the Institute of Filipino Values
and Ethicology (IFVE) within the National Commission for Culture and the Arts
(NCCA).

       The NCCA has a Values Program that can be strengthened into an institution
solely dedicated to leading sustained nationwide efforts to study, document, archive,
teach, and promote Filipino values as part of our intangible cultural heritage. Notably,
in 2021, the NCCA has taken a significant step, having identified the Filipino values
upheld across the country through surveys and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs).

       The measure also strengthens the integration of Filipino values across
society, including education, media and public communications, tourism, and
government service, so that these values remain visible and relevant in everyday life.
By embedding values in key institutions, the Bill aims to develop citizens who are
rooted in identity while ready for a global world.

      This Bill is aligned with Chapter 7 of the Philippine Development Plan toward
AmBisyon Natin 2040, calling for “the inculcation of values to promote the common
good.” Ultimately, this recognizes that Filipino families and communities grow
stronger when shared values are preserved, practiced, and passed on.

       Upholding Filipino values is not just cultural work, but an investment in the
future of our nation. Accordingly, the immediate passage of this Bill is respectfully
requested.

                               AN ACT
INSTITUTIONALIZING THE FILIPINO VALUES INITIATIVE, ESTABLISHING THE
INSTITUTE OF FILIPINO VALUES AND ETHICOLOGY WITHIN THE NATIONAL
COMMISSION FOR CULTURE AND THE ARTS (NCCA), AND PROMOTING THE
INTEGRATION OF FILIPINO VALUES ACROSS SOCIETY, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

        SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Filipino Values
Act.”

       SEC 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is the policy of the State to recognize,
promote, and preserve Filipino values as an essential component of the nation’s
intangible cultural heritage, serving as foundations of our identity, unity, and shared
sense of responsibility.

       Towards this end, the State shall support efforts to study, document,
safeguard, and strengthen these values, ensuring that they are reflected throughout
society and transmitted to future generations.

        SEC 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

       a) Filipino Values refer to culturally rooted principles, beliefs, norms, and
ethical orientations that guide Filipino behavior, decisions, and relationships, which,
based on research and national consensus, promote personal well-being, social
harmony, civic responsibility, national productivity, and responsible global citizenship.
This is inclusive of values from Indigenous Cultural Communities / Indigenous
Peoples.



                                           3
      b) Ethicology refers to the specialized study of ethics, values, moral
reasoning, and behavioural norms as practiced and understood within a cultural
community.

       c) National Values Audit refers to a periodic, nationwide study utilizing
qualitative and quantitative methods to document Filipino values, assess value shifts,
and identify factors that strengthen or erode these values.

       d) Digital Values Repository refers to an open-access multimedia archive
containing research outputs, historical materials, ethnographies, oral histories, and
teaching-learning resource on Filipino values.

        SEC 4. The Institute of Filipino Values and Ethicology. – There is hereby
created within the National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA), the
Institute of Filipino Values and Ethicology, hereinafter referred to as the “Institute”.

       a) Purpose. – The Institute shall serve as the lead national body for research,
documentation, archiving, and dissemination of Filipino values. It shall have the
following powers and functions:

           1) Conduct the National Values Audit every five (5) years;
           2) Develop and maintain the Digital Values Repository;
           3) Undertake continuing interdisciplinary research on Filipino values;
           4) Document values expressed in Philippine languages, oral traditions,
              literature, indigenous customary laws, historical narratives, and
              contemporary contexts;
           5) Collaborate with relevant government agencies on Filipino values
              integration;
           6) Facilitate public engagement programs, including exhibitions,
              conferences, and youth fellowships;
           7) Provide guidance and technical support to agencies in implementing
              Filipino values initiatives; and
           8) Perform such other functions necessary to carry out the intent of this
              Act.

       b) Officers. – The Institute shall be administered by a Director and Assistant
Director, both appointed by the NCCA Chairperson.

       The Director and Assistant Director must have a master’s degree or higher in
ethics, philosophy, sociology, anthropology, cultural studies, education, public
administration, or a related field, with at least three (3) years of relevant experience
in research, policy development, cultural preservation, education, or program
management. They must also demonstrate knowledge of Filipino cultural values,
indigenous practices, and interdisciplinary methods, as well as leadership and
management competence.

                                           4
      Both officers shall be responsible for overseeing the operations, programs,
and research activities of the Institute.

      c) Staff. – The Institute shall have a regular staff of researchers and
administrative personnel.

       SEC 5. Integration of Filipino Values in Education. – The Department of
Education (DepEd) shall ensure that Filipino values documented and endorsed by
the Institute are integrated into the Good Manners and Right Conduct (GMRC) and
Values Education (VE) curriculum, consistent with Republic Act No. 11476, otherwise
known as the GMRC and Value Education Act.

        The DepEd, in partnership with the Institute and Commission on Higher
Education (CHED), shall develop mandatory teacher training and certification on
Filipino Values Pedagogy for all GMRC/VE teachers, guidance counsellors, and
school heads. Learning resources, including textbooks and digital modules, shall be
updated to reflect Filipino values.

    SEC 6. Integration of Filipino Values in Media and Public
Communications. – Filipino values shall be reflected in media and public
communications.

       The National Council for Children’s Television (NCCT) shall develop
guidelines for television and digital content promoting Filipino values. It shall also
encourage and provide incentives for the production of programs, series, and
content that are culturally grounded and values-oriented.

       The Philippine Information Agency (PIA) shall integrate Filipino values in
nationwide communication campaigns and public information programs. Likewise, it
shall disseminate educational and values materials produced by the Institute through
regional and community channels.

       SEC 7. Integration of Filipino Values in Tourism. – The Tourism
Promotions Board (TPB) shall promote Filipino values in tourism programs,
campaigns, and activities. Tourism-related training, promotion, and initiatives shall
reflect and encourage the practice of Filipino values among professionals,
communities, and visitors.

       SEC 8. Integration of Filipino Values in Government Service. – Filipino
values shall be embedded in the operations and culture of government service, in
particular:

       a) The Civil Service Commission (CSC) shall integrate core Filipino values
into the Civil Service Examinations, eligibility assessments, and competency
frameworks.




                                          5
      b) Government agencies shall incorporate Filipino values into Performance
Management Systems to recognize and reward ethical behavior, civic responsibility,
and public service values.

        c) All public service training programs, including induction, continuing
education, and leadership development modules, shall include mandatory instruction
on Filipino values.

        SEC 9. Whole of Government Approach. – For the effective
institutionalization of the Filipino values initiatives, a whole of government approach
shall be adopted.

       SEC 10. Filipino Values Month. – The month of November of every year is
hereby declared “Filipino Values Month” to promote awareness, appreciation, and
practice of Filipino values across all sectors of society.

       SEC 11. Appropriations. – Funding for the Institute of Filipino Values and
Ethicology shall be drawn from the current year’s appropriations and other internally
generated income of the NCCA, and shall thereafter be included in the annual
General Appropriations Act.

     This funding mechanism shall likewise apply to other concerned agencies
mandated under this Act.

        SEC 12. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (90) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the NCCA, in coordination with the DepEd, CHED,
NCCT, PIA, TPB, CSC, National Library of the Philippines (NLP), National Archives
of the Philippines (NAP), National Historical Commission of the Philippines (NHCP),
Komisyon sa Wikang Filipino (KWF), National Commission on Indigenous Peoples
(NCIP), National Youth Commission (NYC), and other relevant stakeholders as may
be determined by the NCCA, shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations
for the effective implementation of this Act.

       SEC 13. Separability Clause. – If any provision or part hereof is held invalid
or unconstitutional, the remainder of the law or the provision or part not otherwise
affected shall remain valid and subsisting.

       SEC 14. Repealing Clause. – Any law, presidential decree or issuance,
executive order, letter of instruction, administrative order, rule, or regulation contrary
to or inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, modified, or
amended accordingly.

        SEC 15.         Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days
after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.

       Approved,
