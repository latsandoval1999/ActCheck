                                     EXPLANATORY NOTE

       No worker should have to endure daily humiliation, intimidation, or
exclusion simply to keep a paycheck.

       This bill institutes comprehensive policies to prevent and address workplace
bullying, harassment, and other forms of violence. It covers physical, verbal,
psychological, social, cyber, and gender-based bullying, as well as abuse of authority
and management by threat or intimidation. It requires all government and private
offices to adopt written anti-bullying policies and report compliance to the
Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) or the Civil Service Commission
(CSC), as applicable.

       A nationwide poll by the Lloyd’s Register Foundation and Gallup found that
twenty-two percent (22%) of Filipino women and eighteen percent (18%) of men
have experienced violence or harassment at work, with psychological harassment
the most commonly reported form and sixty percent (60%) of victims saying it
happened three (3) or more times.1 Yet, a majority of victims never report the abuse:
forty-seven percent (47%) of women and sixty percent (60%) of men who
experienced it stayed silent, largely out of fear of repercussions, or the belief that
nothing would come of speaking up.1 This silence poses no surprise given how thin
the country’s legal protections remain outside of the school setting.

      The Philippines currently has no standalone law against workplace bullying.
Republic Act (RA) No. 10627, otherwise known as the “Anti-Bullying Act”, applies
only to schools, leaving working adults to rely on a patchwork of statutes never
designed to address bullying directly, including RA No. 11058, otherwise known as
the “Occupational Safety and Health Standards Act”, RA No. 11313, otherwise known
as the “Safe Spaces Act”, RA No. 7877, otherwise known as the “Anti-Sexual
Harassment Act”, and general Labor Code due process rules, none of which squarely

Philippines ratified International Labour Organization Convention No. 190 in
February 2024, becoming the first country in Asia to commit to eliminating violence
and harassment in the world of work.2 In the absence of a unified statute, employers
are left to assemble their own policies from overlapping and sometimes inconsistent
frameworks, with no clear, government-wide standard for investigation timelines,
reporting channels, or penalties.3

       This bill creates a single and enforceable statute, defining workplace bullying
in all its forms and applying uniformly across government and private
establishments. It fixes concrete investigation timelines, in place of the vague
standards that currently leave complainants uncertain of when their case will be
resolved, and extends coverage to remote work and third parties such as clients and
vendors, consistent with how modern workplaces actually operate. It also
introduces a graduated schedule of administrative fines, rather than leaving
penalties entirely to future rules, and expressly harmonizes this Act with the Safe
Spaces Act, the Anti-Sexual Harassment Act, the Occupational Safety and Health
Standards Act, and the Mental Health Act, so that victims of gender-based or sexual
misconduct continue to receive the specific protections those laws provide, without
being caught between overlapping and potentially conflicting processes.

       A worker who is bullied into silence loses more than comfort at work. They
lose their health, productivity, and often their zest for life. This Representation
therefore urges the immediate approval of this measure, so that Filipino workers
finally have a clear, enforceable law that treats their dignity at work as a right, not a
privilege left to the goodwill of their employer.

      In view of the foregoing, the immediate approval of this measure is earnestly
sought.

                          AN ACT
INSTITUTING COMPREHENSIVE POLICIES TO PREVENT AND ADDRESS
ACTS OF BULLYING, HARASSMENT, AND OTHER FORMS OF VIOLENCE IN
THE WORKPLACE, AND PRESCRIBING PENALTIES THEREFOR

      Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

      Section 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Comprehensive
Anti-Bullying in the Workplace Act”.

       SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. – It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
uphold the dignity of labor, promote the welfare of the workforce, and ensure a just,
humane, and safe working environment for all. The State shall afford full protection
to labor, local and overseas, whether organized or unorganized, against any form of
abuse, harassment, violence, or exploitation. Towards this end, the State shall
institute measures to protect the physical, mental, and emotional well-being of
workers from all forms of bullying and related misconduct in the workplace.

       SEC. 3. Definition of Terms. – As used in this Act:

          a. Workplace Bullying refers to any severe or repeated use by one or
       more employees of a written, verbal, or electronic expression or a physical act
       or gesture, or any combination thereof, directed at an employer, co-employee,
       or any person with whom he/she has professional relations or dealings
       which infringes on the right of another at work premises; or has the effect of
       actually causing or placing the latter in reasonable fear of physical or
       emotional harm or damage to its property, creating a hostile environment for
       the employee; or materially and substantially disrupting the work process or
       the orderly operation of an institution or business.​




                                            1
         b.​ Workplace refers to the physical office, premises, or any location where
      an employee performs their professional duties. This includes, but is not
      limited to, company-sponsored events, business trips, work-related social
      functions, and online communication platforms used for work purposes.​

         c.​ Prohibited Acts include, but are not limited to, the following:​

              i.​ Physical Bullying refers to any unwanted physical contact, act,
            or gesture between a person and an employer or co-employee causing
            physical or bodily harm, notwithstanding any action that may be taken
            by the victim under the Revised Penal Code or the Civil Code of the
            Philippines.​

              ii.​ Verbal and Psychological Bullying refers to any act that causes
            harm to a victim’s psyche, emotional, and/or moral well-being. This
            includes slanderous statements, accusations, name-calling, tormenting,
            making negative comments on a person’s appearance, and other forms
            of verbal abuse.​

             iii.​ Social and Relational Bullying refers to any act that undermines
            a person’s reputation, professional status, or social standing. This
            includes spreading rumors, malicious gossip, false information, and
            social exclusion.​

             iv.​ Cyberbullying refers to any form of bullying done through the
            use of technology or any electronic means, such as social media, email,
            or messaging applications, which causes distress to the victim.​

              v.​ Gender-Based Bullying refers to any act that humiliates or
            excludes a person on the basis of perceived or actual sexual
            orientation, gender identity, or expression.​

             vi.​ Management by Threats and Intimidation refers to the use of
            power or authority to threaten, intimidate, or coerce a subordinate or
            co-employee.​

            vii.​ Abuse of Authority refers to unfairly and maliciously stealing
            credit, taking advantage of a co-employee’s work, or preventing access
            to career and office opportunities without just cause.

      SEC. 4. Adoption of Anti-Bullying Policies. – All government and
non-government offices and business establishments are hereby directed to adopt,

implement, and regularly update comprehensive anti-bullying policies. Such
policies shall include provisions that:

        a.​ Clearly define and prohibit the acts enumerated in Section 3;​

        b.​ Establish clear, confidential, and accessible procedures for reporting
     prohibited acts;​

        c.​ Mandate a prompt, fair, and impartial investigation of all reports;​

        d.​ Ensure the protection of the person who reports a prohibited act,
     provides information, or is a witness, from any form of retaliation or reprisal;​

        e.​ Enable employees to anonymously report prohibited acts, provided
     that no disciplinary action shall be based solely on an anonymous report;​

         f.​ Prescribe appropriate disciplinary administrative action against
     perpetrators, including those who knowingly make false accusations;
     andOutline mechanisms for providing support, counseling, and assistance to
     victims of bullying.

     SEC. 5. Mechanisms to Address Workplace Bullying. –

        a.​ The Human Resources (HR) department, or a designated Human
     Relations Officer or a comparable body, shall be responsible for the
     implementation and oversight of the anti-bullying policies.​

        b.​ All offices and establishments shall provide their employees with a
     copy of the anti-bullying policies and include them in the employee
     handbook. The policies shall also be conspicuously posted on office walls,
     bulletin boards, and the official website, if any.​

       c.​ Any employee who witnesses, or becomes aware of, any instance of
     workplace bullying shall immediately report it to the designated office.​

        d.​ Upon receipt of a report, the designated office shall promptly initiate a
     thorough investigation.​

        e.​ If it is determined that bullying has occurred, the designated office
     shall notify the appropriate law enforcement agency if criminal charges may
     be pursued and shall take appropriate disciplinary administrative action.

     SEC. 6. Reportorial Requirements and Capacity Building. –




                                         3
          a.​ Non-Government Offices: Within six (6) months from the effectivity of
      this Act, all non-government offices and business establishments shall furnish
      the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) with a copy of their
      anti-bullying policies. This shall be a prerequisite for obtaining or renewing
      business permits.​

         b.​ Government Offices: Within six (6) months from the effectivity of this
      Act, all government offices shall furnish the Civil Service Commission (CSC)
      with a copy of their anti-bullying policies.​

          c.​ Capacity Building: All offices and establishments shall conduct
      mandatory trainings and seminars to disseminate anti-bullying policies and
      to educate employees and management on creating respectful and safe work
      environment.

       SEC. 7. Sanctions for Non-Compliance. In the rules and regulations to be
implemented pursuant to this Act, the Secretary of the DOLE and the Chairman of
the CSC shall prescribe appropriate administrative sanctions for any administrative
office or officer, or any person or office holding a comparable role, who shall fail to
comply with the requirements of this Act. In addition, erring non-government
offices and business establishments shall suffer the penalty of suspension or
revocation of their permits to operate.

      SEC. 8. Criminal and Administrative Penalties. –

         a.​ Any person who commits an act of bullying as defined in this Act shall
      be subject to the appropriate administrative disciplinary actions as prescribed
      by the respective office’s policies, without prejudice to any criminal or civil
      charges that may be filed under existing laws.​

         b.​ Any administrative officer who maliciously fails to investigate or act
      on a report of bullying shall be held administratively liable.​

         c.​ The penalties shall be specified in the implementing Rules and
      Regulations (IRR) of this Act.

       SEC. 9. Implementing the Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the DOLE and the CSC shall jointly promulgate the
necessary rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this Act.

       SEC. 11. Repealing Clause. – All laws, presidential decrees, executive orders,

      SEC. 12. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen days (15) after its
