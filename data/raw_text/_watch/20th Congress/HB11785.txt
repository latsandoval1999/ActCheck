AN ACT
ESTABLISHING A REGULATORY FRAMEWORK FOR THE PROTECTION,
HANDLING, CREMATION, BURIAL, AND OTHER AFTER-LIFE CARE SERVICES
FOR COMPANION ANIMALS, PROVIDING FOR THE REGISTRATION AND
LICENSING OF SERVICE PROVIDERS, PRESCRIBING STANDARDS AND
SAFEGUARDS, AND IMPOSING PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS THEREOF

EXPLANATORY NOTE

For many Filipino families. companion animals are more than pets. They are valued
companions and members of the household. The bond formed with them does not end when
they die, Pet owners who entrust the remains of their companion animals to after-life care
service providers do so with the expectation that these will be treated with dignity, respect. and
professionalism.

The growing demand for pet cremation, burial. and other after-life care services has given rise
to businesses and establishments offering to collect. transport, handle. store. cremate, bury.
and otherwise dispose of deceased companion animals. These services address an important
need among pet owners. However, the Provision of such services also carries significant
responsibilities, particularly because the remains entrusted to these establishments are often
irreplaceable and hold considerable emotional significance for the owners.

Recent reports of the alleged improper handling and disposal of companion animal remains in
Bulacan have brought attention to the need for stronger regulation and accountability in this
sector. Such reports raise serious concerns not only about the treatment of deceased animals,
but also about the protection of pet owners who entrust their animals’ remains to service
providers in good faith. They underscore the need for clear standards. effective oversight, and
appropriate mechanisms to ensure that these services are carried out lawfully, safely, and with
due regard for the dignity of the animals and the rights of their owners.

===== [page 2] =====
At present, there is a need for a comprehensive and coordinated regulatory framework
specifically addressing after-life care services for companion animals. The collection.
transportation. identification. handling. temporary storage, cremation, burial, and final
disposition of animal remains involve considerations relating to animal welfare, consumer
protection, environmental protection. sanitation, and public health. Without clear and uniform
standards governing these activities, gaps in regulation, monitoring, and enforcement may
expose pet Owners to deceptive practices. improper handling, unauthorized disposal, and other
violations.

This proposed measure seeks to address these concerns by establishing a regulatory
framework for after-life care services for companion animals. It mandates the registration and
licensing of service providers, prescribes minimum standards for their facilities. equipment,
personnel, and operations, and establishes safeguards for the proper identification. handling,
transportation, storage, cremation. burial, and final disposition of animal remains.

The measure likewise requires proper documentation and chain-of-custody procedures to
ensure that animal remains can be identified and accounted for from the time they are received
until their final disposition or release to the owner. It requires meaningful disclosure and
informed written consent, particularly with respect to individual and communal cremation, so
that pet owners are fully informed of the services they avail themselves of and the manner in
which their animals' remains will be handled.

Recognizing that after-life care services also have implications for the environment, sanitation,
and public health, the proposed measure provides for compliance with applicable
environmental, sanitation. and public-health requirements. It further establishes the respective
roles of national government agencies and local government units in setting standards.
licensing. inspecting, monitoring, and enforcing compliance. Appropriate administrative and
criminal liabilities are likewise provided to ensure accountability for violations.

Ultimately. this measure recognizes that the responsibility to protect companion animals
should not end upon their death. Animals whose remains are entrusted to after-life care service
providers deserve dignified treatment, while their owners deserve the assurance that the trust
they have placed in these establishments will not be abused.

In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Section 1. Short Title. - This Act shall be known as the “Beyond Pet Loss and Care Act.”

Sec. 2. Declaration of Policy. - \t is the policy of the State to recognize the special
relationship between humans and companion animals and to promote their humane and
dignified treatment throughout their lives, including after death.

The State recognizes that companion animals are often regarded as members of the family and
that their owners or custodians entrust their remains to service providers with the expectation
that they will be handled with care. respect. transparency, and accountability.

Toward this end. the State shall establish a regulatory framework governing the collection,
transportation, handling. storage. cremation. burial, and other after-life care services for
companion animals. It shall ensure that such services are conducted in a manner that respects
the dignity of deceased companion animals, protects the interests and trust of pet owners,
safeguards public health and the environment, and prevents deceptive. abusive, or improper
practices.

Sec. 8. Minimum Standards for F. acilities, Personnel, and Handling of Animal Remains. -
All after-life care service providers shall maintain facilities. equipment. personnel, and
operational procedures that ensure the safe. sanitary, secure, and dignified handling of
deceased companion animals from receipt until their final disposition.

For this purpose, all service providers shall comply with the following minimum standards:

a. Reception and Identification. Every deceased companion animal received shall be
assigned a unique identification number or tracking reference. The provider shall
record, at a minimum, the date and time of receipt. the identity and contact details of
the owner or authorized representative, the animal's description, the service requested,
and the condition of the remains upon receipt. An identification tag or label shall be
securely attached to the container holding the remains and maintained throughout the
service process.

b. Collection and Handling. Animal remains shall be handled with dignity and care, using
appropriate protective equipment and handling procedures. They shall be placed in
suitable, leak-proof, properly identified containers and shall not be dragged, exposed
unnecessarily, abandoned. or handled in a manner that causes avoidable damage.
contamination, or misidentification.

c. Transportation. The transport of animal remains shall be undertaken using appropriate,
secure, and properly maintained vehicles or containers that prevent leakage, exposure,
unauthorized access. loss, and mixing of remains. Transport containers and vehicles
shall be cleaned and disinfected as necessary.

d. Temporary Storage. Animal remains awaiting cremation, burial, or other authorized
disposition shall be stored in a designated, secure, sanitary, and appropriately
maintained area. Storage conditions shall prevent decomposition. leakage.
contamination, unauthorized access, and deterioration beyond what is reasonably
unavoidable. The remains shall be properly identified and segregated to prevent
accidental mixing or substitution.

===== [page 8] =====
e. Facility Requirements. Facilities shall have designated areas for receiving, identifying,
handling, preparing, storing. processing. and releasing animal remains, as appropriate
to the services offered. These areas shall be maintained in a sanitary condition and
protected against pests, unauthorized entry, and environmental contamination,

f. Personnel and Training. Personnel assigned to receive, handle. transport. store.
cremate, bury, or release animal remains shal] be adequately trained in proper handling
procedures, sanitation. occupational safety, identification, documentation. and
chain-of-custody requirements. The provider shall designate a person responsible for
supervising compliance with these standards.

g. Sanitation and Biosecurity. Appropriate cleaning, disinfection, waste-management. and
biosecurity procedures shall be implemented to prevent the spread of infectious agents.
contamination. and occupational exposure. Where the circumstances indicate a
possible infectious or zoonotic disease risk. the provider shall observe applicable
veterinary, public-health, and regulatory requirements.

h. Security and Access Control. Access to areas where animal remains are received.
stored. processed. or prepared for release shall be restricted to authorized personnel.
The provider shall adopt measures to prevent unauthorized removal. substitution,
tampering, or disposal of remains.

i. Incident Reporting. Any loss, misidentification, accidental mixing, damage.
unauthorized access. or other incident affecting the identity, condition, or disposition of
animal remains shall be documented and promptly reported to the owner or authorized
representative and the appropriate regulatory authority.

j. Compliance with Technical Standards. All facilities, equipment, and procedures shall
comply with the technical specifications and minimum operational requirements
prescribed by the Bureau of Animal Industry and other concerned regulatory agencies
under this Act and its implementing rules and regulations.

Sec. 9. Standards for Cremation and Burial Services. - All| cremation, burial. and other final
disposition services shall be conducted in accordance with the standards prescribed under this
Act and its implementing rules and regulations. No service provider shall undertake any
irreversible process or final disposition without the prior informed written consent of the
owner or authorized representative, except where otherwise authorized by law.

For this purpose, service providers shall observe the following requirements:

a. Disclosure of Services. Before accepting the remains, the provider shall clearly
disclose the nature. process, limitations. and charges of the service offered, including
whether cremation is individual or communal, and whether the resulting ashes will be
returned to the owner or authorized representative.

b. Individual Cremation. Individual cremation shall be conducted under procedures and
safeguards reasonably designed to ensure that the ashes returned to the owner belong
exclusively to the companion animal entrusted to the provider. The provider shall
maintain appropriate identification. segregation, monitoring. and documentation
throughout the process.

c. Communal Cremation. Communal cremation shall involve the cremation of two or
more animals together without the segregation of their remains. The provider shall
expressly inform the owner that the ashes cannot be individually identified or
represented as belonging exclusively to their companion animal. Written consent shall
be obtained before proceeding.

d. Cremation Equipment and Operations. Cremation shall be performed only using
equipment designed and suitable for the purpose, operated and maintained in

===== [page 9] =====
accordance with applicable technical, environmental. occupational safety. and
emissions-control requirements. The provider shall maintain operational and
maintenance records.

e. Handling and Release of Cremated Remains. Ashes intended for return shall be
properly collected, identified, placed in suitable containers. securely labeled, stored,
and released only to the owner or authorized representative. The provider shall issue
appropriate documentation identifying the service performed and confirming the
release of the cremated remains.

f. Burial and Interment. Burial shall be conducted only in an authorized and properly
designated burial facility or site, in accordance with applicable zoning. land-use,
environmental, sanitation, and public-health requirements. Each burial shall be
documented. and the location of the burial shall be recorded and identifiable.

g. Other Methods of Final Disposition. Any other method of disposition shall be lawful.
environmentally sound. sanitary, and consistent with the standards prescribed under
this Act and its implementing rules and regulations. No animal remains shall be
abandoned, dumped, or disposed of in an unauthorized location.

h. Prohibition Against Misrepresentation and Unauthorized Disposition. No provider
shall substitute. misidentify, conceal, abandon, dump, or improperly dispose of animal
remains. mix remains without the informed written consent required for communal
cremation or another lawful agreed service. or make any false representation
concerning the cremation, burial. or final disposition service rendered. Administrative
liability for violations of these duties shall be determined under Section 14: criminal
liability shatl arise only where the elements of an offense expressly defined in Section
12 are established.

i. Unclaimed Remains. The provider shall establish a written procedure for handling
unclaimed remains, including reasonable efforts to notify and contact the owner or
authorized representative. No remains shall be cremated. buried, transferred, or
otherwise disposed of without the consent or lawful authority required under this Act.

expressly defined in Section 12, including a responsible individual identified under Section
16, shall. upon conviction and proof beyond reasonable doubt of all its elements. be punished
with a fine of not less than One Hundred Thousand Pesos (P100,000.00) but not more than
Five Hundred Thousand Pesos (P500.000.00), or imprisonment of not less than one (1) year
but not more than four (4) years. or both, at the discretion of the court. The administrative fine
ranges in Section 14 shall not be treated as additional criminal penalties.

Where the violation involves fraud, deliberate falsification or misrepresentation, unauthorized
abandonment or improper disposal of remains, or results in serious environmental or
public-health harm, the maximum penalty may be imposed. subject to the circumstances of the
case.

In addition to the penalties imposed under this Section. the court may order the cancellation or
revocation of the offender's license to operate and recommend to the appropriate government
agency the cancellation or revocation of other permits or authorizations issued in connection
with the establishment's operations.

The penalties under this Section shall be without prejudice to civil liability and liability under
other applicable laws.

See. 16. Liability of Juridical Persons, - Where an offense under Section 12 is committed in
the operation of a corporation, partnership, association. or other juridical entity, criminal
liability shall attach only to the officer, director, manager, employee, or other natural person
who directly committed or authorized the prohibited act. or knowingly permitted it despite
having actual authority and ability to prevent it. The elements of the applicable offense and the
individual's participation or culpable omission shall be proved beyond reasonable doubt.

The juridical entity shall remain subject to administrative liability under Section 14, including
suspension or revocation of its local license. and civil liability under applicable taw.

For offenses under Section 12(b) or (i) based on gross negligence, an officer or employee shall
be criminally liable only upon proof beyond reasonable doubt that the individual had a
specific legal or assigned operational duty and actual authority and ability to perform it, and
that the individual's own grossly negligent act or omission caused the prohibited result or
danger. Corporate title, board membership, or a general supervisory position alone shall not
establish criminal liability. The fact that an employee or subordinate carried out the act shall
not exempt an individual whose own conduct satisfies the requirements of this Section.

Sec. 19. Implementing Rules and Regulations. - Within one hundred eighty (180) days from
the effectivity of this Act. the Department of Agriculture (DA), through the Bureau of Animal
Industry (BAI), and in coordination with the Department of the Interior and Local
Government (DILG), the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), the
Department of Health (DOH), the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), concerned local
government units (LGUs), and other relevant government agencies, shall promulgate the rules
and regulations necessary for the effective implementation of this Act.

The implementing rules and regulations shall include provisions on the following:

a. The registration. licensing. inspection. monitoring, and regulation of after-life care
service providers and their establishments:

b. The minimum requirements and standards for facilities, equipment, personnel, and the
collection, identification, handling, transportation, storage, and safekeeping of animal
remains:

c. The technical and operational requirements for cremation, including individual and
communal cremation, and the proper identification, handling. storage, and release of
cremated remains;

d. The establishment, operation, maintenance. and closure of pet cemeteries and burial
facilities. including site requirements. burial procedures, and safeguards against
environmental contamination:

e. Environmental protection, emissions control. sanitation, public health, wastewater
management, and the handling and disposal of waste generated by after-life care
operations:

f. Service agreements, disclosure requirements. informed consent, transparent service
fees and charges, consumer protection. and procedures for receiving and resolving
complaints, without displacing the sanggunian's authority to fix local regulatory fees
by ordinance:

===== [page 15] =====
g. Identification and documentation systems, recordkeeping. chain-of-custody
procedures, and the retention and submission of records:

h. Inspection safeguards, investigation, enforcement, administrative notice and hearing,
reasoned decisions, reconsideration, and coordination and referral among competent
authorities. consistent with Sections 5. 13. and 14 and without altering judicial
procedures governed by the Rules of Court: and

i. Such other rules and measures as may be necessary to carry out the purposes and
provisions of this Act.

In formulating the implementing rules and regulations, the DA, through the BAI, shall consult
concerned government agencies, LGUs. animal welfare organizations, veterinarians. pet
owners, environmental and public-health experts, and legitimate after-life care service
providers.

The implementing rules and regulations shall prescribe uniform minimum national standards
within the purposes, duties. and limits established by this Act. They shall not create additional
criminal offenses, change the elements of offenses under Section 12. increase the penalties or
administrative fine limits, or confer sanctioning powers withheld by this Act. LGUs may,
through appropriate ordinances, adopt additional or more stringent measures suited to local
conditions, provided these are consistent with this Act. its implementing rules and regulations.
and other applicable laws.

Sec. 20. Transitory Provision. - After-life care service providers already operating before the
effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations shall be given one (1) year from the
effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations to secure the new local license under
Section 6 and comply with the new facility, equipment, and personnel requirements. The new
licensing requirement shall become enforceable against such providers upon expiration of that
period. A provider commencing operations on or after the effectivity of the implementing
rules and regulations shall secure the new local license before operating.

Before the effectivity of the implementing rules and regulations, the new local licensing
requirement under this Act shall not be enforced through the penalties in Sections 12(a), 14,
and 15. Existing business-permit and other licensing requirements under applicable laws shall
continue to apply.

During the transition period, concerned LGUs shall undertake an inventory of existing
establishments and provide information regarding registration and licensing requirements.

The transition period shall not excuse fraud. substitution. unauthorized mixing or disposition.
abandonment. dumping. or other criminal acts under Section 12(b) to (k) committed after the
effectivity of this Act. Duties capable of compliance without further technical rules, including
truthful disclosure. informed written consent, identification, traceability, and incident
reporting, shall apply upon the effectivity of this Act. The transition period shall not exempt
any establishment from existing environmental, sanitation. public-health. fire-safety,
business-permit, and other applicable laws and regulations.
