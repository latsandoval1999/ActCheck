                            AN ACT
AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10149, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “GOCC
                  GOVERNANCE ACT OF 2011”

                              EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Republic Act No. 10149, otherwise known as the “GOCC Governance Act of
2011,” was enacted to promote transparency, accountability, and financial discipline
among Government-Owned or -Controlled Corporations (GOCCs). It established the
Governance Commission for GOCCs (GCG) as the central oversight body mandated
to ensure that GOCCs operate efficiently, remain relevant to national development
objectives, and uphold sound corporate governance.

       More than a decade after its enactment, experience in the implementation of
the law has revealed the need to strengthen and update several provisions in order
to address emerging governance challenges, clarify regulatory authority, and provide
the GCG with more effective tools to rationalize the GOCC sector.

        This measure seeks to amend Republic Act No. 10149 to strengthen the
institutional capacity of the GCG and improve the governance framework of GOCCs.

       First, the bill expands and clarifies key definitions and policy mechanisms
within the law, including the concepts of merger, consolidation, and regularization of
GOCCs. These reforms will enable the government to rationalize entities whose
mandates overlap, whose corporate form is no longer appropriate, or whose
functions are better suited within the structure of regular government agencies. By
providing clear statutory authority for these restructuring mechanisms, the measure
supports the efficient use of public resources and the elimination of redundant
functions within government.

       Second, the bill further clarifies the coverage and classification of
GOCCs under the law, ensuring that entities that are effectively government-
controlled fall within the regulatory jurisdiction of the GCG. At the same time, it

                                                                                    1
recognizes certain institutions—such as government hospitals—whose operational
nature requires exclusion from the coverage of the Act.

       Third, the measure strengthens the powers and functions of the GCG by
granting it additional regulatory and quasi-judicial authorities necessary for effective
oversight. These include the authority to issue subpoenas, conduct administrative
proceedings, impose sanctions for noncompliance, and require the submission of
information necessary for governance and accountability. These mechanisms will
allow the Commission to more effectively enforce corporate governance standards
and ensure compliance with national policies governing GOCC operations.

        Fourth, the bill introduces reforms to improve the institutional structure and
independence of the GCG. It provides clearer qualifications and fixed terms of office
for the Chairperson and Commissioners, establishes the position of Executive
Director, and defines the organizational structure of the Commission. These reforms
are intended to professionalize the leadership of the GCG and ensure continuity in
policy implementation.

      Fifth, the measure provides legal protection mechanisms, such as qualified
immunity and indemnification, for the officials and employees of the Commission
who act in good faith in the performance of their duties. These provisions recognize
the complex and sensitive nature of GOCC oversight and ensure that officials can
perform their mandate without undue legal risk.

       Finally, the bill introduces additional administrative and financial provisions
that will enable the GCG to effectively carry out its mandate, including the authority
to retain a portion of its income for operational requirements, reorganize its internal
structure, and impose penalties for acts that obstruct or undermine its regulatory
functions.

      Through these reforms, the measure seeks to reinforce the principles of good
governance, accountability, fiscal discipline, and institutional efficiency in the
management of GOCCs. By strengthening the regulatory framework and
empowering the Governance Commission for GOCCs, the bill ensures that
government corporations remain aligned with their public mandates and contribute
meaningfully to national development.

       In view of the foregoing, the approval of this measure is earnestly sought.
