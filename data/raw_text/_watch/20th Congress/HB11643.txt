                         AN ACT
EXPANDING THE PRIVILEGES OF PERSONS WITH DISABILITY TO
INCLUDE FREE PARKING IN ALL ESTABLISHMENTS AND EXEMPTION
FROM THE NUMBER CODING SCHEME, FURTHER AMENDING FOR THE
PURPOSE SECTION 32 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7277, AS AMENDED,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “MAGNA CARTA FOR PERSONS WITH
DISABILITY”

                                             EXPLANATORY NOTE

The Bible reminds us in Proverbs 31:8–9: "Speak up for those who cannot speak for
themselves, for the rights of all who are destitute. Speak up and judge fairly; defend
the rights of the poor and needy." This is a call to stand beside those whom society
too often leaves behind, so that persons with disabilities may live with dignity,
independence, and equal opportunity to take part in the life of the nation.

The 2020 Census of Population and Housing found that about 8.47 million Filipinos
aged five and over have difficulty in at least one functional domain, which the PSA
reported as 8.7 percent of the household population. Among them, 1,895,026 have
difficulty walking or climbing steps. For these Filipinos, the distance from the parking
space to the door is only part of the journey. Over 135,000 have severe difficulty
walking, and over 288,000 have moderate difficulty.1 For many persons with physical
disabilities, walking to a destination or using available public transportation may not
be feasible, making travel by private vehicle a necessity. Accessibility therefore
extends beyond the distance from a parking space to the door: it also depends on
whether they can afford the journey itself. Every trip may require careful planning,
and even modest parking fees can add to the financial burden of reaching medical
appointments, workplaces, and other essential destinations.

Batas Pambansa Blg. 344 recognizes the right of persons with disabilities to access
physically accessible parking facilities. However, the provision of a reserved parking
space offers limited benefit when parking fees impose an additional financial burden
on households already managing the costs of medicines, therapy, and assistive
devices. Similarly, such accessibility measures lose their practical value when
vehicle-number schemes compel persons with disabilities to postpone or cancel
medical appointments, work obligations, or family engagements. These
circumstances should not be regarded as mere inconveniences. Rather, they
constitute recurring barriers that, over time, can significantly restrict a person’s
mobility, independence, and ability to participate fully in community life.

Some local governments have chosen to act. Taguig City maintains a dedicated PWD
Affairs Office, provides free rehabilitation and therapy services, and delivers assistive
devices to people's homes.2 Pasig City exempts vehicles carrying PWDs from its
number-coding scheme3, and Marilao exempts PWDs from parking fees in business
establishments and similar places.4 Their example demonstrates that genuine
inclusion extends beyond the recognition of rights on paper. It requires ensuring that
essential services and support are accessible, affordable, and within reach of those
who need them most.

The proposed "Free Parking and Coding Exemption for Persons with
Disability Act" carries this compassion into national policy. It seeks to:

     1. Grant free parking in commercial establishments and government facilities
        that charge parking fees; and
     2. Exempt vehicles carrying or driven by a person with disability from the Unified
        Vehicular Volume Reduction Program, or number coding scheme.

No Filipino should have to choose between a necessary trip and the cost or
restriction that comes with it. By removing these barriers, the State affirms that
persons with disabilities have an equal right to move freely, to work, to heal, to
worship, and to be with their loved ones.

In view of the foregoing, the early approval of this bill is earnestly sought.

                         AN ACT
EXPANDING THE PRIVILEGES OF PERSONS WITH DISABILITY TO
INCLUDE FREE PARKING IN ALL ESTABLISHMENTS AND EXEMPTION
FROM THE NUMBER CODING SCHEME, FURTHER AMENDING FOR THE
PURPOSE SECTION 32 OF REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7277, AS AMENDED,
OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE “MAGNA CARTA FOR PERSONS WITH
DISABILITY”

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Free Parking and
Coding Exemption for Persons with Disability Act”.

SECTION 2. Section 32 of Republic Act No. 7277, as amended by Republic Act No.
9442 and Republic Act No. 10754, is hereby further amended to read as follows:

      "SEC. 32. Persons with disability shall be entitled to:

                                          XXX

      (d) To the extent possible, the government may grant special discounts in
      special programs for PWD on purchase of basic commodities, subject to the
      guidelines to be issued for the purpose by the Department of Trade and
      Industry (DTI) and the Department of Agriculture (DA); and

      (e) Provision of express lanes for PWD in all commercial and government
      establishments; in the absence thereof, priority shall be given to them.;

      (F) FREE PARKING IN ALL COMMERCIAL ESTABLISHMENTS AND
      GOVERNMENT FACILITIES THAT CHARGE PARKING FEES, FOR
      VEHICLES DRIVEN BY OR CARRYING A PERSON WITH DISABILITY;
      AND
       (G) EXEMPTION FROM THE UNIFIED VEHICULAR VOLUME
       REDUCTION PROGRAM OR NUMBER CODING SCHEME OF THE
       GOVERNMENT FOR VEHICLES WHERE A PERSON WITH DISABILITY
       IS EITHER THE DRIVER OR A PASSENGER.

                                           X X X”

SECTION 3. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within sixty (60) days
from the effectivity of this Act, the Department of Social Welfare and Development
(DSWD), in coordination with the National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA), the
Department of Transportation (DOTr), the Land Transportation Office (LTO), the
Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA), the Department of the Interior
and Local Government (DILG), and the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI),
shall promulgate the necessary rules and regulations for the effective
implementation of this Act.

SECTION 4. Separability Clause. – If any provision of this Act is held invalid or
unconstitutional, the remainder of the Act or the provisions not otherwise affected
shall remain valid and subsisting.

SECTION 5. Repealing Clause. – Any law, presidential decree or issuance,
executive order, letter of instruction, administrative rule or regulation contrary to or
inconsistent with the provisions of this Act is hereby repealed, modified or amended
accordingly.

SECTION 6. Effectivity. – This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) national newspapers of
general circulation.
