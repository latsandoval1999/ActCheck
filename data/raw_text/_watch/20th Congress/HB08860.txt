                                  EXPLANATORY NOTE

        The 1987 Constitution is unequivocal: “the Congress shall give highest priority
to the enactment of measures that protect and enhance the right of all the people to
human dignity, reduce social, economic, and political inequalities, and remove cultural
inequities by equitably diffusing wealth and political power for the common good”
(Section 1, Article XIII). It further mandates Congress to “evolve a progressive system
of taxation” (Section 28(1), Article VI).

         Through this proposed “Patas na Ambag sa Tax para sa Angat-Sahod” (PATAS)
Act, the TUCP Party-list fulfills its constitutional duty with urgency and resolve,
especially in this era of extreme inequality and extraordinary challenges that continue
to crush and leave behind middle-income earners, who bear the brunt not only of this
crisis, but of inaction.

        The Forbes 2026 World’s Billionaires List includes 15 Filipino billionaires with
a combined net worth of $44.5 billion or about ₱2.49 trillion.1 In comparison, the wealth
of just 15 wealthiest Filipinos amounts to more than one-third of our national budget
this year of ₱6.793 trillion. Yet, as one column aptly observes, it is the middle class that
“bears a disproportionately heavy tax burden” through withholding income tax while
the wealthiest benefit from existing tax structures and loopholes and worse, middle-
income earners are often excluded from government social assistance programs
reserved for the poorest households.2

        That is why the TUCP Party-list is filing this comprehensive progressive tax
reform package to advance social equity by easing the tax burden on middle-income
earners while ensuring that the ultra-rich, and those reaping windfall profits,
particularly in the oil and electricity sectors, pay their fair share.

         First, this bill provides immediate meaningful tax relief to the Filipino middle
class. It raises the income tax exemption to ₱600,000 annually, increases the tax-exempt
ceiling for 13th month pay and other benefits, and removes taxes on holiday pay,
overtime pay, night shift differential pay, hazard pay, and service charges. This ensures
that workers earning approximately ₱50,000 monthly or below, which approximates
the latest indicative monthly family income for lower middle-income households,3 are
exempted and no longer burdened by personal income taxes. It also affirms that income
earned through additional labor or longer hours should not be further penalized by
additional taxation.

        Second, to address concerns on revenue sustainability, the bill introduces a
Solidarity Wealth Tax on the ultra-rich. Individuals with net worth of at least ₱1 billion,
determined under uniform valuation standards, and subject to audit procedures and
robust anti-avoidance rules, shall be taxed on a progressive basis. IBON Foundation
estimates that a wealth tax on the country’s 3,000 billionaires could generate at least
₱500 billion annually.4 This substantial additional fiscal space can offset potential
revenue losses from higher income tax exemptions while strengthening funding for
essential public services. Moreover, a study by the University of the Philippines Center
for Integrative and Development Studies (UP CIDS) comprehensively rebuts that far
from harming the economy, evidence suggests that (i) it is extreme inequality, not
wealth taxation, that constrains sustainable growth; (ii) wealth tax is not double taxation
as it applies to accumulated wealth rather than income flows subjected to income tax;
(iii) wealth tax targets only extreme concentration of wealth, which is often passive or
speculative in nature, without burdening ordinary business activity. In fact, since taxing
and redistributing wealth can further stimulate economic growth and generate decent
jobs, the study concludes that “a wealth tax is now widely accepted as an urgent,
necessary, and feasible solution” especially as inequality is rising faster than incomes.5

        Third, this bill imposes a Windfall Profits Tax on corporations that earn
extraordinary or excess profits, particularly in the oil and electricity industries. These
industries, shaped by decades of deregulation and privatization, have repeatedly
demonstrated the capacity to generate excessive profits, especially during crises, while
consumers bear the burden of high prices. This ensures that profits exceeding a defined
normal profit threshold, whether based on historical earnings or the long-recognized
12% reasonable rate of return for public utilities6, are fairly taxed. This tax is neither
novel nor punitive. Across Europe, Germany, Italy, Spain, Portugal and Austria have
called for a windfall tax on profits of energy companies in response to surging fuel

        Taken together, this proposed PATAS Act forms a more progressive, targeted,
balanced, and most importantly, a more humane public fiscal strategy that provides
higher tax exemption relief to our hardworking middle class while asking those who
have gained the most, through extraordinary wealth and profit, to contribute their fair
share to the nation and our people. For far too long, the burden has weighed heaviest
on those who can least afford it, such as our over five million minimum wage earners
as well as our struggling middle-income earners. It is high time to reaffirm the simple
but powerful truth that those who have more must contribute more, and those who have
less must be protected more. Together, let us push for this measure that breathes life
and meaning to the promise of our Constitution: a progressive tax system that is not
only efficient but equitable and not only capable of raising revenue but committed to
serving social justice.

        In view of the foregoing, the passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Patas na Ambag sa Tax
para sa Angat-Sahod” (PATAS) Act.

SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy. - It is the policy of the State to promote social justice
in all phases of national development, mandating that the Congress shall give highest
priority to the enactment of measures that protect and enhance the right of all the people
to human dignity, reduce social, economic, and political inequalities, and remove
cultural inequities by equitably diffusing wealth and political power for the common
good such as through evolving a progressive system of taxation.

SEC. 5. - A new section designated as Section 27 under Chapter III, Title II of the
National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended, is hereby inserted to read as
follows:

       SEC. 27. SOLIDARITY WEALTH TAX. - THERE IS HEREBY
       IMPOSED AN ANNUAL TAX ON EVERY INDIVIDUAL WHOSE
       NET WORTH AMOUNT TO AT LEAST ONE BILLION PESOS
       (P1,000,000,000).

       (A) NET WORTH REFERS TO THE FAIR MARKET VALUE OF
       ALL ASSETS, REAL OR PERSONAL, TANGIBLE OR
       INTANGIBLE, WHEREVER SITUATED, OWNED DIRECTLY
       OR INDIRECTLY BY THE TAXPAYERS, LESS LIABILITIES, AS
       DETERMINED AS OF 31 DECEMBER OF THE TAXABLE YEAR.
       ASSETS HELD THROUGH CORPORATIONS, PARTNERSHIPS,
       TRUSTS, OR SIMILAR ENTITIES AND ARRANGEMENTS
       SHALL BE ATTRIBUTED TO THE BENEFICIAL OWNER. THE
       SECRETARY OF FINANCE, UPON RECOMMENDATION OF
       THE COMMISSIONER OF INTERNAL REVENUE, SHALL
       ISSUE RULES AND REGULATIONS PRESCRIBING UNIFORM
       VALUATION STANDARDS, INCLUDING AUDIT PROCEDURES
       AND ANTI-AVOIDANCE MEASURES.

       (B) THE SOLIDARITY WEALTH TAX SHALL BE IMPOSED IN
       ACCORDANCE WITH THE FOLLOWING TAX SCHEDULE:

       NET       WORTH     OF   P1,000,000,000  UP   TO
       P2,000,000,000………………………………………………………..1%
       NET      WORTH     OVER   P2,000,000,000 UP   TO
       P3,000,000,000………………………………………………………..2%
       NET                   WORTH                 OVER
       P3,000,000,000…….…………………………………………...……..3%

       (C) EVERY TAXPAYER SUBJECT TO THE SOLIDARITY
       WEALTH TAX SHALL FILE AN ANNUAL SOLIDARITY
       WEALTH TAX RETURN ON OR BEFORE APRIL 15 OF THE
       FOLLOWING TAXABLE YEAR.

       CHAPTER:

       AVERAGE ANNUAL TAXABLE INCOME OF THE TAXPAYER
       FOR THE IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING THREE (3) YEARS,
       ADJUSTED FOR INFLATION, OR A TWELVE PERCENT (12%)

       WINDFALL PROFITS OF OVER 50% ABOVE NORMAL PROFIT
       THRESHOLD………………………………………………………35%

SEC. 7. Separability Clause. - If any provision or part of this Act is declared invalid or
unconstitutional, the remaining provisions or parts of this act shall remain in full force
and effect, and the application of such provision to other persons or circumstances shall
not be affected.

SEC. 8. Repealing Clause. - All laws, orders, issuances, rules, and regulations or parts
thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed, amended, or
modified accordingly.
