                           EXPLANATORY NOTE

       Higher education is a cornerstone of national development, playing a
central role in building human capital, advancing innovation, promoting social
mobility, and enhancing global competitiveness. At the heart of a strong higher
education system are its faculty members—teachers, researchers, mentors, and
academic leaders whose qualifications, competencies, and scholarly productivity
directly influence instructional quality, student achievement, and the creation and
application of knowledge.

        Despite sustained efforts to reform the higher education sector, faculty
development and research in the Philippines continue to face persistent and
structural challenges. Existing scholarship, research, and capacity-building
initiatives have often been fragmented, short-term, and dependent on changing
administrative priorities. The absence of a unified, statutory, and long-term
framework has contributed to uneven faculty qualifications across regions and
institution types, underdeveloped institutional research systems, limited graduate
supervision capacity, and weak alignment between higher education outputs and
national development objectives.

       Significant disparities remain between well-resourced institutions and
those located in geographically isolated, disadvantaged, or emerging areas. Many
higher education institutions (HEIs) experience difficulty in meeting faculty
qualification standards, sustaining graduate programs, or developing a critical
mass of research-active faculty. These constraints adversely affect teaching
quality, restrict innovation, weaken graduate education, and diminish the
contribution of higher education to economic growth, social progress, and
evidence-based policymaking.

       This proposed measure, entitled the “Philippine Higher Education Faculty
Development and Research Act,” seeks to address these challenges by
establishing a comprehensive, coherent, and sustainable national framework for
faculty development and research. It explicitly affirms faculty development and
research as core, integral, and continuing functions of higher education, alongside
instruction and extension.

       The bill mandates the formulation of a National Faculty Development and
Research Framework to be led by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED),
providing a unified policy direction for faculty development, research capacity
strengthening, and graduate education enhancement. The framework adopts a
developmental, differentiated, and equity-sensitive approach that respects
institutional autonomy while recognizing variations in institutional mandates,
typologies, stages of development, and regional contexts.

       The measure institutionalizes a broad range of faculty development and
research interventions, including graduate and post-graduate studies, teaching
and learning innovations, research capability-building, academic leadership
development, industry and community immersion, and mentoring and peer-
learning systems. It likewise strengthens the higher education research ecosystem
through competitive research initiatives, support for early-career researchers,
strengthened research ethics and governance, enhanced graduate research
supervision, and mechanisms for research dissemination and utilization.

       The bill further reinforces governance and accountability by establishing a
permanent Faculty Development and Research Service within CHED,
institutionalizing systematic monitoring and evaluation, requiring regular
                                                                                 2
reporting to Congress, and creating a National Higher Education Research
Repository to promote transparency, accessibility, and knowledge sharing.
       Ultimately, this measure provides a long-overdue statutory foundation to
professionalize, sustain, and equalize faculty development and research across
Philippine higher education, ensuring that improvements in teaching quality,
research capacity, and academic leadership translate into meaningful and lasting
contributions to national development.

      In view of the foregoing, approval of this bill is earnestly requested.

SECTION 1. Title — This Act shall be known as the “Philippine Higher
Education Faculty Development and Research Act.”


SEC. 2. Declaration of Policy — It is hereby declared the policy of the State to
strengthen Philippine higher education as a strategic driver of inclusive growth,
social mobility, innovation, and national competitiveness by institutionalizing a
comprehensive, coherent, adequately funded, and outcomes-oriented framework
for faculty development and research.
The State recognizes that faculty members constitute the intellectual backbone of
the higher education system. They are the primary agents through whom teaching
quality is assured, student learning outcomes are realized, graduate education is
sustained, research and innovation are advanced, and knowledge is translated into
policy, industry, and community impact. The quality, relevance, and credibility
of higher education institutions are inextricably linked to the qualifications,
competencies, research capacity, and ethical standards of their faculty.


The State further recognizes that structural and systemic challenges—such as
uneven faculty qualifications across regions, limited access to graduate education
and research funding, fragmented and short-term faculty development programs,
weak institutional research systems, and the absence of a permanent statutory
framework—have constrained the ability of Philippine higher education to
respond effectively to evolving national development priorities, workforce
transformation, technological disruption, and global academic competition.


Accordingly, the State shall adopt a developmental, differentiated, and equity-
sensitive approach to faculty development and research that:


   (a) Strengthens institutional capacity across all regions and institution types;
   (b) Promotes excellence, accountability, and continuous improvement;
   (c) Supports emerging and resource-constrained institutions without
      undermining standards; and
   (d) Ensures alignment with national education reform priorities, science and
      technology strategies, innovation agendas, and the National Research and
      Development Agenda.


                                                                                  2
SEC. 3. Objectives — This Act shall pursue the following objectives:


   (a) To establish a unified, statutory, and long-term national framework for
      faculty development and research that transcends fragmented programs
      and shifting administrative priorities;
   (b) To systematically improve faculty qualifications, teaching effectiveness,
      research competencies, innovation capacity, and academic leadership
      across disciplines, sectors, and institution types;
   (c) To strengthen the research ecosystem of higher education institutions by
      enhancing research governance, ethics, supervision, infrastructure,
      publication, utilization, and innovation pathways;
   (d) To institutionalize sustainable, transparent, performance-based, and
      equity-sensitive funding mechanisms that support both excellence and
      developmental catch-up;
   (e) To professionalize and strengthen the institutional capacity of the
      Commission on Higher Education (CHED) to govern, coordinate, monitor,
      and evaluate faculty development and research initiatives using evidence-
      based and outcomes-oriented approaches; and
   (f) To ensure that faculty development and research investments translate into
      measurable contributions to national development, workforce readiness,
      public policy, technological advancement, and balanced regional growth.

higher education institutions (HEIs), including state universities and colleges
(SUCs), local universities and colleges (LUCs), and private HEIs duly recognized
by the Commission on Higher Education.

SEC. 6. Faculty Development and Research as Core Higher Education
Functions — Faculty development and research are hereby declared core,
inseparable, and continuing functions of higher education institutions, alongside
instruction and extension, and shall be recognized as essential institutional
responsibilities fundamental to the delivery of quality, relevant, and responsive
higher education.


As such, higher education institutions shall systematically plan, support, and
sustain faculty development and research activities as integral components of
their academic mission, organizational structure, and resource allocation, and
shall not treat such functions as incidental, optional, or ancillary to instruction.


The Commission on Higher Education shall ensure that faculty development and
research are fully integrated and institutionalized within higher education policies
and practices, including, but not limited to, institutional development and
strategic plans, outcomes-based education frameworks, graduate program design
and strengthening, accreditation and quality assurance systems, faculty workload
and career progression frameworks, and institutional performance evaluation and
accountability mechanisms.


CHED shall further adopt policies, standards, and monitoring mechanisms to
ensure that the effective performance of faculty development and research
functions contributes to continuous improvement in teaching quality, research

productivity, graduate education outcomes, and institutional and system-level
performance.


SEC. 7. Faculty Development and Research Framework — The Commission
on Higher Education (CHED) shall formulate, adopt, and periodically review and
update a National Faculty Development and Research Framework, which shall
serve as the primary and overarching policy instrument governing the design,
implementation, coordination, and evaluation of all faculty development and
research initiatives under this Act.


The Framework shall articulate a coherent national direction for faculty
development and research, ensure alignment with national development
priorities, and provide a common reference for higher education institutions in
planning, implementing, and assessing faculty development and research
programs, consistent with the principles of institutional autonomy and academic
freedom.


In the formulation, review, and updating of the Framework, CHED shall
undertake meaningful consultation and stakeholder engagement, which shall
include, but not be limited to, public and private higher education institutions,
faculty organizations and academic staff associations, recognized professional
and disciplinary bodies, research institutions, industry and employer groups,
relevant government agencies, and other stakeholders as may be determined by
the Commission.


The Framework shall:
   (a) Establish clear national standards, developmental pathways, progression
       models, and performance benchmarks for faculty development and

                                                                               8
      research, taking into account disciplinary norms, professional standards,
      and international best practices;
   (b) Recognize and operationalize differentiated institutional missions,
      typologies, mandates, and development stages, including distinctions
      among teaching-focused, research-oriented, and hybrid institutions, and
      allow for varied approaches consistent with such differentiation;
   (c) Integrate teaching excellence, research productivity, innovation and
      creative work, graduate education strengthening, and community
      engagement into a unified and mutually reinforcing faculty development
      and research agenda;
   (d) Promote coherence and alignment with outcomes-based education,
      accreditation systems, quality assurance mechanisms, faculty career
      progression frameworks, and other relevant higher education policies and
      standards; and
   (e) Provide practical guidance to higher education institutions in the
      formulation, implementation, monitoring, and periodic review of their
      respective institutional faculty development and research plans, including
      priority-setting, resource allocation, and performance assessment.


CHED shall ensure that the Framework is regularly updated to respond to
emerging trends, evidence from monitoring and evaluation, and evolving national
and global higher education and research contexts.

   (a) Be a faculty member of a public or private higher education institution

       applicable rules;
   (b) Be formally endorsed by the concerned higher education institution
       through its duly authorized academic or administrative authority,
       certifying that the proposed faculty development activity is consistent with
       the institution’s approved faculty development plan and institutional
       development priorities;

   (c) Demonstrate readiness, commitment, and capacity to complete the

SEC. 10. Selection and Prioritization — In the selection and prioritization of
beneficiaries, the Commission on Higher Education shall adopt criteria that
promote relevance, equity, and system-wide capacity-building, which may
include:


   (a) Alignment of the proposed faculty development activity with institutional
      mandates, priority disciplines, and national development needs;
   (b) The developmental stage, resource constraints, and geographic location of
      the higher education institution, with particular consideration given to
      emerging, underserved, and geographically isolated institutions;
   (c) The potential contribution of the applicant to improvements in teaching
      quality, research productivity, graduate education strengthening, academic
      leadership, or institutional performance;
   (d) The need to address disparities in faculty qualifications, disciplinary
      coverage, or research capacity across regions, sectors, or institution types;
      and
   (e) The demonstrated commitment of the higher education institution to
      support the reintegration, deployment, and effective utilization of the
      faculty member upon completion of the faculty development program.

SEC. 15. Return Service and Accountability — Recipients of faculty

investments in faculty development and research generate tangible, sustained, and
system-wide institutional and societal benefits.


Return service obligations may be rendered through teaching, research, graduate
supervision, curriculum and program development, institutional capacity-
building, community engagement, or other forms of academic or public service,
as may be determined by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) in
consultation with the concerned higher education institution, taking into account


                                                                                18
institutional needs, faculty deployment considerations, and the nature of the
support received.


Return service may likewise be rendered within the home institution or, where
appropriate, through inter-institutional, regional, or national service arrangements

Provided, that return service obligations shall be proportionate to the level,

CHED shall prescribe clear guidelines on return service duration, modes of
service, reporting requirements, monitoring mechanisms, incentives for
exemplary service, and remedies for non-compliance, consistent with applicable
laws and regulations.

has already accrued, or where compliance is rendered impracticable by serious
illness, permanent disability, fortuitous events, further approved studies or
research engagements, or other analogous circumstances beyond the control of

institutional capacity-building, policy advisory work, curriculum development,

non-compliance.

continuous improvement of faculty development initiatives, research capacity
strengthening, graduate     education enhancement, research infrastructure
development, and other related interventions necessary to advance teaching
excellence, research productivity, innovation, and institutional performance
across the higher education system.

Commission on Higher Education in accordance with the policies, standards, and

The Fund shall be designed to ensure sustained and predictable support for faculty

SEC. 18. Sources of the Fund — The Faculty Development and Research Fund
(FDRF) shall be sourced from the following:


   (a) Annual appropriations specifically provided for the purposes of this Act
      under the General Appropriations Act, which shall constitute the primary
      and recurring source of funding for faculty development and research
      programs;
   (b) Ten percent (10%) of the Higher Education Fund, as may be provided by
      law, which shall be earmarked and automatically allocated to the Faculty
      Development and Research Fund to ensure sustained and predictable
      financing for faculty development, research capacity strengthening, and
      graduate education enhancement across the higher education system;
   (c) Grants, donations, endowments, bequests, and contributions from
      domestic and foreign sources, whether public or private, subject to existing
      laws, rules, and regulations governing the acceptance and utilization of
      such funds;
                                                                               21
   (d) Additional funding to be derived from provision of memoranda-of
       agreements entered into by donor-parties pursuant to Section 3 of Republic
       Act No. 8525 or the Adopt-A-School Act of 1998;
   (e) Income derived from research and innovation activities, including
       royalties, licensing fees, intellectual property income, and proceeds from
       research commercialization or technology transfer initiatives supported
       under this Act, subject to policies on revenue sharing and reinvestment as
       may be prescribed by the CHED
   (f) Contributions from partnerships and collaborative arrangements with
       government agencies, industry, civil society organizations, international
       development partners, and other stakeholders, including co-funding,
       matching grants, and joint financing mechanisms aligned with national

   (g) Other sources as may be authorized by law, including special purpose
       funds, trust funds, or future financing mechanisms consistent with the
       objectives of this Act.


Funds accruing to the Faculty Development and Research Fund shall be used
exclusively for the purposes authorized under this Act, shall not revert to the
general fund of the National Treasury at the end of the fiscal year, and shall be
subject to applicable government accounting, auditing, and reporting rules.


The Commission on Higher Education CHED shall ensure that the mobilization,
allocation, and utilization of the Fund are conducted in a transparent, accountable,
and performance-informed manner, and shall adopt safeguards to prevent
duplication, misuse, or diversion of funds.




                                                                                 22
SEC. 19. Performance-Based and Differentiated Allocation — The Faculty
Development and Research Fund shall be allocated through a performance-based
and differentiated funding framework designed to promote continuous
improvement,     institutional   capacity-building,   equity,   and   system-wide
development, rather than to impose punitive or exclusionary measures.


Such funding framework shall recognize the diversity of institutional mandates,
typologies, development stages, and regional contexts within the higher education
system, and shall apply performance-based mechanisms in a manner that is
developmental, contextualized, and supportive of institutional growth.


The Commission on Higher Education shall design and implement the allocation
framework to ensure that all eligible higher education institutions have access to
baseline support for faculty development and research, while providing additional
incentives and targeted funding to encourage excellence, innovation, strategic
alignment, and capacity-building in priority areas.
To this end, CHED shall establish multiple funding windows, which may include,
but shall not be limited to, the following:
   (a) Baseline Institutional Support Window, which shall provide predictable
       and minimum levels of funding to support essential faculty development
       and research activities of higher education institutions, particularly to
       ensure equitable participation and system-wide capacity development;
   (b) Performance-Based Incentive Window, which shall allocate additional
       resources based on demonstrated improvements and outcomes in faculty
       qualifications, research productivity, graduate education capacity,
       innovation outputs, and institutional or societal impact, using indicators
       appropriate to institutional typology and development stage;


                                                                               23
   (c) Strategic National Priority Window, which shall support faculty
       development and research initiatives aligned with nationally identified
       priority disciplines, research themes, or development challenges, including
       mission-oriented, interdisciplinary, and collaborative programs; and
   (d) Capacity-Building and Catch-Up Window, which shall provide targeted
       support to emerging, underserved, geographically isolated, or resource-
       constrained institutions to address identified gaps in faculty qualifications,
       research infrastructure, and institutional research systems.
CHED shall adopt clear criteria, indicators, and procedures governing access to
each funding window, including provisions for institutional eligibility, proposal
evaluation, fund release, monitoring, and performance review.


Provided, that no higher education institution shall be denied access to baseline
institutional support solely on the basis of performance classification; provided,
further, that performance-based allocations shall take into account improvement
trajectories and contextual factors, and shall not penalize institutions for structural
disadvantages beyond their control.


CHED shall ensure that the allocation of the Fund is transparent, evidence-
informed, and subject to regular monitoring and evaluation, and that funding
decisions contribute to sustained improvements in faculty development, research
capacity, and overall higher education system performance.



SEC. 20. Performance Indicators — Performance-based allocations under this
Act shall be guided by a balanced, transparent, and differentiated set of
performance indicators designed to assess outcomes, improvement trajectories,
and impact of faculty development and research investments, and to support
evidence-informed funding, planning, and policy decisions.
                                                                                    24
Performance indicators may include, but shall not be limited to, the following:


   (a) Faculty    qualification    and    development      outcomes,     including
      improvements in the proportion of faculty with graduate and post-graduate
      qualifications, participation in faculty development programs, teaching
      effectiveness, pedagogical innovation, and professional advancement;
   (b) Research, innovation, and creative outputs, including the quantity,
      quality, relevance, and impact of publications, creative works, patents,
      intellectual property, externally funded research, and other recognized
      scholarly outputs appropriate to institutional mandate and discipline;
   (c) Graduate education and research supervision capacity, including the
      strength and sustainability of graduate programs, faculty capability to
      supervise theses and dissertations, graduate completion rates, mentoring
      systems, and compliance with quality assurance and ethical standards;
   (d) Institutional and societal impact, including contributions to public policy,
      industry and workforce development, innovation ecosystems, community
      engagement, and the utilization or application of research and academic
      outputs for social and economic benefit; and
   (e) Regional and sectoral contributions, including responsiveness to regional
      development priorities, service to underserved or priority sectors, inter-
      institutional collaboration, and support for balanced regional growth.


The formulation, collection, processing, and use of performance data under this
section shall be consistent with the budget execution, performance-informed
budgeting, and expenditure accountability frameworks of the Department of
Budget and Management, as well as with the accounting, auditing,


                                                                                  25
documentation, and reporting standards of the Commission on Audit, including
applicable rules on financial management, internal control, and audit trails.


SEC. 21. Safeguards — The Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall
establish and enforce comprehensive institutional, procedural, and technical
safeguards to ensure fairness, transparency, integrity, and accountability in the
application of performance indicators and performance-based mechanisms under
this Act.


Such safeguards shall include the adoption of clearly defined and publicly
disclosed indicators, standardized and verifiable data sources, and consistent
measurement methodologies that are uniformly applied across similarly situated
higher education institutions, taking into account differentiated institutional
mandates, typologies, and development stages.


CHED shall further institute robust mechanisms for data validation, verification,
reconciliation, and correction, including internal controls, audit trails,
documentation standards, and data quality assurance processes, consistent with
the performance-informed budgeting framework of the Department of Budget
and Management and the accounting and auditing rules of the Commission on
Audit.


To ensure due process and institutional participation, CHED shall provide
structured and accessible opportunities for institutional feedback, clarification,
and appeal of performance assessments, including reasonable timelines for
submission, review, and resolution, and the issuance of written determinations
explaining the basis of funding or performance-related decisions.


                                                                                26
The Commission shall periodically conduct systematic reviews and refinements
of performance indicators, methodologies, and safeguard mechanisms, informed
by monitoring and evaluation results, stakeholder consultations, audit findings,
and evolving national development priorities and higher education system needs,
to ensure continued relevance, accuracy, proportionality, and policy coherence.



SEC. 22. Faculty Development and Research Service — There is hereby created
a Faculty Development and Research Service (FDRS) within the Commission on
Higher Education (CHED), which shall serve as the primary policy,
programmatic, technical, and coordinating unit responsible for the formulation,
governance, implementation, monitoring, and continuous improvement of faculty
development and research initiatives under this Act.


The FDRS shall function as the central institutional mechanism within CHED to
ensure coherence, consistency, and effectiveness in faculty development and
research policies, programs, and funding interventions, and shall provide
sustained technical leadership in strengthening faculty qualifications, research
capacity, graduate education, and innovation across the higher education system.


In the performance of its mandate, the FDRS shall have the following powers and
functions:


   (a) Formulate, harmonize, and coordinate national policies, standards,
      guidelines, and strategic programs on faculty development and research,
      consistent with this Act, national education reform priorities, the National
      Research and Development Agenda, and applicable international
      commitments and best practices;


                                                                               27
(b) Administer, manage, and oversee the Faculty Development and Research
   Fund, including the development of funding guidelines, evaluation and
   approval of proposals, release and utilization of funds, financial reporting,
   and enforcement of fiscal accountability, in accordance with the budget
   execution rules of the Department of Budget and Management and the
   accounting and auditing standards of the Commission on Audit;
(c) Design, implement, and maintain comprehensive monitoring and
   evaluation systems to assess program implementation, outcomes, impact,
   and value for money of faculty development and research initiatives, and
   to generate evidence for planning, performance-based funding, and policy
   refinement;
(d) Develop, operate, and sustain integrated faculty development and research
   data systems and analytics, including centralized databases, registries,
   dashboards, and reporting platforms, to support evidence-based decision-
   making, transparency, public accountability, and compliance with
   government data governance policies;
(e) Coordinate, consult, and collaborate with relevant government agencies,
   higher education institutions, research institutions, industry partners,
   professional associations, civil society organizations, and other
   stakeholders to promote policy coherence, resource mobilization, inter-
   institutional collaboration, and alignment of faculty development and
   research initiatives with national, regional, and sectoral priorities;
(f) Provide technical assistance, capacity-building, and advisory support to
   higher education institutions in the formulation and implementation of
   faculty development and research plans, including assistance in program
   design, monitoring, reporting, and compliance with quality assurance and
   accountability requirements;

   (g) Facilitate partnerships, research clustering, and Centers of Excellence
       initiatives, in coordination with concerned agencies and institutions, to

       research capacity development; and
   (h) Perform such other functions as may be necessary, incidental, or
       complementary to the effective implementation of this Act and the
       attainment of its objectives, as may be authorized by the Commission on
       Higher Education.


The Faculty Development and Research Service shall operate in accordance with
the principles of transparency, accountability, performance orientation, academic

SEC. 24. Equity and Regional Development — The Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) shall adopt and implement comprehensive, equity-sensitive
measures to promote inclusive growth and balanced regional development within
the higher education system, recognizing disparities in institutional capacity,
geographic access, resource availability, and faculty qualifications across regions
and sectors.

                                                                                31
To this end, the Commission on Higher Education shall provide targeted,
differentiated, and sustained support to emerging, geographically isolated,
disadvantaged, and resource-constrained higher education institutions, including
state universities and colleges, local universities and colleges, and private
institutions serving underserved or priority communities.


Such measures may include, but shall not be limited to, preferential access to
baseline institutional support, capacity-building and catch-up funding, targeted
faculty scholarships and research grants, technical assistance for institutional
planning and research governance, shared research infrastructure arrangements,
mentoring and partnership programs with more developed institutions, and
priority consideration under strategic national funding windows.


CHED shall ensure that equity-oriented interventions are designed to address
structural constraints, support institutional improvement trajectories, and enable
institutions in less developed regions to progressively strengthen faculty
qualifications, research capacity, graduate education, and academic offerings,
without compromising quality standards.


In implementing this provision, the Commission shall take into account regional
development plans, sectoral needs, and national development priorities, and shall
coordinate with relevant government agencies, local government units, and
development partners to maximize impact, avoid duplication, and promote
sustainable regional higher education ecosystems.




                                                                               32
The effectiveness of equity and regional development measures shall be subject
to regular monitoring and evaluation, and the results thereof shall inform policy
refinement, funding allocation, and future program design under this Act.


SEC. 25. National Higher Educational Research Repository — The
Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall establish, maintain, and
administer a National Higher Education Research Repository, which shall serve
as a centralized, publicly accessible, and secure online platform for the collection,
archiving, dissemination, and preservation of research outputs produced by
higher education institutions.


The Repository shall include, but shall not be limited to, research papers, theses,
dissertations, technical reports, policy papers, creative works, and other scholarly
outputs generated by faculty members, researchers, and graduate students of
public and private higher education institutions, particularly those funded in
whole or in part by public funds or supported under this Act.


CHED shall prescribe policies, standards, and technical guidelines governing the
submission, metadata requirements, access levels, interoperability, citation, and
long-term digital preservation of research outputs in the Repository, consistent
with existing laws on intellectual property, data privacy, national security, and
open access.


Provided, that higher education institutions shall be required to submit or cause
the submission of eligible research outputs to the Repository within a reasonable
period after completion, publication, or acceptance, subject to allowable embargo
periods, confidentiality restrictions, intellectual property considerations, and
other limitations as may be provided in the implementing rules and regulations.

                                                                                  33
The Repository shall be designed to:


   (a) Promote transparency, knowledge sharing, and public access to publicly
      funded research;
   (b) Support research utilization, policy translation, innovation, and
      collaboration;
   (c) Serve as an evidence base for higher education planning, monitoring, and
      evaluation; and
   (d) Strengthen the visibility, impact, and global competitiveness of Philippine
      higher education research.


CHED shall ensure that the development, operation, and maintenance of the
Repository comply with applicable government information and communications
technology standards, data governance policies, and accounting, auditing, and
reporting requirements


SEC. 26. Mandatory Submission and Publication of Research Outputs — All
higher education institutions shall require the submission and publication of
eligible research outputs through the National Higher Education Research
Repository established under this Act, in accordance with the policies, standards,
and technical guidelines prescribed by the Commission on Higher Education
(CHED).


For purposes of this Act, eligible research outputs shall include, but shall not be
limited to, peer-reviewed research papers, theses, dissertations, technical and
policy papers, terminal reports, and other scholarly or creative works produced
by faculty members, researchers, and graduate students of public and private

                                                                                34
higher education institutions, particularly those funded in whole or in part by
public funds or supported under this Act.


Submission to the Repository shall be a mandatory condition for:


   (a) The completion and close-out of research projects funded or supported
      under this Act;
   (b) The release of final tranches of research grants or institutional research
      funding;
   (c) Compliance with faculty scholarship, research grant, and return service
      requirements; and
   (d) Institutional reporting, monitoring, and performance evaluation by CHED.


Provided, that voluntary submission of research outputs to the Repository by
faculty members, researchers, or higher education institutions, even when such
outputs are not required to be mandatorily deposited under this Act, shall be
recognized as valid compliance with publication, dissemination, or public access
requirements imposed by CHED or by institutional research policies, unless a
more stringent requirement is expressly prescribed.


Provided, further, that CHED may allow reasonable embargo periods, restricted
access levels, or deferred publication in cases involving intellectual property
protection, publication agreements, national security considerations, data
privacy, or other justifiable grounds, subject to clear criteria and documentation
requirements to be provided in the implementing rules and regulations.


Higher education institutions shall integrate compliance with Repository
submission requirements into their internal research governance systems,

                                                                               35
including research management offices, ethics review processes, intellectual
property manuals, and institutional reporting mechanisms.


Failure to comply with the mandatory submission and publication requirements
under this section, without justifiable cause, shall be a ground for the withholding,
suspension, or recovery of funding, or the imposition of other appropriate
administrative measures, subject to due process and applicable laws.



SEC. 27. Non-Impairment Clause — Nothing in this Act, nor in the
implementation of any provision hereof, shall be interpreted to impair or restrict
academic freedom, institutional autonomy, or the right to due process, as
guaranteed under the Constitution and existing laws, nor to authorize any action
that would unduly interfere with the lawful governance, academic judgment, or
internal management of higher education institutions.


SEC. 28. Monitoring and Evaluation — The Commission on Higher Education
(CHED) shall establish, institutionalize, and maintain a comprehensive, results-
oriented, and integrated monitoring and evaluation system to assess the
implementation, effectiveness, efficiency, and impact of faculty development and
research programs, projects, and activities under this Act.


Such monitoring and evaluation system shall cover, among others, program
implementation progress, performance outcomes, utilization of funds,
compliance with policy and accountability requirements, regional and sectoral
distribution of benefits, and the contribution of faculty development and research
investments to institutional improvement and national development objectives.



                                                                                  36
SEC. 29. Annual Reporting to Congress — The Commission on Higher
Education (CHED) shall submit to Congress an Annual Faculty Development and
Research Report, which shall present a consolidated and evidence-based account
of outcomes achieved, funding utilization and financial performance, geographic
and institutional distribution of resources, challenges encountered, best practices
identified, and policy gaps observed, and which shall include specific
recommendations for legislative action, budgetary adjustments, or policy reforms
as may be necessary to strengthen the implementation of this Act.


The Annual Report shall be submitted within a reasonable period after the close
of each fiscal year, in accordance with standards prescribed by law, and shall
serve as a key instrument for congressional oversight, performance-informed
budgeting, and continuous policy improvement.


SEC. 30. Implementing Rules and Regulations — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) shall
promulgate the necessary implementing rules and regulations.


SEC. 31. Appropriations — The amount necessary for the initial implementation
of this Act shall be charged against the current appropriations of the Commission
on Higher Education (CHED). Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.


SEC. 32. Separability Clause — If any provision of this Act is declared invalid
or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect.


SEC. 33. Repealing Clause — All laws, rules, regulations, and issuances
inconsistent with this Act are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

                                                                                 37
SEC. 34. Effectivity — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
