                                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

       The State, in fulfillment of its constitutional duty to protect life and property, must
continuously strengthen its capacity to anticipate, prepare for, and respond to disasters.
This commitment is aligned with Republic Act No. 10121, or the Philippine Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Act of 2010, as well as the Climate Change Act of 2009, as
amended by Republic Act No. 10174. These laws underscore the urgency of addressing
the root causes of vulnerability, mitigating the impacts of natural hazards, and
mainstreaming climate change adaptation in local and national governance. Yet, despite
this legal framework, recent events have demonstrated that community-level
preparedness, particularly at the barangay level, remains inadequate in the face of
increasingly severe and frequent climate-induced disasters.

        In November 2025, Typhoon Tino (international name “Kalmaegi”) and Super
Typhoon Uwan (international name “Fung-Wong”) struck the Philippines only days
apart, revealing significant gaps in evacuation readiness and local disaster response.
Combined fatalities reached close to 300 people, with reports documenting up to 297
deaths across affected regions1. Cebu Province suffered one of the worst impacts, with
Typhoon Tino alone claiming an estimated 150 fatalities, while 451 individuals sustained
injuries. According to a report from the Office of Civil Defense (OCD), several
communities also reported missing persons as authorities struggled to conduct
search-and-rescue operations under rapidly deteriorating conditions.

       The destruction extended beyond loss of life. Hundreds of thousands were
displaced by widespread flooding, landslides, and structural damage throughout the
Visayas and Luzon. Evacuation centers were quickly overwhelmed, and humanitarian

destructive weather events occurring in close succession. Climate research group
Greenpeace Philippines have warned that rising sea temperatures and intensifying
rainfall patterns are consistent with accelerating climate change4. These trends reaffirm
the need for proactive, localized disaster preparedness rather than reactive, last-minute
response.

        Given these realities, it is imperative to adopt more systematic and
institutionalized mechanisms for preparing communities for disasters. Annual,
community-level evacuation drills have proven effective in minimizing casualties during
sudden-onset events. Regular rehearsals enable households, barangay volunteers,
schools, and local responders to identify evacuation routes, assist vulnerable
individuals, and execute coordinated responses with greater precision. Barangays with
practiced protocols consistently report fewer casualties and faster mobilization during
real emergencies.

       Institutionalizing drills also prepares communities for the increasing likelihood of
consecutive disasters. The back-to-back arrival of Tino and Uwan demonstrated how
rapidly hazard events can overwhelm government resources. By strengthening
barangay-level preparedness through trained evacuation teams, standardized protocols,
and coordinated drills, communities become less dependent on overstretched national
response units.

       This proposed measure institutionalizes annual community evacuation drills in all
barangays and requires LGUs to include these drills in their local DRRM plans. It
strengthens planning, enhances responder training, establishes monitoring mechanisms
through the NDRRMC, and ensures funding support through national and local DRRM
allocations. It also promotes widespread public education on early warning systems and
hazard-specific evacuation procedures. By empowering barangays, the measure
ensures that disaster preparedness is systematic, consistent, and measurable.

      Ultimately, this bill seeks to reduce loss of life, protect livelihoods, enhance
community resilience, and align the country’s disaster risk reduction agenda with
national policies and international commitments.

       In view of the foregoing, and with the urgent need to strengthen our national and
local capacities in light of the severe impacts of Typhoons Tino and Uwan, the
immediate passage of this measure is earnestly sought.

                              AN ACT
     AMENDING REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10121, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE
 “PHILIPPINE DISASTER RISK REDUCTION AND MANAGEMENT ACT OF 2010,”
    BY INSTITUTIONALIZING A MANDATORY ANNUAL COMMUNITY-LEVEL
EVACUATION DRILL AND READINESS PROGRAM, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in
Congress assembled:

    Section 1. A new section, Section 12-A, is hereby inserted after Section 12 of
Republic Act No. 10121, to read as follows:

         "Section 12-A. Institutionalization of Mandatory Annual Community
     Evacuation Drills. - All barangays shall conduct at least one (1) community-level
     evacuation drill annually, based on the approved Barangay DRRM Plan
     (BDRRMP) and the Local DRRM Plan (LDRRMP) of the municipality or city.

      The evacuation drill shall include:
         a. Activation and testing of early warning systems;
         b. Execution of hazard-specific evacuation routes;
         c. Assisted evacuation of vulnerable groups;
         d. Mobilization of BDRRMC response teams;
         e. Coordination with the Municipal/City DRRMO;
         f. Inspection and readiness verification of evacuation centers.
         g. Drills must be documented and conducted in accordance with NDRRMC
            guidelines.

         The NDRRMC shall issue standardized guidelines detailing the annual
     schedule, minimum requirements, evaluation tools, simulation protocols, and
     reporting templates for LGUs.”


       Section 2. Repealing clause. All existing laws, orders, decrees, rules and
regulations or parts thereof, inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby
amended, modified or repealed accordingly.

       Section 3. Effectivity. This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
