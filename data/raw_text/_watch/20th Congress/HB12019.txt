EXPLANATORY NOTE

Sa hapon ng bakasyon, may estudyanteng nakaupo sa mesa sa bahay, katabi ang
nakatiklop na school uniform at isang listahang sulat-kamay: pamasahe, proyekto, load para
sa online requirements, gamot sa bahay, at bayaring kailangang mahabol bago magsimula ang
klase. Sa labas ay maingay ang mga batang naglalaro, ngunit sa loob ay tahimik ang pagbilang
ng perang wala pa. Kapag may pagkakataong magtrabaho sa school break, hindi luho ang
hinahanap niya kundi isang marangal na paraan upang makatulong sa pamilya at makabalik
sa paaralan nang hindi nababawasan ang pag-asa. Ngunit kapag napuno na ang mga puwesto
sa Special Program for Employment of Students, ang listahan ay nananatiling listahan, at ang
bakasyon ay nagiging panahon ng pangamba sa halip na tulay tungo sa pagpapatuloy ng pag-
aaral.

The Special Program for Employment of Students, or SPES, is not simply a seasonal
work arrangement. It is a statutory bridge between poverty, education, and dignified work.
The original Special Program for Employment of Students law was enacted to help poor but
deserving students pursue their education by encouraging employment during summer and
Christmas vacations, with employers paying part of the wage and government support
covering the remainder. The later law, An Act Strengthening and Expanding the Coverage of
the Special Program for Employment of Students, Amending for the Purpose Provisions of
R.A. No. 7323, otherwise known as the Special Program for Employment of Students,
broadened that framework. The presently operative statute, An Act Amending Certain
Provisions of Republic Act No. 9547, Otherwise Known as An Act Strengthening and
Expanding the Coverage of the Special Program for Employment of Students, Amending for
the Purpose Provisions of Republic Act No. 7323, Otherwise Known as the Special Program
for Employment of Students, recognizes that students, out-of-school youth, and dependents
of displaced workers may need a pathway to earnings and eventual enrollment. The central
truth is that the program already exists because Congress has long understood the problem;
what remains unfinished is ensuring that its reach and wage support match the need it was
created to meet.

Hindi dapat ang estudyanteng gipit ang unang sumusuko kapag kulang ang kita ng
pamilya. Ang isang school-break job, kapag malinaw ang aplikasyon, patas ang pagpili, may
kaugnayan sa kakayahan ng kabataan, at dumarating sa oras ang bayad, ay nagbibigay ng
higit sa panggastos. Nagbibigay ito ng pagkakataong maipakitang kaya niyang pumasok sa
opisina, tumupad sa oras, makitungo nang may respeto, at umuwi na may hawak na sariling
kinita para sa kaniyang pag-aaral. Sa sandaling gumana ang batas na ito, ang mararamdaman

SPES was established by Republic Act No. 7323 in 1992 to help poor but deserving
students through subsidized employment during school breaks. As amended by Republic Act
10917, the law now covers poor but deserving students, out-of-school youth, and dependents
of displaced or would-be displaced workers, aged fifteen (15) to thirty (30). In a 2021 study
of the program, economists Emily Beam and Stella Quimbo reported that as of 2016, SPES
had an annual budget of Php817.96 million and reached 229,674 participants a year.’ In 2025,
151,316 SPES beneficiaries received P686 million in wages.”

That record is an achievement worth protecting, because it demonstrates a functioning
national vehicle for school-break employment. Yet it also underscores the public
responsibility to make every additional slot count, particularly where demand exceeds
available placements. The current law already requires employers to pay wages no lower than
the applicable minimum wage or government hiring rate, sets the national government share
at forty per centum of wages, permits a share of up to seventy-five per centum for low-income
local government units depending on their capacity, and requires payment of the national
government share within thirty working days after the employer’s report. This bill proposes
a durable improvement to the wage-support rule while supporting a broader, equitably
administered national reach through the annual appropriations process.

May mga naitayo nang mahalagang mekanismo na dapat kilalanin at palakasin, hindi
palitan. Ang DOLE ay may SPES na at kinikilala nito na ang SPES ay isang "youth
employment-bridging program" na naglalaan ng pansamantalang trabaho.? May malinaw na
papel din ang mga Public Employment Service Office sa pagpapadali ng aplikasyon at
paglalagay ng mga benepisyaryo, gaya ng itinatadhana ng kasalukuyang SPES law. May
registry o database rin na inaatasang panatilihin ng DOLE para sa pagsubaybay sa mga
benepisyaryo. Ngunit ang umiiral na pundasyon ay kailangan ng isang mas matibay na
susunod na hakbang: mas mataas na bahagi ng pamahalaan sa sahod ng estudyante, mas
maraming pagkakataong pondohan sa General Appropriations Act, at pamantayang titiyak na
ang limitadong puwesto ay mapupunta sa mga estudyanteng tunay na nangangailangan,
saanman sila nakatira. The pending National Student Allowance Program may complement
student support, but a general allowance proposal cannot substitute for a work opportunity
that enables a young Filipino to earn, gain work exposure, and remain connected to education.

The antagonist addressed by this measure is not the employer who has limited
openings, the PESO worker who must process many applicants, or the student who seeks
work because the household budget is stretched. It is the absence of a sufficiently funded,
transparent, and accountable system that converts qualified demand into fair placements. A
first-come, first-served process can favor those with easier access to information,

May tapat na dahilan upang mag-ingat sa pagpapalawak, at hindi ito dapat ikubli.
Ayon sa Innovations for Poverty Action (IPA), mixed ang ebidensiya sa bisa ng mga youth-
employment policy tulad ng job training at wage subsidies, kadalasang hindi cost-effective
ang mga ito, at kakaunti ang pananaliksik tungkol sa epekto nito sa school enrollment at
pangmatagalang trabaho. Sa randomized evaluation ng SPES mismo, walang nakitang
pagtaas sa school enrollment, na nasa 95 porsiyento na anuman ang paglahok. Tumaas ang
kumpiyansa ng mga benepisyaryo tungkol sa kanilang work prospects matapos ang
graduation, ngunit walang nakitang epekto sa kanilang self-esteem, self-reported life skills,
at wage expectations.* Gayunman, tumaas nang 4.4 percentage points, o 79 porsiyento, ang
posibilidad na may trabaho ang mga kalahok makalipas ang walo hanggang labindalawang
buwan.> These cautions do not counsel abandonment. They counsel discipline. The proper
response is not to leave financially needy students without a school-break income pathway,
but to expand SPES with measurable standards, relevant placements, transparent targeting,
prompt payment, and outcome monitoring. Every summer and Christmas break that passes
without sufficient slots is a closed window for a student who may need earnings precisely
before the next school term begins.

The governing principle of the SPES na Mas Malawak Act is simple: no financially
needy student should lose the chance to earn during school breaks, and to gain work
experience shown to improve later employment, because a public employment bridge is too
narrow, too weakly supported, or too unevenly delivered. This measure amends the current
SPES framework rather than recreating it, respecting the legislative chain that has already
expanded coverage, defined wage sharing, assigned DOLE implementation, and required
beneficiary monitoring. It pairs a stronger statutory wage-support commitment with an
expansion of participant opportunities that must remain anchored in congressional
appropriations, actual regional demand, and implementation capacity. It also affirms that
temporary employment must support, rather than disrupt, schooling, particularly for
secondary-level students, whom the law allows to work only during summer and/or Christmas
vacations. In this way, the measure completes the existing program: it does not outshine what
government has built, but makes that existing bridge wide enough and reliable enough for
more learners to cross.

AN ACT
INCREASING THE NATIONAL GOVERNMENT SHARE IN THE WAGES OF
BENEFICIARIES OF THE SPECIAL PROGRAM FOR EMPLOYMENT OF
STUDENTS, ESTABLISHING STANDARDS FOR THEIR SELECTION,
PLACEMENT, AND PAYMENT, FURTHER AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE
REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7323, AS AMENDED BY REPUBLIC ACT NOS. 9547 AND
10917, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the "SPES na Mas Malawak
Act".

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to enable
financially needy students and other qualified beneficiaries of the Special Program for
Employment of Students to earn lawful income during periods that do not interfere with their
education, while acquiring work experience under safe, dignified, and skills-relevant
conditions.

The State recognizes that public wage support must be administered fairly and
transparently. Limited program slots shall go first to the qualified applicants in greatest need,
and a fair share shall be reserved for those who live in geographically isolated and
disadvantaged areas, under the published criteria required by this Act.

The number of participant slots shall be subject to appropriations in the annual

General Appropriations Act and to verified regional demand, employer absorption capacity,
and available program funds.

SECTION 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

(a) Geographically isolated and disadvantaged area refers to a barangay
classified as such under the criteria adopted by the Department of Labor and

SECTION 7. Transitory Provision. — The sharing of wages under Section 4 of this
Act shall apply beginning with the fiscal year for which the General Appropriations Act first
provides funds for it. Until then, the sharing of wages under Section 2 of Republic Act No.

9547, as amended by Republic Act No. 10917, shall continue to apply.

SECTION 8. Non-Diminution of Benefits. — Nothing in this Act shall be construed
to diminish any benefit enjoyed by SPES beneficiaries under existing laws, rules, or

agreements,

SECTION 9. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from
the effectivity of this Act, the Secretary of Labor and Employment shall, in consultation with
participating employers, representatives of students, public employment service offices, and
educational institutions, promulgate the rules and regulations necessary for its
implementation. Existing rules on the SPES that are consistent with this Act shall remain in
force until amended or replaced. Delay in issuing the rules shall not suspend the sharing of

wages under Section 4, subject to Section 7 of this Act.

SECTION 10. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary to implement this Act shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
