                               EXPLANATORY NOTE

Many households and communities struggle to ind skilled workers, such as
electricians, plumbers, carpenters, or appliance technicians, near their area, leading
to delays, higher costs, or unsafe work. At the same time, skilled workers often lack
visibility, making it harder for them to connect with clients who need their services.

This bill requires every barangay to establish a Voluntary Skilled Workers Registry,
creating a public directory of workers who opt in. The registry will help residents
easily ind local skilled workers while protecting personal data in full compliance with
the Data Privacy Act. Barangays will assist workers in registering, maintain the
information securely, and update the directory regularly.

By connecting skilled workers to households and communities safely and ef iciently,
this measure supports livelihood opportunities, encourages local employment, and
promotes access to essential services, making communities more self-reliant and
responsive to local needs.

In view of the foregoing, ug sa ngalan sa SERBISYONG TINUORAY, the undersigned
respectfully requests the immediate passage of this measure.

                              AN ACT
MANDATING ALL BARANGAYS TO ESTABLISH A VOLUNTARY REGISTRY OF
SKILLED WORKERS, PROVIDING FOR A PUBLIC DIRECTORY SUBJECT TO DATA
PRIVACY SAFEGUARDS, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress
assembled:

SECTION 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “The Barangay Skilled
Workers Registry Act.”

SECTION 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote access to
household and community services by enabling residents to identify skilled workers
available in their locality, while ensuring that any collection, processing, disclosure,
and retention of personal data complies with the principles of transparency,
legitimate purpose, proportionality, and security under Republic Act No. 10173 and
its implementing rules and regulations.

SECTION 3. De inition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

   (a) Barangay refers to the smallest political unit, as de ined under existing law.

   (b) Skilled worker refers to a person who offers services primarily involving
       technical skills or manual trades for household and community needs,
       including but not limited to plumbing, carpentry, electrical work, masonry,


                                                                            2|Page
      welding, appliance repair, and similar services. For purposes of this Act, skilled
      worker need not be a holder of a TESDA National Certi icate.

   (c) Registry refers to the barangay-maintained database of skilled workers
       who voluntarily register under this Act, containing both (i) con idential
       records for veri ication and administration, and (ii) a public directory
       composed only of ields authorized for disclosure.

   (d) Public directory refers to the limited set of information derived from the
       Registry that may be displayed or made accessible to the public, subject to the
       registrant’s consent and the data privacy principles of transparency, legitimate
       purpose, and proportionality.

SECTION 4. Establishment of the Barangay Skilled Workers Registry. —

   (a) Mandatory establishment; voluntary registration. Every barangay shall
       establish and maintain a Skilled Workers Registry. Registration by skilled
       workers shall be voluntary.

   (b) Purpose. The Registry shall be used solely to facilitate (i) lawful identi ication
       and contact with skilled workers who opt-in to be listed, (ii) skills matching
       for barangay constituents, and (iii) referral to training opportunities, without
       serving as an occupational license or as a guarantee of competence.

   (c) Orientation and assistance. The barangay shall disseminate clear
       instructions on registration and shall provide assistance to registrants in
       completing forms, including a privacy notice consistent with the Data Privacy
       Act and its IRR.

SECTION 5. Data Privacy and Security Compliance. —

   (a) Compliance with RA 10173. The barangay shall process personal data under
       this Act in accordance with the principles of transparency, legitimate purpose,
       and proportionality, and shall collect only data necessary for the declared
       purpose.

   (b) Security measures; con identiality. The barangay shall adopt reasonable
       organizational, physical, and technical security measures, including access
       controls and con identiality obligations for personnel handling records,


                                                                             3|Page
      consistent with the requirements on security organization and human
      resources management.

   (c) Data retention and disposal. The barangay shall adopt a retention schedule
       and secure disposal procedures. Personal data shall not be retained longer
       than necessary for the declared purpose.

   (d) Data sharing agreements. Any sharing of the Registry (or portions thereof)
       with municipal/city governments, TESDA, DOLE, or other agencies for a public
       function or public service shall be covered by a data sharing agreement,
       subject to applicable NPC rules.

SECTION 6. Contents of the Registry; Public Directory Fields. —

   (a) Con idential Registry records. For registration, a skilled worker may submit
       the following, which shall be treated as con idential and shall not be posted
       publicly:

       1. full name;
       2. barangay address (for residency veri ication);
       3. preferred contact details;
       4. skills and services offered;
       5. TESDA National Certi icate details, if any, and/or training certi icates, if the
          worker chooses to submit them;
       6. government-issued ID details for veri ication only, subject to the IRR,
          and without requiring photocopies unless necessary and proportionate;
          and
       7. such other information as may be necessary and proportionate to the
          declared purpose.

   (b) Public directory. Only the following may be included in the public directory,
       and only upon the registrant’s informed consent:

       1. name (or approved display name);
       2. general service category (e.g., electrician, plumber);
       3. general service area (barangay only, without house number);
       4. preferred public contact channel (e.g., mobile number, messaging app, or
          barangay-mediated contact), as chosen by the registrant; and
       5. TESDA NC status (e.g., “NC holder—Yes/No”), if the registrant chooses to
          disclose it.

SECTION 7. Access and Request for Information. —

    (a) Posting and online access. The barangay shall post the public directory at a
        conspicuous place in the barangay hall and may provide online access,
        provided that only public directory ields under Section 6(b) are displayed.

    (b) No mass release of con idential records. Requests for copies shall be
        limited to the public directory. The barangay shall not release the con idential
        Registry in whole or in substantial part, except as authorized by law, by the
        registrant’s consent, or pursuant to a valid data sharing agreement under
        Section 5(d).

    (c) Response time. The barangay shall act on requests within a reasonable
        period to be set in the IRR, balancing accessibility and privacy/security
        safeguards.

SECTION 8. Prohibition on Fees; Optional ID. —

   (a) No registration fee. Registration shall be free of charge.

   (b) Cost recovery. A reasonable fee may be charged only to cover the actual cost
       of printing or photocopying the public directory, subject to existing accounting
       and auditing rules.

   (c) Optional barangay-issued worker card. The barangay may issue an optional
       skilled worker card upon request, charging only the actual cost of production.
       The card shall not be used for political promotion and shall contain only the
       minimum necessary details consistent with this Act and data privacy
       principles.

SECTION 9. Updating; Validation. —

   (a) The barangay shall update the Registry at least once every year, and shall allow
       registrants to request corrections or updates at any time.

   (b) The barangay may periodically validate residency and continued willingness
       to be listed, using procedures that are proportionate and minimally intrusive.

SECTION 10. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days
from effectivity, the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG), in
consultation with the National Privacy Commission, TESDA, DOLE, leagues of LGUs,
and other stakeholders, shall issue the implementing rules and regulations.

The IRR shall include templates for registration forms, privacy notices, consent forms,
directory formats, retention schedules, data sharing agreements, and minimum
security standards, guided by the Data Privacy Act principles.

SECTION 11. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional or invalid, the remainder not affected thereby shall remain in full
force and effect.

SECTION 12. Repealing Clause. — All laws, decrees, orders, issuances or portions
thereof, which are inconsistent with the provisions of this Act are hereby repealed or
modi ied accordingly.

SECTION 13. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect ifteen (15) days after its
publication in the Of icial Gazette or in a newspapers of general circulation.
