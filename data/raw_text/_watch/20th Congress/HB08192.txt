                             AN ACT
  STANDARDIZING APPLICATION-STAGE REQUIREMENTS AND ENABLING
  CONSENT-BASED QR VERIFICATION THROUGH eGOVPH, AMENDING FOR
    THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11261, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

         Article II, Section 13 of the 1987 Constitution recognizes the vital role of the youth in
nation-building and mandates the State to promote their well-being. This is complemented by the
State’s policy to promote full employment and equality of employment opportunities for all.

        To reduce the financial barriers faced by first-time jobseekers, Congress enacted Republic
Act No. 11261, otherwise known as the “First Time Jobseekers Assistance Act,” which waives
government fees and charges for the issuance of pre-employment documents commonly required in
hiring. While this relief remains important, the realities of today’s job market show that financial
cost is only one barrier: applicants still face fragmented, repetitive, and sometimes unsafe
documentary demands at the earliest stage of recruitment—often across multiple applications, even
before any job offer is made.

        This measure, to be known as the “Ginhawa sa Paghahanap ng Trabaho Act,”
strengthens and modernizes Republic Act No. 11261 through a practical, two-part reform:

         First, it standardizes the minimum government-issued documentary requirements
that may be required during the application stage and prohibits abusive practices such as
requiring applicants to submit, surrender, deposit, or leave behind original civil registry documents
(e.g., birth or marriage certificates) as a condition for screening or interviews. It also adopts a No
Multiple Clearance Rule to prevent duplicative security clearances for the same purpose.

         Second, it institutionalizes a consent-based Employment Verification Service integrated
with the eGovPH platform, enabling QR-based verification of select job-application
information. This reduces repeated hardcopy submissions, speeds up hiring decisions, and improves
verification—while ensuring compliance with the Data Privacy Act, cybersecurity standards,
auditability, and assisted access channels for applicants without smartphones or reliable internet
connectivity.

        This measure forms part of the broader GINHAWA K.I.T. (Kuryente–Internet–
Trabaho) framework—an integrated set of policy reforms designed to move Filipino families from
survival to opportunity by easing everyday burdens that block upward mobility. Within this
framework, the Trabaho component focuses on removing the practical barriers that prevent
jobseekers—especially first-time jobseekers—from entering decent work quickly and fairly.

        To encourage employers to invest in training and absorb first-time jobseekers—particularly
during the crucial early months of employment—this bill aligns implementation with the CREATE
MORE Act (Republic Act No. 12066) through guidance and recognition of allowable labor and
training deductions under the Enhanced Deductions Regime (EDR) for Registered Business
Enterprises (RBEs) with Strategic Investment Priority Plan (SIPP)-qualified registered
projects or activities. This provides a performance-based environment for employer participation
without creating a broad or unmanageable fiscal burden.

                             AN ACT
  STANDARDIZING APPLICATION-STAGE REQUIREMENTS AND ENABLING
  CONSENT-BASED QR VERIFICATION THROUGH eGOVPH, AMENDING FOR
    THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 11261, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES
Be it enacted by the Senate and the House of Representatives of the Philippines in Congress

Section 1. Short Title. — This Act shall be known as the “Trabaho Agad Act.”

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. — It is the policy of the State to promote full employment and
equality of employment opportunities for all, and to strengthen support for jobseekers—especially
first-time jobseekers—by reducing both monetary and procedural barriers to entry into the labor
force.

Accordingly, the State shall: (a) standardize application-stage government documentary
requirements; (b) protect applicants against unreasonable or unsafe document-handling practices; (c)
enable consent-based, secure, and auditable digital verification through the eGovPH platform; and
(d) ensure accessibility so that digital reforms do not exclude jobseekers without smartphones or
reliable internet connectivity.

Section 3. Definition of Terms. — As used in this Act:

    a. Application Stage refers to the period covering submission of an application, initial
       screening, interview scheduling, interviews, and other preliminary assessments prior to a
       written or provable conditional offer of employment.

    b. Pre-Employment Stage refers to the period after a written or provable conditional offer of
       employment, covering onboarding requirements prior to commencement of work.

    c. Jobseeker refers to any person applying for employment locally or abroad. References to
       first-time jobseekers shall have the meaning provided under Republic Act No. 11261 and its
       implementing rules.

    d. Employer refers to any person, natural or juridical, in the private or public sector, that hires
       employees and requires applicants to submit documentary requirements in connection with
       employment.

    e. National ID refers to the Philippine Identification System (PhilSys) National ID, including
       the PhilSys number and its government-issued equivalents or verifiable formats (e.g., printed
       or digital formats issued under PhilSys and eGovPH systems), as may be recognized under
       the implementing rules.

    f. NBI Clearance refers to a clearance issued by the National Bureau of Investigation.

    g. CTC of Transcript of Records (TOR) refers to a transcript of records certified as a true
       copy by the issuing school or other competent authority under applicable rules; and includes
       equivalent certified proof of educational attainment, training completion, or competency
       credentials as may be recognized under the implementing rules.

    h. Employment Verification Service refers to the consent-based service integrated with
       eGovPH or its successor platform that enables employers to verify select job-application
         information through QR-enabled verification, subject to privacy, cybersecurity, and audit
         requirements.

    i.   Covered Government Agencies refers to all government agencies and instrumentalities
         including government- owned and controlled corporations (GOCC), state universities and
         colleges (SUCs) and local government units (LGUs), enumerated under Section 3 of
         Republic Act No. 11261.

Section 4. Standardization of Required Documents for Application-Stage. — For purposes of employment,
and insofar as reasonably necessary for screening during the Application Stage, employers shall
require only the following government-issued documentary requirements:

    a. Identification, through the Philippine National ID;

    b. Security clearance, through an NBI Clearance, where reasonably required by the nature of
       the position; and

    c. Competency credentials, through certified true copies of school records or equivalent proof
       of educational attainment.

No additional government-issued documentary requirements shall be imposed during the
Application Stage unless expressly required by law or by the nature of the position as may be
justified under the implementing rules.

Nothing in this Section shall prevent employers from requiring non-government documentary
submissions customary in hiring, such as résumés, application forms, portfolios, work samples, or
employment history declarations.

Section 5. Prohibited Acts at the Application Stage. — At the Application Stage, it shall be unlawful for
an employer to:

    a. Require the submission, surrender, deposit, or retention of any original civil registry
       document, including but not limited to a birth certificate, marriage certificate, or similar
       record, as a condition for proceeding with screening or interviews;

    b. Require an applicant to leave any original document with the employer for safekeeping,
       verification, or any similar purpose;

    c. Impose duplicative documentary requirements that are not reasonably necessary for
       application-stage screening.

Section 6. No Multiple Clearance Rule. — Where an employer requires NBI Clearance at the
Application Stage, the employer shall not additionally require a police clearance, or similar local
clearance for the same security- screening purpose, unless a specific law or regulation expressly
requires otherwise for the position.
Section 7. Transparency in Job Postings and Application Forms. — Employers shall disclose in job
postings or application forms, in a manner prescribed by the implementing agencies, the standard
application documentary requirements under this Act, including the prohibition on requiring original
civil registry documents and the No Multiple Clearance Rule.


Section 8. Establishment of the eGov PH Employment Verification Service. — The Department of
Information and Communications Technology (DICT), in coordination with the Department of
Labor and Employment (DOLE), the Civil Service Commission (CSC), and other relevant agencies,
shall establish an Employment Verification Service integrated with eGovPH or its successor
platform. The Service shall enable employers to verify select job-application information through
consent-based digital verification, consistent with data privacy and cybersecurity standards.

The Service shall support QR-enabled verification, whereby a jobseeker may generate and present
a QR code, or obtain a printed verification receipt through assisted channels, that an employer may
scan to receive a verification response from the system, subject to the safeguards of this Act and its
implementing rules.

Section 9. Covered Information for Verification; Minimum Scope. — The Employment Verification
Service shall, at a minimum, support consent-based verification of standard required documents as
provided under Section 4 of this Act.

Subject to technical readiness and inter-agency agreements, the Service may progressively support
verification of other employment-relevant government records, including professional licenses, civil
service eligibility, and training certifications, consistent with the implementing rules.

Section 10. Duty to Accept Verifiable Digital Equivalents; Non-Duplication. — Where a covered record is
verifiable through the Employment Verification Service, the employer shall accept such verification
response as sufficient for the same purpose, and shall not require repeated hardcopy submissions of
the same record, unless a specific law or regulation expressly requires submission of a physical
original.

This Section shall apply without prejudice to onboarding requirements at the Pre-Employment Stage
that are expressly required by law, including requirements for government-mandated benefits
enrollment.

Section 11. Data Protection, Security, and Auditability. — All processing under this Act shall comply
with the Data Privacy Act and Relevant issuances of the National Privacy Commission. At a
minimum, the DICT and implementing agencies shall ensure:

   a. Affirmative and revocable consent for verification;

   b. Purpose limitation to employment application and onboarding;

   c. Data minimization;
   d. Appropriate security controls; and

   e. Audit logs and verification receipts under the implementing rules

Section 12. Accessibility and Non-Exclusion. — Implementation of digital verification shall not
exclude jobseekers without smartphones or stable internet access. DOLE, in coordination with
DICT and relevant offices, shall provide assisted channels, including through Public Employment
Service Offices (PESOs) or other service points, enabling jobseekers to generate Verification Tokens
or obtain equivalent verification assistance.

Section 13. Good-Faith Reliance; Safe Harbor. — An employer that relies in good faith on a
verification response issued through the Employment Verification Service, and complies with
retention limits and privacy rules, shall be deemed to have exercised due diligence for purposes of
identity and credential checking, without prejudice to additional checks required by law for regulated
professions.

Section 14. Implementation Timeline. —

The DICT shall commence development and initial deployment of the Employment Verification
Service within twelve (12) months from the effectiveness of this Act, prioritizing identity
verification.

Integration for NBI clearance verification and education credential verification shall be implemented
progressively, subject to technical readiness, inter-agency coordination, and standards prescribed in
the implementing rules.

Section 15. Enforcement and Complaints. —

   (a) For the private sector, DOLE shall enforce this Act through existing labor standards
       enforcement mechanisms, including complaint handling, compliance orders, and inspections
       as applicable;

   (b) For the public sector, CSC shall issue guidelines and enforce compliance consistent with civil
       service rules;

   (c) This Act shall be implemented without prejudice to the authority of the National Privacy
       Commission and other agencies under existing laws.

Section 16. Penalties. —

   (a) Any employer who violates this Act shall be subject to administrative penalties, including
       compliance orders and fines, in amounts and under procedures to be provided in the
       implementing rules, taking into account the gravity and frequency of the violation.

   (b) For juridical entities, responsible officers who authorized or enforced prohibited practices
       may be held liable under the implementing rules.
   (c) For government offices, violations shall be grounds for appropriate administrative action
       under civil service rules, without prejudice to other liabilities under existing laws.

Section 17. Fiscal Incentives for Private Sector Participation. — To encourage employer participation in
the streamlined hiring and verification framework under this Act, Registered Business Enterprises
(RBEs) with projects or activities qualified under the Strategic Investment Priority Plan (SIPP) and
availing of the Enhanced Deductions Regime (EDR) under the National Internal Revenue Code, as
amended by Republic Act No. 12066, shall be eligible to claim the incentives below subject to
existing EDR rules, FIRB/IPA regulations, and BIR substantiation and audit requirements.

   (a) Additional Deduction for Training Expenses. — An RBE shall be entitled to an
       additional deduction from gross income equivalent to one hundred percent (100%) of the
       actual costs incurred for the labor training of first-time jobseekers hired under this Act,
       provided such training is directly related to the registered project or activity.

   (b) Additional Deduction for Labor Productivity. — The RBE shall be entitled to an
       additional deduction of fifty percent (50%) of the direct labor expenses (wages) paid to said
       jobseekers during their first six (6) months of employment, subject to existing EDR rules.

   (c) Digital Transition Treatment (Compliance and Integration Costs). — For purposes of
       determining “directly attributable” expenses under Section 295 of the Tax Code and related
       rules, reasonable and documented costs incurred by an RBE to comply with the
       requirements of this Act—including technical integration with the eGovPH Employment
       Verification Service, process redesign, and necessary systems configuration—shall be treated
       as directly attributable administrative expenses to the extent used for and allocated to the
       registered project or activity, and may be considered in the computation of deductible
       expenses and, where applicable, VAT zero-rating eligibility for export enterprises, consistent
       with existing law and regulations.

   (d) Fast-Lane Guidance and Standard Documentation. — Within ninety (90) days from the
       effectivity of this Act, the Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB), in coordination with the
       concerned Investment Promotion Agencies (IPAs) and the Bureau of Internal Revenue
       (BIR), shall issue standardized guidelines and templates to operationalize this Section,
       including:

           1. the minimum documentation and proof of payment requirements for training and
              labor expenses;

           2. the criteria for training programs that qualify under paragraph (b);

           3. the standard method of verifying that a hire is a “first-time jobseeker” for purposes
              of this Section (including, where applicable, the use of the eGovPH verification
              workflow); and
            4. such audit and reporting measures as may be necessary to prevent abuse while
               keeping compliance reasonable.

    (e) Consistency with Governance Rules. — The availment of these incentives shall be
        governed by the Fiscal Incentives Review Board (FIRB) and the respective Investment
        Promotion Agencies (IPAs) to ensure compliance with the performance-based and time-
        bound nature of the CREATE MORE Act.

Section 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — Within ninety (90) days from effectivity, DOLE and
DICT, in coordination with CSC, PSA, NBI, CHED, TESDA, SUCs, the National Privacy
Commission, and other relevant agencies, shall promulgate the implementing rules and regulations
to carry out this Act.

Section 19. Appropriations. — The amounts necessary to carry out this Act shall be charged against
the current appropriations of concerned agencies. Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall
be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.

Section 20. Separability Clause. — If any portion or provision of this Act is declared
unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act or any provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
force and effect.

Section 21. Repealing Clause. — Any law, presidential decree or issuance, executive order, letter of
instruction, rule, or regulation inconsistent with the provisions not affected thereby shall remain in
force and effect.

Section 22. Effectivity. — This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its complete publication in
the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
