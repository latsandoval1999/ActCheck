20                                              EXPLANATORY NOTE
21
22   A stronger Philippine economy begins with Filipinos who have the knowledge and
23   opportunity to build businesses.
24
25   Micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) are undeniably the backbone of the
26   Philippine economy. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) 2023 List of
27   Establishments, MSMEs account for 99.6 percent of all business establishments and
28   67.0 percent of total employment in the country. They also contribute an estimated 25
29   percent of total export revenue and 40 percent of the country's GDP.
30
31   These figures only show that when Filipino entrepreneurs succeed, the Philippine
32   economy grows with them.
33
34   However, starting and sustaining a business remains a challenge for many Filipinos.
35   Access to proper training, business knowledge, technology, mentoring, financing, and
36   markets remains uneven, while government programs for entrepreneurship are spread
37   across different agencies and institutions. The market is here, and the audience is very
38   much alive.
39
40   Hence, there is a need for a dedicated institution that brings these efforts together and
41   makes entrepreneurship education more accessible and practical.
42
43   The proposed measure which establishes the Philippine Entrepreneurs Academy seeks
44   to address this need by establishing a national institution dedicated to entrepreneurship
45   education, training, research, and industry development. This will offer degree
46   programs, technical-vocational courses, short-term training, and research while working
47   with TESDA, DTI, DOST, the private sector, and other stakeholders.
48
 1   The Academy will bridge the gap between learning how to start a business and actually
 2   building one. Through industry partnerships and practical training fit for the selected
 3   industry, it will equip aspiring and current entrepreneurs with the exact skills needed to
 4   establish, sustain, and grow their businesses.
 5
 6   By helping more Filipinos turn ideas into sustainable and profitable enterprises, the
 7   Academy can help create businesses that last, jobs that remain, and communities that
 8   grow.
 9
10   In view of the foregoing, the immediate passage of this bill is earnestly sought.

21                                AN ACT
22        ESTABLISHING THE PHILIPPINE ENTREPRENEURS ACADEMY AND
23                    APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR
24
25   Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the Philippines in
26   Congress assembled:
27
28         SECTION 1. Short Title. – This Act shall be known as the “Philippine
29   Entrepreneurs Academy Act.”
30
31          SEC. 2. Establishment. – There is hereby established a learning institution,
32   under the supervision of the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), to be known
33   as the “Philippine Entrepreneurs Academy”, hereinafter referred to as the Academy.
34
35           SEC. 3. General Mandate. – The Academy shall provide undergraduate and
36   graduate degree programs, short-term technical-vocational nondegree courses and
37   modular trainings that will enhance the core competencies of individuals on
38   entrepreneurship. It shall cater to technical-vocational, college and graduate
39   students. It shall promote the relevance of entrepreneurship to job generation and its
40   significant role to the sustainable economic growth of the country.
41
42          The Academy shall formalize and integrate, under a school system, the
43   nonformal entrepreneurship programs and short-term technical-vocational courses of
44   the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), Department of
45   Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Science and Technology (DOST), and
46   other government agencies. It shall administer education and consultancy, and
47   supervise publication on entrepreneurship. It shall also conduct scientific and policy-
48   oriented research and training.
49
50         The Academy shall comply with the requirements and standards of the
51   CHED and the provisions of Republic Act No. 8292, otherwise known as the “Higher
52   Education Modernization Act of 1997”, and other pertinent laws on higher education.
53
54           SEC. 4. Curricular Offerings. – The Academy shall offer undergraduate and
 1   graduate degree programs and short-term diploma or certificate courses on
 2   entrepreneurship, including entrepreneurship development in the fields of agriculture,
 3   trade, technology, and the manufacturing sector. The curriculum and modules of the
 4   programs shall be designed and developed following the competency-based
 5   curriculum model and in accordance with the mandate of this Act.
 6
 7          SEC. 5. School Sites. - The Academy shall have two (2) campuses, with the
 8   main campus to be established in the Clark Freeport and Special Economic Zone
 9   located in the Provinces of Pampanga and Tarlac, to be known as the “Philippine
10   Entrepreneurs Academy Main Campus-Clark”. The other campus shall be located
11   in the City of Baguio, to be known as the “Philippine Entrepreneurs Academy-
12   Baguio City Campus”.
13
14         SEC. 6. Governing Board. - The governing board of the Academy shall
15   be the Board of Trustees, hereinafter referred to as the Board, which shall be
16   chaired and co-chaired by the Chairperson of the CHED, and the President of the
17   Academy, respectively, with the following as members:
18
19         a) Chairperson of the Committee on Higher, Technical and Vocational Education
20         of the Senate;
21         b) Chairperson of the Committee on Higher and Technical Education of the
22         House of Representatives;
23         c) Director General of the TESDA;
24         d) Director General of the National Economic and Development Authority
25         (NEDA);
26         e) Secretary of the DOST;
27         f) Secretary of the DTI;
28         g) President of the federation of faculty associations of the Academy;
29         h) President of the federation of student councils of the Academy;
30         i) President of the federation of alumni associations of the Academy; and
31         j) two (2) prominent citizens from the private sector who have distinguished
32         themselves in their professions or fields of specialization.
33
34          The members of the Board shall not receive any compensation or remuneration
35   for their services as such, but may be reimbursed for actual and necessary expenses
36   incurred in discharging the business of the Academy.
37
38          SEC. 7. Powers and Duties of the Board. - The Board shall be the highest
39   policymaking body of the Academy. In addition to its general powers of administration
40   and the exercise of all the powers granted to the Board of a corporation under existing
41   laws, the Board shall have the following specific powers and duties:
42

44         policies and pertinent provisions of the Philippine Constitution on education, as
45         well as policies, standards and thrusts of the CHED under Republic Act No.
46         7722, otherwise known as the “Higher Education Act of 1994”;
47
48         b) Adopt rules and regulations not contrary to law, as may be necessary to
49         carry out the purposes and functions of the Academy, and to effectively
50         discharge its responsibilities;
51
52         c) Receive assistance from any legitimate source to augment the internal sources of
 1   funds, as may be provided, for the support of the Academy in the manner it may
 2   determine, in its discretion, to carry out the purposes and functions of the Academy;
 3   d) Constitute the Board, as herein defined;
 4

 6   the President in the administration of the affairs of the Academy;
 7
 8   f) Review periodically the administrative procedures and programs of the Academy;

10   properties of all kinds, and to administer and dispose the same when
11   necessary for the benefit of the Academy, subject to limitations, discretions
12   and instructions of the donors, if any;
13
14   h) Fix the tuition fees and other school charges such as matriculation fees,
15   graduation fees, laboratory fees, and other miscellaneous fees as the Board

19   the CHED; Such fees and charges, including government subsidies and other
20   incomes generated by the Academy, shall constitute its regular fund and shall
21   be deposited in any authorized government depository bank, and all interests that
22   shall accrue therefrom shall form part of the same fund for the use of the
23   Academy. Any provision of existing law, rule and regulation to the contrary
24   notwithstanding, any income generated by the Academy from tuition fees and
25   other charges, as well as from the operation of auxiliary services and land

28   the Academy: Provided, That all fiduciary fees shall be disbursed for the specific
29   purposes for which they were collected. If for reason beyond its control, the
30   Academy shall not be able to pursue any project for which funds have been
31   appropriated and allocated under its approved program of expenditures, the
32   Board may authorize the use of said funds for any reasonable purpose which, in
33   its discretion, may be necessary and urgent for the attainment of the objectives
34   and goals of the Academy;
35
36   i) Adopt and implement a socialized tuition scheme for greater access of poor,

39

41   and other facilities, as well as the purchase and acquisition of real and personal
42   properties, including the necessary supplies, materials, and equipment; Appoint,
43   upon the recommendation of the President of the Academy, the deans, executive
44   directors, heads of departments, faculty members, and other officials and
45   employees;
46

48   employees, subject to the provisions of Republic Act No. 11466, otherwise

51   and regulations governing hours of service and related concerns, and other

36

16          SEC. 8. President of the Academy. - The Academy shall be headed by a
17   President, who shall render full-time service and shall be appointed by the Board upon
18   the recommendation of a duly constituted Search Committee. The President shall also
19   be the chief executive officer of the Academy.
20
21         The President shall have a term of four (4) years and shall be eligible for
22   reappointment to another term. Within six (6) months before the expiration of the
23   term of office of the incumbent President of the Academy, the Board shall constitute
24   the Search Committee for the Presidency.
25
26            The President shall receive such compensation and remuneration in
27   accordance with Republic Act No. 11466 or the “Salary Standardization Law of 2019”,
28   and by such other laws amendatory thereto, which may hereinafter be enacted, and
29   shall be comparable to that being received by the Presidents of similar educational
30   institutions.
31
32         The President shall exercise the following powers and duties:
33
34          a) Submit, for the consideration of the Board and the Executive Committee, the
35   policies and measures which are necessary to carry out the purposes of the Academy;
36
37        b) Recommend, coordinate, and administer the programs and projects of the
38   Academy;
39
40          c) Direct and supervise the operations and internal administration of the
41   Academy, and delegate administrative responsibilities in accordance with the rules and
42   regulations of the Academy;
43
44         d) Submit an annual report to the Board setting forth the accomplishments of
45   the Academy during the year, its financial operations and status, and a program and
46   budget for the ensuing year; and
47
48         e) Exercise other powers and discharge other functions, as may be vested by
49   the Board and the Executive Committee.
50

 2   of vacancy in the Office of the President by reason of death, compulsory retirement,
 3   resignation, removal for cause or incapacity of the President to perform the functions

10
11          SEC. 9. Executive Committee. - There shall be an Executive Committee
12   consisting of the President of the Academy and four (4) other members to be elected by
13   the Board from its members. Each Members of the Executive Committee, other than
14   the President of the Academy, shall hold office for a term of two (2) years, and shall
15   be eligible for re-election, unless at the time of election, a shorter term is specified. The
16    Board shall elect the Chairperson of the Executive Committee from the membership
17   of the Board.
18
19          SEC. 10. Functions of the Executive Committee. - The Executive Committee
20   shall administer the affairs of the Academy in accordance with such functions, powers
21   and responsibilities as may be delegated by the Board.
22
23           SEC. 11. Government Support and Coordination. – The CHED Chairperson is
24   hereby authorized to call upon any department, bureau, agency or instrumentality of the
25   government for such assistance as may be necessary for the effective implementation
26   of this Act.
27
28          SEC. 12. Tax Exemption. – The importation of economic, technical and cultural
29   books and publications, which are for the economic, technical, vocational, scientific,
30   philosophical, historical or cultural purposes of the Academy, upon certification by the
31   CHED, shall be exempt from customs duties in accordance with the provisions of
32   Republic Act No. 10863, otherwise known as the “Customs Modernization and Tariff
33   Act”.
34
35          All grants, bequests, endowments, donations and contributions made to the
36   Academy, to be used actually, directly and exclusively by it, shall be exempt from
37   donor’s tax and the same shall be considered as allowable deduction from the gross
38   income in the computation of the income tax of the donor, in accordance with the
39   provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code of 1997, as amended.
40
41          SEC. 13. Appropriations. – The amount necessary to carry out the provisions of
42   this Act shall be included in the annual General Appropriations Act.
43
44           SEC. 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. – Within ninety (90) days from
45   the effectivity of this Act, the CHED shall, in coordination with the TESDA and the
46   DTI, formulate the necessary rules and regulations to fully implement the provisions
47   of this Act.
48
49           SEC. 15. Separability Clause. – If, for any reason, any part or provision of this
50   Act is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining parts or provisions not affected
51   shall remain in full force and effect.
